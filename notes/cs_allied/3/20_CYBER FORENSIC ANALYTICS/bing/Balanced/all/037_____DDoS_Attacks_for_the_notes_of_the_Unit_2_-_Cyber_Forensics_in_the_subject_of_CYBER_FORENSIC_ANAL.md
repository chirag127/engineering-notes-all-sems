# DDoS Attacks

- DDoS stands for Distributed Denial of Service, which is a type of cyberattack that aims to disrupt the availability of a network or web service by sending multiple requests to it, so large and massive that the service becomes unavailable.
- DDoS attacks are often carried out by hackers who use compromised computers and networks, called botnets, to generate the traffic. Botnets can be created by infecting devices with malware, exploiting vulnerabilities, or hijacking credentials.
- DDoS attacks can target different layers of the network stack, such as the application layer, the protocol layer, or the volumetric layer. Each layer has different characteristics and requires different mitigation strategies .
  - Application layer attacks target the logic and functionality of a web service, such as HTTP requests, DNS queries, or SMTP commands. These attacks can be low in bandwidth but high in complexity and sophistication, and can bypass firewalls and other security measures. Examples of application layer attacks are HTTP floods, DNS amplification, and slowloris .
  - Protocol layer attacks target the network protocols that enable communication between devices, such as TCP, UDP, ICMP, or SYN. These attacks can consume server or network resources, such as memory, CPU, or bandwidth, and cause connection timeouts, packet loss, or network congestion. Examples of protocol layer attacks are SYN floods, UDP floods, ICMP floods, and TCP reset attacks .
  - Volumetric layer attacks target the network infrastructure and bandwidth, such as routers, switches, or links. These attacks can generate a large amount of traffic, measured in bits per second (bps) or packets per second (pps), and overwhelm the network capacity or the bandwidth of the target. Examples of volumetric layer attacks are NTP amplification, UDP fragmentation, and spoofed IP packets .
- DDoS attacks can have various motives and impacts, such as extorting money, disrupting competitors, protesting against policies, or damaging reputations. DDoS attacks can affect the availability, performance, and security of web services, and cause financial losses, customer dissatisfaction, or legal liabilities.
- Cyber forensics is the process of collecting, analyzing, and presenting digital evidence related to cyberattacks, such as DDoS attacks. Cyber forensics can help determine the source, method, intent, and extent of a cyberattack, and support the identification, prosecution, or mitigation of the attackers.
- The forensic procedure in investigating DDoS attacks can vary depending on the type, scale, and complexity of the attack, but it generally involves the following steps :
  - Preparation: This step involves defining the scope, objectives, and resources of the investigation, as well as obtaining the necessary authorization, tools, and equipment. It also involves establishing a chain of custody and a documentation process for the evidence.
  - Preservation: This step involves securing and isolating the affected network or web service, and preventing any alteration, deletion, or tampering of the evidence. It also involves identifying and collecting the relevant data sources, such as logs, packets, or malware samples, and creating forensic copies or images of them.
  - Analysis: This step involves examining and interpreting the collected data, using various techniques and tools, such as network traffic analysis, malware analysis, or data visualization. It also involves identifying and correlating the indicators of compromise, such as IP addresses, domains, ports, protocols, or patterns, and reconstructing the timeline, sequence, and impact of the attack.
  - Presentation: This step involves reporting and presenting the findings and conclusions of the analysis, using clear, concise, and accurate language, and supporting them with evidence and references. It also involves providing recommendations and suggestions for improving the security and resilience of the network or web service, and preventing or mitigating future attacks.
