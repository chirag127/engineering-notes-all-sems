# Internet Gambling

- Internet gambling is the activity of placing bets on sports, games, or contests using a wire communication facility, such as the Internet.
- Internet gambling is illegal in many countries, including the United States, where it violates the Wire Act of 1961 .
- Internet gambling poses various risks and challenges for law enforcement, such as:
  - It allows anonymous and immediate communication that makes it difficult to trace gambling activity.
  - It can facilitate money laundering, fraud, and other criminal activities.
  - It can harm the integrity of sports and gaming industries.
  - It can exploit vulnerable individuals, such as minors and problem gamblers .
- Internet gambling platforms are also vulnerable to cyberattacks, such as:
  - Compromising online gambling accounts and cashing out balances.
  - Infecting devices with malware, such as viruses, Trojan horses, worms, and spyware.
  - Stealing personal and financial information from users.
  - Disrupting or manipulating the outcomes of games or bets.
- Internet gambling users can protect themselves from cyber risks by:
  - Using secure and reputable online gambling sites.
  - Avoiding clicking on suspicious links or attachments.
  - Installing and updating antivirus and firewall software.
  - Creating strong and unique passwords for online gambling accounts.
  - Limiting the amount of money and time spent on online gambling.
