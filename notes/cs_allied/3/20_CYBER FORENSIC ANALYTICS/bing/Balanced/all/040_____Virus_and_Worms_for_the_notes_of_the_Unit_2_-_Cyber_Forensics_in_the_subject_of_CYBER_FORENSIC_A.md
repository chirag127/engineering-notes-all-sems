# Virus and Worms

## Introduction

- Virus and worms are two types of malicious software (malware) that can infect and harm computer systems and networks.
- Virus and worms differ in how they spread and what they do to the infected systems.
- Virus and worms can cause various damages, such as deleting or encrypting files, stealing or modifying data, consuming resources, disrupting services, or opening backdoors for further attacks.
- Virus and worms can be detected and removed by using antivirus software and following cyber hygiene practices.

## Virus

- A virus is a malware that attaches itself to another program or file and executes when the host program is run.
- A virus can only spread when a user or another program activates the infected file or program.
- A virus can infect any type of file, such as executable files, documents, images, or scripts.
- A virus can have different payloads, such as displaying a message, deleting files, corrupting data, or downloading other malware.
- A virus can be classified into different types based on its infection method, such as file infectors, boot sector infectors, macro viruses, or polymorphic viruses.

## Worm

- A worm is a malware that can replicate itself and spread to other systems without user intervention or host program.
- A worm can spread through a network by exploiting vulnerabilities in software, sending email attachments, or using instant messages or spam emails.
- A worm can infect any type of system, such as computers, servers, routers, or mobile devices.
- A worm can have different payloads, such as creating a botnet, consuming bandwidth, crashing systems, or stealing information.
- A worm can be classified into different types based on its propagation method, such as mass-mailing worms, network worms, or internet worms.
