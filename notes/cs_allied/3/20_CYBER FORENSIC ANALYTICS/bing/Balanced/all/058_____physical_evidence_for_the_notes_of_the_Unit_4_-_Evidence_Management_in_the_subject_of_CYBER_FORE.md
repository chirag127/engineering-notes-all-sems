# Physical Evidence

Physical evidence is any tangible object or material that can be used to prove or disprove a fact in a cyber forensic investigation. Physical evidence can be collected from various sources, such as computers, mobile devices, removable media, cables, publications, trash, and other items that may contain digital data or traces of digital activity  .

Some of the characteristics and challenges of physical evidence are:

- It can be easily altered, damaged, or destroyed by improper handling, storage, or transportation .
- It can be encrypted, hidden, deleted, or overwritten by the suspect or by the system itself .
- It can be volatile, meaning that it can be lost or changed when the system is powered off or rebooted .
- It can be fragmented, meaning that it can be scattered across different locations, devices, or media .
- It can be large, meaning that it can require a lot of time, space, and resources to acquire, analyze, and preserve .

Some of the steps and procedures for collecting physical evidence are:

- Identification: The first step is to identify what evidence is present, where it is stored, and in what format it is stored .
- Authorization: The second step is to obtain proper authorization to legally access and seize the equipment used by the suspect .
- Documentation: The third step is to document the scene, the evidence, and the chain of custody, using photographs, sketches, notes, forms, and logs  .
- Preservation: The fourth step is to preserve the evidence by making copies, backups, or images of the data, and by using anti-static bags, tamper-evident seals, and labels to protect the original media  .
- Transportation: The fifth step is to transport the evidence to a secure location or a forensic laboratory, following the chain of custody and avoiding any physical or environmental damage  .
- Analysis: The sixth step is to analyze the evidence using specialized tools and techniques, such as data recovery, decryption, carving, hashing, and searching .
- Presentation: The seventh step is to present the evidence and the findings to the court or the client, using reports, exhibits, and testimony .

Physical evidence is a crucial component of cyber forensic investigations, as it can provide valuable information and insights into the nature, scope, and impact of a cybercrime. However, physical evidence also poses many challenges and requires careful and professional handling, following the principles of legality, authenticity, integrity, and reliability .
