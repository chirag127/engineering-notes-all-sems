## Unit 1 - Cyber Crime

Cyber crime is the use of a computer or a network as an instrument to commit illegal acts, such as fraud, identity theft, data theft, cyberextortion, ransomware, phishing, spoofing, etc. Cyber crime can harm individuals, organizations, or even nations by compromising their security, privacy, or financial well-being. Cyber crime can also affect the functioning of critical infrastructure, such as power grids, transportation systems, or health care services.

Some of the types and examples of cyber crime are:

- **Hacking**: Unauthorized access to a computer system or network, often with malicious intent. For example, hackers may steal confidential data, damage or delete files, install malware, or disrupt services.
- **Denial-of-service (DoS) attack**: An attempt to overwhelm a computer system or network with excessive traffic or requests, making it unavailable to legitimate users. For example, a DoS attack may target a website, an email server, or an online game.
- **Software piracy**: The illegal copying, distribution, or use of software without proper authorization or license. For example, software piracy may involve downloading or sharing copyrighted software, cracking or bypassing software protection, or selling counterfeit software.
- **Phishing**: A fraudulent technique of sending emails or other messages that appear to be from a legitimate source, but are actually designed to trick recipients into revealing personal or financial information, such as passwords, bank account details, or credit card numbers. For example, a phishing email may impersonate a bank, a government agency, or a well-known company, and ask the recipient to click on a link or open an attachment that leads to a fake website or a malware infection.
- **Spoofing**: A deceptive technique of falsifying the identity or origin of a communication, such as an email, a phone call, or a website. For example, spoofing may involve changing the sender's address, the caller ID, or the domain name, to make it look like the communication is coming from a trusted source, such as a friend, a colleague, or a reputable organization.
- **Identity theft**: The illegal acquisition and use of someone else's personal information, such as name, date of birth, social security number, or credit card number, for fraudulent purposes, such as opening bank accounts, applying for loans, or making purchases. For example, identity theft may occur when a cybercriminal obtains personal information from a data breach, a phishing email, a malware infection, or a stolen device.
- **Data theft**: The unauthorized copying, transfer, or disclosure of data, especially sensitive or confidential data, from a computer system or network. For example, data theft may involve stealing customer records, trade secrets, intellectual property, or classified information, for personal gain, espionage, or sabotage.
- **Cyberextortion**: The use of threats or coercion to extort money or other benefits from a victim, by exploiting their vulnerability or fear of a cyber attack. For example, cyberextortion may involve demanding a ransom to decrypt or restore data that has been encrypted or deleted by a ransomware attack, or to stop a DoS attack or a data leak.
- **Ransomware**: A type of malware that encrypts or locks the data or files on a victim's computer or device, and demands a ransom for the decryption key or the release of the data or files. For example, ransomware may infect a computer or device through a phishing email, a malicious attachment, or a compromised website, and display a message or a screen that instructs the victim to pay a certain amount of money, usually in cryptocurrency, to restore access to their data or files.
- **Cryptojacking**: The unauthorized use of someone else's computer or device resources to mine cryptocurrency, such as Bitcoin or Ethereum, without their knowledge or consent. For example, cryptojacking may involve embedding a malicious code or script on a website, an app, or an email, that runs in the background and consumes the CPU, memory, or battery power of the visitor's or user's computer or device, to generate cryptocurrency for the cybercriminal.
