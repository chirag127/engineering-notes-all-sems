# Investigating Computer Intrusions

Computer intrusions are unauthorized or illegal access to computer systems or networks, which may result in data theft, damage, or disruption. Computer intrusions can be perpetrated by individuals, groups, or state-sponsored actors, and can target various types of systems, such as personal computers, servers, routers, or mobile devices.

Investigating computer intrusions is a complex and challenging task that requires specialized skills, tools, and procedures. The following are some general steps that can be followed to conduct a computer intrusion investigation, based on various sources  :

- **Identification**: The first step is to identify the incident, its scope, and its impact. This may involve receiving a report from the victim, a third party, or an automated alert system, and verifying the authenticity and validity of the information. The identification step also involves determining the legal authority and jurisdiction for the investigation, and obtaining any necessary warrants or permissions.
- **Preservation**: The second step is to preserve the evidence and prevent any further damage or tampering. This may involve isolating the affected system or network, making a forensic copy of the data, documenting the scene and the chain of custody, and securing the physical and digital access to the evidence.
- **Collection**: The third step is to collect the relevant data and information from the evidence and other sources. This may involve extracting and analyzing the data from the forensic copy, obtaining logs and records from the victim, the source, or the intermediary systems, and interviewing witnesses or suspects.
- **Examination**: The fourth step is to examine the data and information in detail and identify any anomalies, patterns, or indicators of compromise. This may involve using various forensic tools and techniques, such as file carving, hashing, timeline analysis, malware analysis, network analysis, or reverse engineering.
- **Analysis**: The fifth step is to analyze the findings and draw conclusions about the intrusion. This may involve reconstructing the events and actions of the intruder, determining the motive, intent, and identity of the intruder, assessing the damage and the risk of the intrusion, and identifying any vulnerabilities or weaknesses that facilitated the intrusion.
- **Presentation**: The sixth step is to present the results and recommendations of the investigation to the relevant stakeholders, such as the victim, the law enforcement, the court, or the media. This may involve preparing a written report, a verbal briefing, or a testimony, and providing the evidence and the supporting documentation in a clear, concise, and accurate manner.
- **Incident Response**: The final step is to respond to the incident and prevent or mitigate any future intrusions. This may involve restoring the affected system or network, implementing security measures and best practices, reporting the incident to the appropriate authorities, and following up on the investigation and the prosecution of the intruder.
