# Cyberstalking

- Cyberstalking is the repeated use of electronic communications to harass or frighten someone, for example by sending threatening emails.
- Cyberstalking can take many forms, including: harassment, embarrassment and humiliation of the victim, defamation, slander and libel, monitoring, identity theft, threats, vandalism, solicitation for sex, doxing, or blackmail.
- Cyberstalking is a criminal offense under various state anti-stalking, slander and harassment laws. A conviction can result in a restraining order, probation, or criminal penalties against the assailant, including jail.
- Cyberstalking is different from cyberbullying, which is the use of electronic communication to bully someone, typically by sending messages of an intimidating or threatening nature. Cyberbullying is more common among children and teenagers, while cyberstalking is more common among adults.
- Cyberstalking can have serious psychological and emotional effects on the victim, such as anxiety, depression, fear, paranoia, isolation, low self-esteem, and post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD).
- Cyberstalking can be prevented or stopped by taking some measures, such as: blocking or reporting the stalker, changing passwords and privacy settings, saving evidence of the harassment, informing friends and family, contacting the authorities, and seeking professional help.
