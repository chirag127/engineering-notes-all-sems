# Electronic Governance for the notes of the Unit 5 - Cyber Laws and Authorities in the subject of CYBER FORENSIC ANALYTICS

- Electronic governance (e-governance) is the use of information and communication technologies (ICTs) to improve the efficiency, effectiveness, transparency and accountability of public services and democratic processes.
- E-governance can enhance the delivery of public services, such as health, education, social welfare, taxation, justice and security, by reducing costs, increasing accessibility, improving quality and ensuring citizen participation.
- E-governance can also foster democratic values, such as human rights, rule of law, equality and justice, by enabling citizens to access information, voice their opinions, hold authorities accountable and influence decision-making.
- However, e-governance also poses significant challenges and risks, such as cyberattacks, data breaches, privacy violations, digital divide, digital exclusion, digital illiteracy and digital authoritarianism.
- Therefore, e-governance requires a robust legal and regulatory framework that ensures the protection of fundamental rights and freedoms, the security of critical infrastructure and information systems, the compliance with international standards and best practices, and the cooperation among all stakeholders, including governments, private sector, civil society and international organizations.
- Some of the key cyber laws and authorities that regulate and oversee e-governance are:

  - The U.S. Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency (CISA), which is responsible for leading the national effort to protect and enhance the resilience of the nation's physical and cyber infrastructure.
  - The U.S. Federal Trade Commission (FTC), which is the primary federal agency that enforces consumer protection laws and promotes competition in the marketplace, including in the areas of privacy, data security and identity theft.
  - The U.S. Department of Justice (DOJ), which is the chief law enforcement and legal arm of the federal government, and prosecutes cybercrimes, such as hacking, fraud, espionage and terrorism, under various statutes, such as the Computer Fraud and Abuse Act, the Electronic Communications Privacy Act and the Patriot Act.
  - The U.S. Department of Homeland Security (DHS), which is the cabinet-level department that coordinates the domestic security efforts of the federal government, and provides guidance, resources and assistance to state and local governments, private sector and critical infrastructure owners and operators, and the general public on cybersecurity and cyber resilience.
  - The U.S. Department of Defense (DOD), which is the executive branch department that oversees the military and national security operations of the federal government, and develops and implements policies, strategies and capabilities to deter, defend and respond to cyber threats and attacks against the U.S. and its allies.
  - The U.S. National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST), which is a non-regulatory federal agency that develops and promotes voluntary standards, guidelines and best practices for measurement, science, technology and innovation, including in the fields of cybersecurity and privacy.
  - The U.S. National Security Agency (NSA), which is a military intelligence agency that collects, processes and disseminates signals intelligence and information assurance, and conducts research and development in cryptography and cybersecurity.
  - The U.S. Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency Act of 2018, which established CISA as a standalone federal agency within DHS, and authorized it to carry out various functions and responsibilities related to cybersecurity and infrastructure security, such as risk assessment, incident response, information sharing, technical assistance, education and awareness, and research and development.
  - The U.S. Cybersecurity Enhancement Act of 2014, which amended the National Institute of Standards and Technology Act and the Homeland Security Act of 2002, and authorized NIST to facilitate and support the development of voluntary, consensus-based, industry-led cybersecurity standards and best practices, and DHS to conduct cybersecurity research and development, and coordinate cybersecurity education and awareness programs.
  - The U.S. Cybersecurity Information Sharing Act of 2015, which amended the Homeland Security Act of 2002, and authorized the voluntary sharing of cyber threat indicators and defensive measures among private sector entities and the federal government, and provided liability protection, privacy safeguards and oversight mechanisms for such sharing.
  - The U.S. Federal Information Security Modernization Act of 2014, which amended the Federal Information Security Management Act of 2002, and established a framework for securing federal information systems and data, and assigned roles and responsibilities to the Office of Management and Budget, DHS, NIST and federal agencies for developing and implementing cybersecurity policies, standards, guidelines and procedures.
