# Botnets

- A botnet is a network of compromised computers controlled by attackers.
- Botnets can be used for various malicious purposes, such as spamming, distributed denial-of-service (DDoS) attacks, stealing sensitive information, or hosting illegal content.
- Botnet forensics is the science that determines the scope of the breach and applies the methodology to find out the type of the infection.
- Botnet forensics involves a collection of activities, such as collection, identification, detection, acquisition, and attribution of botnet evidence.
- Botnet forensics can be challenging due to the scalability, diversity, and complexity of botnet architectures, protocols, and life cycles .
- Botnet forensics can be performed at different levels, such as malware analysis, network analysis, or host analysis.
- Botnet forensics can benefit from the use of deep learning mechanisms, such as convolutional neural networks (CNNs), recurrent neural networks (RNNs), or long short-term memory (LSTM) networks, to detect and classify botnet activities in large-scale and heterogeneous IoT systems.
- Botnet forensics can help to mitigate the impact of botnet attacks, identify the attackers, and prevent future infections.
- Some examples of the biggest botnet attacks to date are Mirai, Conficker, Storm, and Zeus.
