### Concepts of Investigation for the notes of the Unit 3 - Cyber Investigation in the subject of CYBER FORENSIC ANALYTICS

- Cyber forensics is a process of extracting data as proof for a crime (that involves electronic devices) while following proper investigation rules to nab the culprit by presenting the evidence to the court.
- Cyber forensics is also known as computer forensics or digital forensics.
- The main concepts of investigation in cyber forensics are:

  - Identification: Identifying what evidence is present, where it is stored, and how it is stored (in which format). Electronic devices can be personal computers, mobile phones, PDAs, etc .
  - Preservation: Data is isolated, secured, and preserved. The evidence must not be destroyed or corrupted, so the storage media must be physically secured and protected from unauthorized access or modification .
  - Analysis: Forensic lab personnel reconstruct fragments of data and draw conclusions based on evidence. They use various tools and techniques to analyze the data, such as keyword lists, grep, file hashing, report writing, etc .
  - Documentation: A record of all the visible data is created. The documentation must include the details of the evidence, such as the source, location, date, time, format, etc. The documentation must also follow the legal and ethical standards of the jurisdiction .
  - Presentation: The findings and conclusions of the analysis are presented to the court or the client in a clear and concise manner. The presentation must be based on facts and logic, and must not include personal opinions or biases. The presentation must also be supported by the documentation and the evidence.

- The main challenges of investigation in cyber forensics are:

  - The large and complex amount of data that needs to be collected and analyzed.
  - The dynamic and volatile nature of digital evidence, which can be easily altered or deleted.
  - The diversity and compatibility of different devices, operating systems, and formats of data.
  - The legal and ethical issues related to privacy, consent, and jurisdiction of digital evidence.
