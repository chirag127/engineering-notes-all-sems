### Electronic Governance

Electronic governance or e-governance is the application of information and communication technology (ICT) for delivering government services, exchange of information, communication transactions, integration of various stand-alone systems between government and its stakeholders, such as citizens, businesses, employees, and other government organizations  .

Some of the objectives of e-governance are:

- To improve the efficiency, transparency, accountability, and accessibility of government functions and processes.
- To enhance the quality and delivery of public services and information to the citizens and businesses.
- To promote the participation and engagement of the citizens and civil society in the governance and decision-making processes.
- To foster the innovation and competitiveness of the economy and society by leveraging the potential of ICT.

Some of the features of e-governance are:

- It uses various ICT tools and platforms, such as websites, portals, mobile applications, social media, cloud computing, big data, artificial intelligence, etc.
- It involves the re-engineering and transformation of the existing government processes and structures to make them more citizen-centric and user-friendly.
- It requires the collaboration and coordination among various government agencies and departments, as well as the private sector and civil society organizations, to ensure the interoperability and integration of the e-governance systems and services.
- It entails the development of the legal, regulatory, institutional, and human capacity frameworks to support and enable the implementation and sustainability of the e-governance initiatives.

There are four types of e-governance, based on the direction of interaction and communication between the government and its stakeholders  :

- Government-to-Citizen (G2C): This type of e-governance involves the provision of information, services, and feedback to the citizens by the government through various ICT channels, such as websites, mobile applications, kiosks, call centers, etc. Examples of G2C e-governance are online tax filing, voter registration, passport application, etc.
- Government-to-Business (G2B): This type of e-governance involves the facilitation of the transactions and interactions between the government and the businesses through various ICT channels, such as websites, portals, e-procurement, e-tendering, etc. Examples of G2B e-governance are online business registration, licensing, permits, customs clearance, etc.
- Government-to-Government (G2G): This type of e-governance involves the exchange of information, data, and services among different government agencies and departments at the same or different levels of administration through various ICT channels, such as websites, portals, databases, networks, etc. Examples of G2G e-governance are online budgeting, payroll, human resource management, etc.
- Government-to-Employee (G2E): This type of e-governance involves the provision of information, services, and feedback to the government employees by the government through various ICT channels, such as websites, portals, intranet, e-learning, etc. Examples of G2E e-governance are online training, performance appraisal, grievance redressal, etc.
