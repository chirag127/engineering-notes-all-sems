### Real evidence for the notes of the Unit 4 - Evidence Management in the subject of CYBER FORENSIC ANALYTICS

- Real evidence is any physical object that can be directly observed or examined by the court .
- Real evidence can be used to prove or disprove a fact or a connection between a suspect and a crime .
- Real evidence can also be used to demonstrate how a crime was committed or what the impact of the crime was .
- Examples of real evidence in cyber forensics are:
  - The actual computer, device, or storage media that contains digital evidence  .
  - The fingerprints, DNA, or other biometric data of the suspect or the victim found on the computer or device .
  - The physical damage or alteration of the computer or device caused by the crime or the suspect .
  - The tools, weapons, or accessories used by the suspect or the victim in relation to the crime .
- Real evidence must be properly collected, preserved, and analyzed by cyber forensic experts following the principles of identification, acquisition, examination, analysis, and presentation  .
- Real evidence must also be properly documented, labeled, and stored in a secure and tamper-proof manner to maintain its integrity and admissibility in court  .
- Real evidence can be challenged or disputed by the opposing party based on its relevance, reliability, authenticity, or chain of custody   .
- Real evidence can be supplemented or corroborated by other types of evidence, such as testimonial, documentary, or demonstrative evidence   .
