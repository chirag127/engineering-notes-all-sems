### Criminal Behaviour

- Criminal behaviour is any kind of antisocial behaviour that is punishable by law or norms stated by the community .
- Criminal behaviour can be committed by act or omission, and can be defined by statutory or common law.
- Criminal behaviour can vary in severity, frequency, and type, depending on the context, motivation, and consequences of the offence.
- Criminal behaviour can be influenced by various factors, such as biological, psychological, social, and environmental factors.
- Criminal behaviour can have negative impacts on the offender, the victim, and the society, such as physical, emotional, financial, and legal harms.

### Cyber Crime

- Cyber crime is a form of criminal behaviour that involves the use of computers, networks, or digital devices to commit or facilitate an offence.
- Cyber crime can be classified into two categories: cyber-dependent crimes and cyber-enabled crimes.
- Cyber-dependent crimes are offences that can only be committed using information and communication technology (ICT), such as hacking, denial-of-service attacks, or malware distribution.
- Cyber-enabled crimes are offences that can be committed without ICT, but are facilitated or enhanced by it, such as fraud, identity theft, or cyberstalking.
- Cyber crime can pose various challenges for law enforcement, such as jurisdictional issues, anonymity, encryption, and evidence collection.
- Cyber crime can also pose various risks for individuals, organizations, and nations, such as data breaches, financial losses, reputational damages, and cyberterrorism.
