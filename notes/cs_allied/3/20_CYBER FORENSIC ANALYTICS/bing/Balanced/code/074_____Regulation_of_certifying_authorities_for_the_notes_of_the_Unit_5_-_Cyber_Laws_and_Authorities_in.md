### Regulation of certifying authorities

- A certifying authority (CA) is an entity that issues digital certificates to verify the identity and authenticity of the users of electronic transactions.
- The Information Technology Act, 2000 (IT Act) has established a regulatory framework for the CAs in India.
- The IT Act empowers the Controller of Certifying Authorities (CCA) to license and supervise the CAs and to lay down the standards and conditions for their functioning.
- The CCA also maintains the Root Certifying Authority of India (RCAI), which is the highest level of trust in the Indian public key infrastructure (PKI).
- The CAs are required to follow the rules and regulations prescribed by the CCA, such as:
  - Obtaining a license from the CCA before commencing their operations.
  - Following the standards and guidelines issued by the CCA for the generation, issuance, renewal, suspension and revocation of digital certificates.
  - Maintaining a secure and reliable system and infrastructure for the management of digital certificates and keys.
  - Conducting regular audits and inspections by the CCA or its authorized agents.
  - Submitting periodic reports and information to the CCA as required.
  - Ensuring the privacy and confidentiality of the subscribers and their data.
  - Cooperating with the CCA and other authorities in the investigation and prosecution of cyber crimes and offences.
- The CAs are also liable for any damages caused to the subscribers or any third parties due to their negligence, fraud, misrepresentation or breach of contract.
- The IT Act also provides for the recognition of foreign CAs and their digital certificates, subject to the approval of the CCA and the fulfillment of certain conditions.
