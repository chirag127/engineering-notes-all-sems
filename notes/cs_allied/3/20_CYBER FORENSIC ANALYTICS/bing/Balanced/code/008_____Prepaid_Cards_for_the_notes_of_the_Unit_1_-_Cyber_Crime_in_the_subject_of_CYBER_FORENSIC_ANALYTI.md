# Prepaid Cards

Prepaid cards are payment cards that are pre-loaded with a certain amount of money and can be used for various purposes, such as gift cards, travel cards, payroll cards, or disaster relief cards. Prepaid cards are different from regular credit and debit cards issued by banks, as they do not require a bank account, a credit check, or a personal identification number (PIN) to use. Prepaid cards can be anonymous, reloadable, or disposable, depending on the type and issuer of the card.

Prepaid cards are convenient and widely accepted, but they also pose significant risks for cyber crime, fraud, and money laundering. Some of the reasons why prepaid cards are attractive for criminals are:

- Prepaid cards can be purchased with cash, stolen payment information, or other illicit funds, making them hard to trace and link to specific identities or transactions.
- Prepaid cards can be used to withdraw cash from ATMs, make online purchases, or transfer funds to other cards or accounts, without leaving a paper trail or arousing suspicion from financial institutions or authorities.
- Prepaid cards can be used to evade taxes, sanctions, or anti-money laundering regulations, as they are not subject to the same reporting and monitoring requirements as bank accounts or credit cards.
- Prepaid cards can be used to facilitate various types of cyber crimes, such as phishing, ransomware, identity theft, card skimming, or card cloning, by exploiting the vulnerabilities and loopholes in the card issuance, activation, and verification processes.

Some of the examples of cyber crimes involving prepaid cards are:

- In 2013, a global cyber crime ring stole $45 million from two Middle Eastern banks by hacking into their prepaid card systems, generating fake cards, and withdrawing cash from ATMs in 27 countries.
- In 2019, a Nigerian cyber crime group known as Scarlet Widow used prepaid cards to launder money from romance scams, where they pretended to be US soldiers or businessmen and convinced their victims to send them money or gift cards.
- In 2020, a Canadian man was arrested for using prepaid cards to buy cryptocurrency and send it to ISIS, a terrorist organization. He also used prepaid cards to buy online services and products related to ISIS propaganda and recruitment.

Some of the measures that can be taken to prevent or mitigate the risks of prepaid cards for cyber crime are:

- Implementing stricter security and verification standards for prepaid card issuers, distributors, and users, such as requiring identity verification, PIN protection, transaction limits, or fraud alerts.
- Enhancing the cooperation and information sharing among law enforcement, regulators, card networks, and financial institutions, to detect and disrupt suspicious or illicit prepaid card activities and transactions.
- Educating and raising awareness among consumers and businesses about the potential dangers and red flags of prepaid card fraud and cyber crime, and how to protect themselves and report any incidents.
