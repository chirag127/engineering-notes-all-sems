### Cyber weapons

- Cyber weapons are pieces of computer software or hardware that are used to commit cyberwarfare .
- Cyberwarfare is the use of cyber attacks to damage, disrupt, or destroy the computers, networks, or information systems of another nation or organization.
- Cyber attacks are malicious actions that exploit the vulnerabilities of computers, networks, or information systems to compromise their confidentiality, integrity, or availability.
- Cyber weapons can be classified into two types: **kinetic** and **non-kinetic**.
  - Kinetic cyber weapons are those that cause physical damage or destruction to the target, such as sabotaging industrial control systems, power grids, or military equipment.
  - Non-kinetic cyber weapons are those that cause functional or mental harm to the target, such as stealing, manipulating, or deleting data, disrupting communications, or influencing public opinion.
- Cyber weapons can also be categorized based on their origin, purpose, target, or delivery method.
  - Origin: cyber weapons can be developed by state actors, non-state actors, or private entities.
  - Purpose: cyber weapons can be used for offensive, defensive, or espionage purposes.
  - Target: cyber weapons can target military, civilian, or critical infrastructure systems.
  - Delivery method: cyber weapons can be delivered through direct or indirect means, such as email attachments, phishing links, removable media, or network connections.
- Cyber weapons pose several challenges and risks for the international security and stability, such as:
  - Attribution: it is difficult to identify the source and responsibility of a cyber attack, which can lead to misperception, escalation, or retaliation.
  - Deterrence: it is unclear how to deter or respond to a cyber attack, given the asymmetry, ambiguity, and anonymity of the cyber domain.
  - Proliferation: it is easy to copy, modify, or distribute cyber weapons, which can increase the number and diversity of potential attackers and targets.
  - Collateral damage: it is hard to control or limit the effects of a cyber attack, which can spill over to unintended or neutral parties.
  - Ethics: it is challenging to define and apply the principles of legality, proportionality, and discrimination in the use of cyber weapons, especially when they affect human lives or rights.
