## Unit 5 - Cyber Laws and Authorities

- Cyber laws, more commonly known as internet laws, are laws that are related to legal informatics, regulating the digital distribution of information, e-commerce, software, and information security.
- Cyber laws usually cover many related areas, such as usage and access to the Internet, freedom of speech, and privacy.
- Cyber laws are important to prevent and punish cybercrimes, such as hacking, identity theft, phishing, cyberstalking, and cyberterrorism .
- Cyber laws vary from country to country, and sometimes from state to state, depending on the jurisdiction and the nature of the cybercrime.
- Cyber authorities are the entities that are responsible for enforcing, regulating, and overseeing cyber laws and cybersecurity policies  .
- Cyber authorities may include government agencies, such as the Department of Homeland Security, the Federal Bureau of Investigation, the National Security Agency, and the Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency in the U.S. .
- Cyber authorities may also include non-governmental organizations, such as the Internet Corporation for Assigned Names and Numbers, the Electronic Frontier Foundation, and the International Association of Privacy Professionals .
- Cyber authorities may also include certifying authorities, which are the entities that issue digital certificates to authenticate the identity and validity of online transactions and communications.
- Cyber authorities may collaborate with each other, as well as with the private sector, academia, and civil society, to enhance cybersecurity and protect the rights and interests of the online users  .
