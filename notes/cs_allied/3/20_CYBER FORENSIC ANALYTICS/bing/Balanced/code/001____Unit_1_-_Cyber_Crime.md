## Unit 1 - Cyber Crime

Cyber crime is any criminal activity that involves a computer, a network, or a device connected to the internet. Cyber crime can affect individuals, organizations, or governments, and can cause various types of harm, such as financial loss, identity theft, privacy violation, data breach, cyber bullying, cyber stalking, cyber terrorism, or cyber warfare.

Some of the main types of cyber crime are:

- **Hacking**: Unauthorized access to a computer system or network, often with malicious intent.
- **Phishing**: Sending fraudulent emails or messages that appear to be from legitimate sources, in order to trick recipients into revealing personal or financial information, or clicking on malicious links or attachments.
- **Malware**: Malicious software that infects a computer or device, and can perform various harmful actions, such as stealing data, encrypting files, displaying unwanted ads, spying on users, or damaging the system.
- **Ransomware**: A type of malware that encrypts the victim's files or locks the system, and demands a ransom for their restoration.
- **Denial-of-service (DoS) attack**: An attempt to overwhelm a computer system or network with excessive traffic or requests, in order to disrupt its normal functioning or availability.
- **Distributed denial-of-service (DDoS) attack**: A type of DoS attack that involves multiple compromised devices, often called a botnet, that are controlled by a hacker and used to launch a coordinated attack on a target system or network.
- **Identity theft**: The fraudulent use of someone else's personal information, such as name, date of birth, social security number, credit card number, or online accounts, for illegal purposes, such as opening new accounts, making purchases, or obtaining benefits.
- **Cyber fraud**: The use of deception or manipulation to obtain money or property from someone online, such as by impersonating a legitimate entity, offering fake products or services, or creating fake websites or platforms.
- **Cyber espionage**: The unauthorized access to or theft of confidential or sensitive information from a computer system or network, for political, military, or economic advantage.
- **Cyber sabotage**: The deliberate damage or destruction of a computer system or network, or the data or programs stored on it, for malicious or ideological reasons.
- **Cyber terrorism**: The use of cyber attacks to intimidate, coerce, or harm a government, an organization, or a population, in order to achieve political, religious, or social objectives.
- **Cyber warfare**: The use of cyber attacks by a state or a non-state actor against another state or a non-state actor, in order to gain a strategic or tactical advantage, or to cause damage or disruption.

Some of the main challenges in preventing and combating cyber crime are:

- **Lack of awareness**: Many users are unaware of the risks and threats of cyber crime, or the best practices and measures to protect themselves and their devices online.
- **Lack of regulation**: Many countries do not have adequate laws or policies to define, prevent, or prosecute cyber crime, or to cooperate with other countries in cross-border cases.
- **Lack of resources**: Many law enforcement agencies and organizations do not have sufficient technical skills, tools, or personnel to investigate and respond to cyber crime, or to collect and preserve digital evidence.
- **Lack of coordination**: Many stakeholders, such as governments, private sector, civil society, and international organizations, do not have effective mechanisms or platforms to share information, intelligence, or best practices, or to collaborate in preventing and combating cyber crime.
- **Lack of deterrence**: Many cyber criminals operate with impunity, as they are difficult to identify, locate, or apprehend, or they operate from jurisdictions that do not cooperate or extradite them.
