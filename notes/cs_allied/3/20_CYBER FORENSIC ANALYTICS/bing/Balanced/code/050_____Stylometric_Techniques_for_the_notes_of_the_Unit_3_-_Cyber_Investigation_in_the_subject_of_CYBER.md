### Stylometric Techniques for the notes of the Unit 3 - Cyber Investigation in the subject of CYBER FORENSIC ANALYTICS

- Stylometry is the statistical analysis of the linguistic features of a text, such as word choice, sentence structure, punctuation, spelling, etc.
- Stylometry can be used to identify the unique writing style of an author, which can be useful for authorship attribution, plagiarism detection, genre classification, and other applications.
- Stylometry can also be applied to computer code and intrinsic plagiarism detection, which is to detect plagiarism based on the writing style changes within the document .
- Stylometric techniques can be divided into two categories: lexical and non-lexical.
- Lexical techniques focus on the frequency and distribution of words, phrases, and n-grams in a text. Examples of lexical features are word length, vocabulary richness, function words, stopwords, etc.
- Non-lexical techniques focus on the syntactic, semantic, and pragmatic aspects of a text, such as sentence length, punctuation, part-of-speech tags, readability, sentiment, etc.
- Stylometric techniques can be further classified into supervised and unsupervised methods.
- Supervised methods require a labeled dataset of texts from known authors, which can be used to train a classifier or a similarity measure to assign a new text to the most likely author.
- Unsupervised methods do not require a labeled dataset, but instead rely on clustering or dimensionality reduction techniques to group texts based on their stylistic similarity or difference.
- Stylometric techniques can be applied to various types of texts, such as e-mails, social media posts, blogs, essays, novels, etc.
- Stylometric techniques can be useful for cyber forensics, as they can help to identify the author of a malicious or anonymous text, or to detect plagiarism or deception in online communication.
- Stylometric techniques can also be challenged by various factors, such as noise, obfuscation, imitation, translation, genre variation, etc., which can affect the accuracy and reliability of the results.
- Stylometric techniques require careful selection and evaluation of the features, methods, and tools, as well as a clear understanding of the research question and the context of the text.
