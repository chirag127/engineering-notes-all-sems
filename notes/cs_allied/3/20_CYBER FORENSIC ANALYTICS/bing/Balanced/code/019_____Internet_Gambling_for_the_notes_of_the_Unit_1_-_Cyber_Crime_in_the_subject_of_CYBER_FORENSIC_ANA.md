### Internet Gambling

- Internet gambling is the activity of placing bets on sports, games, or contests using a "wire communication facility", which includes the Internet.
- Internet gambling is illegal in many countries, including the United States, where federal law prohibits it under the Wire Act of 1961 .
- Internet gambling is attractive to many people because it offers convenience, anonymity, and variety of options.
- Internet gambling is also vulnerable to cybercrime, which is the use of illegal methods directed by means of electronic operations that target the security of computer systems and the data processed by them.
- Some common cybercrime threats to internet gambling are:
  - Compromising online gambling accounts through brute forcing, botnets, or credential-stealing malware, and cashing out balances or transferring funds to other accounts.
  - Using online tools, like TOR networks and Virtual Private Networks (VPNs), to hide the identity and location of the gambling operators or the gamblers, and to evade law enforcement detection.
  - Installing viruses, Trojan horses, computer worms, or spyware on the devices of the gamblers, to steal personal or financial information, or to manipulate the outcome of the games.
- Internet gambling poses challenges to law enforcement, as it requires cooperation and coordination among different jurisdictions, agencies, and sectors.
- Internet gambling also poses risks to the gamblers, such as addiction, fraud, identity theft, and the potential rapid loss of any funds invested in the game.
