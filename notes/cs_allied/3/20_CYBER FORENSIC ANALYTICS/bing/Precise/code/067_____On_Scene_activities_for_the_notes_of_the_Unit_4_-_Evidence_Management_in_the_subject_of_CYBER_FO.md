### On Scene activities for the notes of the Unit 4 - Evidence Management in the subject of CYBER FORENSIC ANALYTICS

1. **Securing the Scene**: The first step in on-scene activities is to secure the scene to prevent contamination or loss of evidence. This involves establishing a perimeter, controlling access to the scene, and documenting all individuals who enter or leave the scene.

2. **Initial Survey**: The next step is to conduct an initial survey of the scene to identify potential evidence and to develop a plan for processing the scene. This involves visually inspecting the scene, taking photographs, and making notes of any observations.

3. **Documentation**: Proper documentation is critical in the collection and preservation of evidence. This includes taking photographs, making sketches, and taking detailed notes of the scene and the evidence collected.

4. **Evidence Collection**: The collection of evidence must be done in a systematic and careful manner to ensure the integrity of the evidence. This involves identifying, collecting, and preserving all relevant evidence, including digital evidence.

5. **Chain of Custody**: Maintaining the chain of custody is essential to ensure the integrity of the evidence. This involves documenting the handling and transfer of evidence from the time it is collected until it is presented in court.

6. **Final Survey**: A final survey of the scene should be conducted to ensure that all evidence has been collected and that the scene has been thoroughly processed.

7. **Release of Scene**: Once all evidence has been collected and the scene has been thoroughly processed, the scene can be released. This involves removing any barriers or tape, and returning the scene to its original state.

These are some of the key on-scene activities that are involved in the evidence management process in the field of cyber forensic analytics. It is important to follow these steps carefully to ensure the integrity and admissibility of the evidence collected.
