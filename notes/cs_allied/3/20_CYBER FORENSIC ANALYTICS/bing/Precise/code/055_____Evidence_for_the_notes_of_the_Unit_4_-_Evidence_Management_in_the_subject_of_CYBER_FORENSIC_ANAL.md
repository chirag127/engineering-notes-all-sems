### Evidence Management in Cyber Forensic Analytics

1. **Introduction:** Evidence management is a crucial aspect of cyber forensic analytics. It involves the identification, collection, preservation, analysis, and presentation of digital evidence in a manner that is admissible in a court of law.

2. **Identification of Evidence:** The first step in evidence management is the identification of potential sources of digital evidence. This may include computer systems, storage devices, network logs, and other digital devices.

3. **Collection of Evidence:** Once potential sources of evidence have been identified, the next step is to collect the evidence in a forensically sound manner. This involves following established procedures and using specialized tools to ensure that the evidence is not altered or damaged during the collection process.

4. **Preservation of Evidence:** After the evidence has been collected, it must be preserved in a manner that maintains its integrity and authenticity. This may involve creating a forensic image of the evidence, storing it in a secure location, and maintaining a chain of custody.

5. **Analysis of Evidence:** The analysis of digital evidence involves using specialized tools and techniques to extract and interpret the data. This may include recovering deleted files, examining file metadata, and analyzing network logs.

6. **Presentation of Evidence:** The final step in evidence management is the presentation of the evidence in a court of law. This involves preparing a report that summarizes the findings of the analysis and presenting the evidence in a manner that is clear and understandable to the court.

In summary, evidence management is a critical component of cyber forensic analytics that involves the identification, collection, preservation, analysis, and presentation of digital evidence in a manner that is admissible in a court of law. It is essential that these steps are followed in a forensically sound manner to ensure the integrity and authenticity of the evidence.
