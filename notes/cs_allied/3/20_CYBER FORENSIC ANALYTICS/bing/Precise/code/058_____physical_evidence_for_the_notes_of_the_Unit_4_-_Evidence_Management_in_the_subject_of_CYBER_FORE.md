### Physical Evidence

Physical evidence refers to any material object that can establish that a crime has been committed or can link a crime and its victim or perpetrator. In the context of cyber forensic analytics, physical evidence can include:

1. **Digital devices**: This includes computers, laptops, smartphones, tablets, and other devices that may contain digital evidence relevant to the investigation.

2. **Storage media**: This includes hard drives, USB drives, memory cards, and other forms of storage media that may contain digital evidence.

3. **Printed documents**: This includes any printed documents that may contain evidence relevant to the investigation, such as emails, chat logs, or other records.

4. **Network logs**: This includes logs from network devices such as routers, switches, and firewalls that may contain evidence of network activity relevant to the investigation.

5. **Surveillance footage**: This includes footage from security cameras or other surveillance systems that may have captured evidence relevant to the investigation.

It is important to properly collect, preserve, and analyze physical evidence in order to ensure its admissibility in court. This involves following proper evidence management procedures, including maintaining a chain of custody and ensuring that the evidence is not tampered with or contaminated.
