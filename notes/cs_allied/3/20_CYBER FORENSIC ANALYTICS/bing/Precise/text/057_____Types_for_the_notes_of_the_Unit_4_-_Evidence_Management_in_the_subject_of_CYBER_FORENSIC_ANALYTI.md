### Types for the notes of the Unit 4 - Evidence Management in the subject of CYBER FORENSIC ANALYTICS

1. **Digital Evidence**: Digital evidence is any information that is stored or transmitted in a digital form and is relevant to a legal case. This can include data from computers, mobile devices, and other electronic devices.

2. **Physical Evidence**: Physical evidence refers to any tangible objects that can be collected and analyzed in a forensic investigation. This can include items such as documents, weapons, and clothing.

3. **Testimonial Evidence**: Testimonial evidence is any information that is provided by a witness in a legal case. This can include statements made by witnesses, as well as any documents or recordings that contain witness testimony.

4. **Documentary Evidence**: Documentary evidence refers to any written or printed materials that are relevant to a legal case. This can include items such as contracts, emails, and financial records.

5. **Demonstrative Evidence**: Demonstrative evidence is any visual or audio material that is used to help explain or illustrate a point in a legal case. This can include items such as photographs, videos, and diagrams.

6. **Exculpatory Evidence**: Exculpatory evidence is any information that can help to exonerate a defendant in a legal case. This can include evidence that contradicts the prosecution's case or supports the defendant's alibi.

7. **Inculpatory Evidence**: Inculpatory evidence is any information that can help to incriminate a defendant in a legal case. This can include evidence that supports the prosecution's case or contradicts the defendant's alibi.

8. **Hearsay Evidence**: Hearsay evidence is any information that is reported by a witness but is not based on the witness's personal knowledge. Hearsay evidence is generally not admissible in court, but there are some exceptions to this rule.

9. **Circumstantial Evidence**: Circumstantial evidence is any information that is not based on direct observation but can be used to infer a conclusion. Circumstantial evidence is often used in legal cases when direct evidence is not available.

10. **Corroborating Evidence**: Corroborating evidence is any information that supports or confirms other evidence in a legal case. Corroborating evidence can help to strengthen a case by providing additional support for the evidence that is already available.
