### Trojans and Backdoors

- **Trojans** are malicious programs that are disguised as legitimate software. They are often used to gain unauthorized access to a computer system or to steal sensitive information.
- Trojans can be spread through various means, such as email attachments, downloads from the internet, or by exploiting vulnerabilities in software.
- Once installed on a system, Trojans can perform a variety of actions, such as logging keystrokes, capturing screenshots, or stealing passwords.
- **Backdoors** are similar to Trojans in that they provide unauthorized access to a computer system. However, backdoors are typically installed intentionally by the system's owner or administrator for maintenance or support purposes.
- Backdoors can be exploited by attackers if they are not properly secured. For example, an attacker may use a default or easily guessable password to gain access to a system through a backdoor.
- Both Trojans and backdoors can be difficult to detect and remove. It is important to keep software up to date and to use reputable antivirus software to protect against these types of threats.
- In the context of cyber forensics, it is important to be able to identify and analyze Trojans and backdoors in order to determine the extent of a security breach and to gather evidence for legal proceedings.
