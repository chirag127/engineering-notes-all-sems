## Unit 4 - Evidence Management

Evidence management is the process of collecting, storing, preserving, and analyzing evidence in a manner that maintains its integrity and admissibility in a court of law. The following are some key points to consider when managing evidence:

1. **Chain of custody:** It is important to establish and maintain a chain of custody for all evidence. This includes documenting the collection, handling, and transfer of evidence to ensure its integrity.

2. **Proper storage:** Evidence must be stored in a secure location to prevent tampering, loss, or damage. Different types of evidence may require different storage conditions, such as temperature or humidity control.

3. **Preservation:** Evidence must be preserved in a manner that maintains its integrity and prevents degradation. This may include measures such as refrigeration, freezing, or the use of preservatives.

4. **Analysis:** The analysis of evidence must be conducted in a manner that is consistent with established scientific principles and methods. This may include the use of specialized equipment or techniques.

5. **Documentation:** All aspects of evidence management, including collection, storage, preservation, and analysis, must be thoroughly documented. This documentation should include details such as the date, time, location, and personnel involved in each step of the process.

6. **Admissibility:** The ultimate goal of evidence management is to ensure that the evidence is admissible in a court of law. This requires that all aspects of the evidence management process are conducted in accordance with established legal standards and procedures.

In summary, evidence management is a critical component of the criminal justice system. Proper evidence management ensures that evidence is collected, stored, preserved, and analyzed in a manner that maintains its integrity and admissibility in a court of law.
