## Unit 4 - Evidence Management

Evidence management is the process of collecting, preserving, analyzing, and presenting evidence in a manner that is admissible in a court of law. It is a crucial aspect of criminal investigations and legal proceedings.

1. **Collection of Evidence**: The first step in evidence management is the collection of evidence. This involves identifying, securing, and gathering physical, digital, and testimonial evidence from the crime scene or other relevant locations.

2. **Preservation of Evidence**: Once evidence has been collected, it must be preserved to prevent contamination, tampering, or degradation. This involves proper storage, handling, and documentation of the evidence.

3. **Analysis of Evidence**: The next step in evidence management is the analysis of the evidence. This involves examining the evidence to extract relevant information and draw conclusions. This may include forensic analysis, such as DNA testing or fingerprint analysis.

4. **Presentation of Evidence**: The final step in evidence management is the presentation of the evidence in a court of law. This involves organizing and presenting the evidence in a clear and concise manner to support the case.

Proper evidence management is essential to ensure that evidence is admissible in court and can be used to support the case. It requires careful planning, attention to detail, and adherence to established procedures and protocols.
