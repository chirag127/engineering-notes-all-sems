## Unit 2 - Cyber Forensics

1. **Introduction to Cyber Forensics:** Cyber Forensics, also known as Computer Forensics, is the practice of collecting, analyzing, and reporting on digital data in a way that is legally admissible. It is used in the detection and prevention of crime and in any dispute where evidence is stored digitally.

2. **Objectives of Cyber Forensics:** The main objectives of cyber forensics are to reconstruct events, preserve evidence, and attribute actions to individuals. This is achieved through the identification, preservation, analysis, and presentation of digital evidence.

3. **Process of Cyber Forensics:** The process of cyber forensics involves four main stages: acquisition, examination, analysis, and reporting. Acquisition involves the collection of digital evidence, while examination involves the identification and extraction of relevant data. Analysis involves the interpretation of the data, and reporting involves the presentation of the findings.

4. **Tools and Techniques:** There are a variety of tools and techniques used in cyber forensics, including disk imaging, file carving, and memory analysis. These tools are used to recover and analyze data from digital devices, such as computers, smartphones, and storage media.

5. **Challenges in Cyber Forensics:** Some of the challenges in cyber forensics include the rapidly evolving nature of technology, the increasing use of encryption, and the need for specialized knowledge and training. Additionally, the legal and ethical issues surrounding the collection and use of digital evidence can also present challenges.

6. **Conclusion:** Cyber forensics is an important field that plays a crucial role in the detection and prevention of crime, as well as in the resolution of disputes involving digital evidence. Despite the challenges, the use of advanced tools and techniques, combined with specialized knowledge and training, can help to overcome these challenges and ensure the effective use of digital evidence in legal proceedings.
