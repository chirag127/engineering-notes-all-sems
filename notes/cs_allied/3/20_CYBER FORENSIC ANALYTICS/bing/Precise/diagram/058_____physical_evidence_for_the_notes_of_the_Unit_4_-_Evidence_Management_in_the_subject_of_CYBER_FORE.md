### Physical Evidence

Physical evidence refers to any material object that can establish that a crime has been committed or can link a crime and its victim or perpetrator. In the context of cyber forensic analytics, physical evidence can include:

1. **Digital devices**: Devices such as computers, smartphones, and external storage media can contain valuable evidence in the form of digital data. This can include emails, text messages, call logs, and other forms of electronic communication.

2. **Printed documents**: Printed documents such as invoices, receipts, and contracts can provide evidence of financial transactions and other activities.

3. **Physical traces**: Physical traces such as fingerprints, DNA, and tool marks can link a suspect to a crime scene or a piece of evidence.

It is important to properly collect, preserve, and analyze physical evidence in order to ensure its integrity and admissibility in court. This process is known as evidence management and is a critical component of cyber forensic analytics.
