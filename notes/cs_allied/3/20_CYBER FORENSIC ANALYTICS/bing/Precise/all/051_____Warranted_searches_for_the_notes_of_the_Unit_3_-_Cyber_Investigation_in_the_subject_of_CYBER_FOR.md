### Warranted Searches

Warranted searches are searches that are conducted with a warrant, which is a legal document issued by a judge or magistrate that authorizes law enforcement officers to conduct a search of a person, location, or vehicle for evidence of a crime.

1. A warrant is required for most searches under the Fourth Amendment of the United States Constitution, which protects against unreasonable searches and seizures.
2. To obtain a warrant, law enforcement officers must demonstrate probable cause that evidence of a crime will be found in the place to be searched.
3. The warrant must specify the place to be searched and the items to be seized.
4. Warranted searches are an important tool for law enforcement in the investigation of crimes, as they allow officers to gather evidence that can be used in court to prosecute offenders.
5. However, there are also several exceptions to the warrant requirement, such as searches conducted with the consent of the person being searched, searches incident to a lawful arrest, and searches of vehicles under certain circumstances.
