### Evidence Management in Cyber Forensic Analytics

1. **Introduction:** Evidence management is a crucial aspect of cyber forensic analytics. It involves the identification, collection, preservation, analysis, and presentation of digital evidence in a manner that is admissible in a court of law.

2. **Identification of Evidence:** The first step in evidence management is the identification of potential sources of digital evidence. This may include computers, mobile devices, storage media, and network logs.

3. **Collection of Evidence:** Once potential sources of evidence have been identified, the next step is to collect the evidence in a forensically sound manner. This involves following established procedures and using specialized tools to ensure that the evidence is not altered or damaged during the collection process.

4. **Preservation of Evidence:** After the evidence has been collected, it must be preserved in a manner that maintains its integrity and authenticity. This may involve creating a forensic image of the evidence, storing it in a secure location, and maintaining a chain of custody.

5. **Analysis of Evidence:** The analysis of digital evidence involves using specialized tools and techniques to extract and interpret the data. This may include recovering deleted files, cracking passwords, and analyzing network traffic.

6. **Presentation of Evidence:** The final step in evidence management is the presentation of the evidence in a manner that is understandable and admissible in a court of law. This may involve creating reports, visualizations, and other forms of documentation to support the findings of the analysis.

In conclusion, evidence management is a critical component of cyber forensic analytics, and it is essential to follow established procedures and best practices to ensure that the evidence is admissible in a court of law.
