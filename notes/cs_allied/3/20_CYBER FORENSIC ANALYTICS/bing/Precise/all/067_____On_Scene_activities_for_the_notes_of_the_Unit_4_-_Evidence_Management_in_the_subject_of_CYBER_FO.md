### On Scene activities for the notes of the Unit 4 - Evidence Management in the subject of CYBER FORENSIC ANALYTICS

1. **Securing the scene**: The first step in on-scene activities is to secure the scene to prevent contamination or loss of evidence. This involves establishing a perimeter, controlling access to the scene, and documenting the scene.

2. **Identifying potential evidence**: The next step is to identify potential evidence. This involves conducting a thorough search of the scene and identifying items that may be relevant to the investigation.

3. **Documenting the evidence**: Once potential evidence has been identified, it must be documented. This involves taking photographs, making sketches, and taking detailed notes about the location and condition of the evidence.

4. **Collecting the evidence**: After the evidence has been documented, it must be collected. This involves carefully handling the evidence to prevent contamination or damage, and packaging it in a manner that preserves its integrity.

5. **Preserving the evidence**: Once the evidence has been collected, it must be preserved. This involves storing the evidence in a secure location, and maintaining a chain of custody to ensure that the evidence is not tampered with.

6. **Transporting the evidence**: The final step in on-scene activities is to transport the evidence to the laboratory for analysis. This involves ensuring that the evidence is properly packaged and labeled, and that it is transported in a secure manner.

These are the key on-scene activities that are involved in evidence management in the field of cyber forensic analytics. It is important to follow these steps carefully to ensure that the evidence is properly collected, documented, and preserved, so that it can be used in a court of law.
