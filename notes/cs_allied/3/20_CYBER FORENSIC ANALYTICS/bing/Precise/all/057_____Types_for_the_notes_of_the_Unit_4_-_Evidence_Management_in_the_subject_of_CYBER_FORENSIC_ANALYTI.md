# Types for the notes of the Unit 4 - Evidence Management in the subject of CYBER FORENSIC ANALYTICS

1. **Digital Evidence**: Digital evidence refers to any information that is stored or transmitted in digital form and is relevant to a legal case. This can include data from computers, mobile devices, and other electronic devices.

2. **Physical Evidence**: Physical evidence refers to any tangible object that can be collected and analyzed to provide information about a crime or incident. This can include items such as weapons, clothing, or other physical objects.

3. **Documentary Evidence**: Documentary evidence refers to any written or printed material that can provide information about a case. This can include documents such as contracts, emails, or other written records.

4. **Testimonial Evidence**: Testimonial evidence refers to any information that is provided by a witness or other individual who has knowledge of the case. This can include statements made by witnesses, victims, or other individuals involved in the case.

5. **Demonstrative Evidence**: Demonstrative evidence refers to any visual or audio material that can help to illustrate or explain the facts of a case. This can include photographs, videos, diagrams, or other visual aids.

6. **Forensic Evidence**: Forensic evidence refers to any information that is obtained through the application of scientific methods and techniques. This can include DNA analysis, fingerprint analysis, or other forms of forensic analysis.

These are some of the types of evidence that may be encountered in the field of cyber forensic analytics. Each type of evidence has its own unique characteristics and may require different methods of collection, preservation, and analysis. It is important for investigators to have a thorough understanding of the different types of evidence and how to properly handle and analyze them in order to effectively manage evidence in a cyber forensic investigation.
