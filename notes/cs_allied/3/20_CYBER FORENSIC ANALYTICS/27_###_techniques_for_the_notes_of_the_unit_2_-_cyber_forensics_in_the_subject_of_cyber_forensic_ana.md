Cyber forensics is the process of collecting, analyzing, and preserving digital evidence in order to investigate and prevent cybercrime. In the context of Unit 2 - Cyber Forensics in the subject of Cyber Forensic Analytics, there are several key techniques that are used in cyber forensics.

1. Data Collection: The first step in any cyber forensics investigation is to collect digital evidence. This can include data from computers, servers, mobile devices, and other digital devices. The data collection process must be done in a way that preserves the integrity of the evidence, so that it can be used in a court of law.

2. Data Analysis: Once the digital evidence has been collected, it must be analyzed to determine what it contains and what it can reveal about the cybercrime. This can involve using a variety of tools and techniques, such as data carving, hash analysis, and metadata analysis, to extract information from the digital evidence.

3. Evidence Preservation: The digital evidence must be preserved in a way that ensures its integrity and authenticity. This can involve making a forensically sound copy of the evidence and storing it in a secure location.

4. Report Writing: The final step in a cyber forensics investigation is to write a report that summarizes the findings of the investigation. This report must be clear and concise, and must provide enough detail to support the conclusions of the investigation.

5. Chain of Custody: The chain of custody is a record of who has had access to the digital evidence and what has been done with it. This is important to ensure that the evidence is not tampered with and that its authenticity can be established in a court of law.

In addition to these core techniques, cyber forensics also involves a thorough understanding of the laws and regulations that govern cybercrime, as well as the technical knowledge and skills needed to analyze and preserve digital evidence.

In conclusion, cyber forensics is a complex and multi-disciplinary field that involves the use of several key techniques, including data collection, data analysis, evidence preservation, report writing, and chain of custody. These techniques are used to collect, analyze, and preserve digital evidence in order to investigate and prevent cybercrime.
