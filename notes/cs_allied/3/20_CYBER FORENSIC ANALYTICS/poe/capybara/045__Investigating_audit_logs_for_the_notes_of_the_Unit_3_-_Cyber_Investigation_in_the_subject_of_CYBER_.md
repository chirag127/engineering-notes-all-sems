### Investigating Audit Logs for the Notes of Unit 3 - Cyber Investigation in the Subject of Cyber Forensic Analytics

When investigating cyber crimes, one of the most important sources of information is the audit logs. Audit logs contain a record of all the activities that have taken place on a system, including login attempts, file access, and network connections. In this unit, we will learn how to investigate audit logs to gather evidence for a cyber forensic investigation.

Here are some key points to keep in mind when investigating audit logs:

1. Identify the relevant logs: Before you can start investigating audit logs, you need to identify which logs are relevant to the investigation. This may depend on the type of system that was compromised and the activity that took place. Some common logs that may be useful include system logs, application logs, and security logs.

2. Review the logs: Once you have identified the relevant logs, you need to review them to find evidence of suspicious activity. This can be a time-consuming process, as logs can contain a lot of information. Look for patterns or anomalies in the logs, such as repeated login attempts from an unfamiliar IP address, or access to files outside of normal business hours.

3. Correlate information: When investigating audit logs, it is important to correlate information from different logs to build a complete picture of what happened. For example, if you find evidence of a suspicious login attempt in the system log, you may want to check the application log to see if any files were accessed during that session.

4. Preserve the logs: Once you have found evidence in the audit logs, it is important to preserve them for future use. This may involve creating a forensic image of the system, or copying the logs to a secure location. This ensures that the evidence is not tampered with or lost.

5. Interpret the logs: Finally, it is important to interpret the information found in the audit logs. This may involve analyzing timestamps, IP addresses, and other metadata to build a timeline of events. It may also involve consulting with other experts, such as network analysts or forensic accountants, to build a complete picture of what happened.

In conclusion, investigating audit logs is an important skill for any cyber forensic investigator. By following these key points, you can effectively gather evidence from audit logs to build a case against cyber criminals.
