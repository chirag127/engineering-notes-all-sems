### Contextual Information for the notes of the Unit 4 - Evidence Management in the subject of CYBER FORENSIC ANALYTICS

Here are some important points to consider when studying Evidence Management in Cyber Forensic Analytics:

- Evidence management is the process of collecting, preserving, analyzing, and presenting electronic evidence in a way that is admissible in court.
- The main goal of evidence management is to maintain the integrity and authenticity of the evidence, ensuring that it remains unchanged and reliable throughout the entire process.
- It is crucial to follow proper chain of custody procedures when handling electronic evidence. This involves documenting the movement and handling of the evidence from the time it is collected until it is presented in court.
- Different types of electronic evidence may require different collection and preservation techniques. For example, volatile data such as RAM memory may need to be collected and analyzed on-site, while non-volatile data such as hard drives may be imaged and analyzed in a lab environment.
- It is important to use proper tools and techniques for collecting and analyzing electronic evidence. This may include specialized software, hardware, and forensic techniques such as imaging, hashing, and decryption.
- Proper documentation is essential in evidence management. This includes detailed notes about the evidence, the collection process, and any analysis or processing that has been done. It is also important to document any changes or modifications made to the evidence during the investigation.
- Finally, it is important to adhere to legal and ethical standards when handling electronic evidence. This includes obtaining proper consent and following applicable laws and regulations.

By understanding these key points and following proper procedures, cyber forensic analysts can effectively manage electronic evidence and present it in a way that is admissible in court.
