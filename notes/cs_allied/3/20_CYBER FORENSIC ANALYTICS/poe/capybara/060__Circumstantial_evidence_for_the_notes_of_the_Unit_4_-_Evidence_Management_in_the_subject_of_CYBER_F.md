### Circumstantial Evidence for the Notes of Unit 4 - Evidence Management in the Subject of CYBER FORENSIC ANALYTICS

Circumstantial evidence is a type of evidence that is not direct, but rather is inferred from a set of circumstances. It is often used in cyber forensic investigations to build a case against a suspect. Here are some important points to understand about circumstantial evidence:

- Circumstantial evidence is indirect evidence that requires inference to establish a fact. It is based on a set of circumstances that, when taken together, lead to a logical conclusion. 

- Circumstantial evidence can be used to prove a suspect's motive, opportunity, or intent. For example, if a suspect is found to have accessed a victim's computer shortly before the victim's data was deleted or stolen, it may be inferred that the suspect had the motive and opportunity to commit the crime.

- Circumstantial evidence can also be used to establish a suspect's state of mind. For example, if a suspect searches for information about how to commit a cybercrime, it may be inferred that the suspect had the intent to commit the crime.

- Circumstantial evidence can be strengthened by corroboration from other sources, such as witness testimony or physical evidence. For example, if a witness saw a suspect near the scene of a cybercrime, and the suspect's computer was found to contain evidence of the crime, the circumstantial evidence may be strengthened.

- Circumstantial evidence is not always reliable, and it can be misinterpreted or manipulated. It is important to consider all the facts and circumstances of a case before drawing any conclusions from circumstantial evidence.

- In cyber forensic investigations, circumstantial evidence may be the only evidence available. This is because digital evidence is often difficult to directly link to a suspect. However, circumstantial evidence can still be used to build a strong case against a suspect.

Understanding circumstantial evidence is an important part of evidence management in cyber forensic investigations. By carefully considering all the available evidence, investigators can build a strong case that will stand up in court.
