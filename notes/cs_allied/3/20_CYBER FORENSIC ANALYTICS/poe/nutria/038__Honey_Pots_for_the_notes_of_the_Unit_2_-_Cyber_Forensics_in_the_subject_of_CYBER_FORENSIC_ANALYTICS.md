
### Honey Pots 

Honey pots are a type of security technology used to detect and respond to malicious activity on a network. They are designed to look like a legitimate part of the network, but actually contain no real data or services. Any attempts to access the honey pot will be logged and monitored, allowing the security team to identify and respond to malicious activity quickly.

- Honey pots can be used to detect malicious activity such as port scanning, brute force attacks, malware, and other malicious activity.
- They can be used to distract attackers, allowing the security team to investigate the attack and take appropriate action.
- Honey pots can be used to collect information about the attacker, such as IP addresses, operating systems, and other data.
- Honey pots can be used to study the tactics, techniques, and procedures (TTPs) of attackers.
- Honey pots can be used to detect zero-day attacks, as they are not vulnerable to known exploits.
