
## Unit 2 - Cyber Forensics

1. Cyber forensics is the process of using digital evidence to investigate cyber-related crimes. It involves the collection, analysis and reporting of digital evidence to determine the cause, effect and extent of a cyber-related incident.

2. Cyber forensics involves a variety of techniques and tools, including data recovery, digital forensics, malware analysis, incident response, and network forensics.

3. Cyber forensics is used to investigate a wide range of cyber-related crimes, such as cyberbullying, cyberstalking, fraud, identity theft, and cyberterrorism.

4. Cyber forensics is also used to investigate corporate espionage, intellectual property theft, and other unauthorized access to confidential information.

5. The process of cyber forensics requires the use of specialized software and hardware tools to collect, analyze, and report digital evidence.

6. Cyber forensics experts must have an in-depth understanding of computer systems, networks, and software to be able to effectively investigate cyber-related crimes.

7. Cyber forensics experts must also be familiar with the applicable laws and regulations related to digital evidence and cyber-related crimes.

8. In addition to the technical aspects of cyber forensics, experts must also be familiar with the legal and ethical implications of their work.
