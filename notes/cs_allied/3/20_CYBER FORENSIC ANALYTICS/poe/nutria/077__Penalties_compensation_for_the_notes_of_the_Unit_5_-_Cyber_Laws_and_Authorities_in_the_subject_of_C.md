* Different countries have different laws and regulations in place to protect citizens from cyber crimes. 
* In the United States, the Computer Fraud and Abuse Act (CFAA) is the primary law that provides for criminal penalties and civil remedies for cyber crimes.
* The CFAA criminalizes the intentional access of a computer without authorization or exceeding authorized access. It also criminalizes the intentional access of a computer to obtain information, cause damage, or to commit fraud.
* The CFAA also provides for civil remedies, such as compensatory damages, injunctive relief, and the recovery of attorneys' fees.
* In the United Kingdom, the Computer Misuse Act (CMA) is the primary law that provides for criminal penalties and civil remedies for cyber crimes.
* The CMA criminalizes the unauthorized access of a computer, the unauthorized modification of a computer, and the unauthorized use of a computer.
* The CMA also provides for civil remedies, such as compensatory damages, injunctive relief, and the recovery of attorneys' fees.
* In Australia, the Cybercrime Act 2001 is the primary law that provides for criminal penalties and civil remedies for cyber crimes.
* The Cybercrime Act criminalizes the unauthorized access of a computer, the unauthorized modification of a computer, and the unauthorized use of a computer.
* The Cybercrime Act also provides for civil remedies, such as compensatory damages, injunctive relief, and the recovery of attorneys' fees.
