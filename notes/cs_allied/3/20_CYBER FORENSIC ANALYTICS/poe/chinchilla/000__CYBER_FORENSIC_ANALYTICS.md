# CYBER FORENSIC ANALYTICS

Cyber forensic analytics is a branch of digital forensics that deals with the investigation of digital devices, networks, and systems to collect, analyze and preserve evidence that can be presented in a court of law. Here are some key points to understand about cyber forensic analytics:

## What is Cyber Forensic Analytics?

- Cyber forensic analytics involves the application of techniques and tools to investigate cyber-related incidents such as cyber-attacks, data breaches, intellectual property theft, and other cybercrimes.
- It is an interdisciplinary field that combines computer science, law, and criminology to identify, analyze, and preserve digital evidence.

## Why is Cyber Forensic Analytics Important?

- Cyber forensic analytics is important because it helps to identify and prosecute cyber criminals, protect intellectual property, and prevent future cyber incidents.
- It also helps to identify vulnerabilities in digital systems and networks, and to develop and implement measures to prevent cyber-attacks.

## Techniques Used in Cyber Forensic Analytics:

- **Data Acquisition:** The process of collecting data from digital devices and networks for analysis. This includes the use of forensic imaging tools and techniques to create a bit-by-bit copy of the digital evidence.

- **Data Analysis:** This involves the examination of digital evidence to identify patterns, anomalies, and other indicators of cybercrime. This includes the use of forensic software tools to analyze network traffic, email messages, system logs, and other digital artifacts.

- **Chain of Custody:** The process of documenting the handling of digital evidence to maintain its integrity and admissibility in court. This includes the use of digital signatures and other security measures to prevent tampering.

- **Expert Testimony:** The presentation of expert analysis and opinions in court to help judges and juries understand complex technical issues related to cybercrime.

## Challenges in Cyber Forensic Analytics:

- The rapid evolution of technology and the increasing complexity of digital systems and networks make it difficult to keep up with the latest forensic tools and techniques.

- Cyber criminals are becoming more sophisticated in their methods, making it harder to detect and prevent cyber-attacks.

- The legal and regulatory framework surrounding cybercrime is still evolving, making it difficult to develop consistent and effective investigative procedures.

## Career Opportunities in Cyber Forensic Analytics:

- Cyber forensic analysts are in high demand in both the public and private sectors, including law enforcement agencies, government agencies, and corporations.

- Some common job titles in cyber forensic analytics include digital forensic analyst, cybercrime investigator, and computer forensic examiner.

- A career in cyber forensic analytics requires a strong background in computer science, cybersecurity, and digital forensics, as well as knowledge of legal and regulatory frameworks related to cybercrime.
