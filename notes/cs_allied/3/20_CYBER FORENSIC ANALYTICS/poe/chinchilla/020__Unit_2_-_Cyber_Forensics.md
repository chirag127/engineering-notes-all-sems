## Unit 2 - Cyber Forensics

Cyber forensics is the process of collecting, analyzing, and preserving digital evidence to investigate and prevent cybercrime. It involves the use of various techniques and tools to extract data from digital devices and networks. In this unit, we will explore the key concepts and techniques of cyber forensics.

### Key Concepts of Cyber Forensics

1. Digital evidence: It refers to any data or information that can be used as evidence in a cybercrime investigation. It includes files, emails, chat logs, network traffic, and system logs.

2. Chain of custody: It is the process of maintaining a secure and documented trail of evidence from its collection to its presentation in court. It ensures that the evidence is not tampered with or corrupted.

3. Data acquisition: It is the process of collecting data from digital devices and networks using various techniques such as imaging, live analysis, and remote collection.

4. Analysis techniques: It includes various techniques such as keyword searches, file carving, data recovery, and timeline analysis to extract useful information from digital evidence.

5. Reporting: It involves documenting the findings of the investigation in a clear and concise report that can be presented in court.

### Techniques and Tools used in Cyber Forensics

1. Imaging: It is the process of creating a bit-by-bit copy of a digital device's storage media. It is used to preserve the integrity of the evidence and to allow for non-destructive analysis.

2. Live analysis: It is the process of analyzing a running system in real-time to collect volatile data such as running processes, network connections, and registry entries.

3. File carving: It is the process of extracting files from unallocated space, damaged or partially overwritten files, and deleted files.

4. Data recovery: It is the process of recovering data from damaged or inaccessible storage media.

5. Timeline analysis: It involves creating a timeline of events based on the timestamps of various digital artifacts such as files, system logs, and network traffic.

6. Forensic tools: Various forensic tools such as EnCase, FTK, and Autopsy are used to perform digital investigations.

### Legal Considerations in Cyber Forensics

1. Admissibility of evidence: The admissibility of digital evidence in court depends on various factors such as its relevance, authenticity, and reliability.

2. Privacy concerns: The collection and analysis of digital evidence must comply with privacy laws and regulations.

3. Chain of custody: The chain of custody must be maintained to ensure the admissibility of evidence in court.

4. Expert testimony: Cyber forensic experts may be required to provide expert testimony in court to explain the technical aspects of the investigation.

In conclusion, cyber forensics is an essential field that plays a crucial role in investigating and preventing cybercrime. It involves the use of various techniques and tools to extract and analyze digital evidence. Legal considerations such as admissibility of evidence, privacy concerns, and expert testimony must be taken into account during a cyber forensic investigation.
