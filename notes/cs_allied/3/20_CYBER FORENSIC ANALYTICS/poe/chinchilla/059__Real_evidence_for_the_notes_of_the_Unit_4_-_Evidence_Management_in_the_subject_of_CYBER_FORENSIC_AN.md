### Real Evidence for the Notes of the Unit 4 - Evidence Management in the Subject of CYBER FORENSIC ANALYTICS

As a cyber forensic analyst, it is crucial to understand the concept of real evidence and its importance in the legal system. Real evidence refers to physical evidence that is tangible and can be presented in court to prove a fact. In the context of cyber forensic analytics, real evidence can include electronic devices, storage media, and network logs.

Here are some key points to consider when dealing with real evidence in the context of evidence management:

1. Proper Documentation: Real evidence should be properly documented to maintain the chain of custody. The chain of custody is a record of the evidence's movement from the moment it was collected to the time it is presented in court. It is crucial to ensure that the evidence is not tampered with or altered in any way.

2. Preservation of Evidence: Preservation of real evidence is important to ensure that it remains in its original state. This involves storing the evidence in a secure location and ensuring that it is not exposed to any external factors that may damage it.

3. Analysis of Evidence: Real evidence should be analyzed thoroughly to obtain relevant information. This involves using forensic tools and techniques to extract data from electronic devices, storage media, and network logs.

4. Admissibility in Court: Real evidence must be admissible in court to be used as evidence. This means that it must be relevant to the case, reliable, and not hearsay.

5. Expert Testimony: Expert testimony may be required to explain the significance of real evidence in court. As a cyber forensic analyst, it is important to be able to provide expert testimony and explain technical concepts to non-technical audiences.

In conclusion, real evidence plays a crucial role in the field of cyber forensic analytics. Proper documentation, preservation, analysis, admissibility, and expert testimony are all important factors to consider when dealing with real evidence. By following these guidelines, cyber forensic analysts can ensure that real evidence is properly managed and presented in court to prove a fact.
