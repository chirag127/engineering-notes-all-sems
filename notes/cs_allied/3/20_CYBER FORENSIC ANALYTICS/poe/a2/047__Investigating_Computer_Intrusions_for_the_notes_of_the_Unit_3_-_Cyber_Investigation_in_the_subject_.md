### Investigating Computer Intrusions

- Identify the intrusion: The first step is to identify if there was an intrusion. Look for indicators like slow system performance, unexpected reboots, files disappearing or getting corrupted, etc. Check log files for any suspicious activity.
- Preserve the evidence: Once an intrusion is identified, it is important to preserve the system in its current state. Make a bit-by-bit copy of the hard drive and work on the copy. This preserves the original evidence intact.
- Determine the origin: Analyze the system to determine how the attacker gained access. This could be via an exploit on a software vulnerability, stolen credentials, etc. Check for any malicious software or backdoors left behind by the attacker. Analyze log files and network traffic to trace the origin of the attack.
- Understand the motivation: Assess the nature of data stolen or changed to determine the motivation and objective of the attack. Was data exfiltrated? Was the system used to launch other attacks? Analyze the attacker's movements and actions on the system to infer the motivation.
- Strengthen security: Document lessons learned from the investigation and apply necessary measures to strengthen system security and prevent future intrusions. This could include patching software vulnerabilities, using strong credentials, enabling logging/monitoring, etc.

The above points cover the key steps to investigate a computer intrusion. A formal and thorough investigation can help understand the nature and scope of an attack and apply remedies to bolster cybersecurity.
