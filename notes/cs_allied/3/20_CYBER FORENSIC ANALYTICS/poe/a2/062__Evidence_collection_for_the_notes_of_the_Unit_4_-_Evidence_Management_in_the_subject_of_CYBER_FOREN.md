### Evidence collection for the notes of the Unit 4 - Evidence Management in the subject of CYBER FORENSIC ANALYTICS

1. Identify the potential sources of evidence
- Live system: Volatile data such as RAM, running processes, open network connections, etc.
- Hard drives: Data stored on hard drives such as files, logs, etc.
- Removable media: External hard drives, USB drives, CDs/DVDs, etc.
- Mobile devices: Smartphones, tablets, etc.
- Cloud: Data stored on cloud services such as emails, documents, etc.

2. Determine appropriate evidence collection methodology
- Manual inspection: Going through the files/data manually to identify relevant evidence
- Targeted collection: Collecting specific types of files/data using appropriate tools
- Image acquisition: Taking a bit-by-bit image of the storage media to analyze it later in a controlled environment
- Live analysis: Analyzing a running system to collect volatile data
- Network traffic capture: Capturing ongoing network traffic to analyze network-based activities

3. Preserve integrity of evidence
- Write blockers: Preventing any writing to the storage media to preserve the original data
- Documenting chain of custody: Maintaining logs of people who handle the evidence to establish accountability
- Cryptographic hashing: Generating hashes of evidence to detect any changes or tampering
- Archiving: Storing the evidence carefully in tamper-proof containers or encrypted archives
- Duplicating: Creating duplicates of evidence and analyzing the duplicates instead of original evidence

4. Extract and analyze evidence
- Manual analysis: Browsing through the evidence to look for relevant data
- Keyword searching: Searching for specific keywords or patterns in the evidence
- Data visualization: Visualizing evidence data to identify patterns or relationships
- Statistical analysis: Applying statistical techniques to evidence to discover useful information
- Tool-based analysis: Using forensic tools to parse and analyze evidence to extract relevant data
