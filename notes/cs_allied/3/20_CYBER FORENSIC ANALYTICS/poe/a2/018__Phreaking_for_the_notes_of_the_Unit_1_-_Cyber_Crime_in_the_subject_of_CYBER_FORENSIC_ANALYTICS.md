### Phreaking

- Phreaking refers to the hacking of telephone systems to get free calls or access features.
- Phreakers explore and exploit weaknesses in telephone systems to get free calls, access operator functions, or explore the telephone network architecture.
- Phreaking was popular in the 1960s and 1970s but declined with the introduction of modern digital telephone exchanges. However, some old analogue systems are still vulnerable.
- Phreakers may use devices known as blue boxes or red boxes to manipulate telephone signals and compromise the system.
- While phreaking is illegal, it is considered a predecessor to modern phone hacking and computer hacking. Understanding old phreaking techniques provides insight into vulnerabilities in communications systems.
- Phreaking is relevant to cyber forensics as phone records and systems may be a source of evidence, and phreaking attacks could be used to compromise a phone-based investigation or access data held by phone companies. Analysts need to be aware of phreaking techniques to properly investigate and understand attacks on phone systems.
