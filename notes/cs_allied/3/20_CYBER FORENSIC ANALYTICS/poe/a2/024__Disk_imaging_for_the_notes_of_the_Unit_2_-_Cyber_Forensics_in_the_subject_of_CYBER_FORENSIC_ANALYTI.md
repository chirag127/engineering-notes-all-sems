### Disk imaging for the notes of the Unit 2 - Cyber Forensics in the subject of CYBER FORENSIC ANALYTICS

1. Disk imaging is the process of copying the contents of a disk to another medium. It is often done for backup, data recovery, or analysis purposes.
2. In cyber forensics, disk imaging is crucial to acquire a bit-by-bit copy of the digital evidence without altering the original evidence. The forensic image can then be analyzed safely using various forensic analysis tools.
3. The common steps involved in disk imaging for cyber forensics are:

a. Attach the digital device to the forensic workstation.
b. Use a write-blocking device to prevent any writes to the original evidence device.
c. Use a forensic imaging tool to create an exact sector-by-sector image of the disk.
d. Hash the forensic image and original evidence device to verify integrity.
e. Analyze the forensic image using appropriate tools.
f. Preserve the original evidence device intact.

4. Some popular disk imaging tools for cyber forensics are Guidance Software EnCase, AccessData FTK, and Digital Forensics Framework (DFF).
5. Disk imaging allows non-intrusive acquisition and analysis of digital evidence while preserving the original evidence intact, which is a key requirement for cyber forensics.
