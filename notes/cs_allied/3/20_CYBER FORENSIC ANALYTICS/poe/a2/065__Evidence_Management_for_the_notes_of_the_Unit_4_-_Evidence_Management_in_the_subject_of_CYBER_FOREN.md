### Evidence Management for the notes of the Unit 4 - Evidence Management in the subject of CYBER FORENSIC ANALYTICS

1. Evidence acquisition - Collecting potential evidence from the crime scene in a forensically sound manner. This includes both digital and physical evidence. The evidence should be preserved in its original state.
2. Evidence examination - The collected evidence is examined to analyze its relevance to the case. The evidence is inspected, interpreted, and evaluated. Various forensic tools and techniques are employed to extract data and derive insights from the evidence.
3. Evidence analysis - The examined evidence is analyzed to understand connections and draw conclusions. The analysis involves reconstructing events and timelines, finding patterns, and determining the importance and implications of the evidence.
4. Evidence presentation - The analyzed evidence and results are presented in a structured format. This could be in the form of reports, presentations, or as testimony in courts. The evidence and analysis should be presented in a comprehensive, objective, and easy to understand manner.
5. Evidence storage and retention - The acquired evidence needs to be properly stored and retained for an appropriate time period. This ensures the evidence is preserved in case it is required again for reference or additional analysis. Laws may mandate specific storage and retention requirements for different types of evidence.

The above points cover the key steps involved in properly managing digital evidence. Following established procedures and guidelines for evidence management is crucial to ensure the evidence lifecycle is comprehensive, robust, and stands up to scrutiny.
