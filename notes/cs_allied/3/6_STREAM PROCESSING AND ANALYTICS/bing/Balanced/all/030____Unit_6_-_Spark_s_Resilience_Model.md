## Unit 6 - Spark’s Resilience Model

- Resilience is the ability to cope with stress and adversity, and to bounce back from challenges and difficulties.
- Resilience is not a fixed trait that some people have and others don't. It is a dynamic process that can be learned and developed over time.
- Spark's resilience model is a framework that helps to understand and enhance resilience at individual, team and organizational levels.
- The model consists of four components: **Self**, **Situation**, **Support** and **Strategies**.
- **Self** refers to the personal factors that influence resilience, such as self-awareness, self-regulation, self-efficacy, self-care and self-compassion.
- **Situation** refers to the external factors that influence resilience, such as the nature, intensity and duration of the stressor, the degree of control and predictability, and the opportunities for learning and growth.
- **Support** refers to the social factors that influence resilience, such as the availability, quality and diversity of the support network, the level of trust and reciprocity, and the sense of belonging and community.
- **Strategies** refers to the behavioral factors that influence resilience, such as the coping skills, problem-solving skills, communication skills and stress management skills that are used to deal with the stressor and its consequences.
- The model suggests that resilience can be enhanced by:
  - Increasing the positive aspects and reducing the negative aspects of each component.
  - Balancing the components and finding the optimal level of each component for different situations and contexts.
  - Integrating the components and using them in a holistic and synergistic way.
