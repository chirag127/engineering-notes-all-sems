## Unit 2 - Stream-Processing Model

- A stream-processing model is a computational model that processes data as a continuous sequence of elements, called a stream.
- A stream can be finite or infinite, depending on the source and the termination condition of the computation.
- A stream-processing model consists of three main components: sources, operators, and sinks.
- Sources are the entities that generate or provide the data elements for the stream, such as sensors, files, or network connections.
- Operators are the functions that transform, filter, aggregate, or combine the data elements in the stream, such as map, filter, reduce, or join.
- Sinks are the entities that consume or output the data elements from the stream, such as displays, files, or network connections.
- A stream-processing model can be represented as a directed graph, where the nodes are the sources, operators, and sinks, and the edges are the streams that connect them.
- A stream-processing model can be executed in different ways, such as batch processing, micro-batch processing, or real-time processing, depending on the latency and throughput requirements of the application.
- A stream-processing model can handle dynamic and unbounded data, such as events, messages, or sensor readings, that are generated at high rates and volumes.
- A stream-processing model can enable low-latency and scalable analytics, such as anomaly detection, fraud detection, or recommendation systems, on streaming data.
