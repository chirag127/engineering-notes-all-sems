## Unit 6 - Spark’s Resilience Model

- Resilience is the ability to cope with stress, adversity, and change.
- Spark’s resilience model is a framework that helps individuals and organizations develop resilience skills and strategies.
- The model consists of four components: **Self**, **Situation**, **Support**, and **Strategies**.
- **Self** refers to the personal factors that influence resilience, such as self-awareness, self-regulation, self-efficacy, and self-care.
- **Situation** refers to the external factors that influence resilience, such as the nature, duration, and intensity of the stressor, the resources available, and the opportunities for learning and growth.
- **Support** refers to the social factors that influence resilience, such as the quality and quantity of relationships, the availability and accessibility of support, and the diversity and reciprocity of support.
- **Strategies** refers to the actions and behaviors that influence resilience, such as problem-solving, coping, communication, and adaptation.
- The model suggests that resilience is not a fixed trait, but a dynamic process that can be enhanced by applying the four components in a balanced and flexible way.
- The model also suggests that resilience is not a one-size-fits-all concept, but a context-specific and person-specific concept that depends on the interaction of the four components and the individual's goals and values.
- The model can be used as a tool for self-assessment, self-development, and self-management of resilience, as well as a guide for providing and receiving support, feedback, and coaching on resilience.
