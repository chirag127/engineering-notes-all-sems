### Immutable Streams Defined from One AnotherTransformations and Aggregations for the notes of the Unit 2 - Stream-Processing Model in the subject of STREAM PROCESSING AND ANALYTICS

Immutable streams are streams of data that cannot be modified once they are created. In stream processing, the data is processed as a continuous flow of events, and the processing logic is applied to each event as it arrives. The processed data is then used to update the state of the system, and the updated state is used to process the next event.

Transformations are operations performed on a stream of data to produce a new stream of data. Examples of transformations include filtering, mapping, and reducing. Transformations are typically performed on a per-event basis, and the output of a transformation is used as the input for the next transformation.

Aggregations are operations performed on a stream of data to produce a single value or a set of values. Examples of aggregations include summing, averaging, and counting. Aggregations are typically performed on a per-window basis, where a window is a set of events processed over a specified time interval.

In conclusion, immutable streams, transformations, and aggregations are fundamental concepts in stream processing. Immutable streams provide a way to process data as it arrives, transformations provide a way to modify the data as it is processed, and aggregations provide a way to summarize the data and produce meaningful insights. These concepts are used in a variety of applications, including financial services, telecommunications, and social media, to process and analyze data in real-time.
