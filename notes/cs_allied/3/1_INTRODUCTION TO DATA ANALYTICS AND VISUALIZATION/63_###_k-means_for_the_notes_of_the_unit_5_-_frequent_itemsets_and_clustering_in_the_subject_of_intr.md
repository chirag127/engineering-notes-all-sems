K-Means

K-Means is a popular clustering algorithm used to divide a dataset into k clusters, where k is a user-defined parameter. The goal of K-Means is to minimize the sum of squared distances between the data points and the centroid of the cluster to which they belong.

K-Means is an unsupervised learning algorithm, meaning that it does not require labeled data. Instead, it uses the data itself to identify patterns and group similar data points into clusters. K-Means is a simple and efficient algorithm, and it is widely used in a variety of applications, including market segmentation, customer segmentation, and image segmentation.

K-Means works by initializing k centroids, and then assigning each data point to the closest centroid. The centroids are then updated based on the mean of the data points assigned to them, and the process is repeated until the centroids converge or until a maximum number of iterations is reached.

In conclusion, K-Means is a popular clustering algorithm used to divide a dataset into k clusters. K-Means is an unsupervised learning algorithm that uses the data itself to identify patterns and group similar data points into clusters.
