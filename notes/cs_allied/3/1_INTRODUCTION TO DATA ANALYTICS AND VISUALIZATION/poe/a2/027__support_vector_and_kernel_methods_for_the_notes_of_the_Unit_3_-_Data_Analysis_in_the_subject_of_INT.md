### Support Vector and Kernel Methods

- Support Vector Machines (SVMs) are a set of supervised learning methods used for classification and regression.
- They are based on the idea of finding a hyperplane that separates the data points of two classes. The hyperplane is chosen to have the maximum margin, that is, the maximum distance between the hyperplane and the nearest data point.
- The points that are closest to the hyperplane are called support vectors. The hyperplane is constructed to have maximum distance from support vectors.
- SVMs can be extended to non-linear classifiers using the kernel trick. The kernel replaces the dot product between two vectors with a non-linear function, which enables finding a non-linear hyperplane that separates the data.
- Some commonly used kernels are:
-- Linear kernel: No change, dot product is used
-- Polynomial kernel: Enables learning non-linear functions of higher degrees
-- Radial Basis Function (RBF) kernel: Puts a Gaussian kernel over each training data point, useful for learning infinitely complex non-linear functions.
- SVMs are powerful methods and are less prone to overfitting due to their large margin approach. However, the non-linear SVMs with complex kernels can be slow to train due to high computational costs. They also require careful tuning of meta-parameters like the kernel scale and regularization constant.
