## Unit 7 - Human Vision

1. The human eye is like a camera. It has a lens that focuses light on the retina, which detects light and converts it into electrical signals sent to the brain.

2. The lens is located behind the iris, which controls the amount of light that enters the eye. The lens focuses light on the retina for sharp images.

3. The retina contains photoreceptors called rods and cones that detect light and color. Rods are responsible for peripheral and night vision, while cones allow for central and color vision.

4. The optic nerve carries electrical signals from the eye to the brain, which reconstructs the images from the signals into what we see. The brain interprets the images and gives them meaning.

5. Visual perception involves both bottom-up and top-down processing. Bottom-up processing builds up perceptions from sensory input, while top-down processing involves expectations and interpretations of the sensory input.
