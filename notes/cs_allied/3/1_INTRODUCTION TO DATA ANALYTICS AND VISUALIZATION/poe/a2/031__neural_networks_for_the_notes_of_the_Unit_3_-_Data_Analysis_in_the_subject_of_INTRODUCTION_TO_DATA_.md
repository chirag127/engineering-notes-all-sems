### Neural Networks

1. Neural networks are a type of machine learning algorithm that is inspired by the human brain. They are made up of interconnected nodes that must be trained on large amounts of data to learn how to perform a task.
2. The nodes are organized into layers:
- Input layer: The data is fed into the network through this layer.
- Hidden layer(s): The layers between the input and output layers that determine the patterns in the data. The number of hidden layers and nodes in each layer vary based on the complexity of the task.
- Output layer: The results of the network's predictions or decisions are output through this layer.
3. As the network is exposed to large amounts of data, it automatically learns by adjusting the connections between nodes in an iterative process. The strengths of the connections are weighted based on how accurate the network's predictions were. Over time and with enough data, the network can learn to make predictions on new data.
4. The power of neural networks comes from their ability to automatically learn complex patterns in large data sets. However, they are "black boxes" that are difficult to interpret, and they require significant amounts of data to train the network. They also have limitations with handling structured/symbolic data.
