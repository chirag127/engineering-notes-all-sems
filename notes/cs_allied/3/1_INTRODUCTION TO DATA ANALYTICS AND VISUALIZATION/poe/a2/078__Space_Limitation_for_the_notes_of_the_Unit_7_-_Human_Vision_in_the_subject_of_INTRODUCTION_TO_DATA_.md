### Space Limitation for the notes of the Unit 7 - Human Vision

1. Human visual system has limitations in perceiving and processing information. Some of the key limitations are:
- Limited resolution: The human eye has limited number of photoreceptors, so we can't see very small details or distinguish between very small changes in size or color.
- Limited visual field: We can only see a limited region around our focal point. Our peripheral vision has low resolution.
- Limited dynamic range: The range of light intensities that we can see is limited. We can't see well in very low or very high light conditions.
- Limited processing speed: The human visual system can get overwhelmed when shown a large amount of information simultaneously or in quick succession. We need time to scan, perceive and process the visual information.
- Perceptual biases: Human perception is subjective and prone to various cognitive biases. We tend to perceive what we expect or want to see, not necessarily the objective reality.

2. These limitations of human vision should be kept in mind while designing data visualizations and displays. The visualizations should be designed based on the capabilities and limitations of the human visual system to be effective. Some tips for designing within human visual system limitations are:
- Use appropriate scale and resolution for the data and tasks
- Minimize clutter and unnecessary details
- Use adequate contrast and dynamic range suitable for the viewing conditions
- Present information sequentially or with suitable pacing, avoid overloading the visual system
- Be aware of and minimize potential perceptual biases
