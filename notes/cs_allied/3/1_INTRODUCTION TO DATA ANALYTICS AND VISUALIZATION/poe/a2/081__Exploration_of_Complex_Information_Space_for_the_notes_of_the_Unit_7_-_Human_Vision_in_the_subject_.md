### Exploration of Complex Information Space for the notes of the Unit 7 - Human Vision in the subject of INTRODUCTION TO DATA ANALYTICS AND VISUALIZATION

1. Human vision system: The human vision system consists of the visual receptors in the eye, the optic nerve that transmits signals from the eye to the brain, and the visual processing components in the brain. The eye has photoreceptors that detect light and convert it into electrical signals. The optic nerve transmits these signals to the brain, where they are processed and interpreted.

2. Visual perception: Visual perception is the ability to interpret the surrounding environment using light in the visible spectrum reflected by the objects in the environment. The visual system in the brain is responsible for processing the visual signals and deriving perceptions and meaning from the signals. Visual perception involves both bottom-up processing and top-down processing.

3. Bottom-up processing: Bottom-up processing refers to the flow of visual information from the eyes to the brain. The visual stimuli are processed in a hierarchical fashion, starting from basic features like color and edges to more complex interpretations. This is data-driven processing.

4. Top-down processing: Top-down processing refers to the influence of prior knowledge and expectations on perception. Higher-level cognition influences lower-level perception. Attention and focus are aspects of top-down processing. Context and experience play a role in guiding perception via top-down processing.

5. Perceptual organization: The perceptual system organizes the visual inputs into coherent and meaningful structures through grouping principles such as proximity, similarity, continuity, closure, etc. This allows us to perceive objects and scenes rather than just individual components. Perceptual organization is important for making sense of complex visual information.
