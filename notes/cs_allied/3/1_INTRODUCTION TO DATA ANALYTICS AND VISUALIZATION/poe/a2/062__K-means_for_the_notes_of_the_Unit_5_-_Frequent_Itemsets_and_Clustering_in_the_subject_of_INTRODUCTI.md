### K-means for the notes of the Unit 5 - Frequent Itemsets and Clustering in the subject of INTRODUCTION TO DATA ANALYTICS AND VISUALIZATION

K-means is an unsupervised learning algorithm that clusters data by trying to separate samples into k clusters of equal variance, minimizing a criterion known as the inertia or within-cluster sum-of-squares.

Steps involved in K-Means Clustering:

1. Choose the number of clusters k.
2. Randomly initialize k centroids.
3. Assign each data point to the closest centroid (forms k clusters).
4. Compute and place the new centroid of each cluster.
5. Reassign each data point to the new closest centroid.
6. Repeat steps 4 and 5 until convergence (centroids no longer change).

Advantages:

- Simple and efficient algorithm.
- Useful for exploratory data analysis to find hidden patterns.
- Robust to outliers as it uses centroid.

Disadvantages:

- Needs to specify the number of clusters k.
- Sensitive to the initial randomly chosen centroids.
- Tends to find spherical clusters, fails on non-convex clusters.
- Does not produce unique clustering (different runs may produce different clustering).

Applications:

- Image segmentation.
- Recommender systems.
- Document clustering.
- Bioinformatics.
