### Discovery

1. Define the business problem: Identify the key business problem or question you are trying to solve. This could be increasing revenue, reducing costs, understanding customer churn, etc. Clearly defining the problem is critical to the success of any data analytics project.

2. Gather initial data: Review what data is already available that could help solve the problem. This could be data from the company's databases, external data sources, or data that needs to be gathered from customer surveys or other methods. The goal is to get an initial sense of what data is available and what additional data may be needed.

3. Refine and expand data gathering: If additional data is needed, work to gather that data. This may require coordination with other teams in the company. The more quality data you have, the better insights you will be able to gain from the data analytics process.

4. Explore the data: Briefly explore the data to get a sense of what insights could be gained and what analytic techniques could be applied. Even simple statistics or data visualizations can provide an initial sense of the key patterns and relationships in the data. This exploration helps determine if the right data has been gathered and if the key business problem can likely be solved with the available data.

5. Determine analytics approach: Based on the initial exploration, determine what analytic techniques would be appropriate to apply to the data to solve the key business problem. This could include deeper statistical analysis, machine learning, data mining, or other approaches. The approach should be tailored to the problem and available data.
