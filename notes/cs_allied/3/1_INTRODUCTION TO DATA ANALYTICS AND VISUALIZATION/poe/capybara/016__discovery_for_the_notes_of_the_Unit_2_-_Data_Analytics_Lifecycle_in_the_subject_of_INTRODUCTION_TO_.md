### Discovery for the notes of the Unit 2 - Data Analytics Lifecycle in the subject of INTRODUCTION TO DATA ANALYTICS AND VISUALIZATION

In the data analytics lifecycle, the first step is Discovery, which involves identifying the problem, understanding the data, and formulating a hypothesis. Here are some key points to keep in mind during the Discovery phase:

- **Identify the problem**: The first step in the Discovery phase is to identify the problem that needs to be solved. This could be a business problem, such as improving sales or reducing costs, or a research problem, such as finding a cure for a disease. It is essential to clearly define the problem and understand its scope and context.
- **Understand the data**: Once the problem is identified, the next step is to understand the data that is available. This involves collecting and analyzing data to gain insights into the problem. It is important to identify the sources of data, the data types, and the quality of the data.
- **Formulate a hypothesis**: Based on the insights gained from analyzing the data, formulate a hypothesis that explains the problem and suggests a solution. The hypothesis should be testable and falsifiable, and should be based on sound reasoning and evidence.

During the Discovery phase, it is important to involve stakeholders, such as business managers, domain experts, and data scientists, to ensure that the problem is well-defined and the hypothesis is sound. It is also important to document the process and the findings, as this will help in the subsequent phases of the data analytics lifecycle.

In conclusion, the Discovery phase is a crucial step in the data analytics lifecycle, as it sets the stage for the subsequent phases of data preparation, modeling, evaluation, and deployment. By following the key points outlined above, you can ensure that the Discovery phase is well-planned, well-executed, and well-documented, leading to better insights and better solutions.
