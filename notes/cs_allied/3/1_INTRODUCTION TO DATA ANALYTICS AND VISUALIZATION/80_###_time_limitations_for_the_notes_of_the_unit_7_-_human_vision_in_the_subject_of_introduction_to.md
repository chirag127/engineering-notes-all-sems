Time Limitations

Human vision is subject to time limitations, which refer to the limitations on the amount of information that can be processed in a given period of time. These limitations can have a significant impact on the effectiveness of visualizations, and they can affect the ability of users to understand and interpret visualizations.

One of the main time limitations of human vision is the persistence of vision, which refers to the amount of time that an image can be held in the visual field after it has been presented. The persistence of vision is typically in the range of 100-200 milliseconds, which means that images must be presented at a rate of at least 5-10 frames per second in order to be perceived as continuous.

Another time limitation of human vision is the flicker fusion threshold, which refers to the minimum rate at which images must be presented in order to avoid flicker. The flicker fusion threshold is typically in the range of 50-80 Hz, which means that images must be presented at a rate of at least 50-80 frames per second in order to be perceived as continuous.

In conclusion, human vision is subject to time limitations, including the persistence of vision and the flicker fusion threshold. These limitations can have a significant impact on the effectiveness of visualizations, and they can affect the ability of users to understand and interpret visualizations.
