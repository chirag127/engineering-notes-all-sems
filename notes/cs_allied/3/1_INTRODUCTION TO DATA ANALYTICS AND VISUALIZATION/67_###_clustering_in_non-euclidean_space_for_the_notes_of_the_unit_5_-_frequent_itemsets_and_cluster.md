Clustering in Non-Euclidean Space in Data Analytics:
- Clustering is a data analysis technique used to group similar data points into clusters.
- Euclidean space is a mathematical space where distances are defined using the Euclidean distance metric.
- Clustering in non-Euclidean space refers to the process of clustering data points in spaces where the Euclidean distance metric is not appropriate.
- Non-Euclidean spaces can include spaces where distances are defined using metrics such as cosine similarity, Jaccard similarity, or Hamming distance.
- Clustering in non-Euclidean spaces can be performed using a variety of algorithms, including:
  1. K-Means
  2. Hierarchical Clustering
  3. DBSCAN
- Clustering in non-Euclidean spaces is important for the analysis and interpretation of data in domains where the Euclidean distance metric is not appropriate, such as text data, image data, or network data.
- Understanding clustering in non-Euclidean space is important for the development of data-driven solutions to real-world problems.
