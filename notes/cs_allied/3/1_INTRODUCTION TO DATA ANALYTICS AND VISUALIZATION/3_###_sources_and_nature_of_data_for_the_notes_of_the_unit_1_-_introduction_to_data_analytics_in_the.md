Unit 1: Introduction to Data Analytics

Sources and Nature of Data

Data is the foundation of data analytics, and it comes from a variety of sources, including databases, spreadsheets, and web APIs. Data can also be collected directly from users, such as through surveys or online forms.

Data can be structured or unstructured, and it can be either quantitative or qualitative. Structured data is data that is organized into tables or spreadsheets, and it is easy to analyze and manipulate. Unstructured data is data that is not organized into tables or spreadsheets, and it can be more difficult to analyze and manipulate. Quantitative data is data that can be measured, such as height or weight, and qualitative data is data that cannot be measured, such as opinions or attitudes.

Data can also be continuous or categorical. Continuous data is data that can take on any value within a range, such as height or weight. Categorical data is data that can only take on a limited number of values, such as gender or color.

In conclusion, data is the foundation of data analytics, and it comes from a variety of sources, including databases, spreadsheets, and web APIs. Data can be structured or unstructured, quantitative or qualitative, and continuous or categorical, and understanding the sources and nature of data is an important aspect of the subject of Introduction to Data Analytics and Visualization.
