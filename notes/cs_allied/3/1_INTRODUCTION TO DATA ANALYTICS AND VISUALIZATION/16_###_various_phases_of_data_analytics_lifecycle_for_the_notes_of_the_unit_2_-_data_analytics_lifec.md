### various phases of data analytics lifecycle for the notes of the Unit 2 - Data Analytics Lifecycle in the subject of INTRODUCTION TO DATA ANALYTICS AND VISUALIZATION

The data analytics lifecycle is the process of transforming raw data into insights and actionable information. It involves several phases, including data collection, data preparation, data analysis, and data visualization.

1. Data Collection: The first phase of the data analytics lifecycle is data collection, which involves gathering data from various sources, such as databases, spreadsheets, and APIs.

2. Data Preparation: The second phase of the data analytics lifecycle is data preparation, which involves cleaning, transforming, and merging data to make it ready for analysis. This phase also involves identifying and handling missing and inconsistent data.

3. Data Analysis: The third phase of the data analytics lifecycle is data analysis, which involves exploring and modeling the data to identify patterns, relationships, and trends. This phase also involves testing hypotheses and making predictions based on the data.

4. Data Visualization: The fourth phase of the data analytics lifecycle is data visualization, which involves presenting the insights and information generated from the data analysis in a clear and intuitive manner. This phase involves creating charts, graphs, and other visualizations that help to communicate the insights and information to stakeholders.

5. Advantages: The data analytics lifecycle has several advantages, including the ability to turn raw data into actionable information, the ability to identify patterns and trends in the data, and the ability to make predictions based on the data.

6. Limitations: Despite its advantages, the data analytics lifecycle also has several limitations, including the difficulty of ensuring the accuracy of the data, the difficulty of ensuring the quality of the data, and the difficulty of ensuring the scalability of the data.

7. Applications: The data analytics lifecycle is used in a wide range of applications, including business, finance, healthcare, and social sciences.
