### clustering for streams and parallelism for the notes of the Unit 5 - Frequent Itemsets and Clustering in the subject of INTRODUCTION TO DATA ANALYTICS AND VISUALIZATION

Clustering is a technique in data analysis and machine learning for grouping similar data points into clusters. The goal of clustering is to partition the data into groups such that the data points within each group are more similar to each other than to those in other groups. There are several popular clustering algorithms, including:

1. K-means
2. Hierarchical clustering
3. Density-based clustering
4. Gaussian Mixture Models

Clustering is used in a variety of applications, including image processing, customer segmentation, and anomaly detection.

Streaming refers to processing large amounts of data in real-time, as the data is being generated. This is in contrast to batch processing, where data is processed in chunks after it has been collected. Streaming algorithms are designed to handle data in real-time, and must be able to process data quickly and efficiently.

Parallelism refers to the ability to split a computational task into smaller parts, which can be executed simultaneously on multiple processors or cores. Parallelism is often used to speed up computations, as the smaller parts can be executed in parallel, reducing the overall time required to complete the task.

In summary, clustering is a technique for grouping similar data points, while streaming and parallelism are techniques for processing large amounts of data efficiently in real-time.
