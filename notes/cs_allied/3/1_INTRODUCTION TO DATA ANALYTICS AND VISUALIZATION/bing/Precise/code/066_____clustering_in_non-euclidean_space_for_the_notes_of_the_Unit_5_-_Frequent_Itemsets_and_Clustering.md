### Clustering in Non-Euclidean Space

Clustering is the process of grouping similar objects together. In non-Euclidean space, the distance between objects is not measured using the standard Euclidean distance formula. Instead, other distance measures such as the Manhattan distance or the Minkowski distance can be used.

1. Non-Euclidean space refers to any space that is not Euclidean, meaning it does not follow the standard Euclidean geometry.
2. Clustering in non-Euclidean space can be more challenging than in Euclidean space because the distance between objects is not as straightforward to calculate.
3. Common distance measures used in non-Euclidean space include the Manhattan distance, the Minkowski distance, and the Mahalanobis distance.
4. Clustering algorithms that can be used in non-Euclidean space include k-means, hierarchical clustering, and DBSCAN.
5. When clustering in non-Euclidean space, it is important to choose an appropriate distance measure and clustering algorithm for the data at hand.
