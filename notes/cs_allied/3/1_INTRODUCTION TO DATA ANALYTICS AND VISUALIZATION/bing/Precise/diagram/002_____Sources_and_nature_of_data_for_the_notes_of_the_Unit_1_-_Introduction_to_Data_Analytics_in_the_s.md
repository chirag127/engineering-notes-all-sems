### Sources and nature of data

Data is a collection of facts, such as numbers, words, measurements, observations or even just descriptions of things. Data can come from a variety of sources, including:

1. **Surveys**: Data can be collected through surveys, where individuals or organizations are asked to provide information about themselves or their opinions on a particular topic.

2. **Experiments**: Data can also be collected through experiments, where researchers manipulate one or more variables to observe the effect on an outcome.

3. **Observational studies**: Data can be collected through observational studies, where researchers observe and record the behavior or characteristics of individuals or groups without manipulating any variables.

4. **Administrative data**: Data can also be obtained from administrative sources, such as government records, medical records, or financial records.

5. **Big data**: With the rise of technology, large amounts of data are being generated and collected from sources such as social media, online transactions, and sensor networks. This type of data is often referred to as big data.

The nature of data can vary depending on the source and the context in which it is collected. Data can be quantitative or qualitative, continuous or discrete, and can be measured at different levels of measurement, such as nominal, ordinal, interval, or ratio.

It is important to carefully consider the sources and nature of data when conducting data analysis, as the quality and reliability of the data can have a significant impact on the results of the analysis.
