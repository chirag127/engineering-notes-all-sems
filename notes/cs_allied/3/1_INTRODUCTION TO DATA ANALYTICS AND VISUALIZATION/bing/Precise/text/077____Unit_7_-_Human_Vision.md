## Unit 7 - Human Vision

1. Human vision is the ability to interpret the surrounding environment using light in the visible spectrum reflected by the objects in the environment.
2. The human eye is the primary organ responsible for vision and is made up of several parts, including the cornea, iris, pupil, lens, retina, and optic nerve.
3. Light enters the eye through the cornea, which is a clear, dome-shaped surface that covers the front of the eye.
4. The iris, which is the colored part of the eye, controls the amount of light that enters the eye by adjusting the size of the pupil.
5. The lens, which is located behind the pupil, focuses the light onto the retina, which is a layer of light-sensitive cells at the back of the eye.
6. The retina converts the light into electrical signals, which are then transmitted to the brain via the optic nerve.
7. The brain interprets these signals to create the images that we see.
8. Humans have binocular vision, which means that we use both eyes to see and the brain combines the images from each eye to create a single, three-dimensional image.
9. The human eye is capable of detecting a wide range of colors and can adapt to different levels of light.
10. However, human vision is not perfect and can be affected by various conditions, such as nearsightedness, farsightedness, and color blindness.
