### Unit 7 - Human Vision

1. Human vision is the ability to interpret the surrounding environment using light in the visible spectrum reflected by the objects in the environment.
2. The human eye is the primary organ for vision and is capable of detecting light and color.
3. The retina, located at the back of the eye, contains photoreceptor cells that convert light into electrical signals.
4. These signals are then transmitted to the brain via the optic nerve, where they are interpreted as visual images.
5. The visual system is capable of perceiving a wide range of colors and brightness levels, and can adapt to changes in lighting conditions.
6. The perception of color is influenced by the wavelength of light, with different wavelengths corresponding to different colors.
7. The perception of brightness is influenced by the intensity of light, with brighter light appearing more intense.
8. The visual system is also capable of perceiving depth and motion, allowing us to navigate our environment and interact with objects.
9. The study of human vision is important for the design of effective visualizations, as it provides insights into how we perceive and interpret visual information.
10. By understanding the capabilities and limitations of the visual system, we can design visualizations that effectively communicate information to the user.
