# Discovery

Discovery is the first phase of the Data Analytics Lifecycle. It is the process of understanding the business requirements, defining the scope of the problem, and identifying the data sources that will be used in the analysis. The following are the key points to consider during the discovery phase:

1. **Understand the business requirements:** It is important to understand the business objectives and the questions that need to be answered through the analysis. This will help in defining the scope of the problem and identifying the relevant data sources.

2. **Define the scope of the problem:** The scope of the problem should be clearly defined to ensure that the analysis is focused and relevant. This includes identifying the key variables and metrics that will be used in the analysis.

3. **Identify the data sources:** The data sources that will be used in the analysis should be identified and assessed for their relevance and reliability. This includes both internal and external data sources.

4. **Assess the data quality:** The quality of the data should be assessed to ensure that it is accurate and reliable. This includes checking for missing or inconsistent data, and identifying any potential biases or errors.

5. **Prepare a project plan:** A project plan should be prepared to outline the key steps and milestones in the analysis. This will help to ensure that the project is well-organized and on track.

In summary, the discovery phase is a crucial first step in the Data Analytics Lifecycle. It involves understanding the business requirements, defining the scope of the problem, and identifying the data sources that will be used in the analysis. This phase sets the foundation for the rest of the analysis and is essential for ensuring that the analysis is relevant and focused.
