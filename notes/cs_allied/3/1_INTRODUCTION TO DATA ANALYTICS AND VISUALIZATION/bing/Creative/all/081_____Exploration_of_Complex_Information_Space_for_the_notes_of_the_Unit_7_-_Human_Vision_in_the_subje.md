# Exploration of Complex Information Space

- Complex information spaces are information spaces that are not only large but also heterogeneous in their structure, meaning that they contain different types of data, such as text, images, audio, video, etc.
- Exploration of complex information spaces is the process of interacting with data in order to extract meaningful information, such as patterns, trends, outliers, relationships, etc.
- Exploration of complex information spaces requires presentation techniques that provide intuitive orientation aids and navigation techniques, such as overviews, zooming, filtering, highlighting, etc.
- Exploration of complex information spaces also requires information visualizations that represent the data in a graphical or symbolic way that facilitates perception, cognition, and communication, such as maps, graphs, charts, diagrams, etc.
- Exploration of complex information spaces can benefit from cluster analysis, which is a technique that groups similar data items together based on some similarity measure, such as distance, density, or connectivity.
- Cluster analysis can help to reduce the complexity of the information space, reveal the underlying structure of the data, and support the user's exploration tasks, such as browsing, searching, comparing, etc.
- Cluster analysis can be combined with 3D visualization and interaction techniques to create a visual exploration system that allows the user to navigate in a 3D space, where each cluster is represented by a 3D object, such as a sphere, a cube, or a polyhedron.
- The 3D objects can be arranged in a hierarchical or a flat layout, depending on the level of detail and the number of clusters.
- The 3D objects can also be colored, sized, labeled, or textured, depending on the attributes of the clusters, such as the number of data items, the average similarity, the cluster name, or the cluster content.
- The user can interact with the 3D objects by selecting, rotating, scaling, moving, or opening them, depending on the exploration task, such as inspecting, focusing, filtering, or drilling down.
- The user can also switch between different views, such as a 2D overview, a 3D perspective, or a 3D fisheye, depending on the exploration goal, such as getting an overview, getting a detail, or getting a context.
