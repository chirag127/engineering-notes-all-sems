# Computational Support for Visualization

- Visualization is the process of creating and manipulating graphical representations of data to enhance human understanding and communication .
- Visualization can be classified into two broad categories: data visualization and computational visualization.
- Data visualization deals with abstract data, such as numbers, text, or categories, that do not have a direct spatial or physical correspondence. Examples of data visualization are charts, graphs, infographics, and dashboards.
- Computational visualization deals with data that have a spatial or physical correspondence, such as images, volumes, surfaces, or meshes. Examples of computational visualization are medical imaging, computer-aided design, scientific simulation, and virtual reality.
- Computational support for visualization refers to the hardware and software tools that enable the creation, manipulation, and interaction of visual representations of data.
- Computational support for visualization can be divided into three main components: data sources, visualization systems, and display devices.
- Data sources are the origin of the data to be visualized, such as databases, files, sensors, or simulations. Data sources can provide static or dynamic, structured or unstructured, and scalar or vector data.
- Visualization systems are the software applications or libraries that perform the visualization tasks, such as data processing, mapping, rendering, and interaction. Visualization systems can be general-purpose or domain-specific, standalone or integrated, and serial or parallel.
- Display devices are the hardware components that present the visual representations of data to the user, such as monitors, projectors, or head-mounted displays. Display devices can vary in size, resolution, color, and dimensionality.
- Computational support for visualization can also involve other aspects, such as user interface, interaction techniques, visual design, evaluation methods, and ethical issues.
- Computational support for visualization is an active and interdisciplinary research field that draws from computer science, mathematics, statistics, psychology, art, and domain knowledge.
