# Limitation for the notes of the Unit 6 - Introduction to Visualization

- Visualization is the process of creating and displaying graphical representations of data or information to communicate insights, patterns, trends, or relationships.
- Visualization can be used for various purposes, such as exploration, analysis, presentation, or storytelling.
- However, visualization also has some limitations that need to be considered and addressed, such as:

  - Data quality and accuracy: Visualization can only reflect the quality and accuracy of the data that is used to create it. If the data is incomplete, inconsistent, erroneous, or biased, the visualization will also be misleading or inaccurate. Therefore, data quality and accuracy are essential for effective visualization.
  - Data complexity and volume: Visualization can help to simplify and summarize complex and large data sets, but it can also introduce challenges such as information overload, clutter, or distortion. Therefore, data complexity and volume need to be managed and reduced by using appropriate techniques, such as filtering, aggregation, sampling, or dimensionality reduction.
  - Data interpretation and communication: Visualization can facilitate data interpretation and communication, but it can also cause confusion, misunderstanding, or misinterpretation if the visualization is not clear, intuitive, or appropriate for the intended audience or purpose. Therefore, data interpretation and communication need to be supported and enhanced by using proper design principles, such as color, shape, size, layout, annotation, or interaction.
  - Data ethics and privacy: Visualization can expose and reveal data or information that may be sensitive, confidential, or personal, which can raise ethical and privacy issues, such as data ownership, consent, security, or anonymization. Therefore, data ethics and privacy need to be respected and protected by following relevant laws, regulations, or guidelines, such as GDPR, HIPAA, or IRB.
