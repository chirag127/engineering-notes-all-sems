# Case studies for the notes of the Unit 4 - Mining Data Streams in the subject of INTRODUCTION TO DATA ANALYTICS AND VISUALIZATION

- Mining data streams is the process of extracting knowledge structures represented in models and patterns from continuous and unbounded streams of information.
- Data streams pose many challenges for data mining, such as high volume, high velocity, concept drift, limited memory, and real-time processing .
- Data stream mining algorithms need to be efficient, scalable, adaptive, and incremental .
- Some applications of data stream mining include network monitoring, web analytics, sensor networks, social media analysis, and fraud detection  .
- Here are some case studies of data stream mining:

  - **A Case Study: Stream Data Mining Classification**
    - This case study presents a stream data mining classification system that uses a sliding window technique to handle concept drift and a decision tree algorithm to build a predictive model from streaming data.
    - The system is applied to a real-world problem of predicting the quality of service (QoS) of web services based on their response time and availability.
    - The system is evaluated using synthetic and real datasets and compared with other stream data mining classification methods.
    - The results show that the system can achieve high accuracy and adapt to concept drift effectively.

  - **Improvised methods for tackling big data stream mining challenges: case study of electricity price prediction**
    - This case study proposes a holistic data stream mining approach that integrates different components of data stream mining, such as feature selection, ensemble learning, and concept drift detection, to improve the prediction performance of big data streams.
    - The approach is applied to a real-world problem of forecasting the electricity price in the Australian National Electricity Market (NEM) using streaming data from smart meters and weather stations.
    - The approach is evaluated using real datasets and compared with other data stream mining methods.
    - The results show that the approach can achieve high accuracy and robustness and handle concept drift and noise effectively.

  - **An update on global mining land use**
    - This case study presents a global dataset of mining land use that covers both active and inactive mining sites from 2000 to 2019.
    - The dataset is derived from satellite imagery and validated by expert knowledge and ground truth data.
    - The dataset provides information on the location, extent, type, and status of mining land use, as well as the associated environmental and social impacts.
    - The dataset can be used for various purposes, such as monitoring the trends and patterns of mining land use, assessing the environmental and social impacts of mining, and informing policy and decision making.
