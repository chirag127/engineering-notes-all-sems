# Unit 7 - Human Vision in Data Analytics and Visualization

- Human vision is the process of perceiving and interpreting visual information from the environment.
- Data analytics and visualization are techniques to transform data into meaningful and actionable insights using graphical representations.
- Human vision plays a crucial role in data analytics and visualization, as it enables the detection of patterns, trends, outliers, and relationships in the data.
- Some of the characteristics of human vision that are relevant for data analytics and visualization are:

  - Pre-attentive processing: the ability to quickly and automatically process basic visual features, such as color, shape, size, orientation, and motion, without conscious attention.
  - Gestalt principles: the tendency to perceive visual elements as organized groups or wholes, rather than isolated parts, based on principles such as proximity, similarity, continuity, closure, and symmetry.
  - Visual hierarchy: the order of importance or salience of visual elements, based on factors such as contrast, size, position, alignment, and color.
  - Visual memory: the capacity to store and recall visual information, which can be influenced by the familiarity, complexity, and distinctiveness of the stimuli.

- Some of the challenges and opportunities of human vision for data analytics and visualization are:

  - Data complexity: as the volume, variety, and velocity of data increase, human vision may face limitations in processing and comprehending the data, requiring effective methods of data reduction, abstraction, and aggregation.
  - Data uncertainty: as the data may contain errors, noise, gaps, or ambiguity, human vision may be prone to biases, illusions, or misinterpretations, requiring appropriate methods of data quality assessment, uncertainty visualization, and user feedback.
  - Data exploration: as the data may reveal unexpected or hidden insights, human vision may benefit from interactive and dynamic visualization techniques, such as filtering, zooming, panning, brushing, linking, and animation, that allow the user to manipulate and query the data.
  - Data communication: as the data may need to be communicated to different audiences and contexts, human vision may require different visualization styles, formats, and narratives, that suit the purpose, message, and medium of the communication.
