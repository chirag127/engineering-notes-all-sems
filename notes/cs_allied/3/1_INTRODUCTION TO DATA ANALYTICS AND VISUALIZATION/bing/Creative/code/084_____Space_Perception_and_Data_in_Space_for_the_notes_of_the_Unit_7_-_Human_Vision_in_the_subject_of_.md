### Space Perception and Data in Space

- Space perception is the process through which humans and other organisms become aware of the relative positions of their own bodies and objects around them.
- Space perception provides cues, such as depth and distance, that are important for movement and orientation to the environment.
- Space perception is influenced by various factors, such as visual, auditory, haptic, vestibular, and proprioceptive information, as well as prior knowledge, expectations, and attention.
- Space perception can be divided into three main scales: near, middle, and far range.
  - Near range is the space that can be reached by the body, and is mostly perceived through haptic feedback and binocular vision.
  - Middle range is the space that can be walked to within a few minutes, and is mostly perceived through monocular vision and motion parallax.
  - Far range is the space that extends beyond the horizon, and is mostly perceived through aerial perspective and landmarks.
- Space perception can be affected by the absence of gravity, as experienced by astronauts in space.
  - Gravity provides a constant reference for the orientation of the body and the environment, and helps to stabilize the vestibular system.
  - In microgravity, the vestibular system can be disrupted, leading to symptoms such as nausea, dizziness, and spatial disorientation.
  - Astronauts also have to adapt to the changes in their visual perception of depth, distance, and size, as well as their proprioception of their limbs and posture.
  - NASA conducts research on how human senses adapt to space, and how to improve the design of spacecraft and habitats to enhance the comfort and performance of the crew.
- Data in space refers to the information collected by satellites, probes, rovers, and other instruments that explore the outer space.
  - Data in space can include images, videos, sounds, measurements, and observations of various celestial bodies, phenomena, and events.
  - Data in space can help to advance the scientific knowledge of the universe, the solar system, the Earth, and the life forms that inhabit it.
  - Data in space can also help to monitor the changes in the environment, the weather, the climate, and the natural disasters that affect the planet.
  - Data in space can also support the navigation, communication, and security of various human activities on Earth and in orbit.
  - NASA provides open access to its data in space, and encourages the public to explore, analyze, and visualize it using various tools and platforms.
