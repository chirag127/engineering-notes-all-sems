### Navigation Links

- Navigation links are interactive elements that allow users to move between different pages or sections of a website or an application.
- Navigation links can be placed in different locations, such as the header, the footer, the sidebar, or the main content area.
- Navigation links can have different styles, such as text, icons, buttons, tabs, or menus.
- Navigation links should be clear, consistent, and accessible to users, following the principles of user interface design and web accessibility.
- Navigation links should also be responsive, meaning that they adapt to different screen sizes and devices.

Some examples of navigation links are:

- A horizontal menu bar at the top of a website, with text links to the main sections of the site.
- A vertical sidebar on the left side of a website, with icons and text links to the sub-sections of the site.
- A breadcrumb trail at the top of a web page, with text links to the previous pages in the hierarchy of the site.
- A pagination bar at the bottom of a web page, with buttons or numbers to navigate to the next or previous pages of a list or a table.
- A drop-down menu at the top right corner of a website, with text links to the user profile, settings, or logout options.
