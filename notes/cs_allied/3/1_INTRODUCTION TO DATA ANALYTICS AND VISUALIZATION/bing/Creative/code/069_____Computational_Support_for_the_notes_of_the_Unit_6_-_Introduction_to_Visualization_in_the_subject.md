### Computational Support for Visualization

- Visualization is the process of creating and manipulating graphical representations of data to enhance human understanding and communication .
- Visualization can be classified into two main types: data visualization and computational visualization.
- Data visualization deals with abstract data, such as numbers, text, or categories, that do not have a natural spatial mapping. Examples of data visualization are charts, graphs, infographics, and dashboards.
- Computational visualization deals with data that have a natural spatial mapping, such as images, videos, or simulations. Examples of computational visualization are volume rendering, surface rendering, and vector field visualization.
- Visualization requires computational support to handle the large and complex data sets, the interactive and dynamic nature of the visualizations, and the diverse and specific needs of the users.
- Computational support for visualization can be provided by various software tools, libraries, and frameworks that enable the creation, manipulation, and analysis of visual representations of data.
- Some examples of computational support for visualization are:
  - Visualization libraries, such as Matplotlib, Plotly, and D3.js, that provide basic functionality and components for creating and customizing visualizations.
  - Visualization applications, such as Tableau, Power BI, and Paraview, that provide user-friendly interfaces and features for creating and exploring visualizations.
  - Visualization platforms, such as RStudio, Jupyter, and Google Colab, that provide integrated environments for data analysis and visualization.
  - Visualization systems, such as VisIt, ParaView, and VTK, that provide scalable and parallel solutions for large-scale and high-performance visualization.
- Computational support for visualization can also be provided by hardware devices, such as processors, graphics cards, and displays, that enable the efficient and effective rendering and display of visualizations.
- Some examples of hardware devices that support visualization are:
  - Processors, such as CPUs and GPUs, that perform the computations and operations required for generating and manipulating visualizations.
  - Graphics cards, such as NVIDIA and AMD, that provide specialized hardware and software for accelerating the rendering and display of graphics and visualizations.
  - Displays, such as monitors, projectors, and VR headsets, that provide the output devices for presenting and interacting with visualizations.
