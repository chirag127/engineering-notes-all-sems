### Different Types of Tasks for the notes of the Unit 6 - Introduction to Visualization in the subject of INTRODUCTION TO DATA ANALYTICS AND VISUALIZATION

Data visualization is the process of transforming data into graphical or visual representations, such as charts, graphs, maps, or diagrams. Data visualization can help to communicate data insights, reveal patterns and trends, and support decision-making. Data visualization can also be used for exploratory, descriptive, or explanatory purposes, depending on the goals and audience of the analysis.

There are different types of tasks that can be performed with data visualization, depending on the data sources, the data types, the data dimensions, and the data analysis techniques. Some of the common types of tasks are:

- **Data collection and cleaning**: This task involves gathering data from various sources, such as databases, files, web pages, APIs, or sensors, and preparing it for analysis. Data cleaning involves removing errors, duplicates, outliers, or missing values, and ensuring data quality and consistency. Data collection and cleaning can be done using tools such as Excel, Python, R, SQL, or Power BI.
- **Data exploration and discovery**: This task involves examining the data to understand its characteristics, such as distribution, range, correlation, or outliers. Data exploration and discovery can help to generate hypotheses, identify patterns, or find anomalies. Data exploration and discovery can be done using tools such as Tableau, Qlik, or D3.js .
- **Data analysis and modeling**: This task involves applying statistical or mathematical methods to the data to test hypotheses, infer relationships, or make predictions. Data analysis and modeling can help to answer questions, solve problems, or support decisions. Data analysis and modeling can be done using tools such as R, Python, MATLAB, or SPSS .
- **Data presentation and communication**: This task involves choosing the appropriate data visualization techniques and formats to convey the data insights, stories, or recommendations to the intended audience. Data presentation and communication can help to inform, persuade, or educate the audience. Data presentation and communication can be done using tools such as PowerPoint, Tableau, Qlik, or D3.js .

Some of the common data visualization techniques that can be used for different types of tasks are:

- **Pie chart**: A pie chart is a circular chart that shows the proportion of each category or group in a whole. Pie charts are useful for showing relative sizes, percentages, or fractions of a total. Pie charts are often used for descriptive or explanatory purposes, such as showing market share, customer segments, or budget allocation.
- **Bar chart**: A bar chart is a chart that shows the value of each category or group using horizontal or vertical bars. Bar charts are useful for comparing values, quantities, or frequencies across categories or groups. Bar charts are often used for descriptive or explanatory purposes, such as showing sales, revenue, or population by region, product, or year.
- **Histogram**: A histogram is a chart that shows the frequency or density of values in a continuous variable using bars. Histograms are useful for showing the distribution, shape, or spread of a variable. Histograms are often used for exploratory or descriptive purposes, such as showing the age, income, or height of a population.
- **Gantt chart**: A Gantt chart is a chart that shows the schedule, progress, or status of tasks or activities over time using horizontal bars. Gantt charts are useful for showing the duration, sequence, or dependencies of tasks or activities. Gantt charts are often used for descriptive or explanatory purposes, such as showing the timeline, milestones, or deliverables of a project.
- **Heat map**: A heat map is a chart that shows the intensity or magnitude of values in a matrix or grid using colors. Heat maps are useful for showing the variation, correlation, or clustering of values in a matrix or grid. Heat maps are often used for exploratory or descriptive purposes, such as showing the temperature, traffic, or sentiment by location, time, or category.
