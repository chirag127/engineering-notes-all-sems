### Various phases of data analytics lifecycle

The data analytics lifecycle is a framework that guides the process of transforming data into insights and actions that can help achieve business or project goals. It consists of six phases that are based on the CRISP-DM methodology, which is an open-standard analytics model developed by IBM . The six phases are:

- **Business understanding**: This phase involves defining the problem or opportunity, identifying the objectives and success criteria, and understanding the context and constraints of the situation . This phase helps to align the data analytics project with the business needs and expectations.
- **Data understanding**: This phase involves collecting, exploring, and describing the data that is relevant to the problem or opportunity . This phase helps to assess the quality, availability, and suitability of the data, as well as to identify any potential issues or gaps in the data.
- **Data preparation**: This phase involves cleaning, transforming, and integrating the data into a format that can be used for analysis . This phase helps to improve the accuracy, completeness, and consistency of the data, as well as to reduce the noise and outliers in the data.
- **Modeling**: This phase involves applying various analytical techniques and algorithms to the data to discover patterns, relationships, and insights . This phase helps to test different hypotheses, generate predictions, and evaluate the performance and validity of the models.
- **Evaluation**: This phase involves assessing the results and outcomes of the analysis, and comparing them with the objectives and success criteria . This phase helps to determine the value and relevance of the insights, as well as to identify any limitations or risks of the models.
- **Deployment**: This phase involves communicating and presenting the insights and recommendations to the stakeholders, and implementing the actions or solutions that are derived from the analysis . This phase helps to ensure that the insights and actions are understood, accepted, and applied in the real-world context.

The data analytics lifecycle is not a linear or rigid process, but rather an iterative and flexible one that can be adapted to different situations and needs . The phases can be repeated, modified, or skipped depending on the nature and scope of the problem or opportunity, the availability and quality of the data, and the feedback and requirements of the stakeholders . The data analytics lifecycle is a useful tool that can help data analysts and data scientists to plan, execute, and evaluate their data analytics projects in a systematic and effective way .
