### Time Limitations for the notes of the Unit 7 - Human Vision in the subject of INTRODUCTION TO DATA ANALYTICS AND VISUALIZATION

- Human vision is the process of perceiving and interpreting visual information from the environment.
- Human vision has some limitations that affect how we perceive and understand data and visualizations.
- Some of the time limitations of human vision are:

  - **Persistence of vision**: The ability of the eye to retain an image for a fraction of a second after the stimulus is removed. This allows us to perceive motion and smooth transitions in animations and videos, but also creates some optical illusions and afterimages.
  - **Saccadic suppression**: The suppression of visual information during rapid eye movements (saccades). This prevents us from perceiving the blur and motion of the eye, but also reduces our visual acuity and awareness of the surroundings during saccades.
  - **Change blindness**: The failure to notice changes in visual stimuli when they occur during a saccade, a blink, or a brief interruption. This limits our ability to detect and remember changes in data and visualizations, especially when they are subtle or complex.
  - **Attentional blink**: The reduced ability to perceive a second target stimulus when it follows a first target stimulus within a short time interval (about 200-500 ms). This affects our ability to process multiple events or data points in rapid succession, such as in dynamic or interactive visualizations.
  - **Visual crowding**: The impairment of object recognition when the object is surrounded by other objects in the peripheral vision. This reduces our visual acuity and resolution in the periphery, and limits our ability to perceive details and patterns in dense or cluttered visualizations.
