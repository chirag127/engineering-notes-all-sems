### Learning and Generalisation for the Notes of the Unit 3 - Data Analysis in the Subject of Introduction to Data Analytics and Visualization

- Learning is the process of finding patterns or relationships in data that can be used to make predictions or classifications.
- Generalisation is the ability of a learning model to perform well on new data that was not used in the training process.
- Learning and generalisation are closely related, as the goal of learning is to find a model that can generalise well to unseen data and avoid overfitting or underfitting.
- Overfitting is when a model learns too much from the training data and fails to generalise to new data, resulting in high variance and low bias.
- Underfitting is when a model learns too little from the training data and fails to capture the underlying patterns or relationships, resulting in low variance and high bias.
- A good learning model should balance the trade-off between variance and bias, and achieve a low error rate on both the training and the testing data.
- To evaluate the generalisation performance of a learning model, various methods can be used, such as cross-validation, holdout sets, bootstrapping, or Bayesian inference .
- To improve the generalisation performance of a learning model, various techniques can be applied, such as regularisation, feature selection, dimensionality reduction, ensemble methods, or transfer learning.
