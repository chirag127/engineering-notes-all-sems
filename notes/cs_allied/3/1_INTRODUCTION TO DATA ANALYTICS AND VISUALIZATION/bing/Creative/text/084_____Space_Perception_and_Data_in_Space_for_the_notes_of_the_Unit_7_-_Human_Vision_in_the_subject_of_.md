### Space Perception and Data in Space

- Space perception is the process through which humans and other organisms become aware of the relative positions of their own bodies and objects around them.
- Space perception provides cues, such as depth and distance, that are important for movement and orientation to the environment.
- Space perception is influenced by various factors, such as visual, auditory, haptic, vestibular, and proprioceptive information, as well as prior knowledge, expectations, and attention.
- Space perception can be studied in different scales, such as near, middle, and far range, which correspond to different modes of interaction and engagement with the environment.
- Space perception can also be affected by the absence or alteration of gravity, such as in space flight, which can cause changes in the perception of motion, orientation, and distance.
- Data in space refers to the collection, analysis, and visualization of information related to space exploration, such as astronomy, astrophysics, planetary science, and astronautics.
- Data in space can be displayed in immersive interfaces, such as virtual reality, augmented reality, or mixed reality, which can create a sense of presence and immersion in the data.
- Data in space can also be displayed in non-immersive interfaces, such as desktops, laptops, or mobile devices, which can provide more flexibility and accessibility to the data.
- Data in space can be used for various purposes, such as scientific discovery, education, entertainment, and public outreach.
- Data in space can also pose various challenges, such as data quality, data volume, data complexity, data integration, data interpretation, and data communication.
