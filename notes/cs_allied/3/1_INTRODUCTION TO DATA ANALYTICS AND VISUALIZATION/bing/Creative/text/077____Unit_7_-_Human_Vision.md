## Unit 7 - Human Vision

- Human vision is the ability to perceive and interpret visual information from the environment.
- Human vision involves the following components:
  - The eye: the organ that captures light and converts it into electrical signals.
  - The optic nerve: the bundle of nerve fibers that carries the signals from the eye to the brain.
  - The visual cortex: the part of the brain that processes the signals and forms the visual perception.
- Human vision has the following characteristics:
  - Acuity: the sharpness or clarity of vision, measured by the smallest detail that can be resolved.
  - Field of view: the extent of the visible area, measured by the angle subtended by the eye.
  - Depth perception: the ability to judge the relative distance and position of objects, based on binocular and monocular cues.
  - Color vision: the ability to distinguish different wavelengths of light, based on the activation of three types of cone cells in the retina.
  - Motion perception: the ability to detect and track the movement of objects, based on the changes in the retinal image and the vestibular system.
  - Face recognition: the ability to identify and remember human faces, based on the configuration of facial features and the expression.
