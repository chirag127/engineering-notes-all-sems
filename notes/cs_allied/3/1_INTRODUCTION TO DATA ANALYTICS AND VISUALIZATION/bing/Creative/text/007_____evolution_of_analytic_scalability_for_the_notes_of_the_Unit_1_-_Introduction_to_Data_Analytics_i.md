### Evolution of Analytic Scalability

- Analytic scalability refers to the ability to handle large and complex data sets for analysis purposes.
- In the past, analytic scalability was limited by the separation of the data environment and the analytic environment, which required data to be extracted, transformed, and loaded (ETL) into a separate analytic platform.
- This process was time-consuming, costly, and prone to errors, and often resulted in data quality and consistency issues.
- As the volume, variety, and velocity of data increased, the traditional ETL approach became inadequate and inefficient for big data analytics.
- To overcome these challenges, several new paradigms emerged that enabled greater analytic scalability, such as:
  - The convergence of the data and analytic environments, which eliminated the need for ETL and allowed analytics to be performed directly on the data sources  .
  - Massively parallel processing (MPP) architectures, which distributed the data and computation across multiple nodes and cores, and leveraged parallel algorithms and techniques to speed up the analysis  .
  - The cloud, which provided on-demand, scalable, and elastic computing and storage resources, and enabled analytics to be performed as a service  .
  - Grid computing, which connected multiple computers and devices over a network, and allowed them to share data and processing power for large-scale analytics  .
  - MapReduce, which was a programming model and framework for processing and analyzing large data sets in a distributed and parallel manner, using two functions: map and reduce  .
- These paradigms have enabled analytics to scale up to handle big data, and have also opened up new possibilities and opportunities for data analysis and discovery  .
