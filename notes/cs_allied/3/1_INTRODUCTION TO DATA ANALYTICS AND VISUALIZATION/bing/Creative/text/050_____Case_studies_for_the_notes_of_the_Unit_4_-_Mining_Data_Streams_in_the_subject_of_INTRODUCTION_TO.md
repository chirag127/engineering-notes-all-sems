### Case studies for the notes of the Unit 4 - Mining Data Streams in the subject of INTRODUCTION TO DATA ANALYTICS AND VISUALIZATION

- Mining data streams is the process of extracting knowledge structures represented in models and patterns from continuous and unbounded streams of information.
- Data stream mining has many applications and challenges in domains such as sensor networks, web mining, social media, network monitoring, fraud detection, etc .
- Data stream mining algorithms need to cope with data sizes many times greater than memory, and provide real-time results with limited resources.
- Some of the key characteristics of data streams are:
  - High volume and velocity: Data streams are generated at a very fast rate and in large quantities, which makes it impossible to store or process them using traditional methods.
  - Dynamic and evolving: Data streams are subject to changes over time, which may affect the underlying distribution, concept, or structure of the data. This requires adaptive and incremental learning methods that can handle concept drift and novelty detection.
  - Uncertain and noisy: Data streams may contain missing, incomplete, or erroneous data, which may affect the quality and reliability of the results. This requires robust and resilient methods that can handle uncertainty and noise.
  - Infinite and unbounded: Data streams have no predefined end or boundary, which makes it difficult to define a global view or summary of the data. This requires online and anytime methods that can provide approximate and incremental results.
- Some of the common tasks and techniques for data stream mining are :
  - Classification: The task of assigning a label to an incoming data instance based on a predefined set of classes. Some of the techniques for data stream classification are decision trees, naive Bayes, k-nearest neighbors, ensemble methods, etc.
  - Clustering: The task of grouping similar data instances into clusters based on some similarity or distance measure. Some of the techniques for data stream clustering are k-means, density-based methods, grid-based methods, micro-clustering, etc.
  - Frequent pattern mining: The task of finding frequent or recurring patterns or items in the data stream. Some of the techniques for data stream frequent pattern mining are sliding window, landmark window, tilted time window, lossy counting, etc.
  - Outlier detection: The task of identifying data instances that deviate significantly from the normal or expected behavior of the data stream. Some of the techniques for data stream outlier detection are distance-based methods, density-based methods, clustering-based methods, etc.
- Some of the case studies for data stream mining are  :
  - A Case Study: Stream Data Mining Classification: This case study presents a comparison of different data stream classification algorithms on a real-world dataset of network intrusion detection. The dataset contains 41 features and 5 classes, and is generated by simulating a typical US Air Force LAN. The results show that the Hoeffding tree algorithm performs better than other algorithms in terms of accuracy, memory usage, and processing time.
  - Improvised methods for tackling big data stream mining challenges: case study of online shopper’s purchasing intention prediction: This case study proposes a holistic data stream mining approach that combines different auxiliary parts of data stream mining, such as feature selection, concept drift detection, and ensemble learning, to improve the prediction performance of online shopper’s purchasing intention. The approach is tested on a real-world dataset of online shopping behavior, which contains 18 features and 2 classes. The results show that the proposed approach outperforms the baseline methods in terms of accuracy, kappa statistic, and F-measure.
  - An update on global mining land use: This case study presents a global dataset of mining land use, which covers both active and inactive mining sites across 171 countries. The dataset is derived from satellite imagery and other sources, and contains information on the location, area, type, and status of the mining sites. The dataset can be used for various applications, such as environmental impact assessment, land use planning, and resource management.
