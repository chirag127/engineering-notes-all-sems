### Neural Networks

Neural networks are a subset of machine learning and are at the heart of deep learning algorithms. They are composed of artificial neurons or nodes that mimic the way that biological neurons signal to one another. Neural networks can learn from data and perform tasks such as function approximation, classification, pattern recognition, novelty detection, and sequential decision making.

Some key points about neural networks are:

- A neural network consists of an input layer, one or more hidden layers, and an output layer. Each layer contains a number of neurons that are connected by weighted links. The input layer receives the data, the hidden layers process the data, and the output layer produces the result.
- A neural network learns by adjusting the weights of the links based on the error between the actual output and the desired output. This process is called training or learning. There are different methods for training a neural network, such as backpropagation, gradient descent, or genetic algorithms.
- A neural network can have different architectures, such as feedforward, recurrent, convolutional, or generative. Each architecture has its own advantages and disadvantages depending on the type and complexity of the problem.
- A neural network can have different activation functions, such as sigmoid, tanh, relu, or softmax. The activation function determines how the output of a neuron is calculated from its input. Different activation functions have different properties and effects on the learning process.
- A neural network can have different types of layers, such as dense, dropout, batch normalization, or attention. Each type of layer has a specific function and role in the network. For example, dropout layers reduce overfitting, batch normalization layers improve convergence, and attention layers enhance the representation of the input.
