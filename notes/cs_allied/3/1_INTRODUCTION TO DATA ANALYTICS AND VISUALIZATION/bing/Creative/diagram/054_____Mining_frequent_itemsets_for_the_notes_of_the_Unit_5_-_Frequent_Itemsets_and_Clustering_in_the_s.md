### Mining frequent itemsets

- Frequent itemset mining (pattern mining) is a technique used to derive relationships such as regularities in customers’ shopping behavior in physical and online stores to discover association rules out of the relationships.
- Association rules are statements of the form {X} -> {Y}, where X and Y are sets of items, that indicate that if a transaction contains X, it is likely to contain Y as well. For example, {bread, butter} -> {jam} is an association rule that means that customers who buy bread and butter are likely to buy jam as well.
- Frequent itemsets are sets of items that appear together in a dataset with a frequency above a given threshold (called minimum support). For example, {bread, butter, jam} is a frequent itemset if it occurs in more than 10% of the transactions in a dataset.
- Mining frequent itemsets is the process of finding all the frequent itemsets in a dataset. This is usually the first step to generate association rules, which can then be evaluated by measures such as confidence, lift, or conviction.
- There are several algorithms for mining frequent itemsets, such as Apriori, Eclat, and FP-growth. These algorithms differ in how they search the space of possible itemsets and how they prune the candidates that are unlikely to be frequent.
- Apriori is the most commonly used algorithm for mining frequent itemsets. It works by generating candidate itemsets of increasing size (from 1 to n, where n is the number of items in the dataset) and scanning the dataset to count their frequencies. It uses the property that any subset of a frequent itemset must be frequent as well (called the Apriori property) to prune the candidates that have infrequent subsets.
- Eclat is a variant of Apriori that uses a depth-first search strategy and a vertical representation of the dataset (where each item is associated with a set of transaction identifiers that contain it) to avoid repeated scans of the dataset and generate fewer candidates.
- FP-growth is another algorithm for mining frequent itemsets that uses a compressed representation of the dataset called an FP-tree (frequent pattern tree) to avoid generating candidates and scanning the dataset. It works by dividing the dataset into smaller projected datasets based on the frequent items and recursively mining each projected dataset.
