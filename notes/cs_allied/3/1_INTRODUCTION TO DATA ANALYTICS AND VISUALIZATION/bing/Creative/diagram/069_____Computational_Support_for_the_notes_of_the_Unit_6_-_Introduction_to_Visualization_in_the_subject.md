### Computational Support for Visualization

- Visualization is the process of creating and manipulating graphical representations of data to enhance human understanding and communication .
- Visualization can be classified into two main types: data visualization and computational visualization.
- Data visualization focuses on presenting abstract data, such as numbers, text, or symbols, using common graphics, such as charts, plots, infographics, and animations .
- Computational visualization focuses on presenting spatial data, such as images, volumes, surfaces, or meshes, using advanced graphics, such as 3D models, textures, lighting, and shading.
- Visualization requires computational support at different levels, such as:
  - Data acquisition and processing: collecting, cleaning, transforming, and analyzing data from various sources, such as sensors, databases, files, or web services.
  - Data mapping and encoding: choosing appropriate visual elements, such as shapes, colors, sizes, or positions, to represent data attributes, such as values, categories, or relationships.
  - Data rendering and interaction: generating, displaying, and manipulating graphical output, such as pixels, vectors, or polygons, using hardware and software, such as graphics cards, monitors, or libraries.
- Visualization can benefit from various computational techniques and tools, such as:
  - Parallel and distributed computing: using multiple processors or machines to speed up data processing and rendering, such as in high-performance computing (HPC) or cloud computing environments.
  - Virtualization and emulation: using software to create and run virtual machines or devices that can simulate different hardware or software configurations, such as in virtual reality (VR) or augmented reality (AR) applications.
  - Machine learning and artificial intelligence: using algorithms and models to learn from data and perform tasks, such as classification, clustering, regression, or prediction, that can enhance data analysis and visualization .
