### Linear Systems Analysis & Nonlinear Dynamics

- Linear systems are systems whose behavior can be described by linear equations or matrices. They have the properties of superposition, homogeneity, and proportionality. They can be solved exactly using methods such as eigenvalue decomposition, matrix inversion, or Laplace transform.
- Nonlinear systems are systems whose behavior cannot be described by linear equations or matrices. They have the properties of nonlinearity, complexity, and sensitivity to initial conditions. They can exhibit chaotic, unpredictable, or counterintuitive behavior. They can be solved approximately using methods such as numerical integration, perturbation theory, or bifurcation analysis.
- Linear stability analysis is a technique to determine the stability of an equilibrium point of a nonlinear system by linearizing the system around the equilibrium point and analyzing the eigenvalues of the Jacobian matrix. The sign of the real part of the dominant eigenvalue determines the stability of the equilibrium point.
- Nonlinear dynamic analysis is a technique to determine the response of a system to dynamic loads or excitations by accounting for the nonlinear behavior of the system components. It can capture the inelastic deformations and strength demand/capacity ratios of the system components. It can also reveal the nonlinear modes and frequencies of the system.
