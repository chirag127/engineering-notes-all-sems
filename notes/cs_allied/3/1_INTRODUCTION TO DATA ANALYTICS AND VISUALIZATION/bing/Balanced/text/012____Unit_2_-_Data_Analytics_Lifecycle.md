## Unit 2 - Data Analytics Lifecycle

- The data analytics lifecycle is a framework for planning and executing data analysis projects, from defining the business problem to deploying the solution.
- The lifecycle consists of six phases: discovery, data preparation, model planning, model building, communicating results, and operationalization.
- Each phase has a specific purpose, inputs, outputs, and tasks to be performed.

### Discovery
- The discovery phase is where the business problem or opportunity is identified and defined, and the objectives and requirements of the data analysis project are established.
- The inputs for this phase are the business context, the stakeholders, and the available data sources.
- The outputs for this phase are the problem statement, the project scope, the project plan, and the initial hypotheses.
- The tasks for this phase include:
  - Understanding the business context and the stakeholders' needs and expectations
  - Identifying and prioritizing the business questions to be answered by the data analysis
  - Assessing the available data sources and their quality, relevance, and accessibility
  - Defining the problem statement and the project scope, including the deliverables, timeline, and resources
  - Developing the project plan, outlining the tasks, milestones, and dependencies for each phase of the lifecycle
  - Formulating the initial hypotheses or assumptions to be tested by the data analysis

### Data Preparation
- The data preparation phase is where the data is collected, cleaned, integrated, and transformed into a suitable format for analysis.
- The inputs for this phase are the data sources identified in the discovery phase and the data quality requirements.
- The outputs for this phase are the prepared data sets and the data dictionary.
- The tasks for this phase include:
  - Collecting the data from the data sources, using appropriate methods and tools
  - Performing data quality checks and resolving any issues or anomalies
  - Integrating and consolidating the data from different sources, ensuring consistency and alignment
  - Transforming the data into a suitable format for analysis, such as creating new variables, aggregating, or normalizing the data
  - Documenting the data sets and their attributes, such as the data source, the data type, the data range, and the data description

### Model Planning
- The model planning phase is where the analytical techniques and methods are selected and applied to the prepared data sets, in order to test the hypotheses and answer the business questions.
- The inputs for this phase are the prepared data sets, the data dictionary, the hypotheses, and the business questions.
- The outputs for this phase are the analytical models and the model results.
- The tasks for this phase include:
  - Exploring the data sets, using descriptive statistics and visualization techniques, to gain insights and identify patterns and trends
  - Selecting the appropriate analytical techniques and methods, such as regression, classification, clustering, or association, based on the data type, the data distribution, and the business objectives
  - Applying the selected techniques and methods to the data sets, using suitable tools and software
  - Evaluating the analytical models and their results, using various criteria and metrics, such as accuracy, precision, recall, or lift
  - Refining and optimizing the analytical models and their parameters, based on the evaluation results and feedback

### Model Building
- The model building phase is where the analytical models are validated and verified, using different data sets and scenarios, to ensure their robustness and reliability.
- The inputs for this phase are the analytical models, the model results, and the validation data sets.
- The outputs for this phase are the final models and the model documentation.
- The tasks for this phase include:
  - Splitting the data sets into training, validation, and testing subsets, using appropriate methods and ratios
  - Training the analytical models on the training data sets, using the selected techniques and methods
  - Validating the analytical models on the validation data sets, using the same techniques and methods, and comparing the results with the training results
  - Testing the analytical models on the testing data sets, using the same techniques and methods, and comparing the results with the training and validation results
  - Verifying the analytical models on different data sets and scenarios, using the same or different techniques and methods, and assessing the model performance and generalization
  - Documenting the final models and their specifications, such as the model name, the model type, the model inputs, the model outputs, and the model assumptions
