### Evolution of Analytic Scalability

- Analytic scalability refers to the ability to handle large and complex data sets for analysis purposes.
- In the past, analytic scalability was limited by the separation of the data environment and the analytic environment, which required data to be extracted, transformed, and loaded (ETL) into a separate analytic platform.
- This process was time-consuming, costly, and prone to errors, and often resulted in data quality and consistency issues.
- As the volume, variety, and velocity of data increased, the traditional ETL approach became inadequate and inefficient for analytic scalability.
- To overcome these challenges, several new paradigms emerged that enabled the convergence of the data and analytic environments, such as:
  - Massively parallel processing (MPP) architectures, which distribute data and computation across multiple nodes or servers, allowing for faster and more scalable processing of large data sets.
  - Cloud computing, which provides on-demand access to scalable and elastic computing resources over the internet, reducing the need for upfront investment and maintenance of hardware and software.
  - Grid computing, which leverages the idle or underutilized resources of a network of computers, creating a virtual supercomputer that can perform complex and parallel tasks.
  - MapReduce, which is a programming model and framework that simplifies the processing of large and distributed data sets by breaking them into smaller and independent chunks (map) and aggregating the results (reduce).
- These paradigms have enabled new levels of analytic scalability, allowing for more efficient and effective analysis of big data.
