### Space Limitation

- Space limitation refers to the constraint of the available space on a data visualization, such as a chart, a dashboard, or a report.
- Space limitation affects how much data and information can be displayed, how clearly and accurately they can be perceived, and how easily and quickly they can be understood by the human vision.
- Some of the factors that contribute to space limitation are:
  - The size and resolution of the display device, such as a monitor, a tablet, or a smartphone.
  - The number and complexity of the data variables, dimensions, and measures that need to be visualized.
  - The type and style of the data visualization, such as a bar chart, a pie chart, a scatter plot, or a map.
  - The design and layout of the data visualization, such as the use of colors, fonts, labels, legends, axes, grids, and annotations.
  - The purpose and audience of the data visualization, such as the level of detail, interactivity, and customization required.
- Some of the challenges and trade-offs that arise from space limitation are:
  - Reducing the amount of data and information to fit the available space, which may lead to loss of detail, context, or insight.
  - Increasing the amount of data and information to fill the available space, which may lead to clutter, confusion, or distraction.
  - Choosing the appropriate type and style of data visualization to match the data and the message, which may involve balancing simplicity, clarity, and aesthetics.
  - Optimizing the design and layout of the data visualization to enhance the visual perception and cognition, which may involve applying principles of visual hierarchy, contrast, alignment, and proximity.
  - Adapting the data visualization to different display devices and audiences, which may involve using responsive, adaptive, or progressive design techniques.
