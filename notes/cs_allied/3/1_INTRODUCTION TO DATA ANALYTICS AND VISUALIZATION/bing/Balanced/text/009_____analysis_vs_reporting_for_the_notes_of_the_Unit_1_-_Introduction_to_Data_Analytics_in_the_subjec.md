### Analysis vs Reporting

- Reporting and analysis are two different processes that involve data and information, but have different purposes, tasks, outputs, and skills.
- Reporting is the process of organizing data into informational summaries in order to monitor how different areas of a business are performing.
- Analysis is the process of exploring data and reports in order to extract meaningful insights, which can be used to better understand and improve business performance.
- Some of the main differences between reporting and analysis are:

  - Reporting follows a push approach, where reports are pushed to users who are then expected to extract meaningful insights and take appropriate actions for themselves (i.e., self-serve). Analysis follows a pull approach, where analysts pull data and information from various sources and provide recommendations to users based on their findings.
  - Reporting translates raw data into information. Analysis transforms data and information into insights.
  - Reporting is more about organizing and summarizing data in a digestible manner. Analysis is more about reading between the data lines to drive conclusions.
  - Reporting is objective as it involves presenting accurate data without bias or interpretation. Analysis is subjective as it involves applying logic, reasoning, and judgment to the data and information.
  - Reporting requires basic skills such as data collection, data manipulation, data visualization, and data communication. Analysis requires advanced skills such as data mining, data modeling, data interpretation, data storytelling, and data-driven decision making.
  - Reporting is usually done before analysis, as it provides the data and information needed for analysis. Analysis is usually done after reporting, as it provides the insights and recommendations based on the data and information.
  - Reporting is more time-consuming and repetitive, as it involves generating and updating reports on a regular basis. Analysis is more dynamic and creative, as it involves exploring and discovering new patterns and trends in the data.
