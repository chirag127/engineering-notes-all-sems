## Unit 4 - Mining Data Streams

- Data streams are continuous and unbounded sequences of data elements that arrive at high speed and need to be processed in real time.
- Data stream mining is the process of extracting useful information, patterns, and knowledge from data streams using various techniques and algorithms.
- Data stream mining has many applications, such as network monitoring, sensor networks, web analytics, social media analysis, fraud detection, and recommender systems.
- Data stream mining faces many challenges, such as:
  - The data is transient and cannot be stored or revisited.
  - The data is noisy, incomplete, and may contain outliers or concept drifts.
  - The data is heterogeneous and may have different formats, sources, and types.
  - The data is massive and requires scalable and efficient methods to handle it.
- Data stream mining requires different approaches and techniques than traditional data mining, such as:
  - Data summarization and approximation methods, such as sampling, sketching, histograms, and synopsis data structures, to reduce the data size and complexity.
  - Incremental and online learning methods, such as sliding windows, fading factors, and ensemble methods, to update the models and adapt to the data changes.
  - Distributed and parallel processing methods, such as MapReduce, Spark, and Storm, to leverage the computational power and storage of multiple machines.
  - Stream query processing methods, such as continuous queries, operators, and windows, to specify and execute the data analysis tasks on the streams.
