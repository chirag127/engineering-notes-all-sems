### Clustering for Streams and Parallelism

- Clustering is the process of grouping data objects into clusters such that objects within a cluster are similar to each other and dissimilar to objects in other clusters.
- Clustering for streams and parallelism is the problem of clustering parallel streams of real-valued data, that is to say, continuously evolving time series.
- Parallel data streams are data streams that are generated by multiple sources simultaneously, such as sensor networks, web logs, or financial transactions .
- Clustering parallel data streams can be useful for applications such as anomaly detection, trend analysis, load balancing, and data compression .
- Clustering parallel data streams poses significant challenges, such as:
  - The data is unbounded, dynamic, and potentially noisy.
  - The data cannot be stored or revisited, and only one scan is allowed.
  - The number of streams and the number of clusters may change over time.
  - The clusters may evolve, merge, split, appear, or disappear over time.
  - The data may have different sampling rates, delays, or missing values.
- To address these challenges, clustering parallel data streams requires efficient and adaptive algorithms that can:
  - Summarize the data streams using compact and representative data structures, such as sketches, histograms, wavelets, or Fourier transforms .
  - Update the summaries and the clusters incrementally and online as new data arrives .
  - Detect and handle changes in the data streams and the clusters using techniques such as sliding windows, forgetting factors, or change detection tests .
  - Measure the similarity or dissimilarity between data streams and clusters using metrics such as correlation, distance, or divergence .
  - Optimize the clustering quality and the computational efficiency using criteria such as compactness, separation, stability, or scalability .
- Some examples of algorithms for clustering parallel data streams are:
  - The algorithm proposed by Chen et al. , which uses correlation analysis and hierarchical clustering to group multiple data streams with similar behavior and trend together.
  - The algorithm proposed by Beringer and Hullermeier , which uses discrete Fourier transformation and online K-means clustering to cluster parallel data streams based on their frequency components.
  - The algorithm proposed by Aggarwal et al. , which uses a two-phase scheme that consists of an online component that processes data stream points and produces summary statistics, and an offline component that uses the summary data to generate the clusters.
