### Display Space for the notes of the Unit 6 - Introduction to Visualization in the subject of INTRODUCTION TO DATA ANALYTICS AND VISUALIZATION

- Data visualization is the graphical representation of data for understanding and communication.
- Data visualization can be used for two primary purposes: exploration and presentation.
  - Exploration: You are trying to explore and understand patterns and trends within your data. This can help you generate hypotheses, discover insights, and guide your analysis.
  - Presentation: You are trying to communicate your findings, arguments, or recommendations to an audience. This can help you persuade, inform, or educate your audience.
- Data visualization can be classified into two main types: information visualization and scientific visualization.
  - Information visualization: Visualization of abstract data, such as numbers, text, or networks. This can include charts, graphs, maps, diagrams, etc.
  - Scientific visualization: Visualization of physical data, such as images, videos, or simulations. This can include volume rendering, surface rendering, contour plots, etc.
- Data visualization requires three different skills: substantive knowledge, statistical skill, and artistic sense.
  - Substantive knowledge: You need to understand the context and meaning of your data, and the questions you want to answer or the messages you want to convey.
  - Statistical skill: You need to apply appropriate methods and techniques to analyze and summarize your data, and to choose the best way to represent it visually.
  - Artistic sense: You need to design your visualization in a way that is clear, effective, and appealing, and that follows the principles of analytic design.
- Data visualization follows the principles of analytic design, which are:
  - Show the data: The visualization should show the data as accurately and completely as possible, without distorting or hiding it.
  - Simplify the message: The visualization should focus on the main point or message, and avoid unnecessary clutter or noise.
  - Maximize the data-ink ratio: The visualization should use as much of the available space as possible to show the data, and minimize the use of non-data elements, such as borders, labels, or backgrounds.
  - Use color and size effectively: The visualization should use color and size to encode information, such as categories, values, or relationships, and to highlight important features or patterns.
  - Integrate text and graphics: The visualization should use text and graphics together to create a coherent and consistent story, and to provide context and explanation.
  - Balance beauty and function: The visualization should be aesthetically pleasing and engaging, but not at the expense of clarity and accuracy.
