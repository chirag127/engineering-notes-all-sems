# Space Limitation

- Space limitation refers to the constraint of the available space on a display or a page for presenting data and information.
- Space limitation affects the design and effectiveness of data visualization, as it influences the choice of visual elements, the level of detail, the amount of data, and the layout of the visualization.
- Some factors that contribute to space limitation are:
  - The size and resolution of the display or the page
  - The orientation and aspect ratio of the display or the page
  - The number and type of visual elements used in the visualization
  - The amount and complexity of data to be shown
  - The intended audience and purpose of the visualization
- Some strategies to cope with space limitation are:
  - Reducing the size of visual elements or using smaller fonts
  - Simplifying the visual elements or using less colors
  - Filtering or aggregating the data to show only the most relevant or important information
  - Using multiple views or interactive features to allow the user to explore the data in different ways
  - Using visual cues or annotations to highlight the key points or trends in the data
  - Using whitespace or margins to create a balanced and clear layout of the visualization
- Some examples of data visualization techniques that deal with space limitation are:
  - Sparklines: small, simple line charts that show the variation of a variable over time
  - Small multiples: a series of similar charts that show the same data for different categories or groups
  - Treemaps: a hierarchical representation of data using nested rectangles of different sizes and colors
  - Word clouds: a visual representation of text data using words of different sizes and colors
  - Bullet graphs: a variation of bar charts that show the performance of a measure against a target and a range of values
  - Pie charts: a circular representation of data using sectors of different sizes and colors
