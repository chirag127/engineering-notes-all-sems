# Stochastic Search Methods

- Stochastic search methods are optimization techniques that use randomness in some way, either in the objective function or in the search algorithm.
- Stochastic search methods are useful for finding approximate solutions to complex problems that are hard to solve exactly or efficiently by deterministic methods.
- Stochastic search methods can explore a large and diverse search space, escape from local optima, and handle noisy or uncertain data.
- Some examples of stochastic search methods are:

  - Simulated annealing: a method that mimics the physical process of annealing, where a material is heated and then slowly cooled to reach a low-energy state. Simulated annealing starts with a high temperature that allows the search to move freely in the search space, and then gradually lowers the temperature to converge to a near-optimal solution.
  - Genetic algorithms: a method that mimics the biological process of evolution, where a population of candidate solutions is evolved over generations by applying genetic operators such as selection, crossover, and mutation. Genetic algorithms maintain a diversity of solutions and can exploit the best ones to find better ones.
  - Particle swarm optimization: a method that mimics the social behavior of a swarm of birds or fish, where each individual (particle) moves in the search space according to its own velocity and the influence of its neighbors. Particle swarm optimization can adapt to dynamic environments and find a balance between exploration and exploitation.
  - Ant colony optimization: a method that mimics the foraging behavior of ants, where each ant lays down a pheromone trail that attracts other ants to follow it. Ant colony optimization can find optimal or near-optimal paths in graphs or networks by using positive feedback and distributed computation.
