# Issues for the notes of the Unit 6 - Introduction to Visualization in the subject of INTRODUCTION TO DATA ANALYTICS AND VISUALIZATION

- Data visualization is the process of transforming data into graphical or pictorial representations that can communicate information effectively and efficiently to the audience.
- Data visualization can help to reveal patterns, trends, outliers, and relationships in the data, as well as to convey insights, stories, and arguments based on the data.
- However, data visualization also faces some issues and challenges that can affect its quality, accuracy, and usefulness. Some of these issues are:

  - **Misleading color contrast**: Color is a powerful visual element that can influence the perception and interpretation of the data. However, color can also be used to manipulate or deceive the audience by creating false impressions of the data. For example, using high degrees of color contrast can exaggerate the differences between data points or groups, while using low degrees of color contrast can hide or minimize them .
  - **Correlation is not causation**: Correlation is a statistical measure that indicates how two variables are related to each other. However, correlation does not imply causation, meaning that one variable does not necessarily cause or affect the other. Correlation can be due to chance, confounding factors, or reverse causality. Therefore, data visualization should not present correlation as causation without proper evidence or explanation.
  - **Similarities now do not mean similarities forever**: Data visualization can show the current state or trend of the data, but it cannot predict the future or generalize the past. Data can change over time due to various factors, such as seasonality, cycles, outliers, or external events. Therefore, data visualization should not assume that the data will remain the same or similar in the future or the past without proper justification or context.
  - **Axes make all the difference**: Axes are the horizontal and vertical lines that define the scale and range of the data in a chart or graph. Axes can affect the appearance and interpretation of the data by changing the perspective, proportion, or emphasis of the data. For example, using different scales or units can make the data look larger or smaller, while using different ranges or intervals can make the data look more or less variable .
  - **Abusing the Law of Large Numbers (LLN)**: The LLN is a mathematical theorem that states that as the sample size increases, the sample mean approaches the population mean. However, the LLN does not guarantee that the sample is representative of the population, or that the population is homogeneous or stable. Therefore, data visualization should not rely on the LLN to justify the validity or reliability of the data without proper sampling methods or assumptions.
  - **Bad data visualization format**: Data visualization format refers to the type or style of the chart or graph that is used to display the data. The format should match the nature and purpose of the data, as well as the intended audience and message. However, some data visualization formats can be unsuitable or inappropriate for the data, such as using pie charts for quantitative data, using 3D effects for 2D data, or using misleading icons or symbols for categorical data .
  - **Lack of better data visualization literacy**: Data visualization literacy is the ability to create, read, and interpret data visualizations effectively and critically. Data visualization literacy can help to avoid or detect errors, biases, or manipulations in the data or the visualization. However, data visualization literacy is not widely taught or practiced, and many people lack the skills or knowledge to use or understand data visualization properly .
