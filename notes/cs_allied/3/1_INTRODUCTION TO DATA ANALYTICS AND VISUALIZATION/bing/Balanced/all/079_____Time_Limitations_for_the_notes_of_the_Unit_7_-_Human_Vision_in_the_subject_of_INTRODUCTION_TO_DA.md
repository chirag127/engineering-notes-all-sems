# Time Limitations

- Human vision is limited by the speed of light and the processing speed of the brain.
- The speed of light is about 300,000 km/s, which means that it takes about 0.000003 seconds for light to travel from an object to the eye.
- The processing speed of the brain is about 100 ms, which means that it takes about 0.1 seconds for the brain to perceive and interpret the visual information from the eye.
- Therefore, the total time lag between seeing an object and being aware of it is about 0.100003 seconds, which is negligible for most purposes.
- However, this time lag can affect the perception of fast-moving objects, such as a ball thrown at high speed or a car driving past.
- To compensate for this time lag, the brain uses a mechanism called **predictive coding**, which anticipates the future position and motion of the object based on the past and present information.
- Predictive coding helps the brain to create a smooth and continuous perception of motion, even when the object is moving faster than the eye can track.
- However, predictive coding can also lead to errors and illusions, such as the **flash-lag effect**, the **wagon-wheel effect**, and the **phi phenomenon**.
- The flash-lag effect is when a flash of light appears to lag behind a moving object, even though they are aligned in reality.
- The wagon-wheel effect is when a rotating wheel appears to stop or reverse its direction of rotation, depending on the frequency of the flashes of light or the frame rate of the video.
- The phi phenomenon is when two stationary lights appear to move back and forth when they are flashed alternately at a certain frequency.
