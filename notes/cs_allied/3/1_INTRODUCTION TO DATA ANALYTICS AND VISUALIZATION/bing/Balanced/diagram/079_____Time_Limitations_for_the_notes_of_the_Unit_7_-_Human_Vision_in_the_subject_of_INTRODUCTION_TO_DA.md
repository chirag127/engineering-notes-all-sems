### Time Limitations

- Human vision is not only limited by the spatial resolution and color perception of the eyes, but also by the temporal resolution and motion perception of the brain.
- Temporal resolution refers to the ability to distinguish between two events that occur in a short time interval. For example, if two flashes of light are shown in quick succession, they may appear as one flash if the interval is too short. This is called the **flicker fusion threshold**.
- Motion perception refers to the ability to detect and track the movement of objects in the visual field. For example, if a car is moving across the street, we can perceive its speed and direction. This is called the **optic flow**.
- Both temporal resolution and motion perception depend on several factors, such as the brightness, contrast, size, and distance of the stimuli, as well as the attention and expectation of the observer.
