### Display Space

- The display space is the area where the data is presented visually to the user.
- The display space can be physical (such as a paper, a screen, a wall, etc.) or virtual (such as a web page, a mobile app, a VR headset, etc.).
- The display space has some properties that affect the design and perception of the visualization, such as:
  - Size: the dimensions of the display space in terms of length and width.
  - Resolution: the number of pixels or dots per inch (dpi) that the display space can show.
  - Aspect ratio: the ratio of the width to the height of the display space.
  - Color depth: the number of colors or shades of gray that the display space can show.
  - Brightness: the amount of light emitted by the display space or reflected from it.
  - Contrast: the difference between the brightest and the darkest areas of the display space.
  - Viewing angle: the angle at which the user can see the display space without distortion or loss of quality.
  - Viewing distance: the distance between the user and the display space.
- The display space can also have some constraints or limitations that affect the design and perception of the visualization, such as:
  - Bandwidth: the amount of data that can be transferred or downloaded to the display space per unit of time.
  - Memory: the amount of data that can be stored or cached in the display space or the device that supports it.
  - Processing power: the speed and capacity of the device that supports the display space to perform calculations and operations on the data.
  - Interactivity: the degree and mode of interaction that the user can have with the display space or the visualization.
  - Accessibility: the extent to which the display space or the visualization can be used by people with different abilities, preferences, or needs.
- The display space can also have some affordances or opportunities that affect the design and perception of the visualization, such as:
  - Scalability: the ability of the display space or the visualization to adapt to different sizes, resolutions, or levels of detail.
  - Portability: the ability of the display space or the visualization to be moved or accessed from different locations or devices.
  - Immersiveness: the ability of the display space or the visualization to create a sense of presence or engagement for the user.
  - Novelty: the ability of the display space or the visualization to attract attention or interest from the user.
  - Aesthetics: the ability of the display space or the visualization to appeal to the user's emotions or preferences.
