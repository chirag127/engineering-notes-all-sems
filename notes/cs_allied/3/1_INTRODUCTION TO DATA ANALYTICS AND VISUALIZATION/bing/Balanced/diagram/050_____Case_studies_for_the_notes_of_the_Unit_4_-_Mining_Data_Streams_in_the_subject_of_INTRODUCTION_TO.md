### Case studies for the notes of the Unit 4 - Mining Data Streams in the subject of INTRODUCTION TO DATA ANALYTICS AND VISUALIZATION

- Mining data streams is the process of extracting knowledge structures, such as models and patterns, from continuous and unbounded streams of information .
- Data stream mining poses many challenges, such as high data volume, high data velocity, concept drift, limited memory and processing resources, and evolving data distributions .
- Data stream mining algorithms need to be scalable, adaptive, incremental, and robust to handle these challenges .
- Some examples of case studies for mining data streams are:

  - **A Case Study: Stream Data Mining Classification**: This paper presents a case study of applying stream data mining classification to a real-world problem of predicting the customer churn rate for a telecommunication company. The paper compares different stream data mining algorithms, such as Hoeffding Tree, Naive Bayes, and k-Nearest Neighbors, and evaluates their performance on accuracy, memory usage, and processing time. The paper also discusses the challenges and benefits of stream data mining classification in this context.
  - **Improvised methods for tackling big data stream mining challenges: case study of electricity price prediction**: This paper proposes a holistic data stream mining approach that integrates different components of data stream mining, such as feature selection, ensemble learning, concept drift detection, and model update, to improve the prediction performance of electricity price. The paper applies this approach to a real-world dataset of electricity price from the Australian National Electricity Market and compares it with other existing methods. The paper also analyzes the impact of different factors, such as data preprocessing, feature selection, and ensemble size, on the prediction accuracy and stability.
  - **Data Streams in Data Mining Simplified 101**: This article provides a comprehensive overview of data streams in data mining, including the definition, characteristics, challenges, and applications of data stream mining. The article also introduces some popular data stream mining techniques, such as sliding window, landmark window, reservoir sampling, and sketching, and explains how they work and when to use them. The article also gives some examples of data stream mining applications, such as network monitoring, fraud detection, and recommender systems.
