## Unit 7 - Human Vision

Human vision is the ability to perceive and interpret visual information from the environment. Human vision involves the interaction of the eyes, the brain, and the nervous system. Human vision has the following features and limits:

- **Binocular vision**: Humans have two eyes that are located on the left and right sides of the face. Each eye forms a different image on the retina, which is the light-sensitive layer at the back of the eye. The brain combines these two images to create a single perception of vision that allows humans to see objects in three dimensions and estimate their depth and distance .
- **Eye structure**: The human eye is a complex organ that consists of several parts, such as the cornea, the pupil, the iris, the lens, the vitreous, the retina, the optic nerve, and the extraocular muscles. Each part has a specific function in the process of vision. For example, the cornea is the clear front layer of the eye that bends light to help the eye focus. The pupil is the opening in the center of the iris that controls the amount of light that enters the eye. The lens is the transparent structure behind the pupil that changes shape to focus light onto the retina. The retina is the layer of nerve cells that converts light into electrical signals that are sent to the brain via the optic nerve. The extraocular muscles are the six muscles that control the movements of the eye .
- **Color perception**: Humans can perceive different colors because of the presence of three types of cone cells in the retina. Cone cells are photoreceptors that respond to different wavelengths of light. The three types of cone cells are sensitive to red, green, and blue light, respectively. The brain interprets the signals from these cone cells to create the sensation of color. Humans can see about 10 million colors, but some people have color vision deficiencies that affect their ability to distinguish certain colors.
- **Visual acuity**: Visual acuity is the measure of how well a person can see the details of an object at a certain distance. Visual acuity is usually expressed as a fraction, such as 20/20, which means that a person can see an object that is 20 feet away as clearly as a normal person can. Visual acuity can be affected by various factors, such as the clarity of the eye's optical system, the size and contrast of the object, the lighting conditions, and the presence of refractive errors or eye diseases. Normal visual acuity is about 20/20, but some people have higher or lower visual acuity.
- **Field of view**: Field of view is the extent of the visible area that a person can see at any given moment. Field of view depends on the position and orientation of the eyes, the shape and size of the eye sockets, and the presence of any obstacles or blind spots. The human field of view is about 180 degrees horizontally and 135 degrees vertically, but it varies from person to person. The field of view can be divided into central and peripheral vision. Central vision is the area of the field of view that is directly in front of the eyes and is used for detailed and focused vision. Peripheral vision is the area of the field of view that is around the edges of the central vision and is used for detecting motion and changes in the environment.
