### Learning and Generalisation for the Notes of the Unit 3 - Data Analysis in the Subject of Introduction to Data Analytics and Visualization

- Learning is the process of finding patterns or relationships in data that can be used to make predictions or classifications.
- Generalisation is the ability of a learning model to perform well on new data that was not used in the training process.
- Learning and generalisation are closely related, as the goal of learning is to find a model that can generalise well to unseen data, and not overfit or underfit the training data.
- Overfitting is when a model learns too much from the training data and captures noise or irrelevant details that do not generalise well to new data.
- Underfitting is when a model learns too little from the training data and fails to capture the essential patterns or relationships that are needed for accurate predictions or classifications.
- A good learning model should balance between complexity and simplicity, and avoid overfitting and underfitting.
- Learning analytics is the application of data analysis techniques to understand and improve learning outcomes and environments.
- Learning analytics can be used for various purposes, such as:
  - Providing feedback and guidance to learners and instructors.
  - Identifying at-risk learners and providing interventions.
  - Evaluating the effectiveness of learning materials and activities.
  - Enhancing learner engagement and motivation.
  - Discovering new insights and knowledge from learning data.
- Learning analytics can involve different types of data, such as:
  - Demographic data, such as age, gender, background, etc..
  - Behavioral data, such as clicks, time spent, actions, etc..
  - Cognitive data, such as performance, scores, errors, etc..
  - Affective data, such as emotions, attitudes, preferences, etc..
  - Social data, such as interactions, collaborations, networks, etc..
- Learning analytics can use different methods and techniques, such as:
  - Descriptive analytics, which summarise and visualise what has happened in the learning process.
  - Diagnostic analytics, which identify and explain why something has happened in the learning process.
  - Predictive analytics, which forecast what is likely to happen in the future based on the learning data.
  - Prescriptive analytics, which suggest what actions should be taken to optimise the learning outcomes.
  - Exploratory analytics, which discover new patterns and relationships in the learning data.
- Learning analytics can face different challenges and limitations, such as:
  - Data quality, which refers to the accuracy, completeness, consistency, and timeliness of the data.
  - Data privacy, which refers to the protection of the personal and sensitive information of the learners and instructors.
  - Data ethics, which refers to the moral and legal implications of collecting, analysing, and using the learning data.
  - Data interpretation, which refers to the validity, reliability, and generalisability of the results and findings from the data analysis.
  - Data actionability, which refers to the feasibility, effectiveness, and impact of the actions and decisions based on the data analysis.
