### Ontological representation of social relationships

Ontological representation of social relationships refers to the use of ontologies to model and represent social relationships in a formal and structured manner. This is an important aspect of social network analytics, as it allows for the aggregation and analysis of social network data.

1. **Ontologies** are formal representations of knowledge, typically in the form of a hierarchy of concepts and relationships between them. In the context of social network analytics, ontologies can be used to represent different types of social relationships, such as friendship, kinship, or professional relationships.

2. **Social relationships** can be complex and multi-faceted, and ontologies provide a way to capture this complexity in a structured manner. For example, a friendship relationship may be represented as a combination of shared interests, mutual trust, and frequent communication.

3. **Aggregation** of social network data involves combining data from multiple sources to create a more complete picture of the social network. Ontologies can be used to facilitate this process by providing a common vocabulary for representing social relationships.

4. **Analysis** of social network data can be enhanced by the use of ontologies, as they provide a structured and formal representation of social relationships. This can facilitate the identification of patterns and trends in the data, and support the development of predictive models.

In summary, ontological representation of social relationships is an important aspect of social network analytics, as it provides a formal and structured way to model and represent social relationships, and supports the aggregation and analysis of social network data.
