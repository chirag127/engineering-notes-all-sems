## Unit 1 - Social Network Analysis

Social Network Analysis (SNA) is the study of social structures through the use of network and graph theories. It is used to investigate the interactions between individuals, groups, organizations, and societies.

Some key concepts in SNA include:

1. **Nodes**: These represent the individual actors within the network.
2. **Edges**: These represent the relationships or interactions between the nodes.
3. **Degree**: This is the number of edges connected to a node.
4. **Centrality**: This is a measure of the importance of a node within the network.
5. **Density**: This is the proportion of possible edges that are present in the network.
6. **Clustering**: This refers to the tendency of nodes to form groups or clusters within the network.

SNA can be used for a variety of purposes, including understanding the spread of information or disease, identifying influential individuals within a network, and detecting communities or subgroups within a larger network.

SNA is a powerful tool for understanding social structures and can be applied to a wide range of fields, including sociology, anthropology, psychology, and business. It is an interdisciplinary field that draws on theories and methods from a variety of disciplines.
