### Ontological representation of social individuals

Ontological representation of social individuals refers to the way in which individuals and their actions are represented in the context of social or historical groups, processes, and events. Ontological individualism is the thesis that these social entities are nothing more than complexes of individuals and individual actions.

Social ontology is a subfield of general ontology that focuses on the basic entities, properties, and kinds studied by the social sciences. There are two kinds of social entities: social individuals and social complexes or collectives.

A prominent topic in social ontology is the analysis of social groups. One example of an ontological representation of social individuals is the Friend-of-a-Friend (FOAF) ontology, which is an OWL-based format for representing personal information and an individual’s social network.

In summary, ontological representation of social individuals is concerned with how individuals and their actions are represented within the context of social entities, and there are various approaches and formats for achieving this representation.
