### Network Analysis

Network analysis is a method used to study the relationships between entities in a network. In the context of social network analysis, these entities are often individuals or organizations, and the relationships between them can represent a variety of interactions, such as friendship, communication, or collaboration.

Some key concepts in network analysis include:

1. **Nodes**: Nodes represent the entities in a network. In a social network, nodes can represent individuals, organizations, or other entities.

2. **Edges**: Edges represent the relationships or connections between nodes. Edges can be directed or undirected, depending on whether the relationship is reciprocal.

3. **Degree**: The degree of a node is the number of edges connected to it. In a directed network, the in-degree and out-degree of a node represent the number of incoming and outgoing edges, respectively.

4. **Centrality**: Centrality measures are used to identify the most important nodes in a network. There are several different measures of centrality, including degree centrality, closeness centrality, and betweenness centrality.

5. **Clustering**: Clustering refers to the tendency of nodes to form groups or communities within a network. Clustering can be measured using metrics such as the clustering coefficient.

6. **Paths**: A path is a sequence of edges that connects two nodes in a network. The shortest path between two nodes is known as the geodesic distance.
