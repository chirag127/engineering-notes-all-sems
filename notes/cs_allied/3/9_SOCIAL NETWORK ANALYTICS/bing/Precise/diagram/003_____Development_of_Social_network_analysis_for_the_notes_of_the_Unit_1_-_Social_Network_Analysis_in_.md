### Development of Social Network Analysis

Social network analysis (SNA) is a method of studying social structures through the use of network and graph theories. It is a way to map and measure relationships and flows between people, groups, organizations, computers, or other information/knowledge processing entities.

1. The origins of SNA can be traced back to the work of sociologist Georg Simmel in the early 20th century. Simmel studied the patterns of social relationships and interactions, and his work laid the foundation for the development of SNA.

2. In the 1930s, Jacob Moreno introduced the sociogram, a visual representation of social relationships, which became a key tool in SNA.

3. In the 1950s and 1960s, researchers such as John Barnes and Elizabeth Bott further developed SNA by applying mathematical and statistical methods to the analysis of social networks.

4. The 1970s saw the emergence of computer software for SNA, which allowed for the analysis of larger and more complex networks.

5. In the 1980s and 1990s, SNA gained popularity in a variety of fields, including anthropology, psychology, and organizational studies.

6. Today, SNA is widely used in many fields, including public health, marketing, and political science. The rise of social media and online social networks has also led to an increased interest in SNA.

7. SNA has also been applied to the study of non-human social networks, such as those of animals and plants.

8. The development of SNA has been influenced by advances in other fields, such as graph theory, statistics, and computer science.

9. SNA continues to evolve, with new methods and techniques being developed to analyze increasingly complex social networks.
