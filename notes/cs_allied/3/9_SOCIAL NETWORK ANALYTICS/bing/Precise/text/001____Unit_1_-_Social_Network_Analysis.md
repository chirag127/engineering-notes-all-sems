## Unit 1 - Social Network Analysis

Social Network Analysis (SNA) is the study of social structures through the use of network and graph theories. It is a method for analyzing the relationships and patterns of interaction between individuals, groups, and organizations.

Some key concepts in SNA include:

1. **Nodes**: These represent the individual actors within the network.
2. **Edges**: These represent the relationships or connections between the nodes.
3. **Degree**: This is the number of edges connected to a node.
4. **Centrality**: This is a measure of the importance of a node within the network.
5. **Density**: This is the proportion of possible edges that are present in the network.
6. **Clustering coefficient**: This measures the degree to which nodes in a network tend to cluster together.

SNA can be used for a variety of purposes, including understanding the spread of information or disease, identifying influential individuals within a network, and detecting communities or subgroups within a larger network. It is a powerful tool for understanding the complex relationships and dynamics within social systems.
