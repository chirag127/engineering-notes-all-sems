### Optimizing goodness of fit for the notes of the Unit 5 - Evaluation of Social Network Analysis

- Goodness of fit is a measure of how well a statistical model fits the observed data. It can be used to compare different models and select the best one for a given dataset.
- In social network analysis, goodness of fit can be applied to various types of models, such as exponential random graph models (ERGMs), relational event models (REMs), or two-mode networks.
- ERGMs are models that specify the probability of observing a network based on a set of structural features, such as density, reciprocity, transitivity, or homophily. ERGMs can capture complex patterns of interdependence among actors in a network.
- REMs are models that specify the probability of observing a sequence of events in a network, such as communication, collaboration, or conflict. REMs can capture the dynamics and timing of network interactions over time.
- Two-mode networks are networks that consist of two sets of nodes, such as actors and events, or organizations and affiliations. Two-mode networks can capture the co-occurrence and co-membership of actors in different contexts.
- To assess the goodness of fit of a social network model, one can use various methods, such as:
  - Comparing the observed network statistics with the simulated network statistics from the fitted model. This can be done by using graphical or numerical methods, such as histograms, boxplots, or chi-square tests. The goal is to check whether the model can reproduce the observed features of the network, such as degree distribution, clustering, or centrality .
  - Comparing the observed network events with the predicted network events from the fitted model. This can be done by using methods such as cross-validation, holdout samples, or receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves. The goal is to check whether the model can accurately forecast the future events in the network, such as who will communicate with whom, when, and how often.
  - Comparing the observed two-mode network with the projected one-mode network from the fitted model. This can be done by using methods such as correspondence analysis, multidimensional scaling, or cluster analysis. The goal is to check whether the model can capture the latent structure of the two-mode network, such as the similarity or dissimilarity of actors or events, or the emergence of groups or communities.
- To optimize the goodness of fit of a social network model, one can use various strategies, such as:
  - Choosing the appropriate model for the research question and the data. Different models have different assumptions, strengths, and limitations, and may not be suitable for all types of networks or events. For example, ERGMs may not be able to handle large or sparse networks, REMs may not be able to handle multiple types of events, and two-mode networks may not be able to handle weighted or directed ties.
  - Selecting the relevant features or parameters for the model. Too many or too few features or parameters may lead to overfitting or underfitting, respectively. Overfitting means that the model fits the data too well, but may not generalize to new data. Underfitting means that the model fits the data too poorly, and may not capture the essential patterns of the data. One can use methods such as likelihood ratio tests, Akaike information criterion (AIC), or Bayesian information criterion (BIC) to compare and select the best features or parameters for the model.
  - Evaluating the model fit using multiple methods and criteria. No single method or criterion can provide a definitive answer to the goodness of fit of a model. Different methods or criteria may have different advantages and disadvantages, and may yield different results or interpretations. One should use a combination of methods and criteria to assess the model fit from different perspectives and dimensions, and to check the robustness and validity of the model.
