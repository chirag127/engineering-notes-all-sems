# Ontological representation of social individuals

- Ontological representation of social individuals is the process of describing the entities, properties, and relations that constitute the social world using a formal language or logic.
- Ontology is a branch of philosophy that studies the nature and categories of being and existence. Ontology can also refer to a specific system or theory of such categories, or to a domain-specific vocabulary or model that captures the essential features of a particular domain of knowledge or discourse.
- Social ontology is a subfield of ontology that focuses on the social domain, which includes social individuals, groups, institutions, norms, practices, and artifacts. Social ontology aims to provide a clear and consistent account of the basic entities, properties, and kinds of the social world, and how they relate to each other and to the natural world.
- Social individuals are entities that have a social identity, such as persons, agents, roles, or positions. Social individuals can have various attributes, such as names, preferences, beliefs, goals, skills, or affiliations. Social individuals can also participate in various relations, such as friendship, kinship, membership, authority, or communication.
- Ontological representation of social individuals can be done using various formal languages or logics, such as first-order logic, description logic, or Web Ontology Language (OWL). These languages or logics allow for expressing the definitions, axioms, and rules that govern the social domain, and for reasoning about the validity, consistency, and entailment of statements or queries about the social world.
- One example of ontological representation of social individuals is the Friend-of-a-Friend (FOAF) ontology, which is an OWL-based format for representing personal information and an individual's social network. FOAF allows for describing the basic properties and relations of social individuals, such as name, gender, email, homepage, interests, friends, family, colleagues, etc. FOAF can also be linked to other ontologies or vocabularies, such as Dublin Core, GeoNames, or Schema.org, to enrich the information and enable interoperability across different domains and applications.
