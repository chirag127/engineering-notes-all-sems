### Personal networks

Personal networks are subsets of social networks that focus on the relationships and interactions of a specific individual, group, or entity. Personal networks can be used to study and understand various aspects of social life, such as social support, social capital, social influence, and social identity. Personal networks can also reveal the diversity, density, centrality, and multiplexity of one's social ties.

Some key concepts and methods of personal network analysis are:

- **Ego network**: An ego network is a personal network that consists of a focal node (ego) and the nodes that are directly connected to it (alters), as well as the ties among the alters. Ego networks can be used to measure the size, composition, and structure of one's personal network, as well as the types and quality of the relationships with the alters.
- **Name generator**: A name generator is a survey question or technique that asks respondents to list the names of people they know or interact with in a specific context or domain. Name generators can be used to elicit ego networks or personal network samples, as well as to collect information about the attributes and roles of the alters.
- **Name interpreter**: A name interpreter is a survey question or technique that asks respondents to provide additional information about the names they generated, such as the frequency, duration, and nature of the contact, the strength and closeness of the relationship, the resources and support exchanged, and the perceived similarity and trustworthiness of the alter. Name interpreters can be used to assess the functional content and quality of one's personal network ties.
- **Personal network visualization**: A personal network visualization is a graphical representation of a personal network, usually in the form of a node-link diagram, where nodes represent actors and links represent relationships. Personal network visualizations can be used to illustrate the patterns and properties of one's personal network, such as the clusters, bridges, and holes in the network structure, and the attributes, roles, and labels of the network actors.

Personal network analysis can be applied to various domains and disciplines, such as sociology, psychology, anthropology, communication, education, health, and business. Some examples of personal network analysis applications are:

- Studying how personal networks provide social support, emotional well-being, and coping resources for individuals facing stress, illness, or life transitions.
- Examining how personal networks influence one's attitudes, beliefs, behaviors, and outcomes, such as political opinions, consumer choices, health practices, and career success.
- Exploring how personal networks reflect and shape one's social identity, such as gender, ethnicity, culture, and religion, and how they affect one's sense of belonging, trust, and solidarity.
- Comparing and contrasting personal networks across different contexts, groups, and cultures, and identifying the factors that affect the formation, maintenance, and dissolution of personal network ties.
