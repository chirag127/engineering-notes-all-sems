### Comparison across methods and networks for the notes of the Unit 5 - Evaluation of Social Network Analysis

- Social network analysis (SNA) is a set of techniques for studying the structure and dynamics of social relationships among actors, such as individuals, groups, organizations, or countries.
- SNA can be applied to different types of networks, such as personal networks, communication networks, affiliation networks, citation networks, collaboration networks, or trade networks.
- SNA can use different methods for collecting, representing, analyzing, and visualizing network data, such as surveys, interviews, observation, archival records, matrices, graphs, centrality measures, clustering algorithms, network models, or software tools  .
- SNA can be compared across methods and networks based on several criteria, such as:
  - The level of analysis: whether the focus is on the whole network, a subnetwork, or an individual actor.
  - The type of data: whether the network data is binary, valued, directed, or undirected.
  - The size of the network: whether the network is small, medium, or large, and how this affects the feasibility and validity of the methods.
  - The purpose of the analysis: whether the goal is to describe, explain, predict, or intervene in the network phenomena.
  - The theoretical framework: whether the analysis is guided by a specific theory or perspective, such as structuralism, relationalism, social capital, social exchange, social influence, or social embeddedness .
  - The ethical issues: whether the analysis respects the privacy, confidentiality, consent, and autonomy of the network actors .
- SNA can provide valuable insights for evaluating the impact, effectiveness, efficiency, and sustainability of social interventions, policies, or programs, by examining how the network structure and dynamics affect the outcomes and processes of interest .
- SNA can also identify the strengths and weaknesses of the network, the opportunities and threats for the network, and the best practices and recommendations for improving the network .
