# Determining equality

- Equality is a concept that refers to the degree of similarity or difference between two or more entities, such as individuals, groups, or networks.
- Equality can be measured in various ways, depending on the context and the purpose of the analysis.
- Some common dimensions of equality are:

  - **Structural equality**: This refers to the similarity or difference in the patterns of connections or relations among the entities. For example, two networks are structurally equal if they have the same number of nodes and edges, and the same distribution of degrees and distances among the nodes.
  - **Attribute equality**: This refers to the similarity or difference in the characteristics or properties of the entities, such as their attributes, behaviors, or preferences. For example, two individuals are attribute equal if they have the same age, gender, occupation, and hobbies.
  - **Relational equality**: This refers to the similarity or difference in the quality or strength of the relations among the entities, such as their trust, friendship, or influence. For example, two groups are relationally equal if they have the same level of cohesion, diversity, and reciprocity among their members.

- Determining equality can be useful for various applications, such as:

  - **Comparing and contrasting**: Equality can help to identify the similarities and differences between two or more entities, and to evaluate their relative performance, status, or impact. For example, equality can help to compare and contrast the structure, attributes, and relations of different social networks, such as Facebook, Twitter, and LinkedIn.
  - **Clustering and grouping**: Equality can help to group or cluster the entities based on their similarity or difference, and to discover the underlying patterns or structures among them. For example, equality can help to cluster or group the individuals based on their attributes, behaviors, or preferences, and to discover the communities or segments within a social network.
  - **Matching and recommending**: Equality can help to match or recommend the entities based on their similarity or difference, and to facilitate the exchange of information, resources, or services among them. For example, equality can help to match or recommend the individuals based on their trust, friendship, or influence, and to facilitate the social interactions or collaborations within a social network.
