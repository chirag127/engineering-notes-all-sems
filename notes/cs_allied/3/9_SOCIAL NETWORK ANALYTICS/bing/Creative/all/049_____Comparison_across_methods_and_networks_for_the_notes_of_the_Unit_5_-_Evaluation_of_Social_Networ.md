# Comparison across methods and networks for the notes of the Unit 5 - Evaluation of Social Network Analysis

Social network analysis (SNA) is a set of techniques for analysing social phenomena starting from micro or meso units, such as individuals, groups, organizations, or communities, and their relationships. SNA emphasizes the importance of the structure of relationships, not just their attributes and attitudes: society is not seen as an aggregate of individuals, but as a structure of interpersonal ties.

SNA can be applied to different types of networks, such as personal networks, ego networks, whole networks, or multiplex networks, depending on the level and scope of analysis. SNA can also use different methods for collecting, representing, measuring, and modelling network data, such as surveys, interviews, observations, experiments, matrices, graphs, indices, statistics, or algorithms  .

Some of the main goals of SNA are to:

- Describe the patterns and properties of networks and their elements, such as size, density, centrality, cohesion, clustering, reciprocity, homophily, or transitivity  .
- Compare and contrast networks and their elements, such as similarity, difference, equivalence, diversity, or heterogeneity  .
- Explain and predict the outcomes and effects of networks and their elements, such as influence, diffusion, contagion, cooperation, or innovation  .
- Visualize and communicate the structure and dynamics of networks and their elements, such as graphs, maps, diagrams, or animations .

To compare across methods and networks in SNA, some of the key questions and criteria are:

- What is the research question or problem that motivates the use of SNA?  
- What is the unit of analysis and the level of aggregation of the network?  
- What is the type and source of the network data and how reliable and valid are they?  
- What is the method and technique for collecting, representing, measuring, and modelling the network data and how appropriate and rigorous are they?  
- What is the method and technique for analysing, interpreting, and reporting the network data and how robust and transparent are they?  
- What are the main findings and implications of the network analysis and how generalizable and applicable are they?  

These questions and criteria can help to evaluate the strengths and weaknesses of different methods and networks in SNA, as well as to identify the best practices and challenges for conducting and using SNA in various contexts and domains  .
