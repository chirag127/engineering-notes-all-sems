## Unit 5 - Evaluation of Social Network Analysis

- Social network analysis (SNA) is the process of investigating social structures through the use of network and graph theories.
- SNA can be used to study various phenomena, such as communication, collaboration, influence, trust, power, diffusion, and contagion, among social actors.
- SNA can be applied to different domains, such as sociology, psychology, anthropology, economics, politics, health, education, and business.
- SNA can be performed at different levels of analysis, such as individual, group, organization, community, or society.
- SNA can be conducted using different methods, such as surveys, interviews, observations, experiments, or online data collection.
- SNA can be visualized using different tools, such as graphs, matrices, maps, or dashboards.
- SNA can be evaluated using different criteria, such as validity, reliability, generalizability, usability, and impact.

### Validity
- Validity refers to the extent to which the SNA measures what it claims to measure.
- Validity can be assessed by comparing the SNA results with other sources of information, such as existing theories, empirical evidence, or expert opinions.
- Validity can be enhanced by using appropriate sampling techniques, data collection methods, network measures, and analytical techniques.
- Validity can be threatened by various sources of error, such as measurement error, sampling error, non-response error, or model misspecification.

### Reliability
- Reliability refers to the extent to which the SNA results are consistent and reproducible.
- Reliability can be assessed by examining the stability, equivalence, and internal consistency of the SNA results.
- Reliability can be enhanced by using standardized procedures, clear definitions, and rigorous quality control.
- Reliability can be threatened by various sources of variation, such as temporal variation, spatial variation, contextual variation, or human error.

### Generalizability
- Generalizability refers to the extent to which the SNA results can be applied to other populations, settings, or situations.
- Generalizability can be assessed by comparing the SNA results with other studies, meta-analyses, or systematic reviews.
- Generalizability can be enhanced by using representative samples, diverse data sources, and robust analytical techniques.
- Generalizability can be threatened by various sources of bias, such as selection bias, response bias, publication bias, or confirmation bias.

### Usability
- Usability refers to the extent to which the SNA results are understandable, interpretable, and actionable for the intended users.
- Usability can be assessed by soliciting feedback, conducting usability tests, or measuring user satisfaction, engagement, or behavior change.
- Usability can be enhanced by using clear language, effective visualizations, and interactive features.
- Usability can be threatened by various sources of confusion, such as complexity, ambiguity, or inconsistency.

### Impact
- Impact refers to the extent to which the SNA results have positive and lasting effects on the social actors, the social structures, or the social issues under study.
- Impact can be assessed by measuring the outcomes, outputs, or impacts of the SNA results, such as changes in knowledge, attitudes, behaviors, relationships, or performance.
- Impact can be enhanced by using relevant, timely, and actionable SNA results, and by engaging the stakeholders, disseminating the findings, and implementing the recommendations.
- Impact can be threatened by various sources of resistance, such as inertia, skepticism, or opposition.
