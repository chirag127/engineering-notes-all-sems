# Optimizing goodness of fit for the notes of the Unit 5 - Evaluation of Social Network Analysis

- Goodness of fit is a measure of how well a statistical model fits the observed data. It can be used to compare different models and select the best one for a given dataset.
- In social network analysis, goodness of fit can be applied to various types of models, such as exponential random graph models (ERGMs), relational event models (REMs), or two-mode networks.
- ERGMs are models that specify the probability of observing a network based on a set of structural features, such as density, reciprocity, transitivity, homophily, etc. ERGMs can capture complex dependencies among network ties and account for network heterogeneity.
- REMs are models that describe the occurrence and timing of events in a network, such as communication, collaboration, or conflict. REMs can capture the dynamics and evolution of network structures over time and account for temporal dependencies among events.
- Two-mode networks are networks that consist of two sets of nodes, such as actors and events, and links between nodes of different sets, such as participation or affiliation. Two-mode networks can reveal the dual nature of social structures and the interplay between individual agency and institutional constraints.
- To assess the goodness of fit of social network models, various methods and criteria can be used, such as:
  - Likelihood ratio tests: These tests compare the log-likelihood of a model with a null model or a nested model and determine whether the model provides a significantly better fit to the data.
  - Akaike information criterion (AIC) and Bayesian information criterion (BIC): These criteria balance the fit and complexity of a model and penalize models with more parameters. The model with the lowest AIC or BIC is preferred.
  - Graphical diagnostics: These methods compare the observed network statistics with the simulated network statistics from the fitted model and check whether the model can reproduce the empirical patterns in the data.
  - Predictive accuracy: These methods evaluate how well a model can predict future network events or missing network data and measure the error or accuracy of the predictions.
- To optimize the goodness of fit of social network models, various strategies can be used, such as:
  - Model selection: This involves choosing the most appropriate type of model and the most relevant set of parameters for a given network dataset and research question.
  - Model specification: This involves defining the functional form and the distributional assumptions of the model and ensuring that they are consistent with the data and the theory.
  - Model estimation: This involves finding the best-fitting values of the parameters and ensuring that the estimation method is reliable and convergent.
  - Model validation: This involves testing the robustness and sensitivity of the model and checking for potential problems, such as overfitting, underfitting, degeneracy, or misspecification.
