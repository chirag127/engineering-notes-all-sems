# Personal Networks

Personal networks are the sets of people or groups that an individual interacts with or perceives as relevant in their social life. Personal networks can provide various types of social support, such as emotional, instrumental, informational, or appraisal support. Personal networks can also influence an individual's behavior, attitudes, opinions, health, and well-being.

Personal network analysis is the application of social network analysis (SNA) techniques to the study of personal networks. SNA is a way of conceptualizing, describing, and modeling society as sets of nodes (actors) and ties (relationships) that connect them . SNA can measure various aspects of network structure, such as density, centrality, clustering, reciprocity, homophily, and embeddedness. SNA can also measure various aspects of network composition, such as size, diversity, multiplexity, and strength of ties. SNA can also measure various aspects of network function, such as social capital, social influence, social cohesion, and social identity.

Personal network analysis can help us understand how personal networks are formed, maintained, and changed over time. It can also help us understand how personal networks affect and are affected by individual and social outcomes. Personal network analysis can be used for various purposes, such as:

- Exploring the diversity and dynamics of personal networks across different contexts, cultures, and populations.
- Examining the role of personal networks in providing social support and coping with stress, trauma, or illness.
- Investigating the impact of personal networks on health behaviors, such as smoking, drinking, or physical activity.
- Analyzing the diffusion of information, innovation, or contagion through personal networks.
- Assessing the social capital and social resources available to individuals through their personal networks.
- Evaluating the effectiveness of interventions or policies that target personal networks or use personal networks as channels of communication or influence.

Personal network analysis can be conducted using various methods, such as:

- Surveys or interviews that ask individuals to name or rate their personal network members or relationships.
- Observations or recordings that capture the interactions or communications among personal network members.
- Experiments or simulations that manipulate or model the structure or function of personal networks.
- Data mining or web scraping that extract or generate personal network data from online platforms or digital traces.

Personal network analysis can be performed using various tools, such as:

- Software or packages that can visualize, analyze, or simulate personal networks, such as UCINET, Pajek, Gephi, R, or Python.
- Online platforms or applications that can collect, store, or share personal network data, such as EgoWeb, EgoNet, or NameGenWeb.
- Devices or sensors that can measure, monitor, or track personal network interactions or communications, such as smartphones, wearables, or RFID tags.
