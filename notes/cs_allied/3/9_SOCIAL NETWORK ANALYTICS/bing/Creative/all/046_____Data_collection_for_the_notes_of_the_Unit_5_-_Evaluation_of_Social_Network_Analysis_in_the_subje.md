# Data collection for the notes of the Unit 5 - Evaluation of Social Network Analysis

- Social network analysis (SNA) is a method of studying the patterns of relationships among social actors, such as individuals, groups, or organizations.
- Data collection for SNA involves identifying the network boundaries, the types of actors and relationships, and the methods of measuring and recording the network data.
- There are two main approaches to data collection for SNA: socio-centric and ego-centric.
  - Socio-centric studies focus on closed networks that are defined by a common attribute, such as membership in a group, organization, or community.
  - Ego-centric studies focus on open networks that are defined by the personal ties of a focal actor, such as friends, family, or colleagues.
- The most common method of data collection for SNA is web-based surveys, which allow the researcher to customize the questions and the response options.
  - In socio-centric surveys, each respondent rates his or her interaction with all other network members for the question at hand, such as frequency, intensity, or quality of communication.
  - In ego-centric surveys, each respondent lists and rates his or her interaction with a number of alters (other actors) for the question at hand, such as trust, support, or influence.
- Other methods of data collection for SNA include archival records, tracking devices, or mining the Internet, which can provide objective and longitudinal data on network ties, but may also raise ethical and privacy issues.
- The data collected for SNA can be represented and analyzed using three types of interrelated tools: matrices, maps, and measures.
  - Matrices are tables that show the presence or absence, or the strength or direction, of ties between pairs of actors.
  - Maps are graphical representations that show the nodes (actors) and the edges (ties) of the network, and can reveal the overall structure and patterns of the network.
  - Measures are numerical indicators that describe the properties of the network, such as density, centrality, cohesion, or diversity.
- Data collection for SNA is a crucial phase of the research process, as it determines the quality and validity of the network data and analysis.
- Data collection for SNA should follow the principles of ethical research, such as informed consent, confidentiality, and respect for the rights and dignity of the participants.
- Data collection for SNA should also be guided by the research questions, objectives, and hypotheses, as well as the available resources, time, and budget.
