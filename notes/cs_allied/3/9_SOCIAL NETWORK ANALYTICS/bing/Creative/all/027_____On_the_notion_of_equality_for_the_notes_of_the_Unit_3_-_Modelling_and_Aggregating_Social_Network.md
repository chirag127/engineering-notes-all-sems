# On the notion of equality for the notes of the Unit 3 - Modelling and Aggregating Social Network Data in the subject of SOCIAL NETWORK ANALYTICS

- Equality is a complex and contested concept that can be understood in different ways depending on the context and perspective.
- In social network analysis, equality can refer to various aspects of the structure and dynamics of social networks, such as:
  - The distribution of resources, opportunities, and outcomes among network actors (distributive equality).
  - The degree of similarity or difference between network actors in terms of their attributes, behaviors, and preferences (relational equality).
  - The extent to which network actors have equal access, influence, and recognition in the network (participatory equality).
  - The quality of the relationships and interactions between network actors, and the extent to which they respect and value each other (relational equality).
- Different notions of equality may have different implications for social network analysis, such as:
  - Distributive equality may be measured by indicators such as network centrality, degree, closeness, betweenness, or eigenvector, which capture the relative position and power of network actors.
  - Relational equality may be assessed by methods such as homophily, heterophily, assortativity, or diversity, which examine the similarity or difference between network actors based on their attributes or ties.
  - Participatory equality may be evaluated by approaches such as network governance, brokerage, or social capital, which explore the access, influence, and recognition of network actors in the network.
  - Relational equality may be explored by techniques such as qualitative network analysis, social support, or social identity, which investigate the meaning, quality, and value of the relationships and interactions between network actors.
- Different notions of equality may also have different normative and ethical implications for social network analysis, such as:
  - Distributive equality may raise questions about the fairness, justice, and efficiency of the allocation of resources, opportunities, and outcomes in the network.
  - Relational equality may pose challenges about the diversity, inclusion, and tolerance of the network actors and their attributes or ties.
  - Participatory equality may entail issues about the representation, empowerment, and accountability of the network actors and their access, influence, and recognition in the network.
  - Relational equality may involve concerns about the respect, dignity, and solidarity of the network actors and their relationships and interactions in the network.
