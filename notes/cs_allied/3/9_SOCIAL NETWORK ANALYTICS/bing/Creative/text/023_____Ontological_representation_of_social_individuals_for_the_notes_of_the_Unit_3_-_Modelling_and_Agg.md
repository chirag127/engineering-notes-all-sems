### Ontological representation of social individuals

- Ontological representation of social individuals is the process of describing the entities, properties, and relations that constitute the social world using a formal language or logic.
- Ontology is a branch of philosophy that studies the nature and categories of being and existence. Ontology can also refer to a specific system or theory of such categories, or to a domain-specific vocabulary or model that defines the entities and relations in a particular field of knowledge.
- Social ontology is a subfield of ontology that focuses on the basic entities, properties, and kinds that are studied by the social sciences, such as social individuals, groups, institutions, norms, roles, actions, events, etc.
- Social individuals are entities that have a social identity, such as persons, agents, actors, or roles. Social individuals can be distinguished from natural individuals, which are entities that exist independently of social interaction, such as physical objects, animals, or biological organisms.
- Social individuals can be represented using different ontological languages or frameworks, depending on the level of abstraction, expressiveness, and formality required. Some examples of ontological languages or frameworks are:
  - RDF (Resource Description Framework): a standard model for data interchange on the Web, based on triples of subject-predicate-object, where each element can be a URI (Uniform Resource Identifier), a literal, or a blank node. RDF can be serialized in different formats, such as XML, Turtle, or JSON-LD.
  - OWL (Web Ontology Language): a family of knowledge representation languages for authoring ontologies, based on description logic, a subset of first-order logic. OWL can be used to define classes, properties, individuals, and axioms, and to reason about their logical consequences. OWL can be expressed in different syntaxes, such as RDF/XML, OWL/XML, or Manchester syntax.
  - FOAF (Friend of a Friend): an ontology for representing personal information and social networks, based on RDF and OWL. FOAF defines terms for describing basic attributes of persons, such as name, gender, age, email, homepage, etc., as well as relations among persons, such as friend, colleague, family, etc.
  - SIOC (Semantically-Interlinked Online Communities): an ontology for describing online communities and social media platforms, based on RDF and OWL. SIOC defines terms for describing the structure and content of online communities, such as sites, forums, blogs, posts, comments, users, etc., as well as relations among them, such as creator, subscriber, reply, etc.
  - SKOS (Simple Knowledge Organization System): an ontology for representing knowledge organization systems, such as thesauri, taxonomies, classification schemes, etc., based on RDF and OWL. SKOS defines terms for describing concepts, labels, definitions, notes, etc., as well as relations among concepts, such as broader, narrower, related, etc.
