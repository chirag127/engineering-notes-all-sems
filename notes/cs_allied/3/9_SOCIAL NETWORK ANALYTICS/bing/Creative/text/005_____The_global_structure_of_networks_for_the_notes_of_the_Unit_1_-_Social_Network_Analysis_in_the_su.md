### The global structure of networks

- The global structure of networks refers to the overall pattern of relationships among the nodes (individuals, groups, organizations, etc.) in a network.
- The global structure of networks can reveal important properties and characteristics of the network, such as its density, diameter, clustering, centrality, modularity, and community structure.
- The global structure of networks can also influence the behavior and outcomes of the nodes, such as their access to information, resources, opportunities, and risks.
- The global structure of networks can vary depending on the type, context, and purpose of the network, such as social media networks, business networks, knowledge networks, disease transmission networks, etc.
- The global structure of networks can be studied and analyzed using social network analysis (SNA), which is a general approach for examining the social network using network and graph theory concepts.
- Social network analysis can help understand the phenomena that emerge from the interaction of nodes, such as diffusion, influence, contagion, cooperation, competition, etc.
- Social network analysis can also help identify the roles, positions, and functions of the nodes in the network, such as leaders, brokers, bridges, hubs, etc.
- Social network analysis can be applied to various domains and disciplines, such as sociology, psychology, economics, politics, public health, education, etc.
