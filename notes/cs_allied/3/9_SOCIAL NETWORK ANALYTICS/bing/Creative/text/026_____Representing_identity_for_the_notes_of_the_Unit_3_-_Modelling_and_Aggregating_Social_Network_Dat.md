### Representing identity for the notes of the Unit 3 - Modelling and Aggregating Social Network Data in the subject of SOCIAL NETWORK ANALYTICS

- Social network analysis (SNA) is the practice of representing networks of people as graphs and then exploring these graphs.
- A typical social network representation has nodes for people, and edges connecting two nodes to represent one or more relationships between them.
- The nodes and edges can have attributes that describe their characteristics, such as name, age, gender, location, type, weight, direction, etc.
- The representation of identity in social network analysis depends on the level of granularity and the purpose of the analysis.
- For example, if the goal is to identify the most influential member or the representative of the group, one can use centrality measures, such as degree, closeness, betweenness, or eigenvector centrality, to rank the nodes based on their importance.
- If the goal is to discover the structure of the group or the reference to whom a person of interest is connected, one can use clustering methods, such as modularity, community detection, or k-core decomposition, to partition the nodes into subgroups based on their similarity or density.
- If the goal is to summarize the interests and opinions of the users, one can use feature extraction methods, such as principal component analysis, latent semantic analysis, or deep representation learning, to reduce the dimensionality of the node attributes and capture the latent factors that explain the variation in the data.
- The representation of identity in social network analysis can also be influenced by the data source, the data quality, the data preprocessing, and the data visualization.
- For example, the data source can be online platforms, such as social media, blogs, forums, etc., or offline sources, such as surveys, interviews, observations, etc.
- The data quality can be affected by noise, missing values, outliers, errors, biases, etc.
- The data preprocessing can involve cleaning, filtering, transforming, aggregating, sampling, etc.
- The data visualization can involve choosing the layout, the color, the size, the shape, the label, etc., of the nodes and edges to enhance the readability and the interpretability of the graph.
- Therefore, representing identity in social network analysis is a complex and multifaceted task that requires careful consideration of the data, the methods, and the objectives of the analysis .
