- Social network analysis (SNA) is the process of investigating social structures through the use of networks and graph theory.
- SNA studies the behavior of the individual at the micro level, the pattern of relationships (network structure) at the macro level, and the interactions between the two.
- SNA can be used to study various phenomena, such as communication, collaboration, influence, trust, power, diffusion, contagion, and innovation .
- SNA can be applied to different domains, such as health, education, business, politics, media, and the web .
- SNA uses various methods and techniques to collect, analyze, and visualize network data, such as surveys, interviews, observation, web scraping, network metrics, centrality measures, clustering, community detection, network models, and network graphs .
- SNA requires careful evaluation of the validity, reliability, and generalizability of the network data and analysis, as well as the ethical and practical implications of the network research.
- SNA can provide valuable insights into the structure and dynamics of social systems, as well as the opportunities and challenges for social change and intervention .
