### The global structure of networks

- The global structure of networks refers to the overall pattern of relationships among the nodes (individuals, groups, organizations, etc.) in a network.
- The global structure of networks can reveal important properties and characteristics of the network, such as its density, diameter, clustering, centrality, modularity, and community structure .
- The global structure of networks can also influence the behavior and outcomes of the nodes, such as their access to information, resources, opportunities, and risks .
- The global structure of networks can be studied using various methods and techniques from social network analysis (SNA), which is a general study of the social network using network and graph theory concepts.
- Some of the common methods and techniques for studying the global structure of networks are:

  - Network visualization: using graphical tools to represent the network as a set of nodes and links, and to highlight its structural features.
  - Network metrics: using mathematical formulas to measure and quantify various aspects of the network structure, such as its size, density, diameter, average path length, clustering coefficient, degree distribution, centrality measures, modularity, and community detection algorithms .
  - Network models: using theoretical frameworks to explain and predict the formation and evolution of the network structure, such as random graphs, small-world networks, scale-free networks, preferential attachment, and network growth models .
  - Network comparison: using statistical methods to test and compare the similarities and differences between the network structures of different groups, populations, or contexts.

- The global structure of networks is an important topic for social network analysis, as it can provide insights into the social phenomena that emerge from the interaction of individuals or institutions, and the implications for their health, well-being, performance, and innovation .
