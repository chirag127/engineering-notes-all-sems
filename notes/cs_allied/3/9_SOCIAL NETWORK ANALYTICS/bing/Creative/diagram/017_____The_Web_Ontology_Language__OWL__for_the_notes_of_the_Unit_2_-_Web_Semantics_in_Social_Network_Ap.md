### The Web Ontology Language (OWL)

- OWL is a **Semantic Web language** that is designed to **process and integrate information over the web**, making sense of it in a manner similar to human reasoning.
- OWL is intended to **facilitate interpretability** among web content using vocabulary and formatting that allows **automatic machine processing**.
- OWL can be used to **describe the classes and relations** between them that are inherent in Web documents and applications.
- OWL can also be used to **describe the properties of Web resources** and the characteristics of those objects.
- OWL is a **family of knowledge representation languages** for authoring ontologies.
- Ontologies are a **formal way to describe taxonomies and classification networks**, essentially defining the structure of knowledge for various domains.
- OWL has three sublanguages with different levels of expressiveness and complexity: **OWL Lite, OWL DL, and OWL Full**.
- OWL Lite is the **least expressive** sublanguage, but also the **most efficient** to process. It supports **basic classification hierarchy** and **simple constraints**.
- OWL DL is based on **description logic**, a subset of first-order logic. It supports **maximum expressiveness** while maintaining **computational completeness** and **decidability**. It allows **complex class expressions**, **cardinality restrictions**, and **disjointness**.
- OWL Full is the **most expressive** sublanguage, but also the **least efficient** to process. It allows **arbitrary RDF graphs** to be combined with OWL constructs, but **loses computational guarantees**. It supports **metamodeling** and **higher-order logic**.
- OWL is a **W3C recommendation** and has a **standard syntax** based on RDF/XML. It also has alternative syntaxes such as **Manchester syntax**, **Turtle syntax**, and **Functional syntax**.
