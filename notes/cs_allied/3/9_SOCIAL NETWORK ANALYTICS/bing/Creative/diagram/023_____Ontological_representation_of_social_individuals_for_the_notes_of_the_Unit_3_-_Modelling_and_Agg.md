### Ontological representation of social individuals

- Ontology is a branch of philosophy that studies the nature and categories of being and existence.
- Ontology can also refer to a formal and explicit specification of a conceptualization of a domain of interest, using a set of terms and relations.
- Ontology-based knowledge representation describes the individual instances and roles in the domain that are represented using unary and binary predicates.
- Social ontology is a subfield of ontology that studies the basic entities, properties and kinds of the social world, such as social groups, social actions, social norms, social institutions, etc .
- Social individuals are one kind of social entities that arise from social interaction. They are human beings who have personal information, preferences, beliefs, attitudes, emotions, etc., and who are connected to other social individuals through various social relations, such as friendship, kinship, membership, etc.
- Ontological representation of social individuals is the process of defining and describing the concepts and relations that are relevant for modeling and analyzing social individuals and their social networks.
- One example of ontological representation of social individuals is the Friend-of-a-Friend (FOAF) ontology, which is an OWL based format for representing personal information and an individual’s social network.
- The FOAF ontology defines a set of classes and properties that can be used to describe basic information about a person, such as name, gender, age, email, homepage, etc., as well as their social relations, such as friend, colleague, family, etc.
- The FOAF ontology also allows for linking and integrating data from different sources, such as web pages, blogs, social media platforms, etc., by using the same URI to identify the same person.
- The FOAF ontology is an example of a lightweight ontology, which is a simple and flexible way of representing knowledge, without requiring a high degree of formalization or logical consistency.
- Another example of ontological representation of social individuals is the Social Network Analysis Ontology (SNAO), which is an OWL based format for representing social network data and analysis.
- The SNAO ontology defines a set of classes and properties that can be used to describe the structure and dynamics of social networks, such as nodes, edges, network types, network measures, network models, etc.
- The SNAO ontology also allows for applying various methods and techniques of social network analysis, such as centrality, clustering, community detection, etc., to the social network data.
- The SNAO ontology is an example of a heavyweight ontology, which is a complex and rigorous way of representing knowledge, requiring a high degree of formalization and logical consistency.
