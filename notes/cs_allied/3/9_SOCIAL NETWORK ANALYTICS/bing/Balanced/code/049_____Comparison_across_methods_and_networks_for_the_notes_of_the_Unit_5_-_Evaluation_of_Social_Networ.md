### Comparison across methods and networks for the notes of the Unit 5 - Evaluation of Social Network Analysis

- Social network analysis (SNA) is a set of techniques for studying the structure and dynamics of social relationships among actors, such as individuals, groups, organizations, or countries.
- SNA can be applied to different types of networks, such as personal networks, communication networks, affiliation networks, citation networks, collaboration networks, or trade networks.
- SNA can use different methods to collect, represent, analyze, and visualize network data, such as surveys, interviews, observation, archival sources, matrices, graphs, centrality measures, clustering algorithms, network models, or software tools  .
- SNA can be used for different purposes, such as describing network patterns, identifying influential actors, detecting communities, testing hypotheses, explaining outcomes, or evaluating interventions  .
- SNA can be compared across methods and networks based on several criteria, such as:

  - The level of analysis: whether the focus is on the whole network, a subnetwork, or an individual actor.
  - The type of data: whether the network data is binary, valued, directed, or undirected.
  - The size of the network: whether the network is small, medium, or large, and how this affects the feasibility and validity of the methods.
  - The quality of the data: whether the network data is complete, reliable, valid, and consistent.
  - The complexity of the network: whether the network has multiple types of actors, relations, or attributes, and how this affects the analysis and interpretation of the results.
  - The dynamics of the network: whether the network is static or changing over time, and how this affects the choice of methods and models.
  - The context of the network: whether the network is embedded in a specific social, cultural, or historical setting, and how this affects the meaning and implications of the network structure and processes.
