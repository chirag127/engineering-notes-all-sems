### On the notion of equality for the notes of the Unit 3 - Modelling and Aggregating Social Network Data in the subject of SOCIAL NETWORK ANALYTICS

- Equality is a social and ethical concept that implies that all individuals or groups have the same rights, opportunities, and status in a society.
- Social network analysis (SNA) is a method of studying social structures and interactions through the use of networks and graph theory .
- SNA can be used to measure and compare the level of equality or inequality in different social networks, based on various criteria such as centrality, diversity, homophily, segregation, and cohesion  .
- Centrality is a measure of how important or influential a node (person or entity) is in a network, based on the number and strength of its connections to other nodes  .
- Diversity is a measure of how heterogeneous or diverse a network is, based on the attributes or characteristics of the nodes and the ties (relationships or interactions) that connect them  .
- Homophily is a tendency for nodes to form ties with similar others, based on shared attributes or preferences    .
- Segregation is a measure of how separated or isolated different groups or categories of nodes are in a network, based on the presence or absence of ties between them    .
- Cohesion is a measure of how connected or integrated a network or a subgroup of nodes is, based on the density and transitivity of ties among them  .
- Equality in social networks can be assessed by examining how these measures vary across different nodes or groups, and how they affect the access to resources, information, and opportunities that the network provides  .
- Inequality in social networks can be caused by various factors, such as social norms, power relations, geographical barriers, institutional policies, and historical legacies, that create or reinforce patterns of homophily, segregation, and centrality  .
- Inequality in social networks can have various consequences, such as social exclusion, discrimination, polarization, conflict, and injustice, that affect the well-being, rights, and liberties of individuals and groups  .
- Equality in social networks can be promoted by various strategies, such as awareness, education, empowerment, participation, collaboration, and intervention, that aim to increase the diversity, cohesion, and balance of the network structure and the ties that constitute it  .
