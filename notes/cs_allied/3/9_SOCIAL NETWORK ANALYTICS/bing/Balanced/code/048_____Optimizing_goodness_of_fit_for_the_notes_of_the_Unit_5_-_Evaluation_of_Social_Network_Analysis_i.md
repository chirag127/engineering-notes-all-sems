### Optimizing goodness of fit for the notes of the Unit 5 - Evaluation of Social Network Analysis

- Goodness of fit is a measure of how well a statistical model fits the observed data.
- In social network analysis, goodness of fit can be used to evaluate the performance and validity of different network models, such as exponential random graph models (ERGMs), stochastic actor-oriented models (SAOMs), or latent space models (LSMs).
- Goodness of fit can be assessed by comparing various network statistics, such as degree distribution, clustering coefficient, geodesic distance, triad census, etc., between the observed network and the simulated networks generated by the model.
- Goodness of fit can also be assessed by comparing the predictive power and discrimination ability of the model, i.e., how well the model can predict new links or missing links in the network, and how well the model can distinguish between different types of links or nodes in the network.
- Some methods for assessing goodness of fit are:

  - Visual inspection: plotting the network statistics of the observed network and the simulated networks on the same graph and checking for any discrepancies or outliers.
  - Hypothesis testing: testing the null hypothesis that the model fits the data versus the alternative hypothesis that the model does not fit the data, using a suitable test statistic and significance level.
  - Link strength comparison: comparing the link strengths (or probabilities) of the observed network and the simulated networks, and checking for any systematic biases or deviations.
  - Model selection criteria: comparing the values of different criteria that balance the fit and complexity of the model, such as Akaike information criterion (AIC), Bayesian information criterion (BIC), or likelihood ratio test (LRT).

- Some challenges and limitations of assessing goodness of fit are:

  - The choice of network statistics may depend on the research question and the type of network data, and there is no consensus on which statistics are the most important or relevant for evaluating network models.
  - The simulation of networks from the model may be computationally intensive or infeasible, especially for large or complex networks, and may introduce sampling errors or biases.
  - The comparison of network statistics may be sensitive to the number and size of the simulated networks, and may require appropriate normalization or standardization methods.
  - The interpretation of goodness of fit may be subjective or context-dependent, and may not reflect the causal mechanisms or processes underlying the network formation or evolution.
