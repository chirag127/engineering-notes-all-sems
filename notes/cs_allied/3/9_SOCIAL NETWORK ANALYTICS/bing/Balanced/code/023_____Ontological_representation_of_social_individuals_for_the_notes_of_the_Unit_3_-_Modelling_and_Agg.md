### Ontological representation of social individuals

- Ontology is the study of the nature and categories of being and existence.
- Ontology-based knowledge representation describes the individual instances and roles in a domain using predicates and relations.
- Social ontology is a subfield of ontology that studies the nature and properties of the social world, such as social groups, social actions, social norms, and social institutions.
- Social individuals are one kind of social entities that arise from social interaction and have properties that are not reducible to their physical or psychological components.
- Ontological individualism is the thesis that social or historical groups, processes, and events are nothing more than complexes of individuals and individual actions.
- Ontological representation of social individuals aims to capture the personal information and social network of an individual using a formal language, such as the Web Ontology Language (OWL).
- One example of ontological representation of social individuals is the Friend-of-a-Friend (FOAF) ontology, which is an OWL-based format for representing personal information and an individual’s social network.
- FOAF uses classes and properties to define the basic concepts and relations in a social network, such as person, name, email, friend, knows, etc.
- FOAF allows individuals to describe themselves and their social connections in a machine-readable way, and to link their profiles with other FOAF profiles on the web.
- FOAF enables knowledge sharing, processing, reuse, capturing and communication among social individuals and applications.
