### Evaluation of web-based social network extraction

- Web-based social network extraction is the process of identifying and describing actors and their relations from online sources, such as web pages, social media platforms, or online databases .
- Web-based social network extraction can benefit many web mining and social network analysis applications, such as community detection, sentiment analysis, recommendation systems, or social influence measurement.
- Web-based social network extraction can be performed using different techniques, such as superficial methods, supervised methods, or unsupervised methods  .
- Superficial methods rely on the textual or structural similarity of web pages to extract actors and relations, such as using keywords, hyperlinks, or metadata .
- Supervised methods use machine learning algorithms to train models that can classify web pages or segments as actors or relations, such as using support vector machines, decision trees, or neural networks.
- Unsupervised methods use clustering or topic modeling techniques to group web pages or segments based on their content or features, such as using k-means, hierarchical clustering, or latent Dirichlet allocation.
- Web-based social network extraction can be evaluated using different criteria, such as accuracy, precision, recall, F1-score, or network metrics, such as density, centrality, or modularity  .
- Accuracy measures the proportion of correctly extracted actors and relations out of the total number of web pages or segments.
- Precision measures the proportion of correctly extracted actors and relations out of the total number of extracted actors and relations.
- Recall measures the proportion of correctly extracted actors and relations out of the total number of actual actors and relations.
- F1-score is the harmonic mean of precision and recall, and it balances the trade-off between them.
- Network metrics measure the structural properties of the extracted social network, such as how dense, central, or modular it is .
- Density measures the ratio of the number of actual relations to the number of possible relations in the network.
- Centrality measures the importance or influence of an actor in the network, based on different criteria, such as degree, closeness, betweenness, or eigenvector centrality.
- Modularity measures the extent to which the network can be divided into communities or clusters of actors that are more connected within than between them.
