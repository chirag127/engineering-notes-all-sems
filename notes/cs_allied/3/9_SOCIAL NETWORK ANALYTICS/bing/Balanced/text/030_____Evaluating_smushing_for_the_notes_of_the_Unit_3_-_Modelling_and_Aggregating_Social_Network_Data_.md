### Evaluating smushing for the notes of the Unit 3 - Modelling and Aggregating Social Network Data in the subject of SOCIAL NETWORK ANALYTICS

- Smushing is a technique for aggregating social network data by combining nodes that share some common attributes or characteristics.
- Smushing can help reduce the complexity and size of a social network graph, as well as reveal patterns and clusters that are otherwise hidden.
- Smushing can also help protect the privacy and anonymity of the individuals in the network, by masking their identities and relationships.
- However, smushing also has some drawbacks and limitations, such as:
  - Smushing can introduce errors and biases in the network analysis, by losing some information and creating artificial links or distances between nodes.
  - Smushing can affect the validity and reliability of the network metrics, such as centrality, density, and modularity, by changing the structure and dynamics of the network.
  - Smushing can be difficult to implement and evaluate, as it requires choosing appropriate criteria and methods for grouping and merging nodes, as well as assessing the trade-offs and impacts of smushing on the network analysis.
- Therefore, smushing should be used with caution and justification, and its results should be compared and validated with the original network data and other methods of network aggregation.
