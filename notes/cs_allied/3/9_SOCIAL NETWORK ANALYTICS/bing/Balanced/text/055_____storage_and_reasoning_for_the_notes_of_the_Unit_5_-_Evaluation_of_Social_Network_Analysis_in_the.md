### Storage and Reasoning for Social Network Analysis

- Social network analysis (SNA) is the process of exploring or examining the social structure by using graph theory.
- SNA helps to measure relationships and flows between groups, organizations, and other connected entities.
- SNA can be applied to various domains and applications, such as sentiment analysis, opinion mining, community detection, influence analysis, etc.
- SNA requires data that represents the social network, such as nodes (actors), edges (relations), and attributes (properties) of nodes and edges.
- The data can be obtained from various sources, such as text, databases, sensor networks, communication systems, and social media.
- The data can be stored in different formats, such as sociogram, sociomatrix, adjacency matrix, edge list, etc .
- Sociogram is a graph that depicts the relations between people in the form of nodes and edges.
- Sociomatrix is a matrix data structure that stores the graph points and lines of a sociogram.
- Adjacency matrix is a square matrix that indicates whether pairs of nodes are adjacent or not in the graph.
- Edge list is a list of pairs of nodes that are connected by an edge in the graph.
- The choice of data format depends on the size, complexity, and sparsity of the network, as well as the analytical tools and methods used.
- Reasoning is the process of drawing conclusions or inferences from the data and the network structure.
- Reasoning can be done by using various methods and techniques, such as centrality measures, clustering algorithms, network models, network metrics, etc .
- Centrality measures are used to identify the most important or influential nodes in the network, such as degree centrality, closeness centrality, betweenness centrality, eigenvector centrality, etc .
- Clustering algorithms are used to partition the network into groups of nodes that are more densely connected to each other than to the rest of the network, such as k-means, hierarchical clustering, modularity-based clustering, etc .
- Network models are used to describe the network structure and dynamics, such as random graphs, small-world networks, scale-free networks, etc .
- Network metrics are used to quantify the network properties and characteristics, such as density, diameter, average path length, clustering coefficient, assortativity, etc .
- Reasoning can help to understand the network behavior, patterns, trends, and anomalies, as well as to test hypotheses, make predictions, and provide recommendations .
