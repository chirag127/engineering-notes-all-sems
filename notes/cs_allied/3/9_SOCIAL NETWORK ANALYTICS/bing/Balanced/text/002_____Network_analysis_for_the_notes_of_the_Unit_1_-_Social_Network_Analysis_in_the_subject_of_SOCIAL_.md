### Network analysis for the notes of the Unit 1 - Social Network Analysis in the subject of SOCIAL NETWORK ANALYTICS

- Network analysis is a method of studying the structure and behavior of social entities (such as individuals, groups, organizations, etc.) and their relationships (such as connections, interactions, influences, etc.) using networks and graph theory.
- A network is a representation of a set of entities (called nodes or vertices) and the links (called edges or ties) that connect them. A network can be visualized as a graph, where nodes are depicted as points and edges are depicted as lines.
- A social network is a network that consists of social entities and their social relationships. Examples of social networks are friendship networks, collaboration networks, communication networks, etc.
- Social network analysis (SNA) is the process of investigating social networks using various concepts, measures, models, and algorithms. SNA can help answer questions such as:
  - Who are the most influential or central actors in a network?
  - How are the actors connected or clustered in a network?
  - How does information or behavior spread in a network?
  - How does the network structure affect the outcomes or performance of the actors?
  - How can the network be optimized or improved for a specific goal or purpose?
- SNA can be applied to various domains and use cases, such as:
  - Marketing and advertising: SNA can help identify the key influencers, opinion leaders, or target segments in a network, and design effective strategies to reach them or leverage their influence.
  - Business and management: SNA can help analyze the organizational structure, communication patterns, collaboration networks, innovation networks, etc. of a company, and provide insights for improving efficiency, productivity, creativity, etc.
  - Sociology and psychology: SNA can help understand the social behavior, norms, values, attitudes, etc. of individuals and groups, and how they are affected by their network environment.
  - Education and learning: SNA can help measure the learning outcomes, engagement, motivation, etc. of students and teachers, and how they are influenced by their social interactions and relationships.
  - Health and medicine: SNA can help monitor the spread of diseases, epidemics, or behaviors in a population, and design interventions or policies to prevent or control them.
  - Politics and governance: SNA can help study the political opinions, preferences, alliances, conflicts, etc. of actors and groups, and how they are shaped by their network connections and influences.
