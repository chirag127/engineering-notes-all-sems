### Evaluation of web-based social network extraction

- Web-based social network extraction is the process of identifying and describing the actors and their relations from online sources, such as web pages, social media platforms, blogs, etc.  
- Web-based social network extraction can benefit many applications, such as web mining, social network analysis, recommender systems, sentiment analysis, etc. 
- Web-based social network extraction can be performed using different techniques, such as superficial methods, supervised methods, semi-supervised methods, and unsupervised methods.   
- Superficial methods rely on the textual similarity between web pages to determine the actors and their relations. They are simple and fast, but they may not capture the semantic meaning of the web content.  
- Supervised methods use labeled data to train a classifier or a extractor that can identify the actors and their relations from web pages. They are accurate and robust, but they require a lot of annotated data and domain knowledge. 
- Semi-supervised methods use a small amount of labeled data and a large amount of unlabeled data to learn the features and patterns of the actors and their relations from web pages. They are flexible and scalable, but they may suffer from noise and inconsistency. 
- Unsupervised methods use clustering, topic modeling, or graph analysis to discover the actors and their relations from web pages without any prior knowledge. They are general and adaptable, but they may produce ambiguous or irrelevant results. 
- Web-based social network extraction can be evaluated using different criteria, such as precision, recall, F1-score, accuracy, completeness, consistency, etc.  
- Precision measures the fraction of correctly extracted actors and relations among all the extracted ones.  
- Recall measures the fraction of correctly extracted actors and relations among all the existing ones.  
- F1-score is the harmonic mean of precision and recall. It balances the trade-off between precision and recall.  
- Accuracy measures the fraction of correctly extracted actors and relations among all the web pages. 
- Completeness measures the degree to which all the actors and relations in the web pages are extracted. 
- Consistency measures the degree to which the extracted actors and relations are coherent and compatible with each other. 
- Web-based social network extraction can be evaluated using different methods, such as manual evaluation, automatic evaluation, or hybrid evaluation.  
- Manual evaluation involves human experts or users to judge the quality of the extracted actors and relations. It is reliable and intuitive, but it is time-consuming and subjective.  
- Automatic evaluation involves computational tools or metrics to compare the extracted actors and relations with a gold standard or a baseline. It is fast and objective, but it may not capture the human perception or the domain context.  
- Hybrid evaluation combines manual and automatic evaluation to leverage the advantages of both. It is comprehensive and flexible, but it may introduce more complexity and cost.
