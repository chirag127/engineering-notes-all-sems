### On the notion of equality for the notes of the Unit 3 - Modelling and Aggregating Social Network Data in the subject of SOCIAL NETWORK ANALYTICS

- Equality is a concept that refers to the state of being equal in terms of rights, status, opportunities, or treatment among individuals or groups.
- Social equality is a specific type of equality that focuses on the social aspects of human life, such as civil rights, freedom of expression, access to public resources, and social inclusion.
- Social network analysis (SNA) is a method of investigating social structures and patterns of relations among actors (people, organizations, states, etc.) using networks and graph theory.
- SNA can be used to measure and compare different aspects of equality or inequality in social networks, such as:
  - The distribution of resources, power, or influence among actors, using metrics such as degree centrality, betweenness centrality, or eigenvector centrality.
  - The diversity or homogeneity of actors' attributes, such as gender, race, or income, using metrics such as assortativity, diversity index, or Blau's index.
  - The segregation or integration of actors into different groups or communities, using metrics such as modularity, clustering coefficient, or cohesion.
- SNA can also be used to explore the causes and consequences of equality or inequality in social networks, such as:
  - The mechanisms of social tie formation, such as homophily, triadic closure, or preferential attachment, that may generate or reinforce inequality by creating segregated or hierarchical network structures.
  - The effects of network structure on actors' behavior, outcomes, or well-being, such as diffusion of information, innovation, or disease, social influence, social capital, or social support, that may depend on or affect the level of equality in the network.
