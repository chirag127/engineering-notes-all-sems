# Preparing the data for the notes of the Unit 5 - Evaluation of Social Network Analysis in the subject of SOCIAL NETWORK ANALYTICS

- Social network analysis (SNA) is an evaluation approach that uses mathematics and visualization to represent the structure of relationships between people, organizations, goals, interests, and other entities within a larger system.
- SNA helps the understanding of phenomena that emerge from the interaction of individuals or institutions and is useful to assess the impact of interventions, identify key actors or influencers, and monitor changes in network structure over time.
- SNA can be applied to various domains, such as health, education, governance, communication, and innovation, and can address different evaluation questions, such as:
  - Who are the main actors or stakeholders in a network and what are their roles and positions?
  - How are the actors connected and what are the characteristics and strengths of their relationships?
  - How does the network structure affect the flow of information, resources, or influence among the actors?
  - How does the network evolve over time and what are the factors that influence its dynamics?
  - How does the network performance compare to other networks or benchmarks?
  - What are the effects or outcomes of the network activities or interventions on the actors or the system?
- SNA involves four main steps  :
  - Define the network boundaries and the unit of analysis (e.g., individuals, groups, organizations, etc.).
  - Collect data on the actors and their relationships using various methods, such as surveys, interviews, observations, or secondary sources.
  - Analyze the data using software tools, such as UCINET, Gephi, or R, to calculate network metrics, such as density, centrality, cohesion, or modularity, and to visualize the network graphically.
  - Interpret the results and communicate the findings to the relevant stakeholders, using tables, charts, maps, or narratives.
