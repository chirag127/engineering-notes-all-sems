# The macro-structure of social networks

- A social network is a social structure made up of a set of social actors (such as individuals or organizations), sets of dyadic ties, and other social interactions between actors.
- Social networks can be analyzed at different levels of aggregation, such as micro, meso, and macro.
- The micro level focuses on the behavior and attributes of individual actors within the network, such as their centrality, degree, or clustering coefficient.
- The meso level focuses on the patterns of relationships (network structure) between actors, such as their density, reciprocity, or homophily.
- The macro level focuses on the effects of social networks for larger social outcomes, such as civil uprising, state formation, policy diffusion, or economic development.
- Social network analysis is a set of methods and theories for studying the structure and dynamics of social networks, using concepts such as nodes, ties, graphs, matrices, and network measures.
- Social network analysis can help understand the causes and consequences of social phenomena, such as diffusion of information, social influence, social capital, social support, or social movements.
