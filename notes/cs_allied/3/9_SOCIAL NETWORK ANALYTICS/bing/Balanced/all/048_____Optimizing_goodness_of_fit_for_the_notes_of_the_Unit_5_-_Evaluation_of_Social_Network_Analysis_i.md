# Optimizing goodness of fit for social network analysis

- Goodness of fit is a measure of how well a statistical model fits the observed data.
- In social network analysis, goodness of fit can be used to evaluate the adequacy of network models, such as exponential random graph models (ERGMs), stochastic actor-oriented models (SAOMs), or latent space models (LSMs).
- Network models can capture various structural features of social networks, such as degree distribution, clustering, reciprocity, transitivity, homophily, or assortativity.
- To optimize the goodness of fit of network models, one needs to select appropriate model parameters, estimate them using suitable methods, and compare them with the observed network statistics.
- Some common methods for parameter estimation are maximum likelihood estimation (MLE), Markov chain Monte Carlo (MCMC), or variational inference (VI).
- Some common methods for goodness of fit assessment are graphical methods, such as Q-Q plots, P-P plots, or ROC curves, or numerical methods, such as likelihood ratio tests, Akaike information criterion (AIC), Bayesian information criterion (BIC), or deviance information criterion (DIC).
- Optimizing goodness of fit can help to improve the predictive power, discrimination, and generalization of network models, as well as to test hypotheses and infer causal effects of network processes.
