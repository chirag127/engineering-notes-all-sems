# Predicting the goodness of fit for the notes of the Unit 5 - Evaluation of Social Network Analysis

- Social network analysis (SNA) is a method of studying the structure and dynamics of social relationships among individuals or groups.
- SNA can be used to understand the patterns, causes, and consequences of social interactions in various domains, such as health, education, politics, and business.
- SNA can also be used to design and evaluate interventions that aim to change or improve the social network structure or behavior of the actors.
- To perform SNA, one needs to collect and analyze network data, which are usually represented as graphs or matrices, where nodes represent actors and edges represent relationships.
- One of the challenges of SNA is to choose an appropriate model or method to describe and explain the observed network data, and to assess how well the model fits the data .
- A model or method that fits the data well can provide accurate and meaningful insights into the network structure and dynamics, and can also be used to make predictions or simulations .
- A model or method that does not fit the data well can lead to erroneous or misleading conclusions, and can also be unreliable or invalid for further analysis .
- Therefore, predicting the goodness of fit of a model or method is an important step in SNA, as it can help to select the best model or method for a given network data set, and to evaluate the quality and reliability of the results .
- There are different ways to measure and compare the goodness of fit of models or methods in SNA, depending on the type and complexity of the network data, and the assumptions and objectives of the analysis  .
- Some of the common approaches to predicting the goodness of fit of models or methods in SNA are  :

  - **Chi-square test**: This is a statistical test that compares the observed frequencies of network characteristics (such as degree distribution, clustering coefficient, centrality measures, etc.) with the expected frequencies under a null model or hypothesis. The test statistic is the sum of the squared differences between the observed and expected frequencies, weighted by the expected frequencies. The test statistic follows a chi-square distribution with a certain degree of freedom, which depends on the number of network characteristics and the number of parameters in the model. The p-value of the test is the probability of obtaining a test statistic as large or larger than the observed one, under the null model or hypothesis. A small p-value indicates a poor fit of the model or method to the data, and a large p-value indicates a good fit .
  - **Log-likelihood ratio test**: This is a statistical test that compares the log-likelihood of the observed network data under two models or methods, one of which is nested within the other. The log-likelihood is a measure of how probable the observed network data are under a given model or method, based on the parameters estimated from the data. The test statistic is the difference between the log-likelihoods of the two models or methods, multiplied by a factor of 2. The test statistic follows a chi-square distribution with a degree of freedom equal to the difference between the number of parameters in the two models or methods. The p-value of the test is the probability of obtaining a test statistic as large or larger than the observed one, under the null model or method. A small p-value indicates a poor fit of the nested model or method to the data, and a large p-value indicates a good fit.
  - **Akaike information criterion (AIC) and Bayesian information criterion (BIC)**: These are information-theoretic criteria that balance the trade-off between the goodness of fit and the complexity of a model or method. The AIC and BIC are calculated as the negative log-likelihood of the observed network data under a given model or method, plus a penalty term that depends on the number of parameters in the model or method and the size of the data. The AIC and BIC can be used to compare the relative fit of different models or methods for the same network data set, by choosing the one that has the lowest value.
