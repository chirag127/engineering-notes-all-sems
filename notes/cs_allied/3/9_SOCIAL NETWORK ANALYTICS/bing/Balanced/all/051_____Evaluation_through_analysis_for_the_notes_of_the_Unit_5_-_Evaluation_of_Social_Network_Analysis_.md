# Evaluation through analysis for the notes of the Unit 5 - Evaluation of Social Network Analysis

- Social network analysis (SNA) is a tool used in modern sociology to identify the links between individuals in various social systems.
- SNA can also be used in monitoring and evaluation in order to probe deeper into the power of the social network and how it can be used to measure program outcomes and impact.
- SNA analyses the behavior of the individual at the micro level, the pattern of relationships (network structure) at the macro level, and the interactions between the two.
- Social networks are both the cause of and the result of individual behavior.
- SNA can help to understand phenomena that emerge from the interaction of individuals or institutions and is useful to assess adaptive capacity.
- SNA can also help to identify the key actors, the information flows, the gaps and the opportunities for collaboration within a network.
- SNA uses networks and graph theory to examine the relationships among entities within a specified network.
- Networks are composed of nodes (entities) and ties (relationships) that can be represented by graphs (diagrams) with different shapes, colors and sizes.
- SNA uses various metrics and measures to quantify and compare the characteristics of nodes, ties and networks, such as degree, centrality, density, cohesion, etc.
- SNA can be used in different phases of complex healthcare intervention research, such as design, implementation, evaluation and dissemination.
- SNA can provide valuable insights into the context, the mechanisms and the outcomes of complex healthcare interventions, as well as the challenges and facilitators of their implementation and scaling up.
- SNA can also help to engage stakeholders, to monitor network changes, to identify best practices and to disseminate findings and recommendations.
- SNA requires clear research questions, appropriate data collection methods, ethical considerations, rigorous analysis and effective communication of results.
- SNA can be combined with other methods and approaches, such as qualitative interviews, surveys, case studies, etc., to enrich the understanding of the network dynamics and the intervention effects.
