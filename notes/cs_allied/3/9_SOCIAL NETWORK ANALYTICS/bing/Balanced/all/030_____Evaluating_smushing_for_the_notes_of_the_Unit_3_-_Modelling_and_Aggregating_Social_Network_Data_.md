# Evaluating Smushing for the Notes of the Unit 3 - Modelling and Aggregating Social Network Data

Smushing is a technique for aggregating social network data by combining nodes that share some common attributes or characteristics. Smushing can help reduce the complexity and noise of large social networks, and reveal patterns and insights that are otherwise hidden. Smushing can also be used to anonymize sensitive data and protect the privacy of individuals.

Some points to consider when evaluating smushing are:

- The criteria for smushing nodes should be relevant and meaningful for the research question or the analysis goal. For example, smushing nodes based on their location, gender, age, or interests may be appropriate for some studies, but not for others.
- The level of smushing should be balanced between preserving the original structure and information of the network, and simplifying it for easier interpretation and visualization. Too much smushing may result in losing important details and nuances, while too little smushing may not provide any significant benefits.
- The effects of smushing on the network properties and metrics should be carefully examined and reported. Smushing may change the network size, density, degree distribution, centrality, clustering, modularity, and other measures. These changes may have implications for the validity and reliability of the analysis and the conclusions drawn from it.
- The smushed network should be compared and contrasted with the original network, and with other possible ways of smushing. This can help evaluate the advantages and disadvantages of different smushing methods, and the robustness and sensitivity of the results to different smushing choices.
