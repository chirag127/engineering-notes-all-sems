## Unit 5 - Evaluation of Social Network Analysis

1. Metrics to evaluate networks:
- Degree centrality: Number of direct connections a node has. Nodes with high degree centrality are important for information spread.
- Betweenness centrality: Number of shortest paths going through a node. Nodes with high betweenness centrality control information flow and act as bridges between parts of the network.
- Closeness centrality: Average shortest path length from a node to all other nodes. Nodes with high closeness centrality can reach other nodes quickly and are efficient in network coordination.
- Clustering coefficient: Tendency of nodes to form clusters. High clustering coefficient means presence of clusters and dense connections between neighboring nodes.

2. Applications of network metrics:
- Identify influential users: Metrics can find users who can spread information or influence opinions efficiently.
- Detect communities: Clustering coefficient and centrality metrics can reveal tightly-knit communities in a network.
- Understand information flow: Centrality metrics show how information spreads through key nodes in the network.
- Identify critical infrastructure: Nodes with high betweenness centrality are critical for connecting network, so their failure can disconnect the network.

3. Limitations of network metrics:
- They provide only quantitative measures but not qualitative understanding of relationships.
- They do not consider attributes or content of nodes and edges.
- They may not capture influences between indirectly connected nodes.
- They are susceptible to missing data and noise in network data.
