### Structural and cognitive effects on scientific performance

1. Structural effects: The position of an actor in a network and the structure of the network itself can impact performance. For example, occupying a central position in a network can provide access to more resources and opportunities, thereby enhancing performance. The density and hierarchies of a network can also impact performance.

2. Cognitive effects: The way actors perceive and interpret their network connections and positions can influence their attitudes, behaviors, and performance. For example, the extent to which actors believe that their network provides social support or conduces to the flow of useful information can impact their motivation and performance. The discrepancy between actual and perceived network positions can also affect performance, for better or for worse.

3. Interaction of structural and cognitive effects: Structural and cognitive effects interact in complex ways. For example, actual network centrality may lead actors to perceive their positions more positively and experience enhanced performance, but overly positive self-perceptions may also lead to complacency that undermines motivation and performance. The effects of network structure and actors' cognitions about their network positions must be understood as interwoven rather than as separate influences on performance.
