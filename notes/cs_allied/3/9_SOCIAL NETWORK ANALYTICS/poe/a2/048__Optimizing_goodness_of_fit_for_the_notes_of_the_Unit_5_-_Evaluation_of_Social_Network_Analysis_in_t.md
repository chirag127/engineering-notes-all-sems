### Optimizing goodness of fit for the notes of the Unit 5 - Evaluation of Social Network Analysis in the subject of SOCIAL NETWORK ANALYTICS.

1. Evaluating goodness of fit
- Assess how well the model fits the data.
- Check residual plots to see if assumptions are met.
- Calculate fit statistics like R-squared, adjusted R-squared, AIC, BIC, etc.
- high R-squared and low residual values indicate good fit.
- Adjusted R-squared compensates for number of predictors.
- AIC and BIC balance fit and complexity, lower values indicate better fit.

2. Model diagnostics
- Check residual plots for patterns or outliers.
- Calculate Cook's distance to check influential points.
- Calculate variance inflation factor (VIF) to check for multicollinearity.
- Transform variables or use a different model if diagnostics are poor.

3. Comparing models
- Calculate multiple fit statistics for different models.
- Consider trade-offs between fit and complexity (number of parameters).
- Use information criteria (AIC/BIC) and cross-validation to compare non-nested models.
- Choose the model that balances fit and parsimony.

4. Evaluating predictions
- Apply the final model to new data to get predictions.
- Calculate prediction error to assess predictive performance.
- The model may fit the training data well but not generalize to new data if overfit.
- Can use cross-validation techniques during model selection to get more reliable performance estimates.
