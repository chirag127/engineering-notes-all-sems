### On the notion of equality for the notes of the Unit 3 - Modelling and Aggregating Social Network Data in the subject of SOCIAL NETWORK ANALYTICS:

1. Equality implies equal access to opportunities and resources for all individuals in a network. However, in reality, there exist disparities and inequalities in social networks.
2. Degree centrality is a measure of inequality in a network that looks at the number of connections of a node. Nodes with higher degree centrality have more power and influence over those with lower degree centrality, creating inequality.
3. Betweenness centrality is another measure of inequality that looks at the number of shortest paths going through a node. Nodes with higher betweenness centrality have more control over the flow of information, again creating inequality.
4. Closeness centrality also leads to inequality as nodes that are closer to others have greater access to information and can influence others more than those that are further away.
5. Other network metrics like eigenvector centrality also reflect inherent inequalities in a social network based on the relative importance and connections of nodes.

In summary, while equality is an idealistic concept, most real-world social networks exhibit some form of inequality based on the relative positions and connections of actors.
