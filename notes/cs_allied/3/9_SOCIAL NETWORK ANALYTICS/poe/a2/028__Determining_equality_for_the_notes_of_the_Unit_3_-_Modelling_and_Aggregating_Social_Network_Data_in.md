### Determining equality for the notes of the Unit 3 - Modelling and Aggregating Social Network Data in the subject of SOCIAL NETWORK ANALYTICS:

1. Degree Centrality: Degree centrality is a measure of the number of direct connections a node has to other nodes in the network. Nodes with high degree centrality are those that have many adjacent nodes connected to them.
2. Closeness Centrality: Closeness centrality considers the distance of a node to all other nodes in the network. Nodes with high closeness centrality have short paths to others in the network. They can reach all other nodes quickly.
3. Betweenness Centrality: Betweenness centrality considers the number of shortest paths between nodes that pass through a given node. Nodes that occur on many shortest paths between others have high betweenness centrality. They act as bridges connecting otherwise disconnected parts of the network.
4. Eigenvector Centrality: Eigenvector centrality considers the centrality of a node's neighbors. Nodes that are connected to other central nodes will receive a higher eigenvector centrality score. It captures the idea that connections to high-scoring nodes contribute more to the score of the node in question.
5. Key Players: Key players are nodes that are highly central according to one or more centrality measures. They are nodes that are crucial connectors or bridges in the network. Identifying key players can be important for understanding influence and access to information in a network.
