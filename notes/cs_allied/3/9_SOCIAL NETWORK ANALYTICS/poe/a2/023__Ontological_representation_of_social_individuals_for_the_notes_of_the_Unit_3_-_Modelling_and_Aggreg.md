### Ontological representation of social individuals

- An ontological representation models the basic types of entities in a domain and the relationships between them.
- In the context of social networks, the key entities to represent ontologically are social individuals and the relationships between them.
- Some key aspects of social individuals to represent:
-- Demographic characteristics: age, gender, ethnicity, etc.
-- Psychological characteristics: personality, attitudes, beliefs, etc.
-- Social characteristics: social class, profession, organization membership, etc.
-- Skills and competencies
-- Interests and preferences
- Relationships between individuals can also be ontologically represented in terms of:
-- Strength/weakness
-- Direction (symmetric/asymmetric)
-- Type (friendship, family, coworker, etc.)
-- Duration (long-term/short-term)
- A formal ontological representation of social network data enables sophisticated reasoning about individuals, relationships, and networks.
- It provides a means of clarifying assumptions and enabling interoperability between datasets and systems.
