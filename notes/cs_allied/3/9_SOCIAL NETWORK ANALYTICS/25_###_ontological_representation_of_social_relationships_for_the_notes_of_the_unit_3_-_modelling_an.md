### Ontological representation of social relationships for the notes of the Unit 3 - Modelling and Aggregating Social Network Data in the subject of SOCIAL NETWORK ANALYTICS

Ontological representation of social relationships involves using formal, machine-readable representations of concepts and relationships in a social network domain. This includes defining concepts such as individuals, groups, and relationships, and the relationships between these concepts.

The key features of ontological representation of social relationships include:
1. Concept definitions: definitions of the concepts and entities in a social network domain, such as individuals, groups, and relationships.
2. Relationships: definitions of the relationships between concepts, such as is-a-friend-of, is-a-member-of, and has-a-relationship-with.
3. Axioms: logical statements that describe the relationships between concepts and provide additional information about the social network domain.

Ontological representation of social relationships enables the modeling of complex social relationships, such as friendships, group memberships, and interactions, in a structured and machine-readable format. This enables the analysis of social network data and the discovery of patterns and relationships in the data.

In summary, Ontological representation of social relationships involves using formal, machine-readable representations of concepts and relationships in a social network domain, including concept definitions, relationships, and axioms, to enable the modeling and analysis of complex social relationships in a structured and machine-readable format.
