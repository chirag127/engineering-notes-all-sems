### Types of Analytical Techniques in BI –Descriptive for the notes of the Unit 4 - BI – DATA ANALYTICS PROCESS in the subject of BUSINESS INTELLIGENCE AND ANALYTICS KCS

Descriptive analytics is a type of analytical technique used in Business Intelligence (BI) to summarize and describe data. The goal of descriptive analytics is to provide an overview of the data, to help understand its characteristics and patterns, and to identify trends and relationships. Descriptive analytics is used to answer questions about what has happened and why it happened.

Examples of descriptive analytics techniques include:

1. Frequency distributions: A tabular representation of the number of occurrences of each value in a dataset.

2. Measures of central tendency: Mean, median, and mode, which provide a summary of the central location of the data.

3. Measures of dispersion: Range, variance, and standard deviation, which provide a summary of the spread of the data.

4. Box plots: A graphical representation of the distribution of a dataset, which provides information about the median, quartiles, and outliers.

5. Histograms: A graphical representation of the frequency distribution of a dataset, which provides information about the shape of the distribution.

In conclusion, descriptive analytics is a type of analytical technique used in Business Intelligence to summarize and describe data. Descriptive analytics is used to answer questions about what has happened and why it happened, and it provides an overview of the data, its characteristics, and its patterns. Understanding descriptive analytics is important for making informed decisions based on data, and for identifying trends and relationships in the data.
