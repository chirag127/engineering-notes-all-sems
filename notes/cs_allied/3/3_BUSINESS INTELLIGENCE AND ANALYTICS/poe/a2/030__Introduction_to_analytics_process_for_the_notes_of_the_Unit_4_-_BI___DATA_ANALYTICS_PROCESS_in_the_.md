### Introduction to analytics process for the notes of the Unit 4 - BI – DATA ANALYTICS PROCESS in the subject of BUSINESS INTELLIGENCE AND ANALYTICS KCS

1. Define the business problem: Identify the business problem or opportunity where analytics can provide a solution. This could be something like "increase sales", "reduce churn", "optimize marketing spend" etc.
2. Determine the metrics: Identify the key metrics that need to be measured to determine if the solution to the business problem is working. For example, if the problem is "increase sales", then metrics could be "monthly sales", "new customers", "customer spend" etc.
3. Data collection: Gather the required data that will be needed to analyze the metrics and solve the business problem. The data could be internal data like sales data or external data like industry trends.
4. Data preparation: Prepare the data by cleaning, formatting, aggregating or enriching it as required to make it analytical-ready.
5. Model planning: Determine the analytical approach that will be used - this could be predictive analytics, prescriptive analytics, descriptive analytics or a combination of approaches.
6. Model building: Build the analytical models that will be used to analyze the data and provide insights. This could involve data mining, machine learning or statistical techniques based on the type of analytics.
7. Evaluation: Evaluate the output of the analytical models to determine if they are accurately solving the business problem and providing reliable insights and recommendations.
8. Implementation: Implement the insights or recommendations from the analytics by deploying models or taking actions.
9. Monitoring: Monitor the analytics solution to ensure continued accuracy and make improvements as needed. The metrics and data may change over time so the analytics needs to be monitored to sustain the business impacts.
