
### Functionalities & Classifications of Data Mining

Data mining is a process of extracting useful information from large datasets. It involves the use of algorithms to discover patterns and relationships in the data. Data mining can be used for a variety of applications, such as predicting customer behavior, analyzing market trends, and uncovering fraud.

Data mining has two main functionalities:

1. **Classification**: Classification is the process of assigning data points to predefined categories or classes. This can be used to identify customers who are likely to purchase a particular product, or to detect fraudulent transactions.

2. **Clustering**: Clustering is the process of grouping data points based on their similarity. This can be used to identify customer segments, or to identify patterns in the data that can be used to make decisions.

Data mining can also be classified according to the type of data used.

1. **Structured Data Mining**: Structured data mining uses structured data, such as relational databases, to identify patterns and relationships.

2. **Unstructured Data Mining**: Unstructured data mining uses unstructured data, such as text, images, and audio, to identify patterns and relationships.

Data mining can also be classified according to the type of analysis used.

1. **Descriptive Data Mining**: Descriptive data mining is used to describe the data, such as identifying clusters or patterns.

2. **Predictive Data Mining**: Predictive data mining is used to predict future events, such as customer behavior or market trends.

Data mining can also be classified according to the type of algorithm used.

1. **Supervised Learning**: Supervised learning algorithms use labeled data, such as customer data with labels indicating whether or not they purchased a product, to make predictions.

2. **Unsupervised Learning**: Unsupervised learning algorithms use unlabeled data, such as customer data without labels, to make predictions.
