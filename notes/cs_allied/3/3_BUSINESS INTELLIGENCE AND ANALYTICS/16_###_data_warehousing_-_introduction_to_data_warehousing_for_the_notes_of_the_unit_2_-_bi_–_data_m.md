### Data Warehousing - Introduction to Data Warehousing for the notes of the Unit 2 - BI – DATA MINING & WAREHOUSING in the subject of BUSINESS INTELLIGENCE AND ANALYTICS KCS

Data Warehousing is a process of collecting and storing large amounts of structured data from various sources for reporting and analysis purposes. It is a centralized repository of data that is designed to support business intelligence (BI) activities, such as data mining, online analytical processing (OLAP), and reporting.

The main goal of data warehousing is to provide a single version of the truth for an organization. This means that all of the data in the data warehouse is consistent and accurate, and that it is easily accessible to decision-makers.

Data warehousing involves several steps, including data extraction, transformation, and loading (ETL), data modeling, and data storage. In the ETL process, data is extracted from various sources, transformed into a consistent format, and loaded into the data warehouse. Data modeling involves designing the structure of the data warehouse, including the relationships between tables and the attributes of each table. Data storage involves choosing a database management system (DBMS) and designing the physical architecture of the data warehouse.

Data warehousing is used in a variety of industries, including healthcare, finance, retail, and government. It is particularly useful for organizations that have large amounts of data and need to make decisions based on that data.

In conclusion, data warehousing is a critical component of business intelligence and analytics. It provides a centralized repository of data that is consistent, accurate, and easily accessible to decision-makers. By using data warehousing, organizations can make better decisions, improve their operations, and gain a competitive advantage.
