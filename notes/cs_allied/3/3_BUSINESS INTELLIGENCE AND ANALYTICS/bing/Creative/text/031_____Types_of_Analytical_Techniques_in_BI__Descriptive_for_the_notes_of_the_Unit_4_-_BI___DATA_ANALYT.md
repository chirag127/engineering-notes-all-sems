### Types of Analytical Techniques in BI –Descriptive

- Descriptive analytics is the process of summarizing and visualizing historical data to provide insights into what has happened in the past.
- Descriptive analytics is the most basic and common type of analytics, and it is often the first step in any data analysis project.
- Descriptive analytics can help answer questions such as: How many customers visited our website last month? What are the most popular products or services we offer? How satisfied are our customers with our service?
- Descriptive analytics can also help identify patterns, trends, outliers, and anomalies in the data, which can be useful for further analysis or decision making.
- The historical data collection for descriptive analytics is done using two main techniques – data aggregation and data mining.
  - Data aggregation is the process of collecting and organizing data into manageable data sets, such as tables, charts, graphs, or dashboards.
  - Data mining is the process of applying various tools and methods to analyze the data and extract useful information, such as summary statistics, correlations, or associations.
- Some of the common tools and methods used for descriptive analytics are:
  - Descriptive statistics, such as mean, median, mode, standard deviation, variance, frequency, or percentage, which measure the central tendency, dispersion, or distribution of the data.
  - Data visualization, such as histograms, bar charts, pie charts, line charts, scatter plots, or heat maps, which display the data in graphical or pictorial forms to enhance understanding and communication.
  - Data exploration, such as filtering, sorting, grouping, or pivoting, which allow the user to manipulate and interact with the data to discover patterns or insights.
  - Data quality, such as data cleaning, data validation, data transformation, or data integration, which ensure the data is accurate, consistent, complete, and relevant for the analysis.
- Descriptive analytics can provide valuable information for business intelligence and analytics, but it has some limitations, such as:
  - It cannot explain why something happened or what will happen in the future, which requires more advanced types of analytics, such as predictive or prescriptive analytics.
  - It can be influenced by bias, noise, or errors in the data, which can affect the validity and reliability of the results.
  - It can be overwhelming or misleading if the data is too large, complex, or diverse, which requires proper data management and interpretation skills.
