### Role of mathematical models in business intelligence

- A mathematical model is an abstract representation of a real-world system or problem that uses mathematical language and symbols to describe its behavior and outcomes.
- A mathematical model can help in business intelligence by providing decision makers with information and knowledge extracted from data, through the application of mathematical models and algorithms .
- Some of the benefits of using mathematical models in business intelligence are:
  - They can simplify complex and uncertain situations by focusing on the most relevant variables and relationships.
  - They can support logical and rational decision making by providing quantitative and objective measures of performance and trade-offs.
  - They can enable scenario analysis and sensitivity analysis by allowing the manipulation of input parameters and assumptions to explore different outcomes and risks.
  - They can facilitate optimization and learning by finding the best or optimal solutions to the problem or improving the solutions over time based on feedback and data.
- Some of the challenges of using mathematical models in business intelligence are:
  - They can be difficult to develop and validate, requiring a lot of data, expertise, and computational resources.
  - They can be limited by the quality and availability of data, the accuracy and completeness of the assumptions, and the complexity and uncertainty of the system or problem.
  - They can be misinterpreted or misused, leading to erroneous or biased conclusions or decisions.
- Some of the types of mathematical models used in business intelligence are:
  - Descriptive models: These models describe the current or past state of the system or problem, such as statistics, charts, graphs, dashboards, etc.
  - Predictive models: These models predict the future or unknown state of the system or problem, such as regression, classification, forecasting, etc.
  - Prescriptive models: These models prescribe the best or optimal actions or decisions for the system or problem, such as optimization, simulation, decision analysis, etc.
