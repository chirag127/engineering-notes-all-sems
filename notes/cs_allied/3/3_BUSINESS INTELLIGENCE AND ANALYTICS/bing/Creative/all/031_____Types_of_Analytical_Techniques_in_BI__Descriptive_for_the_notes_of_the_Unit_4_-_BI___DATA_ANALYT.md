# Types of Analytical Techniques in BI – Descriptive

Descriptive analytics is the process of summarizing and visualizing historical data to provide insights into what has happened in the past. It is the most basic and common type of analytics used by businesses to understand their performance, customers, operations, and markets. Descriptive analytics can help answer questions such as:

- How many sales did we make last month?
- What are the most popular products or services among our customers?
- How satisfied are our customers with our service quality?
- What are the main sources of revenue and cost for our business?
- How does our performance compare to our competitors or industry benchmarks?

The historical data collection for descriptive analytics is done using two main techniques – data aggregation and data mining. Data aggregation is the process of collecting and organizing data into manageable data sets that can be analyzed and reported. Data mining is the process of applying various tools and methods to extract patterns, trends, and insights from the data. Some of the techniques used in data mining are:

- Summary statistics: These are numerical measures that describe the characteristics of a data set, such as mean, median, mode, standard deviation, variance, etc.
- Frequency distribution: This is a table or graph that shows how often each value or category occurs in a data set.
- Cross-tabulation: This is a table that shows the relationship between two or more variables or categories in a data set.
- Correlation: This is a measure of how strongly two variables are related to each other, ranging from -1 (negative correlation) to +1 (positive correlation).
- Regression: This is a method of modeling the relationship between a dependent variable and one or more independent variables, and predicting the value of the dependent variable based on the values of the independent variables.
- Clustering: This is a method of grouping data points that are similar to each other based on some criteria or distance measure.
- Classification: This is a method of assigning data points to predefined categories or classes based on some rules or criteria.
- Association: This is a method of finding patterns or rules that describe the co-occurrence or relationship between items or events in a data set.

Descriptive analytics can be presented in various formats, such as tables, charts, graphs, dashboards, reports, etc. The choice of format depends on the purpose, audience, and context of the analysis. Some of the benefits of descriptive analytics are:

- It can help identify patterns, trends, and outliers in the data that may not be obvious otherwise.
- It can help monitor and measure the performance and progress of the business against its goals and objectives.
- It can help provide a baseline or benchmark for future analysis and decision making.
- It can help communicate and share the results and insights of the analysis with stakeholders and customers.

Some of the limitations of descriptive analytics are:

- It can only describe what has happened in the past, but not why it happened or what will happen in the future.
- It can be influenced by the quality, accuracy, and completeness of the data, as well as the assumptions and biases of the analyst.
- It can be overwhelming or confusing if the data is too complex, large, or diverse, or if the analysis is not clear, concise, or relevant.
