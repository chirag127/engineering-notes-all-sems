# Functionalities and Classifications of Data Mining

Data mining is the process of discovering useful patterns and insights from large and complex data sets. Data mining can help organizations make better decisions, improve customer satisfaction, enhance operational efficiency, and gain competitive advantage.

Data mining functionalities are used to represent the type of patterns that have to be discovered in data mining tasks. In general, data mining tasks can be classified into two types including descriptive and predictive.

- Descriptive mining tasks define the common features of the data in the database and summarize the characteristics of the data. Examples of descriptive mining tasks are:

  - Association analysis: finding frequent patterns, associations, or correlations among a set of items or attributes in a transaction database, such as market basket analysis.
  - Clustering analysis: grouping a set of data objects into clusters such that objects within a cluster are similar to one another and dissimilar to objects in other clusters.
  - Outlier analysis: identifying data objects that deviate significantly from the normal behavior or the expected pattern, such as fraud detection.
  - Summarization: providing a concise and general description of the data, such as data visualization, report generation, or statistical analysis.

- Predictive mining tasks act inference on the current data in order to make predictions or estimations for future or unknown data. Examples of predictive mining tasks are:

  - Classification: determining a model that describes and differentiates data classes with the intention of using it to predict a class of unknown or anonymous objects, such as spam filtering, credit risk analysis, or medical diagnosis .
  - Regression: determining a model that predicts a continuous-valued function or ordered value, such as sales forecasting, stock market analysis, or weather prediction.
  - Time series analysis: analyzing and modeling temporal data, such as trend analysis, seasonal analysis, or anomaly detection.

Data mining can also perform other types of tasks, such as:

- Data integration: combining data from multiple sources into a coherent data store, such as a data warehouse.
- Data cleaning: removing noise, inconsistencies, or errors from the data.
- Data transformation: converting the data into appropriate formats or structures for the mining process.
- Data evaluation: assessing the quality, validity, or usefulness of the mining results.
- Data presentation: presenting the mining results to the users or stakeholders in an understandable and actionable way, such as dashboards, reports, or charts.

Data mining can be applied to various domains and industries, such as:

- Business: customer relationship management, market segmentation, cross-selling, fraud detection, risk management, etc.
- Education: student performance analysis, curriculum development, course recommendation, etc.
- Healthcare: disease diagnosis, drug discovery, medical image analysis, etc.
- Science: bioinformatics, astronomy, physics, chemistry, etc.
- Government: crime analysis, tax evasion detection, public safety, etc.
- Social media: sentiment analysis, recommender systems, social network analysis, etc.

Data mining is a key component of business intelligence and analytics, which aim to provide data-driven insights and solutions for various organizational problems and opportunities. Data mining tools are built into executive dashboards, harvesting insight from Big Data, including data from social media, Internet of Things (IoT) sensor feeds, location-aware devices, unstructured text, video, and more.
