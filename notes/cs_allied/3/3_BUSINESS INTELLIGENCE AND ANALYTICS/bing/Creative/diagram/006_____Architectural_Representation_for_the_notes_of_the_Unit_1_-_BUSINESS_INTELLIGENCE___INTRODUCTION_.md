### Architectural Representation

- Architectural representation is the process of designing and communicating the structure, components, and relationships of a business intelligence (BI) system.
- A BI system is a collection of technologies and practices that enable an organization to collect, integrate, store, analyze, and present data and information to support decision making and performance improvement.
- A BI architecture is the framework that defines the standards, principles, and guidelines for the BI system, as well as the specific platforms and tools that are used to implement it.
- A BI architecture can be represented using various methods and models, such as diagrams, charts, matrices, documents, and narratives, depending on the purpose, audience, and level of detail required.
- A BI architecture can be divided into several layers or components, such as data sources, data integration, data warehouse, data marts, data analysis, data visualization, and data delivery.
- A BI architecture can also be classified into different types or styles, such as centralized, decentralized, federated, hub-and-spoke, or hybrid, depending on the degree of integration, autonomy, and governance of the BI components.
- A BI architecture can be influenced by various factors, such as business requirements, data characteristics, technology trends, organizational culture, and best practices.
- A BI architecture can provide various benefits, such as improving data quality, consistency, and availability, enhancing data analysis and reporting capabilities, facilitating data governance and security, and enabling scalability and flexibility of the BI system.

Some examples of architectural representation for BI are:

- A data flow diagram (DFD) that shows the movement and transformation of data from the source systems to the target systems in the BI system.
- A star schema diagram that shows the logical structure and relationships of the data warehouse and data marts in the BI system, using facts and dimensions tables.
- A dashboard that shows the graphical presentation and interaction of key performance indicators (KPIs) and metrics derived from the data analysis in the BI system.
- A reference architecture that shows the generic or recommended components and configurations of a BI system, based on industry standards or best practices.
