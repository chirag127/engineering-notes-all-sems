### Role of mathematical models in business intelligence

- A mathematical model is an abstract representation of a real-world system or problem that uses mathematical language and symbols to describe its behavior and outcomes.
- A mathematical model can be classified into different types, such as deterministic or stochastic, linear or nonlinear, discrete or continuous, static or dynamic, etc., depending on the assumptions and methods used to construct it.
- A business intelligence system provides decision makers with information and knowledge extracted from data, through the application of mathematical models and algorithms .
- The role of mathematical models in business intelligence is to:
  - Understand the function and structure of the system or problem under study, such as the relationships, dependencies, constraints, and objectives involved.
  - Analyze the data and identify patterns, trends, anomalies, and insights that can support decision making and action taking.
  - Predict the future behavior and outcomes of the system or problem, based on historical data, current conditions, and possible scenarios.
  - Optimize the performance and efficiency of the system or problem, by finding the best or most desirable solutions, strategies, or policies that satisfy the objectives and constraints.
  - Learn from the data and the feedback from the system or problem, by updating and improving the model and its parameters over time, based on new data and evidence.
- Some examples of mathematical models used in business intelligence are:
  - Regression models, which estimate the relationship between a dependent variable and one or more independent variables, such as sales, price, and advertising.
  - Classification models, which assign a label or category to an observation, such as customer churn, fraud detection, or sentiment analysis.
  - Clustering models, which group similar observations together, such as customer segmentation, market basket analysis, or recommender systems.
  - Optimization models, which find the optimal or near-optimal solutions to a problem, such as resource allocation, scheduling, or portfolio selection.
  - Simulation models, which mimic the behavior and outcomes of a system or problem, such as risk analysis, scenario planning, or forecasting.
  - Machine learning models, which learn from data and make predictions or decisions, such as neural networks, decision trees, or support vector machines.
