### Architectural Representation

- Architectural representation is the process of describing the structure, behavior, and components of a system using diagrams, models, and symbols.
- Architectural representation helps to communicate the design and functionality of a system to different stakeholders, such as developers, users, managers, and customers.
- Architectural representation also helps to analyze the system's requirements, performance, scalability, security, and reliability.
- Architectural representation can be done at different levels of abstraction, such as conceptual, logical, physical, and implementation.
- Architectural representation can use different notations and standards, such as Unified Modeling Language (UML), Business Process Model and Notation (BPMN), Entity-Relationship (ER) model, and Data Flow Diagram (DFD).

#### Conceptual Level

- The conceptual level of architectural representation focuses on the high-level overview and goals of the system, without going into the details of how the system is implemented or operated.
- The conceptual level of architectural representation answers the questions of what, why, and who of the system, such as what are the main functions and features of the system, why the system is needed, and who are the users and stakeholders of the system.
- The conceptual level of architectural representation can use diagrams such as use case diagrams, activity diagrams, and stakeholder maps to show the interactions and relationships between the system and its environment.

#### Logical Level

- The logical level of architectural representation focuses on the functional and behavioral aspects of the system, such as how the system processes data, performs tasks, and responds to events.
- The logical level of architectural representation answers the questions of how and when of the system, such as how the system components communicate and collaborate, and when the system transitions between different states and scenarios.
- The logical level of architectural representation can use diagrams such as class diagrams, sequence diagrams, state diagrams, and business process diagrams to show the structure and dynamics of the system.

#### Physical Level

- The physical level of architectural representation focuses on the technical and operational aspects of the system, such as how the system is deployed, configured, and maintained.
- The physical level of architectural representation answers the questions of where and how much of the system, such as where the system components are located and connected, and how much resources and capacity the system requires and consumes.
- The physical level of architectural representation can use diagrams such as deployment diagrams, component diagrams, network diagrams, and performance diagrams to show the distribution and allocation of the system.

#### Implementation Level

- The implementation level of architectural representation focuses on the detailed and specific aspects of the system, such as how the system is coded, tested, and documented.
- The implementation level of architectural representation answers the questions of how exactly and how well of the system, such as how exactly the system components are implemented and integrated, and how well the system meets the quality and compliance standards.
- The implementation level of architectural representation can use diagrams such as code diagrams, test diagrams, and documentation diagrams to show the realization and verification of the system.
