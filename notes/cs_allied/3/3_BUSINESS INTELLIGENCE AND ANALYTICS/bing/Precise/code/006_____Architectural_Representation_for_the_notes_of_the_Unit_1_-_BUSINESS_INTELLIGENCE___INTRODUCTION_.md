### Architectural Representation

Architectural representation is the graphical representation of an architectural design. It is used to communicate design ideas and concepts to others, including clients, contractors, and other members of the design team. Architectural representation can take many forms, including drawings, models, and computer-generated images.

1. **Drawings**: Drawings are the most common form of architectural representation. They can be created by hand or using computer-aided design (CAD) software. Drawings can include floor plans, elevations, sections, and details.
2. **Models**: Physical models are another common form of architectural representation. They are three-dimensional representations of a design, created using materials such as cardboard, foam board, or 3D printing. Models can be used to study the form and massing of a design, as well as to communicate design ideas to others.
3. **Computer-Generated Images**: Computer-generated images (CGIs) are digital representations of a design, created using computer software. CGIs can include still images, animations, and virtual reality experiences. They can be used to create photorealistic representations of a design, allowing clients and other members of the design team to visualize the finished project.

Architectural representation is an important part of the design process, as it allows architects to communicate their ideas and concepts to others. It is also an important tool for studying and refining a design, as well as for presenting a design to clients and other stakeholders.
