## Unit 1 - BUSINESS INTELLIGENCE – INTRODUCTION

- Business intelligence (BI) is software that ingests business data and presents it in user-friendly views such as reports, dashboards, charts and graphs .
- BI tools enable business users to access different types of data — historical and current, third-party and in-house, as well as semi-structured data and unstructured data like social media.
- BI refers to the procedural and technical infrastructure that collects, stores, and analyzes the data produced by a company’s activities.
- BI is a broad term that encompasses data mining, process analysis, performance benchmarking, and descriptive analytics.
- BI helps businesses and organizations ask and answer questions of their data, and use the insights to inform business decisions .
- BI can provide benefits such as improved efficiency, reduced costs, increased revenue, better customer satisfaction, and competitive advantage .
- There are three major types of BI analysis, which cover many different needs and uses:
  - Descriptive analysis: This type of analysis answers the question of what happened in the past and why. It uses historical data to summarize trends, patterns, and outliers. Examples of descriptive analysis are sales reports, customer feedback, and inventory management.
  - Predictive analysis: This type of analysis answers the question of what is likely to happen in the future and why. It uses statistical models and machine learning to forecast outcomes and probabilities based on historical and current data. Examples of predictive analysis are demand forecasting, risk assessment, and churn prediction.
  - Prescriptive analysis: This type of analysis answers the question of what should be done to achieve a desired goal or outcome. It uses optimization techniques and simulation to recommend actions and evaluate their impact. Examples of prescriptive analysis are pricing optimization, resource allocation, and route planning.
