- Business Intelligence (BI) is a set of processes, architectures, and technologies that convert raw data into meaningful information that drives profitable business actions.
- A business intelligence architecture articulates the technology standards and data management and analytics practices that support an organization's BI efforts, as well as the specific platforms and tools that will be deployed.
- A typical BI architecture consists of the following components:
  - Data sources: These are the original sources of data, such as databases, files, web services, etc. that contain the raw data to be analyzed.
  - Data integration: This is the process of extracting, transforming, and loading (ETL) data from different sources into a centralized data warehouse or data lake, where it can be stored and organized for analysis.
  - Data warehouse or data lake: This is the central repository of data that has been integrated, cleansed, and structured for analysis. A data warehouse follows a relational model, while a data lake follows a schema-on-read model.
  - Data marts: These are subsets of data from the data warehouse or data lake that are tailored for specific business domains or analytical purposes. They can be organized by subject areas, such as sales, marketing, finance, etc.
  - Data analysis and reporting: This is the process of applying various analytical techniques and tools to the data in the data marts to generate insights, reports, dashboards, visualizations, etc. that can help answer business questions and support decision-making.
  - Data delivery: This is the process of delivering the data analysis and reporting results to the end users, such as business managers, analysts, executives, etc. This can be done through various channels, such as web portals, mobile devices, email, etc.

- Business intelligence architectures can vary depending on the size, complexity, and needs of the organization. Some of the factors that can influence the design of a BI architecture are:
  - The volume, variety, and velocity of data
  - The frequency and latency of data integration and analysis
  - The types and levels of analysis and reporting required
  - The number and diversity of data sources and users
  - The security and governance policies and standards
  - The available budget and resources
- Ethics and business intelligence are also important aspects to consider when designing and implementing a BI architecture. Some of the ethical issues that can arise in BI are:
  - Data privacy and security: How to protect the confidentiality and integrity of the data and the users' personal information
  - Data quality and accuracy: How to ensure the reliability and validity of the data and the analysis results
  - Data ownership and access: How to determine who owns the data and who can access and use it
  - Data bias and discrimination: How to avoid or mitigate the potential biases and prejudices that can affect the data and the analysis outcomes
  - Data accountability and transparency: How to monitor and audit the data and the analysis processes and report the results and the assumptions
