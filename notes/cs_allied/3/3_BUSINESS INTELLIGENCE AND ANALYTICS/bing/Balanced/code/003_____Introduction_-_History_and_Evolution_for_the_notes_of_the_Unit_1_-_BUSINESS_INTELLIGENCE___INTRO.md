### Introduction - History and Evolution

- Business intelligence (BI) is the process of collecting, analyzing, and presenting data to support decision making in various domains of business.
- The term BI was coined in 1989 by Howard Dresner, but the concept of using information to gain a competitive advantage dates back to the 19th century .
- The evolution of BI can be divided into four stages   :
  - Pre-digital era: Before the invention of computers, BI was based on manual methods of gathering and processing data, such as surveys, reports, and statistics. Some examples of BI pioneers in this era are Richard Millar Devens, who described how a banker used market trends to succeed in his book "Cyclopaedia of Commercial and Business Anecdotes" in 1865, and Henry Ford, who used assembly line data to optimize his production in the early 20th century.
  - Early digital era: With the advent of computers and databases in the 1950s and 1960s, BI became more automated and efficient. Some examples of BI innovations in this era are the first commercial database management system (DBMS) by IBM in 1966, the first decision support system (DSS) by General Electric in 1968, and the first executive information system (EIS) by Rockwell in 1979.
  - Modern digital era: With the emergence of the internet and data warehousing in the 1980s and 1990s, BI became more accessible and scalable. Some examples of BI developments in this era are the first online analytical processing (OLAP) tool by Express in 1980, the first data warehouse by IBM in 1983, the first data mining tool by IBM in 1989, and the first web-based BI tool by MicroStrategy in 1996.
  - Future digital era: With the advancement of artificial intelligence (AI) and machine learning (ML) in the 21st century, BI became more intelligent and automated. Some examples of BI trends in this era are the first self-service BI tool by Tableau in 2003, the first cloud-based BI tool by Birst in 2005, the first natural language processing (NLP) tool by Qlik in 2014, and the first automated machine learning (AutoML) tool by DataRobot in 2015.

- The main benefits of BI are to improve business performance, enhance customer satisfaction, reduce costs, increase revenues, and gain a competitive edge  .
- The main challenges of BI are to ensure data quality, security, and privacy, to cope with the increasing volume, variety, and velocity of data, to integrate data from multiple sources and formats, to align BI with business goals and strategies, and to foster a data-driven culture within the organization  .
