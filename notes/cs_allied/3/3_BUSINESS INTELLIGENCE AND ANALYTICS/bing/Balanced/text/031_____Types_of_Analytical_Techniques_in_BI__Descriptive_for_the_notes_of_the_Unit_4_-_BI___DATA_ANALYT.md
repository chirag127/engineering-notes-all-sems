### Types of Analytical Techniques in BI –Descriptive

- Descriptive analytics is the process of summarizing and visualizing historical data to provide insights into what has happened in the past.
- Descriptive analytics is the most basic and common type of analytics, and it is often the first step in any data analysis project.
- Descriptive analytics can help answer questions such as: How many customers visited our website last month? What are the most popular products or services we offer? How satisfied are our customers with our service?
- Descriptive analytics can also help identify patterns, trends, outliers, and correlations in the data, which can be useful for further analysis or decision making.
- The historical data collection for descriptive analytics is done using two main techniques – data aggregation and data mining.
  - Data aggregation is the process of collecting and organizing data into manageable data sets, such as tables, charts, graphs, or dashboards.
  - Data mining is the process of applying various tools and methods to analyze the data and extract useful information, such as summary statistics, frequency distributions, or clustering algorithms.
- Some of the benefits of descriptive analytics are:
  - It can help understand the current state and performance of the business or organization.
  - It can help identify strengths, weaknesses, opportunities, and threats in the business environment.
  - It can help communicate and share information with stakeholders, customers, or partners.
  - It can help establish benchmarks and goals for future improvement or growth.
- Some of the challenges or limitations of descriptive analytics are:
  - It cannot explain why something happened or what will happen in the future.
  - It can be influenced by biases, errors, or outliers in the data collection or analysis process.
  - It can be overwhelming or confusing if the data is too complex, large, or diverse.
  - It can be misleading or inaccurate if the data is not representative, relevant, or reliable.
