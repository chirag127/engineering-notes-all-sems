Sockets are a mechanism for inter-process communication (IPC) that are widely used in embedded systems and real-time operating systems (RTOS). Sockets provide a standardized interface for communication between processes, allowing processes to send and receive data over a network, or between processes on the same device.

Sockets are used to implement a variety of communication protocols, including TCP (Transmission Control Protocol) and UDP (User Datagram Protocol), which are widely used in embedded systems and the Internet of Things (IoT).

In an RTOS, sockets are typically implemented as a library or system call, and provide a set of functions for creating, binding, listening, and accepting connections, as well as for sending and receiving data. Sockets are used to implement a wide range of network-based applications, including web servers, file transfer protocols, and remote procedure calls (RPCs).

In the context of embedded systems and real-time operating systems, it is important to understand the role of sockets in inter-process communication, and to be familiar with the different types of sockets and the communication protocols they can be used to implement.

Overall, sockets are an important mechanism for inter-process communication in embedded systems and real-time operating systems, and are an important aspect of the study of embedded systems and real-time operating systems.
