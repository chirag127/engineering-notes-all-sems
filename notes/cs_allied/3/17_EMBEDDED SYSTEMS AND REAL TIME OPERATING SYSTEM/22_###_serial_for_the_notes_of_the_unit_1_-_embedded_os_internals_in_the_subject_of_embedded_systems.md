Serial communication is a method of transmitting data one bit at a time over a communication channel, such as a cable or a wireless connection. In the context of embedded systems and real-time operating systems, serial communication is used to transmit data between microcontrollers and other devices, such as computers, sensors, and actuators.

Serial communication is used in embedded systems and real-time operating systems because it is simple, low-cost, and efficient. Serial communication can be used to transmit data over short distances, such as between a microcontroller and a computer, or over long distances, such as between two microcontrollers located in different parts of a building.

Serial communication can be implemented using a variety of protocols, such as RS-232, RS-485, and USB. Each protocol has its own advantages and disadvantages, and the choice between protocols depends on the specific requirements of the application. For example, RS-485 is well-suited for applications that require long-distance communication, while USB is well-suited for applications that require high-speed communication.
