### Dynamic Versus Static Systems for the notes of the Unit 2 - Real Time Scheduling in the subject of Real Time System

Dynamic and Static Systems refer to the behavior of real-time systems with respect to changes in their environment.

1. Definition: Dynamic and Static Systems refer to the behavior of real-time systems with respect to changes in their environment. A dynamic system is one that changes over time, while a static system remains constant.

2. Dynamic Systems: Dynamic systems are characterized by changes in their environment that require the system to adapt and respond. Examples of dynamic systems include real-time control systems and real-time communication systems.

3. Static Systems: Static systems are characterized by a constant environment that does not change over time. Examples of static systems include batch processing systems and non-real-time control systems.

4. Advantages of Dynamic Systems: Dynamic systems have several advantages, including the ability to respond to changes in the environment, the ability to adapt to changing conditions, and the ability to handle unpredictable events.

5. Advantages of Static Systems: Static systems have several advantages, including the ability to perform predictable and repetitive tasks, the ability to operate with a high degree of accuracy, and the ability to operate efficiently.

6. Limitations of Dynamic Systems: Despite their advantages, dynamic systems also have several limitations, including the difficulty of predicting and handling changes in the environment, the difficulty of ensuring real-time performance, and the difficulty of ensuring consistency and reliability.

7. Limitations of Static Systems: Despite their advantages, static systems also have several limitations, including the difficulty of adapting to changing conditions, the difficulty of handling unpredictable events, and the difficulty of ensuring real-time performance.

8. Applications: Dynamic and Static Systems are used in a wide range of real-time systems, including control systems, communication systems, and batch processing systems.

In conclusion, Dynamic and Static Systems refer to the behavior of real-time systems with respect to changes in their environment. Dynamic systems are characterized by changes in their environment that require the system to adapt and respond, while static systems are characterized by a constant environment that does not change over time. Both dynamic and static systems have advantages and limitations, and are used in a wide range of real-time systems.
