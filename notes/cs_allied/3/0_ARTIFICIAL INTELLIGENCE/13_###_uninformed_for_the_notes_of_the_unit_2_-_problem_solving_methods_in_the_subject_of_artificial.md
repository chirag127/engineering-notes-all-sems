Uninformed Search in Artificial Intelligence:
- Uninformed search is a type of search algorithm in artificial intelligence that does not use any additional information about the problem being solved.
- Uninformed search algorithms rely solely on the structure of the problem and the initial state to find a solution.
- Examples of uninformed search algorithms include:
  1. Breadth-first search: a search algorithm that explores all the nodes at a given depth before moving on to the next depth.
  2. Depth-first search: a search algorithm that explores as far as possible along a branch before backtracking.
  3. Uniform-cost search: a search algorithm that explores nodes in order of increasing cost, where the cost is a measure of the cost of reaching the node from the initial state.
- Uninformed search algorithms are simple and easy to implement, but they are not always efficient, as they may need to explore a large number of nodes before finding a solution.
- Understanding uninformed search is important for the design and development of artificial intelligence systems that can solve problems efficiently and effectively.
