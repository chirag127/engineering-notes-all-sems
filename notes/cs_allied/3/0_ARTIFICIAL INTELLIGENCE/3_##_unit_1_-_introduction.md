Unit 1: Introduction

Real-Time Systems

Real-Time Systems are computer systems that must respond to events within a specified time constraint. Real-time systems are used in a variety of applications, including control systems, safety-critical systems, and multimedia systems.

The key characteristics of real-time systems include determinism, predictability, and responsiveness. Determinism refers to the ability of the system to produce the same results for the same inputs. Predictability refers to the ability of the system to meet its timing constraints. Responsiveness refers to the ability of the system to respond to events in a timely manner.

Real-time systems are typically designed to meet specific requirements, including performance requirements, functional requirements, and non-functional requirements. Performance requirements are the requirements for the system to meet its timing constraints and to perform its intended function. Functional requirements are the requirements for the system to perform its intended function. Non-functional requirements are the requirements for the system to meet specific quality attributes, such as reliability, availability, and security.

In conclusion, Real-Time Systems are computer systems that must respond to events within a specified time constraint. The key characteristics of real-time systems include determinism, predictability, and responsiveness, and real-time systems are typically designed to meet specific requirements, including performance requirements, functional requirements, and non-functional requirements. Understanding Real-Time Systems is an important aspect of the subject of Real-Time Systems.
