### Argumentation among Agents

- Agents may disagree on the best course of action due to different perspectives or information. Argumentation refers to the process by which disagreeing agents exchange reasons and evidence to resolve their differences and reach agreement.
- Argumentation allows agents to constructively deal with conflicts by informing and persuading each other. It enables them to overcome differences in beliefs and determine a course of action that has broader acceptance and possibly better outcomes.
- An argumentation framework defines the process of how agents can exchange arguments, including how to present arguments, how to attack or counter arguments, how conflicts get resolved, how status of arguments get determined, etc. There are several standards for argumentation already defined, including ASPIC, ABA, and CARNEADES.
- The key aspects of argumentation include:
    - Claims: Statements that agents make in support of or against a position
    - Evidence: Data or reasons that provide support for claims
    - Counterarguments: Claims or evidence that rebut opposing arguments
    - Acceptance criteria: Standards or thresholds use to accept a claim as justified or an argument as sufficient
    - Burden of proof: Determining which agent holds the burden to provide sufficient evidence or arguments to resolve a dispute.
- The outcome of argumentation among agents may be consensus on a mutually acceptable course of action, continued disagreement but with a better understanding of each other's positions, agreement to escalate the issue to human intervention, or other possibilities. The effectiveness of argumentation relies on the willingness of agents to engage sincerely and constructively with the goal of resolution.
