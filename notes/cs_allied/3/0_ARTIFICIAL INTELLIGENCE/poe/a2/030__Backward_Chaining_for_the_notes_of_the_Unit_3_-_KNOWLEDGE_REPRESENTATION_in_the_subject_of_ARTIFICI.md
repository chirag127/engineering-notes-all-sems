### Backward Chaining for the notes of the Unit 3 - KNOWLEDGE REPRESENTATION in the subject of ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE KCS

- Backward chaining is a inference method that starts with the goal and works backward to find the steps required to achieve it.
- It is a top-down approach. The process starts with the conclusion and works backwards to find the premises needed to support it.
- The inference engine starts with the goal clause and matches it against the condition part (IF-part) of each rule in its knowledge base.
- When a match is found, the rule's action part (THEN-part) becomes the sub-goal, and the process is repeated.
- This process continues until either all goals have been proved or no applicable rule can be found. In the latter case, the initial goal cannot be achieved.
- Backward chaining is useful for troubleshooting or diagnosis problems where the goal is known but the steps to achieve it are not.
- It is useful for expert systems that help users solve problems, make a diagnosis or determine why something has failed.
