### Language Models

- Language models are statistical models that assign probabilities to sequences of words. They are used to predict the next word in a sequence.
- The task is to find the sequence of words that is most likely to occur given the previous words.
- The most common types of language models are:
  - N-gram models: Models that compute the probability of a word based on the previous N words. For example, a bigram model considers the previous word, a trigram model considers the previous two words, etc.
  - Recurrent neural network models: Models that use a recurrent neural network to compute probabilities based on a variable number of previous words. They can capture longer-range dependencies than N-gram models.
- Language models are used in many applications such as speech recognition, machine translation, autocorrect, and autocomplete.
- To evaluate language models, we can use measures like perplexity which evaluates how well a model predicts a held-out test set. Lower perplexity indicates a better language model.
