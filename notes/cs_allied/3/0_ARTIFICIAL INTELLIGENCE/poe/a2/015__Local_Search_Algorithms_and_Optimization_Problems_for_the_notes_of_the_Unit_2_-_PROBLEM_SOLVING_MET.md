### Local Search Algorithms and Optimization Problems

- Local search algorithms start with an initial solution and iteratively move to neighboring solutions that improve the objective function.
- They are easy to implement and can be applied to a wide variety of optimization problems.
- However, they may get stuck in local optima and not find the global optimum.
- Common local search algorithms include:
-- Hill climbing: Move to the best neighbor at each step.
-- Simulated annealing: Allows moves to worse neighbors with a decreasing probability. This can help the search escape local optima.
-- Genetic algorithms: Mimic natural selection and evolution. A population of solutions is evolved over time toward better solutions.
- Common optimization problems that can use local search include:
-- Travelling salesman: Find the shortest route visiting each city once.
-- Scheduling: Assign tasks to machines or time slots to optimize some objective.
-- Layout: Arrange components in two dimensions to optimize wire length, spacing, etc.
-- Parameter tuning: Adjust parameters to optimal values to maximize performance.
