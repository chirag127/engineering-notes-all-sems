
### Stochastic Games

Stochastic games are two-player, zero-sum games in which players take turns making decisions in order to maximize their own rewards. The game is played in a sequence of discrete stages, with each player knowing the actions taken by the other player in all previous stages.

- In a stochastic game, the players have incomplete information about the game's environment and the outcomes of their actions.
- Players must make decisions based on their beliefs about their opponent's strategies and their own objectives.
- Stochastic games are used to model decision-making problems in which the players have different objectives and the environment is uncertain.
- Stochastic games can be used to solve problems such as resource allocation, pricing, and production scheduling.
- Stochastic games can also be used to model situations in which players must make decisions in the face of uncertainty or risk.
- Stochastic games can be solved using dynamic programming, linear programming, and game theory.
