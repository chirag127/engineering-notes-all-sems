### Forward Chaining

- Forward chaining is a method of reasoning in artificial intelligence in which inference rules are applied to existing data to extract additional data until an endpoint (goal) is achieved   .
- In this type of chaining, the inference engine starts by evaluating existing facts, derivations, and conditions before deducing new information .
- Forward chaining is also known as bottom-up reasoning or data-driven reasoning, as it starts from the bottom (data) and moves up to the top (goal).
- Forward chaining is suitable for problems that have a large number of data and a small number of rules, or when the goal is not clearly defined .
- Forward chaining can be illustrated by the following steps :
  - Initialize the working memory with the given facts.
  - Match the facts in the working memory with the conditions of the rules in the knowledge base.
  - Select one or more rules that are triggered by the facts and fire them to generate new facts or actions.
  - Add the new facts or actions to the working memory and update the knowledge base.
  - Repeat steps 2 to 4 until a goal is reached or no more rules can be fired.
- An example of forward chaining is a medical diagnosis system that starts with the symptoms of a patient and applies rules to infer the possible diseases .
