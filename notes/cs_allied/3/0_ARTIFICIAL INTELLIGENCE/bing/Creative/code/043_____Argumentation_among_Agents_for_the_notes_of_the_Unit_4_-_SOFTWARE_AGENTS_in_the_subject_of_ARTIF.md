### Argumentation among Agents

- Argumentation is a process of reasoning and communication that involves the exchange and evaluation of arguments, which are claims supported by reasons and evidence.
- Argumentation can be used to model the internal reasoning of an agent, as well as the interaction among agents who have different beliefs, goals, preferences, or values.
- Argumentation can help agents to resolve conflicts, persuade others, negotiate agreements, justify decisions, explain actions, and coordinate plans.
- Argumentation can also be seen as a form of dialogue, where agents follow certain rules and protocols to engage in a cooperative or competitive exchange of arguments.
- Argumentation in multi-agent systems is a subfield of artificial intelligence that studies the theory and applications of argumentation for designing and implementing intelligent agents that can argue and interact with other agents.
- Argumentation in multi-agent systems draws on various disciplines, such as philosophy, logic, linguistics, psychology, and law, to provide formal models, methods, and tools for representing, generating, evaluating, and communicating arguments.
- Some of the main topics and challenges in argumentation in multi-agent systems are:

  - How to define the syntax and semantics of arguments and their relations, such as attack, defeat, support, or preference.
  - How to design and implement efficient and effective algorithms and mechanisms for generating, selecting, and updating arguments, as well as for determining the acceptability or strength of arguments or sets of arguments.
  - How to specify and analyse different types of argumentation frameworks and systems, such as abstract, structured, bipolar, probabilistic, or value-based argumentation, and how to relate them to other formalisms, such as logic, game theory, or decision theory.
  - How to model and reason about the dynamics and evolution of arguments and argumentation systems, such as how arguments are revised, retracted, or aggregated over time or across agents.
  - How to design and implement different types of argumentation protocols and dialogues, such as inquiry, persuasion, negotiation, deliberation, or eristic dialogues, and how to ensure their properties, such as correctness, completeness, soundness, or termination.
  - How to evaluate and compare the performance and quality of argumentation systems and dialogues, using criteria such as efficiency, effectiveness, robustness, fairness, or social welfare.
  - How to apply argumentation techniques and systems to various domains and scenarios, such as e-commerce, e-government, e-health, e-learning, e-democracy, or e-law.

- Some of the main sources and references for argumentation in multi-agent systems are:

  - Argumentation in Multi-Agent Systems, a series of workshops and books that provide an overview of the state-of-the-art research and applications in the field .
  - Argumentation in Artificial Intelligence, a special issue of the journal Artificial Intelligence that presents a comprehensive survey of the main topics and challenges in argumentation theory and practice.
  - Handbook of Argumentation Theory, a book that covers the historical, philosophical, logical, linguistic, psychological, and computational foundations and perspectives of argumentation.
