# KCS

KCS stands for Knowledge-Centered Service, which is a methodology for creating and maintaining documentation as part of the service delivery process. KCS aims to improve the quality and efficiency of service organizations by capturing, structuring, and reusing the knowledge of service agents and customers. Some of the benefits of KCS are:

- It reduces the need for repeated requests and escalations by providing accurate and consistent information to customers and service agents.
- It increases the productivity and satisfaction of service agents by enabling them to find and share knowledge easily and quickly.
- It enhances the learning and collaboration of service agents by creating a culture of knowledge sharing and feedback.
- It lowers the costs and risks of service delivery by minimizing errors, redundancies, and gaps in knowledge.
- It improves the service levels and loyalty of customers by delivering faster and better solutions to their problems.

KCS is based on four basic principles:

- Integrate: KCS integrates the creation and maintenance of knowledge into the service delivery process, rather than treating it as a separate activity.
- Evolve: KCS evolves the knowledge base continuously based on the demand and usage of knowledge, rather than following a predefined structure or schedule.
- Collaborate: KCS fosters collaboration among service agents and customers by enabling them to contribute, review, and improve knowledge, rather than relying on a centralized authority or expert.
- Reward: KCS rewards service agents and customers for their participation and contribution to the knowledge base, rather than measuring their performance based on other metrics.

KCS follows a double-loop process that consists of two phases:

- Solve: In this phase, service agents use the knowledge base to find and reuse existing solutions, or create new solutions if none exist, for the customer's problem. They also capture the customer's context and feedback, and update the knowledge base accordingly.
- Evolve: In this phase, service agents and customers evaluate the quality and relevance of the knowledge base, and identify and implement improvements based on the usage and demand of knowledge. They also monitor and measure the impact and value of the knowledge base.

KCS is supported by a set of practices, techniques, and tools that help service organizations implement and sustain the methodology. Some of the key elements of KCS are:

- KCS Practices: These are the guidelines and standards that define how service agents and customers should create, find, use, and improve knowledge. They include the KCS methodology, the KCS roles and competencies, the KCS workflow, the KCS content standard, and the KCS quality framework.
- KCS Techniques: These are the methods and skills that service agents and customers should apply to perform the KCS practices effectively and efficiently. They include the KCS search techniques, the KCS capture techniques, the KCS reuse techniques, and the KCS improvement techniques.
- KCS Tools: These are the systems and applications that service agents and customers should use to support the KCS practices and techniques. They include the KCS knowledge base, the KCS search engine, the KCS capture and reuse tools, and the KCS analytics and reporting tools.

KCS is a widely adopted and proven methodology that has been developed and refined by the Consortium for Service Innovation, a non-profit organization that promotes innovation and excellence in service delivery. KCS is also aligned with other service management frameworks, such as ITIL, ISO, and COBIT, and can be integrated with other service delivery processes, such as incident management, problem management, and change management.

KCS is not only a methodology, but also a mindset and a culture that requires service organizations to embrace and adopt the principles and practices of knowledge-centered service. By doing so, service organizations can achieve higher levels of service quality, efficiency, and customer satisfaction.
