# Heuristics for the notes of the Unit 2 - PROBLEM SOLVING METHODS in the subject of ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE KCS

- Heuristics are techniques that are used to solve problems or make decisions in situations where traditional methods are too slow or impractical .
- Heuristics are often used as an approximation to an ideal solution, where the hope is that the approximate solution is good enough for the specific problem at hand.
- Heuristics are an inevitable and inseparable part of artificial intelligence, as they simulate the human thinking process .
- Heuristics are generally used in conjunction with optimization algorithms to enhance the overall efficiency of the desired results .
- Heuristics can be classified into different types, such as:
  - **Constructive heuristics**: These heuristics build a solution incrementally, adding one element at a time, until a complete solution is obtained or a stopping criterion is met. Examples of constructive heuristics are greedy algorithms, hill climbing, and beam search.
  - **Improvement heuristics**: These heuristics start with an initial solution, and then try to modify it or improve it by applying some local changes, until a better solution is found or a stopping criterion is met. Examples of improvement heuristics are simulated annealing, tabu search, and genetic algorithms.
  - **Metaheuristics**: These heuristics are general frameworks that can be applied to different problems, and that combine different heuristics or strategies to explore the solution space and escape from local optima. Examples of metaheuristics are ant colony optimization, particle swarm optimization, and evolutionary computation.
- Heuristics can be evaluated based on different criteria, such as:
  - **Effectiveness**: This criterion measures how well the heuristic can find a good solution, or how close the solution is to the optimal one. Effectiveness can be measured by the objective function value, the error rate, or the accuracy of the solution.
  - **Efficiency**: This criterion measures how fast the heuristic can find a solution, or how much time or resources it consumes. Efficiency can be measured by the running time, the number of iterations, or the memory usage of the heuristic.
  - **Robustness**: This criterion measures how well the heuristic can cope with different instances of the problem, or how sensitive it is to the problem parameters or the initial conditions. Robustness can be measured by the variance, the standard deviation, or the confidence interval of the solution quality.
  - **Simplicity**: This criterion measures how easy the heuristic is to understand, implement, or modify. Simplicity can be measured by the number of lines of code, the number of parameters, or the readability of the heuristic.
