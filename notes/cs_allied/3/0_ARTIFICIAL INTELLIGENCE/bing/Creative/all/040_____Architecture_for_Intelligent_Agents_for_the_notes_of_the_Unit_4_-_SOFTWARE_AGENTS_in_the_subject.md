# Architecture for Intelligent Agents

An intelligent agent is a system that perceives its environment via sensors and acts rationally upon that environment with its effectors. An agent architecture is a framework that defines the components and interactions of an intelligent agent. There are different types of agent architectures, depending on the level of complexity and intelligence of the agent. Some of the common types are:

- **Reactive agents**: Reactive agents are the simplest type of AI agent. They do not have any internal state or memory, and they act only based on the current percept. They do not have any goals or plans, and they do not reason about the consequences of their actions. Reactive agents are fast and robust, but they are limited in their capabilities and adaptability. An example of a reactive agent is a thermostat that adjusts the temperature based on the current reading of the sensor.

- **Model-based reflex agents**: Model-based reflex agents are more sophisticated than reactive agents. They have an internal state that represents some aspects of the environment, and they update this state based on the percepts. They also have a set of rules that specify how to act based on the state. Model-based reflex agents can handle partially observable and dynamic environments, but they still do not have any goals or plans. An example of a model-based reflex agent is a taxi driver that follows the traffic rules and avoids collisions based on the current state of the road.

- **Goal-based agents**: Goal-based agents are more advanced than model-based reflex agents. They have a set of goals that they want to achieve, and they act based on the expected outcomes of their actions. They also have a search algorithm that helps them find the best sequence of actions to reach their goals. Goal-based agents can handle complex and uncertain environments, but they may not always act optimally or efficiently. An example of a goal-based agent is a chess player that tries to win the game by searching for the best moves.

- **Utility-based agents**: Utility-based agents are more refined than goal-based agents. They have a utility function that assigns a numerical value to each state of the environment, and they act based on the expected utility of their actions. They also have a decision-making algorithm that helps them choose the best action to maximize their utility. Utility-based agents can handle multiple and conflicting goals, and they can act rationally and optimally. An example of a utility-based agent is a stock trader that buys and sells stocks based on the expected profit.

- **Learning agents**: Learning agents are the most sophisticated type of AI agent. They have the ability to learn from their experience and improve their performance over time. They also have a learning algorithm that helps them update their knowledge, goals, utility function, or actions based on the feedback from the environment. Learning agents can handle unknown and changing environments, and they can adapt to new situations and tasks. An example of a learning agent is a self-driving car that learns from its own driving and from other drivers.
