## Unit 3 - Knowledge Representation

- Knowledge representation is the process of encoding and storing information in a form that can be used by an intelligent system, such as an AI agent.
- Knowledge representation involves choosing a suitable representation language, a set of symbols and rules for manipulating them, and a knowledge base, a collection of facts and rules about the domain of interest.
- Knowledge representation languages can be classified into two main categories: logic-based and non-logic-based.
- Logic-based languages use formal logic, such as propositional logic, predicate logic, modal logic, etc., to express facts and rules in a precise and unambiguous way. Logic-based languages can support deductive reasoning, the process of deriving new facts from existing facts and rules using logical inference.
- Non-logic-based languages use other methods, such as semantic networks, frames, scripts, production rules, etc., to express facts and rules in a more intuitive and flexible way. Non-logic-based languages can support inductive reasoning, the process of generalizing from specific examples or observations using heuristics or learning algorithms.
- Knowledge representation languages have different advantages and disadvantages, depending on the characteristics of the domain and the task. Some of the criteria for evaluating knowledge representation languages are expressiveness, efficiency, consistency, completeness, and naturalness.
