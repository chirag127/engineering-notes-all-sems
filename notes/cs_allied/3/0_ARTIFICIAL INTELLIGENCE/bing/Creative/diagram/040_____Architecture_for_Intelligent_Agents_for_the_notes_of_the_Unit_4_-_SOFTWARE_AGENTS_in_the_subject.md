### Architecture for Intelligent Agents

An intelligent agent is a system that perceives its environment via sensors and acts rationally upon that environment with its effectors. The architecture of an agent describes its modules and capabilities, and how these operate together. There are different types of architectures for intelligent agents, depending on their level of complexity, rationality, and interaction. Some of the common types are:

- **Reactive agents**: Reactive agents are the simplest type of AI agent. They do not have any internal state or memory, and they act only based on the current percept. They do not have any goals or plans, and they do not reason about the consequences of their actions. Reactive agents are fast and robust, but they are limited in their capabilities and adaptability.
- **Model-based reflex agents**: Model-based reflex agents are an improvement over reactive agents. They have an internal state that represents some aspects of the environment, and they update this state based on their percepts. They also have a set of rules that specify how to act based on the state and the percept. Model-based reflex agents can handle partially observable and dynamic environments, but they still do not have any goals or plans.
- **Goal-based agents**: Goal-based agents are more sophisticated than model-based reflex agents. They have an internal state, a set of rules, and a set of goals that they want to achieve. They can generate and evaluate different actions based on their state, their goals, and their expected outcomes. Goal-based agents can act more flexibly and intelligently, but they may not always find the optimal or feasible solution.
- **Utility-based agents**: Utility-based agents are an extension of goal-based agents. They have an internal state, a set of rules, a set of goals, and a utility function that assigns a numerical value to each state. They can compare different actions based on their expected utility, and choose the one that maximizes their utility. Utility-based agents can handle uncertainty and trade-offs better than goal-based agents, but they may require a lot of computation and knowledge.
- **Learning agents**: Learning agents are the most advanced type of AI agent. They have an internal state, a set of rules, a set of goals, a utility function, and a learning component that can improve their performance over time. They can learn from their own experience, from other agents, or from external sources. Learning agents can adapt to changing environments and tasks, and they can improve their rationality and efficiency.
