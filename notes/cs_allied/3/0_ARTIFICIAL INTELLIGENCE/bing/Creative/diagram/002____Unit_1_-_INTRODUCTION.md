## Unit 1 - INTRODUCTION

- This unit introduces the basic concepts and principles of artificial intelligence (AI).
- AI is the study of how to create machines and software that can perform tasks that normally require human intelligence, such as reasoning, learning, planning, decision making, natural language processing, computer vision, etc.
- AI can be classified into two main categories: weak AI and strong AI.
  - Weak AI, also known as narrow AI, is the type of AI that can only perform a specific task or domain, such as playing chess, recognizing faces, or translating languages. Weak AI does not have general intelligence or consciousness, and does not understand the meaning or context of its actions.
  - Strong AI, also known as general AI or artificial general intelligence (AGI), is the type of AI that can perform any intellectual task that a human can do, such as understanding natural language, solving problems, reasoning, creating art, etc. Strong AI has general intelligence and consciousness, and can understand the meaning and context of its actions. Strong AI is still a hypothetical and controversial concept, and there is no consensus on how to achieve it or whether it is possible or desirable.
- AI can also be classified into two main approaches: symbolic AI and sub-symbolic AI.
  - Symbolic AI, also known as classical AI or good old-fashioned AI (GOFAI), is the type of AI that uses symbols and rules to represent and manipulate knowledge. Symbolic AI relies on logic, search, and knowledge representation and reasoning techniques to solve problems. Symbolic AI is good at dealing with abstract and structured domains, such as mathematics, logic, and chess, but struggles with noisy and complex domains, such as natural language, vision, and common sense.
  - Sub-symbolic AI, also known as connectionist AI or neural AI, is the type of AI that uses numerical values and networks to represent and manipulate knowledge. Sub-symbolic AI relies on learning, adaptation, and emergence techniques to solve problems. Sub-symbolic AI is good at dealing with noisy and complex domains, such as natural language, vision, and common sense, but struggles with abstract and structured domains, such as mathematics, logic, and chess.
- AI can also be classified into two main goals: human-inspired AI and human-imitative AI.
  - Human-inspired AI, also known as cognitive AI or artificial psychology, is the type of AI that tries to understand and model how humans think and behave. Human-inspired AI aims to create machines and software that can mimic or simulate human cognitive processes, such as perception, memory, attention, emotion, etc. Human-inspired AI is useful for studying and improving human intelligence, as well as for creating more natural and human-like interactions between humans and machines.
  - Human-imitative AI, also known as engineering AI or artificial engineering, is the type of AI that tries to achieve or surpass human performance and capabilities. Human-imitative AI aims to create machines and software that can perform tasks that are difficult or impossible for humans, such as playing chess, driving cars, diagnosing diseases, etc. Human-imitative AI is useful for enhancing and augmenting human intelligence, as well as for creating more efficient and effective solutions for various problems.
