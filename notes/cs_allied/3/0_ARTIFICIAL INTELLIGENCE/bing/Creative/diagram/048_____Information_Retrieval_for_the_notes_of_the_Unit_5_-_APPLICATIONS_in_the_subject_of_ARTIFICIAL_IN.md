### Information Retrieval for the notes of the Unit 5 - APPLICATIONS in the subject of ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE KCS

- Information retrieval (IR) is the process of finding and accessing relevant information from a large collection of documents, such as web pages, books, articles, etc.
- IR systems can benefit from the application of artificial intelligence (AI) techniques to account for the intrinsic uncertainty and imprecision that characterize the subjectivity of this task
- AI methods are employed throughout the standard IR process and for novel value added services
- The standard IR process consists of the following steps:
  - Document analysis: the process of extracting features and representations from the documents, such as keywords, topics, entities, etc.
  - Query analysis: the process of interpreting the user's information need and transforming it into a suitable representation, such as a query language or a natural language expression
  - Matching: the process of comparing the query and the document representations and computing a relevance score for each document
  - Ranking: the process of ordering the documents according to their relevance scores and presenting the most relevant ones to the user
  - Feedback: the process of collecting and using the user's reactions to the retrieved documents, such as clicks, ratings, comments, etc., to improve the IR system's performance
- Some examples of AI techniques applied to IR are:
  - Pattern recognition: the use of statistical and machine learning methods to identify and classify patterns in the documents and queries, such as text classification, topic modeling, sentiment analysis, etc.
  - Representation: the use of symbolic and sub-symbolic methods to encode the meaning and structure of the documents and queries, such as ontologies, logic, neural networks, embeddings, etc.
  - Problem solving and planning: the use of search and optimization methods to find the best solution to the IR problem, such as heuristic search, genetic algorithms, reinforcement learning, etc.
  - Heuristics: the use of rules of thumb and common sense knowledge to guide the IR process, such as relevance feedback, query expansion, query reformulation, etc.
  - Learning: the use of supervised, unsupervised, and semi-supervised learning methods to adapt the IR system to the data and the user, such as classification, clustering, ranking, recommendation, etc.
- Some examples of novel value added services based on AI for IR are:
  - Question answering: the use of natural language processing and knowledge representation methods to answer natural language questions from the user, such as factoid, definition, list, or complex questions
  - Summarization: the use of natural language generation and text compression methods to produce concise and informative summaries of the documents or the query results, such as extractive, abstractive, or query-focused summaries
  - Visualization: the use of graphical and interactive methods to display the documents or the query results in a meaningful and intuitive way, such as charts, maps, networks, or timelines
  - Personalization: the use of user modeling and preference elicitation methods to tailor the IR system to the user's profile, context, and goals, such as adaptive filtering, collaborative filtering, or contextual suggestion
  - Evaluation: the use of experimental and analytical methods to measure and compare the effectiveness and efficiency of the IR system, such as precision, recall, F-measure, MAP, NDCG, etc.
