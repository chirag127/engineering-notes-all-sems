# Stochastic Games

- Stochastic games are a generalization of Markov decision processes (MDPs) to the case where there are multiple agents in a common environment .
- Stochastic games are also called Markov games or Markov random games .
- Stochastic games model situations where the outcome of an agent's action depends not only on the state of the environment, but also on the actions of other agents .
- Stochastic games can be classified into different types based on the following criteria :
  - The number of agents: two-player or multi-player.
  - The information available to the agents: perfect-information or imperfect-information.
  - The nature of the rewards: zero-sum or general-sum.
  - The structure of the transitions: simultaneous-move or sequential-move.
- Stochastic games can be used to model various artificial intelligence applications, such as playing chess and Go games, autonomous driving, robotics, and multi-agent coordination  .
- Stochastic games are challenging to solve because they involve strategic interactions among agents, uncertainty about the environment, and possibly incomplete or imperfect information  .
- Stochastic games can be solved by various methods, such as dynamic programming, reinforcement learning, game theory, and neural networks  .
