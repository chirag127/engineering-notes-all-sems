# Mental Events and Mental Objects

- Mental events and mental objects are concepts used in artificial intelligence to represent the knowledge and reasoning processes of an agent.
- Mental events are occurrences or changes in the mental state of an agent, such as thoughts, feelings, decisions, dreams, and realizations.
- Mental objects are entities or structures that exist in the mind of an agent, such as beliefs, goals, plans, rules, concepts, and categories.
- Mental events and mental objects are related to the mind-body problem, which is the question of how the physical brain and the immaterial mind interact.
- Some approaches to artificial intelligence assume that mental events and mental objects can be modeled by complex physical events and structures, such as neural networks, algorithms, and data structures.
- Other approaches to artificial intelligence explore the possibility of creating artificial agents that can have genuine mental events and objects, such as emotions, consciousness, and self-awareness .
- Mental events and mental objects are important for knowledge representation in artificial intelligence, because they allow an agent to reason about its own knowledge and reasoning processes, as well as the knowledge and reasoning processes of other agents.
- Some examples of mental events and mental objects in artificial intelligence are:
  - A chess-playing agent has a mental event of realizing that it is in checkmate, and a mental object of a goal to avoid checkmate.
  - A face-recognition agent has a mental event of recognizing a familiar face, and a mental object of a concept of a face.
  - A natural-language agent has a mental event of understanding a sentence, and a mental object of a rule for parsing sentences.
  - An emotion-recognition agent has a mental event of detecting an emotion, and a mental object of a category of emotions.
  - A self-driving car agent has a mental event of deciding to change lanes, and a mental object of a plan for changing lanes.
  - A chatbot agent has a mental event of generating a response, and a mental object of a belief about the user's intention.
