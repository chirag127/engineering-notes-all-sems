## Unit 1 - INTRODUCTION

This unit provides an overview of the following topics:

- What is artificial intelligence (AI) and why is it important?
- What are the main subfields and applications of AI?
- What are the main challenges and limitations of AI?
- What are the ethical and social implications of AI?

### What is artificial intelligence (AI) and why is it important?

- Artificial intelligence (AI) is the study and design of intelligent agents that can perceive, learn, reason, and act in complex environments.
- AI is important because it can enhance human capabilities, automate tasks, solve problems, and create new opportunities in various domains.
- AI can also pose risks and challenges, such as ethical dilemmas, social impacts, and technical limitations.

### What are the main subfields and applications of AI?

- AI can be divided into four main subfields: machine learning, natural language processing, computer vision, and robotics.
- Machine learning is the study of algorithms and models that can learn from data and improve their performance over time.
- Natural language processing is the study of methods and systems that can understand, generate, and interact with natural languages, such as speech and text.
- Computer vision is the study of techniques and systems that can analyze, interpret, and manipulate visual information, such as images and videos.
- Robotics is the study of machines and devices that can sense, move, and manipulate physical objects and environments.
- AI has many applications in various domains, such as health care, education, entertainment, business, security, and social good.

### What are the main challenges and limitations of AI?

- AI faces many challenges and limitations, such as data quality, scalability, explainability, robustness, and generalization.
- Data quality refers to the availability, accuracy, and diversity of the data that is used to train and evaluate AI systems.
- Scalability refers to the ability of AI systems to handle large and complex data sets and tasks efficiently and effectively.
- Explainability refers to the ability of AI systems to provide understandable and transparent reasons for their decisions and actions.
- Robustness refers to the ability of AI systems to cope with uncertainty, noise, and adversarial attacks in their inputs and outputs.
- Generalization refers to the ability of AI systems to transfer their knowledge and skills to new and unseen situations and domains.

### What are the ethical and social implications of AI?

- AI has many ethical and social implications, such as fairness, accountability, privacy, and human dignity.
- Fairness refers to the principle that AI systems should not discriminate or harm individuals or groups based on their characteristics, such as race, gender, or age.
- Accountability refers to the principle that AI systems and their developers should be responsible and liable for their outcomes and impacts.
- Privacy refers to the principle that AI systems should respect and protect the personal and sensitive information of individuals and groups.
- Human dignity refers to the principle that AI systems should not undermine or violate the inherent worth and rights of human beings.
