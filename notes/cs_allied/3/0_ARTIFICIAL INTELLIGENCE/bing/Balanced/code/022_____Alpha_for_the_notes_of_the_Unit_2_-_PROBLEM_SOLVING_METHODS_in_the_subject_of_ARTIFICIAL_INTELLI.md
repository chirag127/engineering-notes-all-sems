### Alpha

- Alpha is a computer program that can play the board game Go, developed by Google DeepMind.
- Alpha uses a combination of deep neural networks and reinforcement learning to learn from its own moves and improve its performance.
- Alpha is different from previous Go programs that relied on human expertise and extensive search trees to evaluate possible moves.
- Alpha can play against itself or other human or computer opponents, and has achieved superhuman level of skill in Go.
- Alpha has several versions, such as AlphaGo, AlphaGo Zero, and AlphaZero, each with different features and capabilities.

Some of the main points about Alpha are:

- Alpha is based on the idea of general artificial intelligence, which aims to create systems that can learn and perform any intellectual task that humans can do.
- Alpha uses a Monte Carlo tree search (MCTS) algorithm to explore the possible moves and outcomes in a game of Go, guided by two neural networks: a policy network and a value network.
- The policy network predicts the most promising moves to play, while the value network estimates the probability of winning from a given board position.
- The neural networks are trained by self-play, where Alpha plays millions of games against itself and learns from its own successes and failures.
- The self-play method allows Alpha to discover new strategies and tactics that are not known or used by human players, and to generalize its knowledge to different board sizes and rules.
- Alpha can also transfer its learning to other domains, such as chess and shogi, by using the same algorithm and neural network architecture, but with different input and output layers.
- Alpha demonstrates the potential and challenges of artificial intelligence, as it shows how a computer program can surpass human experts in a complex and intuitive domain, but also raises ethical and social questions about the impact and implications of such systems.
