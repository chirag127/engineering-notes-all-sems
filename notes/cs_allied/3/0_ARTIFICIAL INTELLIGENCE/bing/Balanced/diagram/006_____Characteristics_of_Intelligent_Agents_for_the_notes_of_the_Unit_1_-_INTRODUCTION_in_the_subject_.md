- An intelligent agent is a computer software system that can perceive its environment, act rationally, and learn from its experience  .
- Intelligent agents have the following distinguishing characteristics  :
  - **Autonomy**: They can perform certain tasks on their own, without human intervention or guidance.
  - **Adaptivity**: They can learn from their own actions and feedback, and improve their performance over time.
  - **Sociability**: They can interact with other entities, such as agents, humans, and systems, using communication protocols and languages.
  - **Goal-orientation**: They can pursue specific objectives or outcomes, and select the best actions to achieve them.
  - **Knowledge-based**: They can store and manipulate information about their environment, their goals, and their actions, using symbolic or sub-symbolic representations.
- Intelligent agents can be classified into different categories based on their degree of rationality, complexity, and learning ability :
  - **Simple reflex agents**: These agents perform actions using the current percept, rather than the percept history. The condition-action rule is used as the basis for decision making.
  - **Model-based reflex agents**: These agents maintain an internal state of the environment, and use it to choose the best action. They can handle partially observable environments.
  - **Goal-based agents**: These agents have explicit goals that guide their actions. They can reason about the future consequences of their actions, and select the ones that lead to their goals.
  - **Utility-based agents**: These agents have preferences over the possible outcomes, and use a utility function to measure their happiness. They can compare different actions based on their expected utility, and choose the ones that maximize their utility.
  - **Learning agents**: These agents can improve their knowledge and behavior through learning. They have four components: a learning element, a performance element, a critic, and a problem generator.
