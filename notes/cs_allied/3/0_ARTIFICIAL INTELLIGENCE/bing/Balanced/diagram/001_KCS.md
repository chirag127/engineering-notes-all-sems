# KCS

KCS stands for Knowledge-Centered Service, which is a methodology for creating and maintaining documentation as part of the service delivery process. KCS aims to improve service quality, efficiency, and customer satisfaction by capturing and reusing the knowledge of service teams. Some of the benefits of KCS are:

- It reduces the need for repeated requests and escalations by providing accurate and consistent information to customers and employees.
- It increases the productivity and morale of service teams by enabling them to share and leverage their expertise and experience.
- It decreases the costs and risks of service delivery by minimizing errors, redundancies, and gaps in knowledge.
- It enhances the learning and innovation of service teams by encouraging them to capture and refine their knowledge continuously.

KCS is based on four basic principles:

- Integrate: KCS integrates the creation and maintenance of knowledge into the service delivery workflow, rather than treating it as a separate activity.
- Evolve: KCS evolves the knowledge base dynamically based on the demand and usage of knowledge, rather than following a predefined structure or process.
- Collaborate: KCS fosters collaboration among service teams and stakeholders, rather than relying on individual experts or authorities.
- Reward: KCS rewards service teams for their contribution and participation in the knowledge base, rather than measuring them by other metrics.

KCS follows a double-loop process that consists of two phases:

- Solve: In this phase, service teams capture, structure, and reuse knowledge while solving customer or employee issues. They also improve the quality and findability of existing knowledge articles by flagging, linking, or updating them as needed.
- Evolve: In this phase, service teams analyze the usage and performance of the knowledge base, and identify opportunities for improvement. They also review and validate the knowledge articles that have been flagged or updated, and provide feedback and coaching to the authors.

KCS requires a shift in culture and mindset from service teams and organizations, as well as a set of practices and tools that support the implementation and adoption of the methodology. Some of the key elements of KCS are:

- Roles and responsibilities: KCS defines different roles and responsibilities for service teams, such as contributor, coach, publisher, and leader, based on their level of participation and proficiency in the knowledge base.
- Licensing and certification: KCS offers a licensing and certification program that validates the knowledge and skills of service teams and organizations in applying the methodology. There are four levels of certification: Foundation, Practitioner, Coach, and Trainer.
- Standards and guidelines: KCS provides a set of standards and guidelines for creating and maintaining knowledge articles, such as the KCS article quality index, the KCS article lifecycle, and the KCS style guide.
- Technology and tools: KCS requires a technology and tools platform that supports the integration, evolution, collaboration, and reward aspects of the methodology, such as a knowledge management system, a search engine, a feedback mechanism, and a reporting and analytics tool.
