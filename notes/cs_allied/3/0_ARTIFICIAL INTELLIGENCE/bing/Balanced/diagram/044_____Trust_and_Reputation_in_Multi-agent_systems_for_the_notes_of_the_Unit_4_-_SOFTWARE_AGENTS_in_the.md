### Trust and Reputation in Multi-agent systems

- Trust and reputation are important concepts for effective interactions in open multi-agent systems (MAS) where agents can cooperate, coordinate, and negotiate with each other.
- Trust is the degree of belief or confidence that an agent has in the reliability, honesty, or competence of another agent.
- Reputation is the collective opinion or evaluation of an agent's behavior or performance by other agents in the system.
- Trust and reputation models aim to provide mechanisms for agents to assess the trustworthiness and reputation of other agents, and to use this information to guide their decisions and actions.
- Trust and reputation models can also help to deter or detect malicious or dishonest agents, and to promote cooperation and social norms in the system.
- Trust and reputation models can be classified into two main categories: personal and social.
  - Personal trust and reputation models are based on the direct experiences or observations of an agent with another agent, and reflect the subjective and individual perspective of the agent.
  - Social trust and reputation models are based on the indirect information or recommendations from other agents, and reflect the objective and collective perspective of the system.
- Trust and reputation models can also be distinguished by the type of information they use, such as binary, discrete, continuous, or fuzzy; the type of aggregation they perform, such as weighted, average, or majority; and the type of update they apply, such as additive, multiplicative, or decay.
- Some examples of trust and reputation models are:
  - Marsh's model, which defines trust as a subjective probability based on the utility and importance of an action, and uses a continuous scale from -1 to 1 to represent trust values.
  - Sen et al.'s model, which defines trust as a function of the similarity and dependability of an agent, and uses a discrete scale from 0 to 5 to represent trust values.
  - SPORAS, which defines reputation as a score based on the frequency and recency of positive and negative feedbacks, and uses a continuous scale from 0 to 1 to represent reputation values.
  - eBay's model, which defines reputation as a feedback rating based on the number of positive, negative, and neutral feedbacks, and uses a discrete scale from -100 to 100 to represent reputation values.
  - Certified reputation, which defines reputation as a certificate issued by a trusted authority based on the verification of an agent's credentials, and uses a binary scale of 0 or 1 to represent reputation values.
