### Trust and Reputation in Multi-agent systems

- Trust and reputation are important concepts for effective interactions in open multi-agent systems (MAS), where agents may belong to different owners and have different goals and behaviors.
- Trust is the degree of belief that an agent has about the reliability, honesty, or competence of another agent, based on its own direct experiences or observations.
- Reputation is the collective opinion or evaluation of an agent by a group of other agents, based on their indirect experiences or reports.
- Trust and reputation models aim to provide mechanisms for agents to assess the trustworthiness and reputation of other agents, and to use this information to guide their decisions and actions in MAS.
- Trust and reputation models can be classified into several categories, such as:
  - Subjective vs. objective: Subjective models rely on the personal perspective and preferences of each agent, while objective models assume a common and consistent view of trust and reputation among all agents.
  - Centralized vs. decentralized: Centralized models use a single authority or a trusted third party to manage and disseminate trust and reputation information, while decentralized models allow agents to exchange and aggregate trust and reputation information in a peer-to-peer manner.
  - Qualitative vs. quantitative: Qualitative models use linguistic or ordinal scales to represent trust and reputation values, while quantitative models use numerical or probabilistic values.
  - Global vs. local: Global models compute a single trust or reputation value for each agent, while local models compute different trust or reputation values for each agent depending on the context or the task.
  - Static vs. dynamic: Static models assume that trust and reputation values are fixed and do not change over time, while dynamic models update trust and reputation values based on the latest evidence and feedback.
- Some examples of trust and reputation models are:
  - Marsh's model: A subjective and qualitative model that defines trust as a combination of basic, general, and situational trust, and uses a seven-point scale to represent trust values.
  - Sen et al.'s model: A subjective and quantitative model that defines trust as a function of the expected utility of an agent's actions, and uses a Bayesian approach to update trust values based on the observed outcomes.
  - SPORAS: A decentralized and quantitative model that defines reputation as a function of the frequency and recency of an agent's interactions, and uses a fuzzy logic system to aggregate reputation values from different sources.
  - eBay's model: A centralized and qualitative model that defines reputation as the sum of positive and negative feedback ratings given by other agents, and uses a star-based system to display reputation values.
  - Certified reputation: A decentralized and objective model that defines reputation as a set of certificates issued by trusted third parties, and uses a cryptographic scheme to verify and exchange reputation certificates.
