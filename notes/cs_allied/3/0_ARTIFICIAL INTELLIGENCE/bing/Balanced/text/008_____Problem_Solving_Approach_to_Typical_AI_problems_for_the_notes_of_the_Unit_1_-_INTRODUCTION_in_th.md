### Problem Solving Approach to Typical AI problems

- A typical AI problem is one that involves some form of intelligence, such as reasoning, planning, learning, perception, natural language processing, etc.
- A problem solving approach to AI is a method of finding a solution to a given problem by applying some AI techniques, such as search, knowledge representation, inference, etc.
- A problem solving approach to AI consists of the following steps:

  - Problem formulation: This step involves defining the problem in terms of the initial state, the goal state, and the actions or operators that can change the state.
  - Problem representation: This step involves choosing a suitable data structure or language to represent the problem, such as graphs, trees, logic, etc.
  - Problem solving: This step involves applying a suitable algorithm or technique to find a solution to the problem, such as uninformed search, informed search, constraint satisfaction, etc.
  - Solution evaluation: This step involves checking the validity, optimality, and efficiency of the solution, and comparing it with other possible solutions or alternatives.

- Some examples of typical AI problems and their problem solving approaches are:

  - Route finding: This problem involves finding a path from one location to another on a map or a graph. A possible problem solving approach is to use informed search algorithms, such as A* or greedy best-first search, that use a heuristic function to estimate the distance to the goal.
  - Sudoku: This problem involves filling a 9x9 grid with digits from 1 to 9, such that each row, column, and 3x3 subgrid contains exactly one of each digit. A possible problem solving approach is to use constraint satisfaction techniques, such as backtracking or forward checking, that enforce the constraints on the grid.
  - Chess: This problem involves playing a board game against an opponent, where each player has 16 pieces of different types and moves them according to certain rules. A possible problem solving approach is to use game-playing techniques, such as minimax or alpha-beta pruning, that evaluate the outcomes of different moves and choose the best one.
