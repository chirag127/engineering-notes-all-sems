### Characteristics of Intelligent Agents

- An intelligent agent is a computer software system that can perceive its environment, act rationally, and learn from its experience  .
- Intelligent agents have the following distinguishing characteristics    :
  - **Autonomy**: They can perform certain tasks on their own, without direct human intervention or supervision.
  - **Adaptivity**: They can learn from their experience and improve their performance over time, based on feedback or new information.
  - **Sociability**: They can interact with other entities, such as agents, humans, and systems, using communication protocols or languages.
  - **Goal-orientation**: They can pursue specific objectives or outcomes, based on their preferences or values.
  - **Knowledge-based**: They can use explicit or implicit knowledge to reason about their environment and actions, and to plan or solve problems.
  - **Mobility**: They can move across different platforms or networks, depending on their needs or capabilities.
- Intelligent agents can be classified into different categories, based on their degree of complexity, rationality, and learning ability :
  - **Simple reflex agents**: These agents perform actions using the current percept, rather than the percept history. They use condition-action rules to map percepts to actions. They are fast but limited in their intelligence and adaptivity.
  - **Model-based reflex agents**: These agents maintain an internal state or model of their environment, based on their percept history. They use condition-action rules to map percepts and states to actions. They are more flexible and robust than simple reflex agents, but still lack goal-orientation and learning ability.
  - **Goal-based agents**: These agents have explicit goals or objectives that they try to achieve. They use search or planning algorithms to find the best sequence of actions to reach their goals. They are more rational and intelligent than model-based reflex agents, but still lack adaptivity and learning ability.
  - **Utility-based agents**: These agents have utility functions that measure how desirable a state or outcome is. They use decision theory or optimization techniques to find the best sequence of actions to maximize their utility. They are more flexible and efficient than goal-based agents, but still lack learning ability.
  - **Learning agents**: These agents have learning components that allow them to improve their performance over time, based on feedback or new information. They can modify their condition-action rules, goals, utility functions, or internal models, depending on the type of learning. They are the most intelligent and adaptive agents, but also the most complex and challenging to design and implement.
