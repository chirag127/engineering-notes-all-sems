### Introduction for the notes of the Unit 1 - INTRODUCTION in the subject of ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE KCS

- Artificial Intelligence (AI) is the branch of computer science that aims to create machines and systems that can perform tasks that normally require human intelligence and abilities.
- AI can be broadly classified into two categories: weak AI and strong AI.
  - Weak AI, also known as narrow AI, is the type of AI that can perform specific tasks or solve specific problems within a limited domain or scope. Examples of weak AI include speech recognition, face recognition, chess playing, spam filtering, etc.
  - Strong AI, also known as general AI or artificial general intelligence (AGI), is the type of AI that can perform any intellectual task that a human can do, or even surpass human intelligence and abilities. Examples of strong AI include natural language understanding, common sense reasoning, creativity, self-awareness, etc. Strong AI is still a hypothetical and elusive goal of AI research.
- AI can also be classified into four categories based on the degree of human-like intelligence and rationality: systems that think like humans, systems that act like humans, systems that think rationally, and systems that act rationally.
  - Systems that think like humans are the ones that try to model or simulate the cognitive processes and mechanisms of human mind, such as perception, learning, memory, reasoning, problem-solving, decision-making, etc. Examples of such systems include neural networks, expert systems, natural language processing, etc.
  - Systems that act like humans are the ones that try to behave or perform tasks in a way that is indistinguishable from human behavior or performance, such as passing the Turing test, exhibiting emotions, having a personality, etc. Examples of such systems include chatbots, robots, virtual agents, etc.
  - Systems that think rationally are the ones that try to apply the principles and methods of logic, mathematics, and science to reason and infer about the world and the knowledge, such as using deductive, inductive, or abductive reasoning, applying search algorithms, planning, etc. Examples of such systems include theorem provers, knowledge-based systems, planning systems, etc.
  - Systems that act rationally are the ones that try to achieve their goals or objectives in the most optimal or efficient way, given the available information and resources, such as using utility functions, decision theory, game theory, etc. Examples of such systems include intelligent agents, autonomous vehicles, recommender systems, etc.
- AI is a multidisciplinary field that draws upon and contributes to various domains of knowledge and science, such as computer science, mathematics, logic, philosophy, psychology, linguistics, neuroscience, biology, engineering, etc.
- AI has many applications and benefits for various domains and sectors of society, such as education, health, business, entertainment, security, etc. However, AI also poses many challenges and risks, such as ethical, social, legal, economic, etc. Therefore, AI requires careful and responsible design, development, and use.
