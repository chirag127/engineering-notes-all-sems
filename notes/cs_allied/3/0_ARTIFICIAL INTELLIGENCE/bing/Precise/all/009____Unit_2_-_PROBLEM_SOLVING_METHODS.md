## Unit 2 - PROBLEM SOLVING METHODS

Problem-solving is the process of finding a solution to an issue by using a systematic approach. There are several methods that can be used to solve problems, including:

1. **Trial and Error:** This method involves trying different solutions until the problem is solved. It is often used when there is no clear solution or when the problem is not well understood.

2. **Algorithm:** An algorithm is a step-by-step procedure for solving a problem. It is often used in mathematics and computer science to solve complex problems.

3. **Heuristics:** Heuristics are rules of thumb or mental shortcuts that can be used to quickly find a solution to a problem. They are often used when time is limited or when a perfect solution is not necessary.

4. **Divide and Conquer:** This method involves breaking a problem down into smaller, more manageable parts and solving each part separately. It is often used to solve complex problems that can be divided into smaller, simpler problems.

5. **Means-Ends Analysis:** This method involves identifying the desired end result and working backward to determine the steps needed to achieve that result. It is often used in planning and decision-making.

6. **Working Backward:** This method involves starting with the desired end result and working backward to determine the steps needed to achieve that result. It is similar to means-ends analysis but is often used when the end result is known but the steps to achieve it are not.

7. **Brainstorming:** Brainstorming is a group problem-solving method that involves generating a large number of ideas in a short amount of time. It is often used to generate creative solutions to complex problems.

These are just a few of the many problem-solving methods that can be used to find solutions to problems. The best method to use will depend on the specific problem and the circumstances in which it is being solved.
