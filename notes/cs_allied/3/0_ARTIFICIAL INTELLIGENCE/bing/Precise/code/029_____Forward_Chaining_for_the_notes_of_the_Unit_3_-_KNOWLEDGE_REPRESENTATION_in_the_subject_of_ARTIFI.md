### Forward Chaining

Forward chaining is a method of reasoning in artificial intelligence, specifically in rule-based expert systems. It is one of the two main methods of reasoning when using an inference engine and can be described logically as repeated application of modus ponens. Forward chaining is a popular implementation strategy for expert systems, business and production rule systems.

Here are some key points to remember about forward chaining:

1. Forward chaining starts with the available data and uses inference rules to extract more data until a goal is reached.
2. An inference engine using forward chaining searches the inference rules until it finds one where the antecedent (If clause) is known to be true.
3. When such a rule is found, the engine can conclude, or infer, the consequent (Then clause), resulting in the addition of new information to its data.
4. In forward chaining, the engine keeps repeating this process until a goal is reached or no more progress can be made.
5. Forward chaining is also known as data-driven reasoning or bottom-up reasoning.
