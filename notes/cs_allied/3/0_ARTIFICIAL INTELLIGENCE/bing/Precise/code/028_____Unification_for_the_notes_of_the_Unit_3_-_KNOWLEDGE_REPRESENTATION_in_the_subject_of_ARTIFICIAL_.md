### Unification

Unification is a process in automated reasoning and logic programming that attempts to identify two expressions with each other by finding a substitution that makes them equal. It is used in the resolution principle, which is a fundamental rule of inference in first-order logic.

Here are some key points to remember about unification:

1. Unification is used to find a common instance of two expressions.
2. It is a fundamental operation in many automated reasoning systems, including theorem provers and logic programming languages.
3. Unification is based on the idea of finding a substitution that makes two expressions equal.
4. The most common algorithm for unification is the Robinson's algorithm, which is also known as the unification algorithm.
5. Unification can be used to solve equations, to find the most general unifier (mgu) of two expressions, and to perform type inference.
