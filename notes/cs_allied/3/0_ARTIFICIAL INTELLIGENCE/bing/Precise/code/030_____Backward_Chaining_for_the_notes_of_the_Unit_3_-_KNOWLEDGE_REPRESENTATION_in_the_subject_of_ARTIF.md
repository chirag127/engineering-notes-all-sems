### Backward Chaining

Backward chaining is a method used in artificial intelligence for automated reasoning and problem-solving. It is a form of inference that starts with the goal or conclusion and works backward to determine the facts or premises that support it. This method is commonly used in expert systems and rule-based systems.

Here are some key points to remember about backward chaining:

1. Backward chaining is a goal-driven approach, where the system starts with the desired outcome and works backward to determine the necessary steps to achieve it.
2. It is commonly used in rule-based systems, where the system has a set of rules or conditions that must be met in order to reach the desired outcome.
3. Backward chaining can be useful in situations where there are multiple possible solutions or paths to the desired outcome, as it allows the system to explore different options and determine the most efficient or effective solution.
4. One limitation of backward chaining is that it can be computationally expensive, as the system may need to explore many different paths before finding the solution.
5. Another limitation is that backward chaining may not be effective in situations where the desired outcome is not well-defined or where there are many possible outcomes.
