## Unit 2 - Thermal Sensors: Introduction, Gas thermometric Sensors, Thermal Expansion Type Thermometric Sensors, Acoustic Temperature Sensor, Dielectric Constant and Refractive Index Thermo-sensors, Helium Low Temperature Thermometer, Nuclear Thermometer, Magnetic Thermometer, Resistance Change Type Thermometric Sensors, Thermo-EMF Sensors, Junction Semiconductor Types, Thermal Radiation Sensors, Quartz Crystal Thermo-electric Sensors, NQR Thermometry, Spectroscopic Thermometry, Noise Thermometry, Heat Flux Sensors.

Thermal sensors are devices that measure the temperature of a physical system. There are many different types of thermal sensors, each with its own unique set of advantages and disadvantages. The following is an overview of some of the most common types of thermal sensors:

1. Gas thermometric sensors: These sensors use the change in pressure of a gas to measure temperature.

2. Thermal expansion type thermometric sensors: These sensors use the change in length of a material as it expands or contracts with temperature.

3. Acoustic temperature sensor: These sensors use the speed of sound in a gas to measure temperature.

4. Dielectric constant and refractive index thermometric sensors: These sensors use the change in the dielectric constant or refractive index of a material with temperature to measure temperature.

5. Helium low temperature thermometer: These sensors use the change in pressure of helium to measure temperature at very low temperatures.

6. Nuclear thermometer: These sensors use the decay of radioactive isotopes to measure temperature.

7. Magnetic thermometer: These sensors use the change in magnetic properties of a material with temperature to measure temperature.

8. Resistance change type thermometric sensors: These sensors use the change in resistance of a material with temperature to measure temperature.

9. Thermo-EMF sensors: These sensors use the change in the electromotive force of a material with temperature to measure temperature.

10. Junction semiconductor types: These sensors use the change in the electrical properties of a junction between two semiconductors with temperature to measure temperature.

11. Thermal radiation sensors: These sensors use the change in the intensity of thermal radiation with temperature to measure temperature.

12. Quartz crystal thermoelectric sensors: These sensors use the change in the electrical properties of a quartz crystal with temperature to measure temperature.

13. NQR thermometry: These sensors use the change in the nuclear quadrupole resonance of a material with temperature to measure temperature.

14. Spectroscopic thermometry: These sensors use the change in the absorption or emission spectra of a material with temperature to measure temperature.

15. Noise thermometry: These sensors use the change in the thermal noise of a material with temperature to measure temperature.

16. Heat flux sensors: These sensors measure the rate of heat transfer through a material, and can be used to measure temperature.

Overall, thermal sensors play a critical role in a wide range of applications, including temperature control, environmental monitoring, and industrial process control.
