### Digital signal representation

1. A digital signal is a physical signal that represents data as a sequence of discrete values; at any given time it can only take on one of a finite number of values.

2. These values are represented by a series of binary numbers in computers. For example, an 8-bit digital signal can represent values from 0 to 255 using binary numbers.

3. The process of converting an analog signal to digital is called analog-to-digital conversion. The device that performs this conversion is called an analog-to-digital converter (ADC).

4. The rate at which samples are taken from a continuous analog signal to produce the digital signal is known as the sampling rate or sampling frequency. According to the sampling theorem, the sampling rate must be at least twice the highest frequency of the analog signal to capture all the necessary information. This is known as the Nyquist rate.

5. Quantization is the process of mapping the sampled analog signal to discrete values in the digital signal. The resolution refers to the number of discrete values used to represent the signal and determines the quantization error. A higher resolution (more discrete values) leads to a lower quantization error.
