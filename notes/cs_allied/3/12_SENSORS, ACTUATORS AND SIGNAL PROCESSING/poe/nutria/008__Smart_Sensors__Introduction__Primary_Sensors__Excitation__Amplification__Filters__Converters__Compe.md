
### Smart Sensors: Introduction

Smart sensors are devices that can sense physical parameters, process the information and transmit it to other systems. They are used in a variety of applications, such as industrial automation, robotics, healthcare, and military.

Primary Sensors

Primary sensors are the first step in the sensing process. They convert physical parameters such as temperature, pressure, light, sound, and force into electrical signals. These signals are then converted into digital signals by transducers.

Excitation

Excitation is the process of providing an external stimulus to the primary sensors. This can be done by providing an electrical voltage or current, or by using optical, mechanical, magnetic, or acoustic excitation.

Amplification

Amplification is the process of increasing the amplitude of the signal obtained from the primary sensors. This is usually done using operational amplifiers or other electronic components.

Filters

Filters are used to eliminate unwanted frequencies from the signal obtained from the primary sensors. This allows the signal to be more accurately measured.

Converters

Converters are used to convert the electrical signals obtained from the primary sensors into digital signals. These digital signals can then be processed by computers or other devices.

Compensation

Compensation is the process of removing any errors in the signals obtained from the primary sensors. This is usually done using software or hardware.

Information Coding/Processing

Information coding/processing is the process of converting the digital signals into a form that can be understood by computers or other devices. This is usually done using algorithms or software.

Data Communication

Data communication is the process of transferring the digital signals from the sensors to other systems. This is usually done using wired or wireless communication protocols.

Standards for Smart Sensor Interface

There are several standards for the interface between smart sensors and other systems. These standards include the IEEE 1451.1, IEEE 1451.2, and IEEE 1451.3.

The Automation

The automation of the sensing process is the process of using computers or other devices to control the sensing process. This is usually done using algorithms, software, or hardware.
