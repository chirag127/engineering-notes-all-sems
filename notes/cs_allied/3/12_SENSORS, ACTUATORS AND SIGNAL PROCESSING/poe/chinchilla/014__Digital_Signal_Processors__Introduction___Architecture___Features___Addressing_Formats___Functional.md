### Digital Signal Processors: Introduction – Architecture – Features – Addressing Formats – Functional modes – Introduction to Commercial Processors

Digital Signal Processors (DSPs) are specialized microprocessors used for processing digital signals in real-time. They are designed to handle complex mathematical operations and are commonly used in applications such as audio, video, telecommunications, and control systems. Here are some key points about DSPs:

#### Introduction

- DSPs are microprocessors specifically designed for processing digital signals.
- They are optimized for performing complex mathematical operations quickly and efficiently.
- DSPs can be used in a wide range of applications, including audio and video processing, telecommunications, and control systems.

#### Architecture

- DSPs typically have a Harvard architecture, which means that they have separate memory for instructions and data.
- They often have multiple execution units, allowing them to perform multiple operations in parallel.
- DSPs may also have specialized hardware for performing specific signal processing tasks, such as filtering or FFT.

#### Features

- DSPs often have specialized features for processing digital signals, such as SIMD (Single Instruction Multiple Data) instructions, which allow for parallel processing of multiple data elements.
- They may also have built-in hardware accelerators for common signal processing tasks, such as FFT (Fast Fourier Transform) or FIR (Finite Impulse Response) filtering.
- DSPs may also have specialized instructions for performing fixed-point arithmetic, which is commonly used in signal processing applications.

#### Addressing Formats

- DSPs typically support multiple addressing modes, including direct, indirect, and indexed addressing.
- They may also support circular buffering, which is commonly used in signal processing applications to efficiently process streams of data.

#### Functional Modes

- DSPs may support multiple functional modes, such as interrupt-driven, DMA (Direct Memory Access) driven, or polled.
- Interrupt-driven mode is commonly used in real-time signal processing applications, where the DSP needs to respond quickly to external events.
- DMA-driven mode is commonly used for transferring large blocks of data between memory and external devices.

#### Introduction to Commercial Processors

- There are many commercial DSP processors available, including those from companies such as Texas Instruments, Analog Devices, and NXP Semiconductors.
- These processors may have different architectures, features, and addressing modes, but they all share the common goal of efficiently processing digital signals.
- Some common commercial DSP processors include the TI TMS320, the ADI Blackfin, and the NXP LPC5500.
