## Unit 1 - Sensors / Transducers: Principles Classification, Parameters, Characteristics, Environmental Parameters (EP), Characterization

- A **sensor** is an element that senses a variation in input energy to produce a variation in another or same form of energy . A **transducer** is an element that converts one form of energy to another form . The process of conversion of energy from one form to another is called **transduction**.
- Sensors and transducers can be classified based on different criteria, such as:
  - The principle of transduction form used: This classification is based on the principle of transduction as resistive, inductive, capacitive, etc. depending on their conversion into resistance, inductance or capacitance respectively.
  - The nature of output signal: This classification is based on the nature of output signal as analog or digital.
  - The type of input energy: This classification is based on the type of input energy as mechanical, thermal, optical, chemical, biological, etc .
  - The type of output energy: This classification is based on the type of output energy as electrical, mechanical, optical, chemical, biological, etc .
- Sensors and transducers have various parameters that describe their performance and characteristics, such as:
  - **Sensitivity**: The ratio of change in output to change in input .
  - **Range**: The minimum and maximum values of input that can be measured by the sensor or transducer .
  - **Resolution**: The smallest change in input that can be detected by the sensor or transducer .
  - **Accuracy**: The degree of closeness of the output to the true value of the input .
  - **Precision**: The degree of repeatability of the output for the same input .
  - **Linearity**: The degree of proportionality between the output and the input .
  - **Hysteresis**: The difference in output for the same input when the input is increased or decreased .
  - **Response time**: The time required for the output to reach a specified percentage of its final value when the input changes .
  - **Drift**: The change in output over time due to aging, temperature, humidity, etc .
- Environmental parameters (EP) are the external factors that affect the performance and characteristics of sensors and transducers, such as temperature, humidity, pressure, vibration, noise, etc . EP can cause errors, drift, degradation, or damage to the sensors and transducers. Therefore, EP should be considered in the design, selection, and calibration of sensors and transducers.
- Characterization of sensors and transducers is the process of measuring and analyzing their parameters and characteristics under different conditions and EP . Characterization can be done in many ways depending on the types of sensors and transducers, such as electrical, mechanical, thermal, optical, chemical, biological, etc. Characterization can help to evaluate the performance, reliability, and suitability of sensors and transducers for various applications.
