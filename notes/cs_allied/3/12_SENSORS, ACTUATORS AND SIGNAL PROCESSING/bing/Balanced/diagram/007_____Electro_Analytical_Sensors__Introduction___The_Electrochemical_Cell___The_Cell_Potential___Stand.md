### Electro Analytical Sensors

- Electro analytical sensors are devices that measure the concentration or activity of an analyte in a solution by using an electrode as a transducer element .
- Electro analytical sensors can detect various parameters in our everyday lives, such as pH, oxygen, glucose, heavy metals, etc., by using different electrochemical principles, such as amperometry, potentiometry, and conductometry .
- Electro analytical sensors are powerful analytical tools due to their superior sensitivity and selectivity, quick response period, simplicity in operation, and miniaturization.

#### Introduction – The Electrochemical Cell

- An electrochemical cell is a device that converts chemical energy into electrical energy or vice versa by using redox reactions.
- An electrochemical cell consists of two electrodes (anode and cathode) immersed in an electrolyte solution and connected by an external circuit.
- The electrodes are the sites where the oxidation and reduction reactions occur, and the electrolyte is the medium that allows the transfer of ions between the electrodes.
- The external circuit provides a path for the flow of electrons from the anode to the cathode.
- The cell potential is the difference in electrical potential between the two electrodes, which drives the electron flow and determines the cell performance.

#### The Cell Potential – Standard Hydrogen Electrode (SHE)

- The cell potential is the measure of the tendency of a redox reaction to occur spontaneously in an electrochemical cell.
- The cell potential can be calculated by using the Nernst equation, which relates the cell potential to the standard electrode potentials, the concentrations of the reactants and products, and the temperature.
- The standard electrode potential is the potential of an electrode when it is in equilibrium with its own ions at 1 M concentration and 1 atm pressure.
- The standard hydrogen electrode (SHE) is a reference electrode that is assigned a standard electrode potential of zero volts at all temperatures.
- The SHE consists of a platinum wire coated with platinum black, which is immersed in a 1 M HCl solution and bubbled with hydrogen gas at 1 atm pressure.
- The SHE is used as a reference to measure the standard electrode potentials of other electrodes by connecting them to the SHE in an electrochemical cell.

#### Liquid Junction and Other Potentials – Polarization

- Liquid junction potential is the potential difference that arises when two solutions of different concentrations or compositions are in contact with each other.
- Liquid junction potential is caused by the unequal diffusion rates of the ions in the solutions, which creates a charge imbalance at the interface.
- Liquid junction potential can affect the accuracy of the cell potential measurement and should be minimized or eliminated by using a salt bridge or a porous plug.
- Other potentials that can affect the cell potential measurement are the contact potential, the junction potential, and the ohmic potential.
- Contact potential is the potential difference that arises when two different metals are in contact with each other.
- Junction potential is the potential difference that arises when two different electrolytes are in contact with each other.
- Ohmic potential is the potential drop that occurs due to the resistance of the electrolyte and the electrodes.
- Polarization is the deviation of the electrode potential from its equilibrium value due to the passage of current through the electrode.
- Polarization can be caused by the accumulation or depletion of the reactants or products at the electrode surface, which changes the concentration gradient and the reaction rate.
- Polarization can be classified into activation polarization, concentration polarization, and ohmic polarization.

#### Concentration Polarization– Reference Electrodes – Sensor Electrodes

- Concentration polarization is the polarization that occurs due to the change in the concentration of the reactants or products at the electrode surface.
- Concentration polarization can be caused by the mass transfer limitations of the reactants or products, such as diffusion, convection, or migration.
- Concentration polarization can be reduced by increasing the stirring rate, the temperature, or the electrode area.
