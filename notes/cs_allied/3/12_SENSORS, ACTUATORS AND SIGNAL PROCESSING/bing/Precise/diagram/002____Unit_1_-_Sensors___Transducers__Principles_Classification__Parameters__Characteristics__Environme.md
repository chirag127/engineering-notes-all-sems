## Unit 1 - Sensors / Transducers: Principles Classification, Parameters, Characteristics, Environmental Parameters (EP), Characterization

### Principles
- A sensor is a device that detects and responds to some type of input from the physical environment.
- The specific input could be light, heat, motion, moisture, pressure, or any one of a great number of other environmental phenomena.
- The output is generally a signal that is converted to human-readable display at the sensor location or transmitted electronically over a network for reading or further processing.

### Classification
- Sensors can be classified based on the type of input they detect, such as temperature sensors, pressure sensors, light sensors, etc.
- They can also be classified based on the type of output they produce, such as analog or digital.
- Another way to classify sensors is based on the technology used, such as resistive, capacitive, inductive, etc.

### Parameters
- The parameters of a sensor refer to the characteristics that define its performance and capabilities.
- Some common parameters include sensitivity, accuracy, resolution, range, and response time.

### Characteristics
- The characteristics of a sensor refer to the specific features and attributes that make it suitable for a particular application.
- Some common characteristics include robustness, reliability, durability, and ease of use.

### Environmental Parameters (EP)
- Environmental parameters refer to the conditions in the environment that can affect the performance of a sensor.
- Some common environmental parameters include temperature, humidity, pressure, and electromagnetic interference.

### Characterization
- Characterization refers to the process of testing and evaluating a sensor to determine its performance and capabilities.
- This can involve measuring its response to various inputs and environmental conditions, and comparing the results to its specifications and expected performance.
