### Digital Signal Processors: Introduction – Architecture – Features – Addressing Formats – Functional modes – Introduction to Commercial Processors.

Digital Signal Processors (DSPs) are specialized microprocessors designed to perform digital signal processing tasks. They are used in a wide range of applications, including audio and video processing, telecommunications, and control systems.

The architecture of a DSP typically includes a central processing unit (CPU), memory, and peripheral interfaces. The CPU is designed to perform mathematical operations on digital signals, and is optimized for high-speed processing.

The following are some of the key features of DSPs:

1. High-speed processing: DSPs are designed to perform mathematical operations on digital signals at high speeds, making them well-suited for real-time processing applications.

2. Parallel processing: DSPs often include multiple processing units that can work in parallel, allowing for high-speed processing of multiple signals simultaneously.

3. Programmability: DSPs are programmable, allowing for the implementation of custom algorithms and processing functions.

4. Addressing formats: DSPs use a variety of addressing formats, including linear addressing, circular addressing, and bit-reversed addressing, to support different processing tasks.

5. Functional modes: DSPs can operate in different functional modes, including real-time processing, offline processing, and interrupt-driven processing, to support different processing requirements.

Commercial DSPs are widely available from a variety of manufacturers, and are used in a wide range of applications. They are an important tool in the field of digital signal processing, and are used to support a wide range of applications, including audio and video processing, telecommunications, and control systems.
