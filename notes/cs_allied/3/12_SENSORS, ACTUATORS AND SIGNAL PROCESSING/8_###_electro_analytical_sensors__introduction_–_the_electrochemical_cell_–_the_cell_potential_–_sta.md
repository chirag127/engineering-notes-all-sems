### Electro Analytical Sensors: Introduction – The Electrochemical Cell – The Cell Potential – Standard Hydrogen Electrode (SHE) – Liquid Junction and Other Potentials – Polarization – Concentration Polarization– Reference Electrodes – Sensor Electrodes – Electro ceramics in Gas Media .

Electroanalytical sensors are sensors that use an electrochemical process to measure a physical or chemical property. They are widely used in various fields such as analytical chemistry, environmental monitoring, and biomedicine.

An electrochemical cell is the basic unit of an electroanalytical sensor. It consists of two electrodes, a working electrode and a reference electrode, immersed in an electrolyte solution. The working electrode is used to measure the property of interest, while the reference electrode provides a stable voltage to the system.

The cell potential is the difference in voltage between the working electrode and the reference electrode. This voltage can be used to determine the concentration of a chemical species in the electrolyte solution, or to measure other physical properties such as pH or temperature.

The Standard Hydrogen Electrode (SHE) is a widely used reference electrode, which provides a stable voltage and is used as a reference for many electroanalytical measurements.

The liquid junction potential is the potential difference between the reference electrode and the electrolyte solution. Other potentials, such as the concentration polarization potential, can also affect the measurement, and must be taken into account when interpreting the results.

Reference electrodes provide a stable voltage to the system, and are used to minimize the effects of the liquid junction potential and other potentials. Sensor electrodes are specialized electrodes used for specific applications, such as pH sensors or oxygen sensors.

Electroceramics in gas media are materials that are used to make electroanalytical sensors for gas phase measurements, such as oxygen sensors. These materials are used to make highly sensitive and selective sensors, and are an important component of electroanalytical sensors.
