## Unit 5 - Introduction of Signal Processing: Classification of systems: Continuous, discrete, linear, causal, stable, dynamic, recursive, time variance; classification of signals: continuous and discrete, energy and power; mathematical representation of signals; spectral density; sampling techniques, quantization, quantization error, Nyquist rate, aliasing effect.

Signal Processing is the field of study that deals with the analysis, modification, and representation of signals. The signals can be continuous or discrete, and they can be used to represent a variety of physical quantities, such as sound, images, and electromagnetic signals.

1. Classification of Systems:
   a. Continuous: The signal is continuous in time and can take on any value within a specified range.
   b. Discrete: The signal is sampled at regular intervals in time and is represented as a sequence of discrete values.
   c. Linear: The signal can be modeled as a linear combination of other signals.
   d. Causal: The signal depends only on past and present values, and not on future values.
   e. Stable: The signal does not grow unbounded over time.
   f. Dynamic: The signal changes over time.
   g. Recursive: The signal can be modeled as a function of its past values.
   h. Time-variant: The signal changes over time in a manner that cannot be modeled by a time-invariant system.

2. Classification of Signals:
   a. Continuous: The signal is continuous in time and can take on any value within a specified range.
   b. Discrete: The signal is sampled at regular intervals in time and is represented as a sequence of discrete values.
   c. Energy: The signal is characterized by the total energy it contains.
   d. Power: The signal is characterized by the average power it contains.

3. Mathematical Representation of Signals: Signals can be represented mathematically using functions, such as sinusoidal functions, polynomials, and Fourier series.

4. Spectral Density: The spectral density of a signal is a measure of the distribution of energy across different frequencies.

5. Sampling Techniques: Sampling is the process of taking a sequence of measurements of a continuous signal at regular intervals in time.

6. Quantization: Quantization is the process of converting a continuous signal into a discrete signal by rounding the values to a finite set of discrete values.

7. Quantization Error: The error introduced by quantization is the difference between the continuous signal and the quantized signal.

8. Nyquist Rate: The Nyquist rate is the minimum sampling rate required to avoid aliasing, which is the phenomenon where high-frequency components of a signal are misrepresented as low-frequency components.

9. Aliasing Effect: The aliasing effect is the phenomenon where high-frequency components of a signal are misrepresented as low-frequency components due to undersampling.

In conclusion, Signal Processing is the field of study that deals with the analysis, modification, and representation of signals. Signals can be classified into different types based on their properties, such as continuity, linearity, and stability, and they can be represented mathematically using functions. The process of converting a continuous signal into a discrete signal is called quantization, and it introduces quantization error. The Nyquist rate is the minimum sampling rate required to avoid aliasing, which is the phenomenon where high-frequency components of a signal are misrepresented as low-frequency components.
