### SPI for the notes of the Unit 3 - HARDWARE COMMUNICATION in the subject of PROGRAMMING AND INTERFACING WITH MICROCONTROLLERS

1. SPI stands for Serial Peripheral Interface.
2. It is a synchronous serial communication interface standard used for short distance communication between microcontrollers and peripheral devices or integrated circuits.
3. SPI uses a master-slave architecture with a single master and one or more slave devices. The master device controls the clock based on which the data is transmitted.
4. SPI uses 4 wires for communication:
- SCK - Serial Clock line. Used to synchronize the transmission between devices.
- MOSI - Master Out Slave In. Used to send data from the master to the slave.
- MISO - Master In Slave Out. Used to send data from the slave to the master.
- SS - Slave Select. Used to select a particular slave device for communication.
5. The data is transmitted in full duplex mode as the MOSI and MISO lines are using simultaneously.
6. The data is transmitted in packets of 8 bits each. The most significant bit is transmitted first.
7. The clock pulses are generated by the master. The faster the clock speed, the faster the data transmission.
8. Due to the synchronous communication and use of dedicated lines, SPI is faster than I2C and more suitable for high speed data transmission. However, it uses more wires and pins.
9. Some applications of SPI include communicating with SD cards, sensors, LCD displays, etc.
