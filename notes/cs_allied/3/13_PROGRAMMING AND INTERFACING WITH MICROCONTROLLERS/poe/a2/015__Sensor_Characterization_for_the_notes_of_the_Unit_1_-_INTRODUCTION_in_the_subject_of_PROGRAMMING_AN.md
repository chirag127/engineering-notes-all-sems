### Sensor Characterization

- Sensors are devices that convert physical quantities into electrical signals.
- The relationship between the physical quantity and the electrical signal is known as the sensor characteristic.
- Important characteristics of a sensor include:
- Sensitivity - The amount the sensor's output changes for a given change in the measured quantity. High sensitivity is desirable for precise measurement.
- Accuracy - How close the sensor's output is to the true value of the measured quantity. Accuracy depends on calibration, linearity and hysteresis.
- Linearity - The sensor's output changes in direct proportion to the measured quantity. Non-linearity can make calibration and interpretation of measurements difficult.
- Hysteresis - The sensor's output depends on past measurements and not just the present measured quantity. This can affect accuracy and repeatability.
- Repeatability - The variation in the sensor's output for repeated measurements of the same physical quantity. Low variation is desirable for precision.
- Response time - The time taken for the sensor's output to stabilize in response to a change in the measured quantity. Fast response is important for dynamic measurements.
- Durability - The sensor's ability to maintain its characteristics over time and in varying environmental conditions. Robustness and reliability are important for long-term use.

The above points cover the key characteristics to consider when selecting and evaluating a sensor for an application. Understanding the sensor characteristics helps in determining if it is suited for the particular measurement requirements.
