### Analog to Digital Conversion for the notes of the Unit 2 - SOFTWARE FRAMEWORKS in the subject of PROGRAMMING AND INTERFACING WITH MICROCONTROLLERS

Analog to Digital Conversion (ADC) is a key process in programming and interfacing with microcontrollers. It involves converting continuous analog signals, such as those from sensors, into digital signals that can be processed by the microcontroller.

The following are some of the key aspects of ADC:

1. Sampling rate: The sampling rate determines how often the analog signal is sampled and converted into a digital signal. The higher the sampling rate, the more accurate the digital representation of the analog signal will be.

2. Resolution: The resolution of the ADC determines the number of bits used to represent the digital signal, and therefore the number of different values that can be represented. The higher the resolution, the more accurate the digital representation of the analog signal will be.

3. Input range: The input range of the ADC determines the range of analog signals that can be converted into digital signals. The input range should be sufficient to cover the expected range of the analog signals being converted.

4. Conversion time: The conversion time of the ADC determines how long it takes to convert an analog signal into a digital signal. The conversion time should be short enough to allow for real-time processing of the analog signals.

5. Error: The error of the ADC refers to the difference between the actual analog signal and the digital representation of the signal. The error should be minimized to ensure accurate representation of the analog signals.

Overall, ADC is a critical process in programming and interfacing with microcontrollers, and involves converting continuous analog signals into digital signals that can be processed by the microcontroller. Understanding the key aspects of ADC, such as the sampling rate, resolution, input range, conversion time, and error, is important for effective programming and interfacing with microcontrollers.
