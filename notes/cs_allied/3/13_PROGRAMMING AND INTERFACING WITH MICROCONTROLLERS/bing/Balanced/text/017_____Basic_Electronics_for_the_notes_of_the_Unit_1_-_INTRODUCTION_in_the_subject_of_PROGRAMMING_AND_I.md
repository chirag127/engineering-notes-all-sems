### Basic Electronics for the notes of the Unit 1 - INTRODUCTION

- Basic electronics is the branch of engineering which deals with the practical application of electrical components and their use in electrical circuits, logic systems, communication systems and other fields.
- Electronics is a much more subtle kind of electricity in which tiny electric currents (and, in theory, single electrons) are carefully directed around much more complex circuits to process signals (such as those that carry radio and television programs) or store and process information.
- Basic electronics looks at the basic concepts of Voltage, Current, Resistance, capacitance, etc. from a practical point of view.
- There are three basic devices which shape up the working and design of all electronic circuits. They are:
  - Resistor: A resistor works as per Ohm’s Law. If V is the voltage across the resistor, I is the current through it and R is the resistance value, then V=IR.
  - Capacitor: A capacitor fundamentally stores electrical energy. It consists of two conductive plates separated by a dielectric material. The amount of charge stored by a capacitor is proportional to the voltage across it. The capacitance C is defined as C=Q/V, where Q is the charge and V is the voltage.
  - Inductor: An inductor is simply a coil of wire, and in fact they are commonly called coils. An inductor stores electrical energy in the form of magnetic field. The amount of magnetic flux generated by an inductor is proportional to the current through it. The inductance L is defined as L=Φ/I, where Φ is the magnetic flux and I is the current.
- There are also some basic electronic devices that are made of semiconductors, which are materials that have electrical properties between those of conductors and insulators. Some of the common semiconductor devices are:
  - Diode: A diode is a device that allows current to flow in one direction only. It has two terminals, called anode and cathode. The anode is connected to the positive side of the voltage source, and the cathode is connected to the negative side. A diode has a characteristic called forward voltage, which is the minimum voltage required to make the diode conduct.
  - Transistor: A transistor is a device that can amplify or switch electrical signals. It has three terminals, called base, collector and emitter. The base is the input terminal, the collector is the output terminal, and the emitter is the common terminal. A transistor can be either a bipolar junction transistor (BJT) or a field-effect transistor (FET), depending on the type of semiconductor material and the structure of the device.
  - Integrated Circuit (IC): An integrated circuit is a device that contains many electronic components, such as resistors, capacitors, diodes, transistors, etc., on a single chip of semiconductor material. An IC can perform a specific function, such as logic, memory, or processing, depending on the design and layout of the components.
