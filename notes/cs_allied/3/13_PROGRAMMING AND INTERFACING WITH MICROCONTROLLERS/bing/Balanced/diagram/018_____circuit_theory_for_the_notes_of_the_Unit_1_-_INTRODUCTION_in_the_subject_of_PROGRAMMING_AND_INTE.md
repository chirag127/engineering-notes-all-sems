### Circuit Theory

Circuit theory is a set of techniques used to describe the flow of energy around an electrical loop. The theory is comprised of a number of different laws, ideas, and definitions. These include:

- **Ohm’s law**: This law states that the current (I) flowing through a resistor (R) is proportional to the voltage (V) across it. The constant of proportionality is the resistance (R). The formula is: `V = IR`.
- **Kirchhoff’s laws**: These laws are based on the conservation of charge and energy in a circuit. They state that:
  - The sum of currents entering a node (a point where two or more wires meet) is equal to the sum of currents leaving that node. This is called Kirchhoff’s current law (KCL).
  - The sum of voltage drops around a closed loop (a path that starts and ends at the same node) is equal to zero. This is called Kirchhoff’s voltage law (KVL).
- **Resistors**: These are components that oppose the flow of current and convert electrical energy into heat. They have a fixed or variable resistance value, measured in ohms (Ω).
- **Capacitors**: These are components that store electrical energy in an electric field. They have a capacitance value, measured in farads (F), which depends on the size and shape of the plates and the material between them. They can charge and discharge according to the applied voltage.
- **Inductors**: These are components that store electrical energy in a magnetic field. They have an inductance value, measured in henrys (H), which depends on the number of turns of wire and the core material. They can oppose changes in current and generate a voltage according to the rate of change of current.

A circuit can be represented by a schematic diagram, which uses symbols to show the components and their connections. A circuit can also be analyzed using various methods, such as nodal analysis, mesh analysis, source transformation, superposition, Thevenin’s theorem, Norton’s theorem, and maximum power transfer theorem. These methods help to find the unknown voltages and currents in a circuit.
