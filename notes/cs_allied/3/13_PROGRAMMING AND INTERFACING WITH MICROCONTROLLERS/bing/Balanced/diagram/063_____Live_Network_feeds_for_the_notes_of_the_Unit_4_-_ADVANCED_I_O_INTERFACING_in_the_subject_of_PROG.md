### Live Network Feeds

- Live network feeds are streams of data or media that are transmitted over the internet in real time to multiple users or devices.
- Live network feeds can be used for various purposes, such as broadcasting news, events, sports, entertainment, education, or social media content.
- Live network feeds can be delivered using different protocols, formats, and technologies, depending on the source, destination, and quality of the stream.
- Some examples of live network feeds are:

  - Live TV news channels, such as ABC, CBS, FOX, and NBC, that offer free 24-hour news streams online or via over-the-air antennas.
  - Live webcams and streaming video cameras, such as EarthCam, that offer views of city skylines, landmarks, beaches, zoos, and landscapes from around the world.
  - Live video conferencing and communication platforms, such as Skype, FaceTime, and Google Hangouts, that use real-time communication protocols to enable one-to-one or one-to-many interactions.
  - Live social media and gaming platforms, such as Facebook, Instagram, YouTube, Twitch, and Discord, that allow users to create, share, and watch live video or audio content with their followers or communities.
  - Live data feeds and APIs, such as Captivate, that provide real-time information and updates on various topics, such as weather, traffic, sports, stocks, and news, to digital displays in office and residential buildings.
