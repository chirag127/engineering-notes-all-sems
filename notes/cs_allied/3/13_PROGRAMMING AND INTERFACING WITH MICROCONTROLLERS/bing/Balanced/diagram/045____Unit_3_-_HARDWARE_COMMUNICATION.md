## Unit 3 - HARDWARE COMMUNICATION

- Hardware communication refers to the electric device and system for transferring data or information from one place to another.
- Hardware communication can be classified into two types: wired and wireless.
- Wired communication uses physical cables or wires to transmit signals, such as telephone lines, coaxial cables, optical fibers, etc.
- Wireless communication uses electromagnetic waves or radio frequencies to transmit signals, such as Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, cellular networks, satellite, etc.
- Hardware communication devices are the hardware components that enable wired or wireless communication, such as modems, routers, switches, gateways, antennas, etc.
- A communication device is a hardware device capable of transmitting an analog or digital signal over the telephone, other communication wire, or wirelessly .
- A classic example of a communication device is a computer modem, which converts a computer's digital information to an analog signal for transmission over a telephone line .
- Another example of a communication device is a network interface card (NIC), which enables a computer to connect to a network and communicate with other devices.
- Hardware communication devices can be internal or external to a computer system.
- Internal communication devices are installed inside the computer case, such as NICs, sound cards, video cards, etc.
- External communication devices are connected to the computer through ports or wireless connections, such as modems, routers, printers, scanners, etc.
- Hardware communication devices require drivers or software programs to function properly and communicate with the operating system and other devices.
- Hardware communication devices also need protocols or rules to establish and maintain communication with other devices.
- Protocols define the format, timing, sequence, and error control of data transmission.
- Some common protocols are TCP/IP, HTTP, FTP, SMTP, etc.
- Hardware communication devices can also use different standards or specifications to ensure compatibility and interoperability with other devices.
- Some common standards are IEEE 802.11 for Wi-Fi, IEEE 802.3 for Ethernet, USB, HDMI, etc.
