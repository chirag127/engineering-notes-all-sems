# Basic Electronics for the notes of the Unit 1 - INTRODUCTION

Basic electronics is the branch of engineering which deals with the practical application of electrical components and their use in electrical circuits, logic systems, communication systems and other fields. Electronics is a much more subtle kind of electricity in which tiny electric currents (and, in theory, single electrons) are carefully directed around much more complex circuits to process signals (such as those that carry radio and television programs) or store and process information. Electronics is fundamental to modern life and pervades everything we do.

Some of the basic concepts of basic electronics are:

- Voltage / Current / Power: Voltage is the difference in electric potential between two points, which causes electric current to flow. Current is the rate of flow of electric charge, measured in amperes (A). Power is the rate of doing work or transferring energy, measured in watts (W). The relationship between voltage, current and power is given by the equation P = VI, where P is power, V is voltage and I is current.
- Resistor: A resistor is a component that resists the flow of electric current, measured in ohms (Ω). Resistors are used to limit the current, divide the voltage, or create heat in a circuit. The relationship between voltage, current and resistance is given by Ohm's law, which states that V = IR, where V is voltage, I is current and R is resistance.
- Capacitor: A capacitor is a component that stores electric charge, measured in farads (F). Capacitors are used to filter, smooth, or store energy in a circuit. The relationship between voltage, current and capacitance is given by the equation I = C dV/dt, where I is current, C is capacitance, V is voltage and t is time.
- Inductor: An inductor is a component that stores magnetic energy, measured in henrys (H). Inductors are used to filter, block, or store energy in a circuit. The relationship between voltage, current and inductance is given by the equation V = L dI/dt, where V is voltage, L is inductance, I is current and t is time.
- Semiconductor: A semiconductor is a material that has electrical conductivity between that of a conductor and an insulator. Semiconductors are the basis of modern electronics, as they can be used to create devices that can amplify, switch, or convert signals. The most common semiconductor material is silicon, which can be doped with impurities to create different types of semiconductor devices.
- Diode: A diode is a semiconductor device that allows current to flow in one direction only. Diodes are used to rectify, protect, or regulate voltage in a circuit. The most common type of diode is the p-n junction diode, which consists of a p-type and an n-type semiconductor material joined together.
- Transistor: A transistor is a semiconductor device that can amplify or switch signals. Transistors are the building blocks of logic gates, amplifiers, and microprocessors. The most common type of transistor is the bipolar junction transistor (BJT), which consists of two p-n junctions connected back-to-back.
- Integrated Circuit (IC): An integrated circuit is a device that contains many transistors and other components on a single chip of semiconductor material. ICs are used to create complex and compact circuits that perform various functions. ICs can be classified into different categories based on their level of integration, such as small-scale integration (SSI), medium-scale integration (MSI), large-scale integration (LSI), and very-large-scale integration (VLSI).
