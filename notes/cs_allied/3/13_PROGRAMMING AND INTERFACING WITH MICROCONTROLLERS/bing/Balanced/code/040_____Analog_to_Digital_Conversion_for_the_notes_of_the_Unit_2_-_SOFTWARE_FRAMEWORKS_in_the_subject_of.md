### Analog to Digital Conversion

- Analog to digital conversion (ADC) is an electronic process that converts a continuous analog signal into a discrete digital signal without altering its essential content.
- ADC is a system that allows digital circuits to interface with the real world by encoding physical quantities such as sound, light, temperature, pressure, etc. into binary codes that can be processed, manipulated, computed, transmitted or stored   .
- ADC consists of three main components: a sample and hold circuit, a quantizer, and an encoder.
  - The sample and hold circuit captures the analog signal at a regular interval and holds its value constant until the next sample is taken.
  - The quantizer assigns a discrete value to the sampled signal based on a predefined scale or resolution. The resolution determines the number of bits used to represent each sample and the accuracy of the conversion.
  - The encoder converts the quantized value into a binary code that can be transmitted or stored as a digital signal.
- ADC can be classified into different types based on the method of quantization and encoding, such as flash ADC, successive approximation ADC, sigma-delta ADC, dual-slope ADC, etc.
- ADC has many applications in digital imaging, radar, audio, communication, instrumentation, etc  .
