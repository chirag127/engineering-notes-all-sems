### Wired/Wireless Networking

- Wired networking is a method of connecting devices using cables, such as Ethernet, coaxial, or fiber optic cables.
- Wireless networking is a method of connecting devices using radio waves, such as Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, or cellular networks.
- Both wired and wireless networking have advantages and disadvantages in terms of speed, reliability, security, and cost.
- Wired networking typically offers faster and more consistent data transfer rates, lower latency, and higher security than wireless networking, but it also requires more installation and maintenance costs, and it limits the mobility and flexibility of the devices.
- Wireless networking typically offers more convenience and mobility, lower installation and maintenance costs, and easier scalability and integration of the devices, but it also suffers from lower data transfer rates, higher latency, and more security risks than wired networking, and it is affected by environmental factors such as interference, noise, and obstacles.
- Some common wired networking standards are:
  - Ethernet: a family of protocols that use twisted pair, coaxial, or fiber optic cables to connect devices in a local area network (LAN) or a wide area network (WAN). Ethernet supports various data rates, from 10 Mbps to 400 Gbps, and various topologies, such as bus, star, ring, or mesh.
  - USB: a standard that defines the physical and logical interfaces for connecting peripheral devices to a host computer using serial cables. USB supports various data rates, from 1.5 Mbps to 20 Gbps, and various device classes, such as mass storage, human interface, audio, or video.
  - Serial: a standard that defines the physical and logical interfaces for transmitting data one bit at a time over a single wire or a pair of wires. Serial supports various data rates, from a few bps to several Mbps, and various protocols, such as RS-232, RS-422, RS-485, or SPI.
- Some common wireless networking standards are:
  - Wi-Fi: a family of protocols that use radio waves to connect devices in a wireless LAN (WLAN) or a wireless WAN (WWAN). Wi-Fi supports various data rates, from 1 Mbps to 9.6 Gbps, and various frequency bands, such as 2.4 GHz, 5 GHz, or 6 GHz.
  - Bluetooth: a standard that defines the physical and logical interfaces for connecting devices in a personal area network (PAN) using radio waves. Bluetooth supports various data rates, from 1 Mbps to 50 Mbps, and various versions, such as Bluetooth Classic, Bluetooth Low Energy, or Bluetooth Mesh.
  - Cellular: a family of protocols that use radio waves to connect devices in a mobile network or a cellular network. Cellular supports various data rates, from 9.6 kbps to 10 Gbps, and various generations, such as 2G, 3G, 4G, or 5G.
