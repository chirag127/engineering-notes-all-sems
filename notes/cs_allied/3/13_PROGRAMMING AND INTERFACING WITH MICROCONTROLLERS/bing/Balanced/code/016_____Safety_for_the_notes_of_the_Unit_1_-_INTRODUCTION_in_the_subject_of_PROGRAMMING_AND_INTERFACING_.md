### Safety

- Safety is the state of being protected from harm or other non-desirable outcomes.
- Safety can also refer to the control of recognized hazards in order to achieve an acceptable level of risk.
- Safety is important for the design and operation of microcontroller-based systems, as they can interact with the physical world and affect human lives, property, or the environment.
- Some of the safety aspects that need to be considered when programming and interfacing with microcontrollers are:

  - Electrical safety: preventing electric shocks, fires, or damage to the microcontroller or other components due to improper wiring, grounding, insulation, or voltage levels.
  - Thermal safety: preventing overheating, burns, or damage to the microcontroller or other components due to excessive current, power dissipation, or ambient temperature.
  - Mechanical safety: preventing injuries, damage, or malfunction due to physical forces, vibrations, impacts, or movements of the microcontroller or other components.
  - Chemical safety: preventing exposure, poisoning, or damage due to hazardous substances, such as batteries, solvents, or corrosive materials, that may be used or produced by the microcontroller or other components.
  - Software safety: preventing errors, failures, or undesired behaviors of the microcontroller or other components due to faulty or malicious code, logic, or data.
  - Human safety: preventing harm or discomfort to the users, operators, or bystanders of the microcontroller-based system due to ergonomic, psychological, or ethical factors.

- Some of the safety measures that can be applied to improve the safety of microcontroller-based systems are:

  - Following the standards, guidelines, and best practices for the design, development, testing, and deployment of microcontroller-based systems.
  - Using appropriate tools, equipment, and protective gear when working with microcontroller-based systems.
  - Applying the principles of fault tolerance, redundancy, diversity, and fail-safe design to increase the reliability and robustness of microcontroller-based systems.
  - Implementing safety features, such as alarms, indicators, switches, sensors, or interlocks, to monitor, control, or prevent hazardous situations or events.
  - Performing regular maintenance, inspection, and calibration of microcontroller-based systems to ensure their proper functioning and performance.
  - Providing adequate documentation, training, and support for the users, operators, or maintainers of microcontroller-based systems.
  - Evaluating the potential risks, hazards, and impacts of microcontroller-based systems on human health, safety, and welfare, and taking appropriate actions to mitigate them.
