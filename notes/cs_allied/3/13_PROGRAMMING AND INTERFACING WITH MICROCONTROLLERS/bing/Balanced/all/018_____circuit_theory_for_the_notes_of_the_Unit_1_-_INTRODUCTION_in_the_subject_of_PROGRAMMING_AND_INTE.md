# Circuit Theory

Circuit theory is a set of techniques used to describe the flow of energy around an electrical loop. The theory is comprised of a number of different laws, ideas, and definitions. These include Ohm’s law and Kirchhoff’s laws, which describe the relationship between current, voltage, and resistance .

## Basic Concepts

- A circuit is a closed path that allows electric current to flow from one point to another.
- A circuit can be composed of various components, such as resistors, capacitors, inductors, switches, sources, and loads.
- A resistor is a component that opposes the flow of current and converts electrical energy into heat.
- A capacitor is a component that stores electrical energy in an electric field and can block direct current (DC) while allowing alternating current (AC) to pass through.
- An inductor is a component that stores electrical energy in a magnetic field and can block AC while allowing DC to pass through.
- A switch is a component that can open or close a circuit, thereby controlling the flow of current.
- A source is a component that provides electrical energy to a circuit, such as a battery or a generator.
- A load is a component that consumes electrical energy from a circuit, such as a light bulb or a motor.

## Basic Laws

- Ohm’s law states that the voltage across a resistor is proportional to the current through it, and the constant of proportionality is the resistance. The formula is V = IR, where V is the voltage, I is the current, and R is the resistance.
- Kirchhoff’s current law (KCL) states that the algebraic sum of the currents entering and leaving a node (a point where two or more components are connected) is zero. This means that the current that flows into a node is equal to the current that flows out of it.
- Kirchhoff’s voltage law (KVL) states that the algebraic sum of the voltages around a closed loop (a path that starts and ends at the same point) is zero. This means that the voltage rise across a source is equal to the voltage drop across the components in the loop.

## Basic Techniques

- Circuit analysis is the process of finding the unknown quantities (such as voltage, current, power, or resistance) in a circuit using the basic laws and concepts.
- Circuit simplification is the process of reducing a complex circuit into an equivalent simpler circuit using series and parallel combinations of components.
- Series combination is when two or more components are connected end to end, such that they have the same current flowing through them.
- Parallel combination is when two or more components are connected across the same two nodes, such that they have the same voltage across them.
- Circuit theorems are general methods that can be applied to any circuit to simplify the analysis, such as superposition, Thevenin’s theorem, Norton’s theorem, and maximum power transfer theorem.
