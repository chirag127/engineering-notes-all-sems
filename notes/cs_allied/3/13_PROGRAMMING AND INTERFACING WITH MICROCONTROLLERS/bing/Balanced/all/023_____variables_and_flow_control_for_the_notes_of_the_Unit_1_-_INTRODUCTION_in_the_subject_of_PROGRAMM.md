# Variables and Flow Control

## Variables
- Variables are named containers that store data or values in a program.
- Variables have a data type, such as integer, float, string, boolean, etc., that determines what kind of values they can hold and how they are stored in memory.
- Variables can be declared, assigned, and used in a program to perform operations, store results, and pass information between functions or modules.
- Variables can be local or global, depending on their scope. Local variables are only accessible within a specific block of code, such as a function or a loop. Global variables are accessible throughout the entire program, but they can cause conflicts or errors if not used carefully.
- Variables can be constant or mutable, depending on whether their values can be changed or not. Constant variables are declared with a keyword such as `const` or `final`, and their values cannot be modified after initialization. Mutable variables can be reassigned with new values at any point in the program.

## Flow Control
- Flow control is the mechanism that determines the order of execution of statements or instructions in a program.
- Flow control can be influenced by variables, expressions, conditions, and user input, depending on the logic and design of the program.
- Flow control can be divided into three basic types: sequence, selection, and iteration.
- Sequence is the default flow of control, where statements are executed one after another in the order they appear in the program.
- Selection is the conditional flow of control, where statements are executed based on the evaluation of a condition or a comparison. Selection structures include `if`, `if-else`, `switch`, and `ternary` operators.
- Iteration is the repetitive flow of control, where statements are executed multiple times until a condition is met or a limit is reached. Iteration structures include `for`, `while`, `do-while`, and `for-each` loops.
