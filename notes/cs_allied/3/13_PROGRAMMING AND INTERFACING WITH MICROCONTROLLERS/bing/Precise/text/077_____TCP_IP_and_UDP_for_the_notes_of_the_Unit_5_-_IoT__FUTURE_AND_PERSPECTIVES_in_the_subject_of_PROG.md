### TCP/IP and UDP for the notes of the Unit 5 - IoT, FUTURE AND PERSPECTIVES in the subject of PROGRAMMING AND INTERFACING WITH MICROCONTROLLERS

- TCP/IP (Transmission Control Protocol/Internet Protocol) is a suite of communication protocols used to interconnect network devices on the internet.
- TCP/IP specifies how data is exchanged over the internet by providing end-to-end communications that identify how it should be broken into packets, addressed, transmitted, routed and received at the destination.
- UDP (User Datagram Protocol) is an alternative communication protocol to TCP/IP. It is a simpler, connectionless Internet protocol.
- Unlike TCP/IP, UDP does not provide the reliability, ordering, or data integrity that TCP/IP does. Instead, it is used for applications that do not require these features, such as streaming audio and video.
- In the context of IoT (Internet of Things), TCP/IP and UDP are important protocols for enabling communication between devices and the internet.
- TCP/IP is commonly used for reliable data transfer, while UDP is used for real-time communication, such as voice and video streaming.
- Both protocols are essential for the functioning of IoT devices and their ability to connect and communicate with other devices and systems.
