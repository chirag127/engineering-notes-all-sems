### Serial UART Communication

- UART stands for Universal Asynchronous Receiver/Transmitter.
- It is a hardware device for asynchronous serial communication between devices.
- UARTs transmit data in a serial format, meaning one bit at a time, with a start bit, data bits, parity bit, and stop bit.
- The start bit signals the beginning of a new data packet, while the stop bit signals the end of the packet.
- The parity bit is used for error checking and can be set to even, odd, or none.
- The data bits can range from 5 to 9 bits, with 8 bits being the most common.
- The baud rate is the rate at which data is transmitted and is measured in bits per second (bps).
- UARTs can operate in full-duplex mode, meaning they can transmit and receive data simultaneously.
- UARTs are commonly used for communication between microcontrollers and peripherals such as sensors, GPS modules, and Bluetooth modules.
- UART communication can be implemented using hardware or software, with hardware implementations being faster and more reliable.
- Common applications of UART communication include serial ports on computers, RS-232 and RS-485 communication, and communication between microcontrollers and other devices.
