### ADC

ADC stands for Analog to Digital Converter. It is a device that converts an analog signal, such as a sound picked up by a microphone or light entering a digital camera, into a digital signal. ADCs are used in various applications, including audio and video systems, measurement and control systems, and communication systems.

In the context of Unit 2 - SOFTWARE FRAMEWORKS in the subject of PROGRAMMING AND INTERFACING WITH MICROCONTROLLERS, ADCs are important because they allow microcontrollers to interact with the analog world. Microcontrollers are digital devices, meaning they can only process digital signals. However, many sensors and other devices produce analog signals. ADCs are used to convert these analog signals into digital signals that the microcontroller can process.

Some key points to remember about ADCs are:

- ADCs convert an analog signal into a digital signal.
- ADCs are used in various applications, including audio and video systems, measurement and control systems, and communication systems.
- In the context of microcontrollers, ADCs allow microcontrollers to interact with the analog world.
- ADCs are important for interfacing with sensors and other devices that produce analog signals.
