### ADC

Analog-to-Digital Converter (ADC) is a device that converts an analog signal into a digital signal. It is an essential component in the field of electronics, particularly in the design of microcontroller-based systems. ADCs are used in a wide range of applications, including data acquisition, control systems, and digital signal processing.

Here are some key points to remember about ADCs:

1. ADCs convert a continuous analog signal into a discrete digital signal.
2. The resolution of an ADC is determined by the number of bits it uses to represent the digital output.
3. The accuracy of an ADC is affected by factors such as quantization error, non-linearity, and noise.
4. There are several types of ADCs, including successive approximation, flash, and sigma-delta.
5. ADCs are commonly used in microcontroller-based systems to interface with sensors and other analog devices.
