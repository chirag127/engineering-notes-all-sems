# Interfacing to the Real World

- Interfacing is the process of connecting a microcontroller to external devices or systems, such as sensors, actuators, displays, keyboards, etc.
- Interfacing allows the microcontroller to interact with the real world, by receiving inputs from sensors and sending outputs to actuators.
- Interfacing requires the use of appropriate hardware and software components, such as interface circuits, drivers, protocols, etc.
- Interfacing can be classified into two types: parallel and serial.
- Parallel interfacing involves the use of multiple data lines to transfer data between the microcontroller and the external device. Parallel interfacing is faster, but requires more pins and wires.
- Serial interfacing involves the use of one or few data lines to transfer data between the microcontroller and the external device. Serial interfacing is slower, but requires less pins and wires.
- Some examples of parallel interfacing are: LCD display, LED matrix, keypad, etc.
- Some examples of serial interfacing are: UART, SPI, I2C, etc.
