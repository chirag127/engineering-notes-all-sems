# Measurements

- Measurements are the process of obtaining the magnitude of a quantity relative to an agreed standard.
- Measurements are essential for scientific and engineering applications, such as designing, testing, and controlling systems and devices.
- Measurements can be classified into two types: direct and indirect.
  - Direct measurements are obtained by comparing the quantity directly with a standard unit, such as using a ruler to measure length or a balance to measure mass.
  - Indirect measurements are obtained by using a mathematical relationship between the quantity and another measurable quantity, such as using Ohm's law to measure resistance or using a stopwatch to measure speed.
- Measurements can be affected by various factors, such as the accuracy and precision of the measuring instrument, the environmental conditions, and the human errors.
  - Accuracy is the degree of closeness of a measured value to the true value of the quantity.
  - Precision is the degree of consistency or repeatability of a measured value.
  - Environmental conditions, such as temperature, humidity, pressure, and noise, can influence the performance of the measuring instrument and the quantity being measured.
  - Human errors, such as misreading, miscalculating, or misinterpreting the measurement results, can cause deviations from the true value of the quantity.
- Measurements can be expressed using different units, such as the SI units, the imperial units, or the customary units.
  - SI units are the international system of units, based on seven base units: meter (m) for length, kilogram (kg) for mass, second (s) for time, ampere (A) for electric current, kelvin (K) for temperature, candela (cd) for luminous intensity, and mole (mol) for amount of substance.
  - Imperial units are the system of units used in the United Kingdom and some former British colonies, based on units such as inch, foot, yard, mile, ounce, pound, gallon, and degree Fahrenheit.
  - Customary units are the system of units used in the United States and some other countries, based on units such as inch, foot, yard, mile, ounce, pound, gallon, and degree Fahrenheit.
- Measurements can be converted from one unit to another using conversion factors, which are ratios of equivalent values in different units.
  - For example, to convert from meters to feet, the conversion factor is 1 m = 3.28084 ft, so 10 m = 10 x 3.28084 ft = 32.8084 ft.
  - To convert from Fahrenheit to Celsius, the conversion factor is (F - 32) x 5/9 = C, so 68°F = (68 - 32) x 5/9 = 20°C.
