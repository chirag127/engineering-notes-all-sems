### Wired/Wireless Networking for the notes of the Unit 4 - ADVANCED I/O INTERFACING in the subject of PROGRAMMING AND INTERFACING WITH MICROCONTROLLERS

- Wired networking is the process of connecting devices using cables, such as Ethernet, USB, or serial ports, to enable data exchange and communication.
- Wireless networking is the process of connecting devices using radio waves, such as Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, ZigBee, or cellular, to enable data exchange and communication.
- Wired networking has advantages such as higher speed, lower latency, better security, and lower interference, but also disadvantages such as higher cost, lower mobility, more complexity, and more maintenance.
- Wireless networking has advantages such as lower cost, higher mobility, less complexity, and less maintenance, but also disadvantages such as lower speed, higher latency, lower security, and higher interference.
- Microcontrollers are small computers that can be embedded in devices to perform specific tasks, such as controlling sensors, actuators, displays, or communication modules.
- Microcontrollers can interface with wired or wireless networks using different protocols and standards, depending on the application and the requirements.
- Some examples of wired network interfaces for microcontrollers are:
  - Ethernet: a common standard for local area networks (LANs) that uses twisted pair or optical fiber cables to transmit data at speeds up to 10 Gbps. Ethernet can be used for applications that require high bandwidth, reliability, and security, such as industrial automation, smart grid, or video streaming. Microcontrollers can use Ethernet controllers or modules to connect to Ethernet networks.
  - USB: a universal standard for serial communication that uses cables with different connectors to transmit data at speeds up to 10 Gbps. USB can be used for applications that require easy plug-and-play, power delivery, or device enumeration, such as personal computers, peripherals, or mobile devices. Microcontrollers can use USB controllers or modules to connect to USB devices or hosts.
  - Serial: a simple and low-cost standard for serial communication that uses cables with different pinouts to transmit data at speeds up to 115.2 kbps. Serial can be used for applications that require low bandwidth, low power, or long distance, such as sensors, actuators, or modems. Microcontrollers can use serial ports or modules to connect to serial devices or networks.
- Some examples of wireless network interfaces for microcontrollers are:
  - Wi-Fi: a popular standard for wireless local area networks (WLANs) that uses radio waves in the 2.4 GHz or 5 GHz bands to transmit data at speeds up to 1.3 Gbps. Wi-Fi can be used for applications that require high bandwidth, mobility, or internet access, such as smart home, smart city, or cloud computing. Microcontrollers can use Wi-Fi controllers or modules to connect to Wi-Fi networks or devices .
  - Bluetooth: a widely used standard for wireless personal area networks (WPANs) that uses radio waves in the 2.4 GHz band to transmit data at speeds up to 24 Mbps. Bluetooth can be used for applications that require low bandwidth, low power, or short distance, such as wireless headphones, keyboards, or mice. Microcontrollers can use Bluetooth controllers or modules to connect to Bluetooth devices or networks .
  - ZigBee: a low-power and low-cost standard for wireless sensor networks (WSNs) that uses radio waves in the 2.4 GHz band to transmit data at speeds up to 250 kbps. ZigBee can be used for applications that require low bandwidth, low power, or long distance, such as smart lighting, smart metering, or environmental monitoring. Microcontrollers can use ZigBee controllers or modules to connect to ZigBee devices or networks .
  - Cellular: a global standard for wireless wide area networks (WWANs) that uses radio waves in various frequency bands to transmit data at speeds up to 10 Gbps. Cellular can be used for applications that require high bandwidth, mobility, or global coverage, such as smartphones, tablets, or vehicles. Microcontrollers can use cellular controllers or modules to connect to cellular networks or devices .
