### Interfacing to the Real World

- Interfacing is the process of connecting a microcontroller to external devices, such as sensors, actuators, displays, keyboards, etc.
- Interfacing allows the microcontroller to interact with the real world, by receiving inputs from sensors and sending outputs to actuators.
- Interfacing requires the use of appropriate hardware and software components, such as interface circuits, drivers, protocols, libraries, etc.
- Interfacing can be classified into two types: parallel and serial.
- Parallel interfacing involves the use of multiple data lines to transfer data between the microcontroller and the external device. Parallel interfacing is faster, but requires more pins and wires.
- Serial interfacing involves the use of one or few data lines to transfer data between the microcontroller and the external device. Serial interfacing is slower, but requires less pins and wires.
- Some of the common serial interfacing protocols are: UART, SPI, I2C, CAN, USB, etc.
- Some of the common parallel interfacing protocols are: GPIO, LCD, ADC, DAC, etc.
- Interfacing can also be classified into two modes: synchronous and asynchronous.
- Synchronous interfacing involves the use of a common clock signal to synchronize the data transfer between the microcontroller and the external device. Synchronous interfacing is more reliable, but requires an additional clock line.
- Asynchronous interfacing does not involve the use of a common clock signal, but relies on the timing of the data bits or the use of start and stop bits to synchronize the data transfer between the microcontroller and the external device. Asynchronous interfacing is more flexible, but requires more error checking.
- Some of the common synchronous interfacing protocols are: SPI, I2C, CAN, USB, etc.
- Some of the common asynchronous interfacing protocols are: UART, RS-232, RS-485, etc.
