# Arduino

Arduino is an open-source electronics platform based on easy-to-use hardware and software. Arduino boards are able to read inputs - light on a sensor, a finger on a button, or a Twitter message - and turn it into an output - activating a motor, turning on an LED, publishing something online.

Arduino consists of both a physical programmable circuit board (often referred to as a microcontroller) and a piece of software, or IDE (Integrated Development Environment) that runs on your computer, used to write and upload computer code to the physical board.

Arduino is a company, a project, and a user community that designs and manufactures single-board microcontrollers and microcontroller kits for building digital devices.

## Unit 4 - ADVANCED I/O INTERFACING

In this unit, you will learn how to interface Arduino with various input and output devices, such as sensors, displays, motors, and wireless modules. You will also learn how to use interrupts, timers, and serial communication to enhance the functionality and performance of your Arduino projects.

Some of the topics covered in this unit are:

- Analog input and output: how to use analog pins to read analog signals from sensors and generate analog signals for actuators.
- PWM: how to use pulse-width modulation to control the brightness of LEDs, the speed of motors, and the frequency of sound.
- LCD: how to use liquid crystal displays to show text and graphics on your Arduino projects.
- Servo: how to use servo motors to create precise and smooth movements for robotics and automation.
- Stepper: how to use stepper motors to create precise and accurate movements for CNC machines and 3D printers.
- DC: how to use DC motors to create fast and powerful movements for robotics and vehicles.
- Encoder: how to use rotary encoders to measure the rotation and position of motors and wheels.
- IR: how to use infrared sensors and emitters to detect obstacles, measure distance, and communicate wirelessly.
- Bluetooth: how to use Bluetooth modules to connect your Arduino projects to your smartphone, tablet, or computer.
- WiFi: how to use WiFi modules to connect your Arduino projects to the internet and access web services and data.
- RFID: how to use radio frequency identification modules to read and write data from RFID tags and cards.
- GPS: how to use global positioning system modules to get the location and time of your Arduino projects.
- IMU: how to use inertial measurement unit modules to get the orientation and acceleration of your Arduino projects.
