### IEEE 802.11

- IEEE 802.11 is a set of standards for wireless local area networks (WLANs) that operate in the 2.4 GHz, 5 GHz, and 60 GHz frequency bands .
- IEEE 802.11 defines the physical layer (PHY) and the medium access control (MAC) layer specifications for WLANs.
- IEEE 802.11 has several amendments that extend or modify the original standard, such as IEEE 802.11a, IEEE 802.11b, IEEE 802.11g, IEEE 802.11n, IEEE 802.11p, and IEEE 802.11ad .
- Some of the main features and characteristics of IEEE 802.11 standards are:

  - IEEE 802.11 supports data rates from 1 Mbps to 7 Gbps depending on the amendment and the modulation scheme .
  - IEEE 802.11 uses either frequency-hopping spread spectrum (FHSS), direct-sequence spread spectrum (DSSS), orthogonal frequency-division multiplexing (OFDM), or single-carrier frequency-division multiple access (SC-FDMA) as the modulation techniques .
  - IEEE 802.11 uses either contention-based or contention-free access methods to share the wireless medium among multiple stations .
  - IEEE 802.11 supports two modes of operation: infrastructure mode and ad hoc mode. In infrastructure mode, stations communicate through an access point (AP) that connects to a wired network. In ad hoc mode, stations communicate directly with each other without an AP .
  - IEEE 802.11 provides various security mechanisms, such as Wired Equivalent Privacy (WEP), Wi-Fi Protected Access (WPA), and IEEE 802.11i, to protect the confidentiality, integrity, and availability of the wireless data .
  - IEEE 802.11 is also a basis for vehicle-based communication networks with IEEE 802.11p, which enables wireless access in vehicular environments (WAVE) for intelligent transportation systems (ITS) applications.
