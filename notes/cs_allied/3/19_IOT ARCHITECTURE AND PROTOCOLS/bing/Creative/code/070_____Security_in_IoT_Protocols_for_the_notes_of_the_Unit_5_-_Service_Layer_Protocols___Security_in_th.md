### Security in IoT Protocols

- Security is a vital aspect of IoT systems, as they involve pervasive data collection and dissemination, and may affect critical sectors such as economy and national security.
- IoT protocols are designed to operate in multiple layers and provide security at various layers, such as data, network, transport, and application.
- Some of the common security protocols for IoT are:
  - MQTT: Message Queuing Telemetry Transport, a client-server communicating messaging transport protocol that uses a publish-subscribe model and supports encryption and authentication.
  - CoAP: Constrained Application Protocol, a web transfer protocol that uses a request-response model and supports DTLS (Datagram Transport Layer Security) for end-to-end security.
  - LwM2M: Lightweight Machine-to-Machine, a device management protocol that uses CoAP as a transport layer and supports DTLS and OSCORE (Object Security for Constrained RESTful Environments) for security.
  - AMQP: Advanced Message Queuing Protocol, a message-oriented middleware protocol that uses a peer-to-peer model and supports TLS (Transport Layer Security) and SASL (Simple Authentication and Security Layer) for security.
  - DDS: Data Distribution Service, a data-centric publish-subscribe protocol that supports RTPS (Real-Time Publish-Subscribe) for interoperability and DDS Security for data protection, authentication, and access control.
- Some of the common security challenges and threats for IoT are:
  - Data privacy: The protection of sensitive and personal data from unauthorized access, use, or disclosure.
  - Authentication: The verification of the identity and legitimacy of the communicating parties.
  - Authorization: The granting or denying of access rights and privileges to the resources and services.
  - Trust management: The establishment and maintenance of trust relationships among the heterogeneous and distributed entities.
  - Denial-of-service: The disruption of the availability and functionality of the system or service by overwhelming it with malicious traffic or requests.
  - Eavesdropping: The interception and analysis of the transmitted data by unauthorized parties.
  - Replay: The retransmission of the captured data by malicious parties to impersonate or deceive the legitimate parties.
  - Tampering: The modification or alteration of the data or device by malicious parties to compromise the integrity or functionality of the system or service.
  - Spoofing: The falsification of the identity or location of the device or data by malicious parties to deceive or mislead the legitimate parties.
