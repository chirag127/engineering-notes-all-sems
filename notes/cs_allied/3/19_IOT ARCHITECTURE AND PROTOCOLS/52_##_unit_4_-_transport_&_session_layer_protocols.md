## Unit 4 - Transport & Session Layer Protocols

Sure, here's a detailed explanation of the Unit 4 - Transport & Session Layer Protocols in computer networking:

The transport and session layer protocols in computer networking are responsible for ensuring the reliable delivery of data between devices on a network. These protocols operate at the fourth and fifth layers of the OSI (Open Systems Interconnection) model, respectively.

The transport layer provides a communication channel between applications running on different devices, ensuring that data is transmitted reliably and efficiently. The most commonly used transport layer protocol is TCP (Transmission Control Protocol), which provides a reliable, connection-oriented communication channel between devices.

The session layer is responsible for establishing and maintaining connections between devices, and for managing the flow of data between them. The most commonly used session layer protocol is SSL (Secure Sockets Layer) or its successor TLS (Transport Layer Security), which provides encryption and authentication for secure communication between devices.

In addition to TCP and SSL/TLS, there are several other transport and session layer protocols that are used in computer networking, including UDP (User Datagram Protocol), SCTP (Stream Control Transmission Protocol), and DCCP (Datagram Congestion Control Protocol).

In conclusion, the transport and session layer protocols in computer networking are responsible for ensuring the reliable delivery of data between devices on a network. The transport layer provides a communication channel between applications, while the session layer establishes and maintains connections between devices and manages the flow of data between them. There are several different transport and session layer protocols that are used in computer networking, including TCP, UDP, SSL/TLS, SCTP, and DCCP.
