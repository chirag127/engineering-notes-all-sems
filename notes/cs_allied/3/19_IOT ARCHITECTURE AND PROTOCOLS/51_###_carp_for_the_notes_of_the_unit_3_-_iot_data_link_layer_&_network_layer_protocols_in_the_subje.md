### CARP for the notes of the Unit 3 - IOT Data Link Layer & Network Layer Protocols in the subject of IOT ARCHITECTURE AND PROTOCOLS

CARP (Common Address Redundancy Protocol) is a network protocol that provides redundancy for IP addresses in a network. CARP is commonly used in Internet Protocol (IP) networks to provide failover protection for IP addresses, ensuring that if one network device fails, another device can take over its IP address and continue to provide service.

CARP works by allowing multiple network devices to share a common IP address. When a network device fails, another device can take over the IP address and continue to provide service, ensuring that the IP address remains available and that the network continues to function.

CARP operates at the network layer of the IP network, and is compatible with a wide range of network devices, including routers, switches, and firewalls. CARP can also be used in combination with other redundancy protocols, such as Virtual Router Redundancy Protocol (VRRP) and Hot Standby Router Protocol (HSRP), to provide additional levels of redundancy and failover protection.

In summary, CARP is a network protocol that provides redundancy for IP addresses in a network. By allowing multiple network devices to share a common IP address, CARP ensures that if one device fails, another device can take over its IP address and continue to provide service, ensuring that the IP address remains available and that the network continues to function. Understanding CARP and its role in IP networks is an important aspect of the study of IoT Architecture and Protocols.
