### RPL for the notes of the Unit 3 - IOT Data Link Layer & Network Layer Protocols in the subject of IOT ARCHITECTURE AND PROTOCOLS

RPL, or the Routing Protocol for Low-Power and Lossy Networks, is a protocol designed for low-power and lossy networks commonly used in IoT applications. Here are some important notes on RPL:

- RPL is a routing protocol that uses a distance-vector algorithm to determine the best path for packets to travel in a network.
- RPL is designed to support IPv6, making it particularly useful for IoT applications that require large address spaces.
- RPL can be used in both mesh and tree network topologies, making it flexible for different IoT applications.
- RPL supports various metrics for determining the best path, including hop count, link quality, and energy consumption.
- RPL allows for multi-path routing, meaning that packets can be sent through multiple paths to reach their destination.
- RPL uses a Destination Oriented Directed Acyclic Graph (DODAG) to represent the network topology, with nodes acting as parents and children to form a hierarchical structure.
- RPL uses a Trickle algorithm to conserve energy by reducing the frequency of messages sent between nodes.
- RPL supports different modes of operation, including storing mode, non-storing mode, and hybrid mode, to accommodate different network requirements.

In summary, RPL is a flexible and efficient routing protocol designed for low-power and lossy IoT networks. It supports IPv6, can be used in different network topologies, and conserves energy through the use of a Trickle algorithm. Understanding RPL is important for anyone working with IoT networks and protocols.
