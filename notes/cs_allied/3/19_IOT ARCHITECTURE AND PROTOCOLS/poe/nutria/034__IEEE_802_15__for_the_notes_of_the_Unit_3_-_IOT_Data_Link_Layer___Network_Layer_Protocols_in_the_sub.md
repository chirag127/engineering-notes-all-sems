
### IEEE 802.15

IEEE 802.15 is a set of standards for wireless personal area networks (WPANs). It is designed for low-rate data transfer over short distances. It is commonly used in Internet of Things (IoT) applications, such as home automation, medical applications, and industrial automation.

The IEEE 802.15 standard consists of three layers:

1. Physical Layer: The physical layer is responsible for transmitting and receiving data over a wireless medium. It includes specifications for modulation, coding, and frequency hopping.

2. Media Access Control (MAC) Layer: The MAC layer is responsible for managing access to the wireless medium. It includes specifications for contention-based and contention-free access mechanisms.

3. Network Layer: The network layer is responsible for routing data between nodes. It includes specifications for addressing, routing, and quality of service.

IEEE 802.15 is designed for low-rate data transfer over short distances. It is optimized for low power consumption, low complexity, and low cost. It is suitable for applications such as home automation, medical applications, and industrial automation.
