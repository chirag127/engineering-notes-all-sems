### ZWave

ZWave is a wireless communication protocol used for home automation. It was developed by Zensys in 1999 and is now owned by Silicon Labs. ZWave operates in the sub-GHz frequency band, typically 908.42 MHz in the US and 868.42 MHz in Europe, and uses a mesh network topology.

Here are some key points to know about ZWave:

- ZWave is a low-power, low-bandwidth protocol designed for home automation applications such as lighting, security, and climate control.
- ZWave uses a mesh network topology, which means that devices can communicate with each other directly or through other devices in the network.
- ZWave supports up to 232 devices in a single network, which can cover a range of up to 100 meters indoors and 300 meters outdoors.
- ZWave uses a frequency-shift keying (FSK) modulation scheme and can transmit data at a rate of up to 100 kbps.
- ZWave uses a simple ACK/NACK protocol for reliable transmission and also supports retransmission and error correction to improve reliability.
- ZWave supports encryption and authentication to ensure secure communication between devices.
- ZWave devices can be controlled using a variety of interfaces, including mobile apps, voice assistants, and physical controllers.
- ZWave is interoperable with other home automation systems through the use of gateways and bridges.

Overall, ZWave is a reliable and secure protocol for home automation applications that offers a range of features and interoperability with other systems.
