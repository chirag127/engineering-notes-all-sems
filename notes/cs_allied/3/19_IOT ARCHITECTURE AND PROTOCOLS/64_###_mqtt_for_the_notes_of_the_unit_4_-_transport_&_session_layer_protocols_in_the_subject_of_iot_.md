MQTT (Message Queuing Telemetry Transport) is a lightweight, publish-subscribe messaging protocol that is used for IoT (Internet of Things) applications. In the context of Unit 4 - Transport & Session Layer Protocols in the subject of IoT Architecture and Protocols, MQTT is an important protocol for IoT systems, as it provides a simple and efficient mechanism for transmitting data between IoT devices.

MQTT is designed for low-bandwidth, high-latency networks, such as those commonly found in IoT systems. It uses a publish-subscribe model, in which devices can publish data to a central broker, and other devices can subscribe to that data. The broker is responsible for routing the data to the appropriate subscribers.

MQTT provides several key features, including:

1. Lightweight: MQTT is designed to be lightweight and efficient, making it well-suited for use in IoT systems.

2. Publish-subscribe model: MQTT uses a publish-subscribe model, in which devices can publish data to a central broker, and other devices can subscribe to that data.

3. Quality of Service (QoS): MQTT provides three levels of Quality of Service (QoS), which determine the level of reliability of the data transmission.

4. Security: MQTT provides basic security features, such as username and password authentication, and SSL/TLS encryption.

In IoT systems, MQTT is used to transmit data between devices, and to provide a mechanism for devices to subscribe to data from other devices. MQTT is widely used in IoT systems due to its lightweight design, efficient data transmission, and support for Quality of Service (QoS) and security.
