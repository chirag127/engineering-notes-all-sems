### Cluster Computing for the notes of the Unit 1 - Overview of Grid Computing Technology in the subject of High Performance Computing
Cluster computing is a form of parallel computing that involves a group of interconnected computers working together to perform a common task. It is used to solve large-scale problems by dividing the workload among multiple computers. The computers in a cluster communicate with each other through a high-speed network and share data and processing power. Clusters are often used in high-performance computing (HPC) applications, such as scientific simulations, weather forecasting, and financial modeling. They provide a cost-effective alternative to supercomputers, which can be expensive and difficult to maintain. 

In cluster computing, each computer is referred to as a node and the nodes are managed by a central controller, known as a cluster manager. The cluster manager is responsible for distributing the workload, monitoring the health of the nodes, and coordinating the data exchange between nodes. The nodes in a cluster can be either homogeneous or heterogeneous, meaning they can have similar or different hardware and software configurations.

Cluster computing is a popular choice for HPC applications because it provides a high degree of scalability and reliability. As the demand for computing power increases, additional nodes can be added to the cluster to increase its processing power. In the event of a node failure, the cluster manager can automatically redirect the workload to other nodes, ensuring that the computing job continues without interruption.

In conclusion, cluster computing is a cost-effective and reliable way to perform large-scale computations in HPC applications. It provides scalability, reliability, and high performance, making it an important technology in the field of high-performance computing.
