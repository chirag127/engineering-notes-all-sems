Each computer on the internet has a unique address, called an IP address, that allows it to communicate with other computers. Computers are connected to routers, which are devices that forward data packets between networks. Routers are connected to other routers, forming a network of networks. The internet is the global system of interconnected networks that use the Internet Protocol Suite (TCP/IP) to communicate. The Internet Protocol Suite is a set of rules that define how data is formatted, transmitted, and received over the internet. The internet also relies on various protocols and standards, such as the Domain Name System (DNS), which translates human-readable names (such as www.wikipedia.org) into IP addresses (such as 208.80.154.224).

The internet has evolved from its origins in the 1950s and 1960s as a research project of the United States Department of Defense (DoD) and the Advanced Research Projects Agency (ARPA). The first network to use packet switching, a technique that breaks data into small blocks and sends them independently, was the ARPANET, which was launched in 1969. The ARPANET connected four universities in the United States and was later expanded to include other research institutions and government agencies. The ARPANET was also the first network to use the TCP/IP protocols, which were developed by Bob Kahn and Vint Cerf in the 1970s. The TCP/IP protocols enabled different types of networks to interoperate and form the internet.

The internet also grew from the contributions of other researchers and organizations around the world, such as Paul Baran and Donald Davies, who independently proposed the concept of packet switching in the early 1960s, and the National Physical Laboratory (NPL) in the United Kingdom, which developed the first operational packet-switched network in 1969. Other early networks that influenced the development of the internet include the CYCLADES network in France, which introduced the concept of end-to-end reliability, the Merit Network in the United States, which connected several universities in Michigan, and the International Packet Switched Service (IPSS), which provided the first international connections between networks.
