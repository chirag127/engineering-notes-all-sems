### Biology in India and Ancient India

- In the Vedic period, observations of plants and animals were carried out and recorded. 740 plants and 250 animals were recorded in the Vedic literature.
- Indian civilization probably dates back to about 6000 B.C. Information about biology in ancient India comes from the Vedas, a collection of four Sanskrit texts.
- Using proper scientific methods, it has been proclaimed that biology in ancient India was not only at an advanced stage, but even better than 20th century biological knowledge. Information about ancient plants and animals is available from both material (archaeological) and textual sources.
- As early as 2500 B.C., the people of northwestern India had a well-developed science of agriculture. The ruins at Mohenjo-daro have yielded seeds of wheat and barley that were cultivated at that time. Millet, dates, melons, and other fruits and vegetables, as well as cotton, were known to the civilization.
- The Project of History of Indian Science, Philosophy and Culture has a monograph series, Volume 3, titled "Mathematics, Astronomy and Biology in Indian Tradition" edited by D. P. Chattopadhyaya and Ravinder Kumar.
