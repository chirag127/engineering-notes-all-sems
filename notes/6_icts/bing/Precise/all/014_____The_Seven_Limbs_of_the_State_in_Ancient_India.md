### The Seven Limbs of the State in Ancient India

The Seven Limbs of the State, also known as the Saptanga Theory, was formulated by Kautilya in his work Arthashastra. It refers to the seven organs or constituents of the state, which are as follows:

1. **Swamin** - The King: The King is independent and his responsibility for peace, justice, and stability is stressed.
2. **Amatya** - The Minister or Bureaucrats: The Minister or Bureaucrats are responsible for the administration of the state.
3. **Janapada** - The Land and the People or Territory: The Land and the People or Territory refers to the geographical area and population of the state.
4. **Durga** - The Fortress or Fort: The Fortress or Fort refers to the defensive structures of the state.
5. **Kosha** - The Treasury or Treasure: The Treasury or Treasure refers to the financial resources of the state.
6. **Danda** - The Army or Coercive Authority: The Army or Coercive Authority refers to the military power of the state.
7. **Mitra** - The Allies: The Allies refers to the friendly relations and alliances with other states.

This entire set-up of the kingdom was described as the Saptanga Theory in ancient India. The Saptanga Theory categorically identified the ideal type qualities/excellencies of the state, and determined whether a state would evolve into an inferior or superior entity.
