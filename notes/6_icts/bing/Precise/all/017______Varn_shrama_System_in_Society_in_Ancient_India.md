#### Varnāshrama System in Society in Ancient India

The Varnāshrama system, also known as Varnashramadharma, was a social structure that developed in ancient Hindu society. The term ‘Varnashramadharma’ consists of three words - ‘varna’, ‘asrama’, and ‘dharma’.

- **Varna**: Theoretically, the Varna system refers to the segmental division of the Hindu society into four classes. These four classes, or varnas, are Brahman, Kshatriya, Vaishya, and Sudra. Brahman is the class of the priests and teachers. Kshatriya is the class of the kings, warriors, and aristocrats. Vaishya is the class of traders, merchants, and people engaged in other professions. Sudra is the class of laborers and artisans.

- **Asrama**: Asrama is a system of stages of life discussed in Hindu texts of the ancient and medieval eras. The four ashramas are: Brahmacharya (student), Grihastha (householder), Vanaprastha (forest walker/forest dweller), and Sannyasa (renunciate).

- **Dharma**: Dharma refers to the moral and religious duties of an individual. In the context of Varnāshrama, it refers to the duties and responsibilities associated with each varna and asrama.

The Varnāshrama system aimed to provide a framework for individuals to fulfill their moral and religious duties and to maintain social order. However, it is important to note that the system was not always strictly followed and there were instances of individuals moving between varnas and ashramas.
