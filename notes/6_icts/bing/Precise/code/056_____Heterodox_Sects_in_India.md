### Heterodox Sects in India

Heterodox sects refer to the various religious groups and movements that emerged in India as a response to the ritual-ridden religion of the Vedic period. These sects were founded by ascetic wanderers and teachers who attracted groups of followers and established their own philosophies, which encompassed antinomian, materialist, and fatalist elements.

Some of the major heterodox sects that emerged in India include:

1. **Jainism**: Founded by Mahavira, the 24th Tirthankara, in 540 BC. Jainism is an ancient Indian religion that teaches the path to liberation and bliss is to live a life of harmlessness and renunciation.

2. **Ājīvika**: A nāstika or "heterodox" school of Indian philosophy believed to be founded in the 5th century BCE by Makkhali Gosāla. It was a Śramaṇa movement and a major rival of Vedic religion, early Buddhism, and Jainism.

3. **Cārvāka**: Also known as Materialism, this philosophical movement condemned the Vedas as ‘a pious fraud’. The Cārvākas believed in the materialistic view of life and rejected the existence of an afterlife, the soul, and the supernatural.

These are just a few examples of the many heterodox sects that emerged in India. These sects challenged the orthodox beliefs and practices of the time and offered alternative paths to spiritual enlightenment.
