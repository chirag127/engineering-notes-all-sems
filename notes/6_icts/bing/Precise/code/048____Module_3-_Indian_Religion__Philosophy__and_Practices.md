## Module 3- Indian Religion, Philosophy, and Practices

1. India is a country with a diverse range of religious beliefs and practices.
2. Hinduism, Buddhism, Jainism, and Sikhism are four major religions that originated in India.
3. Hinduism is the oldest and the most widely practiced religion in India. It is a complex religion with no single founder or scripture.
4. Buddhism was founded by Siddhartha Gautama, also known as the Buddha, in the 5th century BCE. It teaches the path to enlightenment through the Four Noble Truths and the Eightfold Path.
5. Jainism was founded by Mahavira in the 6th century BCE. It teaches the path to liberation through non-violence, truthfulness, non-stealing, chastity, and non-possessiveness.
6. Sikhism was founded by Guru Nanak in the 15th century. It teaches the belief in one God and the importance of living a truthful and honest life.
7. Apart from these four major religions, there are also many other religions practiced in India, including Islam, Christianity, Zoroastrianism, Judaism, and the Bahá'í Faith.
8. Indian philosophy includes six orthodox schools of Hindu philosophy, namely Samkhya, Yoga, Nyaya, Vaisheshika, Mimamsa, and Vedanta, as well as several heterodox schools such as Buddhism and Jainism.
9. Indian religious practices include rituals, festivals, and pilgrimages. Some of the major festivals celebrated in India include Diwali, Holi, Eid al-Fitr, Christmas, and Vaisakhi.
10. Yoga and meditation are also important practices in Indian religion and philosophy, with their origins in ancient Indian traditions.
