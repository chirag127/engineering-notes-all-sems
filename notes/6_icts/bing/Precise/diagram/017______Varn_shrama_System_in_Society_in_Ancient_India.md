#### Varnāshrama System in Society in Ancient India

The Varnāshrama system, also known as Varnashramadharma, was a social structure that developed in ancient Hindu society. The term ‘Varnashramadharma’ consists of three words - ‘varna’, ‘asrama’, and ‘dharma’. Theoretically, the Varna system refers to the segmental division of the Hindu society into four classes.

The four varnas are:
1. **Brahman**: the class of priests and teachers.
2. **Kshatriya**: the class of kings, warriors, and aristocrats.
3. **Vaishya**: the class of traders, merchants, and people engaged in other professions.
4. **Sudra**: the class of laborers.

In addition to the Varna system, the Ashrama system was also a part of the Varnāshrama system. The Ashrama system is a system of stages of life discussed in Hindu texts of the ancient and medieval eras. The four ashramas are: Brahmacharya (student), Grihastha (householder), Vanaprastha (forest walker/forest dweller), and Sannyasa (renunciate).

The Varnāshrama system was a way to organize society and assign specific duties to each class. It was based on the idea of dharma, or the moral and religious duties of an individual. The system was meant to ensure the smooth functioning of society and the fulfillment of an individual's dharma. However, over time, the system became rigid and was often used to justify discrimination and social inequality.
