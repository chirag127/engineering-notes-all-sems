#### The representation of Women in Historical traditions in Society in Ancient India

- A historical understanding of the status of women in early Indian society shows a declining trend in the position of women.
- Women in ancient India did not share an equal position with men and were recognized only as wives and mothers.
- In the Indus Valley civilization, the worship of the mother goddess highlights the respect for women as mothers. Women were given equal honor along with men in society and enjoyed full freedom.
- Women in ancient India enjoyed high status in society and their condition was good.
- Classical Hindu laws focus almost exclusively on women as wives. Role models and norms for mothers, daughters, and sisters are more apt to appear in folklore and vernacular traditions.
- Most written traditions emphasize women's behavior in relationship to men: wife/husband; mother/son; daughter/father; sister/brother.
- India has rooted its morality in myths and theology for hundreds of years and still has a long way to go towards the fair treatment of women today.
- The condition of women became worse in the Gupta period. The Smritishastras abused them; Manu dictated that a woman would be dependent on her father in childhood, on her husband in youth, and on her son in old age.
- Apart from child marriage and sati, prostitution and the Devadasi system became widespread.
