### Seals as Cultural Heritage of India

- Seals are small objects made of stone, metal, or other materials that have a carved or engraved design on one side and are used to stamp or seal documents, containers, or other items.
- Seals have been used in India since ancient times, especially during the Indus Valley Civilization (2500-1900 BCE), which was one of the earliest urban civilizations in the world.
- Seals of the Indus Valley Civilization are mostly square or rectangular in shape, and measure about 2-3 cm on each side. They are made of steatite, a soft stone that can be easily carved, and coated with alkali and baked to make them harder and more durable.
- Seals of the Indus Valley Civilization usually depict an animal, such as a bull, a buffalo, a rhinoceros, an elephant, a tiger, or a unicorn, along with a few symbols or glyphs that are part of the undeciphered Indus script. The animal may represent a deity, a clan, a trade, or a region. The symbols may indicate the owner, the purpose, or the content of the seal.
- Seals of the Indus Valley Civilization were used for various purposes, such as sealing clay vessels, bales of goods, or wooden doors, to indicate ownership, authenticity, or security. They were also used as amulets, tokens, or badges of identity. Some seals may have been used for ritual or ceremonial purposes, such as offering to a god or goddess.
- Seals of the Indus Valley Civilization are important sources of information about the culture, society, economy, religion, and art of this ancient civilization. They show the diversity and sophistication of the Indus people, their knowledge of animals and plants, their trade and exchange networks, and their beliefs and practices.
- Seals of the Indus Valley Civilization are also part of the cultural heritage of India, as they reflect the continuity and diversity of Indian civilization. They are symbols of the ancient roots and achievements of Indian culture, and they inspire curiosity and admiration for the past. They are also objects of artistic and aesthetic value, as they display the skill and creativity of the Indus craftsmen.
- Seals of the Indus Valley Civilization are preserved and displayed in various museums and institutions in India and abroad, such as the National Museum in Delhi, the Archaeological Survey of India, the British Museum, and the Metropolitan Museum of Art. They are also studied and researched by scholars and enthusiasts who seek to understand and decipher the Indus script and culture.
