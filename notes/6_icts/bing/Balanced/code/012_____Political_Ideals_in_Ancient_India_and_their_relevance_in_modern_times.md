### Political Ideals in Ancient India and their relevance in modern times

- Ancient India had a rich tradition of political thought and practice that spanned from the Vedic period to the medieval period.
- Some of the political ideals that emerged from this tradition are:

  - Democracy: The Rig Veda, the oldest of the four Vedas, advocated the principle of **samjnana**, which means the collective consciousness or will of the people. The Rig Veda also described the **sabha** and the **samiti**, two assemblies that represented the people and participated in decision-making. The sabha was a smaller council of elders, while the samiti was a larger assembly of all the members of the tribe. The sabha and the samiti elected the king, who was accountable to them and had to follow the **dharma**, or the moral law. The king was also assisted by a group of ministers, called the **ratnin**, who advised him on various matters. Democracy in ancient India was not based on universal suffrage, but on the principle of **consensus** among the people.

  - Monarchy: Monarchy was another popular form of government in ancient India, especially after the rise of the Magadha empire in the 6th century BCE. Monarchy was based on the concept of **divine right**, which means that the king was chosen by the gods and had supreme authority over his subjects. The king was also considered to be the **protector** of the people, the **upholder** of the dharma, and the **performer** of the sacrifices. The king was expected to rule with justice, benevolence, and wisdom, and to follow the advice of his ministers, priests, and elders. The king was also bound by the **dandaniti**, or the science of politics, which prescribed the rules and duties of the king and the state. The dandaniti was based on the **arthasastra**, or the treatise on wealth and power, which was written by Kautilya, the prime minister of Chandragupta Maurya, the founder of the Mauryan empire. The arthasastra was a comprehensive manual of statecraft, which covered topics such as administration, taxation, law, diplomacy, war, espionage, and welfare  .

  - Caste: Caste was a social and political institution that divided the society into four main groups, or **varnas**, based on their occupation and birth. The four varnas were the **brahmins** (priests and teachers), the **kshatriyas** (warriors and rulers), the **vaishyas** (merchants and farmers), and the **shudras** (servants and laborers). The caste system was justified by the **purusha sukta**, a hymn in the Rig Veda, which described the creation of the world and the varnas from the body of a cosmic being, called the **purusha**. The caste system was also supported by the **law of karma**, which means that one's actions in the present life determine one's status in the next life. The caste system was a rigid and hierarchical system that determined one's rights, duties, privileges, and obligations in the society. The caste system also influenced the political system, as the brahmins and the kshatriyas were the dominant groups that controlled the religious and secular affairs of the state .

- The relevance of these political ideals in modern times can be seen in the following ways:

  - Democracy: Democracy is the most widely accepted and practiced form of government in the modern world. Democracy is based on the principles of **equality**, **freedom**, **representation**, and **participation** of the people in the political process. Democracy also ensures the **accountability**, **transparency**, and **responsiveness** of the government to the people. Democracy in modern times is based on universal suffrage, which means that every adult citizen has the right to vote and stand for elections. Democracy in modern times also involves the **separation of powers**, which means that the executive, the legislature, and the judiciary are independent and check and balance each other. Democracy in modern times also recognizes the **pluralism** and **diversity** of the society, and respects the **rights** and **interests** of the minorities and the marginalized groups.
