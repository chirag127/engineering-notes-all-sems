### Jain Literature in Pali, Prakrit and Sanskrit

- Jain literature refers to the literature of the Jain religion, which is one of the oldest and major religions of India.
- Jain literature is a vast and ancient literary tradition, which was initially transmitted orally by the teachers and disciples of Jainism.
- Jain literature can be broadly divided into two categories: canonical and non-canonical.
- Canonical literature consists of the sacred texts that are accepted as authoritative by the Jains. They are also known as Agamas or Siddhantas.
- Non-canonical literature consists of the texts that are not accepted as authoritative by all the Jains, but are still valuable for their historical, philosophical, ethical, and literary significance. They include commentaries, biographies, stories, poems, and other genres.
- Jain literature is written in various languages, such as Prakrit, Pali, Sanskrit, Apabhramsha, Hindi, Gujarati, and others.
- Prakrit is a collective term for the Middle Indo-Aryan languages that were spoken in ancient India. Prakrit was the language of the common people, as opposed to Sanskrit, which was the language of the elite and the learned.
- Pali is a form of Prakrit that was used as the language of the Buddhist scriptures and literature. Pali was also influenced by Sanskrit and other regional languages.
- Sanskrit is the classical and literary language of ancient India, which was used for religious, philosophical, scientific, and artistic purposes. Sanskrit is the source of many modern Indian languages and has a rich and diverse literature.
- Jain literature in Prakrit is the oldest and most extensive. It includes the canonical texts, such as the Angas, Upangas, Prakirnas, Chhedab Sutras, and Malasutras, which deal with various aspects of Jain doctrine, ethics, cosmology, history, and ritual.
- Jain literature in Pali is relatively scarce, as Pali was mainly used by the Buddhists. However, some Jain texts, such as the Nandisutta and the Uttarajjhaya, were written in Pali, probably to reach a wider audience.
- Jain literature in Sanskrit is comparatively later and more diverse. It includes the works of famous Jain poets, philosophers, grammarians, and historians, such as Siddharasi, Hemachandra, Haribhadra, Jinasena, Somadeva, and others. Some of these works are original compositions, while others are translations or adaptations of Prakrit or Pali texts.
