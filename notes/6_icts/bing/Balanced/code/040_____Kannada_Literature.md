### Kannada Literature

Kannada literature is the body of written works in the Kannada language, a member of the Dravidian family spoken mainly in the Indian state of Karnataka. Kannada literature has a rich and long history, dating back to the 6th century AD. Some of the main features of Kannada literature are:

- Kannada literature is divided into three main periods: Old Kannada (6th to 13th century), Middle Kannada (14th to 18th century) and Modern Kannada (19th century to present).
- Kannada literature has produced many genres and forms, such as poetry, prose, drama, grammar, philosophy, history, biography, chronicles, cuisine, dictionaries and encyclopedias.
- Kannada literature has been influenced by various literary traditions, such as Jainism, Buddhism, Shaivism, Vaishnavism, Bhakti, Veerashaivism, Haridasa, Vachana, Navya and Dalit.
- Kannada literature has also contributed to the development of other languages and literatures, such as Telugu, Tamil, Malayalam, Marathi, Hindi and English.
- Kannada literature has been recognized by many awards and honors, such as the Jnanpith Award, the Sahitya Akademi Award, the Padma Shri and the Padma Vibhushan.

Some of the notable writers and works of Kannada literature are:

- Pampa, Ranna and Ponna, the three gems of Old Kannada literature, who wrote epic poems based on Hindu mythology and history.
- Basavanna, Akka Mahadevi and Allama Prabhu, the prominent poets of the Vachana movement, who expressed their devotion and social reform through simple and direct verses.
- Harihara and Raghavanka, the pioneers of the shatpadi (six-line verse) meter, who wrote on religious and secular themes.
- Kumara Vyasa and Lakshmisha, the authors of the Kannada versions of the Mahabharata and the Ramayana, respectively, who added local flavor and innovation to the classical epics.
- Sarvajna, the 16th century poet and philosopher, who wrote witty and wise three-line poems called tripadi.
- Purandara Dasa and Kanaka Dasa, the leading figures of the Haridasa movement, who composed devotional songs in praise of Vishnu and his incarnations.
- Bendre, Kuvempu and Shivaram Karanth, the modern Kannada writers who won the Jnanpith Award, the highest literary honor in India, for their works of poetry, fiction and non-fiction.
- Masti Venkatesha Iyengar, S.L. Bhyrappa and U.R. Ananthamurthy, the acclaimed Kannada novelists who explored various themes and styles in their works.
- Girish Karnad, Chandrashekhara Kambara and P. Lankesh, the eminent Kannada playwrights who enriched the Kannada theatre with their original and innovative plays.
- K.S. Narasimhaswamy, Gopalakrishna Adiga and K.S. Nissar Ahmed, the prominent Kannada poets who initiated and influenced the Navya (new) movement, which brought modernism and experimentation to Kannada poetry.
