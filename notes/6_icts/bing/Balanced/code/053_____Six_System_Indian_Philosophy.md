### Six System Indian Philosophy

The six systems of Indian philosophy are:

- **Nyaya**: A school that deals with logic and epistemology. It was founded by Akshapada Gautama in the 2nd century BCE. It proposes four sources of valid knowledge: perception, inference, comparison and testimony. It also analyzes the nature of arguments, fallacies, and proofs .
- **Vaisheshika**: A school that explains the nature of reality in terms of atoms and their combinations. It was founded by Kanada in the 6th century BCE. It proposes six categories of existence: substance, quality, action, generality, particularity and inherence. It also discusses the concepts of causation, liberation and ethics .
- **Samkhya**: A school that describes the dualism of purusha (consciousness) and prakriti (matter). It was founded by Kapila in the 6th century BCE. It proposes 25 principles of reality, including the three gunas (qualities) of sattva (goodness), rajas (activity) and tamas (inertia). It also explains the evolution of the universe, the nature of the self, and the path of liberation .
- **Yoga**: A school that prescribes the practical methods of attaining the highest state of consciousness. It was founded by Patanjali in the 2nd century BCE. It is based on the Samkhya philosophy, but adds the concept of Ishvara (God) as the supreme purusha. It also outlines the eight limbs of yoga, which are: yama (restraints), niyama (observances), asana (postures), pranayama (breath control), pratyahara (withdrawal of senses), dharana (concentration), dhyana (meditation) and samadhi (absorption) .
- **Purva Mimamsa**: A school that focuses on the interpretation and application of the Vedas, the sacred scriptures of Hinduism. It was founded by Jaimini in the 3rd century BCE. It advocates the performance of rituals and duties as the means of attaining happiness and liberation. It also analyzes the nature of language, meaning and authority .
- **Uttara Mimamsa or Vedanta**: A school that expounds the philosophical essence of the Upanishads, the mystical texts of Hinduism. It was founded by Badarayana in the 2nd century BCE. It proposes three main doctrines: Brahman (the absolute reality), Atman (the individual soul) and Maya (the illusion of duality). It also has various sub-schools, such as Advaita (non-dualism), Vishishtadvaita (qualified non-dualism) and Dvaita (dualism) .
