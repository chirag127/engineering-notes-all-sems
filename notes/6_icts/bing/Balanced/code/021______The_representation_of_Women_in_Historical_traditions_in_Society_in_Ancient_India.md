#### The representation of Women in Historical traditions in Society in Ancient India

- Women in ancient India had a complex and varied status depending on the time period, the region, the religion, and the caste they belonged to.
- During the Indus Valley Civilization (c. 2500-1500 BCE), women were worshiped as mother goddesses and enjoyed equal honor and freedom as men.
- During the Vedic period (c. 1500-500 BCE), women held an honored place in society and could participate in war, gymnastics, public activities, decision-making, and education. They also had the right to choose their husbands in a ceremony called swayamvara.
- During the later Vedic period (c. 1000-600 BCE), the status of women declined due to the rise of patriarchy, caste system, and ritualism. Women were confined to the domestic sphere and had to obey their husbands and fathers. They were denied the right to study the Vedas or perform sacrifices.
- During the epic period (c. 600 BCE-200 CE), women were depicted as ideal wives, mothers, and daughters in the Ramayana and the Mahabharata. They were also respected as sages, teachers, and leaders in some cases. However, they also faced oppression, violence, and humiliation in the form of abduction, rape, polygamy, widowhood, and sati (self-immolation on the husband's pyre) .
- During the classical period (c. 200 BCE-1200 CE), women enjoyed some rights and privileges under the influence of Buddhism and Jainism, which advocated non-violence, equality, and compassion. Women could join the monastic orders and attain spiritual liberation. They also contributed to the fields of art, literature, and science. However, they still faced discrimination and subordination in the society dominated by Hinduism, which prescribed strict codes of conduct and duties for women based on the Manusmriti (the law book of Manu) .
- During the medieval period (c. 1200-1750 CE), women faced further deterioration of their status due to the invasions of Muslim rulers, who imposed purdah (veil), jauhar (mass suicide), and zanana (seclusion) on them. Women were treated as objects of lust, property, and honor by the invaders and the defenders alike. They had no voice or agency in the political, social, or religious spheres .
