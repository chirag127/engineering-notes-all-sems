### Conditions of the Welfare of Societies in Ancient India

- The concept of social welfare in ancient India was based on the idea of **duty** as opposed to **rights** . Duty was derived from the notion of **dharma**, which meant the moral and ethical order of the universe, as well as the individual and social obligations that followed from it .
- Dharma was not a fixed or rigid code of conduct, but a dynamic and contextual principle that varied according to time, place, circumstance, and person . Dharma was also not imposed by any external authority, but was self-regulated by the individual and the community through conscience, reason, and tradition .
- Dharma was the basis of the **varna-system**, which divided the society into four broad categories: **Brahmins** (priests and teachers), **Kshatriyas** (warriors and rulers), **Vaishyas** (merchants and farmers), and **Shudras** (servants and laborers) . Each varna had its own specific duties and responsibilities, as well as rights and privileges, according to their respective roles and functions in the society .
- Dharma was also the basis of the **ashramas**, which divided the life of an individual into four stages: **Brahmacharya** (student), **Grihastha** (householder), **Vanaprastha** (retiree), and **Sannyasa** (renunciant) . Each ashrama had its own rules and regulations, as well as goals and aspirations, according to the age and maturity of the individual .
- Dharma was also the basis of the **sanskaras**, which were the rites and ceremonies that marked the important events and transitions in the life of an individual, such as birth, initiation, marriage, death, etc. . Each sanskara had its own significance and symbolism, as well as benefits and blessings, according to the spiritual and social needs of the individual .
- Dharma was also the basis of the **family**, which was the basic unit of the society and the source of social and moral values . The family was a joint and patriarchal system, where the elders had the authority and the responsibility to guide and protect the younger members, and the younger members had the duty and the respect to obey and serve the elders .
- Dharma was also the basis of the **marriage**, which was a sacred and lifelong bond between a man and a woman, based on mutual love, trust, and fidelity . The marriage was not only a personal and emotional relationship, but also a social and religious obligation, as it ensured the continuity of the family and the society .
- Dharma was also the basis of the **position of women**, which was generally subordinate and dependent on men, but also respected and honored for their roles as mothers, wives, and daughters . Women had the right to education, property, and inheritance, but also the duty to follow the norms and customs of their families and communities .
- Dharma was also the basis of the **dresses and ornaments**, which were the expressions of the culture and identity of the people, as well as the indicators of their status and wealth . The dresses and ornaments varied according to the region, climate, occupation, and occasion, but also followed the rules and regulations of the dharma .
- Dharma was also the basis of the **food habits**, which were the reflections of the health and hygiene of the people, as well as the influences of the environment and the religion . The food habits varied according to the taste, preference, and availability, but also followed the rules and regulations of the dharma, such as the prohibition of meat and alcohol for some groups .
