### Theatre as Cultural Heritage of India

- Theatre is a form of performing art that involves actors, dialogue, music, dance, and spectacle to create a dramatic representation of a story or a theme.
- Theatre in India has a long and rich history, dating back to the Vedic period (around 1500 BCE), when rituals and hymns were performed as a part of religious ceremonies.
- The earliest text on theatre in India is the Natya Shastra, written by Bharata Muni around 500 BCE. It is a comprehensive treatise on the theory and practice of theatre, covering aspects such as dramaturgy, aesthetics, stagecraft, genres, styles, and emotions.
- The Natya Shastra influenced the development of various forms of classical theatre in India, such as Sanskrit drama, which flourished from the 2nd century BCE to the 10th century CE. Some of the famous playwrights of Sanskrit drama were Kalidasa, Bhasa, Shudraka, and Vishakhadatta.
- Sanskrit drama was performed in specially designed theatres called natyashala, which followed the guidelines of the Natya Shastra. The performances were patronized by kings, nobles, and wealthy patrons, and were attended by elite audiences.
- Along with Sanskrit drama, India also has a rich tradition of folk theatre, which emerged from the local cultures and communities of different regions. Folk theatre is a composite art form that incorporates elements of music, dance, mime, puppetry, masks, and costumes. Folk theatre is more spontaneous, interactive, and participatory than classical theatre, and reflects the social, political, and religious issues of the people.
- Some of the prominent forms of folk theatre in India are Jatra (Bengal), Nautanki (Uttar Pradesh), Tamasha (Maharashtra), Bhavai (Gujarat), Yakshagana (Karnataka), Koodiyattam (Kerala), and Bhaona (Assam). These forms have their own distinct features, conventions, and styles, and are performed in various languages and dialects.
- Folk theatre in India has been a vital medium of cultural expression and communication, and has contributed to the preservation and transmission of the intangible heritage of India. Some forms of folk theatre, such as Koodiyattam, have been recognized by UNESCO as a Masterpiece of the Oral and Intangible Heritage of Humanity.
- Theatre in India underwent a major transformation in the colonial and post-colonial periods, when it was influenced by the Western theatre and the nationalist movement. Modern Indian theatre emerged as a result of the interaction between the traditional and the contemporary, and the fusion of the indigenous and the foreign.
- Modern Indian theatre experimented with new themes, forms, techniques, and languages, and addressed the issues of identity, culture, society, and politics. Some of the pioneers of modern Indian theatre were Rabindranath Tagore, Mohan Rakesh, Vijay Tendulkar, Girish Karnad, Badal Sircar, and Mahesh Dattani.
- Modern Indian theatre also witnessed the emergence of various movements and groups, such as the Indian People's Theatre Association (IPTA), the Theatre of the Absurd, the Theatre of the Roots, the Street Theatre, and the Theatre of Protest. These movements and groups challenged the conventions and norms of theatre, and used theatre as a tool of social change and awareness.
- Theatre in India today is a diverse and dynamic field, with a variety of forms, genres, styles, and languages. Theatre in India is also a vibrant and vital part of the cultural heritage of India, as it reflects the diversity, creativity, and dynamism of the Indian people.
