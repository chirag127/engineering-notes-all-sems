### North Indian Literature

- North Indian literature refers to the literature produced in the languages of northern India, such as Hindi, Urdu, Punjabi, Kashmiri, Rajasthani, etc.
- North Indian literature has been influenced by the ancient Indian background, which includes two Sanskrit epic poems, the Mahabharata and Ramayana, as well as the Bhagavata-purana and the other Puranas.
- North Indian literature also reflects the cultural diversity and historical changes of the region, such as the Islamic invasions, the Mughal rule, the British colonization, the Partition, and the modern nation-building.
- Some of the prominent features of North Indian literature are:
  - The use of vernacular languages and dialects, often mixed with Persian, Arabic, and English words.
  - The development of various poetic forms, such as the ghazal, the doha, the chaupai, the kavita, etc.
  - The expression of religious, social, and political themes, such as devotion, love, patriotism, nationalism, etc.
  - The incorporation of folk tales, legends, myths, and oral traditions, such as the Panchatantra, the Jataka, the Kathasaritsagara, etc.
  - The influence of classical Sanskrit literature, especially the works of Kalidasa, who wrote plays and poems that are considered masterpieces of world literature .
- Some of the notable authors and works of North Indian literature are:
  - Tulsidas, who wrote Ramcharitmanas, a Hindi version of the Ramayana, which is widely read and recited by Hindus.
  - Surdas, who wrote Sur Sagar, a collection of devotional poems in praise of Krishna, which are considered the epitome of the Bhakti movement.
  - Amir Khusro, who wrote Khamsa-e-Nizami, a series of five epic poems in Persian, which are regarded as the finest examples of Indo-Persian literature.
  - Chand Bardai, who wrote Prithviraj Raso, a historical romance in Hindi, which narrates the life and exploits of Prithviraj Chauhan, a Rajput king who fought against the Muslim invaders.
  - Premchand, who wrote novels and short stories in Hindi and Urdu, which depict the realistic and social problems of the Indian society, such as poverty, caste, gender, etc.
  - Rabindranath Tagore, who wrote poems, novels, short stories, and essays in Bengali and English, which reflect his humanistic and universal vision, and earned him the Nobel Prize in Literature in 1913.
  - Saadat Hasan Manto, who wrote short stories and essays in Urdu, which explore the dark and controversial aspects of the human psyche, such as sexuality, violence, partition, etc.
