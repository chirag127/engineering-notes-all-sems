### Physics in India and Ancient India

Physics is the branch of science that deals with the nature and properties of matter and energy. Physics in India has a long and rich history, dating back to the ancient times. Some of the main topics and achievements of physics in India and ancient India are:

- **Elements and atoms**: Indian philosophers proposed various theories of elements and atoms, based on observation and logic. The Nyaya and Vaisheshika schools of thought identified five basic elements: earth, water, fire, air, and ether. They also postulated that these elements were composed of indivisible and eternal atoms (parmanu), which could combine in different ways to form complex substances. The Buddhist atomists Dharmakirti and Dignaga further developed the concept of atoms and their properties, such as shape, size, motion, and causality. They also suggested that atoms had a dual nature of being both particles and waves, which is similar to the modern quantum theory  .

- **Energy and radiation**: Indian thinkers also explored the concept of energy and its relation to mass and radiation. The Vedas, the ancient scriptures of Hinduism, contain many references to energy (shakti) and its manifestations, such as heat, light, sound, and electricity. The Upanishads, the philosophical texts of Hinduism, describe the equivalence of energy and mass, and the conservation of energy in the universe. The Sankhya school of thought proposed that energy was the fundamental principle of creation, and that it could be divided into two types: active (rajas) and passive (tamas). The active energy was responsible for motion, change, and evolution, while the passive energy was responsible for stability, inertia, and dissolution. The Sankhya also recognized the existence of electromagnetic radiation, and classified it into seven types, corresponding to the seven colors of the rainbow.

- **Astronomy and cosmology**: Indian astronomers made significant contributions to the study of the celestial bodies and their motions. They developed sophisticated mathematical methods and instruments for measuring and predicting the positions and movements of the sun, moon, planets, and stars. They also calculated the size and shape of the earth, the distance and diameter of the moon, the length of the solar and lunar eclipses, and the duration of the sidereal and tropical years. They also proposed various models of the structure and origin of the universe, such as the cyclic model, the expanding and contracting model, and the multiverse model. Some of the notable Indian astronomers and their works are: Aryabhata, who wrote the Aryabhatiya, a treatise on astronomy and mathematics; Varahamihira, who wrote the Panchasiddhantika, a summary of five astronomical schools; Brahmagupta, who wrote the Brahmasphutasiddhanta, a work on algebra and astronomy; Bhaskara, who wrote the Siddhanta Shiromani, a comprehensive work on astronomy and mathematics; and Nilakantha Somayaji, who wrote the Tantrasangraha, a work on the heliocentric model of the solar system .
