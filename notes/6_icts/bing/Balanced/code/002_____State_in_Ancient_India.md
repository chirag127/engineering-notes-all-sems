### State in Ancient India

- The state in ancient India was a social organization with political power that ensured peace, order and happiness.
- The origin of the state in ancient India is not clear, but there are three main theories: social contract theory, divine origin theory and organic theory.
- Social contract theory: This theory suggests that the state was formed by a voluntary agreement among the people to surrender some of their rights and freedoms to a ruler or a group of rulers in exchange for protection and welfare.
- Divine origin theory: This theory claims that the state was created by God or gods and that the ruler or the group of rulers had divine sanction or authority to govern the people.
- Organic theory: This theory holds that the state was a natural and inevitable outcome of the growth and development of human society and that the ruler or the group of rulers represented the collective will or interest of the people.
- The types of states in ancient India were loosely categorized into monarchy, republic and oligarchy.
- Monarchy: This was the most prevalent type of state in ancient India, where the king was the supreme authority and the source of power for the ministers, governors, village councils, town councils and trade guilds . The king was usually hereditary, but sometimes elected or appointed by the people or the nobility. The king was assisted by a council of ministers, who advised him on various matters of administration, law, finance, defense and religion.
- Republic: This was a type of state where the people or their representatives had a direct role in the governance and decision-making. The republics were usually formed by clans, tribes or groups of people who shared a common ancestry, territory or occupation. The republics had a chief or a president, who was elected by the people or the assembly of elders, and who acted as the head of the state and the commander of the army. The republics also had a council of elders or a senate, who assisted the chief or the president in the administration and legislation.
- Oligarchy: This was a type of state where the power was concentrated in the hands of a few individuals or a class of people, such as the nobility, the wealthy or the priests. The oligarchs ruled over the majority of the people, who had little or no say in the affairs of the state. The oligarchs were often oppressive and exploitative, and faced resistance and revolt from the people.
- The earliest known state in ancient India was the Indus Valley Civilization, which flourished from 3300 BCE to 1300 BCE in the regions of modern-day Pakistan and northwest India . The Indus Valley Civilization had a highly developed urban culture, with planned cities, standardized weights and measures, seal carving, metallurgy, trade, writing and sanitation. The Indus Valley Civilization had a centralized administration, but the nature and extent of its political system is not well understood .
- The later states in ancient India were influenced by the Vedic culture, which emerged around 1500 BCE in the northwest of the subcontinent. The Vedic culture was based on the religious and philosophical texts known as the Vedas, which laid down the social, moral and ritual norms for the people. The Vedic culture was dominated by the Brahmins (priests), the Kshatriyas (warriors), the Vaishyas (merchants) and the Shudras (servants), who formed the four varnas or classes of the society. The Vedic culture also gave rise to the concept of dharma (duty), karma (action) and moksha (liberation), which shaped the worldview and ethics of the people.
- The major states in ancient India that emerged after the Vedic period were the Mahajanapadas (great kingdoms), the Mauryan Empire, the Kushan Empire, the Gupta Empire, the Harsha Empire, the Chola Empire, the Pallava Empire, the Rashtrakuta Empire, the Pala Empire, the Chalukya Empire, the Pratihara Empire, the Chahamana (Chauhan) Empire, the Delhi Sultanate and the Vijayanagara Empire.
