### Harappan Technologies in India and Ancient India

- The Harappan Civilization, also known as the Indus Valley Civilization, was one of the earliest urban cultures of the Indian subcontinent, dating from 3300 to 1300 BCE .
- The Harappans developed various technologies and innovations in different fields, such as:
  - **Metallurgy**: They used copper, bronze, lead, and tin to make tools, weapons, ornaments, and vessels. They also knew how to alloy copper and tin to make bronze, which was harder and more durable than copper .
  - **Standardized weights and measures**: They used a decimal system of weights based on a unit of 1.32 grams, and a system of measures based on a unit of 1.32 meters. They also used a scale with a balance and graduated rods for measuring length and mass .
  - **Seal carving**: They made seals of steatite, a soft stone, and engraved them with various motifs, such as animals, plants, and symbols. The seals were used for stamping clay tablets or sealing goods and containers. Some seals also had inscriptions in an undeciphered script .
  - **Hydraulic engineering**: They built sophisticated systems of water management, such as wells, reservoirs, canals, drains, and baths. They also used bricks of uniform size and shape to construct buildings and walls. They had a good knowledge of geometry and town planning, as evident from the layout of their cities .
- The Harappan technologies reflect their advanced level of civilization and their adaptation to their environment and needs. They also influenced the later cultures of India and beyond.
