#### Understanding Gender as a Social Category in Society in Ancient India

- Gender is a social construct that defines the roles, expectations and norms associated with different sexes in a given society.
- In ancient India, gender roles were not fixed or rigid, but varied according to the time period, region, caste, class and religion.
- Generally, ancient India's society greatly favored men over women, and patriarchal values were dominant. Men were expected to be the protectors, providers and leaders of the family and society, while women were expected to be the nurturers, caregivers and supporters of the men.
- However, there were also some exceptions and variations in this pattern. For instance, in the Vedic period (1500-600 BCE), women were considered with respect and dignity, and had access to education, religious rituals and philosophical debates. There were women rishis (sages) and scholars, such as Gargi, Maitreyi and Lopamudra, who were held in high esteem. Women also had some rights to property and inheritance, and could choose their own husbands in some cases.
- In the later periods, such as the Mauryan (321-185 BCE), Gupta (320-550 CE) and post-Gupta (550-1200 CE) periods, the status of women declined gradually, and they faced more restrictions and subordination. Women were confined to the domestic sphere, and their education, mobility and autonomy were curtailed. They were subjected to the authority of their fathers, husbands and sons, and had to follow the codes of conduct prescribed by the Dharmashastras (law books). They were also expected to practice sati (self-immolation on the husband's funeral pyre), child marriage, dowry and polygamy in some cases.
- Despite these challenges, some women still managed to achieve remarkable feats in various fields, such as politics, literature, art, religion and social reform. For example, some notable women rulers were Prabhavati Gupta, Didda, Razia Sultana and Rani Lakshmibai. Some famous women poets and writers were Andal, Mirabai, Lal Ded and Akka Mahadevi. Some influential women saints and reformers were Khana, Avvaiyar, Bhakti poets and Rani Rashmoni.
