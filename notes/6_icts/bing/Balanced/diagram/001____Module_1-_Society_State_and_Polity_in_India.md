## Module 1- Society State and Polity in India

This module aims to provide an overview of the historical and contemporary aspects of the society, state and polity in India. It covers the following topics:

- The concept and evolution of the Indian state and its relation to society and culture.
- The constitutional framework and the political institutions of India, such as the executive, the legislature, the judiciary and the federal system.
- The role and impact of social movements, civil society, media and public opinion on the Indian polity.
- The challenges and opportunities of democracy, development, diversity and diplomacy in India.

Some of the key points to remember are:

- India has a rich and diverse heritage of political thought and practice, dating back to ancient times. Some of the influential thinkers and leaders include Kautilya, Ashoka, Akbar, Gandhi, Nehru and Ambedkar.
- India is the largest democracy in the world, with a population of over 1.3 billion people and a multiparty system. It has a parliamentary form of government, with a president as the nominal head of state and a prime minister as the head of government.
- India has a written constitution, which is the supreme law of the land. It lays down the fundamental rights and duties of the citizens, the directive principles of state policy, and the basic structure of the government. It also provides for a secular, socialist and federal republic.
- India has a bicameral legislature, consisting of the Lok Sabha (the lower house) and the Rajya Sabha (the upper house). The Lok Sabha is directly elected by the people, while the Rajya Sabha is indirectly elected by the state legislatures. The Lok Sabha has more power and influence than the Rajya Sabha, especially in matters of finance and legislation.
- India has an independent judiciary, headed by the Supreme Court, which is the guardian of the constitution and the final arbiter of disputes. The Supreme Court has the power of judicial review, which means it can strike down any law or action that violates the constitution. It also has the power of judicial activism, which means it can intervene in matters of public interest and human rights.
- India has a federal system, which means it has a division of power between the central government and the state governments. The constitution lists the subjects on which the central government, the state governments and both of them have exclusive or concurrent jurisdiction. The central government can also impose emergency rule in a state under certain circumstances.
- India has a vibrant and diverse civil society, which consists of various non-governmental organizations, social movements, media outlets, interest groups and citizens' initiatives. They play an important role in raising awareness, mobilizing people, influencing policies and holding the government accountable.
- India faces many challenges and opportunities in its polity, such as ensuring inclusive and participatory democracy, promoting social justice and human development, managing diversity and pluralism, and enhancing its role and image in the world.
