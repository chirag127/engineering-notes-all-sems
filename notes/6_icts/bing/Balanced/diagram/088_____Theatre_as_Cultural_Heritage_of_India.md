### Theatre as Cultural Heritage of India

- Theatre is a form of performing art that combines music, dance, dialogue, and spectacle to tell stories and express ideas.
- Theatre in India has a long and rich history, dating back to the Vedic period (around 1500 BCE), when rituals and hymns were performed as part of religious ceremonies.
- The earliest text on theatre in India is the Natya Shastra, written by Bharata Muni around 500 BCE. It is a comprehensive treatise on the theory and practice of theatre, covering topics such as dramaturgy, aesthetics, stage design, costumes, makeup, music, dance, acting, and genres.
- The Natya Shastra defines two types of theatre: natya, which is based on mythology and history, and nataka, which is based on contemporary events and social issues. It also classifies theatre into ten types according to the mood (rasa) and the hero (nayaka).
- Theatre in India developed into various regional forms, reflecting the diversity of languages, cultures, and traditions of the country. Some of the prominent forms of theatre in India are:
  - Kutiyattam, a Sanskrit theatre form from Kerala, recognized by UNESCO as a masterpiece of the oral and intangible heritage of humanity. It is known for its elaborate gestures, expressions, and costumes, and can last for several days.
  - Kathakali, another theatre form from Kerala, characterized by its colorful masks, elaborate makeup, and stylized movements. It is based on the epics of Ramayana and Mahabharata, and uses a combination of music, dance, and dialogue to narrate the stories.
  - Yakshagana, a theatre form from Karnataka, which combines music, dance, dialogue, and costumes to depict the stories of gods, goddesses, and demons. It is performed in open-air stages, usually at night, and involves a lot of improvisation and interaction with the audience.
  - Nautanki, a popular theatre form from Uttar Pradesh and Bihar, which is known for its humorous and satirical tone. It deals with social and political issues, such as caste, gender, and corruption, and uses songs, dances, and dialogues to entertain and educate the masses.
  - Tamasha, a theatre form from Maharashtra, which originated as a folk entertainment for the lower classes. It is influenced by the Persian and Mughal cultures, and features a mix of music, dance, comedy, and drama. It often revolves around the love story of a king and a courtesan, and showcases the skills and talents of the female performers.
  - Jatra, a theatre form from West Bengal and Odisha, which is performed on a wooden stage with a curtain. It is based on religious, historical, or social themes, and uses songs, dances, and dialogues to convey the message. It is known for its loud and dramatic style, and its use of live music and sound effects.
  - Bhaona, a theatre form from Assam, which was created by the saint and reformer Sankardeva in the 15th century. It is based on the Bhagavata Purana, and uses music, dance, dialogue, and costumes to depict the life and teachings of Lord Krishna. It is performed in specially built theatres called namghars, which are also used as places of worship and community service.
- Theatre in India also underwent a modern transformation, influenced by the colonial and post-colonial contexts. Some of the notable figures of modern Indian theatre are:
  - Rabindranath Tagore, the Nobel laureate and polymath, who wrote and directed several plays in Bengali, English, and Hindi. He experimented with various forms and themes, such as realism, symbolism, nationalism, and humanism, and created a unique style of theatre that blended the classical and the contemporary.
  - Mohan Rakesh, the pioneer of the Hindi theatre movement, who wrote and directed plays that explored the existential and psychological aspects of human relationships. He is known for his plays such as Ashadh Ka Ek Din, Aadhe Adhure, and Lehron Ke Rajhans, which challenged the conventional norms and expectations of society and theatre.
