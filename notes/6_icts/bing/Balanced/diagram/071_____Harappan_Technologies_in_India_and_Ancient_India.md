### Harappan Technologies in India and Ancient India

- The Harappan Civilization, also known as the Indus Valley Civilization, was a Bronze Age urban culture that flourished from 3300 to 1300 BCE in the northwest of the Indian subcontinent .
- Some of the important innovations of this civilization include:
  - Standardized weights and measures, which facilitated trade and commerce.
  - Seal carving, which was used for stamping clay tablets or containers with symbols or images. Some of the seals depict animals, such as bulls, elephants, rhinoceroses, and unicorns.
  - Metallurgy, which involved the extraction and processing of metals, such as copper, bronze, lead, and tin . The Harappans used metal tools and weapons, such as axes, chisels, arrowheads, spearheads, knives, saws, razors, and fish-hooks.
  - Hydraulic engineering, which involved the construction and management of water systems, such as wells, reservoirs, canals, drains, and baths. The Harappans developed efficient systems for water conservation, harvesting, and storage, as well as sanitation and drainage.
  - Urban planning, which involved the design and layout of cities, streets, buildings, and public spaces. The Harappans built well-planned and fortified cities, such as Harappa, Mohenjo-daro, Dholavira, and Lothal, with grid-like patterns, uniform brick sizes, and standardized measurements.
  - Writing, which involved the use of a script that has not yet been deciphered. The Harappans wrote on clay tablets, seals, pottery, and copper plates, using a combination of pictographs and symbols. The purpose and meaning of their writing is still a mystery.
