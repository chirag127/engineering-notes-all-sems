## Module 3- Indian Religion, Philosophy, and Practices

- India is a diverse and pluralistic country with many religions, philosophies, and practices that have influenced its culture, society, and history.
- The major religions in India are Hinduism, Islam, Christianity, Sikhism, Buddhism, and Jainism. Each of these religions has its own scriptures, doctrines, rituals, ethics, and traditions.
- Hinduism is the oldest and most dominant religion in India, with about 80% of the population identifying as Hindus. Hinduism is not a single creed, but a collection of various schools of thought, sects, and movements that share some common beliefs and practices, such as the concepts of karma, dharma, reincarnation, and moksha (liberation).
- Hinduism has a rich and diverse philosophical tradition, with six orthodox schools of thought (darshanas) that accept the authority of the Vedas (ancient scriptures) and six heterodox schools that reject the Vedas. The six orthodox schools are Nyaya (logic), Vaisheshika (atomism), Samkhya (dualism), Yoga (discipline), Mimamsa (ritual exegesis), and Vedanta (metaphysics). The six heterodox schools are Charvaka (materialism), Jainism (non-violence), Buddhism (enlightenment), Ajivika (fatalism), Lokayata (empiricism), and Ajnana (agnosticism).
- Hinduism also has a variety of religious practices, such as worship (puja), pilgrimage (tirtha), festivals (utsava), fasting (vrata), and ethical codes (dharma shastra). Hinduism also recognizes the diversity of paths to salvation (marga), such as the path of action (karma marga), the path of devotion (bhakti marga), and the path of knowledge (jnana marga).
- Islam is the second largest religion in India, with about 14% of the population identifying as Muslims. Islam is a monotheistic religion that follows the teachings of the Quran (the holy book) and the Sunnah (the example of the Prophet Muhammad). Islam has five pillars of faith: the declaration of faith (shahada), the daily prayers (salat), the alms-giving (zakat), the fasting during Ramadan (sawm), and the pilgrimage to Mecca (hajj).
- Islam has two major sects: Sunni and Shia, which differ on the issue of succession after the death of the Prophet Muhammad. Sunni Muslims follow the consensus of the community (ijma) and the analogy of the Quran and the Sunnah (qiyas) as sources of law and guidance. Shia Muslims follow the teachings of the imams (the descendants of the Prophet's son-in-law Ali) as sources of authority and inspiration.
- Islam also has various schools of thought (madhhabs) that differ on the interpretation of the Quran and the Sunnah, such as the Hanafi, Maliki, Shafi'i, and Hanbali schools of Sunni Islam, and the Twelver, Ismaili, and Zaydi schools of Shia Islam. Islam also has various mystical and reformist movements, such as Sufism, Wahhabism, and Ahmadiyya.
- Christianity is the third largest religion in India, with about 2.3% of the population identifying as Christians. Christianity is a monotheistic religion that follows the teachings of Jesus Christ (the Son of God) and the Bible (the holy book). Christianity has three major branches: Catholicism, Orthodoxy, and Protestantism, which differ on the issues of papal authority, the nature of Christ, and the role of tradition.
- Christianity also has various denominations, such as Anglican, Baptist, Lutheran, Methodist, Pentecostal, and Presbyterian, that differ on the issues of church governance, sacraments, and doctrine. Christianity also has various movements, such as Evangelicalism, Charismatic, and Ecumenical, that emphasize different aspects of the Christian faith, such as personal conversion, spiritual gifts, and social justice.
