### Evolution of languages in India

- India is a multilingual country with over 780 languages spoken by its people.
- The linguistic history of India can be traced back to the prehistoric period when various language families emerged and interacted with each other.
- The major language families in India are the Indo-Aryan, the Dravidian, the Austroasiatic and the Sino-Tibetan.
- The Indo-Aryan languages are derived from the Indo-European languages that originated in Central Asia and spread to India through migrations and invasions. They are spoken by about 75% of the population and include languages such as Hindi, Bengali, Marathi, Gujarati, Punjabi, Urdu and Sanskrit.
- The Dravidian languages are native to South India and are spoken by about 20% of the population. They include languages such as Tamil, Telugu, Kannada, Malayalam and Tulu. They have a distinct grammatical structure and vocabulary from the Indo-Aryan languages and are influenced by the ancient Harappan civilization.
- The Austroasiatic languages are spoken by about 3% of the population, mostly in the eastern and central regions of India. They include languages such as Santali, Mundari, Khasi and Munda. They are related to the languages of Southeast Asia and have a rich oral tradition.
- The Sino-Tibetan languages are spoken by about 1% of the population, mostly in the northeastern and Himalayan regions of India. They include languages such as Tibetan, Nepali, Manipuri and Bodo. They are related to the languages of China and have a complex tonal system.
- The evolution of Indian languages is influenced by various factors such as geography, culture, religion, politics, trade and education. Some of the major influences are:
  - The Vedic period (1500-500 BCE) when the Sanskrit language and literature flourished and became the basis of the Indo-Aryan languages.
  - The Mauryan period (321-185 BCE) when the Prakrit languages and the Brahmi script developed and became the medium of administration and communication.
  - The Gupta period (320-550 CE) when the classical Sanskrit reached its peak and the Nagari script emerged as the precursor of the Devanagari script.
  - The medieval period (600-1500 CE) when the regional languages and scripts evolved from the Prakrits and the Apabhramshas and were influenced by the Persian, Arabic and Turkish languages due to the Islamic invasions and rule.
  - The colonial period (1500-1947 CE) when the English language and the Roman script were introduced and became the language of education, law, trade and administration due to the British rule.
  - The post-independence period (1947-present) when the Hindi language and the Devanagari script were adopted as the official language and script of the Indian Union and the other languages were given the status of scheduled languages and regional languages.
  - The contemporary period (2000-present) when the English language and the Roman script have become the dominant language and script of the internet, media, science and technology and have influenced the other Indian languages.

- The evolution of Indian languages is a dynamic and ongoing process that reflects the diversity and unity of the Indian culture and society.
