### Northern Indian languages

- Northern Indian languages are mainly from the Indo-Aryan branch of the Indo-European language family.
- They include the Hindustani lingua franca, which has two standard forms: Hindi and Urdu. Hindi is the official language of India, while Urdu is the official language of Pakistan and some Indian states. Both languages share a common grammar and vocabulary, but differ in their scripts and some cultural words.
- Other major Northern Indian languages are Punjabi, Kashmiri, Pahari, and Bihari. Punjabi is spoken in Punjab, a state in India and a province in Pakistan. It has two scripts: Gurmukhi and Shahmukhi. Kashmiri is spoken in the Kashmir Valley, a disputed region between India and Pakistan. It has three scripts: Perso-Arabic, Devanagari, and Sharada. Pahari is a group of languages spoken in the Himalayan foothills of India, Nepal, and Pakistan. Bihari is a group of languages spoken in Bihar, a state in India, and neighboring regions. The most prominent Bihari languages are Bhojpuri, Magadhi, and Maithili .
- Northern Indian languages have evolved from the ancient Sanskrit language, which was the language of the Vedic civilization and the classical literature of India. They have also been influenced by various foreign languages, such as Persian, Arabic, Turkish, English, and Portuguese, due to historical contacts and invasions .
- Northern Indian languages have rich and diverse literary traditions, ranging from poetry, drama, fiction, and folklore to religious, philosophical, and scientific texts. Some of the famous writers and poets in Northern Indian languages are Kabir, Mirza Ghalib, Premchand, Rabindranath Tagore, Amrita Pritam, and Mahadevi Varma .
