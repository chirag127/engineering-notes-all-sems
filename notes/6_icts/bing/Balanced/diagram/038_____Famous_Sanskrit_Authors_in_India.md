### Famous Sanskrit Authors in India

Sanskrit is one of the oldest and most influential languages in India. It is the language of the Vedas, the Upanishads, the Mahabharata, the Ramayana, and many other classical texts. Sanskrit literature is rich and diverse, spanning various genres and periods. Some of the famous Sanskrit authors in India are:

- **Kalidasa**: He is considered to be the greatest Sanskrit poet and dramatist, who lived in the 4th or 5th century CE. He wrote two epic poems, Kumārasambhava and Raghuvaṃśa, which depict the lives and deeds of the gods and kings. He also wrote three plays, Mālavikāgnimitra, Vikramorvaśīya, and Abhijñānaśākuntala, which are examples of romantic comedy, tragedy, and drama respectively. His poetry is known for its beauty, elegance, and sophistication .
- **Bharavi**: He was a 6th century CE poet who wrote the Kirātārjunīya, an epic poem that narrates the encounter between Arjuna and Shiva in the form of a hunter. The poem is praised for its vivid descriptions, rhetorical devices, and moral lessons.
- **Bhartṛhari**: He was a 5th century CE poet and philosopher who wrote the Śatakatraya, a collection of three hundred verses on love, renunciation, and wisdom. He also wrote the Vākyapadīya, a treatise on grammar, semantics, and logic.
- **Bhāsa**: He was a 3rd or 4th century BCE playwright who wrote thirteen plays based on the Mahabharata, the Ramayana, and other legends. His plays are known for their realism, humor, and dramatic techniques. Some of his famous plays are Urubhaṅga, Pratijñāyaugandharāyaṇa, and Svapnavāsavadattā .
- **Bhaṭṭa Nārāyaṇa**: He was a 9th or 10th century CE poet who wrote the Venīsaṃhāra, a play that depicts the abduction and rescue of Draupadi by the Pandavas. The play is notable for its emotional intensity, poetic imagery, and dramatic suspense.
- **Bāṇabhaṭṭa**: He was a 7th century CE poet and biographer who wrote the Harṣacarita, the first historical biography in Sanskrit, which narrates the life and achievements of King Harsha. He also wrote the Kādambarī, a romantic novel that tells the story of two lovers who are reincarnated through several births .
- **Budhasvāmin**: He was a 4th or 5th century CE poet who wrote the Bṛhatkathāślokasaṃgraha, a condensed version of the Bṛhatkathā, a lost collection of stories in Sanskrit. The Bṛhatkathāślokasaṃgraha contains tales of adventure, romance, magic, and morality, and is the source of many later works of literature .
