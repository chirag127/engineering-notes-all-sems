## Module 4-Science, Management and Indian Knowledge System

This module covers the following topics:

- Astronomy in India: The ancient Indian astronomers made significant contributions to the fields of observational astronomy, mathematical astronomy, cosmology, and astrology. Some of the notable achievements include the discovery of the heliocentric model, the calculation of the sidereal year, the estimation of the diameter of the Earth, the concept of zero, and the development of the decimal system  .
- Chemistry in India: The ancient Indian chemists developed various techniques and methods for the extraction, purification, and synthesis of metals, minerals, dyes, medicines, perfumes, and cosmetics. They also invented the process of distillation, fermentation, and crystallization. Some of the important works in this field are the Rasashastra, the Charaka Samhita, and the Arthashastra  .
- Mathematics in India: The ancient Indian mathematicians made remarkable contributions to the fields of arithmetic, algebra, geometry, trigonometry, calculus, and combinatorics. They introduced the concepts of zero, negative numbers, fractions, decimals, irrational numbers, and infinity. They also developed theorems and formulas for solving various problems, such as the Pythagorean theorem, the quadratic equation, the sine and cosine functions, and the binomial theorem  .
- Physics in India: The ancient Indian physicists explored the nature and properties of matter, energy, force, motion, sound, light, heat, and electricity. They proposed theories and models to explain the phenomena of gravitation, magnetism, optics, acoustics, thermodynamics, and electrostatics. They also invented devices and instruments, such as the balance, the sundial, the water clock, the iron pillar, and the lightning rod  .
- Agriculture in India: The ancient Indian agriculturists developed various methods and practices for the cultivation, irrigation, fertilization, pest control, and harvesting of crops. They also domesticated and bred animals, such as cows, horses, elephants, and chickens. They also produced and traded various agricultural products, such as grains, fruits, vegetables, spices, oils, and textiles  .
- Medicine in India: The ancient Indian physicians and surgeons developed a holistic system of medicine, known as Ayurveda, which is based on the balance of the five elements and the three doshas in the human body. They also classified and described various diseases, symptoms, causes, and treatments. They also performed various surgical procedures, such as cataract surgery, rhinoplasty, lithotomy, and caesarean section  .
- Metallurgy in India: The ancient Indian metallurgists mastered the art and science of extracting, refining, alloying, shaping, and decorating metals, such as iron, copper, bronze, gold, and silver. They also produced and used various tools, weapons, ornaments, and coins made of metals. They also discovered and utilized various properties of metals, such as corrosion resistance, ductility, malleability, and hardness  .
- Geography in India: The ancient Indian geographers studied and mapped the physical features and boundaries of the Indian subcontinent and the surrounding regions. They also identified and named various rivers, mountains, lakes, islands, and forests. They also described the climate, flora, fauna, and resources of different regions. They also calculated the latitudes and longitudes of various places  .
- Biology in India: The ancient Indian biologists observed and classified various living organisms, such as plants, animals, and humans. They also studied the anatomy, physiology, morphology, and behavior of different organisms. They also discovered and explained various biological phenomena, such as reproduction, heredity, adaptation, and evolution  .
- Harappan Technologies: The Harappan civilization, which flourished in the Indus Valley from 2500 BCE to 1900 BCE, developed various technologies and innovations, such as urban planning, sanitation, drainage, water supply, brick making, pottery, seals, weights and measures, writing, and trade   .
