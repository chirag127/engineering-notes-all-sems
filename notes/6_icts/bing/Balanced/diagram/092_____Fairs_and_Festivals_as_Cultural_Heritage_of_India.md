### Fairs and Festivals as Cultural Heritage of India

- India is a land of diverse cultures, religions, languages, and traditions. It is also a land of vibrant celebrations, where various fairs and festivals are held throughout the year to mark important occasions, honor deities, commemorate saints, express gratitude, and showcase the rich and colorful heritage of the country.
- Fairs and festivals in India reflect the diversity and unity of the people, as well as the values and beliefs that have shaped the Indian civilization for thousands of years. They are also a source of joy, entertainment, social bonding, and economic activity for the participants and spectators.
- Some of the most famous fairs and festivals in India are:

  - **Makar Sankranti**: A harvest festival celebrated in January, when the sun enters the zodiac sign of Capricorn. It is also known as Pongal, Lohri, Bihu, and Maghi in different regions. People fly kites, light bonfires, prepare sweets, and worship the sun god .
  - **Holi**: A festival of colors and love, celebrated in March, to mark the arrival of spring and the victory of good over evil. People smear each other with colored powder and water, play music, dance, and share sweets.
  - **Ugadi**: A new year festival celebrated in March or April by the people of Andhra Pradesh, Telangana, Karnataka, and Maharashtra. It is also known as Gudi Padwa, Cheti Chand, and Navreh. People decorate their homes, wear new clothes, make rangoli, and eat a special dish called Ugadi Pachadi, which has six different tastes to symbolize the various aspects of life.
  - **Baisakhi**: A harvest festival celebrated in April by the people of Punjab, Haryana, and Delhi. It is also the day when the Khalsa Panth, the Sikh brotherhood, was founded by Guru Gobind Singh in 1699. People perform bhangra and gidda dances, visit gurdwaras, and take part in processions.
  - **Rath Yatra**: A chariot festival celebrated in June or July by the devotees of Lord Jagannath, an incarnation of Vishnu, in Puri, Odisha. It is also known as the Car Festival, as three huge wooden chariots carrying the idols of Jagannath, Balabhadra, and Subhadra are pulled by thousands of people along a 3 km route to the Gundicha Temple. It is one of the oldest and largest religious gatherings in the world.
  - **Ganesh Chaturthi**: A festival dedicated to the elephant-headed god Ganesh, celebrated in August or September, especially in Maharashtra, Goa, and Karnataka. People install clay idols of Ganesh in their homes or public places, and worship him for 10 days with offerings of modak, a sweet dumpling. On the last day, the idols are immersed in water bodies, with chants of "Ganpati Bappa Morya".
  - **Onam**: A harvest festival celebrated in August or September by the people of Kerala. It is also the day when the mythical king Mahabali, who was banished by Vishnu, returns to visit his subjects. People decorate their homes with flower carpets, wear traditional clothes, play games, perform dances, and enjoy a grand feast called Onasadya.
  - **Dussehra**: A festival that celebrates the victory of Rama over Ravana, the demon king of Lanka, in the epic Ramayana. It is celebrated in September or October, on the tenth day of the Hindu month of Ashwin. People enact the story of Ramayana in plays called Ramleela, and burn effigies of Ravana, Kumbhakarna, and Meghnad, along with fireworks. It is also known as Vijayadashami, as it marks the triumph of good over evil .
  - **Navratri**: A nine-night festival that honors the nine forms of the goddess Durga, the supreme mother of the universe. It is celebrated in September or October, coinciding with Dussehra.
