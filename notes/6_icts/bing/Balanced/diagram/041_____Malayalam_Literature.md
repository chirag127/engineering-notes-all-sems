### Malayalam Literature

Malayalam literature is the literature written in Malayalam, a South-Dravidian language spoken in the Indian state of Kerala. It has a rich and diverse history that spans over a thousand years. Some of the main features and periods of Malayalam literature are:

- The earliest known literary work in Malayalam is **Ramacharitam**, a poem composed in the late 12th or early 13th century by Cheeraman, a Chera king. It narrates the story of Rama in a simple and elegant style.
- The next phase of Malayalam literature is marked by the emergence of **Manipravalam**, a hybrid style that combines Malayalam and Sanskrit words and grammar. This style was used mainly for erotic poetry and courtly literature by poets such as Cherusseri Namboothiri, Thunchaththu Ezhuthachan, and Punam Namboothiri .
- The 16th and 17th centuries saw the development of **Pattu**, a popular song literature that used native meters and idioms. The most famous Pattu works are **Krishnagatha** by Cherusseri Namboothiri, **Ramakathapattu** by Kadungathur Narayanan Nambudiri, and **Kannassaramayanam** by Ramapurathu Warrier .
- The 18th and 19th centuries witnessed the rise of **modern Malayalam literature**, influenced by Western education, journalism, and social reform movements. The pioneers of this period include **Kerala Varma Valiya Koil Thampuran**, who translated Kalidasa's works into Malayalam, **Kumaran Asan**, who wrote revolutionary poems on social issues, **Vallathol Narayana Menon**, who revived the classical art form of Kathakali through his lyrics, and **Ulloor S. Parameswara Iyer**, who enriched Malayalam poetry with his historical and cultural themes .
- The 20th and 21st centuries have seen the flourishing of **contemporary Malayalam literature**, characterized by diversity, experimentation, and global awareness. Some of the prominent writers of this period include **Basheer**, who introduced realism and humor into Malayalam fiction, **Thakazhi Sivasankara Pillai**, who depicted the lives of the oppressed and marginalized, **M. T. Vasudevan Nair**, who explored the human psyche and the Malayali identity, **O. V. Vijayan**, who blended fantasy and satire in his novels, **Kamala Das**, who expressed her feminine sensibility and sexuality in her poems and memoirs, and **M. Mukundan**, who portrayed the social and political changes in Kerala  .

Malayalam literature is a rich and vibrant field of expression that reflects the culture, history, and aspirations of the Malayali people. It has also contributed to the national and global literary scene with its originality, diversity, and excellence.
