### Conditions of the Welfare of Societies in Ancient India

- The welfare of societies in ancient India was based on the concept of **duty** rather than **rights**. The rulers, the priests, the warriors, the merchants, and the workers had different duties and responsibilities according to their **varna** or social class. The varna system was a hierarchical and hereditary division of society that regulated the occupations, privileges, and obligations of each group .
- The welfare of societies in ancient India was also influenced by the **ashramas** or stages of life. These were four stages that each individual was expected to follow according to their age and status. The four ashramas were: **brahmacharya** (student life), **grihastha** (householder life), **vanaprastha** (retired life), and **sannyasa** (renounced life). Each ashrama had its own rules and duties that aimed to prepare the individual for the ultimate goal of **moksha** or liberation from the cycle of rebirth.
- The welfare of societies in ancient India was further shaped by the **sanskaras** or sacraments. These were rites of passage that marked the important events and transitions in a person's life, such as birth, initiation, marriage, death, etc. The sanskaras were meant to purify, sanctify, and educate the individual and the society. They also reinforced the social norms and values of the varna and ashrama systems.
- The welfare of societies in ancient India was also dependent on the **family** as the basic unit of society. The family was a patriarchal and joint structure that included several generations of relatives living together under the authority of the eldest male. The family was responsible for the economic, social, religious, and educational needs of its members. The family also performed various rituals and ceremonies to honor the ancestors and the gods.
- The welfare of societies in ancient India was also affected by the **marriage** system. Marriage was considered a sacred and lifelong bond between a man and a woman of the same varna. The marriage was arranged by the parents or elders of the bride and groom, who had to pay a **dowry** or a gift to the groom's family. The marriage was solemnized by the performance of various rites and vows in the presence of a priest and witnesses. The marriage also established the rights and duties of the husband and wife, who had to be faithful, loyal, and supportive of each other.
- The welfare of societies in ancient India was also influenced by the **position of women**. Women in ancient India had a subordinate and dependent status in relation to men. They had limited rights and freedoms in the public and private spheres. They had to follow the rules of **purdah** or seclusion, which restricted their mobility and visibility. They had to obey their fathers, husbands, and sons, who were their guardians and protectors. They had to perform the domestic chores and bear and raise children. They had to observe the virtues of **pativrata** or chastity, **sati** or self-immolation on the husband's funeral pyre, and **jauhar** or mass suicide to avoid capture by enemies .
- The welfare of societies in ancient India was also related to the **dresses and ornaments**. The dresses and ornaments of the people in ancient India reflected their varna, ashrama, gender, region, and occasion. The common materials used for clothing were cotton, silk, wool, and linen. The common colors used were white, red, yellow, green, and blue. The common ornaments worn were earrings, necklaces, bracelets, rings, etc. made of gold, silver, copper, iron, ivory, etc. The dresses and ornaments also had symbolic and religious meanings, such as purity, auspiciousness, wealth, etc.
- The welfare of societies in ancient India was also connected to the **food habits**.
