### Seals as Cultural Heritage of India

- Seals are small objects made of stone, metal, or other materials that have a carved or engraved design on one side and are used to stamp or seal documents, containers, or other items.
- Seals have been used in India since ancient times, especially during the Indus Valley Civilization (2500-1900 BCE), which was one of the earliest urban civilizations in the world.
- Seals of the Indus Valley Civilization are mostly square or rectangular in shape, and measure about 2-3 cm on each side. They are made of steatite, a soft stone that can be easily carved, and coated with alkali and baked to make them harder and more durable.
- Seals of the Indus Valley Civilization usually depict an animal, such as a bull, a buffalo, a rhinoceros, an elephant, a tiger, or a unicorn, along with a few symbols or signs that are considered to be part of an undeciphered script. The animal may represent a deity, a clan, a trade, or a region. The symbols may indicate the name, title, or affiliation of the owner or user of the seal.
- Seals of the Indus Valley Civilization were used for various purposes, such as sealing clay vessels or wooden boxes that contained goods for trade, marking clay tablets or documents that recorded transactions or contracts, or identifying personal belongings or property. Seals were also worn as amulets or charms for protection or luck.
- Seals of the Indus Valley Civilization reflect the culture, society, economy, religion, and art of the people who lived in the Indus region, which covered parts of present-day Pakistan, India, Afghanistan, and Iran. They show the diversity and complexity of the Indus civilization, which had a high degree of urban planning, sanitation, standardization, and trade. They also show the influence and interaction of the Indus civilization with other cultures, such as Mesopotamia, Persia, and Central Asia.
- Seals of the Indus Valley Civilization are valuable sources of information and evidence for the study of the history and archaeology of India and South Asia. They are also part of the cultural heritage and identity of India, as they represent one of the oldest and most advanced civilizations of the world. They are preserved and displayed in various museums and institutions in India and abroad, such as the National Museum in Delhi, the Archaeological Survey of India, the British Museum, and the Metropolitan Museum of Art.
