### Society in Ancient India

- The society in ancient India was based on the concept of **varna**, which means color or class. There were four main varnas: **Brahmins** (priests and scholars), **Kshatriyas** (warriors and rulers), **Vaishyas** (merchants and farmers), and **Shudras** (servants and laborers). 
- The varna system was not rigid or hereditary in the early Vedic period, but became more hierarchical and oppressive in the later periods. The varnas were also subdivided into many **jatis** or castes, which were based on occupation, region, and lineage. The jatis had their own rules, customs, and rituals, and were often endogamous (marrying within the group). 
- Below the four varnas, there were the **untouchables** or **Dalits**, who were considered impure and outcaste. They performed the most menial and polluting tasks, such as scavenging, leather work, and cremation. They faced discrimination and oppression from the upper varnas, and were denied access to education, temples, and public facilities. They were also known as **Harijans** (children of God) by Mahatma Gandhi, who fought for their rights and dignity. 
- The society in ancient India was also influenced by the concept of **ashrama**, which means stage of life. There were four main ashramas: **Brahmacharya** (student life), **Grihastha** (householder life), **Vanaprastha** (retired life), and **Sannyasa** (renounced life). Each ashrama had its own duties, responsibilities, and goals, and was meant to help the individual attain **moksha** or liberation from the cycle of rebirth. 
- The society in ancient India was also shaped by the diverse religions and cultures that emerged and coexisted in the region. The oldest religion in India is **Hinduism**, which is based on the Vedas, the Upanishads, and the epics of Ramayana and Mahabharata. Hinduism is a polytheistic and henotheistic religion, which means it worships many gods and goddesses, but also recognizes one supreme deity. Hinduism also believes in the concepts of **karma** (action and consequence), **dharma** (duty and morality), **samsara** (rebirth and reincarnation), and **atman** (soul or self). 
- Other religions that emerged in ancient India include **Buddhism** and **Jainism**, which are both offshoots of Hinduism. Buddhism was founded by Siddhartha Gautama, who became known as the Buddha or the Enlightened One. Buddhism teaches the Four Noble Truths and the Eightfold Path, which are meant to help the individual overcome **dukkha** (suffering) and attain **nirvana** (enlightenment). Jainism was founded by Mahavira, who became known as the Jina or the Conqueror. Jainism teaches the principles of **ahimsa** (non-violence), **anekantavada** (non-absolutism), and **aparigraha** (non-attachment), which are meant to help the individual achieve **kaivalya** (isolation) and **moksha** (liberation). 
- The society in ancient India was also enriched by the various civilizations and cultures that flourished and interacted in the region. The oldest civilization in India is the **Indus Valley Civilization**, which existed from 2500 BCE to 1900 BCE. It was a highly urbanized and sophisticated civilization, which had well-planned cities, advanced drainage and sanitation systems, standardized weights and measures, and a pictographic script. The Indus Valley Civilization is also known as the **Harappan Civilization**, after one of its major sites, Harappa.
