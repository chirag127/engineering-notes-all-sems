### Socio religious reform movement of 19th century in India

- Socio religious reform movement refers to the efforts of various individuals and groups to reform and modernize the Indian society and religion in the 19th century.
- The main causes of the reform movement were:
  - The impact of western education and liberal ideas, which exposed the Indians to the concepts of democracy, human rights, rationalism, and scientific temper.
  - The criticism of the orientalists and the Christian missionaries, who highlighted the evils and superstitions of the Hindu religion and society, such as idolatry, caste system, sati, child marriage, etc.
  - The rise of nationalism and the desire to revive the glory of the ancient Indian civilization, which inspired the Indians to reform their religion and culture from within.
- The main types of the reform movement were:
  - The Brahmo Samaj, founded by Raja Ram Mohan Roy in 1828, which advocated monotheism, abolition of caste system, widow remarriage, women education, and social service.
  - The Prarthana Samaj, founded by Atmaram Pandurang in 1867, which followed the ideals of the Brahmo Samaj and also worked for the upliftment of the depressed classes and the emancipation of women.
  - The Arya Samaj, founded by Swami Dayanand Saraswati in 1875, which rejected idolatry, polytheism, rituals, priesthood, and caste system, and promoted the Vedas as the source of true knowledge and social reform.
  - The Ramakrishna Mission, founded by Swami Vivekananda in 1897, which propagated the message of Vedanta, universal brotherhood, service to humanity, and religious harmony.
  - The Theosophical Society, founded by Madame Blavatsky and Colonel Olcott in 1875, which aimed to revive the ancient wisdom of the East and to promote the study of comparative religion, philosophy, and science.
  - The Aligarh Movement, founded by Sir Syed Ahmed Khan in 1875, which sought to reform the Muslim society and religion by promoting western education, rationalism, and social service.
  - The Singh Sabha Movement, founded by Khem Singh Bedi and others in 1873, which aimed to reform the Sikh religion and society by purging it of the Hindu and Muslim influences and by promoting the teachings of the Guru Granth Sahib.
- The main impacts of the reform movement were:
  - It challenged the orthodoxy and conservatism of the Indian society and religion and paved the way for social and religious change.
  - It created a new class of educated and enlightened Indians, who became the leaders of the nationalist movement and the social reformers of the 20th century.
  - It fostered a sense of pride and self-respect among the Indians and inspired them to resist the colonial domination and exploitation.
  - It contributed to the growth of a composite and modern Indian culture, which was based on the synthesis of the best elements of the East and the West.
