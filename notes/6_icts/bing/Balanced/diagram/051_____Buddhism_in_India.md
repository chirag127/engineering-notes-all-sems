### Buddhism in India

- Buddhism is an ancient Indian religion that originated in the 6th century BCE in the region of Magadha (now Bihar) and spread throughout northern India and beyond.
- Buddhism is based on the teachings and life experiences of Siddhartha Gautama, also known as the Buddha (the awakened one), who attained enlightenment under a bodhi tree and taught the four noble truths and the eightfold path to end suffering and achieve nirvana.
- Buddhism has two main branches: Theravada, which follows the Pali canon and emphasizes the monastic life and personal liberation; and Mahayana, which incorporates the Sanskrit scriptures and emphasizes the bodhisattva ideal and universal salvation.
- Buddhism flourished in India for over a thousand years, reaching its peak during the reigns of Ashoka (3rd century BCE), who patronized and propagated the religion throughout his empire and beyond, and Harsha (7th century CE), who supported the Nalanda university and the Buddhist pilgrims.
- Buddhism also interacted with other Indian religions, such as Hinduism and Jainism, and influenced their doctrines, practices, and art forms. Buddhism also absorbed elements from the local cultures and traditions of the regions where it spread, such as Tibet, China, Japan, and Southeast Asia.
- Buddhism declined in India from the 8th century CE onwards, due to various factors, such as the Islamic invasions, the revival of Hinduism, the corruption and degeneration of the monastic institutions, and the loss of royal patronage and popular support.
- Buddhism survived in some pockets of India, such as Ladakh, Sikkim, Arunachal Pradesh, and the Himalayan regions, where it was preserved by the Tibetan and Nepalese traditions. Buddhism also experienced a revival in the 20th century, especially among the Dalits and other marginalized groups, who embraced it as a means of social emancipation and empowerment.
- Buddhism is one of the recognized religions of India, and has a significant presence and influence in the country. India is also the host of many important Buddhist sites, such as Bodh Gaya, Sarnath, Kushinagar, and Ajanta and Ellora caves, which attract pilgrims and tourists from all over the world. India is also the home of the Dalai Lama, the spiritual leader of Tibetan Buddhism, who resides in Dharamsala.
