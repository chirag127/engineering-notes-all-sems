#### The representation of Women in Historical traditions in Society in Ancient India

- Women in ancient India had a complex and varied status depending on the time period, the region, the religion, and the caste system.
- During the Indus Valley Civilization (c. 2500-1500 BCE), women were worshiped as mother goddesses and held an honored place in society. They could participate in war, gymnastics, public activities, decision-making, and education.
- During the Vedic period (c. 1500-500 BCE), women were respected and revered as wives, mothers, and sages. They could perform rituals, compose hymns, and study the Vedas. Some of the famous women sages were Gargi, Maitreyi, and Lopamudra  .
- During the later Vedic period (c. 500-200 BCE), the status of women declined due to the rise of patriarchy, caste system, and Brahmanism. Women were confined to the domestic sphere and subjected to the authority of their fathers, husbands, and sons. They were denied education, property rights, and freedom of choice. They had to practice sati (self-immolation of widows), child marriage, and polygamy  .
- During the Mauryan period (c. 321-185 BCE), the status of women improved slightly due to the influence of Buddhism and Jainism, which advocated non-violence, equality, and compassion. Women could join the monastic orders, preach, and attain salvation. Some of the prominent women figures were Chandragupta Maurya's chief queen Durdhara, Ashoka's daughter Sanghamitra, and the Buddhist nun Dhammawati  .
- During the Gupta period (c. 320-550 CE), the status of women deteriorated further due to the revival of Hinduism and the codification of the Manusmriti, which prescribed the ideal duties and behavior of women. Women were considered inferior, dependent, and impure. They were expected to be obedient, loyal, and chaste. They were barred from education, inheritance, and remarriage. They had to endure dowry, domestic violence, and social stigma  .
- During the medieval period (c. 600-1700 CE), the status of women varied depending on the region, the dynasty, and the religion. The Muslim invasions and rule introduced new customs and laws that affected the women of both Hindu and Muslim communities. Some of the positive aspects were the emergence of women poets, saints, warriors, and rulers, such as Mirabai, Lal Ded, Rani Durgavati, and Razia Sultana. Some of the negative aspects were the imposition of purdah (veil), jauhar (mass suicide of women), and zina (adultery) laws  .
- During the colonial period (c. 1700-1947 CE), the status of women was influenced by the British policies, the social reform movements, and the nationalist struggle. Some of the positive aspects were the abolition of sati, the legalization of widow remarriage, the promotion of female education, and the participation of women in the freedom movement, such as Rani Lakshmibai, Sarojini Naidu, and Aruna Asaf Ali. Some of the negative aspects were the exploitation, oppression, and discrimination of women by the colonial rulers, the orthodox sections of society, and the patriarchal norms  .
