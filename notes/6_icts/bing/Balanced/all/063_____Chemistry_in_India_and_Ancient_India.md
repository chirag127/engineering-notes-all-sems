# Chemistry in India and Ancient India

- Chemistry in ancient India was called **Rasayana** in Sanskrit, which means **extract** or **essence** of substances  .
- Chemistry in ancient India was not confined to theoretical knowledge, but found practical applications in various fields such as metallurgy, medicine, alchemy, cosmetics, perfumery, dyeing, agriculture, and warfare   .
- Some of the notable achievements of chemistry in ancient India are:
  - The discovery and extraction of metals such as iron, copper, zinc, tin, lead, silver, and gold from ores and minerals   . The iron pillar of Delhi, which dates back to the 4th century CE, is an example of the high quality of iron produced in ancient India .
  - The development of alloys such as brass, bronze, steel, and panchaloha (a five-metal alloy used for making idols and statues)   . The famous iron pillar of Delhi is also an example of an alloy, as it contains traces of phosphorus, manganese, and carbon .
  - The invention and use of various chemical devices and apparatus such as crucibles, furnaces, retorts, alembics, filters, condensers, and distillation units for performing chemical experiments and processes   . The ancient Indian text Rasaratnasamuccaya, written by Vagbhata in the 12th century CE, describes many of these devices in detail  .
  - The preparation and purification of various chemical substances such as mercury, sulfur, arsenic, borax, saltpeter, alum, salt, lime, and acids   . The ancient Indian texts Rasarnava, Rasahridaya, and Rasendramangala, written by various authors between the 9th and 13th centuries CE, describe many of these substances and their properties and uses  .
  - The synthesis and application of various organic compounds such as sugar, alcohol, vinegar, oils, fats, resins, gums, dyes, perfumes, and cosmetics   . The ancient Indian texts Charaka Samhita, Sushruta Samhita, and Vagbhata Samhita, written by various authors between the 6th century BCE and the 7th century CE, describe many of these compounds and their medicinal and cosmetic uses  .
  - The development and practice of alchemy, which aimed at transmuting base metals into gold, creating the philosopher's stone, and attaining immortality   . The ancient Indian texts Rasaratnakara, Rasarnava, and Rasendracudamani, written by various authors between the 8th and 13th centuries CE, describe many of the alchemical theories and experiments in ancient India  .
  - The contribution to the atomic theory, which postulated that all matter is composed of indivisible and indestructible atoms, which combine in various ways to form molecules and compounds   . The ancient Indian philosophers Kanada and Pakudha Katyayana, who lived in the 6th century BCE, are credited with the earliest formulation of the atomic theory in their texts Vaisheshika Sutra and Pakudha Katyayana Sutra  .
