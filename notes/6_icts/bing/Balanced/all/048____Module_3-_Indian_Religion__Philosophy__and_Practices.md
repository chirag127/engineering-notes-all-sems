## Module 3- Indian Religion, Philosophy, and Practices

- This module covers the main aspects of Indian religion, philosophy, and practices, such as Hinduism, Buddhism, Jainism, Sikhism, and their historical and cultural influences.
- The module also explores the concepts of dharma, karma, reincarnation, moksha, nirvana, and the ethical and spiritual teachings of the Indian traditions.
- The module aims to provide a comprehensive and balanced overview of the diversity and richness of Indian religious thought and practice, as well as the challenges and opportunities they face in the modern world.

### Learning Outcomes

By the end of this module, you should be able to:

- Identify and describe the basic beliefs, practices, and scriptures of Hinduism, Buddhism, Jainism, and Sikhism.
- Compare and contrast the similarities and differences among the four major Indian religions, and their sects and schools.
- Explain the meaning and significance of dharma, karma, reincarnation, moksha, nirvana, and other key concepts in Indian philosophy and ethics.
- Analyze the historical and cultural factors that shaped the development and spread of Indian religions, both within and outside India.
- Evaluate the contemporary issues and challenges faced by Indian religions, such as social justice, gender equality, environmentalism, pluralism, and secularism.

### Topics Covered

The module is divided into four units, each covering a major Indian religion and its philosophy and practices.

- Unit 1: Hinduism
  - Introduction to Hinduism: origins, scriptures, diversity, and worldview.
  - The Hindu concept of dharma: varna, ashrama, purushartha, and the law of karma.
  - The Hindu concept of moksha: the paths of action, devotion, and knowledge, and the role of yoga and meditation.
  - The Hindu gods and goddesses: the Trimurti, the Devi, and the various forms and manifestations of the divine.
  - The Hindu rituals and festivals: the rites of passage, the daily worship, the pilgrimage, and the seasonal celebrations.
- Unit 2: Buddhism
  - Introduction to Buddhism: the life and teachings of the Buddha, the Four Noble Truths, and the Noble Eightfold Path.
  - The Buddhist concept of nirvana: the three marks of existence, the five aggregates, and the doctrine of dependent origination.
  - The Buddhist schools and traditions: Theravada, Mahayana, and Vajrayana, and their distinctive features and practices.
  - The Buddhist ethics and values: the five precepts, the four sublime states, the six perfections, and the bodhisattva ideal.
  - The Buddhist art and culture: the symbols, images, architecture, literature, and music of Buddhism.
- Unit 3: Jainism
  - Introduction to Jainism: the historical and doctrinal origins, the Tirthankaras, and the Jain scriptures.
  - The Jain concept of dharma: the dualistic ontology, the theory of karma, and the cycle of rebirth.
  - The Jain concept of moksha: the three jewels of right faith, right knowledge, and right conduct, and the ascetic lifestyle.
  - The Jain principles and practices: the five vows, the twelve reflections, the six essential duties, and the festivals and rituals.
  - The Jain contributions and challenges: the philosophy of non-violence, the ecology of interdependence, the social reforms, and the modern adaptations.
- Unit 4: Sikhism
  - Introduction to Sikhism: the historical and spiritual context, the Gurus, and the Guru Granth Sahib.
  - The Sikh concept of dharma: the monotheistic belief, the divine name, and the divine will.
  - The Sikh concept of moksha: the liberation from ego, the union with God, and the service to humanity.
  - The Sikh practices and institutions: the five Ks, the Khalsa, the langar, the gurdwara, and the sangat.
  - The Sikh identity and issues: the role of women, the caste system, the political struggles, and the interfaith dialogue.
