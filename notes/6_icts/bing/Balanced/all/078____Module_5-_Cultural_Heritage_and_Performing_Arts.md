# Module 5- Cultural Heritage and Performing Arts

- Cultural heritage is the legacy of physical and intangible attributes of a group or society that are inherited from past generations, maintained in the present and bestowed for the benefit of future generations.
- Cultural heritage includes tangible culture (such as buildings, monuments, landscapes, books, works of art, and artefacts), intangible culture (such as folklore, traditions, language, and knowledge), and natural heritage (such as culturally significant landscapes, and biodiversity).
- Performing arts are forms of art that use the body, voice, or objects to convey artistic expression, such as music, dance, theatre, opera, mime, puppetry, circus arts, and performance art.
- Performing arts are often performed in front of an audience, but may also be performed for a specific purpose, such as ritual, education, or therapy.
- Performing arts are part of the intangible cultural heritage of many cultures and play a vital role in preserving and transmitting the values, beliefs, and identity of a group or society.
- Performing arts can also reflect human creativity and innovation, and contribute to the social, economic, and cultural development of a community or a country.
- Some examples of performing arts that are recognized by UNESCO as intangible cultural heritage are:

  - Urtiin Duu, traditional folk long song from Mongolia, which is sung in a specific vocal technique that requires great skill and endurance.
  - Kutiyattam, Sanskrit theatre from India, which is one of the oldest living theatrical traditions in the world and combines drama, dance, music, and ritual.
  - Flamenco, an artistic expression fusing song, dance, and musicianship from Spain, which is rooted in the cultural diversity and history of Andalusia.
  - Nôgaku theatre, a classical Japanese theatre form that combines elements of dance, drama, music, and poetry, and is based on a rich repertoire of stories and characters.
  - Tango, a social dance and musical genre from Argentina and Uruguay, which is characterized by its expressive and sensual movements and its complex musicality.

- Cultural heritage research is a multidisciplinary field that studies the various aspects and dimensions of cultural heritage, such as its definition, identification, preservation, management, interpretation, valorization, and impact.
- Cultural heritage research draws on the methods and theories of various disciplines in the humanities and social sciences, such as history, archaeology, anthropology, sociology, geography, art history, museology, and cultural studies.
- Cultural heritage research aims to enhance the understanding and appreciation of cultural heritage, to foster its safeguarding and transmission, and to promote its contribution to sustainable development and social cohesion.
- Some of the current topics and challenges in cultural heritage research are:

  - The impact of globalization, digitalization, and climate change on cultural heritage and its preservation.
  - The role of cultural heritage in fostering intercultural dialogue, diversity, and inclusion.
  - The ethical and legal issues related to cultural heritage ownership, access, and use.
  - The participation and empowerment of local communities and stakeholders in cultural heritage management and decision-making.
  - The evaluation and measurement of the social, economic, and cultural value and impact of cultural heritage.
