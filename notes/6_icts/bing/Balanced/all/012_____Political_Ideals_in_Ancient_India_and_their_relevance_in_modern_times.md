# Political Ideals in Ancient India and their relevance in modern times

- Ancient India had a rich tradition of political thought and practice that spanned from the Vedic period to the medieval period.
- Some of the political ideals that emerged from this tradition are:

  - Democracy: The Rig Veda, the oldest of the four Vedas, extolled the concept of **Samjnana**, which means the collective consciousness of the people, the national mind to which the individual mind is to pay its homage as the source from which it derives its potency. The Rig Veda also mentioned the institution of **Sabha** and **Samiti**, which were assemblies of the people that participated in the governance of the society. These assemblies were based on the principles of consultation, deliberation, and consensus. Democracy in ancient India was not limited to the political sphere, but also extended to the social and economic spheres, where people enjoyed freedom of speech, expression, occupation, and religion.
  - Dharma: Dharma is a Sanskrit term that means duty, law, morality, justice, or righteousness. It is one of the core concepts of Hinduism, Buddhism, Jainism, and Sikhism. Dharma was the basis of the political order in ancient India, as it prescribed the duties and responsibilities of the king and the subjects, as well as the norms and values of the society. Dharma was also the source of legitimacy and authority for the king, who was expected to rule in accordance with the welfare of the people and the cosmic order. Dharma was not a rigid or fixed code, but a dynamic and flexible one that adapted to the changing times and circumstances.
  - Arthashastra: Arthashastra is a Sanskrit term that means the science of wealth, politics, or statecraft. It is the name of a treatise attributed to Kautilya, also known as Chanakya, who was a minister and advisor to Chandragupta Maurya, the founder of the Mauryan Empire in the 4th century BCE. Arthashastra is considered to be one of the earliest and most comprehensive works on political theory and practice in the world. It covers topics such as the nature and functions of the state, the qualities and duties of the king, the administration of justice, the management of the economy, the conduct of diplomacy, the art of war, and the methods of espionage. Arthashastra is based on the principle of **Niti**, which means policy, strategy, or expediency. It advocates a pragmatic and rational approach to politics, where the king should use all means, fair or foul, to achieve the goals of the state.

- The relevance of these political ideals in modern times can be seen in the following ways:

  - Democracy: Democracy is widely regarded as the best form of government in the modern world, as it ensures the participation, representation, and accountability of the people in the decision-making process. Democracy also promotes the values of equality, liberty, and fraternity, which are essential for the development of a harmonious and prosperous society. Democracy in India has its roots in the ancient tradition of Samjnana, Sabha, and Samiti, which have been revived and adapted to the contemporary context through the Constitution of India, the Parliament of India, and the Panchayati Raj system.
  - Dharma: Dharma is still a relevant concept in the modern world, as it provides a moral and ethical framework for the conduct of individuals and groups in the society. Dharma also inspires a sense of duty and responsibility towards oneself, others, and the environment, which are crucial for the sustainability of the planet. Dharma also fosters a spirit of tolerance and respect for the diversity of beliefs, cultures, and lifestyles, which are essential for the promotion of peace and harmony in the world. Dharma in India has been incorporated and interpreted in various ways by the different religions, philosophies, and movements that have emerged in the country over the centuries.
  - Arthashastra: Arthashastra is still a relevant concept in the modern world, as it provides a comprehensive and practical guide for the management of the state and the society. Arthashastra also offers valuable insights and lessons on the various aspects of politics, such as the role and qualities of the leader, the importance and challenges of governance, the strategies and tactics of diplomacy, the principles and techniques of war, and the secrets and dangers of espionage.
