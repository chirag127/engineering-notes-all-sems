# Socio-religious reform movement of 19th century in India

- Socio-religious reform movement refers to the efforts of various individuals and groups to reform and revitalize the Indian society and religion in the 19th century.
- The main causes of the emergence of the reform movement were:
  - The impact of western education and liberal ideas, which exposed the Indians to the concepts of reason, rationality, humanism, democracy, equality, liberty and nationalism.
  - The contact with the British rule and culture, which created a sense of inferiority and humiliation among the Indians and made them aware of the social evils and superstitions prevalent in their society.
  - The influence of Christian missionaries, who criticized the Hindu religion and tried to convert the Indians to Christianity, which provoked a defensive and reformist response from the Hindus.
  - The rise of a new middle class of educated and professional Indians, who were influenced by the modern ideas and values and wanted to reform their society and religion according to them.
  - The social and religious movements of the lower castes and tribal communities, who challenged the dominance and oppression of the upper castes and demanded equal rights and dignity.
- The main types of the reform movement were:
  - The Brahmo Samaj, founded by Raja Ram Mohan Roy in 1828, which aimed to reform the Hindu religion by rejecting idolatry, polytheism, rituals, priesthood, caste system, sati, child marriage and purdah, and promoting monotheism, rationalism, humanism, social service and women's education and emancipation .
  - The Prarthana Samaj, founded by Atmaram Pandurang in 1867, which followed the ideals of the Brahmo Samaj and also worked for the upliftment of the depressed classes and the abolition of untouchability .
  - The Arya Samaj, founded by Swami Dayanand Saraswati in 1875, which aimed to revive the pure and original form of Hinduism by rejecting the later corruptions and interpolations in the Vedas, and advocating the principles of swadeshi, swaraj, swadharma and swabhasha .
  - The Ramakrishna Mission, founded by Swami Vivekananda in 1897, which propagated the message of Vedanta and the universal brotherhood of man, and also engaged in various social and educational activities for the welfare of the masses .
  - The Theosophical Society, founded by Madame Blavatsky and Colonel Olcott in 1875, which aimed to promote the study of ancient religions and philosophies, especially Hinduism and Buddhism, and to establish a universal brotherhood of humanity irrespective of race, creed or color .
  - The Aligarh Movement, founded by Sir Syed Ahmed Khan in 1875, which aimed to reform the Muslim society and religion by promoting modern education, rationalism, social service and loyalty to the British government .
  - The Singh Sabha Movement, founded by Khem Singh Bedi and others in 1873, which aimed to reform the Sikh religion and society by removing the corruptions and superstitions, and restoring the purity and glory of Sikhism .
  - The Satyashodhak Samaj, founded by Jyotirao Phule in 1873, which aimed to liberate the lower castes and women from the exploitation and oppression of the upper castes, and to establish a society based on truth, justice and equality .
  - The Young Bengal Movement, led by Henry Vivian Derozio and his students in the 1820s and 1830s, which aimed to spread the spirit of free inquiry, rationalism, liberalism and nationalism among the youth of Bengal, and to challenge the orthodoxy and conservatism of the Hindu society .
