# Bhakti Movement in India

- The Bhakti movement was a significant religious movement in medieval Hinduism that sought to bring religious reforms to all strata of society by adopting the method of devotion to achieve salvation.
- The movement preached against the caste system, ritualism, idolatry, and social inequalities, and emphasized the equality of all human beings, the importance of moral values, and the personal experience of God .
- The movement originated in South India in the 7th-8th centuries CE with the devotional hymns of the Alvars and the Nayanars, who were devoted to Vishnu and Shiva respectively .
- The movement spread to North India in the 12th-13th centuries CE with the influence of the Vaishnava saints like Ramananda, Kabir, and Nanak, and the Shaiva saints like Basava, Gorakhnath, and Ravidas .
- The movement also had a strong impact on the development of regional languages, literature, music, art, and culture, as the bhakti poets and saints used the vernacular languages to express their devotion and to reach out to the masses  .
- The movement contributed to the growth of a pluralistic and tolerant society in India, as it fostered a spirit of harmony, respect, and dialogue among different religious traditions and sects .
