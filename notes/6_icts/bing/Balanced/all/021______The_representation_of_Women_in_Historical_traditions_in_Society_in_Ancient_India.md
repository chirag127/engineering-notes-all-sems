#### The representation of Women in Historical traditions in Society in Ancient India

- Women in ancient India had a complex and varied status depending on the time period, the religion, the region, and the caste they belonged to.
- During the Indus Valley Civilization (around 2500-1500 BCE), women were worshiped as mother goddesses and enjoyed equal honor and freedom as men. They could participate in war, gymnastics, public activities, decision-making, and education.
- During the Vedic period (around 1500-500 BCE), women also held an honored place in society and were respected as scholars, teachers, and priests. They could study the Vedas, perform rituals, and compose hymns. Some of the famous women sages of this period were Gargi, Maitreyi, Lopamudra, and Ghosha  .
- During the later Vedic period (around 500-200 BCE), the status of women began to decline due to the rise of patriarchy, caste system, and Brahmanism. Women were gradually excluded from the religious and educational spheres and were confined to the domestic domain. They were expected to be obedient, loyal, and chaste wives and mothers. They had to follow the norms of pativrata (devotion to husband), sati (self-immolation on husband's pyre), and anuloma (marriage within or above one's caste)  .
- During the Mauryan period (around 321-185 BCE), women enjoyed some political and economic rights under the rule of Ashoka, who was a Buddhist emperor. Women could own property, inherit land, and work as traders, artisans, and officials. Some women also joined the Buddhist sangha (monastic order) and became nuns, teachers, and missionaries. Some of the prominent Buddhist women of this period were Sanghamitra, Dhammadinna, Khema, and Uppalavanna  .
- During the Gupta period (around 320-550 CE), women faced further restrictions and subordination due to the revival of Hinduism and the codification of laws such as the Manusmriti, which prescribed the ideal duties and behaviors of women in different stages of life. Women were denied the right to education, property, and remarriage. They were subjected to the authority of their fathers, husbands, and sons. They were also discriminated against on the basis of their caste, class, and complexion. However, some women still excelled in the fields of literature, art, music, and drama. Some of the notable women of this period were Kalidasa's Shakuntala, Banabhatta's Yashomati, and Harsha's Rajyashri  .
- In conclusion, women in ancient India had a diverse and dynamic role in society, which changed according to the historical, cultural, and religious contexts. They were not passive or homogeneous, but active and heterogeneous agents of social change. They contributed to the development of civilization, culture, and religion in various ways. They also faced many challenges and struggles due to the patriarchal and hierarchical structures of society. They were both revered and oppressed, celebrated and silenced, empowered and marginalized.
