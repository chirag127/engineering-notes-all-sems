# Module 4-Science, Management and Indian Knowledge System

This module covers the following topics:

- Astronomy in India: The development of astronomy in India from the Vedic period to the modern era, the contributions of Indian astronomers such as Aryabhata, Varahamihira, Brahmagupta, Bhaskara, and others, the concepts of sidereal and tropical zodiacs, the calculation of eclipses, the heliocentric model, the concept of zero, the decimal system, and the use of astronomical instruments such as the gnomon, the armillary sphere, the astrolabe, and the observatories of Jantar Mantar.
- Chemistry in India: The origin and evolution of chemistry in India from the ancient to the medieval period, the achievements of Indian chemists such as Nagarjuna, Kanada, Charaka, Sushruta, and others, the concepts of atomism, metallurgy, alchemy, medicine, cosmetics, perfumery, dyeing, and distillation, the discovery of metals such as zinc, iron, steel, and mercury, the production of alloys such as wootz steel and panchaloha, the invention of gunpowder, fireworks, and rockets, and the influence of Indian chemistry on other civilizations.
- Mathematics in India: The history and development of mathematics in India from the Indus Valley Civilization to the colonial period, the contributions of Indian mathematicians such as Pingala, Panini, Katyayana, Apastamba, Baudhayana, Manava, Shulba Sutras, Sulba Sutras, Mahavira, Aryabhata, Brahmagupta, Bhaskara, Mahaviracharya, and others, the concepts of geometry, algebra, trigonometry, calculus, combinatorics, number theory, and logic, the discovery of irrational numbers, negative numbers, fractions, zero, infinity, and the Pythagorean theorem, the invention of the decimal system, the place value system, and the symbols for numbers, and the development of algorithms, series, and equations.
- Physics in India: The emergence and growth of physics in India from the ancient to the modern period, the contributions of Indian physicists such as Kanada, Kapila, Patanjali, Charaka, Sushruta, Varahamihira, Aryabhata, Brahmagupta, Bhaskara, and others, the concepts of matter, energy, force, motion, gravity, magnetism, electricity, light, sound, heat, and optics, the classification of elements, the theory of relativity, the law of conservation of mass and energy, the atomic theory, the quantum theory, and the string theory, and the development of devices such as the balance, the wheel, the pulley, the lever, the compass, the battery, the telescope, the microscope, and the radio.
- Agriculture in India: The origin and evolution of agriculture in India from the prehistoric to the contemporary period, the diversity and richness of Indian crops, such as rice, wheat, millets, pulses, oilseeds, spices, fruits, vegetables, and flowers, the techniques and practices of Indian farmers, such as irrigation, fertilization, crop rotation, pest control, and organic farming, the innovations and inventions of Indian agriculturists, such as the plough, the sickle, the hoe, the drill, the seed drill, the harrow, the threshing machine, and the tractor, and the challenges and opportunities of Indian agriculture in the global context.
- Medicine in India: The origin and development of medicine in India from the ancient to the modern period, the achievements and contributions of Indian physicians and surgeons, such as Charaka, Sushruta, Vagbhata, Nagarjuna, Madhava, and others, the systems and schools of Indian medicine, such as Ayurveda, Siddha, Unani, Yoga, Naturopathy, and Homeopathy, the concepts and principles of Indian medicine, such as the theory of tridosha, the theory of panchamahabhuta, the theory of prana, the theory of karma, the theory of dhatu, the theory of mala, and the theory of srotas, the diagnosis and treatment of diseases, such as fever, cough, asthma, diabetes, leprosy, tuberculosis, malaria, cholera, plague, smallpox, and cancer, the methods and procedures of Indian medicine, such as pulse reading, urine analysis, bloodletting, purgation, emesis, enema, and surgery, the instruments and tools of
