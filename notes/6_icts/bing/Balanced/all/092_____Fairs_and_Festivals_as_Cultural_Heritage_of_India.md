# Fairs and Festivals as Cultural Heritage of India

- India is a land of diverse cultures, religions, languages, and traditions. It is also a land of vibrant celebrations, where various fairs and festivals are held throughout the year to mark important occasions, honor deities, commemorate saints, express gratitude, and showcase the rich and colorful heritage of the country .
- Fairs and festivals in India reflect the diversity and unity of the people, as well as their beliefs, values, customs, and lifestyles. They also provide an opportunity for people to interact, exchange, and enjoy with each other, as well as to showcase their arts, crafts, music, dance, and cuisine  .
- Some of the most famous fairs and festivals in India are:

  - **Makar Sankranti**: A harvest festival celebrated in January, when the sun enters the zodiac sign of Capricorn. It is also known as Pongal, Lohri, Bihu, and Maghi in different regions. People fly kites, light bonfires, cook special dishes, and worship the sun god .
  - **Holi**: A festival of colors celebrated in March, when the arrival of spring is celebrated. People smear each other with colored powder and water, play with water guns and balloons, and dance to music. It is also a festival of love, forgiveness, and joy.
  - **Diwali**: A festival of lights celebrated in October or November, when the victory of good over evil is celebrated. People light lamps, candles, and fireworks, decorate their homes, exchange gifts and sweets, and worship Lakshmi, the goddess of wealth.
  - **Dussehra**: A festival of triumph celebrated in September or October, when the defeat of the demon king Ravana by Lord Rama is reenacted. People watch plays, processions, and effigies of Ravana being burned. It is also a festival of culture, art, and music.
  - **Ganesh Chaturthi**: A festival of the elephant-headed god Ganesh celebrated in August or September, when idols of Ganesh are installed in homes and public places. People worship Ganesh, offer him sweets, and immerse the idols in water after 10 days.
  - **Navratri**: A festival of the nine forms of the goddess Durga celebrated in September or October, when people worship Durga, perform dances, and wear colorful clothes. It is also a festival of power, devotion, and femininity.
  - **Pushkar Mela**: A fair held in the holy town of Pushkar in Rajasthan in November, when thousands of camels, horses, and cattle are traded. People also take a dip in the sacred lake, visit the temples, and enjoy the cultural and musical events .
  - **Jaisalmer Desert Festival**: A festival held in the golden city of Jaisalmer in Rajasthan in February, when the desert comes alive with camel races, folk dances, puppet shows, turban tying contests, and firework displays. It is also a festival of the desert culture and heritage .
  - **Goa Carnival**: A carnival held in the coastal state of Goa in February or March, when the streets are filled with colorful floats, masks, costumes, and music. People also enjoy the beach parties, food stalls, and cultural shows. It is also a festival of fun, frolic, and freedom .
  - **Konark Dance Festival**: A festival held in the ancient sun temple of Konark in Odisha in December, when classical dancers from all over the country perform in front of the temple. People also enjoy the crafts fair, the sand art exhibition, and the cultural programs. It is also a festival of the dance and music heritage of India .
