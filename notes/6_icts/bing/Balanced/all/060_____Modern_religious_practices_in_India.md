# Modern religious practices in India

- India is a diverse and pluralistic country, where different religions coexist and influence each other. According to the 2011 census, the major religions practiced in India are Hinduism (79.8%), Islam (14.2%), Christianity (2.3%), Sikhism (1.7%), Buddhism (0.7%) and Jainism (0.4%). 
- Religion plays a significant role in the lives of most Indians, as it shapes their personal, social and cultural identities. Religion also affects the political, economic and legal aspects of the country, as different religious groups have different interests, demands and rights.
- Some of the common religious practices and beliefs that are shared by most Indians are:
  - Visiting houses of worship regularly, such as temples, mosques, churches, gurdwaras, monasteries, etc. The survey conducted by Pew Research Center in 2019-2020 found that 77% of Indians say they visit a place of worship at least once a month, and 40% say they do so at least once a week.
  - Giving to charitable causes, such as donating money, food, clothes, etc. to the poor, needy, religious institutions, etc. The same survey found that 71% of Indians say they gave money to charity in the past year, and 64% say they gave food or other goods.
  - Celebrating religious festivals, such as Diwali, Holi, Eid, Christmas, Guru Nanak Jayanti, Buddha Purnima, Mahavir Jayanti, etc. These festivals are marked by various rituals, customs, prayers, songs, dances, etc. that express the religious and cultural values of the communities.
  - Performing religious rites and ceremonies, such as puja, namaz, mass, kirtan, meditation, etc. These are acts of worship, devotion, gratitude, etc. that are done individually or collectively, at home or in public, on daily or special occasions, etc.
  - Following religious norms and values, such as dharma, karma, ahimsa, zakat, halal, haram, etc. These are ethical and moral principles that guide the actions, choices, behaviors, etc. of the believers, and that have implications for their personal and social lives.
- Some of the distinctive religious practices and beliefs that are specific to certain religious groups are:
  - Practicing caste system, which is a hierarchical and hereditary division of society based on occupation, birth, purity, etc. that is mainly prevalent among Hindus, but also affects other religious groups. The caste system has four main categories: Brahmins (priests and scholars), Kshatriyas (warriors and rulers), Vaishyas (merchants and farmers), and Shudras (servants and laborers). There is also a fifth category of Dalits (untouchables), who are considered to be outside the caste system and face discrimination and oppression.
  - Practicing cow protection, which is a belief that cows are sacred and should not be harmed, killed or eaten. This belief is mainly held by Hindus, who consider cows as symbols of fertility, wealth, motherhood, etc. and as manifestations of the goddess. Cow protection is also a source of conflict and violence, as some Hindu groups attack or lynch those who are accused of harming or consuming cows, especially Muslims and Dalits.
  - Practicing vegetarianism, which is a dietary choice that excludes meat, fish, eggs, etc. from the food. This choice is mainly motivated by religious reasons, such as respect for life, non-violence, purity, etc. Vegetarianism is more common among Hindus, Jains and Buddhists, who account for 40%, 87% and 55% of vegetarians in India respectively. However, vegetarianism is not a universal or mandatory practice among these groups, as some of them also consume meat, fish, eggs, etc. depending on their region, caste, sect, etc.
