# Indian Traditions, Culture and Society

- India is a diverse country with a rich and varied heritage of languages, religions, arts, cuisines, philosophies and customs.
- India's culture and traditions are influenced by its ancient history, geography, migrations, invasions, and interactions with other cultures and peoples.
- Some of the main aspects of Indian culture and traditions are:

  - **Arts and Language**: India has a long and distinguished tradition of literature, music, dance, architecture, painting, sculpture and other forms of artistic expression. India has 22 official languages and hundreds of regional languages and dialects. The most ancient and influential language is Sanskrit, which is the source of many Indo-European languages and the language of Hindu scriptures and classical literature.
  - **Philosophy and Religion**: India is the birthplace of four major world religions: Hinduism, Buddhism, Jainism and Sikhism. India also has a large and diverse population of Muslims, Christians, Sikhs, Parsis, Jews and other faiths. India's philosophical traditions include the six schools of Hindu philosophy, the Buddhist schools of thought, the Jain doctrines of non-violence and karma, and the Sikh teachings of monotheism and equality.
  - **Food and Drink**: India's cuisine is diverse and regional, reflecting the variety of climates, cultures, crops and preferences of its people. Indian food is known for its use of spices, herbs, grains, pulses, dairy products, fruits and vegetables. Some of the common dishes include rice, wheat breads, lentils, curries, pickles, chutneys, sweets and snacks. India also has a rich tradition of beverages, such as tea, coffee, lassi, sherbet, and alcoholic drinks like toddy, arrack and feni.
  - **Society and Customs**: India's society is complex and hierarchical, based on factors such as caste, class, religion, region, gender, occupation and education. India has a tradition of joint family system, where several generations live together under one roof. India also has a tradition of respect for elders, hospitality for guests, and reverence for teachers and gurus. India's customs include festivals, ceremonies, rituals, rites of passage, dress codes, etiquette, greetings, and gestures  .
