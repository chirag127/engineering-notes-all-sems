# Persian Literature in India

- Persian literature in India refers to the literary works produced in the Persian language by writers, poets, historians, and scholars who lived in the Indian subcontinent or visited it.
- Persian literature in India began with the rise of the Delhi Sultanate in the 13th century, when Persian became the official language of the court and administration. The Delhi Sultanate was ruled by Persian-speaking dynasties, such as the Ghaznavids, the Khiljis, the Tughlaqs, the Sayyids, and the Lodis, who patronized Persian literature and culture.
- Some of the prominent Persian writers of the Delhi Sultanate period were Amir Khusrow, Minhaj al-Siraj, Ziauddin Barani, Shamsuddin Firoz Shah, and Afif. They wrote poetry, history, biography, geography, ethics, and mysticism in Persian. Amir Khusrow was the most prolific and versatile poet, who composed in various genres and styles, such as ghazal, masnavi, qasida, rubai, and khamsa. He also introduced new poetic forms, such as the tarana, the qawwali, and the doha. He is considered the father of Indo-Persian literature and culture.
- Persian literature in India reached its zenith under the Mughal Empire, which was founded by Babur, a descendant of Timur and Genghis Khan, in 1526. The Mughals were also Persian speakers and admirers of Persian literature and art. They established a sophisticated and cosmopolitan court culture, where Persian was the language of literature, diplomacy, and education. The Mughals also encouraged the translation of Sanskrit, Arabic, and Turkish works into Persian, and vice versa, creating a rich and diverse literary heritage.
- Some of the notable Persian writers of the Mughal period were Abul Fazl, Faizi, Nizamuddin Ahmad, Badauni, Abdul Qadir Badauni, Khwaja Nizamuddin Ahmad, Khafi Khan, and Ghulam Husain Tabatabai. They wrote on various subjects, such as history, politics, religion, philosophy, astronomy, medicine, and music. Abul Fazl was the chief minister and historian of Akbar, the greatest Mughal emperor. He wrote the Akbarnama, a comprehensive history of Akbar's reign, and the Ain-i-Akbari, a detailed account of Akbar's administration, society, and culture. Faizi was Abul Fazl's brother and the poet laureate of Akbar's court. He wrote poetry in Persian and Hindi, and translated several Sanskrit works into Persian, such as the Mahabharata, the Ramayana, and the Lilavati.
- Persian literature in India also flourished in the regional courts and kingdoms, such as the Deccan Sultanates, the Bengal Sultanate, the Awadh Nawabs, the Hyderabad Nizams, and the Sikh Empire. These courts and kingdoms had their own patronage and style of Persian literature, which reflected their local influences and identities. Some of the famous Persian writers of the regional courts and kingdoms were Muhammad Quli Qutb Shah, Wali Deccani, Mirza Mazhar Jan-i-Janan, Mir Taqi Mir, Mirza Ghalib, and Khwaja Hafiz Shirazi. They wrote poetry, prose, and letters in Persian, and contributed to the development of Urdu literature as well.
- Persian literature in India declined in the 19th century, with the advent of British colonial rule and the rise of English and vernacular languages. However, Persian literature in India left a lasting legacy on the Indian literary and cultural landscape, influencing the languages, genres, forms, themes, and styles of Indian literature. Persian literature in India also represents a remarkable example of cross-cultural exchange and synthesis, where Persian and Indian traditions enriched and transformed each other.
