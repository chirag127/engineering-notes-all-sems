### Theatre as Cultural Heritage of India

- Theatre is a composite art form that combines music, dance, drama, and other elements to create a live performance that communicates with the audience.
- Theatre in India has a long and rich history that dates back to the Vedic period, when religious rituals and hymns were performed as a form of worship and entertainment.
- The Natya Shastra, written by Bharat Muni around 500 BC, is the oldest and most authoritative text on theatre art in India. It describes the theory and practice of various aspects of theatre, such as the origin, purpose, structure, genres, styles, techniques, emotions, aesthetics, and conventions of drama.
- Theatre in India has evolved through three main periods: the classical period, the traditional period, and the modern period. Each period has its own distinctive features and forms of theatre that reflect the social, cultural, and political changes of the times.
- The classical period (from 500 BC to 1000 AD) was marked by the development of Sanskrit drama, which followed the rules and principles of the Natya Shastra. Sanskrit drama was mainly based on epics, myths, and legends, and was performed in royal courts and temples. Some of the famous playwrights of this period were Kalidasa, Bhasa, Shudraka, and Vishakhadatta.
- The traditional period (from 1000 AD to 1800 AD) was characterized by the emergence and growth of regional and folk forms of theatre, which were influenced by local languages, cultures, and traditions. These forms of theatre were more popular and accessible to the masses, and often incorporated elements of music, dance, puppetry, and storytelling. Some of the prominent forms of theatre in this period were Kutiyattam, Kathakali, Yakshagana, Bhavai, Nautanki, Tamasha, Jatra, and Bhaona.
- The modern period (from 1800 AD to present) was initiated by the introduction of Western theatre and influences, such as realism, naturalism, social issues, and experimentation. The modern theatre in India also responded to the colonial rule, the nationalist movement, and the post-independence challenges. Some of the notable playwrights and theatre personalities of this period were Rabindranath Tagore, Mohan Rakesh, Vijay Tendulkar, Girish Karnad, Badal Sircar, Habib Tanvir, and Satyadev Dubey.
- Theatre in India is a cultural heritage that showcases the diversity, creativity, and dynamism of the Indian people. It is also a medium of expression, education, and empowerment that reflects the values, beliefs, and aspirations of the society. Theatre in India is a living and evolving art form that continues to adapt and innovate in the contemporary context.
