### Indian Architecture in Ancient India and present times

- Indian architecture is rooted in the history, culture, and religion of India. It reflects the diversity and richness of Indian civilization and its interactions with other cultures and regions.
- Indian architecture can be broadly classified into two main categories: Hindu temple architecture and Indo-Islamic architecture. Both have distinctive features, styles, and influences that evolved over time and across regions.
- Hindu temple architecture is based on the principles of Vastu Shastra, a traditional system of architecture that prescribes the layout, design, and proportions of temples and other buildings. Hindu temples are usually dedicated to a specific deity and consist of a sanctum (garbhagriha), a hall (mandapa), and a tower (shikhara). Hindu temples can be further divided into Nagara, Dravidian, and Vesara styles, depending on the shape and ornamentation of the tower .
- Indo-Islamic architecture is the result of the fusion of Islamic and Indian architectural elements, especially during the medieval period. It is characterized by the use of arches, domes, minarets, and geometric patterns, as well as the incorporation of Hindu motifs and symbols. Some of the prominent examples of Indo-Islamic architecture are Rajput architecture, Mughal architecture, and Indo-Saracenic architecture.
- Rajput architecture is the style of architecture developed by the Rajput kingdoms of northern India from the 11th to the 19th century. It is influenced by both Hindu and Islamic styles, and features elaborate fortifications, palaces, temples, and cenotaphs. Some of the famous Rajput monuments are the Chittor Fort, the Amber Fort, and the Hawa Mahal.
- Mughal architecture is the style of architecture developed by the Mughal emperors of India from the 16th to the 18th century. It is influenced by Persian, Turkish, and Indian styles, and features symmetrical layouts, large domes, slender minarets, and intricate decorations. Some of the famous Mughal monuments are the Taj Mahal, the Red Fort, and the Jama Masjid.
- Indo-Saracenic architecture is the style of architecture developed by the British colonialists in India from the 19th to the early 20th century. It is influenced by Gothic, Renaissance, and Islamic styles, and features pointed arches, domes, turrets, and stained glass windows. Some of the famous Indo-Saracenic monuments are the Victoria Memorial, the Gateway of India, and the Rashtrapati Bhavan.
- The evolution of architecture in India is also influenced by the social, economic, and political changes that occurred in the country over time. The advent of the 20th century saw a growth in the economy and the population, which created a high demand for housing and urban planning. Globalization and migration also led to a shift in Indian architecture from a holistic point of view to a more individualistic and modern one.
- Some of the contemporary Indian architects who have contributed to the development of Indian architecture are Charles Correa, B.V. Doshi, Raj Rewal, Laurie Baker, and Balkrishna Doshi. They have experimented with different materials, techniques, and forms, and have created innovative and sustainable designs that reflect the Indian context and culture.
- Indian architecture is a living and dynamic expression of the Indian identity and heritage. It is a blend of tradition and modernity, diversity and unity, and continuity and change. It is a source of pride and inspiration for the people of India and the world.
