Shankaracharya was a philosopher and theologian who expounded the doctrine of Advaita Vedanta, which is a school of Hindu philosophy that asserts the non-duality of reality. He wrote commentaries on the Brahma-sutra, the principal Upanishads, and the Bhagavadgita, which are the foundational texts of Vedanta. He also established four monastic centers in the four corners of India, which are still active today. He is regarded as one of the most influential thinkers of Indian philosophy and religion.

Advaita Vedanta is based on the idea that Brahman, the supreme reality, is the only reality and that everything else, including the individual self (atman), the world, and God (Ishvara), is an illusion (maya) or a projection of Brahman. The goal of Advaita Vedanta is to realize the identity of one's true self with Brahman, which is the source of bliss and liberation (moksha). Advaita Vedanta uses various methods of reasoning, scriptural interpretation, and meditation to achieve this realization.
