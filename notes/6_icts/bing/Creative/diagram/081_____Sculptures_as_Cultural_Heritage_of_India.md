### Sculptures as Cultural Heritage of India

- Sculpture is one of the oldest and most diverse forms of artistic expression in India, dating back to the Indus Valley Civilization (c. 3300-1300 BCE) and continuing to the present day.
- Sculptures in India reflect the rich and diverse cultural, religious, and historical influences that have shaped the subcontinent over millennia. They also serve as a medium of communication, education, and devotion for the people.
- Sculptures in India are made from various materials, such as stone, metal, wood, clay, ivory, and terracotta, depending on the availability, suitability, and symbolism of the material. Some of the most famous sculptures in India are made from bronze, such as the Chola bronzes of Tamil Nadu, or from sandstone, such as the erotic sculptures of Khajuraho.
- Sculptures in India depict a range of subjects, such as gods and goddesses, kings and queens, saints and sages, animals and plants, and scenes from mythology, history, and everyday life. Some of the most iconic sculptures in India are those of Buddha, Shiva, Vishnu, and Ganesha, who represent different aspects of the Hindu and Buddhist faiths.
- Sculptures in India are not only found in temples, monuments, and museums, but also in public spaces, such as parks, gardens, and streets, where they serve as a source of inspiration, beauty, and pride for the people. Some of the most unique sculptures in India are those of contemporary artists, such as Subodh Gupta, Anish Kapoor, and Nek Chand, who use unconventional materials and techniques to create innovative and provocative works.
- Sculptures in India are an integral part of the country's cultural heritage, as they preserve and showcase the artistic genius, spiritual wisdom, and social diversity of the Indian people. They also contribute to the global recognition and appreciation of India's rich and varied artistic traditions.
