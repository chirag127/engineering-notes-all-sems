The evolution of languages in India is a complex and fascinating topic that involves multiple language families, historical influences, and sociolinguistic factors. According to the web search results, the major language families of South Asia are Indo-Aryan, Dravidian, Tibeto-Burman, and Munda. The Indo-Aryan languages, which are the most widely spoken in India, evolved from Old Indo-Aryan such as Sanskrit, through the Middle Indo-Aryan Prakrit languages and the Apabhramsha of the Middle Ages, to the modern New Indo-Aryan languages such as Hindi, Urdu, Bengali, Gujarati, Marathi, etc. The Dravidian languages, which are mainly spoken in southern India, include Tamil, Telugu, Kannada, Malayalam, etc. The Tibeto-Burman languages, which are mostly spoken in the northeastern and Himalayan regions of India, include Meitei, Bodo, Mizo, etc. The Munda languages, which are spoken by some tribal groups in eastern and central India, include Santali, Mundari, Ho, etc.
