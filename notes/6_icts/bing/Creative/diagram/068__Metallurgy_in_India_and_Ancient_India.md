Metallurgy is the science and technology of extracting and processing metals from ores. Metallurgy in ancient India dates back to the prehistoric times, when copper, bronze, iron and steel were produced and used for various purposes. According to the web search results, some of the salient features of metallurgy in ancient India are:

- Copper metallurgy in India dates back to the Chalcolithic societies in the subcontinent, according to spectrometric tests on copper ore samples discovered from ancient mine pits at Khetri in Rajasthan and metal samples cut from representative Harappan artefacts unearthed from Mitathal in Haryana.
- Zinc was extracted in India as early as in the 4th to 3rd century BCE. Zinc production may have begun in India, and ancient northwestern India is the earliest known civilization that produced zinc on an industrial scale. The distillation technique was developed around 1200 CE at Zawar in Rajasthan.
- Iron metallurgy in India began prior to the 3rd millennium BCE. Metals and related concepts were mentioned in various early Vedic age texts. The Rigveda already uses the Sanskrit term Ayas (आयस) (metal). The independent origin of iron has been convincingly argued by Chakrabarti (1992). The earliest available date, 3050–90 BP, is from Raja‐Nala‐Ka‐Tila in Uttar Pradesh (Tiwari 2003).
- Perhaps as early as 300 BCE, although certainly by 200 CE, high quality steel was being produced in southern India by what Europeans would later call the crucible technique. In this system, high-purity wrought iron, charcoal, and glass were mixed in crucibles and heated until the iron melted and absorbed the carbon. The resulting high-carbon steel, called fūlāḏ فولاذ in Arabic and wootz by later Europeans, was exported throughout much of Asia and Europe.
