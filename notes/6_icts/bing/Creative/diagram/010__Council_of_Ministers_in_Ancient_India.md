According to the ancient Indian science of Society and Polity, as defined in Kauṭilya’s Arthaśāstra, the council of ministers (mantri-pariṣad) was the second highest authority after the king (raja) in the administration of the kingdom. The council of ministers consisted of various officials who assisted the king in different matters of governance, such as finance, defense, justice, foreign affairs, etc. The council of ministers was also responsible for checking the power of the king and ensuring that he followed the dharma. The council of ministers was selected by the king based on their qualifications, character, loyalty and experience. The council of ministers was also expected to be well-versed in the scriptures, politics, economics, law and military affairs.
