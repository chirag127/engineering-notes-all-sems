### The Seven Limbs of the State in Ancient India

- The seven limbs of the state (saptanga) are a political concept given by Kautilya in his treatise Arthashastra  .
- The seven limbs are: King, Amatya (Bureaucrats), Janapada (Territory), Durga (Fort), Kosa (Treasure), Danda (Coercive Authority), and Mitra (Ally)  .
- The seven limbs are interdependent and interrelated, and the state cannot function without any of them .
- The king is the head of the state and the source of law and justice. He is responsible for the welfare and security of his subjects .
- The amatya are the ministers and officials who assist the king in administration and policy-making. They are selected on the basis of merit and loyalty .
- The janapada is the territory or population of the state. It is the source of revenue and manpower for the state. The king should protect and expand his janapada .
- The durga is the fort or capital of the state. It is the center of power and defense. It should be well-built and well-guarded .
- The kosa is the treasury or wealth of the state. It is the means of financing the state activities and maintaining the army. It should be well-managed and well-audited .
- The danda is the coercive authority or military force of the state. It is the instrument of enforcing law and order and deterring enemies. It should be well-trained and well-equipped .
- The mitra is the ally or friend of the state. It is the partner of the state in times of peace and war. It should be well-chosen and well-maintained .
