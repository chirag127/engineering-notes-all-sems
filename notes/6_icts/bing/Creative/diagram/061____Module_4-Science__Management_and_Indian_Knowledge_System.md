## Module 4-Science, Management and Indian Knowledge System

This module covers the following topics:

- Astronomy in India: The development of astronomy in India from the Vedic period to the modern era, the contributions of Indian astronomers such as Aryabhata, Brahmagupta, Bhaskara, Varahamihira, etc. to the fields of planetary motion, eclipses, cosmology, mathematics, etc. The astronomical instruments and observatories built by Indian astronomers, such as the Jantar Mantar, the Yantra Mandir, etc. The influence of Indian astronomy on other cultures and civilizations, such as the Arabs, the Greeks, the Chinese, etc.   

- Chemistry in India: The origin and evolution of chemistry in India from the ancient times to the medieval period, the achievements of Indian chemists such as Nagarjuna, Kanada, Charaka, Sushruta, etc. in the fields of metallurgy, alchemy, medicine, cosmetics, perfumery, etc. The chemical processes and techniques developed by Indian chemists, such as distillation, calcination, sublimation, fermentation, etc. The chemical substances and materials discovered or produced by Indian chemists, such as zinc, mercury, steel, glass, dyes, etc. The impact of Indian chemistry on other cultures and civilizations, such as the Arabs, the Europeans, the Chinese, etc.  

- Mathematics in India: The origin and growth of mathematics in India from the Indus Valley Civilization to the colonial period, the contributions of Indian mathematicians such as Pingala, Panini, Katyayana, Apastamba, Baudhayana, etc. to the fields of geometry, algebra, arithmetic, trigonometry, combinatorics, etc. The mathematical concepts and discoveries made by Indian mathematicians, such as zero, decimal system, place value system, negative numbers, fractions, irrational numbers, etc. The mathematical texts and treatises written by Indian mathematicians, such as the Sulba Sutras, the Aryabhatiya, the Lilavati, the Siddhanta Siromani, etc. The influence of Indian mathematics on other cultures and civilizations, such as the Arabs, the Greeks, the Europeans, the Chinese, etc.   

- Physics in India: The origin and development of physics in India from the Vedic period to the modern era, the contributions of Indian physicists such as Kapila, Kanada, Patanjali, Bharadvaja, etc. to the fields of atomism, optics, acoustics, mechanics, hydrostatics, etc. The physical theories and principles formulated by Indian physicists, such as the theory of five elements, the theory of gunas, the theory of karma, the theory of prana, etc. The physical experiments and observations conducted by Indian physicists, such as the measurement of the speed of light, the measurement of the size of the earth, the measurement of the gravitational constant, etc. The influence of Indian physics on other cultures and civilizations, such as the Arabs, the Greeks, the Europeans, the Chinese, etc.  

- Agriculture in India: The origin and evolution of agriculture in India from the Neolithic period to the present day, the achievements of Indian farmers and agronomists such as Surapala, Varahamihira, Kashyapa, etc. in the fields of crop cultivation, irrigation, soil conservation, pest control, animal husbandry, etc. The agricultural practices and techniques adopted by Indian farmers, such as crop rotation, mixed cropping, organic farming, biodynamic farming, etc. The agricultural crops and products grown or produced by Indian farmers, such as rice, wheat, cotton, sugarcane, spices, etc. The impact of Indian agriculture on other cultures and civilizations, such as the Arabs, the Europeans, the Africans, etc.
