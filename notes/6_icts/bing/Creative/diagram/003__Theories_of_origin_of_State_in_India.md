The theories of origin of state in India are different attempts to explain how the state emerged and evolved in ancient India. There are mainly three theories that are discussed by various scholars and texts, namely:

- Social contract theory: This theory holds that the state is a result of a voluntary agreement between the rulers and the ruled, who chose a king to protect them from external and internal threats and to maintain order and justice. The king was bound by the law and the dharma, and could be removed if he failed to fulfill his duties. This theory is found in some Vedic texts, such as the Aitreya Brahmana, and some Buddhist texts, such as the Digha Nikaya.
- Divine origin theory: This theory asserts that the state is a creation of God or gods, who appointed a king as their representative on earth. The king was endowed with divine qualities and authority, and was above the law and the dharma. He was expected to rule benevolently and righteously, and to uphold the cosmic order. This theory is found in some Puranic texts, such as the Vishnu Purana, and some epic texts, such as the Ramayana and the Mahabharata.
- Organic theory: This theory suggests that the state is a natural and inevitable outcome of the social and historical evolution of human society. The state is an organism that grows and develops according to its own laws and needs. The king is the head of the state, and the other organs of the state are the limbs and the senses. The king is responsible for the welfare and the progress of the state, and the other organs are subordinate to him. This theory is found in some philosophical texts, such as the Arthashastra and the Manusmriti.
