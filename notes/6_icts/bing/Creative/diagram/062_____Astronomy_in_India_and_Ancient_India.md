### Astronomy in India and Ancient India

- Astronomy in India has a long history stretching from pre-historic to modern times. Some of the earliest roots of Indian astronomy can be dated to the period of Indus Valley civilisation or earlier.
- Astronomy in ancient India developed to a sophisticated level as the sages of ancient India were keen observers of the sky generation after generation. They recorded their observations and calculations in various texts, such as the Vedas, the Puranas, the Siddhantas, the Aryabhatiya, the Surya Siddhanta, and the Brihat Samhita.
- Astronomy in ancient India can be divided into two major classes. From the third-century BCE, India started having contact with Greece and other nations outside the subcontinent. So, the basic structure of Indian astronomy had outside influence although the astronomical concepts and the major parameters were India’s own.
- The first class of Indian astronomy is called Vedanga Jyotisha, which is based on the Vedas, the sacred scriptures of Hinduism. Vedanga Jyotisha deals with the measurement of time and the calendar, the determination of auspicious moments for rituals, and the prediction of eclipses and planetary movements.
- The second class of Indian astronomy is called Siddhantic astronomy, which is based on the Siddhantas, or treatises, that were composed by various astronomers from the fifth century CE onwards. Siddhantic astronomy deals with the mathematical and geometric aspects of astronomy, such as the calculation of the positions and motions of the Sun, the Moon, the planets, and the stars, the determination of the size and shape of the Earth, and the construction of astronomical instruments.
- Some of the most prominent astronomers of ancient India were Aryabhata, Varahamihira, Brahmagupta, Bhaskara, and Lalla. They made significant contributions to the fields of trigonometry, algebra, arithmetic, geometry, cosmology, and astrology. They also developed methods for solving various astronomical problems, such as the calculation of the sine, cosine, and tangent functions, the solution of quadratic and indeterminate equations, the estimation of pi, the derivation of the heliocentric model, and the explanation of lunar and solar eclipses  .
- Astronomy in ancient India was not only a science but also a part of the culture and religion of the people. Astronomy was used for determining the dates of festivals, ceremonies, and rituals, as well as for casting horoscopes and making predictions. Astronomy was also linked to the philosophy and mythology of the Hindus, who believed that the universe was created, maintained, and destroyed by the gods, and that the cycles of time were governed by the laws of karma and dharma .
