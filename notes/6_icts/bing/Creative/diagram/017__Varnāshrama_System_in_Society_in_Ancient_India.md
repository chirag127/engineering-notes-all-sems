The Varnāshrama system in society in ancient India was a way of dividing the society into four classes (varnas) and four stages of life (ashramas). Each varna and ashrama had specific duties and responsibilities according to their nature and function. The four varnas were Brahmins (priests and teachers), Kshatriyas (warriors and rulers), Vaishyas (traders and farmers), and Shudras (workers and servants). The four ashramas were Brahmacharya (student), Grihastha (householder), Vanaprastha (forest dweller), and Sannyasa (renunciate).
