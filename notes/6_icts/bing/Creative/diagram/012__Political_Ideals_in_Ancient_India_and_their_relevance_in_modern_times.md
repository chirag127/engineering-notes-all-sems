The diagram can be interpreted as follows:

- The outer circle represents the political ideals in ancient India, which were based on various sources such as the Vedas, the Arthashastra, the Ramayana, the Mahabharata, the Manusmriti, the Dharmasutras, the Smritis, the Sukraniti, the Santiparva, etc. Some of the main features of these ideals were:

  - The state was considered as an instrument for social reform, economic progress, welfare of the people, national integration and nation building.
  - The king was the supreme authority and enjoyed all powers of the state, but he was also bound by the dharma (duty, law, morality) and was accountable to the people .
  - The political system was based on the principle of varna (class) and ashrama (stage of life), which determined the rights and duties of different groups of people .
  - The political thinking was realistic, pragmatic, and flexible, and adapted to the changing circumstances and needs of the society .
  - The political thinking was also influenced by various schools of philosophy, such as Samkhya, Yoga, Nyaya, Vaisheshika, Mimamsa, Vedanta, etc., which provided different perspectives on the nature of reality, knowledge, ethics, and politics .

- The inner circle represents the political ideals in modern India, which were shaped by various factors such as the colonial rule, the freedom struggle, the constitution, the democracy, the secularism, the socialism, the nationalism, the globalization, etc. Some of the main features of these ideals are:

  - The state is considered as a sovereign, secular, democratic, and federal republic, which guarantees the fundamental rights and duties of the citizens.
  - The government is based on the principle of separation of powers, checks and balances, and rule of law, and is accountable to the people through regular elections, free media, and civil society.
  - The political system is based on the principle of equality, liberty, fraternity, and justice, which aims to eliminate the discrimination and oppression of any group of people on the basis of caste, class, religion, gender, etc.
  - The political thinking is influenced by various ideologies, such as liberalism, conservatism, socialism, communism, fascism, nationalism, regionalism, etc., which provide different visions and agendas for the society.
  - The political thinking is also influenced by various thinkers, leaders, and movements, both from India and abroad, such as Gandhi, Nehru, Ambedkar, Patel, Bose, Lohia, Marx, Lenin, Mao, etc., who have contributed to the political discourse and action in India.
