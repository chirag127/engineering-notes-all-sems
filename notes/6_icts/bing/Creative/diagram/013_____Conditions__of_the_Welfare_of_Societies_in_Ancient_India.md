### Conditions of the Welfare of Societies in Ancient India

- The concept of social welfare in ancient India was based on the idea of **duty** as opposed to **rights** . The duty of an individual was determined by his or her **varna** (social class) and **ashrama** (stage of life) and was guided by the principles of **dharma** (righteousness) and **karma** (action and consequence)  .
- The welfare of the society was also the responsibility of the **king** and his administration, who were expected to follow the **rajadharma** (duty of the ruler) and to protect and promote the interests of the people  . The king was advised by the **brahmins** (priests and scholars) and assisted by the **kshatriyas** (warriors and nobles) in maintaining law and order, justice, security, and prosperity .
- The ancient Indian texts and inscriptions provide evidence of various welfare measures undertaken by the kings and their officials, such as building roads, bridges, wells, tanks, temples, hospitals, schools, rest houses, and charitable institutions; providing relief during famines, epidemics, and natural disasters; granting land, money, and other gifts to the needy, the meritorious, and the religious; and regulating trade, commerce, and taxation  .
- The welfare of the society was also dependent on the **cooperation** and **harmony** among the different varnas and ashramas, as well as the **respect** and **care** for the elders, women, children, and the weaker sections of the society, such as the **shudras** (servants and laborers), the **chandalas** (outcasts and untouchables), and the **dasis** (slaves and concubines)  .
- The social life of the people in ancient India was enriched by various **cultural** and **religious** practices, such as the performance of **sanskaras** (sacraments) and **yajnas** (sacrifices), the observance of **festivals** and **fasts**, the participation in **music**, **dance**, **drama**, **sports**, and **games**, and the use of **dresses**, **ornaments**, **cosmetics**, and **amulets**  .
