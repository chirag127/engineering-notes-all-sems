### Political Ideals in Ancient India and their relevance in modern times

- Ancient India had a rich tradition of political thought and practice that spanned from the Vedic period to the medieval period.
- Some of the political ideals that emerged from ancient India are:

  - Democracy: The Rig Veda, the oldest scripture of Hinduism, advocated the principle of **Samjnana**, which means the collective consciousness of the people and the source of political authority. The Vedic society also had institutions of **Sabha** and **Samiti**, which were assemblies of elders and commoners that participated in decision-making and governance.
  - Dharma: The concept of **Dharma**, which means the cosmic law or the moral duty, was the basis of political legitimacy and social order in ancient India. The king was expected to uphold Dharma and protect the people from injustice and oppression. The people were also expected to follow their respective Dharma according to their caste, stage of life, and occupation .
  - Arthashastra: The term **Arthashastra** means the science of wealth or the art of politics. It was a branch of knowledge that dealt with the principles and practices of statecraft, administration, diplomacy, and warfare. The most famous exponent of Arthashastra was Kautilya, who wrote a treatise on the subject in the 4th century BCE. Kautilya advocated a pragmatic and ruthless approach to politics, based on the concept of **Rajamandala**, which means the circle of kings or the geopolitical theory of relations among states .

- The relevance of these political ideals in modern times can be seen in the following ways:

  - Democracy: The idea of democracy as the rule of the people by the people and for the people has been adopted by many modern nations, including India, as the preferred form of government. The democratic institutions of ancient India, such as the Sabha and the Samiti, can be seen as the precursors of the modern parliament and the civil society. The concept of Samjnana can also inspire the modern citizens to develop a sense of civic responsibility and national identity.
  - Dharma: The concept of Dharma as the ethical and spiritual foundation of society can still guide the modern leaders and citizens to act with integrity, compassion, and justice. The idea of Dharma can also promote the values of pluralism, tolerance, and harmony among different religions, cultures, and communities. The concept of Dharma can also help to balance the material and spiritual aspects of life.
  - Arthashastra: The principles and practices of Arthashastra can still be useful for the modern states to deal with the challenges and opportunities of the globalized world. The concept of Rajamandala can help the modern states to understand the dynamics and strategies of international relations and alliances. The concept of Arthashastra can also help the modern states to develop a realistic and pragmatic approach to politics, based on the assessment of the strengths and weaknesses of the self and the others.
