## Module 3- Indian Religion, Philosophy, and Practices

- India is a diverse and pluralistic country with many religions, philosophies, and practices that have influenced its culture, society, and history.
- Hinduism is the oldest and most dominant religion in India, which developed from the Vedic religion of the ancient Indo-Aryans. Hinduism is a complex and diverse tradition that encompasses many schools of thought, sects, and rituals. Hinduism is based on the concepts of dharma (duty, law, morality), karma (action, cause and effect), samsara (cycle of rebirth), and moksha (liberation from samsara).
- Hinduism has six orthodox schools of philosophy that accept the authority of the Vedas, the sacred scriptures of Hinduism. They are: Samkhya (dualism of matter and spirit), Yoga (discipline of body and mind), Nyaya (logic and epistemology), Vaisheshika (atomism and naturalism), Mimamsa (exegesis and ritual), and Vedanta (metaphysics and monism).
- Hinduism also has many heterodox schools of philosophy that reject the authority of the Vedas. They are: Buddhism (founded by Gautama Buddha, who taught the Four Noble Truths and the Eightfold Path to end suffering and attain nirvana), Jainism (founded by Mahavira, who taught the principles of non-violence, non-attachment, and non-absolutism), and Charvaka (materialism and skepticism).
- Hinduism has many gods and goddesses, who are manifestations of the supreme reality, Brahman. Some of the most popular deities are Vishnu (the preserver), Shiva (the destroyer), Ganesha (the elephant-headed god of success), Lakshmi (the goddess of wealth), Durga (the goddess of power), and Saraswati (the goddess of learning).
- Hinduism has many festivals and celebrations, such as Diwali (the festival of lights), Holi (the festival of colors), Navratri (the festival of nine nights), Dussehra (the victory of good over evil), and Ganesh Chaturthi (the birthday of Ganesha).
- Hinduism has many practices and rituals, such as puja (worship), mantra (sacred sound), yoga (physical and mental exercise), meditation (concentration and awareness), pranayama (breath control), and ahimsa (non-violence).
- Hinduism has many sacred places and symbols, such as temples, shrines, rivers, mountains, cows, swastikas, and om.
