### Modern religious practices in India

- India is a secular country with a diverse population that follows various religions, such as Hinduism, Islam, Christianity, Sikhism, Buddhism, Jainism, Zoroastrianism, and others .
- Religion has historically influenced Indian society on a political, cultural and economic level, and there is a sense of pride associated with the country’s rich religious history.
- India’s religious groups share several religious practices and beliefs, such as belief in karma, reincarnation, meditation, yoga, vegetarianism, fasting, pilgrimage, and festivals.
- Indians also engage in practices or hold beliefs that are more specific to their own traditions, such as worshiping different gods and goddesses, reading different scriptures, following different rituals, and observing different codes of conduct .
- Indians regularly visit their houses of worship, such as temples, mosques, churches, gurdwaras, and monasteries, to offer prayers, donations, and services.
- Most Indians also give to charitable causes, such as helping the poor, the sick, the elderly, the animals, and the environment, as part of their religious duty or generosity.
- Modern religious practices in India are also influenced by factors such as globalization, urbanization, education, media, and social movements, which may lead to changes in religious affiliation, identity, diversity, and tolerance .
