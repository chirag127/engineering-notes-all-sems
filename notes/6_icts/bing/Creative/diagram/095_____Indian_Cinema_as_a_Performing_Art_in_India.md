### Indian Cinema as a Performing Art in India

- Indian cinema is a form of art that combines storytelling, performing arts, and science to create audio-visual narratives that reflect the culture, history, and diversity of India.
- Indian cinema has a rich and varied history that spans over a century, from the silent era to the present day, and encompasses different genres, languages, and regions of the country.
- Indian cinema has been influenced by various forms of performing arts, such as theater, music, dance, and puppetry, that have been part of the Indian tradition for centuries.
- Indian cinema has also contributed to the development and promotion of performing arts, by providing a platform for artists, performers, and writers to showcase their talents and reach a wider audience.
- Indian cinema has been shaped by the socio-political and economic changes that have occurred in India since its independence, and has reflected the aspirations, challenges, and struggles of the Indian people.
- Indian cinema has also been a source of inspiration and innovation for other forms of art, such as literature, painting, and photography, and has influenced the global film industry and culture.

Some of the main points to remember about Indian cinema as a performing art in India are:

- Indian cinema is a synthesis of various elements of performing arts, such as dialogue, music, song, dance, gesture, and expression, that create an emotional impact on the audience.
- Indian cinema follows the principles of the Natyasastra, an ancient treatise on drama and aesthetics, that defines the concept of rasa, or the essence of emotion, as the core of any artistic expression.
- Indian cinema has a diverse and dynamic nature, as it encompasses different styles, forms, and languages of filmmaking, such as Bollywood, regional cinema, parallel cinema, art cinema, and independent cinema.
- Indian cinema has a social and cultural significance, as it reflects the realities, values, and identities of the Indian society, and also challenges the stereotypes, norms, and prejudices that exist in it.
- Indian cinema has a global and universal appeal, as it transcends the boundaries of language, culture, and geography, and connects with the audiences across the world through its stories, characters, and emotions.
