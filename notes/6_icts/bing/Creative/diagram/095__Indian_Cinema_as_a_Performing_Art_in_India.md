Indian cinema is a form of performing art that has a long and rich history, dating back to the early 20th century. It is one of the most popular and influential mediums of storytelling in India, reflecting the diverse cultures, languages, religions, and social issues of the country. Indian cinema encompasses various genres, styles, and formats, such as feature films, documentaries, short films, animation, and web series. Indian cinema also has a global presence and appeal, with films being produced and screened in various languages and countries.

The following diagram illustrates the basic architecture of Indian cinema as a performing art in India:

### Indian Cinema as a Performing Art in India

```
+-------------------+     +-------------------+     +-------------------+
|                   |     |                   |     |                   |
|   Film Industry   |     |   Film Audience   |     |   Film Culture    |
```

The diagram shows the three main components of Indian cinema as a performing art: the film industry, the film audience, and the film culture. The film industry is responsible for creating, distributing, and regulating films in India. It consists of various stakeholders, such as filmmakers, actors, technicians, producers, distributors, exhibitors, and regulators. The film industry also has various associations and bodies that represent and promote the interests of different segments and regions of the industry.

The film audience is the group of people who watch and consume films in India. It includes various segments, such as urban, rural, regional, national, and international audiences. The film audience also has different preferences, tastes, and expectations from films, depending on their age, gender, education, income, and culture. The film audience is influenced by various factors, such as word-of-mouth, reviews, ratings, marketing, and social media.

The film culture is the set of practices, values, and norms that shape and reflect the meaning and significance of films in India. It includes various aspects, such as film festivals, film awards, film education, film criticism, film appreciation, film archives, and film studies. The film culture also influences and is influenced by the broader social, political, and economic context of India. The film culture also fosters a sense of identity, community, and diversity among film lovers and practitioners in India.
