#### Varnāshrama System in Society in Ancient India

- The varnāshrama system was a theoretical framework of social and moral order in ancient Hindu society .
- It consisted of two main components: varna and ashrama.
- Varna was the segmental division of the society into four classes: Brahman, Kshatriya, Vaishya and Shudra .
- Brahman was the class of priests and teachers, who were responsible for studying and teaching the sacred scriptures, performing rituals and advising the rulers .
- Kshatriya was the class of kings, warriors and aristocrats, who were responsible for protecting the society, administering justice and leading the military .
- Vaishya was the class of traders, merchants and farmers, who were responsible for producing and distributing wealth, engaging in commerce and supporting the other classes .
- Shudra was the class of servants, laborers and artisans, who were responsible for serving the other classes, performing menial tasks and following the instructions of their superiors .
- Ashrama was the system of stages of life that each individual had to go through according to their varna and age.
- There were four ashramas: Brahmacharya, Grihastha, Vanaprastha and Sannyasa.
- Brahmacharya was the stage of studentship, where the individual had to live with a guru and learn the skills and duties of their varna, as well as practice celibacy and discipline.
- Grihastha was the stage of householdership, where the individual had to marry, raise a family and perform the social and economic obligations of their varna, as well as enjoy the pleasures of life.
- Vanaprastha was the stage of retirement, where the individual had to withdraw from worldly affairs, renounce their possessions and attachments, and prepare for the final stage of life.
- Sannyasa was the stage of renunciation, where the individual had to give up their identity, social ties and worldly desires, and dedicate themselves to the pursuit of spiritual liberation.
- The varnāshrama system was meant to provide a harmonious and hierarchical structure of society, where each class and stage of life had its own duties, rights and privileges, and where the individual could progress towards the ultimate goal of moksha or liberation .
- However, the varnāshrama system was also criticized for being rigid, oppressive and discriminatory, especially towards the lower classes and women, who were denied access to education, freedom and equality .
- The varnāshrama system was also challenged by various social and religious movements, such as Buddhism, Jainism, Bhakti and Sikhism, that rejected the authority of the Vedas, the caste system and the ritualistic practices, and advocated for a more egalitarian and universal vision of humanity .
