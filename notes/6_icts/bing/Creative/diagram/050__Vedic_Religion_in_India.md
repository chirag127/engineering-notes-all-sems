The Vedic religion was the religion of the ancient Indo-European-speaking peoples who entered India about 1500 BCE from the region of present-day Iran. It is the oldest stratum of religious activity in India for which there exist written materials. It was one of the major traditions that shaped Hinduism. The Vedic religion was based on the sacred texts known as the Vedas, which consist of four collections of hymns, rituals, and philosophical speculations. The Vedic religion was centered on the performance of sacrifices to various deities, such as Indra, Agni, Varuna, and Soma. The Vedic religion also had a complex social structure, divided into four classes or varnas: Brahmins (priests), Kshatriyas (warriors), Vaishyas (merchants), and Shudras (servants). The Vedic religion gradually evolved into the classical Hinduism of the later periods, incorporating new elements such as the doctrine of karma and reincarnation, the worship of personal gods, and the emergence of various sects and schools of thought.
