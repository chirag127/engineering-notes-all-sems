### Mathematics in India and Ancient India

- Mathematics or 'Ganita' was the 'Science of Calculations' in ancient India.
- Mathematics was considered as one of the highest sciences and the head of all Vedas and shastras.
- Mathematics in ancient India was primarily studied in the context of numerical computation and geometric measurement.
- Mathematics in ancient India was also closely related to astronomy, astrology, medicine, architecture, art, and music.
- Some of the major achievements of mathematics in ancient India are:
  - The concept of zero and the decimal place-value system  .
  - The use of negative numbers, fractions, algebraic identities, quadratic equations, and indeterminate equations .
  - The development of trigonometry, including the sine, cosine, and tangent functions, and the calculation of accurate values of pi and other constants  .
  - The discovery of the Fibonacci sequence, the binomial theorem, and the Pascal's triangle .
  - The invention of various techniques of geometry, such as the Pythagorean theorem, the formula for the area of a triangle, and the concept of similarity .
- Some of the prominent mathematicians of ancient India are:
  - Aryabhata (476-550 CE), who wrote the Aryabhatiya, a treatise on astronomy and mathematics, and introduced the concepts of zero, pi, and the heliocentric model of the solar system  .
  - Brahmagupta (598-668 CE), who wrote the Brahmasphutasiddhanta, a work on arithmetic, algebra, and geometry, and gave the rules for operating with zero and negative numbers  .
  - Bhaskara II (1114-1185 CE), who wrote the Siddhanta Siromani, a comprehensive text on astronomy and mathematics, and solved various problems of algebra, trigonometry, and calculus  .
  - Varahamihira (505-587 CE), who wrote the Panchasiddhantika, a summary of five earlier astronomical works, and the Brihat Samhita, an encyclopedia of various subjects, including mathematics .
