Puppetry is a fascinating art form that has always sparked curiosity from an audience of all ages. It is one of India’s most diverse cultural heritages, with a variety of regional genres. Puppetry shows include the representation of graphic stories using puppets, live music, gestures and narratives. They are therefore at the intersection of theatrics and storytelling. Puppetry has mostly been performed at religious events, ceremonies and temple festivals. Puppetry in India has a divine origin, according to some legends, and it forms an integral part of the culture of performance in the country  .

The diagram shows the three main components of puppetry: the puppeteer, the puppet and the audience. The puppeteer is the person who manipulates the puppet, using strings, rods, gloves or shadows. The puppet is the object that represents a character or a scene, using materials such as wood, leather, cloth or paper. The audience is the people who watch and enjoy the puppet show, using their imagination and emotions.

The diagram also shows the three main aspects of puppetry: the music, the narration and the spectacle. The music is the sound that accompanies the puppet show, using instruments such as drums, flutes, cymbals or bells. The narration is the voice that tells the story, using languages such as Hindi, Tamil, Telugu or Bengali. The spectacle is the visual effect that creates the atmosphere, using lights, colours, props or scenery.

The diagram also shows the three main dimensions of puppetry: the tradition, the story and the culture. The tradition is the history and heritage of puppetry, using sources such as legends, myths, epics or folktales. The story is the content and message of puppetry, using themes such as religion, morality, humour or social issues. The culture is the context and relevance of puppetry, using elements such as geography, language, music or art.
