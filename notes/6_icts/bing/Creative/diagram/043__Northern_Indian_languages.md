The Northern Indian languages are a group of languages that belong to the Indo-Aryan branch of the Indo-European language family. They are spoken by about 78% of the population of India, mainly in the states and union territories of North India.  

The Northern Indian languages can be divided into several subgroups, such as:

- Hindustani: A lingua franca that includes both Hindi and Urdu, as well as many regional dialects. It is the most widely spoken language in India and has official status in several states and union territories. 
- Western Hindi: A group of dialects that are closely related to Hindi, such as Braj Bhasha, Bundeli, Haryanvi and Khari Boli. They are spoken mainly in the states of Uttar Pradesh, Rajasthan, Haryana and Delhi. 
- Eastern Hindi: A group of dialects that are also related to Hindi, but have more influence from other languages, such as Awadhi, Bagheli, Chhattisgarhi and Fiji Hindi. They are spoken mainly in the states of Uttar Pradesh, Madhya Pradesh, Chhattisgarh and Bihar. 
- Bihari: A group of languages that are distinct from Hindi, such as Bhojpuri, Magahi, Maithili and Angika. They are spoken mainly in the state of Bihar and some neighboring regions.  
- Punjabi: A language that is spoken mainly in the state of Punjab and the union territory of Chandigarh, as well as by the Sikh diaspora around the world. It has its own script, called Gurmukhi, and is influenced by Persian and Arabic. 
- Pahari: A group of languages that are spoken in the Himalayan regions of North India, such as Garhwali, Kumaoni, Nepali and Dogri. They have some similarities with Punjabi and Hindi, but also have their own features and scripts. 
- Kashmiri: A language that is spoken mainly in the union territory of Jammu and Kashmir, as well as by the Kashmiri diaspora. It has its own script, called Sharda, and is influenced by Persian and Sanskrit.
