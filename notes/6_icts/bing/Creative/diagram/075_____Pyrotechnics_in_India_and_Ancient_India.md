### Pyrotechnics in India and Ancient India

Pyrotechnics is the science and art of creating fireworks and other effects involving the use of explosives, fire, smoke, sound and light. Pyrotechnics has a long and rich history in India, dating back to the medieval period. Here are some points to note about pyrotechnics in India and ancient India:

- One of the earliest notes of pyrotechnical shows in India is made by Abdur Razzaq, the ambassador of the Timurid Sultan Shahrukh to the court of the Vijayanagar king Devaraya II in 1443. He wrote that he witnessed a grand display of fireworks at the king's palace, which lasted for an hour and filled the sky with various shapes and colors.
- Fireworks and pyrotechnic shows existed as a form of royal entertainment in many medieval Indian kingdoms during festivals, events and special occasions like weddings. Manufacturing formulas for fireworks describing pyrotechnic mixtures are found within Kautukachintamani, a Sanskrit volume by Gajapati Prataparudradeva (1497-1539), a reputed king of Orissa .
- Gunpowder, an essential ingredient of fire crackers, was unknown in ancient India. It was introduced to India by the Mongols and the Arabs in the 13th century. The earliest reference to gunpowder in India is found in the Tarikh-i-Firishta (History of the Deccan) by Muhammad Qasim Firishta, who wrote that the Bahmani Sultanate used gunpowder and cannons in the siege of Warangal in 1363.
- The Mughal Empire, which ruled most of India from the 16th to the 18th century, was also fond of fireworks and pyrotechnics. The Mughal emperors, especially Akbar, Jahangir and Shah Jahan, patronized the art of fireworks and employed skilled pyrotechnicians to create spectacular displays for their courts and celebrations. The Ain-i-Akbari, a record of Akbar's administration, mentions several types of fireworks, such as rockets, fountains, wheels, stars, snakes and birds.
- The British East India Company, which gradually gained control over India from the 18th century onwards, also adopted the use of fireworks and pyrotechnics for their military and ceremonial purposes. They imported fireworks from China and Europe and also developed their own varieties. They used fireworks to celebrate their victories, such as the Battle of Plassey in 1757 and the Battle of Buxar in 1764. They also used fireworks to mark important occasions, such as the coronation of King George III in 1761 and the visit of King George V and Queen Mary to India in 1911.
- The Indian independence movement, which emerged in the late 19th and early 20th century, also used fireworks and pyrotechnics as a form of protest and resistance against the British colonial rule. The Indian National Congress, the main political party of the movement, organized fireworks displays to celebrate important dates, such as the first anniversary of the Quit India Movement in 1943 and the first anniversary of the Indian Independence Act in 1948. The Indian freedom fighters, such as Bhagat Singh, Chandrashekhar Azad and Subhash Chandra Bose, also used fireworks and explosives to carry out attacks on the British targets, such as the Lahore Central Assembly in 1929 and the Chittagong Armoury Raid in 1930.
- Today, fireworks and pyrotechnics are an integral part of the Indian culture and tradition. They are widely used to celebrate various festivals, such as Diwali, Holi, Dussehra, Eid, Christmas and New Year. They are also used to mark personal and social events, such as weddings, birthdays, anniversaries and inaugurations. India is one of the largest producers and consumers of fireworks in the world, with an estimated annual turnover of over Rs. 20,000 crore. The major centers of fireworks production in India are Sivakasi in Tamil Nadu, Champahati in West Bengal, Shivkashi in Uttar Pradesh and Khairatabad in Telangana .
