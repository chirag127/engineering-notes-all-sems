### Music as Cultural Heritage of India

- Music is an integral part of the culture and heritage of India. It reflects the diversity, history, and spirituality of the country.
- Music of India can be broadly classified into two categories: classical and folk. Classical music is based on ancient scriptures and traditions, while folk music is rooted in the regional and local cultures.
- Classical music of India has two main traditions: Hindustani and Carnatic. Hindustani music is prevalent in the northern and central regions of India, and has influences from Persian, Arabic, and Turkish music. Carnatic music is dominant in the southern regions of India, and is more devotional and orthodox in nature.
- Both Hindustani and Carnatic music follow a system of ragas and talas. Ragas are melodic modes that evoke different moods and emotions, and talas are rhythmic cycles that structure the music. There are hundreds of ragas and talas in Indian music, each with its own rules and characteristics.
- Some of the prominent instruments used in Indian classical music are the sitar, sarod, tabla, veena, mridangam, flute, and violin. Some of the renowned musicians of Indian classical music are Ravi Shankar, Ali Akbar Khan, Zakir Hussain, M.S. Subbulakshmi, Bismillah Khan, and Lata Mangeshkar.
- Folk music of India is diverse and varied, reflecting the regional and ethnic identities of the people. Folk music is often associated with festivals, rituals, celebrations, and social occasions. Some of the popular forms of folk music are bhangra, garba, dandiya, lavani, baul, qawwali, and bhajan.
- Folk music of India also uses a variety of instruments, such as the dhol, dholak, harmonium, sarangi, ektara, and bansuri. Some of the famous folk musicians of India are Gurdas Maan, Falguni Pathak, Abida Parveen, Nusrat Fateh Ali Khan, and Mira Bai.
- Music of India is not only a cultural heritage, but also a living and evolving art form. It has been influenced by various external and internal factors, such as colonialism, globalization, modernization, and social movements. It has also influenced and inspired other musical genres, such as rock, pop, jazz, and fusion.
- Music of India is a rich and diverse expression of the Indian ethos, and a source of pride and identity for the people. It is a universal language that transcends boundaries and connects hearts. It is a treasure that needs to be preserved and promoted for the future generations.
