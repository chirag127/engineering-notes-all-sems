### Socio religious reform movement of 19th century in India

- Socio religious reform movement of 19th century in India refers to the wave of reforming activities that influenced the Indian society and religion through the efforts of various samajas, societies and other organisations .
- The main causes of the emergence of the reform movements were:
  - The spread of western education and liberal ideas that exposed the Indians to the rational and scientific outlook of the West and made them question the superstitions and irrationalities of their own society and religion.
  - The contact with the British rule and culture that made the Indians aware of the political, economic and social backwardness of their country and the need for modernisation and progress.
  - The rise of nationalism and patriotism that inspired the Indians to revive and reform their glorious heritage and culture and to resist the cultural imperialism of the British.
  - The social evils and malpractices that plagued the Indian society and religion, such as caste system, untouchability, sati, child marriage, purdah, polygamy, female infanticide, etc. that violated the human rights and dignity of the people, especially women and lower castes.
- The main types of the reform movements were :
  - The Brahmo Samaj, founded by Raja Ram Mohan Roy in 1828, that aimed to reform Hinduism by rejecting idolatry, polytheism, rituals, priesthood, caste system, etc. and promoting monotheism, rationalism, social equality, women's education and emancipation, etc. It also advocated inter-caste and inter-religious marriages and widow remarriage.
  - The Prarthana Samaj, founded by Atmaram Pandurang in 1867, that followed the ideals of the Brahmo Samaj and worked for the social and religious reform of the Hindus in Maharashtra. It also opposed untouchability, child marriage, purdah, etc. and supported widow remarriage, women's education, etc.
  - The Arya Samaj, founded by Swami Dayanand Saraswati in 1875, that aimed to revive and reform Hinduism by going back to the original and pure teachings of the Vedas. It rejected idolatry, polytheism, rituals, priesthood, animal sacrifice, etc. and promoted monotheism, rationalism, social equality, etc. It also encouraged the dissemination of western scientific knowledge and the upliftment of women and lower castes.
  - The Ramakrishna Mission, founded by Swami Vivekananda in 1897, that propagated the philosophy of Vedanta and the universal message of Hinduism. It emphasised the harmony of religions, the service of humanity, the spiritualisation of life, etc. It also established schools, colleges, hospitals, orphanages, etc. for the welfare of the people.
  - The Theosophical Society, founded by Madame Blavatsky and Colonel Olcott in 1875, that aimed to promote the study of ancient religions and philosophies, especially Hinduism and Buddhism. It also worked for the revival and reform of Hinduism by defending it from the attacks of the Christian missionaries and supporting the causes of women's education, inter-caste marriages, etc.
  - The Aligarh Movement, founded by Sir Syed Ahmed Khan in 1875, that aimed to reform and modernise the Muslim community in India by promoting western education, scientific temper, social reforms, etc. It also established the Mohammedan Anglo-Oriental College, later known as the Aligarh Muslim University, as a centre of learning and culture for the Muslims.
  - The Singh Sabha Movement, founded by Khem Singh Bedi and others in 1873, that aimed to reform and revive Sikhism by purifying it from the Hindu and Muslim influences and restoring its original teachings and practices. It also worked for the spread of education, social reforms, etc. among the Sikhs.
- The impact of the reform movements were :
  - They contributed to the social and religious awakening and modernisation of the Indian society and religion by challenging the orthodox and conservative elements and introducing progressive and rational changes.
  - They fostered the spirit of nationalism and patriotism among the Indians by creating a sense of pride and self-respect in their own culture and heritage and by resisting the cultural domination of the British.
  - They prepared the ground for the growth of the Indian National Movement by creating a common platform and a common identity for the Indians of different regions, religions
