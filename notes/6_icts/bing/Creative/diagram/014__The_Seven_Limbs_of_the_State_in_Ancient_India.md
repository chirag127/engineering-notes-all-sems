The Seven Limbs of the State in Ancient India is a political theory formulated by Kautilya in his treatise Arthashastra. It refers to the seven essential elements of a state that are necessary for its stability and prosperity. The seven limbs are:

- King: The ruler of the state who is responsible for peace, justice and security. He is the head of the administration and the supreme authority.
- Amatya: The bureaucrats or ministers who assist the king in governing the state. They are the experts in various fields and the implementers of the king's policies.
- Janapada: The territory or population of the state. It is the source of revenue and manpower for the state. It should be well protected and cultivated.
- Durga: The fort or the capital city of the state. It is the center of power and culture. It should be well fortified and accessible.
- Kosa: The treasure or the wealth of the state. It is the means of financing the state's activities and welfare. It should be well managed and increased.
- Danda: The coercive authority or the military of the state. It is the instrument of enforcing the law and defending the state from enemies. It should be well trained and equipped.
- Mitra: The ally or the friendly state. It is the partner of the state in maintaining the balance of power and mutual support. It should be well chosen and cultivated.
