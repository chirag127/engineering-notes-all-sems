Chemistry in India and Ancient India

Chemistry in ancient India was called Rasayana, which means the art of extracting the essence of substances. It was closely related to metallurgy, medicine, alchemy and other sciences. The development of chemistry in India can be divided into five stages, according to the historian of chemistry Prafulla Chandra Ray:

- The pre-Vedic stage (up to 1500 BCE), which includes the Harappan civilization and its achievements in pottery, faience, mortars, cements and other materials.
- The Vedic and Ayurvedic stage (up to 700 CE), which mentions various fermented drinks, metals, dyes, cosmetics, perfumes and medicines derived from plants, animals and minerals.
- The transitional stage (700 CE to 1100 CE), which marks the emergence of alchemical texts and practices, such as the Rasarnava, Rasaratnakara and Rasendra Chintamani, which aimed at transmuting metals and creating elixirs of immortality.
- The Tantric stage (700 CE to 1300 CE), which is characterized by the influence of Tantric philosophy and practices on alchemy and medicine, such as the use of mercury, sulfur, arsenic and other minerals for various purposes.
- The Iatro-Chemical stage (1300 CE to 1600 CE), which is marked by the integration of chemistry with medicine and pharmacology, such as the works of Nagarjuna, Vagbhata, Chakrapani Datta and others, who described various methods of preparing and administering drugs, metals and minerals.
