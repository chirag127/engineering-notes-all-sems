The diagram shows three main branches of physics in India and Ancient India, namely Vedic Physics, Nyaya-Vaisheshika, and Sankhya-Yoga. Each branch has its own sub-branches and prominent figures.

Vedic Physics is the physics derived from the Vedas, the ancient scriptures of India. It deals with topics such as energy, mass, light, radiation, space-time, and elements. Some of the concepts of Vedic Physics are similar to modern physics, such as the equivalence of energy and mass, the wave-particle duality, and the discrete nature of atoms. Vedic Physics also includes astronomy, which is the study of the celestial bodies and their motions. Aryabhata is one of the most famous astronomers of ancient India, who calculated the value of pi, the circumference of the earth, the length of the year, and the lunar and solar eclipses.

Nyaya-Vaisheshika is a school of philosophy that combines logic (Nyaya) and naturalism (Vaisheshika). It deals with topics such as causation, inference, perception, and classification. Nyaya-Vaisheshika also developed the atomic theory, which states that all physical objects are composed of indivisible and eternal atoms (anu) that combine in different ways to form complex substances. Kanada is the founder of the Vaisheshika school and the author of the Vaisheshika Sutras, which describe the nature and properties of atoms and their combinations.

Sankhya-Yoga is another school of philosophy that combines metaphysics (Sankhya) and practice (Yoga). It deals with topics such as the origin and evolution of the universe, the nature and types of consciousness, and the methods of liberation from suffering. Sankhya-Yoga also developed the cosmology, which is the study of the structure and dynamics of the universe. Sankhya-Yoga proposes that the universe is composed of two fundamental principles: purusha (spirit) and prakriti (matter), which interact and manifest in various forms and levels of existence. Kapila is the founder of the Sankhya school and the author of the Sankhya Karika, which explain the principles and categories of Sankhya cosmology.
