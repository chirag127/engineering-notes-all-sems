Theatre as Cultural Heritage of India is a broad topic that encompasses various forms and styles of performing arts that have evolved over centuries in different regions and communities of India. Theatre in India has its roots in the ancient treatise Natya Shastra, which lays down the principles and techniques of drama, dance, music, and aesthetics. Theatre in India can be broadly classified into three categories: classical, folk, and modern.

Classical theatre refers to the theatre that follows the rules and conventions of Natya Shastra, and is performed in Sanskrit or Prakrit languages. Classical theatre is mainly based on the epics, myths, and legends of Hinduism, Buddhism, and Jainism. Some examples of classical theatre are Koodiyattam, Kutiyattam, Natak, and Prakarana.

Folk theatre refers to the theatre that is rooted in the local traditions, cultures, and languages of various regions and communities of India. Folk theatre is usually performed in open-air spaces, and involves elements of song, dance, music, and improvisation. Folk theatre is often based on the stories of gods, goddesses, heroes, and villains, as well as social and political issues. Some examples of folk theatre are Swang, Yakshagana, Bhand Pather, Tamasha, and Jatra.

Modern theatre refers to the theatre that emerged in the colonial and post-colonial periods, and is influenced by the Western forms and styles of theatre. Modern theatre is performed in various Indian languages, and explores themes such as nationalism, freedom, identity, gender, and social justice. Modern theatre also incorporates elements of realism, naturalism, expressionism, and absurdism. Some examples of modern theatre are Parsi theatre, Indian People's Theatre Association, Theatre of the Absurd, and Theatre of the Roots.
