#### Challenges faced by Women in Society in Ancient India

- Women in ancient India had different roles and rights depending on the time period, the region, the religion, and the caste they belonged to. In general, they faced more challenges and oppression as time went by and patriarchy became stronger.
- Some of the challenges faced by women in ancient India were:
  - Lack of autonomy and mobility: Women were dependent on their male relatives for their livelihood, protection, and decision-making. They had limited access to education, property, inheritance, and legal rights. They were expected to stay inside the home and perform domestic duties, and were restricted from traveling or interacting with strangers.
  - Child marriage and widowhood: Women were often married at a very young age, sometimes even before puberty, to men who were much older than them. They had no say in choosing their husbands or in matters of reproduction. They were subjected to physical, sexual, and emotional abuse by their husbands and in-laws. If their husbands died, they were either forced to commit sati (self-immolation on the funeral pyre of their husbands) or to live a life of misery and isolation as widows. They were considered inauspicious and unworthy of respect or happiness .
  - Social and religious discrimination: Women were considered inferior to men in terms of intellect, morality, and spirituality. They were denied the right to study the Vedas, the sacred scriptures of Hinduism, or to perform rituals and sacrifices. They were also subjected to various forms of exploitation and violence, such as prostitution, devadasi system (dedication of young girls to temples as servants of gods), niyoga system (practice of levirate marriage, where a widow had to cohabit with her husband's brother or relative to produce a son), and dasi system (practice of enslaving women captured in wars or raids)   .
- Despite these challenges, some women in ancient India also showed remarkable courage, wisdom, and leadership in various fields, such as politics, literature, arts, and religion. They contributed to the cultural and social development of the country and inspired generations of women to fight for their rights and dignity. Some examples of such women are Gargi, Maitreyi, Draupadi, Sita, Ahalya Bai Holkar, Mirabai, Rani Lakshmibai, and Savitribai Phule.
