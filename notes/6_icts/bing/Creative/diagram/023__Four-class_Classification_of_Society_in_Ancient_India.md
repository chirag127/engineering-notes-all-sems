The four-class classification of society in ancient India is also known as the varna system. It is based on the division of labor and the functions of different groups of people in the society. The four classes are:

- Brahmins: the priests, scholars, and teachers who perform religious rituals and study the Vedas, the sacred scriptures of Hinduism. They are considered the highest and most pure class in the system.
- Kshatriyas: the warriors, kings, and administrators who protect and govern the society. They are the second highest class and are expected to be brave, generous, and loyal.
- Vaishyas: the farmers, traders, and artisans who produce and distribute goods and services. They are the third highest class and are expected to be honest, industrious, and charitable.
- Shudras: the laborers and servants who perform menial and manual work for the other classes. They are the lowest and most impure class in the system and are expected to be obedient, humble, and respectful.
