Trade and commerce were important aspects of ancient India's economy and society. According to the web search results, trade in ancient India can be divided into three phases: the Early Harappan Phase (3300-2600 BCE), the Mature Harappan Phase (2600-1900 BCE), and the Late Harappan Phase (1900-1300 BCE). Trade was also conducted with foreign countries, such as Mesopotamia, Egypt, Persia, Greece, Rome, and China.

The main commodities that were traded in ancient India were:

- Metals: copper, bronze, lead, tin, iron, gold, silver
- Textiles: cotton, wool, silk
- Spices: pepper, ginger, cardamom, cinnamon, turmeric
- Food: grains, rice, wheat, barley, lentils, fruits, vegetables
- Animals: cattle, sheep, goats, horses, elephants, camels
- Gems: pearls, coral, lapis lazuli, ruby, sapphire, diamond
- Other: ivory, pottery, beads, seals, jewelry, coins, perfumes, medicines, wood, leather, oil, wine
