Seals as Cultural Heritage of India
---
Seals are small objects that have been used for various purposes in different cultures and periods of history. Seals can be made of different materials, such as stone, metal, clay, or wax, and they can have different shapes, such as square, rectangular, circular, or cylindrical. Seals can have images, symbols, or inscriptions carved or engraved on them, which can be used to identify, authenticate, or communicate something.

One of the most ancient and remarkable examples of seals in India is the Indus Valley Civilization, which flourished between 2500 and 1900 BCE in present-day Pakistan and northwestern India. The Indus Valley Civilization was a highly urbanized and sophisticated society, with well-planned cities, efficient drainage and sewage systems, standardized weights and measures, and a complex trade network. The Indus Valley Civilization also produced thousands of seals, mostly made of steatite, a soft stone that can be easily carved and baked to harden. The seals were used for various purposes, such as marking goods, sealing documents, or stamping clay impressions.

The Indus Valley seals typically have a square or rectangular shape, with a round knob or boss on the back, which could be used to hold the seal or to attach a string or cord. The seals have an image of an animal, a plant, or a human figure on one side, and a series of symbols or glyphs on the other side. The animal images include bulls, buffaloes, rhinoceroses, elephants, tigers, and unicorns (one-horned bulls). The human figures include a male deity, possibly Pashupati (lord of animals), sitting on a platform surrounded by animals, and a female deity, possibly a fertility goddess, standing in a pipal tree. The plant images include trees, flowers, and leaves. The symbols or glyphs are arranged in one or more rows, and they are believed to represent a script or a language, although their meaning and pronunciation are still unknown.

The Indus Valley seals are remarkable for their artistic quality, their geometric precision, and their cultural significance. They reflect the religious beliefs, the social organization, and the economic activities of the Indus Valley Civilization. They also show the contacts and exchanges with other regions, such as Mesopotamia and the Persian Gulf, where Indus Valley seals have been found. The Indus Valley seals are part of the rich and diverse cultural heritage of India, and they are valuable sources of information and inspiration for scholars and artists.
