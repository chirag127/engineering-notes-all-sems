# Indian Traditions, Culture and Society

- India is a diverse country with a rich and varied heritage of languages, religions, arts, cuisines, philosophies, and customs.
- India has been influenced by various cultures and civilizations throughout its history, such as the Indus Valley Civilization, the Vedic period, the Mauryan Empire, the Gupta Empire, the Islamic invasions, the Mughal Empire, the British colonial rule, and the modern independence movement.
- India is also home to many indigenous and tribal communities, such as the Adivasis, the Nagas, the Gonds, the Santhals, and the Andamanese, who have their own distinct cultures and traditions.
- Some of the common features of Indian culture and society are:

  - **Arts and Language**: India has a rich and diverse literary and artistic tradition, dating back to the ancient Sanskrit language and literature, which is considered one of the oldest and most influential in the world. India has also produced many renowned poets, writers, philosophers, musicians, painters, sculptors, and architects, such as Kalidasa, Valmiki, Vyasa, Chanakya, Kabir, Mirabai, Tulsidas, Rabindranath Tagore, Raja Ravi Varma, Rukmini Devi Arundale, Ravi Shankar, M.F. Husain, and Amrita Sher-Gil. India has 22 official languages and hundreds of regional and local languages and dialects, reflecting its linguistic diversity and pluralism.
  - **Philosophy and Religion**: India is the birthplace of four major world religions: Hinduism, Buddhism, Jainism, and Sikhism, as well as several other faiths, such as Zoroastrianism, Judaism, Christianity, Islam, and Baha'i. India is also known for its philosophical schools, such as the six orthodox systems of Hindu philosophy, the Buddhist schools of thought, the Jain doctrines, the Sikh principles, and the Sufi mysticism. India has a tradition of religious tolerance and harmony, as well as syncretism and interfaith dialogue, exemplified by the teachings of saints like Guru Nanak, Kabir, Ramanuja, Chaitanya, and Ramakrishna.
  - **Food and Drink**: India has a diverse and delicious cuisine, influenced by its geography, climate, history, and culture. India has a variety of regional and local cuisines, such as the North Indian, South Indian, East Indian, West Indian, and Northeast Indian cuisines, as well as the Mughlai, Awadhi, Hyderabadi, Bengali, Goan, Rajasthani, Gujarati, Punjabi, and Kashmiri cuisines. India is also known for its street food, snacks, sweets, and beverages, such as chaat, samosa, jalebi, lassi, chai, and filter coffee. India has a predominantly vegetarian cuisine, due to the influence of Hinduism, Jainism, and Buddhism, but also has a variety of non-vegetarian dishes, such as chicken, mutton, fish, and seafood, especially in the coastal and Muslim-dominated regions.
  - **Society and Culture**: India has a complex and hierarchical social structure, based on the concepts of caste, class, gender, religion, and ethnicity. India has a system of four varnas or social classes, namely the Brahmins (priests and scholars), the Kshatriyas (warriors and rulers), the Vaishyas (merchants and traders), and the Shudras (servants and laborers), as well as the Dalits (untouchables) and the Adivasis (tribals), who are considered outside the varna system. India also has thousands of jatis or sub-castes, based on occupation, region, and lineage. India has a patriarchal and patrilineal society, where men are considered superior to women, and sons are preferred over daughters. India has a tradition of joint family and arranged marriage, where the elders have a say in the choice of spouse and the upbringing of children.
