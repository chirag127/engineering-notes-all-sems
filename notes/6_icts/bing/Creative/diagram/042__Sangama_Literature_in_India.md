Sangama Literature in India
--------------------------

Sangama literature is the name given to the earliest available Tamil literature, composed between the 3rd century BCE and the 5th century CE. It is also known as the poetry of the noble ones, as it reflects the values and culture of the ancient Tamil people. Sangama literature is divided into two main categories: akam (inner) and puram (outer). Akam poems deal with love, romance, and human emotions, while puram poems deal with heroism, warfare, social life, and ethics. Sangama literature is also classified into two groups based on the period of composition: Patinenmelkanakku (the eighteen major anthologies) and Patinenkilkanakku (the eighteen minor works). Sangama literature is believed to have been produced in three literary academies, or sangams, in Madurai, India. The first sangam is said to have been chaired by the sage Agastya, and its works are lost. The second sangam is said to have been chaired by the poet Tolkappiyar, and its only surviving work is the Tolkappiyam, a book of grammar and rhetoric. The third sangam is said to have been chaired by the poet Nakkirar, and its works include the eight anthologies of akam and puram poems, and the ten idylls of the Pattupattu. The third sangam is also known as the golden age of Tamil literature, as it produced many renowned poets and works. Sangama literature is a valuable source of information about the history, culture, society, and religion of the ancient Tamils. It also showcases the richness and diversity of the Tamil language and literature.
