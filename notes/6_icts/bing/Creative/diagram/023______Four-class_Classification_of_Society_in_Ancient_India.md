#### Four-class Classification of Society in Ancient India

- The four-class classification of society in ancient India is also known as the **varna system** or the **caste system**.
- The word varna means "color" or "quality" and refers to the division of people based on their innate characteristics and duties.
- The varna system is derived from the Hindu scriptures called the **Vedas**, which date back to around 1500 BCE.
- The Vedas described four main social classes: **Brahmins**, **Kshatriyas**, **Vaishyas**, and **Shudras** .
- The Brahmins were the priests and scholars who performed religious rituals and taught the sacred texts . They were considered the highest and purest class in the society.
- The Kshatriyas were the kings, governors, and warriors who protected and ruled the society . They were the second highest class and had the privilege of wearing sacred thread and studying the Vedas.
- The Vaishyas were the cattle herders, agriculturists, artisans, and merchants who produced and traded goods and services . They were the third highest class and also had the right to wear sacred thread and study the Vedas, but not to teach them.
- The Shudras were the laborers and service providers who served the other three classes . They were the lowest class and had no access to the Vedas or the sacred thread.
- The varna system was based on the concept of **dharma**, which means duty, law, or morality. Each class had a specific dharma to follow according to their nature and function in the society.
- The varna system was also influenced by the concept of **karma**, which means action, effect, or fate. According to this belief, the actions of a person in their current life would determine their status and class in their next life.
- The varna system was rigid and hierarchical, meaning that there was no mobility or equality among the classes. The classes were also endogamous, meaning that people could only marry within their own class.
- The varna system was not only a social but also a religious and legal system that governed the rights and duties of the people in ancient India. It was also a source of social identity and pride for the people who belonged to the upper classes.
- The varna system was challenged and criticized by some religious movements and thinkers, such as Buddhism, Jainism, and the Bhakti movement, which advocated for more egalitarian and universal values.
- The varna system also evolved and changed over time, giving rise to new sub-classes and groups, such as the **jatis** or the **castes**. The jatis were based on occupation, region, language, and sect, and numbered in the thousands.
- The varna system still exists in some form in modern India, although it is illegal and unconstitutional to discriminate based on caste. The caste system has been a source of social oppression, violence, and injustice for many people, especially the lower castes and the **Dalits** or the **untouchables**, who were considered outside the varna system and subjected to extreme marginalization and exclusion.
