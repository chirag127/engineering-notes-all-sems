### Evolution of script in India

- A script is a written symbolisation of speech. It consists of a set of characters that represent the sounds of a language.
- India has a long and rich history of writing. It has developed many scripts over time, some of which are still in use today, while others are extinct or historical.
- The earliest evidence of writing in India dates back to the Indus Valley Civilization (c. 2500-1900 BCE), which used a pictographic script that has not been deciphered yet. This script is known as the Indus script or Harappan script.
- The next major phase of writing in India was the Brahmi script, which emerged around the 3rd century BCE and was used to write various languages, including Prakrit, Sanskrit, Pali, and Tamil. The Brahmi script is considered the ancestor of most modern Indian scripts, as well as some scripts of Sri Lanka, Tibet, Southeast Asia, and Central Asia.
- The Brahmi script evolved into different regional variants over time, depending on the linguistic and cultural influences. Some of the major branches of the Brahmi script are:

  - The Kharosthi script, which was used in the northwest of India, especially in Gandhara and Bactria, from the 3rd century BCE to the 4th century CE. It was derived from the Aramaic script and was written from right to left. It was used to write Prakrit and Gandhari languages.
  - The Gupta script, which was used in northern and central India from the 4th to the 6th century CE. It was a refined and elegant form of the Brahmi script and was used to write Sanskrit and Prakrit languages. It was also the basis of the Siddham script, which was used to write Buddhist texts and spread to China and Japan.
  - The Nagari script, which was used in northern India from the 7th century CE onwards. It was a descendant of the Gupta script and was used to write Sanskrit, Hindi, and other languages. It was also the precursor of the Devanagari script, which is the most widely used script in India today.
  - The Southern Brahmi script, which was used in southern India from the 3rd century BCE to the 6th century CE. It was used to write Tamil, Kannada, Telugu, and other languages. It was also the source of the Grantha script, which was used to write Sanskrit and Tamil, and the Vatteluttu script, which was used to write Malayalam and Tamil.
  - The Eastern Brahmi script, which was used in eastern India from the 3rd century BCE to the 13th century CE. It was used to write Magadhi, Pali, Bengali, Assamese, and other languages. It was also the origin of the Bengali-Assamese script, the Oriya script, and the Tibetan script.

- The evolution of the Indian scripts was influenced by various factors, such as the medium of writing, the style of writing, the regional and linguistic variations, the religious and cultural traditions, and the foreign contacts and invasions. Some of the common features of the Indian scripts are:

  - They are alphasyllabic, meaning they consist of consonants and vowels, where each consonant has an inherent vowel that can be modified by diacritical marks.
  - They are written from left to right, except for the Kharosthi script, which was written from right to left.
  - They are usually written in horizontal lines, except for the Tibetan script, which is written in vertical columns.
  - They have a headstroke or a horizontal line above the characters, which connects them and indicates the word boundary.
  - They have a complex system of conjuncts or ligatures, where two or more consonants are combined into a single character, sometimes with a change in shape or sound.
  - They have a rich and diverse set of symbols, such as numerals, punctuation marks, abbreviations, honorifics, and religious signs.
