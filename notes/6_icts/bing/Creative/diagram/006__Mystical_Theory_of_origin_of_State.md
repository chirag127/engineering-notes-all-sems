Mystical theory of origin of state is a theory that claims that the state is a divine creation and that the ruler is a representative of God or a higher power. According to this theory, the state is not a product of human effort or social contract, but a gift of God to maintain order and justice in the world. The ruler derives his authority from God and is accountable to God alone. The subjects have a duty to obey the ruler as a manifestation of God's will. This theory was prevalent in ancient and medieval times, when religion and politics were closely intertwined. Some examples of mystical theory of origin of state are:

- The theory of divine right of kings, which held that the king was appointed by God and had absolute power over his subjects. This theory was supported by some Christian theologians and monarchs in Europe, such as James I of England and Louis XIV of France.
- The theory of caliphate, which held that the caliph was the successor of the prophet Muhammad and the leader of the Muslim community. The caliph had both religious and political authority and was expected to uphold the Islamic law and defend the faith. This theory was followed by some Muslim dynasties, such as the Umayyads and the Abbasids.
- The theory of mandala, which held that the king was the center of a cosmic order and the source of prosperity and harmony. The king had a sacred duty to perform rituals and ceremonies to maintain the balance of the universe and the welfare of his subjects. This theory was followed by some Hindu and Buddhist kingdoms in Southeast Asia, such as the Khmer Empire and the Majapahit Empire.
