The Harappan Technologies in India and Ancient India were the technologies developed by the Indus Valley Civilization, also known as the Harappan Civilization, which flourished from about 3300 BCE to 1300 BCE in the Indus River basin of modern-day Pakistan and India. Some of the important innovations of this civilization include:

- Standardized weights and measures, which facilitated trade and commerce across a vast area.
- Seal carving, which involved engraving images and inscriptions on steatite (soapstone) seals that were used as stamps, amulets, or tokens of identity.
- Metallurgy with copper, bronze, lead, and tin, which enabled the production of tools, weapons, ornaments, and vessels of various shapes and sizes.
- Urban planning and architecture, which involved the construction of well-planned cities with brick buildings, drainage systems, granaries, public baths, and fortifications.
- Writing system, which involved the use of a script of about 400 signs that have not yet been deciphered.

The Citadel was a raised platform on the western side of the city, where the public buildings and the elite residences were located. The Lower Town was the residential and commercial area of the city, where the common people lived and worked. The city had a sophisticated drainage system, with covered channels running along the main streets and smaller drains connecting the houses. The city also had a large public bath, known as the Great Bath, which was a rectangular pool surrounded by corridors and rooms. The city also had a granary, which was a large building with platforms for storing grains. The city also had a number of wells, which provided water for the inhabitants.

The Harappan technologies in India and Ancient India were remarkable for their time and influenced the later civilizations of the region. They reflect the high level of organization, skill, and creativity of the Harappan people.
