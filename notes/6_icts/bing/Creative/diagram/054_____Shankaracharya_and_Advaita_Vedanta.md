### Shankaracharya and Advaita Vedanta

- Shankaracharya, also known as Adi Shankara, was an Indian philosopher and theologian who expounded the doctrine of Advaita Vedanta, the non-dualistic school of Hindu philosophy  .
- Advaita Vedanta is based on the Upanishads, the ancient scriptures that form the final part of the Vedas, the sacred texts of Hinduism. Advaita Vedanta teaches that the ultimate reality is Brahman, the supreme self, which is identical to the individual self (atman) of each person .
- Shankaracharya was born in the 8th century CE in a Brahmin family in Kaladi, a village in present-day Kerala. He renounced the worldly pleasures at a very young age and became a wandering monk. He studied under various teachers, including Govinda Bhagavatpada, a disciple of Gaudapada, the author of the Mandukya Karika, a key text of Advaita Vedanta  .
- Shankaracharya wrote commentaries on the Upanishads, the Brahma Sutras, and the Bhagavad Gita, the three foundational texts of Vedanta. He also composed original works, such as the Vivekachudamani, the Upadesasahasri, and the Crest-Jewel of Discrimination. He defended Advaita Vedanta against the criticisms of other schools of Hindu philosophy, such as Nyaya, Vaisheshika, Samkhya, Yoga, Mimamsa, and Buddhism .
- Shankaracharya established four mathas, or monasteries, in the four corners of India: Sringeri in the south, Dwarka in the west, Puri in the east, and Badrinath in the north. He appointed four of his disciples as the heads of these mathas, and entrusted them with the task of preserving and propagating Advaita Vedanta. He also initiated the Dashanami order of sannyasins, or renunciates, who follow the ten names of Shankara  .
- Shankaracharya is regarded as one of the greatest philosophers and saints of India. He is revered by Advaita Vedantins as the incarnation of Shiva, the destroyer of ignorance. He is also honored by other Hindu traditions as a reformer and a unifier of Hinduism. He is credited with reviving the Vedic culture and the worship of the six deities: Shiva, Vishnu, Shakti, Ganesha, Surya, and Skanda  .
- Shankaracharya's influence on Indian thought and culture is immense. He is considered the founder of the Smarta tradition, which follows the Smriti texts and the Vedanta philosophy. He is also the inspiration for many later philosophers and teachers of Advaita Vedanta, such as Vachaspati Mishra, Padmapada, Sureshvara, Mandana Mishra, and Ramanuja. His works have been translated and interpreted by many scholars and seekers of truth, both in India and abroad .
