Astronomy in India and Ancient India

Astronomy is one of the oldest sciences in India, dating back to the pre-historic times. The earliest references to astronomy are found in the Rig Veda, which are dated 2000 BC.  Astronomy later developed as a discipline of Vedanga, or one of the "auxiliary disciplines" associated with the study of the Vedas, dating 1500 BCE or older.  The oldest known text on astronomy is the Vedanga Jyotisha, dated to 1400–1200 BCE. 

Astronomy in ancient India can be divided into two major classes. From the third-century BCE, India started having contact with Greece and other nations outside the subcontinent. So, the basic structure of Indian astronomy had outside influence although the astronomical concepts and the major parameters were India’s own.  This class of astronomy is known as Siddhantic astronomy, which is based on mathematical calculations and astronomical models. The most famous Siddhantic astronomers are Aryabhata, Brahmagupta, Varahamihira, and Bhaskara.

The other class of astronomy is known as Samhita astronomy, which is based on observational methods and astrological predictions. The most famous Samhita texts are the Surya Siddhanta, the Brihat Samhita, and the Brihat Jataka. Samhita astronomy also deals with the lunar and solar calendars, the nakshatras (lunar mansions), the zodiac signs, and the planetary periods.
