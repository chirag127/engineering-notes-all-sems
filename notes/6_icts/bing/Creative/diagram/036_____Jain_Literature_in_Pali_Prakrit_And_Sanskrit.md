### Jain Literature in Pali, Prakrit and Sanskrit

- Jain literature refers to the literature of the Jain religion, which is one of the oldest and major religions of India.
- Jain literature is a vast and ancient literary tradition, which was initially transmitted orally by the teachers and disciples of Jainism.
- Jain literature can be broadly divided into two categories: canonical and non-canonical.
- Canonical literature consists of the sacred texts that are accepted as authoritative by the Jains. They are also known as Agamas or Siddhantas.
- Non-canonical literature consists of the texts that are not accepted as authoritative by all the Jains, but are still valuable for the study of Jainism. They include commentaries, biographies, histories, poetry, philosophy, grammar, etc.
- Jain canonical literature is mostly written in Prakrit languages, especially Ardhamagadhi, which was the language of Mahavira, the 24th and last Tirthankara (enlightened teacher) of Jainism.
- Prakrit languages are derived from Sanskrit, but are simpler and more colloquial. They were widely spoken in ancient India by the common people.
- Jain canonical literature consists of 45 texts, which are divided into four groups: Angas, Upangas, Prakirnas and Chhedasutras.
- Angas are the 12 primary texts that contain the core teachings of Jainism. They deal with topics such as ethics, cosmology, karma, rebirth, liberation, etc.
- Upangas are the 12 secondary texts that supplement the Angas. They deal with topics such as astronomy, geography, history, legends, etc.
- Prakirnas are the 10 miscellaneous texts that cover various aspects of Jainism. They deal with topics such as logic, mathematics, grammar, rituals, etc.
- Chhedasutras are the 6 texts that prescribe the rules and regulations for the monks and nuns of Jainism. They deal with topics such as conduct, discipline, vows, penance, etc.
- Jain canonical literature was compiled and edited by the councils of Jain monks in different periods of history. The first council was held at Pataliputra (modern Patna) in the 4th century BCE, the second at Mathura in the 2nd century CE, and the third at Valabhi in Gujarat in the 6th century CE.
- Jain non-canonical literature is written in various languages, such as Prakrit, Pali, Sanskrit, Apabhramsha, Hindi, Gujarati, etc.
- Pali is another Prakrit language that was used by the Buddhists as their sacred language. Some Jain texts, such as the Vasudevahindi, were also written in Pali.
- Sanskrit is the classical and literary language of ancient India, which was used by the Brahmins and the elite. Some Jain texts, such as the Tattvarthasutra, were also written in Sanskrit.
- Apabhramsha is a term that refers to the transitional languages that evolved from Prakrit and later gave rise to the modern Indo-Aryan languages. Some Jain texts, such as the Kalpasutra, were also written in Apabhramsha.
- Hindi, Gujarati and other modern languages are the languages that are spoken by the Jains in different regions of India. Some Jain texts, such as the Bhaktamara Stotra, were also written in these languages.
- Jain non-canonical literature covers a wide range of genres and themes, such as poetry, drama, narrative, biography, history, philosophy, logic, grammar, mathematics, medicine, etc.
