### Chemistry in India and Ancient India

- Chemistry in ancient India was called **Rasayana** in Sanskrit, which means **extract** or **essence** of something .
- Chemistry in ancient India developed in various practical fields, such as metallurgy, medicine, alchemy, cosmetics, perfumery, dyeing, fermentation, etc.  .
- Chemistry in ancient India can be divided into five stages, according to Acharya Prafulla Chandra Ray, a renowned Indian chemist and historian of chemistry :
  - The pre-Vedic stage (up to 1500 BCE), including the Harappan period.
  - The Vedic and the Ayurvedic period (up to 700 CE).
  - The transitional period (700 CE to 1100 CE).
  - The Tantric period (700 CE to 1300 CE).
  - The Iatro-Chemical period (1300 CE to 1600 CE).
- Some of the achievements and contributions of chemistry in ancient India are   :
  - The Harappans were skilled in making pottery, faience, mortars, cements, and glazes from various clays, minerals, and oxides.
  - The Rigveda mentions several fermented drinks, such as soma, madhu, and suraa, and methods of fermentation. It also mentions various metals, such as gold, silver, copper, iron, tin, and lead.
  - The Ayurvedic texts, such as Charaka Samhita and Sushruta Samhita, describe various medicinal plants, minerals, metals, and animal products, and their properties, purification, and preparation. They also mention various surgical instruments, such as scalpels, needles, forceps, etc., made of metals.
  - The Arthashastra of Kautilya mentions various chemical techniques for espionage, warfare, and administration, such as making poisons, antidotes, fire-works, ink, paper, etc.
  - The Rasaratnakara of Nagarjuna, a Buddhist monk and alchemist, describes various methods of extraction, purification, and transmutation of metals, minerals, and gems. He also mentions various apparatus, such as crucibles, furnaces, retorts, etc., used for chemical operations.
  - The Rasashastra texts, such as Rasarnava, Rasendra Chintamani, Rasaratna Samuchchaya, etc., deal with various aspects of alchemy, such as mercury, sulfur, salts, metals, elixirs, etc. They also mention various methods of calcination, sublimation, distillation, etc., and various apparatus, such as alembics, stills, condensers, etc., used for alchemical operations.
  - The Tantras, such as Kaulajnananirnaya, Yogini Tantra, etc., deal with various aspects of Tantra, such as mantras, yantras, mandalas, etc. They also mention various substances, such as ashes, powders, oils, perfumes, etc., used for Tantric rituals and practices.
  - The Iatro-Chemical texts, such as Sharngadhara Samhita, Bhavaprakasha, etc., deal with various aspects of medicine, such as diagnosis, prognosis, treatment, etc. They also mention various substances, such as metals, minerals, herbs, animal products, etc., used for medicinal purposes.

- Some of the mnemonics and learning tricks for chemistry in ancient India are:
  - To remember the five stages of chemistry in ancient India, use the acronym **PVTTI** (Pre-Vedic, Vedic, Transitional, Tantric, Iatro-Chemical).
  - To remember the six metals mentioned in the Rigveda, use the acronym **GSCITL** (Gold, Silver, Copper, Iron, Tin, Lead).
  - To remember the three fermented drinks mentioned in the Rigveda, use the acronym **SMS** (Soma, Madhu, Sura).
  - To remember the four apparatus used for alchemical operations, use the acronym **ASDC** (Alembic, Still, Distiller, Condenser).
  - To remember the four substances used for Tantric rituals and practices, use the acronym **APOA** (Ash, Powder, Oil, Perfume).
