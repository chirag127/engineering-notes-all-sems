# Sculptures as Cultural Heritage of India

- Sculpture is one of the oldest and most diverse forms of artistic expression in India, dating back to the Indus Valley Civilization (c. 2500-1500 BCE) and continuing to the present day  .
- Sculpture in India reflects the rich cultural, religious, and historical diversity of the country, as well as the influences of various regions, dynasties, and foreign invaders  .
- Sculpture in India can be classified into three broad categories: stone, metal, and terracotta . Each category has its own distinctive features, techniques, and styles.
- Stone sculpture is the most prevalent and enduring form of sculpture in India, especially in temples, caves, and monuments. Stone sculpture ranges from the intricate carvings of the Khajuraho temples, the colossal statues of the Ellora caves, the monolithic pillars of Ashoka, to the delicate sculptures of the Hoysala and Chola dynasties  .
- Metal sculpture is another important form of sculpture in India, especially in bronze and brass. Metal sculpture showcases the mastery of the Indian artists in casting, molding, and polishing. Metal sculpture includes the iconic Dancing Girl of Mohenjo-daro, the Nataraja (Lord of Dance) of Chola art, the Buddhist and Jain images of Mathura and Gandhara, and the tribal and folk art of various regions  .
- Terracotta sculpture is the oldest and simplest form of sculpture in India, made from clay and baked in fire. Terracotta sculpture is mainly used for making figurines, toys, masks, and votive offerings. Terracotta sculpture reflects the local traditions, customs, and beliefs of the people. Terracotta sculpture can be seen in the Harappan civilization, the Mauryan period, the Bengal school, and the contemporary art of West Bengal and Bihar  .
- Sculpture in India is not only a form of art, but also a form of communication, education, and devotion. Sculpture in India depicts the myths, legends, and stories of the Hindu, Buddhist, Jain, and other religions, as well as the historical events, social issues, and cultural values of the people. Sculpture in India also serves as a medium of worship, meditation, and ritual, as well as a source of inspiration, beauty, and joy   .
- Sculpture in India is a vital part of the cultural heritage of the country, as it represents the creativity, diversity, and continuity of the Indian civilization. Sculpture in India also contributes to the national identity, pride, and unity of the people, as well as the global recognition, appreciation, and preservation of the Indian culture   .
