### Sangama Literature in India

- Sangama literature is the name given to the earliest available Tamil literature, composed between 300 BCE and 500 CE.
- The word 'Sangama' means association, and it refers to the three literary academies that flourished in ancient southern India, especially in Madurai .
- The three Sangamas are known as Muchchangam, and they are:
  - The first Sangama, chaired by the sage Agastya, which produced 4449 poems by 549 poets, of which only a few fragments survive.
  - The second Sangama, chaired by the poet Tolkappiyar, which produced 3700 poems by 59 poets, of which only the Tolkappiyam, a treatise on grammar and poetics, survives.
  - The third Sangama, chaired by the poet Nakkirar, which produced 2381 poems by 473 poets, of which most of the works are extant.
- The Sangama literature is classified into two categories based on the context and interpretation: Aham (inner) and Puram (outer).
  - Aham literature deals with abstract themes such as love, sexual relations, emotions, and human psychology. It is further divided into five subgenres based on the landscape and mood: Kurinji (mountainous region, union of lovers), Mullai (forest region, waiting of the wife), Marutam (agricultural region, infidelity), Neytal (coastal region, separation of lovers), and Palai (desert region, lamentation of the lover).
  - Puram literature deals with concrete themes such as heroism, customs, social life, ethics, philanthropy, and politics. It is further divided into two subgenres based on the nature of the poems: Akam (long poems) and Puram (short poems).
- The Sangama literature is also classified into two groups based on the period of composition: Patinenmelkanakku (the eighteen major works) and Patinenkilkanakku (the eighteen minor works).
  - Patinenmelkanakku consists of eight anthologies (Ettuttokai) and ten idylls (Pattupattu) that belong to the third Sangama period. Some of the famous works in this group are Ainkurunuru, Kuruntokai, Narrinai, Akananuru, Kalittokai, Patirruppattu, Purananuru, and Paripatal .
  - Patinenkilkanakku consists of eighteen didactic works that belong to the post-Sangama period. Some of the famous works in this group are Thirukkural, Palamoli, Naladiyar, Inna Narpathu, Iniyavai Narpathu, and Tirumurugatruppadai.
- The Sangama literature is unique in ancient Indian literature as it is mostly secular and free from religious and mythical influences. It provides a vivid picture of the social, cultural, political, and economic life of the Tamils during the Sangama period. It also showcases the richness and diversity of the Tamil language and its poetic traditions.
