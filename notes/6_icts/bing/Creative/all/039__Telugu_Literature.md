### Telugu Literature

- Telugu literature is the body of works written in the Telugu language, a Dravidian language spoken in an area north of Madras, India, and running inland to Bellary .
- It consists of poems, short stories, novels, plays, and song lyrics, among others.
- It dates back to at least the middle of the first millennium, but the first extant works are from the 11th century when the Mahabharata was first translated to Telugu from Sanskrit by Nannaya.
- It experienced a golden age under the patronage of the Vijayanagara king-poet Krishnadevaraya in the early 16th century.
- It is predominantly religious in subject matter, drawing most of its material from the epics and the Puranas, which are considered to be storehouses of Indian culture.
- Some of the popular forms of Telugu literature are the sataka ("century" of verses), the akhyana or khanda (episodes from the Puranas), the charitra, vijaya, vilasa and abhyudaya (depictions of the fortune of a single hero), and the parinaya, kalyana and vivaha (marriages of heroes) .
- Some of the famous Telugu writers are Nannaya, Tikkana, Errana, Srinatha, Potana, Allasani Peddana, Tenali Ramakrishna, Bammera Potana, Annamacharya, Kshetrayya, Tyagaraja, Gurajada Apparao, Viswanatha Satyanarayana, and Sri Sri.

Mnemonics and learning tricks:

- To remember the names of the three poets who translated the Mahabharata to Telugu, use the acronym NAT: Nannaya, Tikkana, Errana.
- To remember the names of the five forms of Telugu literature that depict the fortune of a single hero, use the acronym CVVAA: Charitra, Vijaya, Vilasa, Abhyudaya, Akhyana.
- To remember the names of the three forms of Telugu literature that depict the marriages of heroes, use the acronym PKV: Parinaya, Kalyana, Vivaha.
