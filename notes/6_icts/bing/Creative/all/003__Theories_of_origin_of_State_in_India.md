#### Theories of origin of State in India

The origin of state in India is a subject of debate among scholars of political science and history. There are different theories that attempt to explain how the state emerged and evolved in ancient India. Some of the prominent theories are:

- **Social Contract Theory**: This theory holds that the state is a result of a contract between the king and his subjects or representatives. The king, thus appointed, was expected to save the state and the subjects from external aggression and establish order and security within the state. This theory is based on the Vedic legend of the war between the gods and the demons, and the election of Soma as the king of the gods. The social contract theory is also supported by some Buddhist texts, which describe the election of a mahasammata (grand elect) by the people to govern them in a republican manner.  

- **Divine Origin Theory**: This theory asserts that the king and the state are of divine origin, and the king is the representative of God on earth. The king, according to this theory, has a divine right to rule and is not accountable to anyone. This theory is based on the Puranic and epic accounts of the origin of kingship from the gods, such as Manu, Ikshvaku, Yayati, etc. The divine origin theory is also supported by some Smriti texts, which prescribe the duties and privileges of the king as a divine agent.  

- **Organic Theory**: This theory maintains that the state is a natural and organic growth of the society, and the king is the head of the social organism. The state, according to this theory, is not a product of any contract or divine intervention, but a result of the evolution of human society from simple to complex forms. The organic theory is based on the Saptanga theory of the state, which identifies seven elements of the state, namely the king, the minister, the territory, the fort, the treasury, the army, and the ally. The organic theory is also supported by some Dharmashastra texts, which define the state as a means of upholding dharma (righteousness) and promoting the welfare of the people.  

These theories of origin of state in India are not mutually exclusive, but rather complementary and overlapping. They reflect the diversity and complexity of the ancient Indian political thought and practice. They also provide insights into the nature, aims, and functions of the state in ancient India.
