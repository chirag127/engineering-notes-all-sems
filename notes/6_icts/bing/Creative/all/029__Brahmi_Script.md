#### Brahmi Script

- Brahmi script is the earliest writing system developed in India after the Indus script, which is still undeciphered. 
- Brahmi script appeared as a fully developed script in the third century BCE and was used for writing Sanskrit, Pali, Prakrit, Tamil and other languages.  
- Brahmi script is an abugida, which means that it uses a system of diacritical marks to associate vowels with consonant symbols. 
- Brahmi script is the ancestor of all modern Indian scripts and several hundred scripts found in Southeast and East Asia, such as Devanagari, Tamil, Sinhala, Thai, Tibetan, etc. These scripts are collectively known as Brahmic scripts.  
- Brahmi script is mentioned in the ancient Indian texts of Hinduism, Jainism and Buddhism, as well as their Chinese translations. For example, the Lalitavistara Sutra lists 64 scripts, with Brahmi script starting the list. 
- Brahmi script was deciphered by James Prinsep in the 1830s, based on the rock-cut edicts of Ashoka in north-central India, dating to 250–232 BCE. These edicts contain moral and political messages of the Mauryan emperor Ashoka.  
- Brahmi script has two main variants: Northern Brahmi and Southern Brahmi. Northern Brahmi gave rise to scripts such as Gupta, Sharada, Nagari, Bengali, etc. Southern Brahmi gave rise to scripts such as Tamil-Brahmi, Pallava, Grantha, Khmer, etc. 
- Brahmi script has 33 basic consonants and 9 basic vowels. The consonants are arranged in five groups of five, according to the place and manner of articulation. The vowels are written as independent letters or as diacritics above, below, before or after the consonants.  
- Brahmi script also has special symbols for numerals, punctuation, ligatures, conjuncts, etc. The numerals are based on a decimal system, with separate symbols for 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 10, 20, 100 and 1000. The punctuation marks include the danda (|) for sentence ending, the double danda (||) for verse ending, and the dot (.) for abbreviation. The ligatures are combinations of two or more consonants, such as ksa, jna, tra, etc. The conjuncts are clusters of consonants without intervening vowels, such as kta, nda, mpa, etc.  
- Brahmi script can be written from left to right or from right to left, depending on the language and the region. The direction of writing can be indicated by a special symbol called the head mark, which looks like a sideways S.  

Some possible mnemonics and learning tricks for Brahmi script are:

- To remember the order of the consonants, use the phrase "Ka Tha Pa Ya Sha" (क थ प य श), which contains the first consonant of each group. 
- To remember the shapes of the consonants, use the following associations: 
  - Ka (क) looks like a key
  - Ga (ग) looks like a gun
  - Cha (च) looks like a chair
  - Ja (ज) looks like a jar
  - Ta (त) looks like a tea cup
  - Da (द) looks like a door
  - Tha (थ) looks like a thumb
  - Dha (ध) looks like a dholak (drum)
  - Na (न) looks like a nose
  - Pa (प) looks like a pot
  - Ba (ब) looks like a ball
  - Ma (म) looks like a mouth
  - Ya (य) looks like a yawn
  - Ra (र) looks like a rake
  - La (ल) looks like a lamp
  - Va (व) looks like a vase
  - Sha (श) looks like a shark
