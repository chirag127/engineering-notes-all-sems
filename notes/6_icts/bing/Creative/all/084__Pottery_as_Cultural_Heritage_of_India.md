### Pottery as Cultural Heritage of India

- Pottery is one of the oldest and most widespread forms of art in India. It reflects the cultural diversity, creativity and history of the Indian subcontinent.
- Pottery in India can be traced back to the Indus Valley Civilization (2500-1800 BCE), where evidence of pottery making has been found in sites like Harappa and Mohenjo-Daro  .
- Pottery in India has evolved over time, influenced by various factors such as geography, climate, religion, trade, invasions and social customs. Different regions and periods have developed their own distinctive styles, techniques and motifs of pottery.
- Some of the major types of pottery in India are:

  - **Terracotta**: This is the most common and basic form of pottery, made from clay that is baked in a kiln or sun-dried. Terracotta pottery is usually unglazed and reddish-brown in color. It is used for making everyday utensils, toys, figurines, sculptures and architectural elements. Terracotta pottery is found in almost every part of India, but some of the famous centers are Bishnupur (West Bengal), Nizamabad (Uttar Pradesh), Khurja (Uttar Pradesh), Pokhran (Rajasthan) and Molela (Rajasthan).
  - **Glazed pottery**: This is a type of pottery that is coated with a layer of glassy material, which gives it a shiny and colorful appearance. Glazed pottery is usually made from white or red clay, and the glaze is applied before or after firing. Glazed pottery is used for decorative purposes, as well as for making tableware, tiles and lamps. Glazed pottery is found in many regions of India, but some of the notable centers are Delhi, Jaipur, Srinagar, Khurja, Rampur and Agra.
  - **Blue pottery**: This is a special type of glazed pottery that is characterized by its blue color, derived from cobalt oxide. Blue pottery is made from quartz and not clay, and it is fired only once. Blue pottery is decorated with floral, geometric and animal motifs, inspired by Persian and Mughal art. Blue pottery is a unique craft of Jaipur, Rajasthan, where it was introduced by the Mongol artisans in the 14th century.
  - **Black pottery**: This is a type of pottery that is made from black clay, which is obtained from river beds or ponds. Black pottery is fired in a reduction atmosphere, which prevents oxygen from reaching the clay and gives it a metallic black color. Black pottery is polished with oil or vegetable juices to enhance its luster. Black pottery is used for making water pots, bowls, vases and lamps. Black pottery is found in several regions of India, but some of the prominent centers are Nizamabad (Uttar Pradesh), Manipur, Longpi (Manipur) and Azamgarh (Uttar Pradesh).
  - **Painted pottery**: This is a type of pottery that is painted with various colors and designs, either before or after firing. Painted pottery is usually made from red or buff clay, and the colors are derived from natural or synthetic sources. Painted pottery is used for both functional and decorative purposes, and it often depicts religious, mythological, floral and animal themes. Painted pottery is found in many parts of India, but some of the famous centers are Kutch (Gujarat), Mithila (Bihar), Khurja (Uttar Pradesh), Srikalahasti (Andhra Pradesh) and Jaipur (Rajasthan).

- Pottery in India is not only a craft, but also a cultural expression. It reflects the beliefs, values, traditions and aesthetics of the people who make and use it. Pottery in India is also a source of livelihood for many artisans, who have inherited the skills and knowledge from their ancestors. Pottery in India is a living heritage, that continues to evolve and adapt to the changing times and tastes.
