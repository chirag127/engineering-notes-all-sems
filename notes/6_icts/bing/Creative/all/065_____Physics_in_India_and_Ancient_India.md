# Physics in India and Ancient India

Physics is the branch of science that deals with the nature and properties of matter and energy. Physics in India has a long and rich history, dating back to the ancient times. Here are some points to summarize the main achievements and contributions of physics in India and ancient India:

- Indian philosophers and sages developed various schools of thought that explored the concepts of elements, atoms, energy, motion, gravity, light, sound, and cosmology. Some of the prominent schools were Nyaya, Vaisheshika, Samkhya, Yoga, Mimamsa, Vedanta, and Buddhism.  
- The concept of atomism was first proposed by Maharishi Kanada, the founder of the Vaisheshika school, around the 6th or 2nd century BCE. He postulated that all matter is composed of indivisible and eternal atoms (parmanu) that combine in different ways to form complex substances. He also suggested that atoms have different shapes, sizes, and motions, and that they are affected by a force called adrishta, which is similar to gravity.  
- The concept of energy and its equivalence to mass was also explored by the ancient Indian thinkers. They believed that energy (shakti) is the inherent property of matter, and that it can manifest in various forms, such as heat, light, sound, and electricity. They also recognized that energy can be transformed from one form to another, and that it can neither be created nor destroyed. They also speculated that light consists of discrete particles (anu) that have both wave and particle nature, a concept that is now known as wave-particle duality. 
- The concept of motion and its laws was also developed by the ancient Indian physicists. They distinguished between two types of motion: natural and forced. Natural motion is the inherent tendency of an object to move in a certain direction, such as a stone falling to the ground or a fire rising upwards. Forced motion is the result of an external agent or cause, such as a push or a pull. They also formulated the concept of inertia, which states that an object at rest or in uniform motion will remain so unless acted upon by an external force. They also studied the effects of friction, air resistance, and gravity on motion. 
- The concept of sound and its properties was also investigated by the ancient Indian scientists. They understood that sound is a form of vibration that travels through a medium, such as air, water, or metal. They also classified sound into two types: audible and inaudible. Audible sound is the sound that can be heard by the human ear, and it has a frequency range of 20 to 20,000 hertz. Inaudible sound is the sound that is either too low or too high for the human ear to perceive, such as infrasound and ultrasound. They also studied the phenomena of reflection, refraction, interference, and resonance of sound waves. 
- The concept of light and its properties was also explored by the ancient Indian scholars. They recognized that light is a form of electromagnetic radiation that travels in straight lines and has a finite speed. They also studied the phenomena of reflection, refraction, dispersion, diffraction, polarization, and interference of light waves. They also developed various instruments and devices to measure and manipulate light, such as mirrors, lenses, prisms, sundials, and pinhole cameras. 
- The concept of cosmology and astronomy was also advanced by the ancient Indian astronomers. They observed and recorded the movements and positions of the sun, moon, planets, stars, and constellations. They also calculated the size and distance of the earth, moon, and sun, and the duration of the day, month, year, and various celestial cycles. They also developed various methods and systems to predict and explain the phenomena of eclipses, comets, meteors, and tides. They also devised various calendars and almanacs to keep track of time and seasons. 

These are some of the highlights of the physics in India and ancient India. The ancient Indian physicists made remarkable contributions to the development of science and technology, and their legacy is still relevant and inspiring today.
