### Pyrotechnics in India and Ancient India

- Pyrotechnics is the science and art of creating firework displays and other effects using explosives, chemicals, and fire.
- Pyrotechnics in India has a long history that dates back to the medieval period, when gunpowder was introduced from China and used for both warfare and entertainment.
- One of the earliest records of pyrotechnic shows in India is by Abdur Razzaq, the ambassador of the Timurid Sultan Shahrukh to the court of the Vijayanagar king Devaraya II in 1443. He described the use of fireworks during the Mahanavami festival, a celebration of the Hindu god Vishnu.
- Another Italian traveler, Ludovico di Varthema, who visited India around the same time, also mentioned the use of fireworks by the Vijayanagar empire, especially to scare away elephants.
- Pyrotechnics was also a part of the royal entertainment in many other medieval Indian kingdoms, such as the Gajapati dynasty of Orissa, the Mughal empire, and the Maratha confederacy. They used fireworks for festivals, events, and special occasions like weddings, coronations, and victories   .
- Some of the sources that describe the pyrotechnic formulas and mixtures used in India are the Kautukachintamani, a Sanskrit volume by Gajapati Prataparudradeva (1497-1539), a reputed patron of arts and sciences ; the Ain-i-Akbari, a Persian document by Abul Fazl (1551-1602), a historian and minister of Akbar; and the Nighantu Ratnakara, a Marathi treatise by Nana Phadnavis (1742-1800), a statesman and diplomat of the Maratha empire.
- Pyrotechnics in India became more popular and widespread after the British colonial rule, when fireworks were imported from Europe and China, and also manufactured locally by Indian entrepreneurs. Fireworks became more affordable and accessible to the common people, and were used for various religious and cultural festivals, such as Diwali, Holi, Eid, Christmas, and New Year .
- Pyrotechnics in India today is a major industry that employs thousands of workers and produces a variety of fireworks, such as sparklers, rockets, flower pots, chakras, and bombs. However, pyrotechnics also poses several challenges, such as environmental pollution, health hazards, safety risks, and legal issues. There is a need for more regulation, awareness, and innovation to ensure that pyrotechnics in India can be enjoyed responsibly and sustainably .
