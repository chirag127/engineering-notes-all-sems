### Dance as Cultural Heritage of India

- Dance is an ancient and celebrated cultural tradition in India that reflects the diversity and richness of its history, culture, and religion.
- Dance in India can be broadly classified into two categories: classical and folk. Classical dances are based on ancient texts and traditions, while folk dances are rooted in local customs and practices.
- Classical dances of India are usually performed in temples, courts, or theatres, and require rigorous training and discipline. They are often accompanied by classical music and instruments, and use elaborate costumes and makeup. Folk dances of India are performed in villages, festivals, or weddings, and are more spontaneous and expressive. They are often accompanied by folk music and instruments, and use simple costumes and props.
- There are eight recognized classical dance forms of India: Bharatanatyam, Kathak, Kathakali, Kuchipudi, Manipuri, Mohiniyattam, Odissi, and Sattriya. Each dance form has its own style, technique, and origin. Some of the common elements of classical dances are the use of mudras (hand gestures), abhinaya (facial expressions), and nritya (rhythmic movements).
- Bharatanatyam is one of the oldest and most popular classical dance forms of India, originating from Tamil Nadu. It is based on the Natyashastra, an ancient treatise on theatre written by Bharata Muni. It is a dance of devotion, depicting Hindu religious stories and themes. It is characterized by bent legs, rhythmic footwork, and graceful hand gestures. A mnemonic to remember the name of this dance is to think of the syllables as an acronym: Bha for Bhava (emotion), Ra for Raga (melody), Ta for Tala (rhythm), and Natyam for dance.
- Kathak is a classical dance form of North India, originating from Uttar Pradesh. It is derived from the word Katha, meaning story. It is a dance of love, portraying the stories of Radha and Krishna, as well as other Hindu epics and legends. It is characterized by fast footwork, spins, and stylized gestures. A mnemonic to remember the name of this dance is to think of it as a short form of Kathakar, meaning storyteller.
- Kathakali is a classical dance form of Kerala, originating from the 17th century. It is a dance drama, presenting the stories of the Ramayana, the Mahabharata, and other Hindu texts. It is characterized by heavy makeup, colorful costumes, and elaborate headgear. The dancers use their eyes, eyebrows, and facial muscles to convey emotions and expressions. A mnemonic to remember the name of this dance is to think of it as a combination of Katha (story) and Kali (play).
- Kuchipudi is a classical dance form of Andhra Pradesh, originating from the village of Kuchipudi. It is a dance drama, depicting the stories of Hindu gods and goddesses. It is characterized by fluid movements, leaps, and acrobatic skills. The dancers also speak dialogues and sing songs during the performance. A mnemonic to remember the name of this dance is to think of it as a rhyme with the name of the village where it originated.
- Manipuri is a classical dance form of Manipur, originating from the 15th century. It is a dance of joy, celebrating the love of Radha and Krishna, as well as the seasons and festivals of Manipur. It is characterized by smooth and graceful movements, circular patterns, and delicate hand gestures. The dancers wear long skirts, veils, and jewelry, and often use props such as drums, cymbals, and flowers. A mnemonic to remember the name of this dance is to think of it as a suffix of the name of the state where it originated.
- Mohiniyattam is a classical dance form of Kerala, originating from the 16th century. It is a dance of beauty, portraying the feminine aspects of Hindu gods and goddesses, especially Mohini, the female avatar of Vishnu. It is characterized by gentle and lyrical movements, swaying hips, and expressive eyes. The dancers wear white or cream-colored sarees, gold ornaments, and jasmine flowers in their hair. A mnemonic to remember the name of this dance is to think of it as a combination of Mohini (the enchantress) and Attam (dance).
