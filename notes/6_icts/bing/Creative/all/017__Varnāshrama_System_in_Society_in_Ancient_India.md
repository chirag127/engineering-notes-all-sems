#### Varnāshrama System in Society in Ancient India

- The Varnāshrama system was a social and ethical framework that divided the Hindu society into four classes (varnas) and four stages of life (ashramas).
- The four varnas were: Brahmins (priests and teachers), Kshatriyas (warriors and rulers), Vaishyas (traders and farmers), and Shudras (servants and laborers).
- The four ashramas were: Brahmacharya (student), Grihastha (householder), Vanaprastha (retired), and Sannyasa (renunciant).
- The Varnāshrama system was based on the principle of guna (quality) and karma (action) of each individual, not on birth or lineage .
- The Varnāshrama system aimed to maintain the order and harmony of the society, as well as the spiritual growth and fulfillment of each individual .
- The Varnāshrama system was first mentioned in the Rig Veda, the oldest of the four Vedas, in the Purusha Suktam hymn, which describes the cosmic sacrifice of the primordial being Purusha, from whose body the four varnas emerged .
- The Varnāshrama system was further elaborated and codified in the later texts, such as the Manu Smriti and the Dharma Shastras, which prescribed the specific duties, rights, and obligations of each varna and ashrama .
- The Varnāshrama system was not rigid or static, but allowed for some flexibility and mobility, depending on the individual's aptitude, interest, and conduct .
- The Varnāshrama system was not a system of oppression or discrimination, but a system of mutual cooperation and interdependence, where each varna and ashrama contributed to the welfare of the whole society .
- The Varnāshrama system was not a system of hierarchy or superiority, but a system of diversity and complementarity, where each varna and ashrama had its own role and value in the cosmic order .

| Varna/Ashrama | Quality | Duty |
|---------------|---------|------|
| Brahmin | Sattva (purity, wisdom, peace) | Study and teach the Vedas, perform and officiate sacrifices, give and receive alms |

| Grihastha | Marriage, family, society
