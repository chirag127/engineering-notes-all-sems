### Current developments in Arts and Cultural in India

- India is a diverse country with a rich cultural heritage that manifests itself in various forms of art, music, dance, literature, architecture, etc. India has one of the world’s largest collections of intangible cultural heritage (ICH) that are recognized by UNESCO.
- India is also witnessing a surge of contemporary art and culture that reflects the changing socio-economic and political realities of the country. India has a vibrant art scene with many artists, galleries, museums, festivals, and biennales that showcase the creativity and innovation of Indian artists.
- Some of the current developments in arts and cultural in India are:

  - India has nominated **Hoysala Temples** in Karnataka for inscription in the **World Heritage List** of UNESCO. The Hoysala Temples are a group of 50 temples built between the 11th and 14th centuries by the Hoysala dynasty. They are known for their intricate carvings, sculptures, and architectural styles that blend Hindu, Jain, and Buddhist influences.
  - India has also got back a **Goat-Headed Yogini Statue** that was stolen from a temple in Uttar Pradesh in 1993 and smuggled to France. The statue is a rare example of the **Tantric Yogini cult** that flourished in India between the 8th and 13th centuries. The statue depicts a female deity with a goat head and eight arms, holding various weapons and attributes.
  - India has launched a project called **‘Devayatanam’** to document and showcase the diversity and evolution of **Indian temple architecture**. The project aims to create a digital repository of 100 temples across India, covering various regions, periods, styles, and faiths. The project will also produce documentaries, books, exhibitions, and educational materials on Indian temple architecture.
  - India has celebrated the **75th anniversary** of its independence with a series of events and initiatives under the theme of **‘Azadi Ka Amrit Mahotsav’**. The celebrations include cultural programs, exhibitions, seminars, webinars, competitions, quizzes, etc. that highlight the achievements, contributions, and aspirations of India and its people.
  - India has also witnessed the emergence of various **contemporary artists** who are shaping the Indian art scene with their innovative and experimental works. Some of the notable artists are:

    - **Nalini Malani**, who is known for her multimedia installations, paintings, and video art that explore themes of feminism, violence, migration, and mythology.
    - **Subodh Gupta**, who is known for his sculptures and installations that use everyday objects such as utensils, bicycles, luggage, etc. to create symbolic representations of Indian culture, identity, and globalization.
    - **Bharti Kher**, who is known for her sculptures, paintings, and collages that use materials such as bindis, saris, animal skins, etc. to explore issues of gender, sexuality, and hybridity.
    - **Jitish Kallat**, who is known for his paintings, drawings, sculptures, and installations that use motifs such as bones, teeth, maps, etc. to comment on the history, politics, and ecology of India.
    - **Shilpa Gupta**, who is known for her interactive and participatory installations, videos, and sound works that challenge the notions of borders, boundaries, and identity.

- India has also seen the growth of various **cultural institutions** and **platforms** that promote and support the arts and culture in the country. Some of them are:

  - The **Kiran Nadar Museum of Art**, which is India's first private museum of modern and contemporary art. It has a collection of over 6,000 artworks by Indian and international artists. It also organizes exhibitions, workshops, lectures, and outreach programs.
