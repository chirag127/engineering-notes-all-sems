### Sculptures as Cultural Heritage of India

- Sculptures are one of the oldest and most enduring forms of artistic expression in India, dating back to the Indus Valley civilization (c.2500-1800 BCE) .
- Sculptures in India reflect the diversity and richness of the cultural, religious, and historical traditions of the subcontinent, as well as the influences of various foreign invaders and traders  .
- Sculptures in India can be classified into three broad categories: stone, metal, and terracotta. Stone sculptures are the most abundant and widespread, especially in religious architecture and monuments. Metal sculptures, mostly of bronze, are known for their exquisite craftsmanship and intricate details. Terracotta sculptures are mostly small and portable, depicting human and animal figures .
- The main themes of Indian sculptures are the representation of the divine, the depiction of the natural world, and the expression of human emotions and ideals. The divine figures include the Hindu gods and goddesses, the Buddha and the Jinas, and the various spirits and deities of the local and tribal traditions. The natural world includes animals, plants, and cosmic elements, often symbolizing aspects of the divine or the human. The human emotions and ideals include love, beauty, heroism, devotion, and enlightenment   .
- The main styles and traditions of Indian sculptures are the Mauryan, the Gandhara, the Mathura, the Gupta, the Pallava, the Chola, the Hoysala, the Khajuraho, and the Mughal. Each of these styles and traditions has its own distinctive features, influences, and regional variations, but they also share some common elements, such as the use of symbolism, iconography, and proportion  .
- Some of the most famous and iconic examples of Indian sculptures are the Pillars of Ashoka, the Sanchi Stupa, the Ajanta and Ellora Caves, the Elephanta Caves, the Brihadeshwara Temple, the Konark Sun Temple, the Khajuraho Temples, and the Taj Mahal  .
- Indian sculptures are not only a source of aesthetic pleasure, but also a means of education, communication, and inspiration. They convey the teachings and stories of the various religions and philosophies, the values and ideals of the different cultures and societies, and the aspirations and achievements of the human spirit   .
