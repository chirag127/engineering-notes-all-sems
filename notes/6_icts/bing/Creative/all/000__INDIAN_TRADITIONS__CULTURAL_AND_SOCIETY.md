# INDIAN TRADITIONS, CULTURE AND SOCIETY

- India is one of the oldest and most diverse civilizations in the world, with a history dating back to 4500 years ago.
- India is home to more than 1.3 billion people, belonging to various ethnic, linguistic, religious and regional groups.
- India has a rich and varied cultural heritage, influenced by its ancient philosophy, art, literature, architecture, music, dance, cuisine and customs.
- Some of the major aspects of Indian culture and traditions are:

  - **Arts and Language**: India has 22 official languages and hundreds of regional dialects, reflecting its linguistic diversity. The most widely spoken languages are Hindi, Bengali, Telugu, Marathi, Tamil and Urdu. India also has a classical language, Sanskrit, which is one of the oldest Indo-European languages and the source of many scientific, religious and literary terms. India has a vibrant and diverse artistic tradition, ranging from folk and tribal arts to classical and modern forms. Some of the famous forms of Indian art include painting, sculpture, pottery, textiles, jewelry, metalwork, woodwork, architecture, dance, music, theater and cinema.

  - **Philosophy and Religion**: India is the birthplace of four major world religions: Hinduism, Buddhism, Jainism and Sikhism. Hinduism is the oldest and the most widely practiced religion in India, with about 80% of the population identifying as Hindus. Hinduism is a diverse and complex faith, with various sects, schools, gods, goddesses, rituals and festivals. Hinduism is based on the concepts of karma (action and its consequences), dharma (duty and morality), samsara (cycle of rebirth) and moksha (liberation from samsara). Buddhism and Jainism are offshoots of Hinduism, which emerged in the 6th century BCE as reform movements. They share some common beliefs with Hinduism, such as karma, dharma and samsara, but reject the authority of the Vedas (the sacred scriptures of Hinduism) and the caste system (a hierarchical social order based on birth). Sikhism is a monotheistic religion that originated in the 15th century CE in the Punjab region. It is based on the teachings of Guru Nanak and his successors, who emphasized the equality of all humans, the importance of service, the rejection of idolatry and the devotion to one God, known as Waheguru. India is also home to other religions, such as Islam, Christianity, Zoroastrianism, Judaism and Baha'i, which have contributed to the diversity and tolerance of Indian society.

  - **Food and Drink**: The cuisine of India varies considerably across its various regions and ethnic communities. It is influenced by the local climate, geography, history, religion and culture. Some of the common features of Indian cuisine are the use of spices, herbs, grains, legumes, vegetables, fruits, dairy products and meat (except for Hindus, Jains and some Buddhists, who are mostly vegetarian). Some of the popular dishes of Indian cuisine are dal (lentil soup), roti (flatbread), rice, curry (spicy gravy), biryani (rice with meat or vegetables), samosa (fried pastry with savory filling), dosa (rice and lentil pancake), idli (steamed rice cake), chaat (snack), lassi (yogurt drink), chai (tea with milk and spices) and sweets such as jalebi (deep-fried flour spiral dipped in sugar syrup), ladoo (ball-shaped sweet) and halwa (pudding).

    - The Indus Valley Civilization (2500-1500 BCE), which was one of the earliest urban civilizations in the world. It is known for its sophisticated urban planning, drainage system, granaries, baths, seals, pottery and sculptures.
