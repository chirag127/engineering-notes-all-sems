## Module 4-Science, Management and Indian Knowledge System

This module covers the following topics:

- Astronomy in India: The ancient Indian astronomers made significant contributions to the field of astronomy, such as the concept of zero, the heliocentric model, the calculation of the solar and lunar eclipses, the estimation of the size and distance of the moon and the sun, the discovery of the precession of the equinoxes, the division of the sky into 27 nakshatras or constellations, the development of the sidereal and tropical zodiacs, the invention of various astronomical instruments, and the formulation of the Vedanga Jyotisha, the oldest known text on astronomy in India .
- Chemistry in India: The ancient Indian chemists were adept at various chemical processes, such as distillation, calcination, sublimation, extraction, purification, metallurgy, dyeing, tanning, fermentation, and making perfumes, cosmetics, medicines, and explosives. They also discovered many chemical substances, such as zinc, mercury, sulfur, arsenic, alkali, and acids. They developed the concepts of atomism, the five elements, and the three qualities of matter. They wrote several treatises on chemistry, such as the Rasaratnakara, the Rasendra Sara Sangraha, and the Rasarnava .
- Mathematics in India: The ancient Indian mathematicians made remarkable advances in the field of mathematics, such as the decimal system, the place value system, the concept of infinity, the zero, the negative numbers, the fractions, the square roots, the cube roots, the algebra, the geometry, the trigonometry, the calculus, the combinatorics, the binomial theorem, the Fibonacci sequence, and the Pythagorean theorem. They also developed various methods of calculation, such as the sutras, the ganita, the shulba, the chhanda, the jaina, and the bakhshali. They wrote several works on mathematics, such as the Sulba Sutras, the Aryabhatiya, the Brahmasphutasiddhanta, the Lilavati, the Siddhanta Siromani, and the Ganita Sara Sangraha .
- Physics in India: The ancient Indian physicists explored various aspects of physics, such as the nature of matter, energy, motion, force, gravity, magnetism, electricity, light, sound, heat, and thermodynamics. They also developed various theories of physics, such as the Vaisheshika, the Nyaya, the Sankhya, the Yoga, the Mimamsa, the Vedanta, the Samkhya Karika, and the Vaisesika Sutra. They also invented various devices and machines, such as the yantra, the danda, the chakra, the gola, the dhanur, the shalaka, and the sutratika .
- Agriculture in India: The ancient Indian agriculturists were skilled at various agricultural practices, such as irrigation, crop rotation, soil conservation, fertilization, pest control, animal husbandry, and horticulture. They also cultivated various crops, such as rice, wheat, barley, millet, pulses, oilseeds, cotton, sugarcane, indigo, spices, fruits, and vegetables. They also developed various systems of agriculture, such as the Vedic, the Buddhist, the Jain, the Mauryan, the Gupta, and the Chola. They also wrote several manuals on agriculture, such as the Krishi Parasara, the Kashyapiya Krishi Sukti, the Vrikshayurveda, and the Surapala's Vrikshayurveda .
- Medicine in India: The ancient Indian physicians were proficient at various medical sciences, such as anatomy, physiology, pathology, pharmacology, surgery, dentistry, ophthalmology, obstetrics, gynecology, pediatrics, geriatrics, and psychiatry. They also developed various systems of medicine, such as the Ayurveda, the Siddha, the Unani, the Yoga, the Naturopathy, and the Homeopathy. They also wrote several texts on medicine, such as the Charaka Samhita, the Sushruta Samhita, the Ashtanga Hridaya, the Sarangadhara Samhita, and the Bhavaprakasha .
