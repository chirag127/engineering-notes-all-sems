# Mathematics in India and Ancient India

Mathematics is the science of numbers, shapes, patterns and logic. It has been developed and refined by various civilizations over thousands of years. In this article, we will focus on the history and achievements of mathematics in India and ancient India.

## Ancient India (1200 BCE - 400 CE)

- Ancient India refers to the period from the Indus Valley Civilization (around 2500 BCE) to the beginning of the classical period (around 400 CE).
- The Indus Valley Civilization was one of the earliest urban civilizations in the world. It had a sophisticated system of weights and measures, a decimal system of numeration, and a script that has not been deciphered yet.
- The earliest evidence of mathematical activity in India comes from the Sulba Sutras, which are appendices to the Vedas, the sacred texts of Hinduism. The Sulba Sutras deal with the construction of altars and geometric shapes using ropes and simple rules. They contain approximations of the square root of 2 and the value of pi, as well as the Pythagorean theorem.
- The Vedanga Jyotisha is another ancient text that deals with astronomy and mathematics. It contains rules for calculating the positions of the sun, moon and planets, as well as the length of the day, the seasons and the lunar and solar eclipses. It also proclaims mathematics as the head of all sciences.
- The earliest Indian mathematician whose name is known is Pingala, who lived around the 3rd or 2nd century BCE. He wrote the Chandaḥśāstra, a treatise on prosody, the study of poetic meters. He introduced the concept of binary numbers and the use of zero as a placeholder.
- Another important mathematician of this period was Panini, who lived around the 5th or 4th century BCE. He wrote the Ashtadhyayi, a comprehensive grammar of Sanskrit, the classical language of India. He used a form of algebraic notation to describe the rules of grammar and syntax.

## Classical India (400 CE - 1200 CE)

- Classical India refers to the period from the Gupta Empire (around 320 CE) to the Islamic invasions (around 1200 CE).
- The Gupta Empire is considered the golden age of Indian culture, art, literature and science. It was during this period that the decimal system of numeration, the use of zero as a number, and the symbols for the nine digits (1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9) and zero (0) were developed and popularized in India.
- The most influential mathematician of this period was Aryabhata, who lived around 476 CE. He wrote the Aryabhatiya, a concise treatise on astronomy and mathematics. He gave the value of pi as 3.1416, calculated the circumference of the earth, the length of the solar year, and the lunar and solar eclipses. He also introduced the concepts of sine, cosine and tangent, and solved quadratic equations and indeterminate equations.
- Another prominent mathematician of this period was Brahmagupta, who lived around 598 CE. He wrote the Brahmasphutasiddhanta, a comprehensive work on astronomy and mathematics. He gave the rules for arithmetic operations on zero, negative numbers, fractions and surds. He also solved linear and quadratic equations, and gave the formula for the area of a cyclic quadrilateral.
- A third notable mathematician of this period was Bhaskara II, who lived around 1114 CE. He wrote the Siddhanta Siromani, a four-part work on astronomy and mathematics. He gave the value of pi as 3.14159265359, calculated the length of the sidereal year, and the diameter of the earth. He also solved cubic equations, and gave the proof of the Pythagorean theorem and the formula for the sum of a geometric series.

## Medieval India (1200 CE - 1800 CE)

- Medieval India refers to the period from the Islamic invasions (around 1200 CE) to the beginning of the colonization by Great Britain (around 1800 CE).
- The Islamic invasions brought new cultural and scientific influences to India, as well as political and social turmoil. Many Indian mathematicians and astronomers migrated to other regions, such as Persia, Arabia and Central Asia, where they contributed to the development of Islamic mathematics and astronomy.
