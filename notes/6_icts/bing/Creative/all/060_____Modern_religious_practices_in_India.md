# Modern religious practices in India

- India is a diverse and pluralistic country, where different religions coexist and influence each other. According to the 2011 census, the major religions practised in India are Hinduism (79.8%), Islam (14.2%), Christianity (2.3%), Sikhism (1.7%), Buddhism (0.7%) and Jainism (0.4%). 
- Religion plays a significant role in the lives of most Indians, as it shapes their personal, social and cultural identities. Religion also affects the political, economic and legal aspects of the country, as different religious groups have different interests, demands and rights.
- Religious practices in India vary widely across regions, sects, denominations and communities. However, some common elements can be identified, such as the following:
  - Indians generally believe in the existence of one supreme reality, which can be called by different names and manifested in different forms. They also believe in the concepts of karma, reincarnation, liberation and the cycle of birth and death.
  - Indians regularly visit their houses of worship, such as temples, mosques, churches, gurdwaras, monasteries and shrines. They also perform rituals, prayers, offerings, fasting, pilgrimage and festivals to express their devotion and seek blessings from their deities.
  - Indians value the importance of giving to charitable causes, such as helping the poor, the needy, the sick, the animals and the environment. They also practice various forms of service, such as volunteering, donating, teaching, feeding and caring for others.
  - Indians respect the diversity and tolerance of their religious traditions, and often engage in practices or hold beliefs that are more closely associated with other religions than with their own. For example, many Hindus, Muslims, Christians and Sikhs celebrate festivals such as Diwali, Eid, Christmas and Guru Nanak Jayanti. Many Buddhists and Jains follow vegetarianism, which is a common practice among Hindus. Many Sikhs and Muslims wear turbans, which are also worn by some Hindu sects.
