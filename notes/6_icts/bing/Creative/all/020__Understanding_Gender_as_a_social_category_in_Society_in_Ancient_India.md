#### Understanding Gender as a social category in Society in Ancient India

- Gender as a social category refers to the social construction of roles, expectations, and identities of men and women in a given society.
- Gender roles and relations are influenced by various factors, such as culture, religion, history, economy, politics, and environment.
- In ancient India, the status and position of women varied across different periods, regions, and communities.
- Some of the main aspects of gender as a social category in ancient India are:

  - **Indus Valley Civilization**: This was one of the earliest urban civilizations in the world, dating back to 2500-1500 BCE. Women in this civilization enjoyed a respectable and dignified position in society. They participated in economic, religious, and artistic activities. They wore elaborate jewelry and ornaments, and some of them may have been priestesses or rulers. There is evidence of female figurines, seals, and terracotta art depicting women in various roles.
  - **Early Vedic Period**: This was the period when the earliest Vedic texts, such as the Rig Veda, were composed, dating back to 1500-1000 BCE. Women in this period had access to education and learning. They could compose hymns, attend assemblies, and perform rituals. They had the freedom to choose their husbands and remarry if widowed. There were many female sages, such as Gargi, Maitreyi, and Lopamudra, who were revered for their wisdom and knowledge .
  - **Later Vedic Period**: This was the period when the later Vedic texts, such as the Sama Veda, Yajur Veda, and Atharva Veda, were composed, dating back to 1000-500 BCE. Women in this period witnessed a decline in their status and rights. They lost their political and religious participation. They were confined to the domestic sphere and subjected to the authority of their fathers, husbands, and sons. They were denied education and learning. They faced social evils, such as child marriage, sati, and dowry. They were considered inferior and dependent on men .
  - **Epic Period**: This was the period when the two great epics, the Ramayana and the Mahabharata, were composed, dating back to 500 BCE-500 CE. Women in this period had a mixed position in society. On one hand, they were idealized and glorified as virtuous and loyal wives, mothers, and daughters, such as Sita, Draupadi, and Kunti. On the other hand, they were exploited and oppressed as objects of desire, violence, and revenge, such as Surpanakha, Shurpanakha, and Amba. They had limited autonomy and agency in their lives .
  - **Period of Dharmashastras and Puranas**: This was the period when the texts on law, ethics, and mythology, such as the Manusmriti and the Bhagavata Purana, were composed, dating back to 500-1000 CE. Women in this period were subjected to the strict and rigid codes of conduct prescribed by the Dharmashastras. They were denied education, property, and inheritance. They were expected to obey and serve their husbands, sons, and in-laws. They were regarded as impure, weak, and sinful. They were discriminated and marginalized on the basis of caste, class, and age .
  - **Period of Buddhism and Jainism**: This was the period when the two heterodox religions, Buddhism and Jainism, emerged and flourished, dating back to 600-200 BCE. Women in this period were accorded respect and dignity by the teachings of Buddha and Mahavira. They were given the opportunity to renounce the worldly life and join the monastic orders. They could study and practice the doctrines of these religions. They could attain spiritual liberation and enlightenment. They were considered equal and capable of wisdom and compassion .
