# The Mahabharata

The Mahabharata is one of the two major Sanskrit epics of India, along with the Ramayana. It is a long and complex narrative that covers the history, culture, religion, and philosophy of ancient India. It is also a story of war, family, duty, and destiny. Here are some of the main points of the Mahabharata:

- The Mahabharata tells the story of the conflict between two groups of cousins, the Kauravas and the Pandavas, who are descendants of the legendary king Bharata. The Kauravas are the sons of Dhritarashtra, the blind king of Hastinapura, while the Pandavas are the sons of Pandu, Dhritarashtra's younger brother who died young. The Kauravas are led by Duryodhana, the eldest son, who is jealous and ambitious. The Pandavas are led by Yudhishthira, the eldest son, who is virtuous and righteous. The other Pandavas are Bhima, Arjuna, Nakula, and Sahadeva. They also have a common wife, Draupadi, who was won by Arjuna in a contest.
- The Mahabharata begins with the birth and childhood of the Kauravas and the Pandavas, and their education under the guidance of their teachers, Drona and Kripa. The Kauravas and the Pandavas grow up as rivals, and often compete and quarrel with each other. The Kauravas try to harm and kill the Pandavas several times, but fail. The Pandavas are exiled to the forest for 13 years, after losing a game of dice to the Kauravas, who cheat with the help of their uncle, Shakuni. During their exile, the Pandavas meet many sages, gods, and demons, and have many adventures. They also prepare for the inevitable war with the Kauravas, with the help of their allies, such as Krishna, the avatar of Vishnu, and the king of the Yadavas.
- The Mahabharata culminates in the Kurukshetra War, a massive battle that lasts for 18 days, and involves millions of warriors, chariots, elephants, and weapons. The war is fought on the plains of Kurukshetra, near Delhi, and is witnessed by many gods and sages. The war is a result of the failure of diplomacy and compromise, and is driven by the lust for power, greed, pride, and hatred. The war is also a test of dharma, or moral duty, for both sides, as they face ethical dilemmas and moral crises. The most famous episode of the war is the Bhagavad Gita, a dialogue between Arjuna and Krishna, in which Krishna teaches Arjuna the essence of dharma, karma, and yoga, and motivates him to fight for justice and truth.
- The Mahabharata ends with the victory of the Pandavas, who defeat and kill the Kauravas, and inherit the throne of Hastinapura. However, the victory is also a tragedy, as the war causes immense death and destruction, and leaves both sides bereaved and remorseful. The Pandavas rule for 36 years, and then renounce their kingdom and go on a final pilgrimage to the Himalayas, where they die one by one, except for Yudhishthira, who ascends to heaven. The Mahabharata also predicts the end of the Kali Yuga, the current age of darkness and decay, and the dawn of a new cycle of creation, in which a new avatar of Vishnu will appear to restore dharma and harmony.
