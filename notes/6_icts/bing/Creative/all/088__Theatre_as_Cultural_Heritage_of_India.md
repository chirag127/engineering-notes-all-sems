### Theatre as Cultural Heritage of India

- Theatre is one of the oldest and most vibrant forms of art in India, dating back to at least the 2nd century BCE.
- Theatre in India has a rich and diverse history, reflecting the cultural, religious, linguistic, and regional diversity of the country.
- Theatre in India can be broadly classified into three periods: the classical period, the traditional period, and the modern period.
- The classical period of theatre in India is based on the Natya Shastra, a treatise on drama and dance attributed to Bharata Muni, written between 500 BCE and 500 CE .
- The Natya Shastra defines drama as a "mimicry of the actions and conduct of people, rich in various emotions, depicting different situations" and lays down the principles of theatre, such as genres, styles, aesthetics, and techniques.
- The Natya Shastra also introduces the concept of rasa, which is the essence of art and the emotional response of the audience to the performance.
- The Natya Shastra identifies eight types of rasa: erotic, comic, pathetic, furious, heroic, terrible, odious, and marvelous.
- The classical period of theatre in India produced two major genres of plays: natakas and prakaranas. Natakas are based on mythology, history, and legends, while prakaranas are based on contemporary life and society.
- Some of the famous playwrights of the classical period are Kalidasa, Bhasa, Sudraka, Vishakhadatta, and Harsha.
- The traditional period of theatre in India is characterized by the emergence and development of various regional and folk forms of theatre, influenced by local cultures, languages, and customs .
- The traditional period of theatre in India also witnessed the impact of Islamic invasions and rule, which led to the suppression of public performances and the adaptation of theatre to private and rural settings .
- The traditional period of theatre in India also saw the incorporation of social and political themes, as well as the use of song, dance, music, and improvisation in theatre .
- Some of the examples of traditional theatre forms in India are: Koodiyattam from Kerala, Swang from Rajasthan, Haryana and UP, Yakshagana from Karnataka, Bhand Paather from Kashmir, Tamasha from Maharashtra, Ankiya Naat Bhaona from Assam, Therukoothu from Tamil Nadu, Jatra from Orissa and Bihar, Dashavtaar from Maharashtra and Goa, Bhavai from Gujarat, and Karyala from Himachal Pradesh .
- The modern period of theatre in India began in the 19th century, with the influence of Western theatre and the rise of nationalism and social reform movements .
- The modern period of theatre in India also saw the emergence of urban and professional theatre, as well as the experimentation with new forms, styles, and themes of theatre .
- Some of the prominent playwrights of the modern period are Rabindranath Tagore, Girish Karnad, Vijay Tendulkar, Badal Sircar, Mahesh Dattani, and Mohan Rakesh .
- Theatre in India is a cultural heritage that reflects the diversity, creativity, and dynamism of the Indian people and their society. It is also a medium of expression, education, and entertainment that has a lasting impact on the audience and the performers.
