## Module 2- Indian Literature, Culture, Tradition, and Practices

- This module covers the evolution of script and languages in India, the major literary works and genres in different periods, and the cultural and religious diversity and unity of India.
- The main topics are:

  - Evolution of script and languages in India: Harappan Script and Brahmi Script.
    - Harappan Script is the writing system used by the Indus Valley Civilization, which flourished from 2500 BCE to 1900 BCE. It is still undeciphered and its relation to other languages is unknown. It consists of about 400 symbols, mostly pictographic, that were inscribed on seals, pottery, tablets, and other artifacts.
    - Brahmi Script is the ancestor of most modern Indian scripts, as well as some Southeast Asian scripts. It was used to write Sanskrit, Prakrit, Pali, and other languages from the 3rd century BCE to the 6th century CE. It is derived from the Aramaic script, which was brought to India by the Persian Empire. It has 33 consonants and 9 vowels, and is written from left to right.
  - The Vedas, the Upanishads, the Ramayana and the Mahabharata, Puranas, Buddhist And Jain Literature in Pali, Prakrit And Sanskrit, Kautilya’s Arthashastra, Famous Sanskrit Authors, Telugu Literature.
    - The Vedas are the oldest and most sacred texts of Hinduism, composed in Vedic Sanskrit between 1500 BCE and 500 BCE. They consist of four collections of hymns, rituals, and philosophical discussions, called the Rigveda, the Yajurveda, the Samaveda, and the Atharvaveda. They are considered to be revealed by the gods and transmitted orally by the sages.
    - The Upanishads are the philosophical and mystical texts that form the core of Vedanta, the school of Hindu thought that deals with the nature of reality, the self, and the supreme. They are composed in Sanskrit between 800 BCE and 500 BCE, and are attached to the Vedas as commentaries or supplements. They explore the concepts of Brahman (the ultimate reality), Atman (the individual soul), and Moksha (the liberation from the cycle of rebirth).
    - The Ramayana and the Mahabharata are the two great epics of India, composed in Sanskrit between 500 BCE and 400 CE. They are based on historical events and legends, and depict the heroic deeds and moral dilemmas of the kings, princes, sages, and gods of ancient India. The Ramayana tells the story of Rama, the ideal king, who rescues his wife Sita from the demon king Ravana, with the help of his loyal brother Lakshmana and the monkey god Hanuman. The Mahabharata tells the story of the Kurukshetra War, a civil war between the Pandavas and the Kauravas, who are cousins and claimants to the throne of Hastinapura. The Mahabharata also contains the Bhagavad Gita, a dialogue between the warrior Arjuna and the god Krishna, who advises him on the duty, action, and ethics.
    - The Puranas are a genre of Hindu texts that narrate the myths, legends, and history of the gods, goddesses, sages, kings, and dynasties of India. They are composed in Sanskrit between 300 CE and 1000 CE, and are classified into six categories, based on the main deity they worship: Vishnu, Shiva, Brahma, Devi, Agni, and Surya. They also contain cosmological, geographical, genealogical, and ethical information.
