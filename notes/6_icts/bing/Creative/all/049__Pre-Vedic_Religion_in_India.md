### Pre-Vedic Religion in India

- Pre-Vedic religion refers to the religious traditions that existed in the Indian subcontinent before the arrival of the Indo-Aryan speakers who brought the Vedic religion with them. 
- The most prominent pre-Vedic civilization was the Indus Valley Civilization (IVC), which flourished from c. 2500 to 1900 BCE in present-day Pakistan and northwest India.  
- The IVC is known for its urban planning, sophisticated drainage system, standardized weights and measures, and writing system, which remains undeciphered. 
- The religious beliefs and practices of the IVC are not well understood, but some evidence suggests that they worshipped a mother goddess, a horned deity, a proto-Shiva figure, and various animals and trees.  
- They also practiced ritual bathing, fire worship, and possibly yoga and meditation.  
- Some scholars have proposed that the IVC was influenced by or had contacts with other pre-Vedic cultures, such as the Elamites, the Sumerians, and the Dravidians.  
- The IVC declined around 1900 BCE, possibly due to environmental changes, invasions, or internal conflicts. 
- After the collapse of the IVC, various regional cultures emerged, such as the Ochre Coloured Pottery culture, the Gandhara Grave culture, and the Painted Grey Ware culture. 
- These cultures may have had different religious traditions, some of which may have survived in the form of folk religions, tribal religions, or Āgamic traditions.  
- The Āgamas are non-Vedic texts that prescribe rituals, ethics, and theology for the worship of Shiva, Vishnu, Shakti, and other deities. 
- The Āgamas are considered to be either post-Vedic or pre-Vedic in origin, depending on the school of thought. 
- The pre-Vedic religions may have influenced or been influenced by the Vedic religion, which emerged around 1500 BCE with the arrival of the Indo-Aryans.  
- The Vedic religion was based on the four collections of hymns, rituals, and philosophies known as the Vedas, which are the oldest scriptures of Hinduism.  
- The Vedic religion was polytheistic, with gods such as Indra, Varuna, Agni, Surya, and Rudra representing natural forces and cosmic principles.  
- The Vedic religion also involved sacrificial offerings, ancestor worship, and the concept of karma and rebirth.  
- The Vedic religion evolved over time, giving rise to various schools of thought, such as the Upanishads, the Brahmanas, the Sutras, and the six systems of Hindu philosophy.  
- The Vedic religion also interacted with other religious movements, such as Jainism, Buddhism, and the Bhakti movement, which shaped the development of Hinduism.
