### The Seven Limbs of the State in Ancient India

- The Seven Limbs of the State (Saptanga) is a political concept that was formulated by Kautilya in his treatise Arthashastra  .
- It refers to the seven essential elements or organs of a state that are interdependent and interrelated .
- The seven limbs are:

  1. King (Svamin): The king is the supreme authority and the head of the state. He is responsible for the peace, justice and stability of the state. He is independent and has the power to appoint and dismiss his ministers and officials. He is also the commander-in-chief of the army and the protector of the people .
  2. Amatya (Minister or Bureaucrat): The amatya is the second in rank after the king. He is the chief adviser and the administrator of the state. He assists the king in policy-making, governance and diplomacy. He is loyal, efficient and competent. He supervises the various departments and officials of the state .
  3. Janapada (Territory or Population): The janapada is the third limb of the state. It consists of the land and the people of the state. The land should be fertile, well-cultivated and rich in natural resources. The people should be loyal, patriotic and obedient to the king. They should also be happy, prosperous and well-protected .
  4. Durga (Fort or Capital): The durga is the fourth limb of the state. It is the fortified city or the capital of the state. It is the center of administration, commerce and culture. It should be well-built, well-defended and well-supplied. It should also be strategically located and easily accessible .
  5. Kosa (Treasure or Revenue): The kosa is the fifth limb of the state. It is the financial resource or the revenue of the state. It is derived from taxation, trade and other sources. It should be sufficient, stable and well-managed. It should also be used for the welfare of the people and the defense of the state .
  6. Danda (Coercive Authority or Army): The danda is the sixth limb of the state. It is the military force or the coercive authority of the state. It is used to maintain law and order, to enforce justice and to protect the state from external and internal enemies. It should be strong, disciplined and loyal. It should also be well-trained, well-equipped and well-led  .
  7. Mitra (Ally or Friend): The mitra is the seventh and the last limb of the state. It is the external support or the ally of the state. It is a friendly state or a power that shares common interests and goals with the state. It is used to enhance the security, prestige and influence of the state. It should be reliable, trustworthy and cooperative .

- A possible mnemonic to remember the seven limbs of the state is: **K**ing **A**matya **J**anapada **D**urga **K**osa **D**anda **M**itra or **KAJDKDM**. Alternatively, one can use the phrase: **K**ing **A**nd **M**inister **J**oined **D**urga **K**osa **D**anda or **KAMJDKD**.
