### Various Philosophical Doctrines in India

- Indian philosophy refers to the diverse systems of thought and reflection that were developed by the civilizations of the Indian subcontinent. 
- Indian philosophy can be classified into two broad categories: orthodox (astika) and heterodox (nastika) schools, depending on their acceptance or rejection of the authority of the Vedas, the ancient scriptures of Hinduism.  
- The orthodox schools are Nyaya, Vaisheshika, Samkhya, Yoga, Mimamsa, and Vedanta. The heterodox schools are Jainism, Buddhism, Ajivika, Ajnana, and Charvaka.  
- Some of the common themes that Indian philosophies share are the concepts of dharma (duty, law, morality), karma (action, cause and effect), samsara (cycle of rebirth), dukkha (suffering), renunciation (detachment, asceticism), meditation (concentration, awareness), and moksha (liberation, nirvana).  
- The main goal of Indian philosophy is to attain liberation from suffering and rebirth through various spiritual practices and ethical disciplines. 
- The following is a brief overview of the six orthodox and five heterodox schools of Indian philosophy:

#### Nyaya
- Nyaya is the school of logic and epistemology. It investigates the sources and validity of knowledge (pramana) and the nature of inference (anumana).  
- Nyaya holds that there are four valid means of knowledge: perception (pratyaksha), inference (anumana), comparison (upamana), and testimony (shabda).  
- Nyaya also develops a detailed theory of categories (padartha) and a system of syllogism (avayava) to establish valid arguments.  
- Nyaya is concerned with the analysis of concepts such as self (atman), mind (manas), consciousness (chaitanya), and liberation (moksha).  
- Nyaya is also known for its debates with the Buddhists, the Jains, and other schools on various philosophical issues.  

#### Vaisheshika
- Vaisheshika is the school of atomism and metaphysics. It studies the nature and classification of reality (padartha) and the principles of causation (karya-karana).  
- Vaisheshika holds that there are six categories of reality: substance (dravya), quality (guna), action (karma), generality (samanya), particularity (vishesha), and inherence (samavaya).  
- Vaisheshika also posits that there are nine types of substance: earth, water, fire, air, ether, time, space, self, and mind.  
- Vaisheshika maintains that all physical substances are composed of indivisible atoms (anu) that combine and recombine in different ways to form complex entities.  
- Vaisheshika is closely allied with Nyaya and shares many of its concepts and methods.  

#### Samkhya
- Samkhya is the school of dualism and cosmology. It explains the origin and evolution of the universe (srishti) and the diversity of beings (bheda) in terms of two fundamental principles: purusha (spirit) and prakriti (matter).  
- Samkhya holds that purusha is the pure consciousness, the witness, the knower, and the enjoyer. Prakriti is the primordial nature, the source of all material phenomena, the object, the known, and the enjoyed.  
- Samkhya also postulates that prakriti consists of three modes (gunas) of energy: sattva (purity, harmony, light), rajas (activity, passion, motion), and tamas (inertia, dullness, darkness).
