### Indian Architecture in Ancient India and present times

- Indian architecture is rooted in the history, culture, and religion of India. It has developed various styles and traditions over the centuries, reflecting the diversity and complexity of the Indian subcontinent. 
- Some of the earliest examples of Indian architecture are the rock-cut structures, such as the caves, chaityas, viharas, and rathas, that were excavated for Buddhist and Jain monks as places of worship and residence. These structures date back to the 3rd century BCE and show the influence of Persian, Greek, and Mauryan art.  
- Another important feature of ancient Indian architecture is the temple, which emerged as the predominant form of religious and secular building in the early medieval period. Hindu temples developed two main styles: the Nagara style in northern India, characterized by a curvilinear spire (shikhara) over the sanctum (garbhagriha), and the Dravidian style in southern India, characterized by a pyramidal tower (vimana) over the sanctum. Both styles also incorporated elaborate carvings, sculptures, and paintings depicting gods, goddesses, and mythological scenes.  
- The temple architecture of India also influenced the development of other types of buildings, such as palaces, forts, tombs, and mosques. Some of the most notable examples of these are the Khajuraho temples, the Qutub Minar, the Taj Mahal, and the Red Fort. These buildings reflect the interaction and fusion of different cultures, such as Hindu, Islamic, Persian, Turkish, and European, that shaped the history of India.  
- In the modern period, Indian architecture has continued to evolve and adapt to the changing needs and aspirations of the society. Some of the prominent architects and movements that have contributed to the modern Indian architecture are Rabindranath Tagore, Charles Correa, Laurie Baker, Le Corbusier, B.V. Doshi, Raj Rewal, and the Bengal School of Art. These architects and movements have experimented with various forms, materials, techniques, and concepts, such as regionalism, sustainability, vernacular, and social justice, to create innovative and diverse buildings that reflect the contemporary Indian identity and culture.
