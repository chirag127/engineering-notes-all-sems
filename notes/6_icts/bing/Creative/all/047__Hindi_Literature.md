### Hindi Literature

Hindi literature is the literature written in Hindi, the official language of India. It covers a wide range of genres, such as poetry, drama, fiction, essays, criticism, and history. Hindi literature has a rich and diverse history, spanning from the medieval period to the present day. Some of the main features and topics of Hindi literature are:

- The earliest form of Hindi literature is the **Bhakti** or devotional literature, which emerged in the 12th-16th centuries. It was influenced by the religious movements of **Vaishnavism**, **Shaivism**, and **Shaktism**, and expressed the love and devotion of the poets to their chosen deities. Some of the prominent Bhakti poets are **Kabir**, **Surdas**, **Tulsidas**, **Meerabai**, and **Raidas**.
- The **Riti** or ornamental literature, which flourished in the 17th-18th centuries, was characterized by the use of **figures of speech**, **metaphors**, **imagery**, and **musicality**. It focused on the themes of **love**, **beauty**, **nature**, and **courtly life**. Some of the notable Riti poets are **Bihari**, **Keshavdas**, **Bhoj**, and **Ghananand**.
- The **Adhunik** or modern literature, which began in the 19th century, was influenced by the **social reform movements**, **nationalist movements**, **Western literature**, and **Indian languages**. It introduced new genres, such as **novels**, **short stories**, **essays**, and **drama**, and explored the issues of **identity**, **culture**, **politics**, **gender**, and **class**. Some of the prominent Adhunik writers are **Premchand**, **Jaishankar Prasad**, **Mahadevi Varma**, **Nirala**, and **Harivansh Rai Bachchan**.
- The **Pragativad** or progressive literature, which emerged in the 1930s, was inspired by the **Marxist ideology**, **socialist realism**, and **anti-imperialism**. It aimed to expose the **injustices**, **oppressions**, and **exploitations** of the **colonial rule**, **feudal system**, and **capitalist society**. Some of the leading Pragativad writers are **Yashpal**, **Rahi Masoom Raza**, **Nagarjun**, **Muktibodh**, and **Ismat Chughtai**.
- The **Nayi Kahani** or new story movement, which started in the 1950s, was influenced by the **existentialism**, **modernism**, and **psychoanalysis**. It experimented with the **form**, **style**, and **language** of the short story, and focused on the **psychological**, **emotional**, and **moral** aspects of the **individual** and **society**. Some of the prominent Nayi Kahani writers are **Nirmal Verma**, **Rajendra Yadav**, **Mannu Bhandari**, **Mohandas Naimishray**, and **Kamleshwar**.
- The **Dalit** or oppressed literature, which emerged in the 1970s, was inspired by the **Dalit movement**, **Ambedkarism**, and **feminism**. It challenged the **caste system**, **patriarchy**, and **dominant culture**, and expressed the **pain**, **anger**, **resistance**, and **aspiration** of the **marginalized** and **subaltern** groups. Some of the notable Dalit writers are **Omprakash Valmiki**, **Surajpal Chauhan**, **Mohan Das Namishray**, **Urmila Pawar**, and **Sharan Kumar Limbale**.
