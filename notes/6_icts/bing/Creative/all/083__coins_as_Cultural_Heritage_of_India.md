### Coins as Cultural Heritage of India

- Coins are one of the most valuable sources of information about the history, culture, religion, economy, and politics of ancient India.
- Coins reveal the names, titles, dates, symbols, and portraits of the rulers and dynasties that issued them, as well as the regions and territories they controlled.
- Coins also reflect the artistic, literary, and religious traditions of different periods and regions of India, as well as the influences and interactions with other cultures and civilizations.
- Coins can be classified into various types based on their shape, material, weight, and design. Some of the main types of coins in ancient India are:

  - **Punch-marked coins**: These are the earliest coins of India, dating back to the 6th century BC. They are made of silver or copper and have various symbols or marks punched on them by dies or stamps. The symbols include geometric shapes, animals, plants, human figures, and religious symbols. The punch-marked coins were issued by various kingdoms and republics of the Mahajanapadas, as well as by the Mauryan Empire and its successors.
  - **Cast coins**: These are coins that were made by pouring molten metal into a mould or a clay impression. They are usually round or square in shape and have a uniform weight and design. The cast coins were issued by various dynasties and regions of India from the 3rd century BC to the 3rd century AD, such as the Indo-Greeks, the Indo-Scythians, the Indo-Parthians, the Kushans, the Satavahanas, the Western Kshatrapas, and the Guptas. The cast coins often have legends in Greek, Kharoshthi, Brahmi, or Pahlavi scripts, and depict the portraits and symbols of the rulers and deities.
  - **Die-struck coins**: These are coins that were made by striking a metal blank or flan between two dies or punches that had the design engraved on them. They are usually round or oval in shape and have a more refined and detailed design than the cast coins. The die-struck coins were issued by various dynasties and regions of India from the 4th century AD to the 13th century AD, such as the Huns, the Vakatakas, the Pallavas, the Chalukyas, the Rashtrakutas, the Pratiharas, the Pala-Senas, the Cholas, the Chahamanas, the Ghaznavids, and the Delhi Sultanate. The die-struck coins often have legends in Sanskrit, Arabic, or Persian scripts, and depict the portraits and symbols of the rulers and deities.
  - **Machine-struck coins**: These are coins that were made by using a mechanical press or a minting machine that had the design engraved on a steel die or a roller. They are usually round or rectangular in shape and have a more uniform and standardized design than the die-struck coins. The machine-struck coins were issued by various dynasties and regions of India from the 14th century AD to the 19th century AD, such as the Vijayanagara Empire, the Bahmani Sultanate, the Mughal Empire, the Maratha Empire, the Sikh Empire, the British East India Company, and the Princely States. The machine-struck coins often have legends in English, Hindi, Urdu, or other regional languages, and depict the portraits and symbols of the rulers and deities.

The Lion Capital coin is one of the earliest coins to have a political and religious message, and is considered a masterpiece of Indian art and iconography.
