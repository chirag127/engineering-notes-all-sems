### Buddhism in India

- Buddhism is one of the world’s largest religions and originated 2,500 years ago in India.
- Buddhism is based on the teachings and life experiences of Siddhartha Gautama, who was born in Lumbini Province, Nepal in 623 BC.
- Siddhartha Gautama renounced his princely life and sought the truth of human suffering. He attained enlightenment under the Bodhi tree and became known as the Buddha, meaning the awakened one.
- Buddhism teaches the Four Noble Truths: the truth of suffering, the truth of the origin of suffering, the truth of the cessation of suffering, and the truth of the path to the cessation of suffering.
- Buddhism also teaches the concept of karma, which is the law of cause and effect that determines one’s rebirth in the cycle of samsara.
- Buddhism aims to help people achieve nirvana, which is the state of liberation from samsara and the end of suffering.
- Buddhism has three main branches: Theravada, Mahayana, and Vajrayana. Theravada is the oldest and most conservative form of Buddhism, which emphasizes the individual path to enlightenment. Mahayana is the largest and most diverse form of Buddhism, which emphasizes the role of bodhisattvas, who are enlightened beings who help others attain nirvana. Vajrayana is the most esoteric and ritualistic form of Buddhism, which uses tantric practices and visualizations to achieve enlightenment.
- Buddhism spread throughout India and beyond with the help of various rulers, such as Ashoka, who embraced Buddhism and supported its propagation. Buddhism also interacted with other religions, such as Hinduism and Jainism, and influenced their development.
- Buddhism declined in India from the 8th century onwards due to various factors, such as the invasions of the Huns and the Muslims, the revival of Hinduism, and the internal schisms and corruption within the Buddhist community.
- Buddhism was revived in India in the 19th and 20th centuries by various movements, such as the Maha Bodhi Society, which restored the ancient Buddhist sites, the Dalit Buddhist movement, which converted millions of oppressed castes to Buddhism, and the Tibetan Buddhism, which flourished in India after the exile of the Dalai Lama.
- Buddhism in India today is a minority religion, with about 0.7% of the population identifying as Buddhists according to the 2011 census. However, Buddhism still has a significant cultural and spiritual influence in India, and many Indians visit the Buddhist pilgrimage sites, such as Bodh Gaya, Sarnath, and Kushinagar.
