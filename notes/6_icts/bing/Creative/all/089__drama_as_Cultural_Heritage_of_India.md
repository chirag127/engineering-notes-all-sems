### Drama as Cultural Heritage of India

- Drama is one of the oldest and most influential forms of performing arts in India, dating back to the 2nd century BCE.
- Drama in India reflects the diversity and richness of its culture, history, and mythology, as well as its social and political realities.
- Drama in India has three major periods: the classical period, the traditional period, and the modern period.
- The classical period is marked by the emergence of Sanskrit drama, which is based on the treatise Natya Shastra by Bharata Muni. Sanskrit drama is characterized by its poetic language, elaborate costumes and makeup, stylized gestures and movements, and the use of music, dance, and chorus. Sanskrit drama has two main genres: nataka (heroic or mythological plays) and prakarana (social or realistic plays). Sanskrit drama also has two main styles: lokadharmi (naturalistic) and natyadharmi (stylized). Sanskrit drama aims to evoke rasa (aesthetic emotion) in the audience, which can be of eight types: erotic, comic, pathetic, furious, heroic, terrible, odious, and marvelous.
- The traditional period is marked by the decline of Sanskrit drama and the rise of regional and folk drama, which are influenced by Islamic and European cultures. Regional drama includes forms such as jatra (Bengal), tamasha (Maharashtra), nautanki (Uttar Pradesh), bhavai (Gujarat), and yakshagana (Karnataka). Folk drama includes forms such as raslila (Krishna's dance drama), ramleela (Rama's epic drama), and puppetry. Regional and folk drama are characterized by their use of local languages, dialects, and idioms, their incorporation of popular songs and dances, their focus on social and moral issues, and their appeal to the masses.
- The modern period is marked by the emergence of urban and experimental drama, which are influenced by Western and Indian movements such as realism, naturalism, expressionism, absurdism, and modernism. Urban drama includes forms such as Parsi theatre, Indian People's Theatre Association (IPTA), and Indian National Theatre (INT). Experimental drama includes forms such as Theatre of the Absurd, Theatre of the Roots, and Theatre of Protest. Urban and experimental drama are characterized by their use of contemporary themes, issues, and techniques, their exploration of new forms and media, their critique of the status quo, and their challenge to the conventions and traditions of drama.
