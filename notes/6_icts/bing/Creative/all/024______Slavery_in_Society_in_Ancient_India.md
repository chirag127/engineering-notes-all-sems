# Slavery in Society in Ancient India

- Slavery was a widespread social practice in ancient India, but its nature and extent varied depending on the time period, region, and source of information.
- Slavery in ancient India was closely associated with the caste system, which divided the society into four main groups: Brahmins (priests and scholars), Kshatriyas (warriors and rulers), Vaishyas (merchants and farmers), and Shudras (servants and laborers). The lowest and most oppressed group were the Dalits or untouchables, who were considered outside the caste system and performed the most menial and polluting tasks.
- The origin of slavery in ancient India is disputed by scholars, who have different interpretations of the terms dasa and dasyu, which appear in the Vedas, the oldest scriptures of Hinduism. Some scholars argue that these terms refer to the indigenous people who were conquered and enslaved by the Indo-Aryan invaders, while others suggest that they denote enemies, robbers, or sinners, without implying a racial or ethnic distinction.
- The earliest evidence of slavery in ancient India comes from the Arthashastra, a treatise on statecraft and economics written by Kautilya, the prime minister of the Mauryan emperor Chandragupta, in the 4th century BCE. The Arthashastra mentions various categories of slaves, such as war captives, debtors, criminals, and children sold by their parents. It also lays down the rules and regulations for the treatment, sale, and emancipation of slaves.
- Slavery in ancient India was not as harsh or oppressive as in other ancient civilizations, such as Greece or Rome. Most of the slaves in ancient India were domestic slaves, who worked as servants, cooks, maids, or concubines in the households of their masters. They were treated as members of the family and enjoyed certain rights and privileges, such as receiving gifts, inheriting property, and marrying free persons. Some slaves even rose to high positions of power and influence, such as ministers, generals, and governors.
- Slavery in ancient India was also influenced by the religious and ethical teachings of Hinduism, Buddhism, and Jainism, which advocated non-violence, compassion, and charity. The Dharma Shastras, the law codes of Hinduism, prohibited the slave trade and encouraged the liberation of slaves as a meritorious act. The Buddhist and Jain texts also denounced slavery as a form of violence and advocated the equality and dignity of all living beings.
- Slavery in ancient India declined gradually with the rise of feudalism and the Muslim invasions, which introduced new forms of servitude and bondage. The Mughal empire, which ruled most of India from the 16th to the 18th century, abolished slavery as a legal institution, but continued to employ slaves as soldiers, administrators, and artisans. The British colonial rule, which began in the 18th century, also outlawed slavery, but exploited the Indian labor force through taxation, land revenue, and indentured servitude. The Indian independence movement, which culminated in the formation of the Republic of India in 1947, finally ended the legacy of slavery and colonialism in India.
