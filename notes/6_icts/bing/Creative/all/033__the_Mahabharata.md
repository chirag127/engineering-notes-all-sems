### The Mahabharata

- The Mahabharata is an ancient Sanskrit epic poem that tells the story of the kingdom of Kurus and the war between two groups of cousins, the Kauravas and the Pandavas, for the throne of Hastinapura     .
- The Mahabharata is regarded as both a historical account of Hinduism's birth and a code of ethics for the faithful .
- The Mahabharata consists of almost 100,000 couplets, divided into 18 sections, plus a supplement titled Harivamsha.
- The authorship of the Mahabharata is traditionally ascribed to the sage Vyasa, who appears in the work as the grandfather of the Kauravas and the Pandavas.
- The date and historical occurrence of the war are much debated, but it is estimated to have taken place in the 13th or 14th century B.C. .
- The main plot of the Mahabharata can be summarized as follows    :

  - The blindness of Dhritarashtra, the elder of two princes, causes him to be passed over in favour of his brother Pandu as king on their father's death.
  - A curse prevents Pandu from fathering children, so his wife Kunti asks the gods to father children in Pandu's name. As a result, the god Dharma fathers Yudhishthira, the Wind fathers Bhima, Indra fathers Arjuna, and the Ashvins father Nakula and Sahadeva (also twins; born to Pandu's second wife, Madri). These five sons are known as the Pandavas.
  - Dhritarashtra has 100 sons, known as the Kauravas, who are all demons. The eldest son, Duryodhana, leads the other 99 brothers and has a seething hatred for the Pandavas.
  - Both Yudhishthira and Duryodhana are granted kingdoms by Dhritarashtra, but Duryodhana schemes to take Yudhishthira's kingdom by challenging him to a dice game. Yudhishthira loses everything, including his kingdom and his wife Draupadi, who is humiliated by the Kauravas. Dhritarashtra commands Duryodhana to give Yudhishthira one more chance, saying that if Yudhishthira loses, he and the other Pandavas must live in exile for 12 years and spend a 13th year in disguise, and if they are recognized, they must spend another 12 years in exile. Yudhishthira loses again.
  - The Pandavas spend the 12 years of exile in the forest, studying with Brahmins and training. During this period, Arjuna is granted a celestial weapon by the god Indra. Draupadi is kidnapped and brought back. The Pandavas also meet their cousin Krishna, who remains their friend and companion thereafter.
  - During the 13th year, the Pandavas take refuge in the king Virata's palace, posing as former subjects of Yudhishthira. Draupadi poses as a maidservant to maintain her independence. The Pandavas have several adventures, such as Bhima killing a demon, Arjuna winning a princess, and Yudhishthira defeating a king in a game of chess. The Pandavas make it through the 13th year undetected despite these showy exploits.
  - Yudhishthira returns to take his kingdom back, but learns that Duryodhana has no intention of turning it over. Duryodhana clearly wants war, but Krishna advises Yudhishthira to ask for a peaceful transfer of power first. Duryodhana denies this request, even when Krishna offers to settle for five villages for the Pandavas. The two sides prepare for war, with Krishna siding with the Pandavas and providing them with soldiers. The war is to take place on the field of Kurukshetra.
