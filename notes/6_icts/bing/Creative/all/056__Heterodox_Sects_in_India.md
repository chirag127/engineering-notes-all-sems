### Heterodox Sects in India

- Heterodox sects are those that deviate from the orthodox or mainstream views of a religion or philosophy. 
- In the context of ancient India, heterodox sects are those that rejected the authority of the Vedas, the caste system, the ritual sacrifices, and the brahminical priesthood.  
- Heterodox sects emerged as part of the sramana movement, which was a cultural and religious phenomenon that advocated renunciation, asceticism, meditation, and liberation from the cycle of rebirth.  
- Some of the major heterodox sects in ancient India were:

  - Ajivika: Founded by Makkhali Gosala, this sect believed in absolute determinism or fate, and denied the existence of free will, karma, and liberation. They also rejected the concept of God and soul, and held a materialistic view of the universe. They practiced extreme asceticism and formed discrete communities.  
  - Jainism: Founded by Mahavira, this sect believed in the existence of soul or jiva, which is trapped in the cycle of rebirth due to karma. They advocated the principles of ahimsa (non-violence), anekantavada (non-absolutism), and aparigraha (non-attachment) as the means to attain liberation. They also practiced strict asceticism and followed the five vows of non-violence, truthfulness, non-stealing, celibacy, and non-possession. They divided into two main sects: Digambara (sky-clad) and Svetambara (white-clad).  
  - Buddhism: Founded by Gautama Buddha, this sect believed in the four noble truths, which state that life is suffering, suffering is caused by desire, suffering can be ended by eliminating desire, and the way to eliminate desire is to follow the eightfold path. They also believed in the concepts of karma, rebirth, and nirvana (liberation). They rejected the authority of the Vedas, the caste system, and the ritual sacrifices, and emphasized the importance of ethical conduct, wisdom, and compassion. They divided into two main sects: Theravada (the way of the elders) and Mahayana (the great vehicle).  

- Some of the minor heterodox sects in ancient India were:

  - Carvaka: This sect was a materialist and atheist school of philosophy that rejected the existence of soul, karma, rebirth, and liberation. They also rejected the authority of the Vedas, the caste system, and the ritual sacrifices. They advocated the pursuit of sensual pleasure as the only goal of life. 
  - Lokayata: This sect was a rationalist and skeptic school of philosophy that rejected the existence of anything that cannot be perceived by the senses. They also rejected the authority of the Vedas, the caste system, and the ritual sacrifices. They advocated the use of reason and logic as the only means of knowledge. 

- Heterodox sects in ancient India challenged the dominant religious and social order of the time and offered alternative views and practices to the people. They also contributed to the development of Indian philosophy, art, literature, and culture.
