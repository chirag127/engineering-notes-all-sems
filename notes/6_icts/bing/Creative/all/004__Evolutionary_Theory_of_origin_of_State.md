#### Evolutionary Theory of origin of State

- The Evolutionary Theory of origin of State is the theory that explains the state as the product of historical and social evolution, rather than the creation of God, force, convention or family.
- The theory is based on the assumption that the state is a natural and organic institution that emerged gradually from the primitive forms of human society and reached its present form through a series of stages.
- The theory is supported by the evidence of anthropology, sociology, history and political science, which show the diversity and complexity of human societies and their political organization over time.
- The theory identifies several factors that contributed to the evolution of the state, such as kinship, religion, property, defence, force and political consciousness.

##### Kinship
- Kinship is the bond of blood relationship that united the members of the earliest human groups, such as families, clans and tribes.
- Kinship was the basis of social solidarity, mutual cooperation, common identity and loyalty among the members of the group.
- Kinship also determined the authority structure, norms and customs of the group, as well as the rights and duties of the members.
- Kinship was the first step in the evolution of the state, as it created the sense of community and belonging among the people.

##### Religion
- Religion was another factor that strengthened the bond of kinship and provided a common worldview and belief system for the early human groups.
- Religion also influenced the political organization and authority of the group, as the religious leaders often assumed the role of political leaders, or the political leaders claimed divine sanction for their rule.
- Religion also helped to regulate the social and moral behavior of the members of the group, as well as to resolve conflicts and disputes among them.
- Religion was the second step in the evolution of the state, as it created the sense of unity and order among the people.

##### Property and defence
- Property and defence were the factors that led to the expansion and differentiation of the early human groups, as they sought to acquire more resources and protect them from external threats.
- Property and defence also stimulated the development of economic activities, such as agriculture, trade and industry, which increased the wealth and productivity of the group.
- Property and defence also necessitated the establishment of more complex and formal political institutions, such as laws, courts, armies and taxes, which regulated the distribution and management of the resources and the security of the group.
- Property and defence were the third step in the evolution of the state, as they created the sense of progress and stability among the people.

##### Force
- Force was the factor that resulted from the competition and conflict among the different human groups, as they fought for the control and domination of the resources and territories.
- Force also led to the emergence of more powerful and centralized political entities, such as kingdoms, empires and states, which conquered and assimilated the weaker and smaller groups.
- Force also influenced the nature and character of the political authority, as the rulers often relied on coercion, violence and oppression to maintain their power and legitimacy.
- Force was the fourth step in the evolution of the state, as it created the sense of hierarchy and sovereignty among the people.

##### Political consciousness
- Political consciousness was the factor that emerged from the awareness and participation of the people in the political affairs of their group, as they demanded more rights and freedoms and challenged the existing authority and order.
- Political consciousness also led to the development of more diverse and democratic political forms, such as republics, federations and confederations, which recognized and respected the diversity and autonomy of the people and the groups.
- Political consciousness also influenced the values and ideals of the political system, as the people sought to achieve justice, equality, liberty and fraternity in their political life.
- Political consciousness was the fifth and final step in the evolution of the state, as it created the sense of citizenship and democracy among the people.
