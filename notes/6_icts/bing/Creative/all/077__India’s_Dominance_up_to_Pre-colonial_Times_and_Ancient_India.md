### India’s Dominance up to Pre-colonial Times and Ancient India

- India is a subcontinent of South Asia, which has been home to one of the world's oldest and most influential civilizations.
- India's history can be broadly divided into four periods: ancient, medieval, colonial and modern.
- Ancient India refers to the period from the emergence of the Indus Valley Civilization (c. 2500 BCE) to the end of the Gupta Empire (c. 550 CE).
- Medieval India refers to the period from the rise of the Islamic invasions (c. 700 CE) to the decline of the Mughal Empire (c. 1700 CE).
- Colonial India refers to the period from the establishment of the British Raj (c. 1858 CE) to the independence of India and Pakistan (c. 1947 CE).
- Modern India refers to the period from the partition of India and Pakistan (c. 1947 CE) to the present day.

- Ancient India was marked by the development of urban culture, trade, literature, art, science, mathematics, astronomy, religion and philosophy.
- The Indus Valley Civilization was one of the earliest urban civilizations in the world, with sophisticated urban planning, sanitation, drainage, writing, weights and measures, and trade networks .
- The Indus Valley Civilization declined around 1500 BCE, possibly due to environmental changes, invasions, or internal conflicts .
- The Vedic period (c. 1500-500 BCE) was the period of the composition of the Vedas, the oldest scriptures of Hinduism, which contain hymns, rituals, myths and philosophy .
- The Vedic period also saw the emergence of the caste system, the four varnas (social classes) of Brahmins (priests), Kshatriyas (warriors), Vaishyas (merchants) and Shudras (servants), and the concept of karma and reincarnation .
- The later Vedic period (c. 1000-500 BCE) witnessed the rise of the Mahajanapadas, the sixteen kingdoms and republics that dominated northern India, and the development of iron technology, agriculture, trade and urbanization .
- The Mauryan Empire (c. 321-185 BCE) was the first pan-Indian empire, founded by Chandragupta Maurya, who unified most of northern and central India under his rule .
- The Mauryan Empire reached its peak under Ashoka (c. 268-232 BCE), who expanded the empire to include most of South Asia, and promoted Buddhism, non-violence, tolerance and welfare throughout his realm .
- The Mauryan Empire declined after Ashoka's death, and was replaced by various regional kingdoms, such as the Shunga, Satavahana, Kushan, Gupta and Harsha dynasties .
- The Gupta Empire (c. 320-550 CE) is considered the golden age of ancient India, as it witnessed a flourishing of art, literature, science, mathematics, astronomy, medicine, architecture and culture .
- The Gupta Empire was known for its achievements in decimal system, zero, pi, algebra, trigonometry, geometry, surgery, metallurgy, astronomy, literature, drama, poetry, painting, sculpture and temple architecture .
- The Gupta Empire collapsed under the pressure of the Hun invasions, and India entered a period of political fragmentation and cultural diversity .

- Medieval India was marked by the interaction of Hindu, Buddhist, Jain, Sikh and Islamic cultures, and the rise and fall of various empires and kingdoms.
- The Islamic invasions of India began in the 8th century CE, when the Arab conquerors of Sind and Multan established the first Muslim foothold in the subcontinent .
- The Islamic invasions intensified in the 11th and 12th centuries CE, when the Turkic and Afghan warlords, such as Mahmud of Ghazni, Muhammad of Ghor and Qutb-ud-din Aibak, raided and plundered northern India, and established the Delhi Sultanate, the first Muslim empire in India [^3^
