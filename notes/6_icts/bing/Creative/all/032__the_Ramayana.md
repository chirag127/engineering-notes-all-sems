### The Ramayana

The Ramayana is a Hindu epic poem that narrates the life and adventures of Rama, the prince of Kosala and an incarnation of the god Vishnu. The Ramayana consists of seven books and 24,000 verses. The main plot of the Ramayana is as follows:

- Rama is the eldest son of King Dasaratha of Kosala and his first wife Kausalya. He is a virtuous, brave, and handsome prince who is loved by all. He has three younger brothers: Bharata, Lakshmana, and Shatrughna.
- When Rama is about to be crowned as the heir to the throne, his stepmother Kaikeyi, influenced by her maid Manthara, demands that Dasaratha grant her two boons that he had promised her long ago. She asks that Rama be exiled to the forest for 14 years and that Bharata be made the king instead. Dasaratha reluctantly agrees, but he dies of grief soon after.
- Rama accepts his fate and leaves for the forest with his wife Sita and his brother Lakshmana. Bharata refuses to take the throne and goes to the forest to persuade Rama to return, but Rama insists on fulfilling his father's promise. Bharata takes Rama's sandals and places them on the throne as a symbol of Rama's authority. He vows to rule as Rama's regent until he returns.
- In the forest, Rama meets many sages, saints, and animals who help him and become his allies. He also encounters and defeats many demons who disturb the peace and harmony of the forest. One of the demons is Surpanakha, the sister of Ravana, the king of Lanka and the most powerful demon in the world. Surpanakha falls in love with Rama and tries to seduce him, but he rejects her. She then tries to attack Sita, but Lakshmana cuts off her nose and ears. Surpanakha runs to Ravana and tells him about Sita's beauty and virtue. Ravana decides to abduct Sita and make her his wife.
- Ravana sends one of his demons, Maricha, to disguise himself as a golden deer and lure Rama and Lakshmana away from Sita. Sita sees the deer and asks Rama to catch it for her. Rama agrees and chases the deer, but he realizes that it is a trick when he hears Maricha imitate his voice and cry for help. He shoots the deer with an arrow and kills it, but he also worries that Sita might be in danger. He tells Lakshmana to go and check on her, but Lakshmana hesitates, saying that he has sworn to protect Sita and that Rama's voice might be a deception. Sita, however, hears Rama's cry and thinks that he is in trouble. She scolds Lakshmana for being indifferent and accuses him of having ulterior motives. Lakshmana feels hurt and angry, but he obeys Sita and draws a protective circle around her with his arrow. He tells her not to step out of the circle for any reason and leaves to find Rama.
- Ravana takes advantage of the situation and approaches Sita in the guise of a holy man. He asks her for alms, but she refuses to cross the circle. He then reveals his true identity and tries to persuade her to come with him. He praises her beauty and offers her wealth and power, but she rejects him. He then threatens her with violence and force, but she remains defiant. He finally lifts her up and carries her away in his flying chariot. Sita cries out for Rama and Lakshmana and throws her ornaments and flowers on the ground as a trail. She also sees a group of vultures led by Jatayu, a friend of Dasaratha, and asks them to help her.
