### Kingship in states in Ancient India

- Kingship in ancient India was a sovereignty over a territory by a king who functioned as its protector, a role which involved both secular and religious power. 
- The meaning and significance of kingship changed dramatically between the Vedic and Later Vedic period, and underwent further development under the times of the Jain and Buddhist rulers. 
- In the Vedic period, kingship was not always hereditary and the king was not considered divine. He was dependent on the gods and the assembly (samiti) for his authority and legitimacy.  
- In the Later Vedic period, kingship became more hereditary and the king was increasingly associated with the gods through his qualities and rituals. He also gained more power and prestige over the other social groups and the assembly.  
- The king was expected to uphold and enforce dharma, the cosmic and social order, which was also the source of his power. He was guided by the Brahmins, the priests and scholars, who instructed him in the sacred texts and performed the rituals for him.  
- The king was also responsible for the welfare and protection of his subjects, especially from external enemies. He maintained a large army and a bureaucracy to administer his realm. He also collected taxes and tributes from his subjects and allies.  
- The king was often depicted as an ideal ruler, who embodied the virtues of justice, generosity, courage, and wisdom. He was also praised for his heroic deeds and conquests. Some of the famous kings in ancient India were Rama, Yudhishthira, Ashoka, Chandragupta Maurya, and Harshavardhana.  
- The king was also revered as a patron of arts, sciences, and religions. He supported various schools of thought and encouraged cultural diversity and tolerance. He also built temples, monuments, and cities to glorify his rule and benefit his subjects.  
- The king was not an absolute monarch, but was subject to some checks and balances. He had to follow the law (danda) and the custom (achara) of his land. He also had to consult with his ministers, nobles, and elders on important matters. He could be challenged or replaced by his rivals or subjects if he failed to perform his duties or became tyrannical.  
- The theories of origin of kingship and state in ancient India vary from divine, social contract, to historical and evolutionary. Some sources suggest that the king was chosen by the gods, or by the people, or by his own merit and strength. Some sources also suggest that the state emerged from the need for protection, cooperation, or domination.
