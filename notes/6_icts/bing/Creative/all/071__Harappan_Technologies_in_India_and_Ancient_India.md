### Harappan Technologies in India and Ancient India

- The Harappan civilization, also known as the Indus Valley civilization, was one of the earliest urban cultures of the Indian subcontinent, dating from c. 3300 to 1300 BCE.
- The Harappan civilization extended from modern-day northeast Afghanistan to Pakistan and northwest India, covering an area of about 1.25 million square kilometers.
- The Harappan civilization is known for its impressive achievements in various fields, such as urban planning, architecture, sanitation, drainage, water management, metallurgy, crafts, trade, and writing  .
- Some of the notable Harappan technologies are:

  - **Urban planning**: The Harappan cities and towns were laid out on a grid pattern, with straight and wide streets, rectangular blocks, and standardized sizes of bricks . The cities had fortified citadels, granaries, warehouses, public baths, residential quarters, and workshops . The Harappans also built docks, harbors, and canals for facilitating trade and transportation .
  - **Architecture**: The Harappan architecture was characterized by the use of baked bricks, stone, wood, and metal . The buildings were mostly flat-roofed, multi-storied, and had courtyards, balconies, windows, and staircases . The Harappans also built monumental structures, such as the Great Bath at Mohenjo-daro, which was a large pool lined with bricks and surrounded by colonnades and rooms .
  - **Sanitation and drainage**: The Harappan civilization was remarkable for its high standards of hygiene and sanitation . The Harappans had a sophisticated system of underground drainage, which connected the houses, public baths, latrines, and sewers . The drains were covered with stone slabs and had manholes for cleaning and maintenance . The Harappans also had wells, reservoirs, and tanks for storing and supplying water .
  - **Metallurgy**: The Harappan civilization was a Bronze Age civilization, which used copper, bronze, lead, tin, gold, and silver for making various artifacts . The Harappans were skilled in casting, forging, soldering, and alloying metals . They made tools, weapons, ornaments, vessels, seals, and statues of metal . The Harappans also used iron, but only for minor purposes, such as making beads and nails .
  - **Crafts**: The Harappan civilization was known for its excellence in various crafts, such as pottery, terracotta, beads, seals, jewelry, ivory, shell, bone, and textiles . The Harappans made pottery of different shapes, sizes, and colors, with geometric and floral designs . The Harappans also made terracotta figurines of animals, humans, and gods, some of which may have been used for religious purposes . The Harappans made beads of various materials, such as steatite, carnelian, agate, lapis lazuli, turquoise, and gold, and used them for making necklaces, bracelets, and earrings . The Harappans also made seals of steatite, which were engraved with pictographic symbols and animal motifs, and used them for stamping clay tags and sealing goods and documents . The Harappans also made jewelry of gold, silver, copper, and precious stones, and used them for adornment and status symbols . The Harappans also made ivory combs, shell bangles, bone needles, and cotton textiles .
