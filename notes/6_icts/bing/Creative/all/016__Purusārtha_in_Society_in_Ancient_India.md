#### Purusārtha in Society in Ancient India

- Purusārtha (Sanskrit: पुरुषार्थ) is a key concept in Hinduism that refers to the four proper goals or aims of a human life. 
- The four purusārthas are:
  - Dharma (righteousness, moral values, duty)
  - Artha (prosperity, economic values, wealth)
  - Kama (pleasure, love, psychological values, desire)
  - Moksha (liberation, spiritual values, self-actualization)
- These four goals are considered necessary and sufficient for a fulfilling and happy life, and they are interrelated and interdependent.  
- The concept of purusārtha is different from the Western notions of individualism and libertarianism, as it emphasizes the duties and obligations of a human being towards oneself, society, and the cosmic order, rather than the rights and freedoms.  
- The concept of purusārtha also reflects the diversity and complexity of human nature and aspirations, as it acknowledges the different stages and aspects of life, and the different paths and preferences of individuals.  
- The concept of purusārtha is based on the idea of an ideal world, which is fixed and independent of the empirical world, and which can be realized by following the appropriate means and actions. 
- The concept of purusārtha also provides a standard of social ethics, which helps to evaluate the utility and morality of any activity or pursuit, without detriment to the others.  
- The concept of purusārtha is not rigid or dogmatic, but flexible and dynamic, as it allows for adaptation and innovation according to the changing times and circumstances.  

Some possible mnemonics and learning tricks for the purusārtha are:

- To remember the four goals, one can use the acronym DAKM (Dharma, Artha, Kama, Moksha) or the phrase "Do All Kindly, Mindfully".
- To remember the meaning of each goal, one can use the following associations:
  - Dharma: duty, law, order, virtue, righteousness
  - Artha: wealth, prosperity, success, security, economic values
  - Kama: desire, pleasure, love, joy, psychological values
  - Moksha: liberation, freedom, enlightenment, self-actualization, spiritual values
- To remember the interrelation of the four goals, one can use the following diagram:

```
  Dharma
 /     \
Artha   Kama
 \     /
  Moksha
```

- The diagram shows that Dharma is the foundation and the regulator of the other three goals, as it guides the pursuit of Artha and Kama in a moral and harmonious way, and leads to Moksha as the ultimate goal.  
- The diagram also shows that Artha and Kama are the means and the ends of human life, as they provide the material and emotional resources and satisfaction for living, but they are also subject to limitations and imperfections, and therefore need to be transcended by Moksha.  
- The diagram also shows that Moksha is the culmination and the integration of the other three goals, as it represents the realization of the true self and the ideal world, and the liberation from the cycle of birth and death.
