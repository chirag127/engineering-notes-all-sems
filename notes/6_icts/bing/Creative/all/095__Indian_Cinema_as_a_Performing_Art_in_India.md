### Indian Cinema as a Performing Art in India

- Indian cinema is one of the oldest and largest film industries in the world, with a history dating back to 1913, when the first Indian film, Raja Harischandra, was screened to the public.
- Indian cinema is also one of the most diverse and vibrant forms of performing art, as it reflects the cultural, social, political, and economic realities of India through various genres, languages, styles, and themes.
- Indian cinema has evolved over the decades, influenced by various factors such as the independence movement, the Nehruvian vision, the Italian neo-realism, the Bengali art cinema, the parallel cinema, the new wave cinema, the masala cinema, the regional cinema, and the global cinema.
- Indian cinema has also contributed to the development and recognition of various performing arts in India, such as music, dance, theater, and comedy, by showcasing them on the silver screen and providing a platform for talented artists and performers.
- Some of the notable performing arts that have been featured and popularized by Indian cinema are:

  - Classical music: Indian cinema has incorporated elements of classical music, such as ragas, talas, and instruments, in its songs and background scores, creating a unique fusion of tradition and modernity. Some of the legendary composers who have enriched Indian cinema with classical music are Naushad, S.D. Burman, Ravi Shankar, Madan Mohan, and A.R. Rahman.
  - Folk music: Indian cinema has also celebrated the diversity and richness of folk music, which reflects the regional and cultural identities of India. Some of the genres of folk music that have been used and adapted by Indian cinema are bhangra, lavani, dandiya, qawwali, baul, and bhajan.
  - Classical dance: Indian cinema has showcased the beauty and grace of classical dance, which is based on the ancient treatise of Natya Shastra. Some of the forms of classical dance that have been performed and popularized by Indian cinema are bharatanatyam, kathak, kathakali, kuchipudi, odissi, and manipuri.
  - Folk dance: Indian cinema has also depicted the vibrancy and energy of folk dance, which expresses the joy and celebration of life. Some of the forms of folk dance that have been featured and enjoyed by Indian cinema are garba, bhangra, lavani, ghoomar, dandiya, and chhau.
  - Theater: Indian cinema has a strong connection with theater, as many of the early film-makers, actors, and writers were associated with the stage. Indian cinema has also adapted and inspired many theatrical works, such as plays, musicals, and operas, in its narratives and performances. Some of the prominent theater personalities who have influenced and contributed to Indian cinema are Prithviraj Kapoor, Dadasaheb Phalke, P.C. Barua, V. Shantaram, Bimal Roy, Guru Dutt, and Satyajit Ray .
  - Comedy: Indian cinema has also entertained and enlightened the audiences with comedy, which ranges from slapstick to satire, from parody to social commentary. Indian cinema has produced many iconic comedians and comic actors, who have made people laugh and think with their wit and humor. Some of the legendary comedians and comic actors of Indian cinema are Charlie Chaplin, Kishore Kumar, Mehmood, Johnny Walker, Johnny Lever, and Rajpal Yadav.

- Indian cinema, as a performing art, has not only entertained and educated the masses, but also shaped and reflected the national and cultural identity of India. Indian cinema has also influenced and been influenced by other performing arts and media, such as television, radio, internet, and OTT platforms, creating a dynamic and diverse landscape of artistic expression and communication.
