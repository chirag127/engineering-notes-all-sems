### Astronomy in India and Ancient India

- Astronomy has a long history in the Indian subcontinent, stretching from pre-historic to modern times. 
- Some of the earliest roots of Indian astronomy can be dated to the period of Indus Valley civilization or earlier, where some cosmological concepts and notions of the movement of heavenly bodies and the course of the year are present in the Vedas.  
- Astronomy later developed as a discipline of Vedanga, or one of the "auxiliary disciplines" associated with the study of the Vedas, dating 1500 BCE or older.  
- The oldest known text on Indian astronomy is the Vedanga Jyotisha, dated to 1400–1200 BCE (with the extant form possibly from 700 to 600 BCE). It includes the details about the Sun, Moon, nakshatras, lunisolar calendar, and the rules for determining the positions of planets.  
- Indian astronomy was influenced by Greek astronomy beginning in the 4th century BCE and through the early centuries of the Common Era, for example by the Yavanajataka and the Romaka Siddhanta, which are Sanskrit translations of Greek texts.  
- Indian astronomy flourished in the 5th–6th century CE, with Aryabhata, whose work, Aryabhatiya, represented the pinnacle of astronomical knowledge at the time. The Aryabhatiya is composed of four sections, covering topics such as units of time, methods for determining the positions of planets, the cause of day and night, the calculation of eclipses, the value of pi, and several other cosmological concepts.  
- Later, Indian astronomy significantly influenced Muslim astronomy, Chinese astronomy, European astronomy, and others. 
- Other astronomers of the classical era who further elaborated on Aryabhata's work include Brahmagupta, Varahamihira, and Lalla. 
- An identifiable native Indian astronomical tradition remained active throughout the medieval period and into the 16th or 17th century, especially within the Kerala school of astronomy and mathematics, which made significant contributions to trigonometry and calculus, and improved the heliocentric model of planetary motion. 
- Astronomy in ancient India can be divided into two major classes: Siddhantic astronomy, which is based on mathematical calculations and models, and Samhita astronomy, which is based on observational methods and astrological predictions. 
- Astronomy in ancient India was based on naked-eye observation occasionally supported by simple instruments, such as the gnomon, the clepsydra, the armillary sphere, and the astrolabe.  
- Astronomy in ancient India was closely associated with religion, calendar, rituals, and astrology, and was used for practical purposes such as navigation, agriculture, and architecture.  
- Astronomy in ancient India developed a sophisticated system of lunar mansions (nakshatras), solar months (saura masa), and intercalary months (adhika masa) to harmonize the lunar and solar cycles.  
- Astronomy in ancient India also developed a system of planetary periods (dashas) and sub-periods (bhuktis) to predict the effects of planets on human life.  
- Astronomy in ancient India also made remarkable discoveries and achievements, such as the concept of zero, the decimal system, the place-value system, the approximation of pi, the heliocentric model, the elliptical orbits of planets, the precession of equinoxes, the calculation of the Earth's circumference, the estimation of the Earth's gravity, and the explanation of lunar and solar eclipses.
