### North Indian Literature

- North Indian literature is a broad term that encompasses the literary works of the various languages and cultures of the northern part of India.
- North Indian literature can be divided into two main categories: classical and modern.
- Classical North Indian literature refers to the ancient and medieval writings in Sanskrit, Prakrit, Pali, Apabhramsha, and other related languages. These include the Vedas, the Upanishads, the epics of Mahabharata and Ramayana, the Puranas, the Dharmashastras, the Kavyas, the Natakas, and the various schools of philosophy and grammar.
- Modern North Indian literature refers to the writings in the modern languages of Hindi, Urdu, Punjabi, Kashmiri, Rajasthani, Gujarati, Marathi, and others. These include the works of poets, novelists, dramatists, essayists, and critics who have contributed to the development of the regional and national literatures of India.
- Some of the prominent features of North Indian literature are:

  - The influence of Sanskrit as the language of learning, culture, and religion. Sanskrit has been the source of many literary genres, themes, styles, and vocabulary for the North Indian languages.
  - The diversity of languages, dialects, scripts, and cultures. North Indian literature reflects the rich and varied linguistic and cultural heritage of the region, which has been shaped by the interactions of various ethnic, religious, and social groups.
  - The blending of the indigenous and the foreign. North Indian literature has been enriched by the influences of various foreign cultures, such as the Persian, Arabic, Turkish, Mongol, Afghan, Mughal, British, and others, who have invaded, ruled, or traded with India.
  - The expression of the regional and the national. North Indian literature has both a regional and a national dimension, as it portrays the local and the universal aspects of human life and society. North Indian literature has also played a significant role in the formation of the Indian national identity and consciousness.
  - The continuity and the change. North Indian literature has maintained a continuity with the classical traditions, while also adapting to the changing times and contexts. North Indian literature has responded to the challenges and opportunities of modernity, colonialism, nationalism, globalization, and social movements.

- Some of the notable examples of North Indian literature are:

  - The Bhakti poetry of Kabir, Surdas, Tulsidas, Mirabai, and others, who expressed their devotion to God in simple and lyrical verses in the vernacular languages.
  - The Sufi poetry of Amir Khusrau, Nizamuddin Auliya, Abdul Rahim Khan-i-Khanan, and others, who blended the Islamic and the Indian mystical traditions in their Persian and Hindavi compositions.
  - The Mughal literature of Abul Fazl, Faizi, Zeb-un-Nissa, and others, who wrote histories, biographies, memoirs, and poetry in Persian, reflecting the glory and the diversity of the Mughal empire.
  - The modern Hindi literature of Premchand, Jaishankar Prasad, Mahadevi Varma, Harivansh Rai Bachchan, and others, who wrote novels, short stories, poems, and essays on various social, political, and cultural issues of their times.
  - The modern Urdu literature of Mirza Ghalib, Muhammad Iqbal, Faiz Ahmad Faiz, Saadat Hasan Manto, and others, who wrote poetry, prose, and drama in Urdu, expressing their artistic vision and their views on the Indian and the Muslim identity.
  - The modern Punjabi literature of Bhai Vir Singh, Nanak Singh, Amrita Pritam, Shiv Kumar Batalvi, and others, who wrote novels, poems, and songs in Punjabi, depicting the life and the culture of the Punjab region and the Sikh community.
  - The modern Kashmiri literature of Abdul Ahad Azad, Ghulam Ahmad Mahjoor, Amin Kamil, and others, who wrote poetry and prose in Kashmiri, reflecting the beauty and the turmoil of the Kashmir valley and the Kashmiri people.
  - The modern Rajasthani literature of Vijaydan Detha, Kanhaiyalal Sethia, Komal Kothari, and others, who wrote stories, poems, and folktales in Rajasthani, preserving and promoting the rich and colorful culture of Rajasthan.
