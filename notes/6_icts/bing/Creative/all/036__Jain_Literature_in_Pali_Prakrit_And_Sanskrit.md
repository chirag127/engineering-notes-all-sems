### Jain Literature in Pali,Prakrit And Sanskrit

- Jain literature refers to the literature of the Jain religion, which is a vast and ancient literary tradition, initially transmitted orally. 
- The oldest surviving material is contained in the canonical Jain Agamas, which are written in Ardhamagadhi, a Prakrit (Middle-Indo Aryan) language. 
- Prakrit is a form of Sanskrit, the classical language of ancient India. 
- Pali is another form of Prakrit, which was used in Magadha, the region where the Buddha lived and taught.  
- The Jain tradition believes that their religion is eternal, and the teachings of the first Tirthankara Rishabhanatha existed millions of years ago. 
- The Jain scriptures are based on the divine discourses of the Tirthankaras, who are the enlightened and liberated teachers of Jainism.  
- The discourses are remembered and transmitted by the Ganadharas (chief disciples), and are composed of twelve angas (parts, limbs).  
- The angas are symbolically represented by a tree with twelve branches. 
- The twelfth anga comprises fourteen purvas, which are the oldest and most obscure part of the Jain canon.  
- The Jain canon is divided into two main sects: the Digambara and the Śvētāmbara. 
- The Digambara sect believes that the original Jain scriptures were lost, and only some secondary texts are available. 
- The Śvētāmbara sect believes that they have preserved the original Jain scriptures, and have compiled them into forty-five texts. 
- The canonical texts are written in Ardhamagadhi Prakrit, while the commentaries and later works are written in other languages, such as Sanskrit and Maharashtri Prakrit. 
- Sanskrit is the most widely used language for Jain literature, as it is considered the language of the gods and the scholars. 
- Maharashtri Prakrit is a regional language that was popular among the Jain poets and writers. 
- Jain literature covers a wide range of topics, such as philosophy, ethics, cosmology, history, biography, poetry, drama, art, and science.  
- Jain literature also reflects the diversity and richness of the Jain culture and society, as it was influenced by and influenced other religious and literary traditions of India.
