## Module 3- Indian Religion, Philosophy, and Practices

- India is a diverse and devout country, with six major religious groups: Hinduism, Islam, Christianity, Sikhism, Buddhism and Jainism.
- Hinduism is the oldest and largest religion in India, with about 80% of the population identifying as Hindus. Hinduism is a complex and diverse tradition, with many schools of thought, gods, rituals and texts.
- Hinduism originated from the Vedic religion, which was based on the sacred texts called the Vedas, composed between 1500 and 1000 BCE. The Vedas contain hymns, rituals and cosmological theories, and are considered the highest authority in Hinduism.
- Hinduism also developed other philosophical and religious movements, such as the Upanishads, which are mystical and metaphysical texts that explore the nature of the self and the supreme reality. The Upanishads are the source of many Hindu concepts, such as karma, reincarnation, dharma, moksha and yoga.
- Hinduism also has various schools of philosophy, such as the six orthodox schools (Samkhya, Yoga, Nyaya, Vaisheshika, Mimamsa and Vedanta) and the heterodox schools (Charvaka, Jainism and Buddhism). These schools differ in their views on epistemology, metaphysics, ethics and logic.
- Hinduism also has many sects and traditions, such as Shaivism, Vaishnavism, Shaktism, Smartism and Tantrism, which worship different aspects of the divine and follow different practices and rituals. Hinduism also has a rich and diverse culture, with various forms of art, literature, music, dance, architecture and festivals.
- Islam is the second largest religion in India, with about 15% of the population identifying as Muslims. Islam was introduced to India by Arab traders and invaders in the 7th century CE, and later by Persian and Turkish rulers and Sufi saints.
- Islam is a monotheistic religion, based on the belief in one God (Allah) and the teachings of the prophet Muhammad, as revealed in the holy book, the Quran. Islam also has five pillars of faith, which are the basic duties of every Muslim: shahada (declaration of faith), salat (prayer), zakat (charity), sawm (fasting) and hajj (pilgrimage).
- Islam also has various sects and schools of law, such as Sunni, Shia, Ahmadiyya, Sufi and Barelvi, which differ in their interpretation of the Quran and the hadith (traditions of the prophet). Islam also has a rich and diverse culture, with various forms of art, literature, music, architecture and festivals.
- Christianity is the third largest religion in India, with about 2.5% of the population identifying as Christians. Christianity was introduced to India by various missionaries and traders, such as Thomas the Apostle, who is believed to have arrived in India in the 1st century CE, and later by Portuguese, British and American missionaries.
- Christianity is a monotheistic religion, based on the belief in one God and the teachings of Jesus Christ, as recorded in the holy book, the Bible. Christianity also has various denominations, such as Catholic, Protestant, Orthodox and Anglican, which differ in their doctrines, practices and rituals.
- Christianity also has a rich and diverse culture, with various forms of art, literature, music, architecture and festivals.
- Sikhism is the fourth largest religion in India, with about 1.5% of the population identifying as Sikhs. Sikhism was founded by Guru Nanak in the 15th century CE, and later developed by nine other Gurus, who are considered the spiritual leaders of the Sikh community.
- Sikhism is a monotheistic religion, based on the belief in one God and the teachings of the Gurus, as recorded in the holy book, the Guru Granth Sahib. Sikhism also has five articles of faith, which are the symbols of the Sikh identity: kesh (uncut hair), kangha (comb), kara (steel bracelet), kachera (cotton underwear)
