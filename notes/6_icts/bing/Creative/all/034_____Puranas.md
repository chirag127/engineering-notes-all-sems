# Puranas

- Puranas are a genre of ancient Indian literature that contain stories, legends, myths, and other traditional lore about various aspects of Hinduism. 
- Puranas are considered as the fifth Veda, after the four Vedas (Rig, Yajur, Sama, and Atharva), and are part of the Smriti (remembered) literature, as opposed to the Shruti (heard) literature of the Vedas. 
- Puranas are written in Sanskrit verse, mostly in the form of narrative couplets, and have a simple and easy flowing style. 
- Puranas are classified into two categories: Mahapuranas (great puranas) and Upapuranas (minor puranas). There are 18 Mahapuranas and 18 Upapuranas, although some sources mention more or less. 
- Puranas are also categorized according to the three gunas (qualities) of sattva (goodness), rajas (passion), and tamas (ignorance), and are associated with different deities. The sattvic puranas glorify Vishnu, the rajasic puranas glorify Brahma, and the tamasic puranas glorify Shiva. 
- Puranas cover a wide range of topics, such as cosmology, creation, destruction, genealogy, history, geography, philosophy, ethics, rituals, festivals, temples, and pilgrimage. 
- Puranas are meant to be accessible and enjoyable for the common people, and to impart moral and spiritual teachings through stories and allegories. 
- Puranas are not considered as authoritative or infallible as the Vedas, but they are still revered and respected as sacred texts that reflect the cultural and religious diversity of Hinduism.
