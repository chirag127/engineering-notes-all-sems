## Module 5- Cultural Heritage and Performing Arts

- This module covers the various aspects of Indian culture and heritage related to painting, performing arts and architecture.
- It aims to provide an overview of the historical development, major forms, styles, techniques and themes of these artistic expressions in India.
- It also highlights the significance and contribution of Indian culture and heritage to the world.

### Painting

- Painting is one of the oldest and most diverse forms of artistic expression in India.
- It reflects the rich cultural, religious, social and aesthetic values of the Indian people.
- Painting in India can be broadly classified into two categories: mural painting and miniature painting.
- Mural painting is the art of painting on walls, ceilings or other large surfaces, usually in temples, palaces, caves or public buildings.
- Miniature painting is the art of painting on small surfaces, such as paper, cloth, wood, ivory or palm leaf, usually for personal use or as illustrations for manuscripts, albums or cards.
- Some of the famous examples of mural painting in India are the Ajanta and Ellora caves, the Brihadeshwara temple, the Lepakshi temple and the Mattancherry palace.
- Some of the famous examples of miniature painting in India are the Pala and Jain manuscripts, the Mughal and Rajput paintings, the Deccan and Pahari paintings and the Tanjore and Mysore paintings.

### Performing Arts

- Performing arts are the forms of art that involve the use of body, voice, music, dance, drama or puppetry to convey a message, emotion, story or idea to an audience.
- Performing arts in India are very diverse and have a long and rich history.
- They are influenced by various factors, such as geography, climate, religion, language, caste, community and patronage.
- Performing arts in India can be broadly classified into three categories: music, dance and theatre.
- Music is the art of producing sounds, either vocally or instrumentally, that are pleasing to the ear and express a mood, emotion or idea.
- Music in India can be divided into two main traditions: Hindustani and Carnatic.
- Hindustani music is the classical music of North India, which is based on the system of ragas and talas and has influences from Persian and Islamic cultures.
- Carnatic music is the classical music of South India, which is based on the system of ragas and talas and has influences from Tamil, Telugu and Kannada cultures.
- Some of the famous forms of music in India are Dhrupad, Khayal, Thumri, Ghazal, Qawwali, Bhajan, Kirtan, Sufi, Folk, Film and Pop music.
- Some of the famous musical instruments in India are Sitar, Sarod, Veena, Tanpura, Santoor, Sarangi, Violin, Flute, Shehnai, Harmonium, Tabla, Mridangam, Pakhawaj and Dholak.
- Dance is the art of moving the body, either rhythmically or expressively, to express a mood, emotion or idea.
- Dance in India can be divided into two main categories: classical and folk.
- Classical dance is the dance that follows the rules and principles of the ancient treatise on dance, called Natyashastra, written by Bharata Muni.
- Classical dance in India has eight recognized forms: Bharatanatyam, Kathak, Kathakali, Kuchipudi, Manipuri, Mohiniyattam, Odissi and Sattriya.
- Folk dance is the dance that reflects the local traditions, customs, festivals and lifestyles of the people of a particular region or community.
- Folk dance in India has numerous forms, such as Bhangra, Garba, Dandiya, Lavani, Bihu, Chhau, Ghoomar, Kalbelia and Dollu Kunitha.
- Theatre is the art of performing a scripted or improvised drama, either verbally or non-verbally, to entertain, educate or influence an audience.
- Theatre in India can be divided into two main categories: classical and folk.
- Classical theatre is the theatre that follows the rules and principles of the Natyashastra and has three main forms: Sanskrit, Prakrit and Koodiyattam.
