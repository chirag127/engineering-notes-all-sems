### Sufi movement in India

- Sufism is the mystical branch of Islam that seeks to find the truth of divine love and knowledge through direct personal experience of God. 
- The word Sufi comes from the Arabic word for wool (ṣūf), which was the simple clothing of the early Muslim ascetics. 
- Sufism emerged in the 8th century in Baghdad and spread to Persia, Central Asia, India, and other regions.  
- Sufism in India began in the 13th century with the arrival of various Sufi orders (tariqas) from Central Asia and Persia.  
- The Sufis played an important role in spreading Islam in India through their missionary activities, social services, and mystical teachings.   
- The Sufis also influenced the Indian culture, literature, music, and art with their poetry, songs, stories, and symbols.   
- The Sufis had a tolerant and syncretic approach to other religions, especially Hinduism and Buddhism. They adopted some of their concepts, practices, and terminology.   
- The Sufis also had a close relationship with the Bhakti movement, which was a similar devotional movement among the Hindus. They shared common themes of love, devotion, and mysticism.  
- Some of the famous Sufi saints in India were Khwaja Moinuddin Chishti, Nizamuddin Auliya, Amir Khusrau, Baba Farid, and Rumi.   
- Some of the major Sufi orders in India were Chishti, Suhrawardi, Qadiri, Naqshbandi, and Shattari.   

#### Mnemonics and learning tricks

- To remember the names of the Sufi saints, you can use the acronym KANBAR: Khwaja, Amir, Nizamuddin, Baba, and Rumi.
- To remember the names of the Sufi orders, you can use the acronym CSQNS: Chishti, Suhrawardi, Qadiri, Naqshbandi, and Shattari.
- To remember the meaning of Sufi, you can use the phrase "Seeking Ultimate Faith Inwardly".
