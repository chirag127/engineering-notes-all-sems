#### Harappan Script

- Harappan script, also known as Indus script, is a corpus of symbols produced by the Indus Valley Civilization, which flourished in the northwest of the Indian subcontinent from c. 3500 to 1900 BCE. 
- Harappan script has not been deciphered yet, and its underlying language (s) remains unknown.   There is no bilingual inscription to help decipher the script, and the script shows no significant changes over time. 
- Harappan script symbols have been found on various objects, such as stamp seals, pottery, bronze and copper plates, tools, and weapons.   The majority of the inscriptions are very short, consisting of only a few symbols. 
- Harappan script has over 400 distinct signs,   which are mostly pictographic or ideographic in nature, representing animals, plants, objects, or abstract concepts.  Some signs may also be logographic, representing words or morphemes, or phonetic, representing sounds or syllables. 
- Harappan script is written from right to left, sometimes in a boustrophedon style, where alternate lines are reversed.   The order and arrangement of the signs may indicate some syntactic or grammatical rules, but these are not well understood.  
- Harappan script may have some connection with other ancient scripts, such as Brahmi,   or megalithic culture graffiti symbols of southern and central India and Sri Lanka,   but these links are not conclusive. 
- Harappan script may have a relation to a Dravidian language,    or a language isolate,   or a mix of different language families,   but these hypotheses are not widely accepted. 
- Harappan script is a fascinating and mysterious topic of study, as it may reveal more about the culture, history, and society of the Indus Valley Civilization, one of the oldest and most advanced urban civilizations of the ancient world.
