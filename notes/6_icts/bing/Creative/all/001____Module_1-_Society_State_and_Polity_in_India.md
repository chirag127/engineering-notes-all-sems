# Module 1- Society State and Polity in India

This module aims to provide an overview of the historical and contemporary aspects of the society, state and polity in India. It covers the following topics:

- The origins and evolution of the Indian civil polity, based on the principle of freedom and non-violence, as reflected in the ancient texts and traditions of India .
- The constitutional framework of India, which is a parliamentary secular democratic republic, with a federal system of government and a bicameral legislature.
- The major political parties and ideologies in India, and their role in shaping the electoral and policy outcomes in the country.
- The challenges and opportunities of India's foreign policy, which is based on the Five Principles of Peaceful Coexistence, and seeks to balance its national interests with its global responsibilities.
- The social and cultural diversity of India, which is home to various ethnic, linguistic, religious and regional groups, and the issues of identity, integration and conflict that arise from it.
- The processes of social change and modernization in India, and their impact on the everyday life of the people, the state and the society.

The module aims to enable the students to:

- Understand the historical and cultural roots of the Indian polity and society, and appreciate its uniqueness and diversity.
- Analyze the constitutional and institutional features of the Indian political system, and evaluate its strengths and weaknesses.
- Compare and contrast the different political parties and ideologies in India, and assess their influence on the governance and development of the country.
- Examine the various aspects of India's foreign policy, and its implications for the regional and global order.
- Identify the sources and manifestations of social and cultural diversity in India, and explore the ways of managing and resolving the conflicts and tensions that arise from it.
- Appreciate the dynamics of social change and modernization in India, and their effects on the state and the society.
