# Persian Literature in India

- Persian literature in India refers to the literary works produced in the Persian language by writers, poets, historians, and scholars who lived in the Indian subcontinent or visited it.
- Persian literature in India began with the rise of the Delhi Sultanate in the 13th century, when Persian became the official language of the court and administration. The Delhi Sultanate was ruled by Persian-speaking dynasties, such as the Ghaznavids, the Khiljis, the Tughlaqs, the Sayyids, and the Lodis, who patronized Persian literature and culture.
- Some of the notable Persian writers and poets of the Delhi Sultanate period were Amir Khusrow, Hasan Dehlavi, Ziauddin Barani, Minhaj al-Siraj, Amir Hasan Sijzi, and Amir Khurd.
- Persian literature in India reached its zenith under the Mughal Empire, which spanned from the 16th to the 18th century. The Mughal emperors, especially Akbar, Jahangir, and Shah Jahan, were great patrons of Persian literature and art. They also encouraged the translation of Sanskrit, Hindi, and other Indian languages into Persian, and vice versa.
- Some of the famous Persian writers and poets of the Mughal period were Abul Fazl, Faizi, Nizamuddin Ahmad, Abdul Qadir Badauni, Badauni, Urfi Shirazi, Naziri, Kalim Kashani, Saib Tabrizi, and Bedil.
- Persian literature in India also flourished in the regions that were not directly under the Mughal rule, such as Bengal, Gujarat, Kashmir, Deccan, and Awadh. These regions had their own local Persian literary traditions and styles, influenced by the regional languages and cultures.
- Some of the prominent Persian writers and poets of these regions were Malik Muhammad Jayasi, Mirza Ghalib, Mir Taqi Mir, Mir Dard, Mir Hasan, Mir Anis, Mirza Mazhar Jan-e-Janan, Sirajuddin Ali Khan Arzu, and Ghulam Ali Azad Bilgrami.
- Persian literature in India declined in the 19th century, as the British colonial rule replaced Persian with English as the official language. However, some Persian writers and poets continued to produce works in the language, such as Mirza Asadullah Khan Ghalib, Mirza Muhammad Hadi Ruswa, and Muhammad Iqbal.
- Persian literature in India reflects the rich and diverse history, culture, thought, and social structure of the Indian subcontinent, as well as the spirit of the age. It also shows the influence of Persian literature on the literatures of other languages, such as Urdu, Hindi, Bengali, and Punjabi. Persian literature in India is a valuable source of knowledge and inspiration for scholars and readers alike.
