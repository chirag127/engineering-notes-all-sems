### The Upanishads

- The Upanishads are late Vedic Sanskrit texts that supplied the basis of later Hindu philosophy. 
- They are the most recent part of the Vedas, the oldest scriptures of Hinduism, and deal with meditation, philosophy, consciousness, and ontological knowledge; earlier parts of the Vedas deal with mantras, benedictions, rituals, ceremonies, and sacrifices.   
- They are commonly referred to as Vedanta, which means "the end of the Vedas" or "the highest purpose of the Vedas". 
- The aim of all Upanishads is to investigate the nature of Atman (self), and direct the enquirer toward it. 
- They are also called Upanishad, which means "connection" or "equivalence", and refers to the homology between aspects of the human individual and celestial entities or forces. 
- There are over 200 Upanishads, but only 13 are considered to be the principal or mukhya Upanishads. They are: Isha, Kena, Katha, Prashna, Mundaka, Mandukya, Taittiriya, Aitareya, Chandogya, Brihadaranyaka, Shvetashvatara, Kaushitaki, and Maitri. 
- The Upanishads present diverse ideas and teachings, but some of the common themes are: Brahman (the ultimate reality), Atman (the individual self), Maya (the illusion of duality), Karma (the law of cause and effect), Samsara (the cycle of rebirth), Moksha (the liberation from samsara), and Dharma (the ethical duty). 
- The Upanishads influenced the development of various schools of Hindu philosophy, such as Advaita (non-dualism), Vishishtadvaita (qualified non-dualism), and Dvaita (dualism). They also inspired other religious traditions, such as Buddhism, Jainism, Sikhism, and Neo-Vedanta. 
- The Upanishads are regarded as the source of the highest wisdom and spiritual insight in Hinduism, and are revered by Hindus of all sects and traditions. They are also considered to be among the world's oldest and most profound philosophical-religious texts.
