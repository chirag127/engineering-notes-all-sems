### Seals as Cultural Heritage of India

- Seals are small objects made of stone, metal, or other materials that have a carved or engraved design on one side and a projection or handle on the other side.
- Seals were used to make impressions on soft materials such as clay, wax, or metal to indicate ownership, identity, authority, or authenticity of documents, goods, or containers.
- Seals are one of the most important sources of information about the ancient Indus Valley Civilization, which flourished in the northwest of India and Pakistan between 2500 and 1900 BCE.
- Thousands of seals have been found in the archaeological sites of the Indus Valley Civilization, such as Mohenjo-daro, Harappa, Lothal, Kalibangan, and Dholavira.
- The seals of the Indus Valley Civilization are mostly square or rectangular in shape, measuring about 2-3 cm on a side, although some are circular or cylindrical. They are made of steatite, a soft stone that can be easily carved, and sometimes coated with alkali and baked to make them harder and more durable.
- The seals of the Indus Valley Civilization have two main features: an animal motif and a script. The animal motif is usually a single animal shown in profile, such as a bull, a unicorn, a buffalo, a rhinoceros, an elephant, a tiger, or a crocodile. Some seals also show composite animals, such as a bull with an elephant's trunk, or a unicorn with a fish tail. The animal motif may represent a deity, a clan symbol, a totem, or a trade emblem.
- The script of the Indus Valley Civilization is one of the most mysterious aspects of the seals. It consists of about 400 different signs, some of which are pictographic, representing objects or concepts, and some of which are abstract, representing sounds or syllables. The script has not been deciphered yet, despite many attempts by scholars and experts. The script may have been used to write a language or languages related to the Dravidian, Indo-Aryan, or Sumerian families, or a language isolate with no known relatives. The script may have been used to record names, titles, dates, places, transactions, or religious messages.
- The seals of the Indus Valley Civilization were used for various purposes, such as sealing documents, goods, or containers, marking ownership or identity, indicating quality or origin, or expressing religious beliefs or rituals. The seals were also used as amulets, ornaments, or tokens of exchange. The seals were widely circulated within the Indus Valley Civilization and beyond, as they have been found in other regions such as Mesopotamia, Persia, Afghanistan, and Central Asia, indicating the extensive trade and cultural contacts of the Indus people.
- The seals of the Indus Valley Civilization are part of the cultural heritage of India, as they reflect the artistic, technological, linguistic, and religious achievements of one of the earliest and most advanced civilizations of the world. The seals also provide valuable clues about the social, economic, and political organization of the Indus Valley Civilization, as well as its relations with other cultures and regions. The seals are a source of pride and inspiration for the modern Indians, as they represent the ancient roots and diversity of their culture and history.
