### Physics in India and Ancient India

Physics is the branch of science that deals with the nature and properties of matter and energy. Physics in India and ancient India has a long and rich history, dating back to the Vedic period (1500-500 BCE). Some of the main contributions of Indian physics are:

- The concept of **paramanu** (atom) was proposed by the Vaisheshika school of philosophy, founded by Kanada (6th century BCE). According to this school, all matter is composed of indivisible and indestructible atoms, which can combine in different ways to form complex substances. The atoms are also classified into nine types: earth, water, fire, air, ether, time, space, mind and soul  .
- The concept of **shunya** (zero) was developed by the Indian mathematicians and astronomers, such as Aryabhata (5th century CE), Brahmagupta (7th century CE) and Bhaskara (12th century CE). Zero was used as a placeholder, a symbol and a number in the decimal system of numeration, which was also invented by the Indians. Zero also represented the concept of emptiness or nothingness in the Indian philosophy  .
- The concept of **anu** (subatomic particle) was suggested by the Nyaya school of logic, founded by Gautama (2nd century CE). According to this school, the atoms are not the ultimate constituents of matter, but they are further divisible into smaller particles, which have different properties and motions. The Nyaya school also proposed the idea of **parinama** (transformation) of matter, which is similar to the modern concept of chemical reaction  .
- The concept of **prana** (life force) was introduced by the Upanishads (7th-5th century BCE), which are the philosophical texts of the Vedas. According to the Upanishads, prana is the vital energy that pervades all living beings and the universe. Prana is also associated with the concept of **chakras** (energy centers) and **nadis** (energy channels) in the human body, which are the basis of the Indian systems of medicine and yoga  .
- The concept of **akasha** (ether) was developed by the Samkhya school of philosophy, founded by Kapila (6th century BCE). According to this school, akasha is the subtlest of the five elements (earth, water, fire, air and ether) that constitute the physical universe. Akasha is also the source of sound and the medium of transmission of sound waves. The Samkhya school also proposed the idea of **gunas** (qualities) of matter, which are three in number: sattva (purity), rajas (activity) and tamas (inertia)  .
- The concept of **nirvana** (liberation) was taught by the Buddha (6th-5th century BCE), who was the founder of Buddhism. According to the Buddha, nirvana is the state of freedom from suffering and ignorance, which can be attained by following the eightfold path of right view, right intention, right speech, right action, right livelihood, right effort, right mindfulness and right concentration. Nirvana is also the ultimate goal of the Indian spiritual traditions, such as Hinduism, Jainism and Sikhism  .

These are some of the main concepts of physics in India and ancient India, which show the remarkable depth and breadth of the Indian scientific thought. These concepts also have many parallels and similarities with the modern discoveries and theories of physics, such as quantum mechanics, relativity, thermodynamics, electromagnetism and cosmology. The Indian physics also influenced the development of physics in other civilizations, such as China, Persia, Arabia and Europe  .
