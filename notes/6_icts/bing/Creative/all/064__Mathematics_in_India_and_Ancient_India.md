### Mathematics in India and Ancient India

- Mathematics in India and Ancient India is a rich and diverse field of study that spans from the ancient times to the modern era.
- Mathematics in India and Ancient India has contributed to various branches of mathematics, such as arithmetic, algebra, geometry, trigonometry, calculus, combinatorics, and number theory.
- Mathematics in India and Ancient India has also influenced the development of mathematics in other regions, such as the Middle East, China, and Europe.
- Some of the main features and achievements of mathematics in India and Ancient India are:

  - The development of the decimal place-value system and the use of zero as a number, which are the basis of the modern number system  .
  - The discovery of negative numbers, fractions, irrational numbers, and the rules of operations on them .
  - The invention of algebraic methods and techniques, such as the quadratic formula, the solution of linear and quadratic equations, the concept of coefficients and variables, and the use of symbols and notation .
  - The advancement of geometry and trigonometry, such as the construction of geometric shapes, the measurement of angles and lengths, the calculation of areas and volumes, the derivation of the Pythagorean theorem, and the definition of the sine and cosine functions  .
  - The formulation of calculus and infinite series, such as the approximation of pi, the calculation of the area of a circle, the summation of arithmetic and geometric progressions, and the estimation of the value of e.
  - The exploration of combinatorics and number theory, such as the enumeration of permutations and combinations, the study of magic squares and patterns, the investigation of prime numbers and divisibility, and the discovery of the Fibonacci sequence and the Pascal triangle.

- Some of the prominent mathematicians and scholars of mathematics in India and Ancient India are:

  - Pingala (3rd or 2nd century BCE), who wrote the Chandaḥśāstra, a treatise on prosody and metrics, which contains the earliest known description of the binary number system and the use of binary arithmetic.
  - Aryabhata (476–550 CE), who wrote the Āryabhaṭīya, a seminal work on astronomy and mathematics, which contains the earliest known use of the decimal place-value system, the approximation of pi, the solution of quadratic equations, and the calculation of the sine and cosine functions.
  - Brahmagupta (598–668 CE), who wrote the Brahmasphuṭasiddhānta, a comprehensive work on astronomy and mathematics, which contains the first explicit recognition of zero as a number, the rules of operations on zero and negative numbers, the solution of linear and quadratic indeterminate equations, and the formula for the area of a cyclic quadrilateral.
  - Bhaskara II (1114–1185 CE), who wrote the Siddhāntaśiromaṇi, a monumental work on astronomy and mathematics, which contains the proof of the Pythagorean theorem, the solution of cubic equations, the derivation of the differential of the sine function, and the development of the chakravala method for solving indeterminate equations.
  - Madhava of Sangamagrama (c. 1340–1425 CE), who is considered as the founder of the Kerala school of mathematics and astronomy, which made significant contributions to calculus and infinite series, such as the power series expansions of the sine, cosine, and arctangent functions, the infinite series expression of pi, and the tests for convergence of series.
  - Ramanujan (1887–1920 CE), who is regarded as one of the greatest mathematicians of the 20th century, who made remarkable discoveries and conjectures in number theory, modular forms, partitions, continued fractions, and infinite series, many of which have been proved or confirmed by modern mathematics.
