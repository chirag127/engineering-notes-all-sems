#### The representation of Women in Historical traditions in Society in Ancient India

- Women in ancient India were considered as the gift to the society and enjoyed a significant role in various aspects of life, such as religion, education, politics, arts and culture .
- Women in ancient India were also associated with the concepts of sakti (energy/power) and prakrti (nature/matter), which reflect the duality of the female as both the bestower and the destroyer, the creative and the destructive, the active and the passive.
- Women in ancient India had the opportunity to study the Vedas, the sacred texts of Hinduism, and some of them became scholars, teachers, nuns and poets. Some examples of learned women in ancient India are Gargi, Maitreyi, Sulabha, Sanghamitra, Jayanti and Vijayabhattarika .
- Women in ancient India also participated in political affairs, either as rulers or as administrators. Some examples of powerful women in ancient India are Nayanika, the Satavahana queen; Prabhavati, the Vakataka princess; Vijayabhattarika, the Chalukya provincial ruler; and Didda, the Kashmiri queen .
- Women in ancient India enjoyed some freedom and rights, such as choosing their own partners, inheriting property, remarrying and divorcing. They also had access to various professions, such as merchants, artisans, farmers, soldiers and spies .
- However, women in ancient India also faced some challenges and discrimination, such as the practice of sati (widow immolation), child marriage, polygamy, purdah (veil), devadasi (temple prostitution) and dowry. These practices were influenced by the patriarchal norms of the society, the caste system, the religious laws and the foreign invasions  .
- The status of women in ancient India varied according to the time period, the region, the class and the religion. Women in ancient India were not a homogenous group, but had diverse experiences and roles. Women in ancient India contributed to the rich and complex history and culture of the subcontinent    .
