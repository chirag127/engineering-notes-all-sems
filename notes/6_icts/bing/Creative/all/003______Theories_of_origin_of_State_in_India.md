# Theories of origin of State in India

The origin of state in India is a subject of debate among scholars of political science and history. There are different theories that attempt to explain how the state emerged and evolved in ancient India. Some of the prominent theories are:

- **Social Contract Theory**: This theory holds that the state was formed by a voluntary agreement among individuals who gave up some of their natural rights and freedoms in exchange for security and order. The state was seen as a human creation that served the common good of the people. This theory is found in some of the ancient Indian texts such as the Mahabharata, the Arthashastra, and the Manusmriti. According to this theory, the state was based on the consent of the governed and the rule of law .

- **Divine Origin Theory**: This theory maintains that the state was established by the will of God or some supernatural power. The state was seen as a sacred institution that derived its authority and legitimacy from the divine mandate. The king was considered as the representative or the incarnation of God on earth. This theory is also found in some of the ancient Indian texts such as the Vedas, the Ramayana, and the Puranas. According to this theory, the state was based on the obedience of the subjects and the duty of the king .

- **Organic Theory**: This theory suggests that the state was a natural and inevitable outcome of the social and historical development of human society. The state was seen as a living organism that grew and changed according to the needs and aspirations of the people. The state was also influenced by the geographical and cultural factors of the region. This theory is supported by some of the historical and archaeological evidence of the emergence and evolution of the state in ancient India. According to this theory, the state was based on the adaptation of the society and the environment .

These are some of the main theories of origin of state in India. However, none of these theories can fully explain the complexity and diversity of the state formation process in ancient India. The state in ancient India was not a monolithic or uniform entity, but a dynamic and heterogeneous phenomenon that varied across time and space. Therefore, it is important to study the state in ancient India from a multidisciplinary and comparative perspective.
