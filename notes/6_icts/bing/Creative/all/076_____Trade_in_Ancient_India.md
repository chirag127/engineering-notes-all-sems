# Trade in Ancient India

- Trade in ancient India was an important source of commerce and cultural exchange since the Harappan civilization (c. 2600-1900 BCE) .
- The Harappans traded gold, copper, ivory, beads, textiles, spices, and other goods with Mesopotamia, Egypt, Persia, and Central Asia .
- The Harappans used seals, weights, and measures to regulate trade and had a well-developed system of urban planning, sanitation, and drainage .
- After the decline of the Harappan civilization, new cities emerged in the Gangetic plains, such as Pataliputra, Ujjain, Puhar, and Mathura .
- These cities became centers of trade, administration, religion, and culture in the Mauryan (c. 321-185 BCE) and Gupta (c. 320-550 CE) periods .
- Trade in ancient India involved both inland and overseas routes, connecting India with China, Southeast Asia, Rome, Greece, and Arabia   .
- The main commodities traded in ancient India were textiles, spices, precious metals, gems, pearls, ivory, silk, perfumes, and medicines   .
- Trade in ancient India was facilitated by the development of roads, ports, markets, coinage, guilds, and banking  .
- Trade in ancient India also influenced the spread of Indian culture, religion, art, literature, and science to other regions of the world  .
- Trade in ancient India was a dynamic and complex phenomenon that reflected the economic, social, and political changes in the subcontinent and beyond .
