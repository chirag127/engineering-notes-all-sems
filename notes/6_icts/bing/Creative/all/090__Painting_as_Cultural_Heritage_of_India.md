### Painting as Cultural Heritage of India

- Painting is one of the oldest and most diverse forms of artistic expression in India. It reflects the rich cultural and religious heritage of the country, as well as the influences of various historical periods and regions.
- The earliest known paintings in India are the prehistoric rock art found in places like Bhimbetka rock shelters in Madhya Pradesh, dating back to at least 290,000 years ago. These paintings depict animals, humans, and geometric patterns, and are believed to have ritualistic or symbolic meanings.
- The Indus Valley Civilization (2500-1800 BCE) produced terracotta and bronze figurines, seals, and pottery with painted motifs. These artworks show the sophistication and urbanization of the civilization, as well as its trade and cultural contacts with other regions.
- The rise of Buddhism and Jainism in the 6th century BCE led to the development of religious paintings, especially in the form of murals and frescoes in caves and monasteries. The most famous examples are the Ajanta and Ellora caves in Maharashtra, which depict scenes from the life of Buddha and the Jataka tales, as well as Hindu and Jain deities and legends.
- The Gupta period (4th-6th century CE) is considered the golden age of Indian art, as it witnessed the flourishing of classical styles and techniques in painting, sculpture, and architecture. The paintings of this period are characterized by grace, elegance, and naturalism, and often depict courtly and romantic themes. The Bagh caves in Madhya Pradesh and the Sittanavasal caves in Tamil Nadu are some of the surviving examples of Gupta paintings.
- The medieval period (7th-15th century CE) saw the emergence of various regional styles and schools of painting, influenced by the local traditions, cultures, and religions. Some of the prominent styles are:

  - Pala painting: A Buddhist school of painting that originated in eastern India under the Pala dynasty (8th-12th century CE). It is known for its intricate and colorful illustrations of Buddhist scriptures and deities, using palm leaves, cloth, or paper as the medium. The paintings are rich in symbolism and iconography, and often include floral and geometric borders.
  - Rajput painting: A Hindu school of painting that developed in western and northern India under the Rajput kingdoms (16th-19th century CE). It is influenced by the Mughal, Persian, and Deccan styles, and depicts themes from Hindu mythology, epics, poetry, and music, as well as courtly and romantic scenes. The paintings are usually small in size, and use bright colors, fine lines, and gold and silver embellishments. Some of the famous sub-schools of Rajput painting are Mewar, Bundi, Kota, Marwar, Bikaner, Jaipur, and Kangra.
  - Mughal painting: A Muslim school of painting that originated in northern India under the Mughal Empire (16th-19th century CE). It is influenced by the Persian, Indian, and European styles, and depicts themes from history, literature, religion, and nature, as well as portraits and miniatures. The paintings are known for their realism, perspective, and detail, and use rich colors, delicate brushwork, and gold and silver inlays. Some of the famous Mughal painters are Bihzad, Abd al-Samad, Daswanth, Basawan, and Govardhan.
  - Deccan painting: A Muslim school of painting that developed in southern India under the Deccan Sultanates (16th-17th century CE). It is influenced by the Persian, Turkish, and Indian styles, and depicts themes from history, literature, religion, and nature, as well as portraits and miniatures. The paintings are known for their vibrant colors, expressive figures, and decorative motifs, and use paper, cloth, or leather as the medium. Some of the famous Deccan painters are Farrukh Beg, Nizam Ali Khan, and Muhammad Khan.
  - Tanjore painting: A Hindu school of painting that originated in Tamil Nadu under the Maratha rulers (17th-19th century CE). It is influenced by the Vijayanagara, Nayaka, and Maratha styles, and depicts themes from Hindu mythology, epics, and saints, as well as portraits and miniatures. The paintings are known for their use of gold foil, gems, and glass beads, and their bright colors, ornate backgrounds, and stylized figures.
