# Socio-religious reform movement of 19th century in India

- The socio-religious reform movement of 19th century in India was a wave of reforming activities that aimed to modernize and rationalize the Indian society and culture in the light of western education and liberal ideas .
- The movement started in Bengal and soon spread to other parts of India, influencing the minds of the people through various organizations, such as Brahmo Samaj, Arya Samaj, Prarthana Samaj, Aligarh movement, etc .
- The main causes of the movement were:
  - The impact of British colonial rule, which exposed the Indians to new political, economic, social and cultural changes, and challenged their traditional values and institutions .
  - The influence of western education, which introduced the Indians to the ideas of rationalism, humanism, liberalism, democracy, nationalism, etc., and inspired them to question the superstitions, customs and practices of their own society .
  - The contact with Christian missionaries, who criticized the Hindu religion and society, and tried to convert the Indians to Christianity, which provoked a defensive and reformist response from the Hindus .
  - The emergence of a new class of educated and enlightened Indians, who were influenced by both the western and the Indian traditions, and who took the lead in initiating and carrying out the reforms .
- The main types of the movement were:
  - The reformist movements, which aimed to reform the Hindu religion and society from within, by removing the evils and defects, and by reviving the ancient and pure values and principles. Examples of such movements are Brahmo Samaj, Prarthana Samaj, Ramakrishna Mission, etc .
  - The revivalist movements, which aimed to revive the Hindu religion and society by going back to the original sources and scriptures, and by rejecting the western influences and innovations. Examples of such movements are Arya Samaj, Ram Mohan Roy, etc .
  - The separatist movements, which aimed to separate from the Hindu religion and society, and to form a new identity and community, based on a different interpretation of religion and history. Examples of such movements are Aligarh movement, Singh Sabha movement, etc .
- The main features of the movement were:
  - The movement was mainly led by the upper-caste and upper-class Hindus, who had access to western education and opportunities, and who had a sense of social responsibility and moral duty .
  - The movement was mainly supported by the urban and educated sections of the society, who were exposed to the new ideas and influences, and who were dissatisfied with the existing conditions and practices .
  - The movement was mainly focused on the issues of social reform, such as caste system, child marriage, widow remarriage, women's education, polygamy, sati, etc., which were seen as the major obstacles to the progress and development of the society .
  - The movement was also concerned with the issues of religious reform, such as idolatry, polytheism, rituals, priesthood, superstitions, etc., which were seen as the sources of corruption and degeneration of the religion .
  - The movement was influenced by both the western and the Indian traditions, and tried to strike a balance between the two, by adopting the best of both, and by rejecting the worst of both .
  - The movement had a positive impact on the Indian society and culture, as it brought about many social and religious changes, and also contributed to the growth of Indian nationalism and the freedom struggle  .
