# Pyrotechnics in India and Ancient India

Pyrotechnics is the art and science of creating and using fireworks for entertainment, military, or ceremonial purposes. Pyrotechnics has a long and rich history in India, dating back to the medieval period. Here are some points to note about pyrotechnics in India and ancient India:

- One of the earliest notes of pyrotechnical shows in India is made by Abdur Razzaq, the ambassador of the Timurid Sultan Shahrukh to the court of the Vijayanagar king Devaraya II in 1443. He wrote that the king had a magnificent display of fireworks on the occasion of his birthday, which lasted for three hours and illuminated the whole city.
- Fireworks and pyrotechnic shows existed as a form of royal entertainment in many medieval Indian kingdoms during festivals, events and special occasions like weddings. Manufacturing formulas for fireworks describing pyrotechnic mixtures are found within Kautukachintamani, a Sanskrit volume by Gajapati Prataparudradeva (1497-1539), a reputed king of Orissa .
- Gunpowder, an essential ingredient of fire crackers, was unknown in ancient India. It was introduced to India by the Mongols and the Arabs in the 13th century. The Indian alchemists and metallurgists experimented with gunpowder and developed various types of fireworks, such as rockets, bombs, mines, fountains, wheels, and sparklers.
- The Mughal emperors, especially Akbar, Jahangir, and Shah Jahan, were fond of fireworks and used them extensively in their celebrations and battles. They employed skilled pyrotechnicians from Persia, Turkey, and China to create spectacular displays of fireworks. The Mughal court also patronized the development of indigenous fireworks, such as the atishbazi, which were made of bamboo tubes filled with gunpowder and iron filings.
- The British colonial rulers also adopted the use of fireworks in India, especially during the Diwali festival, which coincided with their own Guy Fawkes Night. They imported fireworks from China and Europe and also encouraged the local manufacture of fireworks. The Indian pyrotechnic industry flourished under the British rule and exported fireworks to various parts of the world.
- Today, India is one of the largest producers and consumers of fireworks in the world. The Indian pyrotechnic industry is concentrated in the town of Sivakasi in Tamil Nadu, which produces about 90% of the country's fireworks. The Indian fireworks are known for their variety, quality, and affordability. They are used for religious, cultural, and social occasions, as well as for sports and entertainment events .
