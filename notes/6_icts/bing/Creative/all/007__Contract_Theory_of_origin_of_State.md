#### Contract Theory of origin of State

- The contract theory of origin of state is a theory that explains how the state emerged from a voluntary agreement or contract among individuals who lived in a state of nature, without any political authority or law.
- The theory has been developed by various philosophers, such as Thomas Hobbes, John Locke, Jean-Jacques Rousseau, and others, who had different views on the nature of the state of nature, the reasons for entering into a contract, and the form and function of the state.
- The main features of the contract theory of origin of state are:

  - The state of nature was a condition of human existence before the formation of the state, where people enjoyed natural rights and freedoms, but also faced insecurity, violence, and uncertainty.
  - The contract was a deliberate and rational act of individuals who decided to give up some of their natural rights and freedoms in exchange for the protection and order provided by the state.
  - The state was the result of the contract, which established a political community with a common authority, law, and obligation. The state had the power to enforce the contract and to protect the rights and interests of the people.
  - The contract was based on the consent of the people, who agreed to obey the state as long as it fulfilled its duties and respected their rights. The people also had the right to resist, revolt, or change the state if it violated the contract or became tyrannical.

- The contract theory of origin of state has been influential in the history of political thought and practice, as it provided a justification for the legitimacy of the state, the sovereignty of the people, the rule of law, and the principles of democracy and human rights.
- The contract theory of origin of state has also been criticized for its historical inaccuracy, logical inconsistency, moral ambiguity, and ideological bias. Some of the criticisms are:

  - The theory has no historical evidence or empirical support, as there is no record of any actual contract or state of nature in human history.
  - The theory assumes a radical and unrealistic change from the state of nature to the state, without explaining the process, the conditions, or the consequences of such a transition.
  - The theory ignores the diversity, complexity, and evolution of human societies and cultures, and imposes a universal and abstract model of the state that does not reflect the reality or the needs of different people and places.
  - The theory rationalizes the domination, exploitation, and oppression of some groups by others, as it legitimizes the unequal distribution of power, wealth, and rights among the people and the state.
