### Fairs and Festivals as Cultural Heritage of India

- India is a land of diversity and culture, where various religions, languages, and traditions coexist in harmony. One of the ways to witness the rich cultural heritage of India is through its fairs and festivals, which are celebrated with great enthusiasm and fervor throughout the year.
- Fairs and festivals in India are not only occasions of religious and cultural significance, but also of social and economic importance. They provide opportunities for people to interact, exchange, and trade with each other, as well as to showcase their arts, crafts, and skills. They also reflect the regional and seasonal variations of the country, as well as the historical and mythological events associated with them.
- Some of the major fairs and festivals of India are:

  - **Makar Sankranti**: It is celebrated on January 14, marking the transition of the sun into the zodiac sign of Capricorn, and the beginning of the harvest season. It is known by different names and observed with different rituals in different parts of the country, such as Pongal in Tamil Nadu, Uttarayan in Gujarat, Magh Bihu in Assam, and Lohri in Punjab. Kite flying, bonfires, and feasting are some of the common features of this festival .
  - **Pongal**: It is a four-day harvest festival celebrated by the Tamils in mid-January. It is dedicated to the sun god, the earth goddess, and the cattle, who are thanked for their blessings and bounty. The main dish of this festival is Pongal, a sweet or savory rice pudding cooked in earthen pots. Other attractions include bull-taming sport of Jallikattu, folk dances, and kolam (rangoli) designs .
  - **Rann Utsav**: It is a cultural extravaganza that takes place in the white salt desert of Kutch in Gujarat, from November to February. It showcases the vibrant and colorful culture of the state, with folk music, dance, food, handicrafts, and adventure sports. It also offers a chance to witness the natural beauty of the region, such as the flamingos, the full moon, and the sunrise and sunset over the salt crystals .
  - **Onam**: It is the most important festival of Kerala, celebrated in the month of August or September. It commemorates the mythical king Mahabali, who is believed to visit his subjects once a year during this time. The festival is marked by boat races, flower carpets, elephant processions, traditional dances, and a grand feast called Onasadya .
  - **Navratri, Durga Puja, and Dussehra**: These are festivals that celebrate the victory of good over evil, and the power of the goddess Durga, who slayed the demon Mahishasura. They are celebrated in the month of September or October, for nine or ten days, depending on the region. Navratri is celebrated with fasting, worshipping, and dancing (Garba or Dandiya) in Gujarat and other parts of North and West India. Durga Puja is celebrated with elaborate pandals, idols, and cultural programs in West Bengal and other parts of East India. Dussehra is celebrated with burning the effigies of Ravana, Kumbhakarna, and Meghnad, and enacting the scenes from Ramayana in North and Central India .
  - **Diwali**: It is the festival of lights, celebrated in the month of October or November. It signifies the triumph of light over darkness, and the return of Lord Rama to Ayodhya after defeating Ravana. It is celebrated by lighting lamps, candles, and fireworks, exchanging gifts and sweets, and worshipping Lakshmi, the goddess of wealth and prosperity .
  - **Pushkar Mela**: It is one of the largest and oldest cattle fairs in the world, held in the holy town of Pushkar in Rajasthan, in the month of November. It attracts thousands of camels, horses, and cattle, as well as traders, pilgrims, and tourists. The fair offers a glimpse of the rural and nomadic lifestyle of Rajasthan, with various competitions, exhibitions, and cultural events .
