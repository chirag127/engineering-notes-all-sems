# Buddhist Literature in Pali, Prakrit and Sanskrit

- Buddhist literature is the collection of writings that preserve the teachings and traditions of Buddhism.
- Buddhist literature can be divided into two main categories: canonical and non-canonical.
- Canonical literature refers to the texts that are considered authoritative and authentic by the different schools and sects of Buddhism. Non-canonical literature refers to the texts that are not accepted as authoritative by all Buddhists, but may have historical, cultural, or doctrinal significance.
- The canonical literature of Buddhism is also known as the Tripitaka or the Three Baskets, which consist of the Vinaya Pitaka (the basket of discipline), the Sutta Pitaka (the basket of discourses), and the Abhidhamma Pitaka (the basket of higher teachings).
- The canonical literature of Buddhism was originally composed in various languages and dialects, such as Pali, Prakrit, Sanskrit, Tibetan, Chinese, and others. The language and style of the canonical texts may vary depending on the region, time, and school of Buddhism.
- Pali is an ancient language that belongs to the Indo-Aryan branch of the Indo-European language family. It is closely related to Sanskrit, but has some distinctive features, such as the absence of the grammatical gender and the use of the retroflex consonants. Pali is the language of the Theravada school of Buddhism, which is dominant in Sri Lanka and Southeast Asia. Pali is also the language of the oldest and most complete collection of the Buddhist canonical literature, known as the Pali Canon or the Tipitaka.
- Prakrit is a generic term for the vernacular languages and dialects that were spoken in ancient India, in contrast to the classical and literary language of Sanskrit. Prakrit languages and dialects include Magadhi, Ardhamagadhi, Sauraseni, Maharastri, Gandhari, and others. Prakrit is the language of the early Buddhist literature, especially the Sutta Pitaka and the Vinaya Pitaka, which are attributed to the Buddha and his disciples. Prakrit is also the language of the Jain canonical literature and some of the Hindu epics and dramas.
- Sanskrit is an ancient language that belongs to the Indo-Aryan branch of the Indo-European language family. It is the classical and literary language of ancient India, and the language of the Hindu scriptures, such as the Vedas, the Upanishads, the Puranas, and the Mahabharata. Sanskrit is also the language of the Mahayana and Vajrayana schools of Buddhism, which are dominant in Central and East Asia. Sanskrit is the language of the later and more elaborate Buddhist canonical literature, such as the Mahayana Sutras, the Tantras, and the commentaries. Sanskrit is also the language of the Buddhist philosophical and scholastic literature, such as the Abhidharma, the Madhyamaka, the Yogacara, and the logic and epistemology schools.
