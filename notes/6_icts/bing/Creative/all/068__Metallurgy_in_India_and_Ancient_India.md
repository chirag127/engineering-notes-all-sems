### Metallurgy in India and Ancient India

- Metallurgy is the science and art of extracting and processing metals from ores and other sources, and shaping them into useful forms and products.
- Metallurgy in India and ancient India has a long and rich history, dating back to the 3rd millennium BCE or earlier, and involving various metals such as copper, bronze, iron, steel, zinc, gold, silver, and mercury.
- Some of the main features and achievements of metallurgy in India and ancient India are:

  - Copper metallurgy in India dates back to the Chalcolithic societies in the subcontinent, according to spectrometric tests on copper ore samples discovered from ancient mine pits at Khetri in Rajasthan and metal samples cut from representative Harappan artefacts unearthed from Mitathal in Haryana.
  - The Rigveda, one of the oldest texts in the world, mentions the term ayas (metal), and describes various metalworking techniques, such as smelting, forging, casting, soldering, and alloying.
  - Bronze metallurgy in India was well developed by the Indus Valley Civilization (c. 2500-1900 BCE), which produced sophisticated bronze sculptures, tools, weapons, and vessels, using the lost-wax casting method.
  - Iron metallurgy in India may have begun as early as 1800 BCE, according to archaeological evidence from sites such as Malhar, Dadupur, Raja Nala Ka Tila, and Lahuradewa in Uttar Pradesh . The iron technology in India was independent of the West, and involved the direct reduction method of iron extraction, using bloomery furnaces.
  - Steel metallurgy in India was highly advanced, and involved the production of high-quality steel, called wootz or fūlāḏ, by the crucible technique. This method involved melting high-purity wrought iron, charcoal, and glass in crucibles, and producing steel ingots with high carbon content and distinctive patterns. This steel was exported to various parts of Asia and Europe, and was admired for its hardness, toughness, and edge retention .
  - Zinc metallurgy in India was also remarkable, and involved the distillation technique of extracting zinc from its ores. This technique was developed around 1200 CE at Zawar in Rajasthan, and was unique to India until the 18th century. Zinc was used for making brass, an alloy of copper and zinc, and for medicinal purposes .
  - Gold and silver metallurgy in India was also well established, and involved the extraction, purification, and alloying of these precious metals. Gold and silver were used for making coins, jewellery, ornaments, and vessels, and for artistic and religious purposes. India was one of the major sources of gold and silver for the ancient and medieval world .
  - Mercury metallurgy in India was also practiced, and involved the extraction and purification of mercury from its ores, such as cinnabar. Mercury was used for making amalgams, an alloy of mercury and another metal, and for alchemical and medicinal purposes. India was one of the earliest civilizations to use mercury for these purposes .

- Some of the examples of the skill and artistry of the Indian metalworkers are:

  - The iron pillar of Delhi, a 7.21 m high and 6.5 tonne heavy pillar, made of wrought iron, that dates back to the 4th century CE, and is remarkable for its corrosion resistance and metallurgical structure.
  - The bronze statue of Nataraja, a representation of the Hindu god Shiva as the cosmic dancer, that dates back to the Chola period (c. 9th-13th century CE), and is considered one of the finest examples of Indian bronze art, with its intricate details and dynamic pose.
