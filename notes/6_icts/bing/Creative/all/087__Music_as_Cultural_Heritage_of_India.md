### Music as Cultural Heritage of India

- Music is an integral part of Indian culture and has a long and rich history that spans from the ancient Vedic times to the modern era.
- Music in India can be broadly classified into two main traditions: Hindustani music and Carnatic music. Hindustani music is the classical music of North India, while Carnatic music is the classical music of South India.
- Both Hindustani and Carnatic music are based on the concept of raga, which is a melodic framework that defines the notes, intervals, ornaments, and mood of a musical piece. A raga can evoke different emotions or rasas, such as joy, sorrow, devotion, or romance.
- Both traditions also use the concept of tala, which is a rhythmic cycle that consists of a fixed number of beats or matras. A tala can have different subdivisions or vibhagas, and can be played with different patterns or bols.
- Both traditions also have a variety of musical forms, such as kriti, dhrupad, khayal, thumri, bhajan, and kirtan, that are used for different purposes and occasions. Some forms are devotional, some are expressive, and some are improvisational.
- Both traditions also have a rich repertoire of musical instruments, such as sitar, sarod, veena, flute, tabla, mridangam, harmonium, and tanpura, that are used to accompany the vocal or instrumental performance. Some instruments are plucked, some are blown, some are struck, and some are drone.
- Music in India is not only a form of art, but also a form of knowledge, spirituality, and social interaction. Music is considered as a way of attaining self-realization, as well as a way of connecting with the divine, the nature, and the society.
- Music in India is also influenced by and influences other forms of cultural expression, such as folk music, dance, drama, literature, and cinema. Music in India reflects the diversity and unity of the Indian people and their heritage.
