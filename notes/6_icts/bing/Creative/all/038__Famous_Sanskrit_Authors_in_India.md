### Famous Sanskrit Authors in India

- Sanskrit is one of the oldest and most influential languages of India, with a rich literary tradition spanning from the Vedic period (1500-500 BCE) to the medieval period (500-1500 CE).
- Sanskrit literature covers a wide range of genres, such as religious scriptures, poetry, drama, prose, and scientific treatises. It also reflects the diverse cultural, religious, and philosophical views of ancient and medieval India.
- Some of the most famous Sanskrit authors in India are:

  - **Kalidasa**: He is considered to be the greatest poet and dramatist in Sanskrit, and one of the finest in world literature. He lived in the 4th or 5th century CE, during the Gupta dynasty. He wrote three epic poems (Raghuvamsa, Kumarasambhava, and Meghaduta), three plays (Malavikagnimitra, Vikramorvasiya, and Abhijnanasakuntalam), and two lyrical poems (Ritusamhara and Meghasandesa). His works are known for their elegance, beauty, and romanticism.  
  - **Bhasa**: He is one of the earliest and most prolific Sanskrit dramatists, who lived around the 3rd century BCE. He wrote thirteen plays, which were rediscovered in 1912 by an Indian scholar. His plays are based on the epics Mahabharata and Ramayana, and are characterized by their realism, humor, and innovation. Some of his famous plays are Urubhanga, Madhyamavyayoga, and Svapnavasavadatta.  
  - **Bhartrihari**: He is a renowned poet and philosopher, who lived in the 5th or 6th century CE. He wrote three collections of verses, called Satakas, on the themes of love, renunciation, and wisdom. He also wrote a treatise on grammar and linguistics, called Vakyapadiya, which explores the relationship between language, thought, and reality. His poetry is marked by its brevity, wit, and insight. 
  - **Banabhatta**: He is a celebrated prose writer and court poet of King Harshavardhana, who ruled in the 7th century CE. He wrote two historical novels, Harshacharita and Kadambari, which narrate the life and adventures of the king and his ancestors. He also wrote a biography of the king, called Harshasataka. His style is ornate, complex, and imaginative, and his works are full of descriptions, dialogues, and stories. 
  - **Jayadeva**: He is a devotional poet and musician, who lived in the 12th century CE, in the region of Orissa. He wrote the Gita Govinda, a lyrical poem that depicts the love of Krishna and Radha. The poem is divided into twelve chapters, each containing twenty-four verses and a refrain. The verses are composed in various meters and musical modes, and are meant to be sung and danced. The poem is a masterpiece of erotic and mystical poetry, and has influenced many later poets and artists. 

- A mnemonic to remember these five authors is: **K**alidasa, **B**hasa, **B**hartrihari, **B**anabhatta, and **J**ayadeva, or **KBBBJ**.
