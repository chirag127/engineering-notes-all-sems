#### Force Theory of origin of State

- Force Theory of origin of State is a theory that explains the origin and meaning of the State as a result of force, conquest and domination by the strong over the weak.
- According to this theory, the State emerged from the process of human aggression, capture and enslavement of man by man, and subjugation of feebler tribes by stronger ones .
- The theory assumes that man is a bellicose and power-hungry animal, who uses his physical strength to impose his will on others and to secure his own interests.
- The theory traces the evolution of the State from the tribal chief, who became the king by expanding his followers and territory through warfare, to the modern State, which is the outcome of successful warfare among political communities .
- The theory also implies that force is not only a historical factor, but also a present and essential feature of the State, that power is the justification and raison d'etre of the State, and that the maintenance and extension of power within and without is the sole aim of the State.
- The theory has been criticized on several grounds, such as:
  - It ignores the role of other factors, such as social contract, divine right, natural law, consent, cooperation, etc., in the origin and development of the State.
  - It contradicts the evidence of history, which shows that many States were formed by peaceful means, such as federation, confederation, union, etc., and that many States were destroyed by force, such as invasion, revolution, secession, etc.
  - It overlooks the fact that force alone cannot create or sustain a State, unless it is accompanied by some degree of legitimacy, consent, loyalty, obedience, etc., from the people .
  - It fails to explain the diversity and complexity of the modern States, which are not merely the products of force, but also of culture, civilization, law, democracy, etc.
  - It promotes a negative and pessimistic view of human nature, society and politics, which may justify violence, tyranny, oppression, exploitation, etc., as natural and inevitable .
