### Socio religious reform movement of 19th century in India

- The socio religious reform movement of 19th century in India was a collective effort by various individuals and organizations to reform and revitalize the Indian society and religion in the face of colonial domination, social evils, religious superstitions and cultural decline.
- The movement was influenced by the modern ideas of liberty, equality, democracy and justice that were introduced by the British, as well as the growing awareness of India's past glory and the outside world.
- The movement can be broadly classified into two types: reformist and revivalist.
  - Reformist movements aimed to rationalize and modernize the existing social and religious practices, and to remove the abuses and injustices that plagued the Indian society, especially the women and the lower castes. They also advocated for women's education, widow remarriage, abolition of sati, polygamy and child marriage, and promotion of inter-caste and inter-religious harmony. Some of the prominent reformist movements were:
    - Brahmo Samaj: Founded by Raja Ram Mohan Roy in 1828 in Calcutta, it fought against idolatry, polytheism, caste oppression, unnecessary rituals and other social evils. It also strove for women's rights and education, and propagated the idea of monotheism and universalism.
    - Prarthana Samaj: Founded by Atmaram Pandurang in 1867 in Bombay, it was inspired by the Brahmo Samaj and aimed to reform the Hindu society by promoting monotheism, social service, education and moral values. It also supported the causes of widow remarriage, women's education and upliftment of the depressed classes.
    - Aligarh Movement: Founded by Sir Syed Ahmed Khan in 1875 in Aligarh, it was a reform movement among the Muslims that sought to reconcile Islam with modern science and education. It also aimed to improve the social and economic condition of the Muslims and to foster a sense of loyalty and cooperation with the British. The movement established the Mohammedan Anglo-Oriental College, which later became the Aligarh Muslim University.
  - Revivalist movements aimed to revive and restore the purity and glory of the ancient Indian traditions and thoughts, and to resist the western influence and interference in the Indian culture and religion. They also emphasized the importance of self-reliance, education, social service and nationalism. Some of the prominent revivalist movements were:
    - Arya Samaj: Founded by Swami Dayanand Saraswati in 1875 in Bombay, it was a radical reform movement that challenged the authority of the Vedas and the caste system. It advocated the principles of swadeshi, swaraj, swadharma and swabhimana, and opposed idolatry, polytheism, rituals, priesthood, animal sacrifice, child marriage and the caste system. It also encouraged the dissemination of western scientific knowledge and the promotion of social welfare .
    - Ramakrishna Mission: Founded by Swami Vivekananda in 1897 in Calcutta, it was a spiritual and humanitarian movement that propagated the teachings of Ramakrishna Paramahamsa, who was a mystic and a saint. It emphasized the unity of all religions and the service of humanity as the highest form of worship. It also engaged in various social activities such as education, health care, relief work and rural development .
    - Deoband Movement: Founded by Maulana Muhammad Qasim Nanautawi and Maulana Rashid Ahmad Gangohi in 1866 in Deoband, it was a revivalist movement among the Muslims that sought to purify and reform Islam by adhering to the Quran and the Sunnah. It also aimed to resist the British rule and to promote the education and empowerment of the Muslims. The movement established the Darul Uloom Deoband, which became a center of Islamic learning and activism .

- The socio religious reform movement of 19th century in India had a significant impact on the Indian society and the national movement. It helped to create a new social consciousness and a sense of pride among the Indians, and to challenge the colonial stereotypes and policies. It also paved the way for the emergence of new social and political forces, such as the Indian National Congress, the Muslim League, the Hindu Mahasabha and the All India Women's Conference.
