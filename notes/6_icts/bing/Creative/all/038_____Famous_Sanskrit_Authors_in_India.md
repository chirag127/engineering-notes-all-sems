# Famous Sanskrit Authors in India

Sanskrit is one of the oldest and most influential languages of India. It is the language of the Vedas, the Upanishads, the epics, the Puranas, and the classical literature. Sanskrit has produced many great poets and scholars who have enriched the Indian culture and civilization with their works. Here are some of the famous Sanskrit authors in India:

- **Kalidasa**: He is considered to be the greatest Sanskrit poet and dramatist of India. He lived in the 4th or 5th century CE, during the Gupta period. He wrote three epic poems (Mahakavyas), namely Raghuvamsa, Kumarasambhava, and Meghaduta. He also wrote three plays, namely Malavikagnimitra, Vikramorvasiya, and Abhijnanasakuntala. His works are known for their poetic beauty, romantic charm, and cultural values .
- **Bhasa**: He is one of the earliest and most prolific Sanskrit dramatists of India. He lived around the 3rd century CE, before Kalidasa. He wrote thirteen plays, mostly based on the Mahabharata and the Ramayana. His plays are known for their dramatic techniques, characterization, and humor. Some of his famous plays are Urubhanga, Madhyamavyayoga, Pratima, and Svapnavasavadatta.
- **Bhartrihari**: He is a renowned Sanskrit poet and philosopher of India. He lived in the 5th or 6th century CE, during the Gupta period. He wrote three collections of verses (Satakas), namely Nitisataka, Sringarasataka, and Vairagyasataka. His verses deal with the themes of ethics, love, and renunciation. His works are known for their wisdom, wit, and elegance.
- **Banabhatta**: He is a celebrated Sanskrit poet and prose writer of India. He lived in the 7th century CE, during the reign of King Harshavardhana. He wrote two historical romances (Akhyanas), namely Harshacharita and Kadambari. His works are known for their narrative style, imagination, and description.
- **Vidyadhar Shastri**: He is a modern Sanskrit poet and scholar of India. He lived in the 20th century CE, and received the Jnanpith Award, India's highest literary award, in 2009. He wrote two epic poems (Mahakavyas), namely Ramakirtimahakavyam and Brahattaram Bharatam. He also wrote seven shorter poems, three plays, and three songs of praise (Stavanakavyas). His works are known for their creativity, erudition, and devotion.
