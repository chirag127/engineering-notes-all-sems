#### Slavery in Society in Ancient India

- Slavery in ancient India was a complex and contested institution that varied according to time, place, and context. 
- Some sources suggest that slavery was unknown or rare in ancient India, such as the Greek writer Megasthenes, who visited the Mauryan Empire in the 4th century BCE and stated that slavery was banned in Indian society.  
- Other sources indicate that slavery was a common and accepted practice in ancient India, especially among the non-Aryan enemies of the Aryans, who were called Dasyus or Dasas and often captured and enslaved in wars.  
- The Vedic literature, the Buddhist texts, the epics, and the law books all mention different types of slaves, such as domestic slaves, agricultural slaves, war captives, debt slaves, and temple slaves.  
- The status and treatment of slaves varied depending on their origin, gender, occupation, and the attitude of their masters. Some slaves were treated as members of the family, while others were subjected to harsh and cruel conditions.  
- The Dharma Shastras, or the Hindu law books, prescribed various rules and regulations for slavery, such as the prohibition of slave trade, the liberation of slaves under certain circumstances, and the duties and rights of slaves and masters. 
- The Sudras, or the lowest caste in the Hindu social hierarchy, were often considered as slaves or servile to the higher castes, especially the Brahmins. They were denied access to education, religious rites, and wealth, and were expected to perform menial and impure tasks for their superiors. 
- Slavery in ancient India was influenced by the cultural, religious, and political factors of different periods and regions. It was also affected by the interactions with other civilizations, such as the Greeks, the Persians, the Kushans, and the Muslims, who introduced new forms and dimensions of slavery.
