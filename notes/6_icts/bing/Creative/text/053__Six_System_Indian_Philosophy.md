### Six System Indian Philosophy

The Six System Indian Philosophy refers to six schools of thought that originated in the Indian subcontinent and are based on the Vedas, the ancient scriptures of Hinduism. The six systems are:

- **Nyaya**: The school of logic and epistemology, founded by Akshapada Gautama in the 2nd century BCE. Nyaya deals with the methods of acquiring valid knowledge and the criteria of truth. It also discusses the nature of reality, causation, perception, inference, analogy, testimony, and the existence of God.
- **Vaisheshika**: The school of atomism and naturalism, founded by Kanada in the 6th century BCE. Vaisheshika analyzes the physical world into six categories: substance, quality, action, generality, particularity, and inherence. It also postulates that all substances are composed of indivisible atoms, and that the law of karma governs the motion and interaction of atoms.
- **Samkhya**: The school of dualism and realism, founded by Kapila in the 6th century BCE. Samkhya explains the origin and evolution of the universe in terms of two eternal principles: purusha (consciousness) and prakriti (matter). It also describes the three gunas (qualities) of prakriti: sattva (purity), rajas (activity), and tamas (inertia), and the 25 tattvas (elements) that emerge from prakriti. Samkhya aims to achieve liberation by discriminating between purusha and prakriti.
- **Yoga**: The school of discipline and practice, founded by Patanjali in the 2nd century BCE. Yoga is closely related to Samkhya, but adds a theistic element and prescribes a system of eight limbs (ashtanga) for attaining the state of samadhi (absorption). The eight limbs are: yama (restraints), niyama (observances), asana (postures), pranayama (breath control), pratyahara (withdrawal of senses), dharana (concentration), dhyana (meditation), and samadhi (absorption).
- **Purva Mimamsa**: The school of ritual and exegesis, founded by Jaimini in the 3rd century BCE. Purva Mimamsa interprets the Vedic texts, especially the Brahmanas and the Samhitas, and emphasizes the performance of sacrifices and rituals as the means of attaining heaven and happiness. It also develops a theory of language, meaning, and inference, and defends the authority and eternity of the Vedas.
- **Uttara Mimamsa or Vedanta**: The school of metaphysics and monism, founded by Badarayana in the 2nd century BCE. Vedanta is based on the Upanishads, the philosophical portions of the Vedas, and the Brahma Sutras, a systematic exposition of the Upanishadic teachings. Vedanta explores the nature of Brahman (the absolute reality), Atman (the individual self), and the relationship between them. Vedanta has several sub-schools, such as Advaita (non-dualism), Vishishtadvaita (qualified non-dualism), and Dvaita (dualism).
