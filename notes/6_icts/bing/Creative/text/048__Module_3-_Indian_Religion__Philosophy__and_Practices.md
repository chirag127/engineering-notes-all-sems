## Module 3- Indian Religion, Philosophy, and Practices

- India is a diverse and devout country, with six major religious groups: Hinduism, Islam, Christianity, Sikhism, Buddhism and Jainism. Each of these religions has its own beliefs, practices, scriptures, festivals, rituals and traditions. 
- Hinduism is the oldest and largest religion in India, with about 80% of the population identifying as Hindus. Hinduism is not a single doctrine, but a collection of various schools of thought, sects, movements and philosophies that share some common themes, such as the concepts of karma, dharma, reincarnation, moksha, and the worship of multiple gods and goddesses. Hinduism also has a rich and diverse literature, including the Vedas, the Upanishads, the Bhagavad Gita, the Ramayana, the Mahabharata and the Puranas.  
- Islam is the second-largest religion in India, with about 14% of the population identifying as Muslims. Islam is a monotheistic religion that follows the teachings of the Quran, the holy book revealed to the Prophet Muhammad, and the Sunnah, the example of his life and actions. Muslims believe in one God, Allah, and his messengers, prophets, angels, and the Day of Judgment. Muslims practice the five pillars of Islam: declaration of faith, prayer, charity, fasting, and pilgrimage. Islam also has various sects, such as Sunni, Shia, Sufi, and Ahmadiyya, that differ in some aspects of doctrine and practice. 
- Christianity is the third-largest religion in India, with about 2.3% of the population identifying as Christians. Christianity is a monotheistic religion that follows the teachings of Jesus Christ, the Son of God, who was crucified and resurrected for the salvation of humanity. Christians believe in the Trinity, the Father, the Son, and the Holy Spirit, and the Bible, the sacred scripture that consists of the Old and New Testaments. Christians practice baptism, communion, prayer, worship, and service. Christianity also has various denominations, such as Catholic, Protestant, Orthodox, and Anglican, that differ in some aspects of doctrine and practice. 
- Sikhism is the fourth-largest religion in India, with about 1.7% of the population identifying as Sikhs. Sikhism is a monotheistic religion that follows the teachings of the ten Gurus, the spiritual leaders who founded the faith in the 15th and 16th centuries. Sikhs believe in one God, Waheguru, and his revelation, the Guru Granth Sahib, the holy book that contains the hymns and writings of the Gurus and other saints. Sikhs practice the three pillars of Sikhism: meditation on God's name, honest work, and sharing with others. Sikhs also follow the code of conduct, the Rehat Maryada, that prescribes the five Ks, the symbols of Sikh identity: kesh (uncut hair), kangha (comb), kara (steel bracelet), kachera (cotton underwear), and kirpan (sword). 
- Buddhism is the fifth-largest religion in India, with about 0.7% of the population identifying as Buddhists. Buddhism is a non-theistic religion that follows the teachings of the Buddha, the enlightened one, who lived and taught in India in the 6th and 5th centuries BCE. Buddhists believe in the Four Noble Truths, the core teachings of the Buddha that explain the nature and cause of suffering and the way to end it, and the Noble Eightfold Path, the ethical and mental discipline that leads to liberation from the cycle of rebirth. Buddhists practice meditation, mindfulness, morality, and compassion. Buddhism also has various schools, such as Theravada, Mahayana, and Vajrayana, that differ in some aspects of doctrine and practice. 
- Jainism is the sixth-largest religion in India, with about 0.4% of the population identifying as Jains. Jainism is an ancient religion that follows the teachings of the Tirthankaras, the spiritual teachers who attained enlightenment and showed the way to liberation.
