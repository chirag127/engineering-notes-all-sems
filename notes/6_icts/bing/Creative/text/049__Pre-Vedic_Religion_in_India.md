### Pre-Vedic Religion in India

- Pre-Vedic religion refers to the religious traditions that existed in the Indian subcontinent before the arrival of the Indo-Aryan speakers who brought the Vedic religion with them.  
- The most prominent pre-Vedic civilization was the Indus Valley Civilization (IVC), which flourished from c. 2500 to 1900 BCE in the regions of present-day Pakistan and northwest India.  
- The IVC is known for its urban planning, sophisticated drainage system, standardized weights and measures, and writing system, which is still undeciphered.  
- The religious beliefs and practices of the IVC are not well understood, but some evidence suggests that they worshipped a mother goddess, a horned deity, and a proto-Shiva figure.  
- They also practiced ritual bathing, animal sacrifice, fire worship, and possibly yoga.  
- Some scholars have proposed that the IVC was influenced by or had contact with other pre-Vedic cultures, such as the Elamites, the Sumerians, and the Dravidians.  
- The IVC declined around 1900 BCE, possibly due to environmental changes, internal conflicts, or external invasions.  
- The pre-Vedic religion of the IVC may have survived in some form among the non-Aryan peoples of India, such as the Dravidians, the Munda, and the Adivasis.  
- Some aspects of the pre-Vedic religion may have also influenced the later Vedic and Hindu traditions, such as the worship of Shiva, the concept of Shakti, and the practice of yoga.
