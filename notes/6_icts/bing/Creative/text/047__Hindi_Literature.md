### Hindi Literature

Hindi literature is the literature written in the various Hindi languages and dialects of the Indian subcontinent. It includes literature in Awadhi, Braj, Bundeli, Khari Boli, and other regional forms. Hindi literature also encompasses the writings of Muslim writers in the Persian script that have Hindu themes and content. Hindi literature has a long and rich history, dating back to the 7th century CE. It can be divided into five main periods, each with its own characteristics and genres.

- **Ādi Kāl /Vīr-Gāthā Kāl (आदि काल/वीरगाथा काल)**: This period lasted from the 7th to the 14th century CE. It was marked by poems extolling brave warriors and heroes, such as Prithviraj Chauhan, Alha, and Udal. The most famous poet of this period was Chand Bardai, who composed the Prithviraj Raso, an epic poem about the life and deeds of Prithviraj Chauhan. Other notable poets of this period were Jagnayak, Padmanabha, and Kanhadade Prabandha.
- **Bhakti Kāl (भक्ति काल)**: This period spanned from the 14th to the 18th century CE. It was characterized by poems of devotion and love for God, inspired by the Bhakti movement. The Bhakti poets expressed their personal and emotional relationship with God, often using the language of love and romance. They also criticized the social evils and superstitions of their times, and advocated for equality and harmony among all people. Some of the most prominent Bhakti poets were Kabir, Ravidas, Surdas, Tulsidas, Mirabai, and Nanak.
- **Rīti Kāl /Śṛṅgār Kāl (रीति काल/ शृंगार काल)**: This period covered the 18th and the 19th century CE. It was dominated by poems of romance and eroticism, which were highly ornamental and stylized. The Rīti poets followed the rules and conventions of classical Sanskrit poetics, and used various figures of speech and metaphors to describe the beauty and emotions of the lovers. They also wrote about the seasons, nature, and courtly life. Some of the famous Rīti poets were Keshavdas, Bihari, Ghananand, and Matiram.
- **Ādhunik Kāl (आधुनिक काल)**: This period began from the late 19th century CE and continues till the present day. It is also known as the modern period of Hindi literature. It was influenced by the social and political changes brought by the British colonial rule, the nationalist movement, and the Western literary trends. The Ādhunik poets and writers experimented with new forms and themes, such as realism, satire, social criticism, nationalism, feminism, and humanism. They also enriched the Hindi language by incorporating words and expressions from other languages and dialects. Some of the notable Ādhunik writers were Bharatendu Harishchandra, Premchand, Jaishankar Prasad, Mahadevi Varma, Ramdhari Singh Dinkar, and Harivansh Rai Bachchan.
- **Nayvottar Kāl (नव्योत्तर काल)**: This period started from the 1980s and is also known as the post-modern period of Hindi literature. It is characterized by a diversity of styles and genres, such as experimental, avant-garde, post-colonial, Dalit, feminist, and subaltern literature. The Nayvottar writers challenge the established norms and conventions of Hindi literature, and explore the issues of identity, marginalization, globalization, and hybridity. Some of the prominent Nayvottar writers are Nirmal Verma, Krishna Baldev Vaid, Uday Prakash, Geetanjali Shree, and Manohar Shyam Joshi.
