### Famous Sanskrit Authors in India

Sanskrit is one of the oldest and richest languages of India, with a long and diverse literary tradition. Sanskrit literature can be broadly divided into two categories: Vedic and Classical. Vedic literature consists of the sacred texts of the Vedic religion, composed in an archaic form of Sanskrit between 1500 and 500 BCE. Classical literature consists of the secular and religious texts composed in a refined and standardized form of Sanskrit from the 4th century BCE onwards. Some of the famous Sanskrit authors in India are:

- **Kālidāsa**: He is widely regarded as the greatest poet and dramatist of classical Sanskrit literature. He lived in the 4th or 5th century CE and wrote seven works: three plays, two epics, and two lyrical poems. His plays are Mālavikāgnimitram (Mālavikā and Agnimitra), Abhijñānaśākuntalam (The Recognition of Śakuntalā), and Vikramorvaśīyam (Urvaśī Won by Valour). His epics are Raghuvaṃśa (The Dynasty of Raghu) and Kumārasambhava (The Birth of the War-God). His lyrical poems are Meghadūta (The Cloud Messenger) and Ṛtusaṃhāra (The Seasons). 
- **Bhāsa**: He is one of the earliest and most prolific Sanskrit dramatists, who lived around the 3rd century BCE. He wrote thirteen plays, which were rediscovered in 1912 by an Indian scholar, Mahamahopadhyaya Ganapati Sastrigal. His plays are based on the epics, the Purāṇas, and the history of the Nanda dynasty. Some of his famous plays are Svapnavāsavadattā (The Dream of Vāsavadattā), Pratijñāyaugandharāyaṇam (The Vow of Yaugandharāyaṇa), and Urubhaṅga (The Shattered Thigh). 
- **Bharavi**: He is the author of the epic poem Kirātārjunīya (The Encounter of Arjuna with the Kirāta), which is considered one of the six mahākāvyas (great epics) of classical Sanskrit literature. He lived in the 6th century CE and was a court poet of King Durvinita of the Western Ganga dynasty. His poem narrates the episode from the Mahābhārata, where Arjuna obtains the Pāśupatastra (the weapon of Lord Śiva) after fighting with Śiva disguised as a Kirāta (a hunter). 
- **Bhartṛhari**: He is a renowned poet and philosopher, who lived in the 5th or 6th century CE. He is the author of three collections of verses: Śatakatraya (The Three Hundred), which consists of the Nītiśataka (The Century of Morals), the Śṛṅgāraśataka (The Century of Love), and the Vairāgyaśataka (The Century of Renunciation). He is also the author of the Vākyapadīya (The Treatise on Words and Sentences), a seminal work on grammar, semantics, and logic. He is said to have been the king of Ujjayinī before renouncing his throne and becoming a monk. 
- **Bhaṭṭi**: He is the author of the epic poem Bhaṭṭikāvya (The Poem of Bhaṭṭi), which is also known as the Rāvaṇavadha (The Slaying of Rāvaṇa). He lived in the 7th century CE and was a court poet of King Yaśovarman of Kanauj. His poem retells the story of the Rāmāyaṇa, while also illustrating the rules of Sanskrit grammar and poetics.
