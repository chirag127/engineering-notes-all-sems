#### Marriage in Society in Ancient India

- Marriage was a sacred institution that united two halves of life, man and woman, to form a complete and organic whole.
- Marriage was also a social contract that established kinship ties, rights and duties, and mutual support between the spouses and their families .
- Marriage was regulated by various laws and customs, such as caste, gotra, varna, and kula, that determined the eligibility and compatibility of the partners .
- Marriage was performed through various rituals and ceremonies, such as the kanyadan, the saptapadi, the homa, and the mangalsutra, that symbolized the sanctity and permanence of the bond .
- Marriage was classified into eight main types, based on the mode of acquisition of the bride and the consent of the parties involved. These were: Brahma, Daiva, Arsha, Prajapatya, Gandharva, Asura, Rakshasa, and Paisacha .
- Marriage was influenced by various factors, such as economic, political, religious, and cultural, that shaped the norms and expectations of the spouses and their roles and responsibilities in the family and society .
