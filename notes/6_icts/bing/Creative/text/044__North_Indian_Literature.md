### North Indian Literature

- North Indian literature is a broad term that encompasses the literary works of the diverse cultures and languages of the northern part of the Indian subcontinent.
- North Indian literature can be classified into two main categories: classical and modern.
- Classical North Indian literature refers to the writings in Sanskrit and its derived languages, such as Prakrit, Pali, Apabhramsha, and Braj Bhasha. These languages were used by the ancient and medieval poets, scholars, and religious teachers to compose epics, dramas, poetry, prose, and religious texts. Some of the most famous examples of classical North Indian literature are:
  - The Mahabharata and the Ramayana, the two great epics of Hinduism that narrate the stories of the Kurukshetra War and the exile of Rama, respectively.
  - The Puranas, a collection of mythological and historical narratives that describe the creation, destruction, and re-creation of the universe, as well as the lives and deeds of various gods, goddesses, sages, and kings.
  - The works of Kalidasa, the most celebrated poet and dramatist of classical Sanskrit literature, who wrote masterpieces such as Abhijnanashakuntala (The Recognition of Shakuntala), Meghaduta (The Cloud Messenger), and Raghuvamsha (The Dynasty of Raghu).
  - The works of Kabir, a 15th-century mystic poet and saint, who wrote in a variety of languages, including Hindi, Braj Bhasha, and Awadhi, and whose verses express a universal message of love, devotion, and social harmony.
- Modern North Indian literature refers to the writings in the contemporary languages of the region, such as Hindi, Urdu, Punjabi, Kashmiri, Gujarati, Marathi, and Bengali. These languages developed from the interaction of various linguistic and cultural influences, such as Persian, Arabic, Turkish, English, and the local dialects. Some of the most famous examples of modern North Indian literature are:
  - The works of Premchand, a 20th-century novelist and short story writer, who is considered the father of modern Hindi literature, and who wrote realistic and socially conscious works, such as Godaan (The Gift of a Cow), Nirmala (The Pure One), and Kafan (The Shroud).
  - The works of Mirza Ghalib, a 19th-century poet and scholar, who is regarded as the greatest Urdu poet of all time, and who wrote elegant and profound ghazals (lyric poems) that express his personal and philosophical views on life, love, and God.
  - The works of Rabindranath Tagore, a 20th-century poet, novelist, and Nobel laureate, who is the most renowned Bengali writer of all time, and who wrote versatile and innovative works, such as Gitanjali (Song Offerings), Chokher Bali (A Grain of Sand), and Gora (Fair-Faced).
  - The works of Amrita Pritam, a 20th-century poet and novelist, who is the most prominent female Punjabi writer of all time, and who wrote powerful and passionate works, such as Pinjar (The Skeleton), Ajj Aakhaan Waris Shah Nu (Today I Invoke Waris Shah), and Sunehade (Messages).
