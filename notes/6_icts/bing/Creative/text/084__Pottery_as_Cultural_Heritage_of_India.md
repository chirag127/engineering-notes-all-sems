### Pottery as Cultural Heritage of India

Pottery is one of the oldest and most enduring forms of artistic expression in India. It has a long and rich history that spans from the ancient Indus Valley Civilisation to the present day. Pottery is not only a utilitarian craft, but also a cultural art that reflects the diversity and creativity of the Indian people. Some of the main points to know about pottery as a cultural heritage of India are:

- Pottery in India can be traced back to the early settlements of Lahuradewa and Mehrgarh, where evidence of hand-made and wheel-made pottery has been found . The Indus Valley Civilisation, which flourished from 2500 to 1900 BCE, was known for its sophisticated pottery production, which included polished ware, burial pottery, ochre coloured pottery, black-on-red ware, and painted grey ware .
- Pottery in India evolved through various phases and cultures, such as the Vedic, Mauryan, Gupta, Kushan, and Islamic periods. Each period had its own distinctive style and technique of pottery making, influenced by the social, economic, and environmental conditions of the time. Some of the notable types of pottery that emerged in these periods were the painted grey ware, the northern black polished ware, the red ware, and the glazed ware .
- Pottery in India is also a reflection of the regional diversity and local traditions of the country. Different states and regions have their own unique pottery styles, such as the blue pottery of Jaipur, the terracotta pottery of West Bengal, the black pottery of Manipur, the khurja pottery of Uttar Pradesh, and the longpi pottery of Nagaland. These styles are influenced by the availability of raw materials, the climatic conditions, the religious beliefs, and the aesthetic preferences of the local communities .
- Pottery in India is not only a craft, but also a form of art and culture. Pottery is used for various purposes, such as storing water and grains, cooking food, serving dishes, making lamps, idols, toys, and decorative items. Pottery is also a medium of expression, as potters often paint or engrave their pots with motifs, symbols, stories, and messages that convey their cultural identity, values, and emotions .
- Pottery in India is a living and dynamic tradition that is still practiced extensively by millions of potters across the country. Pottery is a source of livelihood, pride, and joy for many potters, who inherit their skills and knowledge from their ancestors. Pottery is also a form of innovation, as potters constantly experiment with new designs, materials, and techniques to meet the changing demands and tastes of the market. Pottery is also a form of preservation, as potters strive to keep their ancient and diverse heritage alive and relevant in the modern world .

Pottery is one of the most tangible and iconic elements of Indian art and culture. It is a testimony of the long and glorious history, the rich and varied diversity, and the vibrant and creative spirit of the Indian people. Pottery is a cultural heritage that deserves to be celebrated, appreciated, and protected for generations to come.
