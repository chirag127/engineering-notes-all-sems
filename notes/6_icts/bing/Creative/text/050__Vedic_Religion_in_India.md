### Vedic Religion in India

- Vedic religion was the religion of the ancient Indo-European-speaking peoples who entered India about 1500 BCE from the region of present-day Iran.
- It takes its name from the collections of sacred texts known as the Vedas, which are the oldest stratum of religious activity in India for which there exist written materials.
- Vedic religion was one of the major traditions that shaped Hinduism, the dominant religion of India and the third largest religion in the world.
- Vedic religion was based on the worship of various deities, especially Indra, Agni, Soma, Varuna, and Mitra, through rituals and sacrifices performed by priests and householders .
- Vedic religion also had a strong ethical dimension, as it emphasized the concepts of rta (cosmic order), dharma (duty), karma (action and its consequences), and samsara (cycle of rebirth).
- Vedic religion gradually evolved and transformed into classical Hinduism, which incorporated new elements such as the Upanishads (philosophical texts), the epics (Ramayana and Mahabharata), the Puranas (mythological texts), and the bhakti (devotional) movement .
- Vedic religion also influenced other religions and cultures, such as Buddhism, Jainism, Sikhism, Zoroastrianism, and Southeast Asian civilizations .
