### Water Management in India and Ancient India

- Water is essential for human existence and civilization, and has influenced the culture, traditions, rituals, and religious beliefs of India.
- India has a long history of hydrologic knowledge and water resources management, dating back to the Harappan civilization (3000-1500 BCE) and the Vedic period (1500-500 BCE).
- Ancient Indians developed various methods and structures to collect, store, distribute, and treat water, such as stepwells, tanks, dams, canals, wells, and filters.
- Some of the key features of water management in ancient India are:

  - The Vedas, especially the Rigveda, Yajurveda, and Atharvaveda, contain many references to the water cycle and its processes, such as precipitation, evaporation, infiltration, runoff, and groundwater. They also mention water quality, hydraulic machines, hydro-structures, and nature-based solutions for water management.
  - The Harappan civilization, also known as the Indus Valley civilization, was one of the earliest urban civilizations in the world, and had a sophisticated system of water management. They built hydraulic structures such as reservoirs, drains, sewers, and baths, and had centralized and decentralized wastewater disposal systems. They also used methods such as sedimentation, filtration, and boiling to treat wastewater .
  - The Mauryan Empire (322-185 BCE) was the first "hydraulic civilization" in India, and constructed dams with spillways, reservoirs, and channels with sluice gates. They also had an understanding of water balance, water pricing, rainfall measurement, and hydrological processes. They used natural materials such as clay, sand, gravel, and charcoal to filter water .
  - Stepwells, also known as baoli, bawri, or vav, are unique architectural features of India, and are wells or ponds that are accessed by a series of steps. They were built in drought-prone regions of India to provide water throughout the year, and also served as social and cultural spaces. They are examples of inverted architecture, as they descend into the ground rather than rise above it. They are often decorated with intricate carvings, sculptures, and pavilions. Some of the famous stepwells in India are Chand Bawri in Abhaneri, Rani ki Vav in Patan, and Agrasen ki Baoli in Delhi .

- Water management in ancient India was based on the principles of sustainability, efficiency, and equity, and was influenced by the local geography, climate, culture, and religion. It also reflected the scientific and engineering skills of the ancient Indians, and their respect for nature and water as a sacred and vital resource.
