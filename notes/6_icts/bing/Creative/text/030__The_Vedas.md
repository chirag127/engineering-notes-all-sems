### The Vedas

- The Vedas are a large body of religious texts originating in ancient India. 
- They are composed in Vedic Sanskrit and constitute the oldest layer of Sanskrit literature and the oldest scriptures of Hinduism. 
- The word Veda means "knowledge" or "vision" and it serves to manifest the language of the gods in human speech. 
- The Vedas are considered to be apauruṣeya, which means "not of a man, superhuman" and "impersonal, authorless," revelations of sacred sounds and texts heard by ancient sages after intense meditation. 
- The Vedas have been orally transmitted since the 2nd millennium BCE with the help of elaborate mnemonic techniques. 
- The Vedas are classified into four volumes: the Rigveda, the Yajurveda, the Samaveda and the Atharvaveda, with the Rigveda serving as the principal text. 
- The four Vedas are collectively known as “Chathurveda”. 
- Each Veda consists of four parts: the Samhitas (hymns), the Brahmanas (rituals), the Aranyakas (theologies) and the Upanishads (philosophies). 
- The Vedas are the source of spiritual knowledge and guidance for Hindus and are a respected source of wisdom for mankind in general.
