### Political Ideals in Ancient India and their relevance in modern times

- Ancient India had a rich tradition of political thought and practice that spanned from the Vedic period to the medieval period.
- Some of the political ideals that emerged from ancient India are:

  - Democracy: The Rig Veda, the oldest of the four Vedas, depicts a form of democracy based on the collective will of the people, called Samjnana. The Vedic society was divided into four varnas or classes, each with its own duties and rights. The people participated in the assemblies called sabha and samiti, where they discussed and decided on various matters of common interest. The king was elected by the people and was accountable to them. He was also guided by a council of elders and priests. Democracy in ancient India was not based on universal suffrage, but on the principle of consent and consensus.
  - Dharma: Dharma is a Sanskrit term that means duty, law, morality, justice, righteousness, or order. It is the basis of the Vedic ideals and the Indian political thought. Dharma regulates the conduct of the individual, the society, and the state. It is derived from the eternal and universal laws of nature and the divine will. Dharma aims at the welfare of all beings and the harmony of the cosmos. The king was the upholder and protector of dharma. He had to follow the dharma himself and ensure that his subjects also followed it. He had to rule with compassion, wisdom, and impartiality. Dharma also implies the respect for diversity and pluralism, as it recognizes the different paths and goals of human life .
  - Artha: Artha is another Sanskrit term that means wealth, material prosperity, power, or polity. It is one of the four goals of human life, along with dharma, kama (pleasure), and moksha (liberation). Artha is the subject matter of the Arthashastra, the ancient treatise on statecraft and economics, attributed to Kautilya or Chanakya, the adviser of the Mauryan emperor Chandragupta. Artha deals with the practical aspects of governance, such as administration, taxation, diplomacy, war, law, and trade. Artha also implies the pursuit of self-interest and rationality, as well as the balance between the ends and the means.
  - Ahimsa: Ahimsa is another Sanskrit term that means non-violence, non-injury, or non-harming. It is one of the cardinal virtues of the Indian religions, such as Hinduism, Buddhism, and Jainism. Ahimsa implies the respect for all forms of life and the avoidance of harm or injury to any living being, either by thought, word, or deed. Ahimsa also implies the promotion of peace, harmony, and compassion in the world. Ahimsa was the basis of the non-violent resistance movements led by Mahatma Gandhi and other freedom fighters against the British colonial rule in India.

- The relevance of these political ideals in modern times are:

  - Democracy: Democracy is widely accepted as the best form of government in the modern world, as it ensures the participation, representation, and accountability of the people. Democracy also fosters the values of freedom, equality, and justice in the society. Democracy in modern India is inspired by the ancient Vedic democracy, as well as the western democratic traditions. The Constitution of India, adopted in 1950, establishes India as a sovereign, socialist, secular, and democratic republic. It grants universal adult suffrage, fundamental rights, and federalism to the citizens of India. It also provides for a parliamentary system of government, with a bicameral legislature, an executive headed by the prime minister, and an independent judiciary.
  - Dharma: Dharma is still relevant in modern times, as it provides a moral and ethical framework for the individual, the society, and the state. Dharma also helps in the preservation and promotion of the cultural and spiritual heritage of India. Dharma also reflects the diversity and pluralism of India, as it accommodates the different religions, languages, regions, and communities of the country. Dharma also guides the foreign policy of India, as it advocates for the principles of non-alignment, peaceful coexistence, and cooperation among the nations .
