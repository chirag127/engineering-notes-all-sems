#### Force Theory of origin of State

- Force Theory of origin of State is a theory that explains the origin and meaning of the State as a result of force, conquest and domination by the strong over the weak.
- According to this theory, the State emerged from the process of human aggression, capture and enslavement of man by man, and the self-seeking domination acquired by superior physical force.
- The theory assumes that man is a bellicose and power-hungry animal, who uses his strength to subjugate others and form a band of followers.
- The theory traces the evolution of the State from the tribe to the kingdom and from the kingdom to the empire, as a continuation of the same process of warfare and subjugation.
- The theory also implies that force is not only a historical factor, but also the present essential feature of the State, that power is the justification and raison d'etre of the State, and that the maintenance and extension of power within and without is the sole aim of the State.
- The theory has been criticized for being historically inaccurate, morally repugnant, and politically dangerous.
- Historically, the theory ignores the role of other factors, such as social contract, divine right, natural law, and consent of the governed, in the origin and development of the State.
- Morally, the theory denies the inherent dignity and rights of human beings, and justifies tyranny, oppression, and exploitation by the powerful.
- Politically, the theory encourages violence, war, and imperialism, and undermines the values of democracy, peace, and cooperation.
- Some examples of the force theory of government are the ancient empires of Assyria, Babylon, Persia, Greece, and Rome, the feudal system of the Middle Ages, the colonial expansion of European powers, and the totalitarian regimes of the 20th century .
