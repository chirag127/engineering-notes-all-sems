#### Slavery in Society in Ancient India

- Slavery in ancient India was a complex and contested institution that varied according to time, place, and context. 
- Some sources suggest that slavery was unknown or banned in ancient India, such as the Greek writer Megasthenes, who visited the Mauryan Empire in the 4th century BCE and stated that all Indians were free.  
- However, other sources indicate that slavery was a recognized and widespread practice in ancient India, especially in the Vedic period and the later Brahmanical law books.  
- The term dasa, which originally meant "enemy" or "adversary" in the Rigveda, came to denote a slave or a servant in later texts.  
- The dasas were mostly non-Aryan people who were captured in wars or raids by the Aryans and reduced to a subordinate status.  
- The dasas were employed in various occupations, such as domestic service, agriculture, crafts, mining, and military service.  
- The dasas had limited rights and freedoms, and were subject to the authority and whims of their masters.  
- The dasas could be bought, sold, gifted, inherited, or liberated by their masters.  
- The dasas were also differentiated by their caste, gender, and origin.  
- The lowest and most oppressed category of dasas were the sudras, who were considered to be the servants of the three higher varnas (brahmins, kshatriyas, and vaishyas).  
- The sudras were denied access to education, religious rites, and social mobility.  
- The sudras were also subjected to harsh punishments and humiliations by their superiors.  
- The female dasas were often exploited sexually by their masters, and had little control over their reproductive choices.  
- The female dasas were also expected to perform household chores, such as cooking, cleaning, and childcare.  
- The foreign dasas were mostly from Central Asia, Africa, and Southeast Asia, who were brought to India by trade, conquest, or migration.  
- The foreign dasas were often treated as exotic commodities or curiosities by their Indian masters, and faced discrimination and isolation.  
- The foreign dasas were also used as soldiers, sailors, spies, or entertainers by their Indian masters.  
- Slavery in ancient India was not a static or monolithic phenomenon, but rather a dynamic and diverse one that changed over time and space.  
- Slavery in ancient India was also influenced by various factors, such as political, economic, social, cultural, and religious ones.  
- Slavery in ancient India was also challenged and resisted by various groups, such as the Buddhists, the Jains, the Bhakti saints, the rebels, and the slaves themselves.  
- Slavery in ancient India was also mitigated and moderated by some humane and benevolent aspects, such as the concept of dharma, the practice of manumission, the bond of affection, and the possibility of upward mobility.
