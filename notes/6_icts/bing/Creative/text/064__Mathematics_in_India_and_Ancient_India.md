### Mathematics in India and Ancient India

- Mathematics in India and Ancient India is the discipline of mathematics as it developed in the Indian subcontinent from ancient times until the end of the 18th century.
- Mathematics in India and Ancient India was influenced by various religious and cultural traditions, such as Hinduism, Jainism, Buddhism, and Islam, and also by the interactions with other regions, such as China, the Middle East, and Europe.
- Mathematics in India and Ancient India was characterized by a diversity of styles, methods, and topics, ranging from arithmetic, algebra, geometry, trigonometry, combinatorics, astronomy, astrology, and numerology, to logic, philosophy, and linguistics.
- Mathematics in India and Ancient India was transmitted orally and in verse form for many centuries, and later written in prose commentaries using various scripts, such as Brahmi, Kharosthi, Devanagari, and Persian. The earliest surviving mathematical texts are the Śulba Sūtras, which date from the Vedic period (c. 1500-500 BCE).
- Mathematics in India and Ancient India made significant contributions to the development of the decimal number system, the concept of zero, negative numbers, fractions, irrational numbers, algebraic equations, quadratic equations, indeterminate equations, trigonometric functions, infinite series, calculus, and geometry.
- Mathematics in India and Ancient India also produced some notable mathematicians, such as Pingala, Aryabhata, Brahmagupta, Bhaskara II, Mahavira, Hemachandra, Madhava, Nilakantha, and Ramanujan, who made original and influential discoveries in various branches of mathematics.
- Mathematics in India and Ancient India had a profound impact on the mathematical traditions of other regions, especially the Middle East, China, and Europe, through trade, migration, and transmission of texts and ideas. Some of the mathematical concepts and techniques that originated or were developed in India and Ancient India are still used in modern mathematics.
