#### Contract Theory of origin of State

- Contract theory of origin of state is a theory that explains how the state emerged from a voluntary agreement among individuals who lived in a state of nature without any political authority or law.
- The theory has its roots in ancient Greek and Roman philosophy, but it was developed and popularized by modern thinkers such as Thomas Hobbes, John Locke and Jean-Jacques Rousseau in the 17th and 18th centuries.
- The theory has three main elements: the state of nature, the contract and the state.

  - The state of nature is the hypothetical condition of human beings before the existence of the state. It is characterized by the absence of any social or political order, and by the freedom and equality of all individuals. However, it is also a state of insecurity, conflict and fear, as everyone is exposed to the violence and aggression of others. Hobbes famously described the state of nature as a "war of every man against every man", where life is "solitary, poor, nasty, brutish and short".
  - The contract is the agreement that individuals make to escape the state of nature and form a political community. By entering into the contract, individuals give up some of their natural rights and freedoms, and submit themselves to the authority and laws of the state. In exchange, they receive the protection and benefits of living in a civil society. The contract is based on the consent and rationality of the individuals, and it establishes the legitimacy and limits of the state's power. The contract theorists differ on the details and terms of the contract, such as who are the parties, what are the rights and duties, and what are the forms and functions of the state.
  - The state is the result and the enforcer of the contract. It is the political institution that has the monopoly of the legitimate use of force within a given territory. It is responsible for maintaining the peace and order, protecting the rights and interests of the individuals, and promoting the common good. The state is also accountable to the individuals, and it can be modified or dissolved if it fails to fulfill its obligations or violates the contract. The contract theorists also differ on the nature and scope of the state, such as whether it is absolute or limited, democratic or authoritarian, and unitary or federal.
