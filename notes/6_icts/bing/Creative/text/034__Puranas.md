### Puranas

- Puranas are a vast genre of Indian literature that deal with a wide range of topics, especially legends and traditional lore. 
- Puranas are composed in Sanskrit and other Indian languages, and are named after major Hindu gods and goddesses, such as Vishnu, Shiva, Brahma, and Adi Shakti. 
- Puranas are also found in Jainism, and they contain diverse topics such as cosmology, genealogy, mythology, theology, philosophy, medicine, astronomy, grammar, and more.  
- Puranas are not considered as scriptures in Hinduism, but as Smritis, or texts that remember and transmit the traditions of the past. 
- Puranas have been influential in Hindu culture, inspiring festivals, rituals, temples, and pilgrimages. 
- Puranas are known for their intricate layers of symbolism and allegory, and their sectarianism is not clear-cut.  
- Puranas are traditionally attributed to Vyasa, the legendary compiler of the Vedas and the Mahabharata, but they are likely the work of many authors over the centuries.  
- Puranas are classified into 18 major (Maha) Puranas and 18 minor (Upa) Puranas, with over 400,000 verses in total.  
- Puranas were composed between the 3rd and 10th century CE, but some of them may contain older material or later interpolations.  
- Puranas are said to treat five subjects, or "five signs": the primary and secondary creation of the universe, the genealogy of gods and sages, the reigns of the Manus (the first humans), and the history of the solar and lunar dynasties. 
- Puranas also include various topics related to religious practices, such as ceremonies, sacrifices, festivals, caste duties, donations, temples, images, and places of pilgrimage. 
- Puranas are mostly written in narrative couplets, in a style similar to the epics, the Mahabharata and the Ramayana. 
- Puranas reflect the popular beliefs and ideas of various castes and regions, and also incorporate vernacular influences and local traditions. 
- Puranas are sources of both religious and historical information, as well as literary and artistic inspiration. 
- Puranas are also associated with different schools of Hindu philosophy and theology, such as Vedanta, Vaishnavism, Shaivism, Shaktism, and Tantrism.  
- Puranas are often recited or read in public gatherings, especially during festivals and holy days. 
- Puranas are considered Vaidika (congruent with Vedic literature), because they do not preach initiation into Tantra, which is considered non-Vedic. 
- Puranas are not fixed or canonical texts, but are constantly revised and updated according to the changing times and contexts.  
- Puranas are not meant to be taken literally, but as symbolic and metaphorical expressions of the eternal truths of Hinduism.
