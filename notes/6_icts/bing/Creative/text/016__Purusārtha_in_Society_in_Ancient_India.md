#### Purusārtha in Society in Ancient India

- Purusārtha (Sanskrit: पुरुषार्थ) literally means "object (ive) of men" or "purpose of human being". 
- It is a key concept in Hinduism, and refers to the four proper goals or aims of a human life. The four puruṣārthas are Dharma (righteousness, moral values), Artha (prosperity, economic values), Kama (pleasure, love, psychological values) and Moksha (liberation, spiritual values, self-actualization).  
- All four Purusharthas are important, but in cases of conflict, Dharma is considered more important than Artha or Kama in Hindu philosophy.   Moksha is considered the ultimate goal of human life. 
- Purusārtha is a fundamental principle of social ethics, which takes into account the whole man and his relationship with himself and the society.  
- Purusārtha defines the duties and obligations of each individual, as well as the behaviors that enable social order, right conduct, and virtue.  
- Purusārtha also reflects the pursuit of an ideal world, which is fixed and independent of the immediate world, and which can be attained by following the proper conduct and working towards the generation of prosperity and well-being in society.  
- Purusārtha is not only meant for the individual alone, but also for the collective good of the society and the universe.  
- Purusārtha is also a sequential process towards the liberation from the bonds of the world (mukti), which is the ultimate aim of all the darsanas (philosophical schools) in Hinduism.
