### Harappan Technologies in India and Ancient India

- The Harappan civilization, also known as the Indus Valley civilization, was one of the earliest urban cultures of the Indian subcontinent, dating from c. 3300 BCE to 1300 BCE.
- The Harappan civilization extended from modern-day northeast Afghanistan to Pakistan and northwest India, covering an area of about 1.25 million square kilometers.
- The Harappan civilization is known for its achievements in various fields, such as urban planning, architecture, engineering, sanitation, metallurgy, crafts, trade, and writing  .
- Some of the notable technologies of the Harappan civilization are:

  - **Urban planning**: The Harappan cities were well-planned and organized, with a grid-like layout of streets, rectangular blocks, and standardized brick sizes . The cities had public buildings, such as granaries, citadels, baths, and assembly halls, as well as private houses, workshops, and wells . The cities also had elaborate drainage and sewage systems, which ensured hygiene and sanitation .
  - **Architecture**: The Harappan architecture was characterized by the use of baked bricks, stone, wood, and metal, and the application of geometric and floral motifs . The Harappan buildings were mostly flat-roofed and multi-storied, with courtyards, balconies, and windows . Some of the remarkable examples of Harappan architecture are the Great Bath of Mohenjo-daro, the granary of Harappa, and the dockyard of Lothal .
  - **Engineering**: The Harappan engineers demonstrated their skills in constructing dams, reservoirs, canals, wells, and irrigation systems, which enabled them to harness the water resources of the Indus River and its tributaries . They also built roads, bridges, and fortifications, which facilitated communication and defense . They also invented the plow, the wheel, and the cart, which improved agriculture and transportation .
  - **Metallurgy**: The Harappan civilization is referred to as a Bronze Age civilization, as they used copper, bronze, lead, and tin for making tools, weapons, ornaments, and vessels . They also used gold, silver, and iron, though in lesser quantities . They developed techniques of casting, forging, soldering, and alloying, and produced various artifacts, such as axes, chisels, knives, razors, arrowheads, spearheads, fish-hooks, beads, bangles, rings, and seals .
  - **Crafts**: The Harappan craftspeople excelled in various forms of art, such as pottery, terracotta, sculpture, painting, jewelry, and textiles . They used clay, stone, wood, bone, shell, ivory, and metal to create various objects, such as pots, vases, figurines, toys, seals, tablets, and coins . They also used cotton, wool, and silk to weave fabrics, and dyed them with natural colors . They also decorated their products with geometric, floral, animal, and human designs .
  - **Trade**: The Harappan civilization had a flourishing trade network, both within and outside the subcontinent . They traded with other regions, such as Mesopotamia, Persia, Central Asia, and the Arabian Peninsula, using land and sea routes . They exchanged goods, such as grains, cotton, spices, metals, gems, ivory, and shells, for items, such as wool, silver, copper, tin, lapis lazuli, and turquoise . They also used a system of standardized weights and measures, and a script, to facilitate trade and commerce .
