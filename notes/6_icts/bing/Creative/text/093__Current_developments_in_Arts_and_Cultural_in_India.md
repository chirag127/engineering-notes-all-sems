### Current developments in Arts and Cultural in India

- India is a country with a rich and diverse cultural heritage that manifests itself in various forms of art, music, dance, literature, architecture, and festivals. India has also been witnessing some significant developments in the field of arts and culture in recent years, both at the national and international level. Some of these developments are:

- India has nominated **Hoysala Temples** in Karnataka for inscription in the **World Heritage List** of UNESCO for the year 2022. The Hoysala Temples are a group of 50 temples built between the 11th and 14th centuries by the Hoysala dynasty, which are known for their intricate carvings and sculptures depicting Hindu mythology and iconography.

- India has also successfully retrieved a **Goat-Headed Yogini Statue** from the United States, which was stolen from a temple in Uttar Pradesh in 1993. The statue is a rare example of the **Tantric cult** of Yoginis, which flourished in India between the 8th and 13th centuries. The statue was handed over to the Indian Embassy in Washington DC by the US Department of Homeland Security in December 2021.

- India has also witnessed the emergence of some contemporary artists who are shaping the global art scene with their innovative and diverse works. Some of these artists are **Nalini Malani**, who is known for her multimedia installations and paintings that explore themes of feminism, violence, and mythology; **Bharti Kher**, who uses bindis, saris, and animal sculptures to create works that challenge stereotypes and cultural norms; **Subodh Gupta**, who transforms everyday objects like utensils, bicycles, and luggage into monumental sculptures that reflect on migration, consumption, and identity; and **Jitish Kallat**, who combines photography, painting, and sculpture to create works that explore the urban and cosmic realities.

- India has also been actively promoting its cultural diversity and heritage through various initiatives and platforms. Some of these are:

  - The **Azadi Ka Amrit Mahotsav**, which is a 75-week long celebration of the 75th anniversary of India's independence, starting from March 2021 to August 2022. The Mahotsav aims to showcase the achievements and contributions of India in various fields, as well as to inspire the youth and future generations to follow the ideals of the freedom struggle.

  - The **UNESCO Global Network of Learning Cities**, which is a network of cities that promote lifelong learning and sustainable development. Recently, three cities in India have joined this network, namely **Thrissur** and **Nilambur** in Kerala and **Warangal** in Telangana. These cities have demonstrated their commitment to enhancing the quality of education, culture, and social inclusion in their urban contexts.

  - The **Kochi-Muziris Biennale**, which is India's largest and most prestigious contemporary art event, held every two years in Kochi, Kerala. The Biennale showcases the works of artists from India and abroad, as well as organizes various cultural and educational programs for the public. The fifth edition of the Biennale is scheduled to take place from December 2021 to April 2022, with the theme of **In Our Veins Flow Ink and Fire**.

- These are some of the current developments in arts and culture in India that reflect the country's vibrant and dynamic cultural landscape. India's arts and culture not only celebrate the past, but also engage with the present and envision the future.
