### Socio religious reform movement of 19th century in India

- Socio religious reform movement of 19th century in India refers to the efforts of various individuals and groups to bring about changes in the social and religious practices of Indian society in order to modernize it and make it compatible with the values of democracy, equality, justice and human rights.
- The movement was influenced by the following factors:
  - The presence of colonial government on Indian soil, which introduced the English language and certain modern ideas of liberty, fraternity, democracy and justice.
  - The religious and social ills that plagued Indian society, such as superstitions, caste oppression, rituals, priesthood, etc.
  - The depressing position of women, who suffered from practices like female infanticide, child marriage, polygamy, purdah system, sati, etc.
  - The spread of education and increased awareness of the world, which exposed the Indians to the ancient glory of their civilization and the achievements of other nations.
  - The growth of nationalist sentiments and the emergence of new economic forces, which demanded a reform and democratization of the social institutions and religious outlook of the Indian people.
- The movement can be broadly classified into two types:
  - Reformist movements, which responded with the time and scientific temper of the modern era and sought to reform Hinduism by eliminating its defects and irrationalities. Some of the reformist movements were:
    - Brahmo Samaj, founded by Raja Ram Mohan Roy in 1828, which fought against idol worship, polytheism, caste system, and other social evils like sati, polygamy, child marriage, etc. It also advocated women's rights like widow remarriage and education of women.
    - Prarthana Samaj, founded by Atmaram Pandurang in 1867, which followed the ideals of Brahmo Samaj and also worked for social reforms like inter-caste dining, inter-caste marriage, upliftment of the depressed classes, etc.
    - Aligarh Movement, founded by Sir Syed Ahmed Khan in 1875, which aimed at the educational and social upliftment of the Muslims. It established the Mohammedan Anglo-Oriental College in Aligarh, which later became the Aligarh Muslim University. It also promoted a rational and modern interpretation of Islam and tried to reconcile it with Western science and culture.
  - Revivalist movements, which started reviving ancient Indian traditions and thoughts and believed that Western thinking ruined Indian culture and ethos. They sought to restore the purity and glory of the original religion by rejecting the later accretions and corruptions. Some of the revivalist movements were:
    - Arya Samaj, founded by Swami Dayanand Saraswati in 1875, which opposed idolatry, polytheism, rituals, priesthood, animal sacrifice, child marriage and the caste system. It also encouraged the dissemination of Western scientific knowledge and the study of the Vedas as the source of true knowledge and religion.
    - Ramakrishna Mission, founded by Swami Vivekananda in 1897, which propagated the message of Ramakrishna Paramahamsa, who believed in the harmony of all religions and the divinity of every soul. It also engaged in various social service activities like education, health care, relief work, etc.
    - Theosophical Society, founded by Madame Blavatsky and Colonel Olcott in 1875, which aimed at promoting the study of ancient wisdom and occult sciences and reviving the interest in Hindu philosophy and culture. It also supported the cause of Indian nationalism and social reform.
