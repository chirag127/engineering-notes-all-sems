### Evolution of script in India

- A script is a collection of characters used to write one or more languages. A language is a method of communication. Scripts are writing systems that allow the transcription of a language, via alphabet sets.
- The first evidence of a writing system in India can be seen in the **Indus Valley Civilization** (IVC), which flourished from 2500 BCE to 1900 BCE. The **Indus script** is a logo-syllabic script, consisting of about 400 pictorial and abstract signs. The script is found on seals, pottery, copper tablets, and other artifacts. The script has not been deciphered yet, and its linguistic affiliation is unknown .
- After the decline of the IVC, there is a gap of about 1500 years in the history of writing in India. The next major script to emerge is the **Brahmi script**, which is the ancestor of most modern Indian scripts. The Brahmi script is an alphasyllabary, meaning that each letter represents a consonant followed by an inherent vowel, and diacritical marks are used to modify the vowel sound. The Brahmi script was developed under Semitic influence around the 7th century BCE, and was originally written from left to right .
- The Brahmi script was used to write various languages, such as Prakrit, Sanskrit, Pali, and Tamil. The earliest inscriptions in Brahmi script date from the 3rd century BCE, during the reign of Emperor Ashoka, who used the script to spread his edicts across his empire. The Brahmi script was also used to write the earliest Buddhist and Jain texts.
- Due to differences in cultures, regions, and writing materials, the Brahmi script evolved into different types depending on the region. Some of the major regional variants of Brahmi are:

  - **Kharosthi**: A script derived from Aramaic, used in the northwest of India, especially in Gandhara and Bactria. It was written from right to left, and had a distinctive horizontal bar on top of each letter. It was used from the 4th century BCE to the 4th century CE.
  - **Gupta**: A script that developed from the late Brahmi, used in northern and central India from the 4th to the 6th century CE. It was characterized by more rounded and stylized shapes, and was used to write Sanskrit and Prakrit. It was the script of the Gupta Empire, which is considered the golden age of Indian culture.
  - **Siddhamatrika**: A script that developed from the Gupta, used in northern India from the 6th to the 12th century CE. It was mainly used for writing Sanskrit, especially for religious and tantric texts. It was also the script of the Pala Empire, which patronized Buddhism and art.
  - **Nagari**: A script that developed from the late Gupta, used in northern and western India from the 7th century CE onwards. It was mainly used for writing Sanskrit and Hindi, and became the basis of the modern Devanagari script, which is used for several languages, such as Hindi, Marathi, Nepali, and Sanskrit.
  - **Sharada**: A script that developed from the late Gupta, used in Kashmir and Punjab from the 8th to the 19th century CE. It was mainly used for writing Sanskrit and Kashmiri, and was also the script of the Kashmiri Shaivism tradition. It was replaced by the Perso-Arabic script and the Devanagari script in the modern period.
  - **Gurmukhi**: A script that developed from the Sharada, used in Punjab from the 16th century CE onwards. It was mainly used for writing Punjabi, and was also the script of the Sikh scriptures, the Guru Granth Sahib. It is still used today by the Sikhs and some Punjabi speakers.
  - **Takri**: A script that developed from the Sharada, used in the western Himalayan region from the 9th to the 19th century CE. It was mainly used for writing various Pahari languages, such as Dogri, Kangri, and Kulluvi. It was replaced by the Devanagari script and the Perso-Arabic script in the modern period
