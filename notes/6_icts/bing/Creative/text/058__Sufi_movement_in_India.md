### Sufi movement in India

- Sufism is a mystical branch of Islam that seeks a direct personal experience of God through love, devotion, and self-purification.
- Sufism emerged in the 8th and 9th centuries in West Asia and spread to India through various invasions, migrations, and trade contacts.
- Sufis were organized into orders or silsilahs, each headed by a spiritual leader or pir, who had a chain of disciples or murids. The murids followed the teachings and practices of their pir and lived in a hospice or khanqah.
- Sufism in India was influenced by Hindu and Buddhist traditions and adopted some of their rituals, such as meditation, yoga, and chanting. Sufis also respected the diversity of religious beliefs and sought to promote harmony and tolerance among different communities.
- Sufism in India can be broadly divided into two categories: ba-shara (those who followed the Islamic law or sharia) and be-shara (those who were not bound by it). The former were more orthodox and conservative, while the latter were more liberal and eclectic.
- Some of the prominent Sufi orders in India were:

  - Chishti order: Founded by Khwaja Moinuddin Chishti in the 12th century, it was the most popular and influential order in India. It emphasized love, service, and music as the means of attaining God. Some of its famous saints were Khwaja Qutbuddin Bakhtiyar Kaki, Khwaja Fariduddin Ganjshakar, Khwaja Nizamuddin Auliya, and Amir Khusrau.
  - Suhrawardi order: Founded by Sheikh Shihabuddin Suhrawardi in the 12th century, it was known for its strict adherence to the sharia and its emphasis on rationalism and philosophy. Some of its famous saints were Sheikh Bahauddin Zakariya, Sheikh Ruknuddin, and Sheikh Hamiduddin Nagori.
  - Qadiri order: Founded by Sheikh Abdul Qadir Jilani in the 12th century, it was known for its miracles and charismatic leadership. Some of its famous saints were Sheikh Nizamuddin Auliya, Sheikh Nasiruddin Chiragh Dehlavi, and Sheikh Abdul Haq Muhaddis Dehlavi.
  - Naqshbandi order: Founded by Khwaja Baha-ud-Din Naqshband in the 14th century, it was known for its silent meditation and avoidance of music and dance. Some of its famous saints were Khwaja Alauddin Sabir, Khwaja Ahmad Sirhindi, and Khwaja Muhammad Masoom.
  - Shattari order: Founded by Sheikh Abdullah Shattar in the 15th century, it was known for its esoteric teachings and practices, such as alchemy, astrology, and numerology. Some of its famous saints were Sheikh Muhammad Ghaus, Sheikh Wajihuddin Alvi, and Sheikh Abdul Quddus Gangohi.

- Sufism had a significant impact on the social, cultural, and religious life of India. It contributed to the growth of regional languages, literature, art, music, and architecture. It also fostered a spirit of syncretism, humanism, and pluralism among the people. It influenced the Bhakti movement, which was a parallel movement of devotional Hinduism. It also attracted many converts to Islam, especially from the lower castes and classes.
