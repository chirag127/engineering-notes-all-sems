### Music as Cultural Heritage of India

- Music is an integral part of the socio-religious and cultural life of India. It has a history spanning several millennia and developed over several geo-locations spanning the sub-continent.  
- Music in India can be classified into various genres, such as classical, folk, popular, rock, and pop. Each genre has its own features, styles, instruments, and exponents.  
- Classical music is one of the oldest and most refined forms of music in India. It is based on a system of ragas (melodic modes) and talas (rhythmic cycles). There are two main traditions of classical music in India: Carnatic and Hindustani.   
- Carnatic music is the classical music of South India. It is characterized by a high degree of improvisation, complex ornamentation, and a rich repertoire of compositions. Some of the prominent composers of Carnatic music are Purandara Dasa, Tyagaraja, Muthuswami Dikshitar, and Syama Sastri. Some of the popular instruments used in Carnatic music are veena, violin, mridangam, ghatam, and flute.    
- Hindustani music is the classical music of North India. It is influenced by ancient Hindu musical traditions, Vedic philosophy, and Persian elements. It is characterized by a more flexible approach to ragas, a greater emphasis on vocal music, and a variety of musical forms such as dhrupad, khyal, thumri, ghazal, and qawwali. Some of the prominent composers of Hindustani music are Amir Khusrau, Tansen, Baiju Bawra, and Miyan Sadarang. Some of the popular instruments used in Hindustani music are sitar, sarod, tabla, harmonium, and bansuri.   
- Folk music is the music of the common people, reflecting their regional, linguistic, and cultural diversity. It is often associated with festivals, rituals, celebrations, and social movements. Some of the popular forms of folk music in India are borgeet, baul, bhajan, kirtan, shyama sangeet, ramprasadi, rabindra sangeet, nazrul geeti, dwijendrageeti, atulprasadi, prabhat samgiita, chaiti, kajari, sufi, and bhangra. Some of the popular instruments used in folk music are dhol, dholak, ektara, dotara, sarangi, and daf.  
- Popular music is the music that appeals to the masses, influenced by various genres of music from within and outside India. It is often produced and consumed through mass media, such as films, radio, television, and internet. Some of the popular forms of popular music in India are filmi, indi-pop, rock, jazz, blues, hip hop, and fusion. Some of the popular singers and composers of popular music in India are Lata Mangeshkar, Kishore Kumar, A.R. Rahman, R.D. Burman, Asha Bhosle, Mohammed Rafi, Shankar-Jaikishan, S.D. Burman, Ilaiyaraaja, K.J. Yesudas, S.P. Balasubrahmanyam, Sonu Nigam, Shreya Ghoshal, Arijit Singh, Vishal-Shekhar, Pritam, and Amit Trivedi.  
- Music is a cultural heritage of India that reflects its diversity, unity, continuity, and creativity. It is a source of inspiration, expression, and enjoyment for millions of people. It is also a medium of communication, education, and social change. Music is an art that transcends boundaries and connects hearts.
