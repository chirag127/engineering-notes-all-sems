### Medicine in India and Ancient India

- Medicine in India has a long and rich history, dating back to the prehistoric times and influenced by various cultures and traditions.
- The earliest evidence of medical practices in India can be traced to the Indus Valley Civilization (c. 2500-1500 BCE), where dentistry, trepanation, hygiene and sanitation were practiced. The Indus people also cultivated and traded medicinal plants and herbs with other regions.
- The Vedic period (c. 1500-500 BCE) saw the development of the Ayurveda system of medicine, which is based on the concept of three elementary substances (air, phlegm and bile) and their balance in the body. The Vedas, especially the Atharvaveda, contain hymns and charms for the treatment of various diseases and ailments, such as fever, cough, diarrhea, edema, abscesses, seizures, tumors and skin diseases. The Vedic healers also used mantras, rituals, medicines and surgery to cure the patients.
- The post-Vedic period (c. 500 BCE-1000 CE) was the golden age of Indian medicine, marked by the production of the Charaka-samhita and Sushruta-samhita, the two most influential treatises on Ayurveda. Charaka was a physician who compiled and edited the Charaka-samhita, which covers topics such as anatomy, physiology, pathology, diagnosis, prognosis, treatment, pharmacology, ethics and hygiene. Sushruta was a surgeon who authored the Sushruta-samhita, which deals with topics such as surgery, anesthesia, obstetrics, gynecology, ophthalmology, dentistry, plastic surgery and cosmetics. Both Charaka and Sushruta described the existence of a large number of diseases and their classifications, as well as the methods of prevention and cure. They also gave detailed descriptions of the anatomy and physiology of the human body, based on observation and dissection. They also introduced the concepts of clinical trials, medical ethics and medical education.
- Another important system of medicine that developed in India was the Unani system, which originated in ancient Greece and was brought to India by the Muslim invaders and rulers. The Unani system is based on the theory of four humors (blood, phlegm, yellow bile and black bile) and their balance in the body. The Unani system also incorporates elements of Persian, Arabic and Indian medicine, and uses herbs, minerals, animal products and other substances for treatment. The most famous Unani physician in India was Hakim Ajmal Khan (1868-1927), who founded the Tibbia College in Delhi and was a pioneer of modern Unani education and research.
- Another system of medicine that emerged in India was the Siddha system, which originated in Tamil Nadu and is based on the concept of eight vital principles (soul, life, sky, air, fire, water, earth and body) and their balance in the body. The Siddha system also uses herbs, metals, minerals, animal products and other substances for treatment. The most famous Siddha physician in India was Agastya, who is considered to be the founder of the Siddha system and the author of the Agastya-samhita, which covers topics such as medicine, alchemy, astrology, yoga and spirituality.
- Apart from these systems, there are also various folk and tribal healing traditions in India, which use local plants, animals, minerals and other substances for treatment. These traditions are based on the beliefs and practices of the different communities and regions, and often involve the use of magic, rituals, charms, amulets and prayers. Some of the examples of folk and tribal healing traditions are the Bhil, Gond, Santhal, Khasi, Naga, Munda and Bodo traditions.
