### Physics in India and Ancient India

Physics is the branch of science that deals with the nature and properties of matter and energy. Physics in India and ancient India has a long and rich history, dating back to the Vedic period and beyond. Some of the contributions of Indian physicists and philosophers to the development of physics are:

- The concept of energy and mass equivalence, as well as the idea of discrete particles of light, were expressed in the Vedas, the ancient scriptures of Hinduism. The Vedas also described the wave-particle duality of matter and radiation, the space-time continuum, and the atomic structure of matter .
- The Nyaya and Vaisheshika schools of philosophy, which emerged around the 6th century BCE, developed a logical and empirical approach to physics, based on observation, inference, and analogy. They proposed the existence of five basic elements (earth, water, fire, air, and ether), and nine types of substances (including atoms, molecules, and souls). They also discussed the concepts of motion, force, gravity, magnetism, sound, light, and heat .
- The astronomer and mathematician Aryabhata (476-550 CE) proposed a heliocentric model of the solar system, and calculated the value of pi and the circumference of the earth with remarkable accuracy. He also gave the correct explanation of lunar and solar eclipses, and the causes of day and night .
- The physicist and mathematician Bhaskara II (1114-1185 CE) wrote a treatise on gravity, in which he stated that the earth attracts all bodies towards its center. He also described the laws of reflection and refraction of light, and the principle of the lever .
- The chemist and metallurgist Nagarjuna (10th century CE) wrote a book on alchemy, in which he described the methods of extracting metals from ores, and the preparation of various alloys and compounds. He also discovered the technique of distillation, and the properties of mercury and sulfur .
- The philosopher and physicist Kanada (6th century BCE) formulated the atomic theory of matter, in which he postulated that all substances are composed of indivisible and indestructible atoms, which combine in different ways to form molecules. He also classified atoms into six types, corresponding to the five elements and the soul .

These are some of the examples of the achievements of physics in India and ancient India, which show the depth and breadth of the scientific knowledge and inquiry of the Indian civilization.
