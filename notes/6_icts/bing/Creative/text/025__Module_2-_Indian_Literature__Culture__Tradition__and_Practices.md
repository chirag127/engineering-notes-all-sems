## Module 2- Indian Literature, Culture, Tradition, and Practices

- Indian literature is the literature produced on the Indian subcontinent until 1947 and in the Republic of India thereafter. It includes literature in 22 officially recognised languages and many other regional languages and dialects.  
- Indian literature has a rich and diverse history, spanning from the oral literature of the Vedas (1500-1200 BCE) to the contemporary literature of the 21st century. 
- Indian literature reflects the cultural, religious, social, and political diversity of India, as well as its interactions with other regions and peoples. 
- Some of the major genres and forms of Indian literature are:
  - Epic poetry: Long narrative poems that depict the heroic deeds and adventures of legendary or historical figures, such as the Ramayana and the Mahabharata. 
  - Lyric poetry: Short poems that express personal emotions, feelings, or thoughts, such as the bhakti (devotional) poems of the medieval saints, the ghazals (love poems) of the Urdu poets, and the modern poems of Rabindranath Tagore and others.  
  - Drama: Plays that are performed on stage, often with music, dance, and costumes, such as the classical Sanskrit plays of Kalidasa, the folk plays of the regional traditions, and the modern plays of Girish Karnad and others.  
  - Prose: Written or spoken language that is not in verse, such as the stories, novels, essays, and biographies of various authors, such as the Pali canon of the Buddhists, the Tamil Sangam literature of the ancient Tamils, the Persian and Urdu literature of the Mughal era, and the English and vernacular literature of the colonial and postcolonial periods.  
- Indian literature has been influenced by various literary traditions and movements, such as the Sanskrit literature of the ancient and classical periods, the Pali and Prakrit literature of the Buddhists and Jains, the Tamil literature of the Sangam period, the Persian and Arabic literature of the Islamic period, the English literature of the colonial period, and the modern literature of the postcolonial and globalized era.  
- Indian literature has also influenced and been influenced by the literature of other regions and countries, such as the literature of Southeast Asia, Central Asia, West Asia, Europe, and America.  
- Indian literature has been recognized and celebrated by various national and international awards, such as the Sahitya Akademi Award, the Jnanpith Award, the Nobel Prize in Literature, and the Booker Prize.
