### India’s Dominance up to Pre-colonial Times and Ancient India

- India is a subcontinent in South Asia that has a long and rich history of civilization and culture. It is home to diverse religions, languages, and ethnic groups. India has also been influenced by various external forces, such as invaders, traders, and colonizers, throughout its history.
- India's pre-colonial history can be divided into four major periods: the ancient period, the classical period, the medieval period, and the early modern period. Each period witnessed the rise and fall of different empires, kingdoms, and dynasties, as well as the development of art, literature, science, and religion.
- The ancient period (c. 2500 BCE - 600 BCE) is marked by the emergence of the Indus Valley Civilization, one of the earliest urban civilizations in the world. The Indus Valley Civilization flourished in the northwest of India, along the Indus River and its tributaries. It had a sophisticated urban planning, sanitation, trade, and writing system. The Indus Valley Civilization declined around 1500 BCE, possibly due to environmental changes, invasions, or internal conflicts.
- The classical period (c. 600 BCE - 600 CE) is characterized by the formation of the first Indian states and empires, such as the Magadha, Maurya, Shunga, Kushan, Satavahana, Gupta, and Harsha empires. This period also saw the emergence of major religious and philosophical movements, such as Hinduism, Buddhism, Jainism, and the Upanishads. The classical period is considered the golden age of Indian culture, as it witnessed remarkable achievements in art, architecture, literature, mathematics, astronomy, medicine, and metallurgy.
- The medieval period (c. 600 CE - 1526 CE) is marked by the invasion and expansion of various foreign powers, such as the Huns, the Arabs, the Turks, the Mongols, and the Afghans, into India. These invaders brought new religions, such as Islam and Sikhism, as well as new cultures, languages, and technologies. The medieval period also saw the rise and fall of several regional kingdoms and dynasties, such as the Chalukyas, the Pallavas, the Rashtrakutas, the Cholas, the Pandyas, the Cheras, the Pratiharas, the Palas, the Senas, the Chandelas, the Hoysalas, the Kakatiyas, the Vijayanagara, the Bahmani, the Delhi Sultanate, and the Deccan Sultanates. The medieval period also witnessed the development of various forms of art, music, dance, literature, and architecture, such as the temple, the mosque, the fort, the minaret, the stupa, the chaitya, the mandapa, the gopuram, the kirtan, the qawwali, the bhakti, the sufism, the kavya, the prabandha, the dastan, the ragamala, and the mughal painting.
- The early modern period (c. 1526 CE - 1857 CE) is characterized by the establishment and decline of the Mughal Empire, one of the largest and most powerful empires in Indian history. The Mughal Empire was founded by Babur, a descendant of Timur and Genghis Khan, who defeated the last Delhi Sultan, Ibrahim Lodi, at the Battle of Panipat in 1526. The Mughal Empire reached its zenith under Akbar, who expanded the empire to cover most of India and promoted religious tolerance, cultural synthesis, and administrative reforms. The Mughal Empire declined after the death of Aurangzeb, who imposed a strict Islamic orthodoxy and waged costly wars against the Marathas, the Sikhs, and the Rajputs. The Mughal Empire also faced challenges from the rise of regional powers, such as the Maratha Confederacy, the Sikh Empire, the Bengal Subah, the Hyderabad State, the Mysore Kingdom, and the Travancore Kingdom. The early modern period also saw the arrival and expansion of the European powers, such as the Portuguese, the Dutch, the French, and the British, who competed for trade and influence in India. The British East India Company gradually gained control over most of India through a combination of diplomacy, warfare, and alliances with local rulers.
