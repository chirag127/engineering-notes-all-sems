### Bhakti Movement in India

- Bhakti movement in Hinduism refers to ideas and engagement that emerged in the medieval era on love and devotion to religious concepts built around one or more gods and goddesses. 
- Bhakti movement preached against the caste system using the local languages so that the message reached the masses. 
- One who practices bhakti is called a bhakta. Bhakti means "attachment, devotion, fondness for, homage, faith or love, worship, piety to something as a spiritual, religious principle or means of salvation".  
- Bhakti movement originated in Tamilakam during 6th century CE, and gained prominence through the poems and teachings of the Vaishnava Alvars and Shaiva Nayanars before spreading northwards. 
- Bhakti movement reached its zenith between the 15th and 17th century CE, and regionally developed around different gods and goddesses, such as Shiva, Vishnu, Shakti, and others. 
- Bhakti movement was inspired by many poet-saints, who championed a wide range of philosophical positions ranging from theistic dualism of Dvaita to absolute monism of Advaita Vedanta. 
- Bhakti movement was considered an influential social reformation in Hinduism in that it provided an individual-focused alternative path to spirituality regardless of one's birth or gender. 
- Bhakti movement also incorporated elements from Islam, such as Sufism and surrender to God. 
- Bhakti movement also influenced India's freedom struggle, as it fostered a sense of unity, nationalism, and resistance against foreign rule. 
- Scriptures of the Bhakti movement include the Bhagavad Gita, Bhagavata Purana, and Padma Purana.
