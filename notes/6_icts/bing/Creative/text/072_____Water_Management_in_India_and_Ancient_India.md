### Water Management in Ancient India and India

- Water management is the planning, development, distribution, and use of water resources for various purposes, such as irrigation, drinking, sanitation, power generation, and ecology.
- Water management in ancient India was influenced by the geographical, climatic, cultural, and religious diversity of the subcontinent.
- Some of the main features of water management in ancient India were:

  - The use of natural and artificial water bodies, such as rivers, lakes, ponds, wells, tanks, canals, and stepwells, for storing and supplying water.
  - The development of hydrologic knowledge and techniques, such as the concept of the water cycle, rainfall measurement, water quality assessment, and water treatment methods.
  - The construction of sophisticated hydraulic structures, such as dams, reservoirs, aqueducts, sluices, and water wheels, for controlling and harnessing water flow.
  - The implementation of water conservation and management practices, such as rainwater harvesting, watershed management, crop rotation, and water sharing systems.
  - The integration of water management with social, economic, and religious aspects of life, such as water rights, water taxes, water festivals, and water worship.

- Some of the examples of water management in ancient India are:

  - The Harappan civilization (c. 3000-1500 BCE), which had a well-planned urban water supply and drainage system, including public and private wells, baths, and latrines .
  - The Vedic period (c. 1500-500 BCE), which had a rich literature on water-related topics, such as the Rigveda, which contains hymns to various water deities, and the Arthashastra, which prescribes rules and regulations for water management.
  - The Mauryan empire (c. 322-185 BCE), which had a centralized administration of water resources, including the appointment of water officers, the collection of water taxes, and the construction of large-scale irrigation works, such as the Sudarshana lake in Gujarat.
  - The Gupta period (c. 320-550 CE), which witnessed the flourishing of science and technology, including the development of water measurement devices, such as the ghatika (water clock) and the jalayantra (rain gauge), and water treatment methods, such as the use of brass vessels and plants to purify water .
  - The medieval period (c. 600-1700 CE), which saw the emergence of various regional kingdoms and cultures, each with their own distinctive styles and innovations in water management, such as the Chola tanks in Tamil Nadu, the Qanat system in Kashmir, the Bawari system in Rajasthan, and the Ahars and Pynes system in Bihar.
  - The Mughal period (c. 1526-1857 CE), which introduced new elements and influences in water management, such as the Persian-style gardens, fountains, and canals, the use of water for aesthetic and recreational purposes, and the patronage of water-related arts and crafts.
