### Pyrotechnics in India and Ancient India

- Pyrotechnics is the art and science of creating and using fireworks for entertainment, military, or ceremonial purposes.
- Fireworks and pyrotechnic shows existed as a form of royal entertainment in many medieval Indian kingdoms during festivals, events and special occasions like weddings .
- One of the earliest notes of pyrotechnical shows in India is made by Abdur Razzaq, the ambassador of the Timurid Sultan Shahrukh to the court of the Vijayanagar king Devaraya II in 1443.
- Manufacturing formulas for fireworks describing pyrotechnic mixtures are found within Kautukachintamani, a Sanskrit volume by Gajapati Prataparudradeva (1497-1539), a reputed king of Orissa .
- Gunpowder, an essential ingredient of fire crackers, was unknown in ancient India. It was probably introduced by the Arabs or the Mongols around the 13th or 14th century .
- The use of fireworks in the celebration of Diwali, which is so common in India now, must have come into existence after about 1400 AD, when gunpowder came to be used in Indian warfare.
- Different chemicals produce different colors in fireworks. For example, blues are made with copper-chloride compounds, reds are made with strontium salts, and greens are made with barium chloride.
- Fireworks are also used for religious and cultural ceremonies, such as weddings, births, deaths, and New Year celebrations. They are also used for political and social events, such as Independence Day, Republic Day, and sports victories .
- Fireworks have a negative impact on the environment and human health. They cause air and noise pollution, fire hazards, injuries, and animal distress. They also contribute to global warming and climate change .
- There have been several regulations and bans on the use of fireworks in India, especially during Diwali, to reduce the harmful effects of fireworks. However, many people still violate the rules and enjoy the spectacle of fireworks .
