## Module 4-Science, Management and Indian Knowledge System

- This module covers the various aspects of science, management and Indian knowledge system that have contributed to the development of human civilization and culture.
- The module aims to provide an overview of the achievements and innovations of Indian scholars and practitioners in various fields of knowledge, such as astronomy, chemistry, mathematics, physics, agriculture, medicine, metallurgy, geography, biology, water management, textile technology, writing technology, etc.
- The module also explores the underlying principles and values of Indian knowledge system, such as holistic approach, interdependence, harmony, sustainability, ethics, spirituality, etc.
- The module also discusses the relevance and application of Indian knowledge system in the contemporary context, such as addressing the global challenges, enhancing the quality of life, promoting the diversity and pluralism, etc.

Some of the topics covered in this module are:

- Astronomy in India: The ancient Indian astronomers observed and calculated the movements and positions of celestial bodies, such as the sun, moon, planets, stars, comets, etc. They developed sophisticated mathematical and astronomical concepts and methods, such as the concept of zero, decimal system, trigonometry, algebra, geometry, etc. They also devised various instruments and calendars for astronomical observations and measurements, such as the gnomon, armillary sphere, water clock, etc. Some of the notable Indian astronomers are Aryabhata, Varahamihira, Brahmagupta, Bhaskara, etc.
- Chemistry in India: The ancient Indian chemists experimented with various substances and processes, such as metals, minerals, plants, animals, etc. They discovered and synthesized various chemical compounds and elements, such as zinc, mercury, iron, copper, tin, lead, gold, silver, etc. They also developed various techniques and applications of chemistry, such as metallurgy, alchemy, medicine, cosmetics, dyeing, tanning, etc. Some of the notable Indian chemists are Nagarjuna, Kanada, Charaka, Sushruta, etc.
- Mathematics in India: The ancient Indian mathematicians developed and refined various mathematical concepts and methods, such as the concept of zero, decimal system, place value system, fractions, negative numbers, irrational numbers, etc. They also solved various mathematical problems and equations, such as linear, quadratic, cubic, indeterminate, etc. They also contributed to various branches of mathematics, such as arithmetic, algebra, geometry, trigonometry, calculus, combinatorics, etc. Some of the notable Indian mathematicians are Aryabhata, Brahmagupta, Bhaskara, Mahavira, etc.
- Physics in India: The ancient Indian physicists studied and explained various phenomena and principles of nature, such as motion, force, gravity, light, sound, heat, electricity, magnetism, etc. They also developed various theories and models of the physical world, such as the atomic theory, the theory of relativity, the theory of vibration, the theory of ether, etc. They also invented and used various instruments and devices for physical experiments and measurements, such as the balance, the sundial, the compass, the telescope, etc. Some of the notable Indian physicists are Kanada, Vaisheshika, Nyaya, etc.
- Agriculture in India: The ancient Indian agriculturists cultivated and domesticated various crops and animals, such as rice, wheat, barley, millet, sugarcane, cotton, indigo, sesame, mustard, etc. They also developed various techniques and practices of agriculture, such as irrigation, fertilization, crop rotation, pest control, animal husbandry, etc. They also classified and categorized various plants and animals according to their characteristics and uses, such as medicinal, edible, ornamental, etc. Some of the notable Indian agriculturists are Kashyapa, Surapala, Varahamihira, etc.
- Medicine in India: The ancient Indian physicians and surgeons diagnosed and treated various diseases and disorders, such as fever, cough, asthma, diabetes, leprosy, tuberculosis, etc. They also developed various systems and branches of medicine, such as Ayurveda, Siddha, Unani, Yoga, etc. They also used various methods and materials of medicine, such as herbs, minerals, metals, animal products, etc. They also performed various surgical procedures and operations, such as cataract, rhinoplasty, lithotomy, etc. Some of the notable Indian physicians and surgeons are Charaka, Sushruta, Vagbhata, etc.
