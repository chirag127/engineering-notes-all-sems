### Fairs and Festivals as Cultural Heritage of India

India is a land of diversity and culture, where every occasion is celebrated with great enthusiasm and fervor. Fairs and festivals are an integral part of the Indian cultural heritage, as they reflect the rich traditions, customs, beliefs and values of the people. Fairs and festivals also provide an opportunity for social interaction, economic exchange, artistic expression and spiritual upliftment. Some of the major fairs and festivals of India are:

- **Makar Sankranti**: This is a harvest festival celebrated on 14th January every year, marking the transition of the sun into the zodiac sign of Capricorn. It is also known as Uttarayan, Pongal, Magh Bihu, Lohri and Khichdi in different regions of India. People fly kites, make bonfires, prepare special dishes and offer prayers to the sun god on this day .
- **Pongal**: This is a four-day-long harvest festival celebrated by the Tamils in mid-January every year. It is dedicated to the sun god, the earth goddess and the cattle. People cook rice in earthen pots, decorate their houses with kolam (rangoli), worship the cattle and enjoy various cultural activities on this occasion .
- **Rann Utsav**: This is a cultural extravaganza that showcases the beauty and diversity of the Kutch region of Gujarat. It is held every year from November to February in the white salt desert of Kutch. It features folk music, dance, art, crafts, food, adventure sports, camel safaris, cultural shows and more.
- **Pushkar Mela**: This is one of the largest and oldest cattle fairs in the world, held every year in the holy town of Pushkar in Rajasthan. It attracts thousands of pilgrims, tourists, traders and animals from across the country and abroad. It is also a religious festival, as people take a dip in the sacred Pushkar lake and worship Lord Brahma, the creator god, at his only temple in India .
- **Jaisalmer Desert Festival**: This is a three-day-long festival that celebrates the culture and heritage of the Thar desert in Rajasthan. It is held every year in February in the golden city of Jaisalmer. It features camel races, polo matches, folk music, dance, puppet shows, firework displays and more .
- **Goa Carnival**: This is a four-day-long festival that marks the onset of Lent, the period of fasting and penance before Easter. It is held every year in February or March in the coastal state of Goa. It is a legacy of the Portuguese colonial rule, and features colourful parades, floats, masks, costumes, music, dance and revelry .
- **Nehru Trophy Boat Race**: This is a prestigious and thrilling boat race that takes place every year on the second Saturday of August in the Punnamada lake in Kerala. It is named after the first Prime Minister of India, Jawaharlal Nehru, who inaugurated the event in 1952. It involves hundreds of snake boats, each manned by over a hundred rowers, competing for the coveted trophy .
- **Konark Dance Festival**: This is a classical dance festival that showcases the various forms of Indian dance, such as Odissi, Bharatanatyam, Kathak, Kuchipudi, Manipuri and Mohiniattam. It is held every year in December in the backdrop of the magnificent Sun Temple of Konark in Odisha. It is a tribute to the ancient art and architecture of India .
- **Holi**: This is a festival of colours that signifies the victory of good over evil, the arrival of spring and the end of winter. It is celebrated on the full moon day of the month of Phalguna, which usually falls in March. People smear each other with coloured powder and water, play with water guns and balloons, light bonfires, sing, dance and enjoy delicacies on this day .
