### Northern Indian languages

- Northern Indian languages are mainly the languages that belong to the Indo-Aryan branch of the Indo-European language family. 
- According to the Ministry of Home Affairs, the states and union territories that are part of the Northern Zonal Council are Chandigarh, Delhi, Haryana, Himachal Pradesh, Jammu and Kashmir, Ladakh, Punjab and Rajasthan.  
- The languages that are official in one or more of these states and union territories are Hindi, Urdu, Punjabi, Kashmiri, Dogri and English.  
- Some of the other languages spoken in this region are Bihari languages (such as Bhojpuri, Magadhi and Maithili), Pahari languages (such as Garhwali, Kumaoni and Nepali), Sindhi, Rajasthani and various tribal languages.  
- These languages have evolved from different stages of the Indo-Aryan languages, such as Old Indo-Aryan (Vedic and Classical Sanskrit), Middle Indo-Aryan (Prakrits and Apabhramshas) and New Indo-Aryan (modern languages). 
- These languages have also been influenced by other language families, such as Dravidian, Austroasiatic, Sino-Tibetan and Turkic, as well as by Persian and Arabic. 
- These languages have a rich literary and cultural heritage, and have contributed to the development of the Ganga-Jamuni tehzeeb, a syncretic culture of Hindu and Muslim traditions.
