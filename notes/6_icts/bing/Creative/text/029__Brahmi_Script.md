#### Brahmi Script

- Brahmi script is a writing system of ancient South Asia that appeared as a fully developed script in the third century BCE. 
- It is an abugida, which means that it uses a system of diacritical marks to associate vowels with consonant symbols. 
- It is the ancestor of all modern Indian scripts and several hundred scripts found in Southeast and East Asia, collectively known as the Brahmic scripts.  
- The earliest and best-known Brahmi inscriptions are the rock-cut edicts of Ashoka, a Mauryan emperor who ruled most of South Asia from 268 to 232 BCE. 
- The Brahmi script was deciphered by James Prinsep, a British scholar and official, in the 1830s, with the help of other scholars and epigraphers. 
- The origin of the Brahmi script is still debated, with some scholars suggesting that it was derived from or influenced by one or more contemporary Semitic scripts, and others favoring an indigenous origin or a connection to the Indus script. 
- The Brahmi script is mentioned in the ancient Indian texts of Hinduism, Jainism and Buddhism, as well as their Chinese translations. For example, the Lalitavistara Sutra, a Buddhist text, lists 64 scripts, with Brahmi being the first one. 
- The Brahmi script only underwent minor evolutionary changes from the Mauryan period to the early Gupta period (4th century CE), and it is thought that a literate person could still read and understand Mauryan inscriptions as late as the 4th century CE. 
- The Brahmi script was gradually replaced by its descendants in different regions of South and Southeast Asia, such as Devanagari, Gujarati, Bengali, Tamil, Sinhala, Khmer, Thai, Lao, etc. 
- The Brahmi script has been encoded in the Unicode standard since 2013, in the range U+11000–U+1107F.
