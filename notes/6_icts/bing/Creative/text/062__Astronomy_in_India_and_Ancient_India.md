### Astronomy in India and Ancient India

- Astronomy has a long history in the Indian subcontinent, stretching from pre-historic to modern times. 
- Some of the earliest roots of Indian astronomy can be dated to the period of Indus Valley civilisation or earlier, when some cosmological concepts and notions of the movement of heavenly bodies and the course of the year were present in the Vedas.  
- Astronomy later developed as a discipline of Vedanga, or one of the "auxiliary disciplines" associated with the study of the Vedas, dating 1500 BCE or older.  
- The oldest known text on Indian astronomy is the Vedanga Jyotisha, dated to 1400–1200 BCE (with the extant form possibly from 700 to 600 BCE). It includes the details about the Sun, Moon, nakshatras, lunisolar calendar, and the rules for determining the positions of planets.  
- Indian astronomy was influenced by Greek astronomy beginning in the 4th century BCE and through the early centuries of the Common Era, for example by the Yavanajataka and the Romaka Siddhanta, which were Sanskrit translations of Greek texts.   
- Indian astronomy flourished in the 5th–6th century, with Aryabhata, whose work, Aryabhatiya, represented the pinnacle of astronomical knowledge at the time. The Aryabhatiya is composed of four sections, covering topics such as units of time, methods for determining the positions of planets, the cause of day and night, and several other cosmological concepts.  
- Later, Indian astronomy significantly influenced Muslim astronomy, Chinese astronomy, European astronomy, and others. Other astronomers of the classical era who further elaborated on Aryabhata's work include Brahmagupta, Varahamihira and Lalla.  
- An identifiable native Indian astronomical tradition remained active throughout the medieval period and into the 16th or 17th century, especially within the Kerala school of astronomy and mathematics. 
- Astronomy in ancient India can be divided into two major classes: Siddhantic astronomy, which was based on mathematical calculations and astronomical models, and Samhita astronomy, which was based on observational methods and astrological predictions. 
- Astronomy in ancient India was based on naked-eye observation occasionally supported by simple instruments, such as the gnomon, the clepsydra, the armillary sphere, and the astrolabe.  
- Astronomy in ancient India was closely associated with religion, ritual, calendar, and astrology, as well as with mathematics, medicine, and other branches of knowledge.  
- Astronomy in ancient India developed to a sophisticated level as the sages of ancient India were keen observers of the sky generation after generation. They discovered many astronomical phenomena, such as the precession of the equinoxes, the heliocentric model, the lunar and solar eclipses, the phases of the moon, the retrograde motion of planets, and the concept of zero.
