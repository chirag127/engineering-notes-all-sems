### Sangama Literature in India

- Sangama literature is the name given to the earliest available Tamil literature, which is one of the oldest languages in the world.
- Sangama literature is thought to have been produced in three literary academies, or sangams, in Madurai, India, from the 1st to the 4th century CE .
- The word 'sangam' literally means association, and it implies an association of Tamil poets that flourished in ancient southern India.
- The ancient Tamil sage Agastya is traditionally believed to have chaired the first Tamil sangam in Madurai. This period is known as the Sangam Period.
- The three chief Tamil kingdoms of this period were the Cheras, the Cholas and the Pandyas.
- The term sangam was coined by later scholars. The original term used by the poets was chankam.
- In total, there are about 2300 poems that are attributable to 473 poets.
- Sangama literature can be classified into two categories based on the context and interpretation: Aham (inner) and Puram (outer).
  - Aham literature deals with abstract topics such as love, sexual relations, human emotions, etc.
  - Puram literature deals with concrete topics such as heroism, customs, social life, ethics, philanthropy, etc.
- Sangama literature can also be classified into two groups based on the period of composition: Patinenmelkanakku and Patinenkilkanakku.
  - Patinenmelkanakku means the collection of eighteen major works, and it consists of the oldest surviving Tamil poetry. It is dated between 200 BCE and 100 BCE.
  - Patinenkilkanakku means the collection of eighteen minor works, and it consists of later poetry compositions. It is dated between 100 CE and 500 CE. Some of the famous works in this group are Thirukkural, Naladiyar, Palamoli, etc.
- The oldest extant Tamil work till date is Tolkappiyam, a book of grammar and rhetoric, composed by Tolkappiyar. It is dated between 4th and 5th century CE, and it belongs to the second sangam .
- Sangama literature is unique in early Indian literature, as it is mostly secular and free from religious influences. It provides valuable information on the social, political, economic and cultural aspects of ancient Tamil society .
