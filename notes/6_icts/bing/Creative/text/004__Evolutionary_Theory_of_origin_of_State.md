#### Evolutionary Theory of origin of State

- According to the Evolutionary Theory of the Origin of the State, State is the product of history. It was formed by social evolution. This theory tells us that the state was formed as a result of several social circumstances and it reached its present form through the process of evolution.
- This theory is more scientific and realistic than the other theories of the origin of the state, such as the Divine Theory, the Force Theory, or the Social Contract Theory, which are based on myths, assumptions, or abstractions.
- The Evolutionary Theory traces the origin of the state from the primitive forms of human society, such as the family, the clan, the tribe, and the village. It shows how these social units gradually developed into larger and more complex political organizations, such as the city-state, the nation-state, and the modern state.
- The Evolutionary Theory also identifies the various factors that contributed to the growth and development of the state. These factors include kinship, religion, property, defence, force, and political consciousness .
- Kinship was the first and strongest bond of unity among the early humans. It was based on blood relationship and common ancestry. Family was the first link in the process of the evolution of the state. With the expansion of the family, new families arose and the multiplication of families led to the formation of clans and tribes. Kinship was the only factor that bound the people together in the absence of any other authority or law .
- Religion provided the second bond of unity in early society. It was based on the belief in supernatural powers and the worship of common ancestors, totems, or gods. Religion gave a sense of identity and solidarity to the people and also a source of authority and law. The priests or the elders were the first rulers and judges of the society. Religion also influenced the social customs, moral values, and political institutions of the people .
- Property and defence were the third and fourth factors that influenced the evolution of the state. Property emerged with the development of agriculture, animal husbandry, and trade. It created a sense of ownership and attachment to the land and the resources. It also led to the division of society into classes, such as the rich and the poor, the owners and the workers, the rulers and the ruled. Defence was necessary to protect the property and the people from external enemies and internal conflicts. It required the organization of military forces and the establishment of laws and order. Property and defence gave rise to the need for a central authority and a common government .
- Force was the fifth factor that played a role in the origin of the state. It was used by the stronger and more powerful groups to conquer, subjugate, or assimilate the weaker and smaller groups. It also helped in the expansion and consolidation of the territory and the population of the state. Force was often justified by the claim of divine right, racial superiority, or historical destiny. Force was also resisted by the oppressed and exploited groups, who fought for their freedom, rights, and justice. Force was a source of both conflict and cooperation in the evolution of the state .
- Political consciousness was the sixth and final factor that contributed to the origin of the state. It was the awareness and understanding of the people about their political rights, duties, and interests. It was also the expression and participation of the people in the political affairs and decisions of the state. Political consciousness developed with the growth of education, communication, and democracy. It also reflected the diversity and complexity of the society and the state. Political consciousness was a factor of both change and stability in the evolution of the state .
