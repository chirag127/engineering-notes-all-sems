### Indian Cinema as a Performing Art in India

- Indian cinema is a form of artistic expression that combines storytelling, performing arts, and science to create audio-visual narratives that reflect the socio-cultural and political realities of India.
- Indian cinema has a history of more than a century, starting with the silent era in the early 1900s and evolving into the diverse and vibrant industry that it is today, producing films in various languages, genres, and styles.
- Indian cinema is influenced by various forms of Indian performing arts, such as theater, dance, music, and folk arts, that have their roots in the ancient traditions of India, such as the Natyashastra, a treatise on drama and aesthetics written by Bharata Muni around 200 BC.
- The Natyashastra is considered the foundational text of Indian performing arts, as it lays down the principles of drama, dance, music, and poetry, and introduces the concept of rasa, or the aesthetic emotion, that is the essence of any artistic expression. The Natyashastra identifies nine rasas: shringara (love), hasya (humor), karuna (compassion), raudra (anger), vira (heroism), bhayanaka (fear), bibhatsa (disgust), adbhuta (wonder), and shanta (peace).
- Indian cinema has adopted and adapted the rasa theory to create films that evoke various emotions in the audience, and to explore the themes of love, social justice, nationalism, identity, morality, and spirituality, among others.
- Indian cinema has also been influenced by the Indian People's Theatre Association (IPTA), a cultural and political movement that emerged in the 1940s as a response to the colonial oppression and the freedom struggle. IPTA aimed to use theater and other performing arts as a medium of social change and awareness, and to promote the values of secularism, democracy, and socialism. IPTA produced plays and films that highlighted the issues of the oppressed classes, such as the peasants, the workers, the women, and the minorities, and challenged the feudal, communal, and imperialist forces.
- Indian cinema has also been influenced by the global trends and movements in cinema, such as realism, expressionism, surrealism, neorealism, new wave, and postmodernism, and has experimented with various techniques, styles, and genres, such as documentary, animation, musical, thriller, comedy, horror, and fantasy, to create films that appeal to a wide range of audiences, both domestic and international.
- Indian cinema, as a performing art, has contributed to the cultural and artistic heritage of India, and has also been a source of entertainment, education, and inspiration for millions of people. Indian cinema has also been a platform for showcasing the talents and skills of various artists, such as actors, directors, writers, composers, singers, dancers, cinematographers, editors, and designers, who have enriched the field of cinema with their creativity and innovation.
