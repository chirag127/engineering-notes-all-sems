### Indian Architecture in Ancient India and present times

- Indian architecture is as old as the history of the civilization and reflects the diversity of its culture, religion, and geography.  
- The earliest remains of recognizable building activity in India date back to the Indus Valley civilization (c. 2500-1500 BCE), which is known for its urban planning, drainage systems, and brick structures.  
- The Mauryan period (c. 322-185 BCE) is considered as the beginning of the classical period of Indian architecture, which saw the development of stone pillars, stupas, and rock-cut caves.  
- The post-Mauryan period (c. 185 BCE-320 CE) witnessed the emergence of regional styles, such as the Gandhara and Mathura schools of art, which blended Indian and foreign influences.  
- The Gupta period (c. 320-550 CE) is regarded as the golden age of Indian art and architecture, which reached its zenith in the construction of temples, sculptures, and paintings.  
- The medieval period (c. 550-1526 CE) saw the rise of Hindu and Islamic dynasties, which competed and collaborated in the creation of diverse architectural forms, such as the Nagara and Dravidian styles of temples, the Rajput and Mughal styles of forts and palaces, and the Indo-Islamic and Indo-Saracenic styles of mosques and tombs.   
- The colonial period (c. 1526-1947 CE) witnessed the introduction of European styles and techniques, which influenced the Indian architecture in various ways, such as the Gothic, Renaissance, Baroque, and Neoclassical styles of churches, public buildings, and monuments.   
- The modern period (c. 1947-present) is characterized by the experimentation and innovation of Indian architects, who have explored various themes and movements, such as the regionalism, modernism, postmodernism, and sustainability.
