#### The representation of Women in Historical traditions in Society in Ancient India

- Women in ancient India had a complex and varied status depending on the time period, the region, the religion, and the caste they belonged to.
- During the Indus Valley Civilization (2500-1500 BCE), women were worshiped as mother goddesses and enjoyed equal honor and freedom as men. They could participate in war, gymnastics, public activities, decision-making, and education.
- During the Vedic period (1500-500 BCE), women also held an honored place in society and were respected as wives, mothers, and scholars. They could perform religious rituals, compose hymns, and study the Vedas. Some of the famous women sages of this period were Gargi, Maitreyi, Lopamudra, and Ghosha  .
- During the later Vedic period (500-200 BCE), the status of women began to decline due to the rise of patriarchy, caste system, and Brahmanical orthodoxy. Women were gradually excluded from education, property rights, and public life. They were subjected to the authority of their fathers, husbands, and sons. They had to follow the norms of chastity, obedience, and loyalty. They were also expected to practice sati (self-immolation on the husband's funeral pyre) or widowhood  .
- During the Mauryan period (321-185 BCE), the status of women improved slightly due to the influence of Buddhism and Jainism, which advocated non-violence, equality, and compassion. Women could join the monastic orders, preach the doctrines, and attain salvation. Some of the prominent women figures of this period were Chandragupta Maurya's chief queen Durdhara, Ashoka's daughter Sanghamitra, and the Buddhist nun Dhammawati  .
- During the Gupta period (320-550 CE), the status of women deteriorated further due to the revival of Hinduism and the codification of laws such as the Manusmriti, which prescribed the ideal duties and behavior of women. Women were confined to the domestic sphere, denied education and independence, and subjected to various restrictions and regulations. They were also exploited and oppressed by the upper castes and classes. However, some women still excelled in the fields of literature, art, and science. Some of the notable women of this period were the poet Kalidasa's wife Malavika, the mathematician Lilavati, and the queen Prabhavatigupta  .
- In conclusion, women in ancient India had a diverse and dynamic role in society, which changed according to the historical, cultural, and religious factors. They were not passive or homogeneous, but active and heterogeneous agents of change and continuity. They contributed to the development and enrichment of the Indian civilization in various ways.
