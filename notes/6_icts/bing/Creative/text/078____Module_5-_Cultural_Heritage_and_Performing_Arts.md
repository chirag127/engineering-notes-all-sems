## Module 5- Cultural Heritage and Performing Arts

- Cultural heritage is the legacy of physical and intangible attributes of a group or society that are inherited from past generations, maintained in the present and bestowed for the benefit of future generations.
- Cultural heritage includes tangible culture (such as buildings, monuments, landscapes, books, works of art, and artefacts), intangible culture (such as folklore, traditions, language, and knowledge), and natural heritage (such as culturally significant landscapes, and biodiversity).
- Performing arts are forms of art that use the body, voice, or objects to convey artistic expression, such as music, dance, theatre, opera, mime, puppetry, circus arts, and performance art.
- Performing arts are often performed in front of an audience, but may also be performed for a specific purpose, such as ritual, education, or social change.
- Performing arts are part of the intangible cultural heritage of many cultures and civilizations, as they reflect the creativity, values, identity, and history of the people who practice them.
- Performing arts can also contribute to the preservation and transmission of tangible cultural heritage, such as musical instruments, masks, costumes, props, and scenery.
- Some examples of performing arts that are recognized by UNESCO as intangible cultural heritage are:

  - Urtiin Duu, traditional folk long song of Mongolia, which is sung in a specific vocal technique that requires great skill and endurance.
  - Wayang puppet theatre of Indonesia, which is a form of storytelling that uses flat leather puppets, accompanied by a gamelan orchestra and a narrator.
  - Flamenco of Spain, which is a musical and dance expression that combines singing, guitar playing, hand clapping, finger snapping, and dancing, influenced by various cultures and regions.
  - Kabuki theatre of Japan, which is a classical drama that combines music, dance, and acting, characterized by elaborate costumes, makeup, and stage devices.
  - Tango of Argentina and Uruguay, which is a social dance and musical genre that originated in the late 19th century among the lower classes of Buenos Aires and Montevideo.

- Some of the benefits of performing arts for cultural heritage are:

  - They foster cultural diversity and intercultural dialogue, as they showcase the richness and variety of human expressions and interactions.
  - They promote social cohesion and inclusion, as they create opportunities for participation, communication, and collaboration among different groups and individuals.
  - They enhance individual and collective well-being, as they provide enjoyment, entertainment, education, and emotional and spiritual enrichment.
  - They support sustainable development, as they generate income, employment, and innovation, and contribute to the conservation and management of natural and cultural resources.
