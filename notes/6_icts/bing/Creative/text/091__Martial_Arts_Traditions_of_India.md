### Martial Arts Traditions of India

- Martial arts are codified systems and traditions of combat practices, which are practised for a variety of reasons – self-defence, competition, physical health and fitness, entertainment, as well as mental, physical, and spiritual development. 
- India has a long history of martial arts, dating back to the Vedic period (1500 BCE - 1100 BCE). Some of the earliest references to martial arts are found in the Vedas, the epics, the Puranas and other ancient texts.  
- India has a rich diversity of martial arts, reflecting the cultural and geographical diversity of the country. Some of the most prominent and ancient martial arts of India are:
  - Kalaripayattu: Often known as the ‘Mother Of All Martial Arts’, this 3000-year old art form originating from Kerala, draws inspiration from the raw power, swift movements and sinuous strength of majestic animals – the lion, tiger, elephant, wild boar, snake and crocodile. It involves the use of various weapons, such as swords, daggers, spears, shields, bows and arrows, as well as unarmed techniques, such as kicks, punches, locks, throws and grappling. It also incorporates elements of yoga, meditation, massage and healing.  
  - Silambam: A traditional stick-fighting art from Tamil Nadu, Silambam is one of the oldest martial arts in the world, with references dating back to the 2nd century BCE. It involves the use of a bamboo staff, ranging from 4 to 6 feet in length, as well as other weapons, such as knives, swords, spears and axes. It also includes unarmed techniques, such as kicks, punches, blocks and dodges. Silambam is known for its fluid and swift movements, as well as its intricate footwork and body rotations.  
  - Gatka: A martial art from Punjab, Gatka is derived from the ancient Sikh art of Shastar Vidya, which means the science of weapons. It involves the use of various weapons, such as swords, daggers, axes, maces, spears, shields, chakrams and kirpans, as well as unarmed techniques, such as kicks, punches, locks and throws. Gatka is known for its rhythmic and coordinated movements, as well as its use of music and chants. Gatka is also a popular sport and cultural performance in Punjab and other parts of India.  
  - Thang-Ta: A martial art from Manipur, Thang-Ta means the art of the sword and the spear. It involves the use of various weapons, such as swords, spears, daggers, shields, bows and arrows, as well as unarmed techniques, such as kicks, punches, locks and throws. Thang-Ta is known for its fast and agile movements, as well as its use of acrobatics and jumps. Thang-Ta is also a part of the ritual and religious traditions of Manipur, and is performed during festivals and ceremonies.  
  - Malla-Yuddha: A martial art of wrestling, Malla-Yuddha is one of the oldest forms of combat in India, with references dating back to the Vedas and the epics. It involves the use of various techniques, such as strikes, holds, locks, throws, takedowns and submissions, to overpower and subdue the opponent. Malla-Yuddha is also a sport and a cultural tradition, and is practised in various forms and styles across India, such as Pehlwani, Kushti, Vajra-Mushti and Malyutham.  
  - Huyen Langlon: A martial art from Manipur, Huyen Langlon means the art of war and knowledge. It involves the use of various weapons, such as swords, spears, daggers, shields, bows and arrows, as well as unarmed techniques, such as kicks, punches, locks and throws. Huyen Langlon is known for its complex and sophisticated movements, as well as its use of strategy and tactics. Huyen Langlon is also a part of the royal and military traditions of Manipur, and is taught in special schools called Huyen Lallong.
