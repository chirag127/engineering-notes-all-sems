### Seals as Cultural Heritage of India

- Seals are small objects that have carved images and inscriptions on them. They were used to make impressions on clay, wax, or other soft materials to mark ownership, identity, or authenticity of goods, documents, or packages.
- Seals are one of the most important sources of information about the ancient Indus Valley Civilization, which flourished in the northwest of India and Pakistan between 2500 and 1900 BCE. Thousands of seals have been found in the archaeological sites of this civilization, such as Mohenjo-daro, Harappa, Lothal, and Dholavira.
- The Indus seals are mostly made of steatite, a soft stone that can be easily carved and baked to make it harder. They are usually square or rectangular in shape, measuring about 2-3 cm on each side. Some seals are also circular or cylindrical. They have a knob or a hole on the back, which was used to attach a string or a stick for handling.
- The Indus seals have two main elements: an image and an inscription. The image is usually of an animal, such as a bull, a unicorn, an elephant, a rhinoceros, a tiger, or a buffalo. Sometimes, the animal is shown with a feeding trough, a tree, or other symbols. The most famous image is that of a horned figure seated on a platform, surrounded by four animals. This figure is often called Pashupati, meaning "lord of animals", and is considered a possible representation of a deity or a ruler.
- The inscription is composed of a series of symbols, ranging from one to 26 in number. The symbols are carved in a script that has not been deciphered yet, despite many attempts by scholars. The script is believed to represent a language that is different from any other known language. The meaning and function of the inscriptions are still a mystery, but they may have been used for identification, communication, or religious purposes.
- The Indus seals were used for various purposes, such as sealing goods, documents, or packages, marking ownership or authority, or expressing beliefs or values. They were also used as amulets, ornaments, or tokens of exchange. They reflect the culture, society, economy, and religion of the Indus civilization, and show its contacts and trade with other regions, such as Mesopotamia, Persia, and Central Asia.
- The Indus seals are part of the cultural heritage of India, as they are the earliest examples of writing, art, and symbolism in the Indian subcontinent. They are also part of the world heritage, as they are the testimony of one of the oldest and most advanced civilizations in human history. They are valuable artifacts that need to be preserved, studied, and appreciated for their beauty and significance.
