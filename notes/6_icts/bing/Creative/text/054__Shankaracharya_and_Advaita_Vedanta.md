### Shankaracharya and Advaita Vedanta

- Shankaracharya, also known as Shankara or Shankaracarya, was a philosopher and theologian who expounded the doctrine of Advaita Vedanta, a school of Hindu philosophy that emphasizes the non-duality of reality   .
- He was born in the 8th century CE, possibly in Kaladi village in Kerala, southern India . He renounced the world and became a sannyasin (ascetic) at a young age, against his mother's will. He studied under Govinda, who was a disciple of Gaudapada, a Vedanta teacher influenced by Mahayana Buddhism .
- He traveled across India, engaging in debates with scholars of various schools of thought, such as Mimamsa, Samkhya, Yoga, Nyaya, Vaisheshika, and Buddhism. He established four monastic centers (mathas) in the four corners of India, and appointed his four main disciples as the heads of these centers . He also revived the tradition of the Dashanami Sampradaya, a sect of sannyasins who follow the ten names of Shankara.
- He wrote commentaries on the Brahma-sutra, the principal Upanishads, and the Bhagavadgita, the foundational texts of Vedanta. He also composed original works, such as the Upadesasahasri, the Vivekachudamani, and the Crest-Jewel of Discrimination, which expound his philosophy and methods of spiritual practice  .
- His main teaching is that Brahman, the supreme reality, is the only reality, and that everything else, including the individual self (atman) and the phenomenal world, is an illusion (maya) or a superimposition (adhyasa) on Brahman. He argues that Brahman is identical to the atman, the innermost essence of one's being, and that this identity can be realized through the means of knowledge (jnana), which is based on the scriptural testimony (sruti) of the Upanishads and the logical reasoning (yukti) of the Vedanta system  .
- He also acknowledges the existence of a personal God (Ishvara), who is the creator, sustainer, and destroyer of the universe, and who is the object of devotion (bhakti) and ethical action (karma) for the seekers of liberation (moksha). He maintains that Ishvara is a manifestation of Brahman, and that the ultimate goal of life is to transcend the duality of Ishvara and the individual self, and to realize one's identity with Brahman  .
- He is regarded as one of the most influential and authoritative philosophers of Advaita Vedanta, and one of the greatest thinkers of Indian philosophy. He is revered by the Advaita tradition and its monastic lineages, and his works have inspired many later philosophers, scholars, and spiritual teachers  .
