## Module 4-Science, Management and Indian Knowledge System

This module covers the following topics:

- Astronomy in India: The ancient Indian astronomers made significant contributions to the fields of observational astronomy, mathematical astronomy, cosmology, calendar systems, and astrology. Some of the notable astronomers include Aryabhata, Varahamihira, Brahmagupta, Bhaskara, and Lalla. They developed concepts such as the heliocentric model, the lunar and solar eclipses, the precession of the equinoxes, the calculation of the sidereal year, the value of pi, and the use of trigonometric functions  .
- Chemistry in India: The ancient Indian chemists were adept at the extraction, purification, and synthesis of various metals, minerals, dyes, drugs, and perfumes. They also developed techniques such as distillation, sublimation, calcination, and fermentation. Some of the notable chemists include Nagarjuna, Kanada, Charaka, Sushruta, and Patanjali. They developed concepts such as the atomic theory, the classification of elements, the theory of combustion, the medicinal properties of plants and minerals, and the preparation of various compounds  .
- Mathematics in India: The ancient Indian mathematicians made remarkable advances in the fields of arithmetic, algebra, geometry, trigonometry, calculus, and combinatorics. Some of the notable mathematicians include Baudhayana, Apastamba, Pingala, Panini, Katyayana, Aryabhata, Brahmagupta, Bhaskara, Mahavira, and Madhava. They developed concepts such as the decimal system, the zero, the negative numbers, the square root, the quadratic equation, the Pythagorean theorem, the sine and cosine functions, the infinite series, and the binomial theorem  .
- Physics in India: The ancient Indian physicists were interested in the nature of matter, energy, motion, sound, light, and gravity. Some of the notable physicists include Kanada, Kapila, Patanjali, Bharata, Varahamihira, Aryabhata, and Bhaskara. They developed concepts such as the five elements, the three gunas, the prana, the yoga, the theory of relativity, the law of conservation of mass, the Doppler effect, the refraction of light, and the gravitational attraction  .
- Agriculture in India: The ancient Indian agriculturists were skilled in the cultivation, irrigation, and harvesting of various crops, fruits, vegetables, and spices. They also practiced animal husbandry, beekeeping, and silk production. Some of the notable agriculturists include Kashyapa, Parashara, Varahamihira, Surapala, and Someshvara. They developed concepts such as the crop rotation, the soil fertility, the pest control, the water management, the grafting, and the hybridization  .
- Medicine in India: The ancient Indian physicians were proficient in the diagnosis, treatment, and prevention of various diseases, injuries, and disorders. They also practiced surgery, dentistry, ophthalmology, and psychology. Some of the notable physicians include Charaka, Sushruta, Vagbhata, Nagarjuna, and Madhava. They developed concepts such as the Ayurveda, the four humors, the six tastes, the eight branches, the pulse diagnosis, the anesthesia, the plastic surgery, and the cataract surgery  .
- Metallurgy in India: The ancient Indian metallurgists were expert in the production, processing, and usage of various metals and alloys, such as iron, steel, copper, bronze, brass, zinc, tin, lead, gold, and silver. They also invented tools, weapons, ornaments, coins, and sculptures. Some of the notable metallurgists include Nagarjuna, Dharmottara, Bhoja, and Someshvara. They developed concepts such as the wootz steel, the iron pillar, the zinc distillation, the coinage, and the lost-wax casting  .
- Geography in India: The ancient Indian geographers were curious about the shape, size, and features of the earth, the continents, the oceans, the rivers
