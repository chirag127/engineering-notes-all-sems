### Political Ideals in Ancient India and their relevance in modern times

- Ancient India had a rich tradition of political thought and practice that spanned from the Vedic period to the medieval period.
- The political ideals of ancient India were based on the concepts of dharma, rita, danda, artha, and rajadharma, which guided the rulers and the subjects in their duties and responsibilities.
- Dharma was the cosmic law that governed the moral and social order of the universe. Rita was the natural order that maintained the harmony and balance of the creation. Danda was the power of punishment that enforced the dharma and rita. Artha was the material and economic well-being that supported the dharma and rita. Rajadharma was the duty of the king to uphold the dharma and rita, protect the artha, and wield the danda.
- The political ideals of ancient India were not static or monolithic, but evolved and diversified over time, reflecting the changing historical and cultural contexts of the Indian subcontinent. Different schools of thought, such as the Vedas, the Upanishads, the Dharmashastras, the Arthashastra, the Buddhist and Jain texts, the Puranas, the Itihasas, and the regional literature, contributed to the development of political thought in ancient India.
- Some of the political ideals of ancient India that have relevance in modern times are:

  - The concept of democracy and republicanism: Ancient India witnessed the emergence of various forms of democratic and republican polities, such as the ganas, sanghas, janapadas, and mahajanapadas, where the people had a voice and a role in the governance. The Rig Veda, the oldest text of India, extolled the virtues of democracy and collective consciousness, and called it Samjnana. The Buddhist and Jain texts also advocated the principles of non-violence, tolerance, and compassion, which are essential for a democratic society.
  - The concept of welfare and justice: Ancient India recognized the importance of welfare and justice for the people, and the responsibility of the king to ensure them. The king was expected to be a benevolent and righteous ruler, who would protect the people from internal and external enemies, provide them with the basic necessities of life, and administer justice impartially. The king was also supposed to follow the dharma and rita, and respect the rights and freedoms of the people. The Arthashastra, the treatise on statecraft by Kautilya, laid down the principles of good governance, welfare, and justice, which are still relevant today.
  - The concept of pluralism and diversity: Ancient India was a land of diversity and pluralism, where different religions, cultures, languages, and regions coexisted and interacted peacefully. The political ideals of ancient India respected and celebrated this diversity, and promoted the values of harmony, tolerance, and mutual respect. The king was supposed to be a patron of all faiths and sects, and not impose his own beliefs on the people. The Vedas, the Upanishads, the Puranas, and the Itihasas, among others, expressed the idea of unity in diversity, and the acceptance of different paths to the truth.
