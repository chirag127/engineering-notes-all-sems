### Drama as Cultural Heritage of India

- Drama is a form of performing arts that involves the enactment of stories using dialogue, music, gesture and dance.
- Drama is an integral part of the cultural heritage of India, as it reflects the diversity, richness and vitality of the Indian civilization.
- Drama in India has a long and glorious history, dating back to the Vedic period (1500-500 BCE), when ritualistic performances were held to appease the gods and goddesses.
- The classical period of Indian drama (500 BCE-1000 CE) witnessed the emergence of Sanskrit theatre, which is the oldest surviving form of drama in the world. Sanskrit theatre was based on the treatise Natya Shastra, written by Bharata Muni, who is regarded as the father of Indian drama. Natya Shastra defined the principles, genres, styles and aesthetics of drama, and introduced the concept of rasa, or the emotional essence of art.
- Sanskrit theatre consisted of two major genres: nataka and prakarana. Nataka dealt with the stories of gods, kings and mythology, while prakarana focused on the lives of ordinary people. Sanskrit theatre also had two major styles: lokadharmi and natyadharmi. Lokadharmi was realistic and naturalistic, while natyadharmi was stylized and symbolic. Sanskrit theatre reached its zenith with the works of Kalidasa, the greatest playwright of India, who wrote masterpieces like Abhijnanasakuntalam, Vikramorvasiyam and Malavikagnimitram.
- The traditional period of Indian drama (1000-1800 CE) saw the development of regional theatre forms, influenced by the local languages, cultures and religions. Some of the prominent theatre forms of this period are: koodiyattam (Kerala), yakshagana (Karnataka), therukoothu (Tamil Nadu), bhavai (Gujarat), nautanki (Uttar Pradesh), jatra (West Bengal), bhaona (Assam) and tamasha (Maharashtra). These theatre forms combined music, dance, dialogue, comedy, satire and spectacle, and entertained both the elite and the masses. They also addressed social, political and religious issues of their times, and expressed the aspirations and struggles of the people.
- The modern period of Indian drama (1800-present) is marked by the influence of Western theatre, especially after the British colonization of India. The modern Indian drama experimented with new themes, forms and techniques, and also incorporated elements from the classical and traditional theatre. The modern Indian drama also witnessed the emergence of prominent playwrights, actors and directors, who contributed to the growth and diversity of the Indian theatre. Some of the notable figures of the modern Indian drama are: Rabindranath Tagore, Bharatendu Harishchandra, Jaishankar Prasad, Mohan Rakesh, Vijay Tendulkar, Girish Karnad, Badal Sircar, Mahesh Dattani, Habib Tanvir, Utpal Dutt, Shyam Benegal, Satyajit Ray, Prithviraj Kapoor, Sombhu Mitra, Shanta Gandhi, Ebrahim Alkazi, Ratan Thiyam and Naseeruddin Shah.
- Drama as cultural heritage of India is a living and dynamic tradition, that continues to evolve and adapt to the changing times and contexts. Drama as cultural heritage of India is a source of pride, identity and inspiration for the Indian people, and a valuable contribution to the world of art and culture.
