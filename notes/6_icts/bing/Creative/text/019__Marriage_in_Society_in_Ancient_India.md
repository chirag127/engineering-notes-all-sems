#### Marriage in Society in Ancient India

- Marriage in ancient India was considered to be a sacred and social institution that united a man and a woman for the fulfilment of their dharma, artha, kama, and moksha.  
- Marriage was also a means of procreation, establishing a family, and experiencing pleasure. Sexual intercourse between a husband and wife was regarded to be important but not the primary purpose of marriage. 
- Marriage was regulated by various rules and customs, such as the age of marriage, the gotra or lineage of the bride and groom, the horoscope or astrological chart of the couple, and the types of marriage.  
- The ideal age of marriage for girls was after menarche, which was usually around 12 years old. Girls were allowed to choose their own husbands if a suitable groom was not found for them.  
- The ideal age of marriage for boys was after completing their education, which could vary depending on their caste and occupation. Boys were expected to marry within their own community and social status.  
- The gotra or lineage of the bride and groom was determined by their descent from a common Vedic sage. Marriage within the same gotra was prohibited, as it was considered to be incestuous. Marriage was also avoided between close relatives, such as cousins.  
- The horoscope or astrological chart of the couple was used to determine their compatibility and auspiciousness of their marriage. The horoscope was based on the date, time, and place of birth of the bride and groom, and the position of the planets and stars at that time. The horoscope was consulted by a priest or an astrologer, who matched the couple's qualities and defects, and suggested remedies for any obstacles or problems.  
- The types of marriage in ancient India were classified into eight categories, according to the Manusmriti and other Dharmashastra texts. The types of marriage were:   
  - Brahma marriage: The most righteous and ideal form of marriage, where the bride was given by her father or another guardian to a learned and virtuous groom, without any dowry or expectation of return. The bride and groom were of the same caste and social status, and the marriage was performed with Vedic rituals and chants.   
  - Daiva marriage: A righteous form of marriage, where the bride was given by her father or another guardian to a priest or a religious teacher, as a gift or an offering to the gods. The bride was usually of a lower caste or social status than the groom, and the marriage was performed with Vedic rituals and chants.   
  - Arsha marriage: A righteous form of marriage, where the bride was given by her father or another guardian to a sage or a hermit, in exchange for a cow and a bull. The bride and groom were of the same caste and social status, and the marriage was performed with Vedic rituals and chants.   
  - Prajapatya marriage: A righteous form of marriage, where the bride was given by her father or another guardian to a groom of his choice, without any dowry or expectation of return. The bride and groom were of the same caste and social status, and the marriage was performed with Vedic rituals and chants. The father or the guardian of the bride blessed the couple with the words "May both of you perform together your duties".   
  - Gandharva marriage: A non-righteous but acceptable form of marriage, where the bride and groom married each other by mutual consent and love, without the consent or knowledge of their parents or guardians. The bride and groom were usually of the same caste and social status, and the marriage was performed without any rituals or chants. The marriage was based on physical attraction and sexual desire, and was often temporary or unstable.
