### Malayalam Literature

Malayalam literature is the literature written in Malayalam, a South-Dravidian language spoken in the Indian state of Kerala and some parts of the union territories of Lakshadweep and Puducherry. Malayalam is one of the six classical languages of India and has been awarded with six Jnanpith awards, the highest literary honor in the country. Malayalam literature has a rich and diverse history that spans over a thousand years. Some of the main features and periods of Malayalam literature are:

- The earliest known literary work in Malayalam is **Ramacharitam**, an epic poem based on the Ramayana, written in the late 12th or early 13th century CE. It is considered to be a mixture of Malayalam and Tamil in its grammar and vocabulary. 
- In the 14th and 15th centuries, a family of poets from Niranam in central Travancore composed translations of the Ramayana, the Bhagavad Gita, and other Sanskrit texts in Malayalam. They are known as the **Niranam poets** and include Rama Panikkar, Madhava Panikkar, and Sankara Panikkar. 
- A special literary dialect called **Manipravalam** emerged in the same period, which was a blend of Malayalam and Sanskrit. It was used to write mainly erotic poetry and courtly romances. The rules and features of this dialect were described in the **Lilatilakam**, a treatise on Malayalam grammar and poetics written in Sanskrit in the 14th century.  
- Some of the notable works in Manipravalam are the **sandesa kavyas** and the **campus**. The sandesa kavyas are poems that convey a message from a lover to his beloved, describing the route, the scenery, and the emotions along the way. The most famous example is the **Unnunili Sandesam**, written in the 14th century. The campus are prose-verse compositions that deal with a given theme, such as love, war, or religion. The most celebrated campus are the **Krishnagatha** by Cherusseri Namboothiri and the **Ramayanam** by Ezhuthachan.  
- In the 16th and 17th centuries, a new form of devotional poetry called **bhakti** emerged, influenced by the Bhakti movement in other parts of India. The bhakti poets expressed their devotion to various gods and goddesses, such as Vishnu, Siva, Devi, and Ayyappa, in simple and lyrical Malayalam. Some of the prominent bhakti poets are **Ezhuthachan**, **Poonthanam**, **Melpathur Narayana Bhattathiri**, and **Kunchan Nambiar**.  
- Ezhuthachan is widely regarded as the **father of modern Malayalam literature**, as he standardized the Malayalam alphabet, grammar, and vocabulary, and popularized the use of the spoken dialect over the literary dialect. He also wrote two epic poems, the **Adhyatma Ramayanam** and the **Mahabharatam**, which are considered to be the masterpieces of Malayalam literature.  
- Poonthanam is best known for his **Jnanappana**, a long poem that expounds the philosophy of devotion and detachment. He also wrote many other poems and songs on various themes, such as the life of Krishna, the stories of the Puranas, and the moral teachings of the Vedas.
