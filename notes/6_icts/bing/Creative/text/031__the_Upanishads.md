### The Upanishads

- The Upanishads are late Vedic Sanskrit texts that supplied the basis of later Hindu philosophy. 
- They are the most recent part of the Vedas, the oldest scriptures of Hinduism, and deal with meditation, philosophy, consciousness, and ontological knowledge.   
- They are commonly referred to as Vedanta, which means "the end of the Vedas" or "the highest purpose of the Vedas". 
- The aim of all Upanishads is to investigate the nature of Atman (self), and direct the enquirer toward it. 
- The Upanishads are also called Upanishad, which means "connection" or "equivalence", and refers to the correspondence between the human and the cosmic realms. 
- The Upanishads are not systematic philosophical treatises, but rather diverse and sometimes contradictory texts that contain various doctrines, rituals, stories, and dialogues.  
- The Upanishads became the subject of many commentaries and subcommentaries, and inspired many later texts that bear the name "Upanishad". 
- The earliest extant Upanishads date roughly from the middle of the 1st millennium BCE, but some may be older or younger. 
- The Upanishads are traditionally classified into four categories: the principal or major Upanishads, the Sannyasa Upanishads, the Yoga Upanishads, and the sectarian Upanishads. 
- The principal Upanishads are 13 in number and are attached to the four Vedas. They are the most authoritative and influential Upanishads. 
- The Sannyasa Upanishads are 16 in number and deal with the renunciation of worldly life and the pursuit of liberation. They are also called the Aranyakas or the forest texts. 
- The Yoga Upanishads are 20 in number and deal with the practice of yoga and its various aspects. They are also called the Darshanas or the views. 
- The sectarian Upanishads are more than 90 in number and deal with the specific doctrines and practices of various Hindu sects, such as Shaivism, Vaishnavism, Shaktism, and Tantrism. They are also called the Agamas or the traditions. 
- Some of the most important and influential concepts and teachings of the Upanishads are: Brahman, the supreme reality; Atman, the individual self; Maya, the illusion of duality; Karma, the law of cause and effect; Samsara, the cycle of birth and death; Moksha, the liberation from samsara; and Tat Tvam Asi, the identity of Brahman and Atman.
