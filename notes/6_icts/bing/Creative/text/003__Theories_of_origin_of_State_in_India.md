#### Theories of origin of State in India

The origin of state in India is a subject of debate among scholars of political science and history. Different theories have been proposed to explain how the state emerged and evolved in ancient India. Some of the prominent theories are:

- **Social Contract Theory**: This theory holds that the state is a result of a voluntary agreement between the rulers and the ruled, who chose to surrender some of their rights and freedoms in exchange for protection and order. The state, thus, is based on the consent of the governed. The social contract theory is found in some of the Vedic texts, such as the Aitreya Brahmana, which narrates a legend of how the gods appointed Soma as their king to fight against the demons. The theory is also supported by some of the Buddhist texts, such as the Digha Nikaya, which describes how the people elected a mahasammata (grand elect) as their leader to establish justice and harmony in the society.

- **Divine Origin Theory**: This theory asserts that the state is a creation of God or gods, who endowed the king with divine authority and legitimacy. The king, thus, is the representative of God or gods on earth, and his commands are sacred and binding. The divine origin theory is found in some of the Puranic texts, such as the Vishnu Purana, which traces the lineage of the kings from the solar and lunar dynasties. The theory is also reflected in some of the epics, such as the Ramayana and the Mahabharata, which depict the kings as the incarnations or the descendants of the gods.

- **Organic Theory**: This theory compares the state to a living organism, which has a body, a soul, and various organs that perform different functions. The state, thus, is a natural and inevitable phenomenon, which grows and develops according to its own laws and principles. The organic theory is found in some of the Dharmashastra texts, such as the Manusmriti, which divides the society into four varnas (castes) and four ashramas (stages of life), each having its own duties and rights. The theory is also elaborated in some of the Arthashastra texts, such as the Kautilya's Arthashastra, which identifies the seven elements of the state (saptanga) as the king, the ministers, the territory, the population, the army, the allies, and the treasury.
