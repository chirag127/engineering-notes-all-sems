### Painting as Cultural Heritage of India

- Painting is one of the oldest and most diverse forms of artistic expression in India. It reflects the rich cultural and religious heritage of the country, as well as the influences of various historical periods and regions.
- The earliest known paintings in India are the rock paintings of Bhimbetka, dating back to at least 290,000 years ago. These paintings depict animal and human figures in simple and geometric forms, using natural pigments and tools.
- With the rise of Buddhism in the 6th century BCE, painting became a medium of religious expression and education. Buddhist paintings were mainly found in the form of murals on the walls and ceilings of caves and monasteries, such as Ajanta, Ellora, and Bagh. These paintings illustrate the life and teachings of the Buddha, as well as the Jataka tales and other Buddhist stories. They also show the influence of Greek and Persian art styles, as well as the local traditions and customs of different regions.
- Hinduism also inspired many forms of painting in India, especially in the medieval period. Hindu paintings often depict the gods and goddesses, the epics of Ramayana and Mahabharata, and the Puranas. Some of the prominent styles of Hindu painting are Pala, Rajput, Mughal, Deccan, Tanjore, and Pahari. These styles vary in their use of colors, motifs, techniques, and themes, depending on the patronage, geography, and culture of the artists and the rulers.
- Islam also had a significant impact on Indian painting, especially under the Mughal Empire (16th-18th centuries). Mughal painting was a fusion of Persian, Indian, and European elements, and was characterized by realism, naturalism, and elegance. Mughal paintings mainly depicted the court life, portraits, battles, hunting scenes, and flora and fauna. They also incorporated elements of Hindu and Sikh religions, such as the Ragamala and the Janamsakhi paintings.
- Colonialism and modernity also influenced Indian painting, as many artists adopted Western styles and techniques, such as oil painting, watercolor, and realism. Some of the pioneers of modern Indian painting were Raja Ravi Varma, Abanindranath Tagore, Nandalal Bose, Amrita Sher-Gil, and Jamini Roy. They experimented with different genres, such as mythological, historical, social, and abstract, and expressed their views on nationalism, identity, and society.
- Contemporary Indian painting is a vibrant and diverse field, with artists exploring various themes, mediums, and styles. Some of the notable contemporary Indian painters are M.F. Husain, S.H. Raza, Tyeb Mehta, Anjolie Ela Menon, Bhupen Khakhar, Arpita Singh, and Atul Dodiya. They draw inspiration from both the traditional and the modern aspects of Indian culture, as well as the global trends and issues.
