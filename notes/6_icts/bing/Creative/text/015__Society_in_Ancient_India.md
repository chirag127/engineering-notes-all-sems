### Society in Ancient India

- Society in ancient India was based on the concept of **varna**, or the division of people into four main classes: Brahmins (priests and scholars), Kshatriyas (warriors and rulers), Vaishyas (merchants and farmers), and Shudras (servants and laborers). 
- The varna system was not rigid or hereditary, but based on one's qualities, actions, and duties. It also allowed for mobility and intermixing among the classes. However, over time, it became more hierarchical and exclusive, leading to the emergence of sub-castes or **jatis**.  
- The varna system was also influenced by the religious and philosophical doctrines of **Hinduism**, **Jainism**, **Buddhism**, and **Sikhism**, which originated in ancient India. These religions offered different paths of salvation and ethical codes for different sections of society.  
- Society in ancient India was also organized according to the stages of life or **ashramas**, which were four in number: Brahmacharya (student), Grihastha (householder), Vanaprastha (retired), and Sannyasa (renunciant). Each stage had its own duties and responsibilities, and was meant to help the individual attain spiritual and moral perfection.  
- Society in ancient India was also characterized by the development of urban and rural settlements, trade and commerce, arts and sciences, and various forms of administration and governance. The most notable example of urban civilization in ancient India was the **Indus Valley Civilization** (c. 3300-1300 BCE), which extended from modern-day Afghanistan to Pakistan and India, and had a highly advanced and sophisticated culture.  
- Society in ancient India was also enriched by the diversity of cultures, languages, ethnicities, and regions that existed in the subcontinent. Ancient India witnessed the migrations and invasions of various peoples, such as the Indo-Aryans, the Persians, the Greeks, the Scythians, the Kushans, the Huns, and the Arabs, who brought their own influences and contributions to the Indian society.
