### Persian Literature in India

- Persian literature in India refers to the literary works produced by Persian-speaking writers and poets in the Indian subcontinent, especially during the medieval period when Persian was the official language and the lingua franca of the region.  
- Persian literature in India was influenced by various factors, such as the arrival and growth of Islam, the patronage of Turkic and Afghan dynasties, the interaction with Sanskrit and other local languages, and the incorporation of Indian themes and motifs.  
- Persian literature in India can be divided into four main phases: 
  - The first phase (10th-13th centuries) was marked by the introduction of Persian language and culture by the Ghaznavids and other Muslim invaders, and the emergence of Lahore and Uch as centers of Persian poetry. Some of the notable poets of this phase were Abu-al-Faraj Runi, Masud Sa'd Salman, Amir Khusrau, and Hasan Dehlvi.  
  - The second phase (13th-16th centuries) was characterized by the establishment of the Delhi Sultanate and the development of a distinct Indo-Persian culture and identity. Persian became the official language of administration, education, and literature, and many Persian works were translated into Sanskrit and vice versa. Some of the prominent poets of this phase were Amir Hasan Sijzi, Amir Khurd, Amir Baraka, Ziauddin Barani, and Abdur Rahman Jami.  
  - The third phase (16th-18th centuries) was dominated by the Mughal Empire, which reached its zenith under Akbar and his successors. Persian literature flourished under the patronage of the Mughal emperors and nobles, and many Indian poets achieved fame and recognition in the Persianate world. Some of the famous poets of this phase were Faizi, Urfi, Kalim, Naziri, Saib, Bedil, and Mirza Ghalib.  
  - The fourth phase (18th-19th centuries) witnessed the decline of Persian literature in India, as the Mughal Empire collapsed and the British colonial rule took over. Persian lost its official status and prestige, and was gradually replaced by Hindustani (Hindi-Urdu) and English as the languages of politics, commerce, and education. Some of the last poets of this phase were Mir Taqi Mir, Mir Dard, Mir Hasan, and Mirza Asadullah Khan Ghalib.  
- Persian literature in India had a significant impact on the development of other Indic languages, especially Urdu, which emerged as a hybrid of Persian and local dialects. Persian also enriched the vocabulary, phonology, grammar, and writing systems of many other languages, such as Punjabi, Kashmiri, Bengali, and Deccani.  
- Persian literature in India is a valuable source of information and insight into the history, culture, society, politics, and religion of the medieval Indian subcontinent. It reflects the diversity, complexity, and creativity of the Indo-Persian civilization, and its contribution to the world literature.
