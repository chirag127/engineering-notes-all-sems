### Indian Cinema as a Performing Art in India

- Indian cinema is a form of storytelling that combines elements of literature, theatre, music, dance, and visual arts.
- Indian cinema has a rich and diverse history that dates back to the late 19th century, when the first short films were made by Indian filmmakers such as Hiralal Sen and Dadasaheb Phalke.
- Indian cinema was influenced by various socio-cultural and political factors, such as the freedom struggle, the partition, the Nehruvian vision, the regional movements, the emergency, the globalization, and the rise of new media.
- Indian cinema has also been inspired by the classical and folk traditions of performing arts in India, such as the Natyashastra, the rasa theory, the nataka, the kathakali, the bharatanatyam, the kathak, the bhangra, the lavani, and the tamasha .
- Indian cinema has produced many genres, styles, and movements, such as the social realism, the parallel cinema, the masala films, the new wave, the art cinema, the regional cinema, the mainstream cinema, the indie cinema, and the diaspora cinema.
- Indian cinema has also given birth to many iconic stars, directors, writers, composers, singers, and technicians, who have contributed to the development and recognition of Indian cinema as a performing art in India and abroad.
- Indian cinema has been a source of entertainment, education, awareness, and empowerment for millions of Indians and has also reflected the changing aspirations, values, and identities of the Indian society.
- Indian cinema has faced many challenges and opportunities in the 21st century, such as the emergence of new technologies, platforms, audiences, markets, and regulations, and has also experimented with new forms, themes, and expressions of storytelling.
- Indian cinema is a dynamic and evolving art form that continues to celebrate the diversity and complexity of India and its culture, and also engages with the global and local issues and trends that affect the world today.
