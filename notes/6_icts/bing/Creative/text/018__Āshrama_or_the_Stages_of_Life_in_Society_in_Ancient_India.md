#### Āshrama or the Stages of Life in Society in Ancient India

- Āshrama (Sanskrit: आश्रम) is a system of stages of life discussed in Hindu texts of the ancient and medieval eras. 
- The four āśramas are: 
  - Brahmacharya (student), marked by chastity, devotion, and obedience to one’s teacher, and focusing on education and celibacy.  
  - Gṛhastha (householder), requiring marriage, raising a family, fulfilling one's duties and obligations towards society and ancestors, and leading a virtuous and productive life.  
  - Vanaprastha (forest dweller), beginning after the birth of grandchildren and consisting of withdrawal from worldly affairs, pursuit of solitude, and ascetic and yogic practices.  
  - Sannyasa (renunciate), involving renouncing all one's possessions and attachments, wandering from place to place begging for food, and seeking union with the supreme reality.  
- The āśrama system is one facet of the Dharma concept in Hinduism, which is the moral and ethical framework of human life.  
- The āśrama system is also a component of the ethical theories in Indian philosophy, where it is combined with four proper goals of human life (Purushartha), namely Dharma (righteousness), Artha (wealth), Kama (pleasure), and Moksha (liberation). 
- The āśrama system is also part of an indigenous developmental psychology, which has shaped the orientations and goals of many people, especially in India, from ancient times until today.  
- The classical system, in the Āśrama Upanishad, the Vaikhanasa Dharmasutra and the later Dharmashastra, presents these as sequential stages of human life and recommends ages for entry to each stage, while in the original system presented in the early Dharmasutras the āśramas were four alternative available ways of life, neither presented as sequential nor with age recommendations.  
- The āśrama system also involves rituals of transition, such as Upanayana (initiation) at the entry to Brahmacharya, Hindu wedding at the entry to Grihastha, and renunciation at the entry to Sannyasa.
