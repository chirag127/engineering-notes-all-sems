### Biology in India and Ancient India

- Biology is the study of living organisms and their interactions with the environment.
- Biology in India has a long and rich history, dating back to the Vedic period (3000-8000 BCE) and beyond.
- The Vedic literature contains references to 740 plants and 250 animals, indicating that the ancient Indians observed and recorded the diversity of life forms.
- The ancient Indians also developed systems of medicine, such as Ayurveda and Siddha, that were based on the knowledge of anatomy, physiology, pharmacology, and pathology of humans and animals.
- Some of the notable contributions of ancient Indian biologists are:

  - Sushruta (6th century BCE), who is considered the father of surgery and performed various operations, such as cataract removal, rhinoplasty, and caesarean section.
  - Charaka (2nd century BCE), who is regarded as the father of medicine and wrote the Charaka Samhita, a comprehensive treatise on diagnosis, treatment, and prevention of diseases.
  - Patanjali (2nd century BCE), who is credited with the compilation of the Yoga Sutras, a system of physical and mental exercises that aim to achieve harmony between the body and the mind.
  - Varahamihira (6th century CE), who was an astronomer, mathematician, and biologist, and wrote the Brihat Samhita, a compendium of natural phenomena, including plants, animals, weather, and astrology.
  - Nagarjuna (2nd century CE), who was a philosopher, alchemist, and biologist, and wrote the Rasendramangalam, a treatise on the preparation and properties of various metals and minerals.

- The ancient Indians also made significant contributions to the fields of botany, zoology, ecology, genetics, and embryology, among others, as evident from their texts, sculptures, paintings, and monuments.
- The ancient Indian biological thought was influenced by their philosophical, religious, and cultural beliefs, such as the concepts of karma, reincarnation, ahimsa, and dharma.
- The ancient Indian biological thought also influenced and was influenced by the biological knowledge of other civilizations, such as the Greeks, Persians, Chinese, and Arabs, through trade, travel, and cultural exchange.
