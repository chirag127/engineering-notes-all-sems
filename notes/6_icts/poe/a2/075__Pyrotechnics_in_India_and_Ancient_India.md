### Pyrotechnics in India and Ancient India

1. The science and art of fireworks originated in ancient China around the 7th century AD. The knowledge of gunpowder and fireworks later spread to other parts of the world, including India.

2. The use of pyrotechnics and fireworks has been recorded in ancient Indian texts such as the Arthashastra by Chanakya (4th century BC) and the Agni Purana (800-1000 AD). The Mughals later introduced more advanced pyrotechnics in India using gunpowder and other chemicals.

3. Ancient Indian fireworks and pyrotechnics employed chemicals and ingredients such as sulphur, charcoal, saltpetre, copper sulphate, cassia bark, turmeric etc. These chemicals were shaped into pellets and discs and thrown into fires to produce different colours and effects. Some fireworks were propelled into the sky using arrows.

4. The festivals of Diwali and Dussehra see widespread use of fireworks and pyrotechnics in India as part of celebrations. However, in recent years there have been concerns about the environmental and safety impact of fireworks. Many states have imposed restrictions on the sale and use of firecrackers.

5. Pyrotechnics continues to be an important special effects technique in Indian cinema and stage shows. However, technicians need to undergo thorough training to handle pyrotechnics safely and responsibly due to the hazardous nature of the chemicals involved.
