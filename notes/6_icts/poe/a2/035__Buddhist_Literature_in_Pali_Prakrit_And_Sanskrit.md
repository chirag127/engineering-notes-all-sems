### Buddhist Literature in Pali, Prakrit and Sanskrit

1. Pali Literature: The Buddhist canon compiled in Pali includes three Pitakas - Vinaya Pitaka, Sutta Pitaka and Abhidhamma Pitaka. The Sutta Pitaka contains discourses delivered by the Buddha and his chief disciples. The Vinaya Pitaka contains rules and regulations for monks. The Abhidhamma Pitaka contains philosophical and psychological teachings and analysis.

2. Prakrit Literature: The most significant Prakrit literature related to Buddhism includes the texts of Mahayana Buddhist traditions, especially those associated with the Madhyamaka school. These includeprajnaparamita sutras and the sutras of Nagarjuna and Aryadeva. Most of these were originally composed in Sanskrit and translated into Prakrit and other languages.

3. Sanskrit Literature: There is a vast body of Mahayana Buddhist literature in Sanskrit. This includes sutras such as the Lotus Sutra, the Vimalakirti Sutra, the Saddharma Pundarika Sutra and others. Important philosophical treatises were composed by Nagarjuna, Asanga, Vasubandhu, Dignaga, Dharmakirti and others. Many of these were also translated into Prakrit, Tibetan, Chinese and other languages. Sanskrit Buddhist literature also includes texts of the Vajrayana tradition such as tantras and shastras.
