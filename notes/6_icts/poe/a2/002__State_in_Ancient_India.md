### State in Ancient India

1. During Vedic period, political authority was decentralized. There were numerous tribes and tribal confederacies. Chief of a tribe was called as Rajan.

2. Gradually, small territories or Janapadas emerged with monarchy as the form of political institution. The monarch was called as Maharaja or Rajadhiraja.

3. The republican states or Ganas and Sanghas also emerged, especially in Eastern India. The leaders were called as Ganapati or Sanghamitra. Examples are Shakya and Malla republics.

4. The large empires also started emerging after 6th century BCE like Avanti, Kosala, Magadha, Vajji, etc. The Magadha empire grew in strength and size under Haryanka and Shishunaga dynasties.

5. Chandragupta Maurya founded the first large all-India empire with the help of Chanakya. He strengthened it through conquests and alliances and spread it over whole of North India and parts of South India.

6. The Mauryan empire was governed in an efficient and authoritarian manner with the King at the top followed by Adhyakshas or officials at provincial, district and village levels. There was a well-organised bureaucracy and espionage system.

7. The concept of Rajdharma as duties of the king towards subjects and concept of social contract between ruler and the ruled emerged during this period. The Arthashastra by Chanakya deals with theory and practice of statecraft and economics during the period.

8. After the decline of Mauryan empire, the Sunga, Kanva and Satavahana dynasties came to power. Indo-Greeks, Kushans and Western Satraps also ruled some parts of India. Gupta empire again unified large parts and is called as Golden age of Ancient India.
