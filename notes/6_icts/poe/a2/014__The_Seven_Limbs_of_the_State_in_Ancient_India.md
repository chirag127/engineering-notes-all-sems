### The Seven Limbs of the State in Ancient India

The ancient Indian political theorists conceived the state as consisting of seven essential organs or 'limbs' -

1. The King - The king was regarded as the most important organ of the state. He was responsible for the welfare of the people and proper functioning of the state.
2. The Minister - The ministers assisted the king in administrative functions and governance. They were representatives of the people in the council of the king.
3. The Territory - The territory consisting of villages and towns with defined boundaries was an essential organ of the state.
4. The Forts - Forts provided security and defense to the state. They were strategically located mainly in hilly and forested tracts.
5. The Treasury - The treasury consisting of the revenues and taxes collected from the people was important to meet the expenses of the state.
6. The Army - The army defended the state from external attacks and internal rebellions. It maintained law and order within the territory.
7. The Allies - The state forged alliances with other states for mutual security, cooperation and benefits. These alliances strengthened the state.

The proper functioning of all these seven organs was necessary for the health and prosperity of the state. A weakness in any one of these organs affected the state. The dharma or duty of the king was to ensure the proper functioning of all these organs through good governance.
