#### Purusārtha in Society in Ancient India

- Purusārtha refers to the four goals of life considered important to lead a meaningful life in ancient Indian philosophy. They are - Dharma (righteousness), Artha (material prosperity), Kama (pleasure) and Moksha (liberation).
- In ancient Indian society, an individual was expected to pursue these four Purusārthas in a balanced way. Excessive pursuit of any one goal at the cost of others was frowned upon. For example, excessive pursuit of material wealth (Artha) without regard for Dharma was criticized. Similarly, pursuit of pleasure (Kama) without consideration of other goals was seen as improper.
- The four Purusārthas were hierarchical - Dharma was considered the foremost goal, then came Artha and Kama, and Moksha was the ultimate goal. The lower goals were means to achieve the higher goals. For example, Artha was necessary to sustain life and support the pursuit of Dharma, but excessive materialism was discouraged.
- The Purusārtha system reflects the ancient Indian view of a balanced and holistic development of society and individuals. It aimed to create individuals who are righteous as well as skilled, capable of enjoyment yet liberated in consciousness. This system influenced various spheres of society - economy, polity, education system, etc.
