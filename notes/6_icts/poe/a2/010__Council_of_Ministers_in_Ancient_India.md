### Council of Ministers in Ancient India

- The king was assisted by a council of ministers in administering the state. The number of ministers varied across regions and periods.
- The ministers were highly experienced and came from privileged backgrounds. They helped the king in decision making and implementation of policies.
- Important ministers included:
-- Purohita - chief priest who advised the king on religious and spiritual matters
-- Senapati - commander of the army who looked after the military
-- Mahamatya - chief minister who supervised all areas of administration
-- Antarvanshika - in charge of the palace and internal security
-- Prathamahastin - commander of the elephant force
-- Dauvarika - officer in charge of gates who controlled access to the king
- The ministers were collectively responsible for the welfare of the subjects and would often check abuse of power by the king. They formed an aristocratic bureaucracy below the king.
- Key roles and responsibilities of the council included:
-- Assisting the king in framing laws and policies
-- Implementing the king's orders and decisions
-- Managing revenue collection and maintaining accounts
-- Maintaining law and order through deployment of armed forces
-- Conducting foreign affairs such as receiving envoys and concluding treaties
