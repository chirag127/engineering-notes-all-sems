#### Harappan Script

- The Harappan script refers to the ancient Indus script used by the Indus Valley Civilization from around 3300 BCE.
- It is one of the earliest writing systems developed in the ancient world.
- The script consists of 37 recognizable signs and more than 400 signs have been found on artifacts unearthed at Harappan sites.
- The script appears on seals, pottery, tools, coins and ornaments but its meaning remains undeciphered.
- Scholars have proposed links to various later writing systems including the Brahmi script, but none are unanimously agreed upon.
- The script's brevity suggests it may not have been capable of representing the full repertoire of the spoken language of the Indus Valley Civilization.
- Many linguists and archaeologists believe that if and when the script is deciphered, it will yield insights into the language(s) spoken by the Indus Valley Civilization.
- However, some scholars argue that the script may not have represented a full language and could have been used to symbolize families, clan names, or property ownership.
- The undeciphered Indus script is one of the world's greatest unsolved mysteries and its decipherment could reveal insights into this ancient civilization.
