## Module 4-Science, Management and Indian Knowledge System

**Science and Indian Knowledge System**

- Ancient Indian knowledge system had a scientific temperament. It was based on observation, experimentation, logical thinking and validation.
- Many branches of science and technology were studied such as mathematics, astronomy, metallurgy, medicine, etc.
- Concepts like zero, decimal system, geometry in mathematics; planetary motions, eclipses, etc. in astronomy; high-quality steel production and surgery in science and technology had their origin in ancient India.
- Panini's Ashtadhyayi is a remarkable work on linguistics. Charaka and Sushruta wrote authoritative texts on medicine and surgery.
- There was an emphasis on the harmony of nature and the presence of the supernatural or divine in the universe.
- With the decline of ancient Indian universities like Nalanda, this knowledge system also declined. It did not go through the rigour of scientific experiments and the scientific method in the later period.

**Management and Indian Knowledge System**

- Chanakya's Arthashastra is an early document on economy and management. It deals with fiscal management, monetary policies, and statecraft.
- Thirukkural by Thiruvalluvar is another seminal work on ethics, polity and management. It deals with domestic life, social life, and administrative skills.
- Ancient Indians followed a holistic development approach through the four Purusharthas (goals) of life - Dharma (moral duty), Artha (wealth), Kama (desire), and Moksha (liberation). Good management was necessary to achieve these.
- Ancient organizations like the Panchayat system espoused the principles of local self-government, participation, and management. The caste system ensured division of labour which aided economic activity.
- However, the traditional Indian knowledge system was static and did not encourage disruptive innovation. It was also increasingly rigid. Modern management has evolved as a separate discipline. Still, many lessons can be drawn from the ancient Indian approaches to management and society.
