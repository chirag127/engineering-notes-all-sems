### Chemistry in India and Ancient India

1. Ancient Indian philosophers such as Kanada, philosopher of the Vaisheshika school, postulated that the atomic theory and the existence of subtler particles. The Nyaya and Vaisheshika schools developed elaborate theories on the existence of atoms (paramanu) and their properties.
2. The Jain philosopher Kundakunda described the universe as consisting wholly of two substances: soul and non-soul. According to him, all object of knowledge can be explained by aggregates of these two substances. The non-soul, which includes matter, is composed of atoms of varying magnitudes peculiar to itself. The magnitude of an atom is characterized by the number of internal subdivisions.
3. The Buddhists rejected the existence of wholes or absolute truths, holding instead that all phenomena are but complexes of individual elements in a state of perpetual flux. The elements themselves were thought to be no more than centers of activity, devoid of an underlying substance. Early Buddhistic literature is replete with terms suggestive of the atom, and commentators enumerate six varieties of atoms, corresponding to the elements.
4. Pingala (300 BCE-200 BCE) invented the binary numeral system and the Fibonacci sequence. He described the formation of meters in Sanskrit poetry mathematically.
5. In the 10th century, Halayudha wrote a commentary on Pingala's work. In the beginning part, Halayudha described the Fibonacci sequence and Pascal's triangle using Sanskrit poetic verses.
