#### Four-class Classification of Society in Ancient India

- The ancient Indian society was divided into four classes based on occupation and social status:
- Brahmins: The priests and teachers. They were the highest ranked and most respected.
- Kshatriyas: The warriors and rulers. They were the second highest rank and protected the society.
- Vaishyas: The traders and merchants. They were involved in business and commerce.
- Shudras: The labourers and servants. They were the lowest rank and served the other three classes.
- This four-fold classification was an ideal distribution of functions and duties in the society. In reality, there was fairly significant mobility and interaction across the classes.
- The caste system later evolved from this initial four-class system and became more rigid and hereditary in nature, severely restricting mobility and interaction between castes.
