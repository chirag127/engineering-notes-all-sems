### Sculptures as Cultural Heritage of India

1. Indian sculptures have a long history and tradition and are an important part of India's cultural heritage. Some of the prominent sculptures from ancient India include:
- The Mauryan pillars and capitals with animal sculptures. The Sarnath lion capital is the national emblem of India.
- The sculptures of gods and goddesses in temples such as the Sun Temple at Konark and temples at Khajuraho.
- The cave paintings and sculptures at Ajanta and Ellora caves including sculptures of Buddha.

2. The Gupta period is known as the Golden Age of Indian sculptures. The sculptures became more lifelike and incorporated more sensuality and grace. The Dashavatara temple at Deogarh is a fine example of Gupta sculpture.

3. The Chola dynasty in southern India produced magnificent bronze sculptures, considered pinnacles of bronze casting. The Nataraja sculpture of Shiva in the 'Lord of the Dance' pose is a famous Chola sculpture.

4. The eastern Indian Pala dynasty created sculptures influenced by Tibetan styles with similarities to South-east Asian art, as seen in the Buddhist sculptures at Nalanda.

5. The Vijayanagara empire produced sculptures with distinct styles, as seen in the Virupaksha temple at Hampi containing ornate pillars, entrance gateways and monuments.

The Indian sculptures represent the artistic traditions, religious beliefs and the legacy of diverse dynasties and regions of India. They hold a valuable place in the cultural heritage of India.
