### Evolution of script in India

1. Indus script (3300 BCE - 1900 BCE): The Indus Valley civilization had a script with around 400 signs. However, the script has not yet been deciphered and its language is unknown.

2. Brahmi script (3rd century BCE): The Brahmi script developed in the 3rd century BCE and was the first script used to write Sanskrit and Prakrit languages. It was the ancestor of many scripts used in South and Southeast Asia including Devanagari. Edicts of Ashoka were written in Brahmi script.

3. Gupta script (4th century CE - 6th century CE): The Gupta script developed during the Gupta Empire and was used to write Sanskrit. It evolved from the Brahmi script and led to the development of scripts such as Siddham and Devanagari. The Gupta script was considered as one of the most beautiful scripts with ornate letters.

4. Nagari script (from 6th century CE): From the Gupta script developed the Nagari script which means "city script". The Devanagari script used to write Hindi and other languages developed from Nagari script. The term "Nagari" is now used as a general term for the family of scripts including Devanagari that are descended from Gupta script.

The evolution of scripts in India has been a continuous process with each script deriving from and modifying the previous one. The Indian scripts have also spread to other parts of Asia with the spread of religions like Hinduism, Buddhism, and Jainism from India.
