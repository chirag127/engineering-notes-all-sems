### Stages of State Formation in Ancient India

1. Janapadas (1500 BCE - 600 BCE): These were small territorial units/kingdoms in the form of clan-based communities. They gradually underwent urbanization and class differentiation, leading to the rise of larger kingdoms.
2. Mahajanapadas (600 BCE - 300 BCE): The janapadas consolidated into around 16 major oligarchies and kingdoms called Mahajanapadas, including Gandhara, Kamboja, Kosala, Magadha, etc. They had more complex political organizations and monarchical states emerged in this phase.
3. The Nanda Empire (424 BCE - 321 BCE): The Nandas were the first empire builders in India, establishing a large kingdom in Magadha with its capital at Pataliputra. However, Alexander's invasion exposed the weakness of the Nanda empire.
4. The Mauryan Empire (321 BCE - 185 BCE): Chandragupta Maurya overthrew the Nandas and established the Mauryan empire, which reached its peak under Ashoka the Great. The Mauryan empire was a collection of territories under centralized imperial rule with a large bureaucracy and army.
5. Post-Mauryan Kingdoms (185 BCE - 320 CE): After the decline of the Mauryans, several new kingdoms emerged, including those of the Sungas, Kanvas, Satavahanas, Kushans, etc. Trade and commerce grew during this period and several advances were made in science and technology. The Gupta empire eventually emerged in this phase.
