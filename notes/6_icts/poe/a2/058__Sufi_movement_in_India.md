### Sufi Movement in India

1. Sufism originated in Persia and emphasized personal devotion and love of God. It spread to India around the 12th century.
2. The early Sufi saints who came to India were Sheikh Ali-ud-din, Sheikh Muin-ud-din Chisti, Qutb-ud-din Bakhtiar Kaki, etc. They settled in various parts of India and attracted Hindu devotees as well with their simple teachings and ascetic way of life.
3. The Sufi saints condemned idol worship and the caste system but adopted many institutions and practices of the Hindu society. For example, the Urs festival commemorating the death anniversary of the Sufi saints was similar to the Hindu practice of celebrating death ceremonies. This mixing of cultures led to the development of a composite Indo-Islamic culture.
4. The religious ideas of Sufism like Wahdat-ul-Wajud (Unity of Being) attracted people of other faiths like Hindus, Buddhists, Jains, Sikhs, etc. It led to the spread of a liberal and humanistic Islam that went beyond the theological complexities. This promoted religious harmony and mutual understanding among people of different faiths in India.
5. However, after the 18th century, Sufism lost its original liberal character under the influence of Wahhabism and other conservative Islamic movements. As a result, the liberal Sufi trends are on the decline in modern India while radical strands are gaining strength.
