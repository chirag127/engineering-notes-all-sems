### Persian Literature in India

1. Persian literature flourished in India during the Delhi Sultanate and Mughal rule between the 13th and 19th centuries. Many Indian writers wrote in Persian, including Amir Khusraw and Mirza Ghalib.

2. The Delhi Sultanate was established in 1206 by Turkic invaders and promoted Persian culture and language in India. Persian became the official language of the courts and administration, resulting in its increased prominence and influence. Many notable Persian poets travelled from Persia to India during this period, such as Amir Khusraw.

3. Persian poetry was especially valued, with poetic forms like masnavi, ghazal and rubai becoming popular in India. Many famous Persian works such as Nizami Ganjavi's Khamsa were translated into local languages. Prominent themes included spirituality and divine love similar to Sufi traditions. King Muhammad Quli Qutb Shah was also a renowned poet who wrote in Persian and Telugu.

4. During Mughal rule from 1526, Persian literature and poetry flourished and reached its zenith. The Mughal emperors, especially Shah Jahan, actively patronized Persian literature and amassed large libraries of Persian manuscripts. Important poets of this period include Ghalib, Zauq and Sauda who wrote on themes of romance and kingship. Persian literature influenced local languages like Urdu which incorporated much of its vocabulary and poetic forms.

5. By the 19th century, Persian's prominence was declining with the rise of Urdu and English. However, Persian literature remains an important part of the cultural heritage of South Asia and has had a lasting impact on the region. Its traces can be found in many modern South Asian languages as well as traditions of poetry and spirituality.
