### Shankaracharya and Advaita Vedanta

1. Adi Shankaracharya was an 8th century Indian philosopher and theologian who consolidated the doctrine of Advaita Vedanta.
2. Advaita Vedanta is a school of Vedanta that teaches nondualism - the identity of the Self (Atman) and the Whole (Brahman). According to Advaita, Brahman is the eternal, infinite, omnipresent, omniscient, omnipotent reality that is the grounds and origin of all being.
3. Shankara systematized the works of preceding philosophers, and synthesized the Upanishadic teachings to give a authoritative exegesis on the Brahma Sutras, which is Shankara's distinct interpretation.
4. According to Shankara, the knowledge of Brahman arises from a rigorous process of discernment and negation of all that is not Brahman - the finite world with its multiplicity of selves and objects is an illusory appearance (maya) of the one ultimate reality.
5. Shankara emphasized meditation and introspection as the primary means to gaining knowledge of the Brahman, which would lead to liberation (moksha) from the cycle of rebirth (samsara). His philosophy is essentially a rigorous logical exposition of the Upanishadic teachings.
6. Shankara's influence on Hindu thought has been deep and pervasive. His monistic philosophy and exegesis of the Upanishads shaped the Indian philosophical tradition and influenced subsequent theistic and Vishishtadvaita traditions of Vedanta.
