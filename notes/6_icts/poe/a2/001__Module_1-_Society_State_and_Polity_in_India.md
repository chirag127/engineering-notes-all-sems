## Module 1- Society State and Polity in India

1. Indian society is diverse with many ethnic, linguistic, religious and caste groups. This diversity emerged from various historical processes including migrations, social reform movements and policies. The Constitution of India envisages a secular polity where equal respect is given to all faiths.

2. The Indian State is the successor state to British India. The Government of India Act 1935 was the last law enacted by the British Parliament and it laid down the structure of the state. The Constitution of India, adopted in 1950, converted India into a sovereign, socialist, secular democratic republic. It defines the structure, roles and functions of the state, sets out the rights and duties of citizens, and distributes powers between the Union and the States.

3. The political system in India has the distinctive institution of the Panchayati Raj (local self-government). Regular elections are held at all levels- rural, urban and national to choose representatives. Democracy in India has contributed to political awareness and participation of ordinary people in the process of decision-making. However, there are socio-economic and gender disparities in citizenship, access to resources and opportunities.

4. The relationship between society, state and polity is dynamic. The state sometimes influences social change through laws, policies and judicial interventions. In turn, social movements play an important role in shaping laws, policies and political discourse. Over time both society and polity have undergone significant changes though legacies of the past persist in many forms.
