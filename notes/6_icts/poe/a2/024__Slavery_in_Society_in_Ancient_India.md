#### Slavery in Society in Ancient India

1. Slavery was prevalent in ancient Indian society and considered acceptable. Slaves were treated as properties that could be bought or sold.
2. Debtors unable to repay loans would often become slaves to the creditors. People would also be enslaved as punishment for crimes or captured during wars and raids on other kingdoms.
3. The rights of slaves were limited. They had to obey their masters and work for them without wages. However, they had some basic human rights and could not be subjected to extreme physical punishments.
4. There were limitations on a master's power over a slave. Slaves could earn and save money to buy their freedom. They could also be freed if they performed certain meritorious acts or if masters chose to free them.
5. The very low social status of slaves meant inter-caste marriages were permitted and slaves were not rigidly restricted within the caste system. They mainly worked as laborers, servants, and concubines.
6. The Arthashastra lays down rules about the treatment of slaves and their rights. It provides for state officers to enforce these rules and penalize masters violating the rights of slaves. This indicates slavery was present but limited in its severity.
7. With the spread of Jainism and Buddhism, the status of slaves improved marginally but slavery as an institution continued to exist till it was abolished in India in the mid-20th century.
