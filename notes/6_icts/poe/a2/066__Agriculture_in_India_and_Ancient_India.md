### Agriculture in India and Ancient India

1. Agriculture has been practiced in India since ancient times. Evidence of cultivation of crops like wheat, barley, rice, lentils, and millets have been found from the remains of the ancient Indus Valley civilization.
2. Irrigation was used during the Indus Valley civilization around 4500 years ago. Wells and canals were built for irrigation. Tanks and reservoirs were also constructed to store water.
3. Crop rotation was practiced where legumes would be grown one season and grains the next, to maintain soil fertility. Fallowing or leaving land uncultivated for a season was also done.
4. Extensive trade of spices, grains, and textiles was done with ancient civilizations like Sumer and Egypt. Spices, cotton, and indigo were exported. Copper, tin, and gold were imported.
5. The advent of metal tools and the ox-drawn plough enabled more clearing of land and increased agricultural production. The Mauryan empire under Ashoka further expanded irrigation infrastructure.
6. In ancient times, peasants or small landowners would cultivate the land. They would keep a portion of the produce and pay taxes in kind to the rulers. The Arthashastra by Kautilya details taxation based on the type of crops produced in a region.
7. Several ancient texts such as the Vedas and the ancient Tamil literature describe agricultural practices and seasons in detail. The importance of agriculture and farmers is highlighted in many ancient Indian scriptures and texts.
