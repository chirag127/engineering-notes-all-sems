#### Force Theory of origin of State

- According to this theory, the State came into existence as a result of conquest or force. The most powerful man in a tribe conquered the other tribes and established his authority over the conquered tribes through force. This gave rise to the institution of the State.
- The State thus originated from conquest, military strength, and violence. Might was right. The ruler ruled over the people through force and they had to obey him.
- The State was the creation of the strong man who had subjugated others by the use of force. The ruler and the ruling class kept their authority intact through armed forces and punishments.
- The force theory emphasizes the role of the ruler and strength and holds that the origin of the State can be traced to the use of force. The advocates of this theory believe that in the primitive tribal society there was anarchy and lawlessness and that the institution of the State put an end to this chaotic condition through the use of force.
- However, this theory is one-sided as it ignores the role of other factors in the origin of the State. Conquest alone cannot explain the origin of the State. There were economic and social factors as well behind the rise of the institution of the State. This theory is no longer accepted in its crude form by scholars and historians.
