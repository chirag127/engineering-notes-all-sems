### Water Management in India and Ancient India

1. Ancient India had a well-developed system of water management, including irrigation networks, aqueducts, reservoirs, and wells. The Indus Valley Civilization had a sophisticated sewerage and drainage system.

2. Large reservoirs were constructed, primarily for irrigation. These include the Sambhar lake in Rajasthan, built in the 4th century CE, and the Kallanai dam, built around 2nd century CE, one of the oldest water-diversion or water-regulator structures in the world which is still in use.

3. Step wells were built to access groundwater, especially in Western India. The wells acted as water reservoirs, providing water even during dry seasons. The Chand Baori near Jaipur is one of the deepest and largest step wells in India.

4. During the Chanakya period (4th century BCE), water management was given significant importance. Chanakya's Arthashastra contains details of water management practices, including construction of dams, canals, and wells. It recommends that the king should take keen interest in the construction of water works and ensure their proper maintenance.

5. Traditional Indian society gave significant importance to the management and conservation of water. This was dictated by the hot climate and dependence on monsoon in most parts of the country. Cultural practices and religious beliefs stressed limited use of water and conservation.
