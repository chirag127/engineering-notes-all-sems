#### Marriage in Society in Ancient India

- Marriage was considered a sacred bond and an important institution in ancient Indian society. It was regarded as a Samskara (sacrament) which was compulsory for all.
- Child marriages were prevalent, especially among the aristocratic families. Boys would marry at ages 11-13 and girls would marry at ages 8-11. The primary reason for child marriages was to reinforce kinship bonds and preserve family lineages.
- Polygamy was allowed for men, especially among kings and the wealthy. However, polyandry (a woman having multiple husbands) was rare and restricted to some communities in the Himalayan region.
- Arranged marriages were the norm, with family elders arranging marriages based on family lineage, varna, patrimony, bride-price etc. The consent of the bride and groom were not required.
- Marriage alliances were forged on the basis of equality of social status as signified by factors like caste, occupation, family reputation etc. Cross-cousin marriages were preferred to keep property within the family.
- After marriage, the bride would move in with the groom's family and was expected to serve her in-laws. Dowry was paid by the bride's family to the groom to maintain their social status.
- Divorce was allowed but highly frowned upon. Only male members of the family could initiate divorce. Remarriage of widows was restricted in many communities.
