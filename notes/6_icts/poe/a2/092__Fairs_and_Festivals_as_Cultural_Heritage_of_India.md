### Fairs and Festivals as Cultural Heritage of India

1. India is a land of fairs and festivals. Fairs and festivals are an integral part of Indian culture. They reflect the entire gamut of human emotions and behavior.

2. Indian festivals and fairs are marked by colors, gaiety, enthusiasm, prayers and rituals. They are tied to important milestones of life like birth, marriage and death. The seasonal festivals celebrate the harvesting of crops and new beginnings.

3. Some of the major festivals in India are:
- Diwali - The festival of lights signifies the victory of good over evil. People light up their homes and offices with diyas and candles.
- Holi - The festival of colors is a celebration of spring and happiness. People play with colors and water.
- Onam - The harvest festival of Kerala, it is a 10 day long festival marked by boat races, dances, and other cultural activities.
- Pongal - The harvest festival of Tamil Nadu, people celebrate the harvest of rice and other crops.
- Ganesh Chaturthi - The festival dedicated to Lord Ganesha who is worshipped as the remover of obstacles. Idols of Ganesha are installed and worshipped for 1-10 days.

4. Indian fairs are marketplaces that gather people from surrounding villages. Some popular fairs are:
- Kumbh Mela - Held at 4 locations, it is the largest gathering of people for a holy dip in the Ganges.
- Pushkar Camel Fair - Held in Rajasthan, it showcases cattle trading along with various cultural events and activities.
- Dussehra Mela of Mysore - A major fair of Karnataka celebrating Dussehra with cultural programs, thrill rides and a torchlight parade.
