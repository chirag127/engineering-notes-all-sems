#### Mystical Theory of origin of State

The Mystical Theory suggests that the origin of the state is divine and that the ruler is chosen by a higher power. Here are some key points to understand this theory:

- The theory believes that the state is not a human creation but a divine creation. It suggests that the state is a manifestation of a divine plan and that the ruler is chosen by a higher power to lead the state.

- According to this theory, the ruler is not chosen based on merit or popular vote, but by the will of the divine. The ruler is seen as the representative of the divine on earth, and their authority is derived from this divine mandate.

- The Mystical Theory also suggests that the state has a spiritual purpose and that the ruler's role is not just to govern but also to guide the people towards spiritual enlightenment. The ruler is seen as a spiritual leader who has a duty to promote the divine will and lead the people towards salvation.

- This theory is often associated with religious traditions and has been used to justify the rule of monarchs and priests. It suggests that the ruler is not just a political figure but also a religious figure who has a divine mandate to lead the people.

- The Mystical Theory's emphasis on the divine origin of the state and the ruler's role as a spiritual leader has led to the development of theocracy in some societies where religion plays a dominant role in governance.

- Critics of the Mystical Theory argue that it is based on unprovable assumptions and that it does not provide a rational basis for understanding the origin of the state. They also argue that it can be used to justify authoritarian rule and the suppression of individual rights and freedoms.

In conclusion, the Mystical Theory of the origin of the state suggests that the state is a divine creation, and the ruler is chosen by a higher power. It emphasizes the spiritual purpose of the state and the ruler's role as a spiritual leader. While this theory has been used to justify the rule of monarchs and priests, it has also been criticized for lacking a rational basis and justifying authoritarian rule.
