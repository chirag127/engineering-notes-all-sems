### Jainism in India

Jainism is an ancient religion that originated in India. It is one of the oldest religions in the world, dating back to the 6th century BCE. Here are some key points about Jainism in India:

- Jainism was founded by Mahavira, who is also known as Vardhamana.
- Jains believe in the concept of karma, which means that every action has consequences, and these consequences determine the future of the individual.
- Jains follow a strict code of conduct, which includes principles like non-violence, truthfulness, non-stealing, celibacy, and non-attachment.
- Jains are vegetarians and do not consume any food that involves killing of animals, including eggs and dairy products.
- Jains worship Tirthankaras, who are spiritual teachers that have attained enlightenment and liberation from the cycle of birth and death.
- Jains believe in the idea of ahimsa, which means non-violence. This is a central principle of Jainism and is reflected in their way of life.
- Jains have contributed significantly to Indian culture and society. They have built temples, contributed to art and literature, and have been involved in various social and educational initiatives.
- There are two major sects of Jainism in India – Digambara and Svetambara. The Digambara sect believes that monks should not wear any clothes, while the Svetambara sect allows monks to wear white clothes.
- The holiest city for Jains in India is Shravanabelagola in Karnataka, which is home to the world's largest monolithic statue, the Gommateshwara statue.
- Jainism has had a significant influence on Indian philosophy and spirituality, and has played a key role in shaping the country's cultural and social fabric.

Overall, Jainism is an important religion in India, and its principles of non-violence and ethical conduct have had a profound impact on Indian society and culture.
