#### Marriage in Society in Ancient India

Marriage was considered a sacred institution in ancient India. It was regarded as a duty towards society and a religious obligation. Here are some points about marriage in ancient India:

- Marriage was considered a sacrament and was considered one of the most important events in a person's life. It was believed that marriage was the means to achieve the four purusharthas – Dharma (righteousness), Artha (wealth), Kama (pleasure), and Moksha (liberation).
- Marriage was arranged by the parents or elders of the family. The bride and groom had little say in the matter. The parents would choose a suitable match based on caste, family background, and social status.
- The wedding ceremony was elaborate and had many rituals and customs. It involved the exchange of gifts, clothes, and jewelry. The couple would take seven vows (Saptapadi) around the sacred fire (Agni) and promise to love, respect, and support each other.
- The bride's family had to pay a dowry to the groom's family. The dowry was seen as a way to compensate for the expenses incurred by the groom's family in raising him. Dowry was also seen as a way of securing the bride's future in case of any unfortunate events.
- Polygamy was practiced but only by the wealthy and the powerful. It was not common among the common people. The practice of polyandry, where a woman had multiple husbands, was also prevalent in some regions.
- Divorce was allowed, but it was not common. It was seen as a failure of the marriage and was frowned upon by society. Divorce was usually initiated by the husband, and the wife would have to return the dowry. In some cases, the wife could also be punished for not fulfilling her duties as a wife.
- Marriage was seen as a way of continuing the family lineage. Having children was essential, and having a son was considered important as he would carry on the family name and perform the last rites of the parents.
- The role of women in marriage was limited. They were expected to be obedient, submissive, and fulfill their duties as a wife and mother. Women were not allowed to inherit property, and their role was limited to the domestic sphere.

In conclusion, marriage in ancient India was a sacred institution that was seen as a religious and social obligation. It involved many customs and rituals, and the bride and groom had little say in the matter. Marriage was seen as a way of continuing the family lineage and achieving the four purusharthas. However, the role of women was limited, and divorce was frowned upon by society.
