#### Evolutionary Theory of origin of State

The Evolutionary Theory of origin of State suggests that the State has evolved over time from the primitive family. This theory was first proposed by Herbert Spencer in the 19th century. According to this theory, the State has evolved through the following stages:

- **Family Stage:** In the beginning, human beings lived in small groups or families. This stage was characterized by the absence of a central authority or government. The head of the family had absolute power over its members.

- **Clan Stage:** As human beings started living in larger groups, families joined together to form clans. The clan was headed by a chief, who exercised authority over all its members. The chief was usually the strongest or the most influential member of the clan.

- **Tribal Stage:** With the growth of population, clans merged to form tribes. The tribal chief was more powerful than the clan chief and exercised greater authority. The tribe was governed by custom and tradition, rather than by written laws.

- **Feudal Stage:** The feudal stage was characterized by the emergence of feudalism. The feudal lord was the ruler of a small area or territory. He provided protection to his subjects in exchange for their loyalty and service. The feudal system was based on personal relationships rather than on a centralized government.

- **Nation-State Stage:** The nation-state stage is the final stage in the evolution of the State. It is characterized by the emergence of a centralized government that exercises authority over a defined territory. The nation-state is based on the principle of sovereignty, which means that the State has the right to exercise power within its borders without interference from external forces.

In conclusion, the Evolutionary Theory of origin of State suggests that the State has evolved over time from the primitive family to the modern nation-state. This theory provides a historical perspective on the development of the State and helps us understand the complex nature of modern governments.
