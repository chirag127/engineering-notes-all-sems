### Jain Literature in Pali, Prakrit and Sanskrit

- Jain literature refers to the religious and philosophical texts of the Jain tradition.
- Jain literature is primarily written in three languages: Pali, Prakrit and Sanskrit.
- Pali is the language of the Tipitaka, the Buddhist canon, and is also used in some Jain texts. 
- Prakrit is a Middle Indo-Aryan language that was widely used in ancient India. It was the language of the common people and was used for everyday communication. 
- Sanskrit is a classical language of India and is known for its rich literary tradition. It was used for writing religious and philosophical texts, as well as works on science, medicine and other subjects. 
- Some of the most important Jain texts in Pali include the Samannaphala-sutta, the Culla-hamsa-sutta and the Dhammapada. 
- Prakrit was used extensively in Jain literature, and some of the most important Jain texts in Prakrit include the Svetambara canon, the Digambara canon and the Prakirnaka-sutra. 
- Sanskrit was also used for writing Jain texts, and some of the most important Jain texts in Sanskrit include the Tattvartha-sutra, the Yogashastra and the Samayasara. 
- Jain literature covers a wide range of topics, including philosophy, ethics, cosmology, mythology, and ritual. 
- Jain literature is known for its emphasis on non-violence, truthfulness, and self-control, and for its unique approach to spiritual liberation. 
- Jain literature has had a profound influence on Indian culture and has contributed significantly to the development of Indian philosophy, religion, and literature.
