

#### Harappan Script

* The Harappan script is an ancient writing system used in the Indus Valley civilization, which flourished in the Indian subcontinent between the 26th and 20th centuries BCE. 
* The script is still undeciphered, but it is believed to be a logo-syllabic writing system, meaning that it combines symbols that represent both syllables and words. 
* It is thought to have been used for a variety of purposes, including religious and commercial.
* The script has been found on a variety of objects, such as seals, pottery, and copper tablets. 
* The script is thought to be related to the Brahmi script, which is the oldest known form of Indian writing. 
* Scholars have attempted to decipher the script by comparing it to other ancient scripts, such as the Sumerian cuneiform script and the Egyptian hieroglyphs. 
* Research suggests that the script may have been used to write a variety of languages, including Dravidian and Indo-Aryan. 
* The script is believed to have been used until the 13th century BCE, when it was replaced by other writing systems.
