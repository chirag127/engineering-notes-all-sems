
#### Marriage in Society in Ancient India

- In ancient India, marriage was an important social institution. It was considered a sacrament and was an integral part of the Hindu religion.
- Marriage was primarily an arrangement between two families, and the bride and groom had little say in the matter. 
- Arranged marriages were the norm, and most marriages were between people of the same caste. 
- Marriages were often used to strengthen alliances between families and to increase the wealth of the family. 
- Dowry was a common practice in ancient India, and was seen as a way for the bride's family to provide the groom's family with a gift. 
- Marriage was considered to be a lifelong commitment and divorce was rare. 
- Women were expected to be devoted to their husbands and to their in-laws, and they had few rights in the marriage. 
- Men, on the other hand, had more freedom and were allowed to take multiple wives. 
- Marriage ceremonies were elaborate and included rituals such as the exchange of garlands, the tying of the knot, and the giving of gifts.
