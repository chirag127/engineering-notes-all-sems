
### Six System Indian Philosophy

1. Nyaya: The Nyaya school of philosophy is one of the six orthodox schools of Hinduism. It is based on the Nyaya Sutras, attributed to the sage Gautama, which is one of the earliest works of logic and epistemology. It focuses on logic, epistemology and metaphysics, and is known for its rigorous analysis of the sources of knowledge.

2. Vaisheshika: The Vaisheshika school of philosophy is one of the six orthodox schools of Hinduism. It is based on the Vaisheshika Sutras, attributed to the sage Kanada, which is one of the earliest works of atomism. It focuses on the nature of reality, the origin and destruction of the universe, and the nature of substance.

3. Samkhya: The Samkhya school of philosophy is one of the six orthodox schools of Hinduism. It is based on the Samkhya Karika, attributed to the sage Kapila, which is one of the earliest works of dualism. It focuses on the duality of Purusha (soul) and Prakriti (matter), the three Gunas, and the path of Yoga to liberation.

4. Yoga: The Yoga school of philosophy is one of the six orthodox schools of Hinduism. It is based on the Yoga Sutras, attributed to the sage Patanjali, which is one of the earliest works of yoga. It focuses on the practice of yoga, the eight limbs of yoga, and the liberation of the soul from the cycle of birth and death.

5. Purva Mimamsa: The Purva Mimamsa school of philosophy is one of the six orthodox schools of Hinduism. It is based on the Mimamsa Sutras, attributed to the sage Jaimini, which is one of the earliest works of Vedic exegesis. It focuses on the interpretation of the Vedas, the duties of a Brahmin, and the nature of Dharma.

6. Uttara Mimamsa (Vedanta): The Uttara Mimamsa (Vedanta) school of philosophy is one of the six orthodox schools of Hinduism. It is based on the Upanishads, which is one of the earliest works of Vedanta. It focuses on the nature of Brahman, the identity of Atman and Brahman, and the path of knowledge (jnana yoga) to liberation.
