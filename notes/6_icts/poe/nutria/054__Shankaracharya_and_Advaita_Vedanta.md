
### Shankaracharya and Advaita Vedanta

- Shankaracharya was an 8th century Indian philosopher who is credited with the establishment of the Advaita Vedanta school of Hindu philosophy.
- Advaita Vedanta is a school of Hindu philosophy that emphasizes the non-dual nature of the ultimate reality, Brahman. It is based on the Upanishads, which are ancient Hindu texts.
- According to Advaita Vedanta, the ultimate reality, Brahman, is without attributes and is beyond the physical world. It is the source of all existence and is one without a second.
- Shankaracharya believed that the individual soul, or atman, is an inseparable part of Brahman. He taught that the only true path to liberation from suffering and rebirth is the realization of the oneness of the soul and Brahman.
- Shankaracharya also emphasized the importance of meditation, self-inquiry and other spiritual practices as means of realizing this oneness. He believed that these practices were essential for the attainment of moksha, or liberation.
