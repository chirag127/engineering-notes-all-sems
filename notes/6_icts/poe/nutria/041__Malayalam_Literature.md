
### Malayalam Literature

Malayalam literature is a body of works written in the Malayalam language, which is a Dravidian language spoken in the Indian state of Kerala. It is one of the 22 scheduled languages of India and was designated a Classical Language in 2013.

Malayalam literature has a rich literary tradition and includes works by authors from various regions of Kerala. It is divided into three distinct periods: Old Malayalam (900-1300 CE), Middle Malayalam (1300-1700 CE), and Modern Malayalam (1700-present).

The earliest known literary work in Malayalam is Ramacharitam, a 12th-century poetic work by Cherusseri Namboothiri. It is considered to be the earliest literary work in any Indian language.

Other important works from the Old Malayalam period include Unnuneeli Sandesam, Unniyadi Charitam, and Ramayanam.

In the Middle Malayalam period, important works include Chandrotsavam, Adhyatmaramayanam, and Bhashakautalam.

In the Modern Malayalam period, important works include Thakazhi Sivasankara Pillai's Chemmeen, O. V. Vijayan's Khasakkinte Itihasam, and M. T. Vasudevan Nair's Randamoozham.

Malayalam literature also includes works of poetry, drama, and short stories. Important poets include Kunchan Nambiar, Poonthanam, and Vallathol Narayana Menon. Important dramatists include K. T. Muhammed and C. V. Raman Pillai. Important short story writers include Uroob, Thakazhi Sivasankara Pillai, and M. Mukundan.
