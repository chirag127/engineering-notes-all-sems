
### Kannada Literature

Kannada literature is the corpus of literary works written in the Kannada language, a language spoken mainly in the Indian state of Karnataka. Kannada literature has a rich and long literary tradition and history, with some of its earliest works dating back to the 6th century. Kannada literature is divided into three major periods: Old Kannada (450-1200 CE), Middle Kannada (1200-1700 CE) and Modern Kannada (1700-present). 

Kannada literature is noted for its range of topics and genres, including epics, poetry, plays, short stories, novels, and essays. Notable works include the Kavirajamarga, a 9th-century guide to poetic composition; the epic poem Jaimini Bharata, written by Ratnakaravarni in the 12th century; and the modernist works of P. Lankesh and U.R. Ananthamurthy. 

Kannada literature has been influenced by a variety of sources, including Sanskrit, Prakrit, Tamil, and Telugu. It has also been influenced by the oral traditions of the region, as well as by modern Indian literary movements such as the Bengal Renaissance and the Indian Independence Movement. Kannada literature has also been enriched by contact with Western literature, particularly the works of William Shakespeare and other English writers. 

Kannada literature continues to be produced today, with new works being published in newspapers, magazines, and online. Kannada literature is celebrated in literary festivals and awards, including the Karnataka Sahitya Academy Awards, which recognize outstanding works in the field.
