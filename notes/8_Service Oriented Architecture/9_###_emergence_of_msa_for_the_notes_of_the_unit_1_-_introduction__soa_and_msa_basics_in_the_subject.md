### Emergence of MSA for the notes of the Unit 1 - Introduction: SOA and MSA Basics in the subject of Service Oriented Architecture
Emergence of Microservice Architecture (MSA) can be traced back to the early 2000s, when organizations started adopting Service Oriented Architecture (SOA) as a way to develop and manage their applications. The goal of SOA was to create a loosely coupled system of services that could be easily maintained and scaled. However, as organizations started to adopt SOA, they encountered several challenges such as complex service interactions, tight coupling, and difficulty in scaling individual services.

In response to these challenges, MSA emerged as a new architectural style that aimed to address the limitations of SOA. MSA is a way of building and organizing software systems as a collection of small, independent services that communicate with each other through APIs. Unlike SOA, MSA is designed to be highly scalable, flexible, and resilient, and it encourages the development of small, focused services that can be easily maintained and updated.

MSA has gained popularity in recent years due to the rise of cloud computing and the need for organizations to be able to quickly respond to changing business requirements. MSA is also well suited to modern software development practices such as DevOps and continuous delivery, which focus on delivering high-quality software quickly and efficiently.

In conclusion, the emergence of MSA can be seen as a response to the limitations of SOA, and it has become a popular architectural style for building and organizing software systems. MSA provides organizations with a way to build scalable, flexible, and resilient software systems that can be quickly updated and maintained, and it is well suited to modern software development practices.
