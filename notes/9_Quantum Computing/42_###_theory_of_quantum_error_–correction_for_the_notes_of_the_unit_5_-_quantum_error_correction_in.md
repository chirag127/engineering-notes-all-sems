### Theory of Quantum Error –Correction for the notes of the Unit 5 - Quantum Error Correction in the subject of Quantum Computing
Quantum Error Correction (QEC) is a technique used to protect quantum information from errors caused by environmental factors, such as noise and decoherence. It works by encoding the quantum state into a larger number of physical qubits, so that the information is distributed over multiple qubits. This makes it possible to detect and correct errors without destroying the quantum state. There are several different QEC codes, including the stabilizer codes and the topological codes. The stabilizer codes are the most widely used, and they work by using parity checks to detect errors, and then applying correction operations to fix the errors. The topological codes use the properties of topological systems to protect the quantum information, and they are considered to be more robust against errors than the stabilizer codes. Overall, QEC is a critical component of quantum computing, as it allows us to build scalable and reliable quantum computers.
