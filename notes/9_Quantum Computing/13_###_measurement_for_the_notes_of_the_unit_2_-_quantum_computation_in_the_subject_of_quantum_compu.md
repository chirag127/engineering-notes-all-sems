### Measurement for the notes of the Unit 2 - Quantum Computation in the subject of Quantum Computing
The measurement in quantum computing is defined as the process of obtaining information about the quantum state of a quantum system. The measurement is a probabilistic process, meaning that the outcome of a measurement is not determined until it is made. The measurement in quantum computing is an important aspect because it determines the final state of the quantum system and influences the outcome of quantum algorithms. Some key points to consider when studying measurement in quantum computing include:

1. State collapse: The measurement process causes the quantum state to collapse into one of its eigenstates, with a probability determined by the corresponding eigenvalue.

2. Observables: The measurement process is performed on observables, which are Hermitian operators that represent physical quantities.

3. Eigenstates: The eigenstates of an observable are the states that are unchanged by the measurement of that observable.

4. Eigenvalues: The eigenvalues of an observable are the possible outcomes of a measurement of that observable, with corresponding probabilities determined by the quantum state.

5. Measurement basis: The measurement basis is the set of eigenstates used to perform the measurement. The choice of measurement basis can influence the outcome of the measurement.
