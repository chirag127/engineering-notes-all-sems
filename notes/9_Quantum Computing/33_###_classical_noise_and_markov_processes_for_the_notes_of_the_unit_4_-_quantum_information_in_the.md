### Classical Noise and Markov Processes for the notes of the Unit 4 - Quantum Information in the subject of Quantum Computing
Classical Noise: Refers to unwanted random fluctuations in a classical system that can affect the accuracy of measurements or computations. Examples include thermal noise, electrical noise, and shot noise.

Markov Processes: A mathematical model for a system whose future state depends only on its current state, not on its past history. In quantum computing, Markov processes can describe the evolution of quantum states over time and the effects of noise on quantum systems.

Quantum Information: The study of quantum systems as information carriers, including quantum communication, quantum cryptography, and quantum computing. In quantum computing, quantum information refers to the quantum states and operations used to perform computation.
