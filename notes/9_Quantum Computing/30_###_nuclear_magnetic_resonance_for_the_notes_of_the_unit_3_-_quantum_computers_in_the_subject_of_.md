### Nuclear Magnetic resonance for the notes of the Unit 3 - Quantum Computers in the subject of Quantum Computing
Nuclear Magnetic Resonance (NMR) is a technique used to study the molecular structure and dynamics of materials. In NMR, a sample is placed in a strong magnetic field, and the magnetic moments of the nuclei in the sample are excited by a radio frequency pulse. The nuclei then emit a signal that is proportional to their magnetic moment and can be detected and analyzed. NMR is a powerful tool for determining the chemical structure of molecules, including their composition, connectivity, and dynamics. NMR spectroscopy is also used in quantum computing for quantum state tomography, a technique for characterizing quantum states. In quantum computing, NMR can be used to implement quantum algorithms and perform quantum simulations. NMR-based quantum computers have been demonstrated with up to a few tens of qubits. However, NMR-based quantum computers are limited by the need for a large number of samples, low coherence times, and high noise levels.
