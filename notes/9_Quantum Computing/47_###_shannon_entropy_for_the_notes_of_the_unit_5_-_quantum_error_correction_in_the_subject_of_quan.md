### Shannon Entropy for the notes of the Unit 5 - Quantum Error Correction in the subject of Quantum Computing
Shannon entropy is a measure of the uncertainty or randomness in a set of data. In quantum computing, it is used to quantify the amount of quantum information stored in a quantum state. The entropy of a quantum state is equal to the logarithm of the number of possible states that can give rise to that state, and is a measure of the amount of quantum information stored in the state. This concept is important in the study of quantum error correction, as it provides a way to quantify the amount of quantum information that needs to be protected from errors or noise in a quantum system. By understanding the entropy of a quantum state, researchers can design quantum error correction codes that can protect the quantum information stored in the state from errors or noise, and ensure that the information can be transmitted accurately.
