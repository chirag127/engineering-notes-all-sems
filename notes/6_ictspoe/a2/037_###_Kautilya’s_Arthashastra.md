### Kautilya’s Arthashastra

- Written by Kautilya (also known as Chanakya) in the 4th century BCE. It is one of the earliest known treatises on statecraft, economic policy and military strategy.
- It consists of 15 books which cover a wide range of topics including taxation, monetary policy, civil service, and diplomacy.
- Some key points:

**The ideal king** - The king should be an ideal leader who is not too lenient or too harsh but delivers impartial justice. He should uphold dharma (righteousness) and achieve the common good.

**Need for secrecy** - Kautilya emphasizes the need for secrecy in statecraft. He argues that the king should not reveal his sources of information or his plans so that others cannot detect his weaknesses or intentions.

**Fortifications and military** - Kautilya goes into great detail on fort construction and maintaining a strong army with cavalry, elephants, and chariots. He recommends creating allies and using spies/secret agents to weaken enemy states.

**Economic policy** - He advocates state control and regulation of the economy. Taxes should be imposed on cultivation, trade, mining and forests. The state should collect revenue in the form of taxes, fines, tolls, and fees. The treasury should maintain a surplus to prepare for difficult times.

**Role of the vizier** - The vizier, or chief minister, plays an important role in assisting the king in administration. Kautilya recommends choosing a vizier who is highly knowledgeable and has the king's trust. The vizier should supervise other ministers and departments.

**Training of princes** - Kautilya emphasizes the proper training of princes so that they can eventually succeed as ideal kings. They should receive both theoretical education and practical training in skills such as martial arts and using weapons. Moral and character development is crucial.
