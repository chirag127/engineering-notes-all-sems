### Shankaracharya and Advaita Vedanta

- Adi Shankaracharya was an 8th century Indian philosopher and theologian who consolidated the doctrine of Advaita Vedanta.
- Advaita means "not two" or "non-dual" and refers to the idea that Brahman (the ultimate reality) and Atman (the individual self) are not two realities, but one and the same. There is no dualism in Advaita Vedanta.
- According to Advaita Vedanta, Brahman is the sole reality, and the world, individual souls, and even God (as a separate entity) are illusions (maya). The ultimate goal is to realize the identity of Atman and Brahman through self-knowledge and meditation.
- Some key ideas in Advaita Vedanta are:

1. Brahman is the only reality, the world is an illusion.
2. Atman (the individual self) is the same as Brahman (the ultimate reality).
3. Maya is the illusion that creates the appearance of duality and differentiation where there is non-duality.
4. Moksha or liberation is achieved by overcoming maya and realizing the identity of Atman and Brahman.

- Mnemonics:

"Not two, only one - Advaita"
"Brahman alone is real, the world is maya"
"Atman is Brahman, know thyself to be free"

- Some learning advantages: Provides a simple and rational method of understanding reality and path to liberation; recognizes the ultimate unity of all existence; encourages self-knowledge through meditation and inquiry rather than reliance on rituals.
- Some criticisms: May encourage a radical monism that denies the reality of the world; its metaphysics can be difficult to comprehend and realize; emphasis on Brahman as the absolute truth can be seen as exclusionary.
