### Kingship in states in Ancient India

- In Ancient India, most states were ruled by monarchs. The king was the supreme authority in the state.
- The concept of divine right of kings was popular. The king was considered to be a representative of God on earth. Hence, his authority was sacred and inviolable.
- The king's primary duties were:
    - Protecting the state from external aggression and internal disorder
    - Dispensing justice
    - Looking after the welfare of the people
- The powers of the king varied in different states and time periods:
    - In the Mauryan empire, the king's powers were limited by the bureaucracy and assemblies.
    - In the Gupta empire, the king was regarded as a benevolent deity and wielded greater powers.
    - In the Southern kingdoms like the Chola empire, the king was an autocrat with vast powers and was considered to be the owner of the land and his subjects.
- Some philosophies and dharmashastra texts also placed certain duties and limits on the king's power:
    - He could not act arbitrarily and had to uphold dharma (duty, righteousness)
    - He had to seek the consent of ministers and assemblies on important matters
    - His actions were subject to the approval of the people. If he became tyrannical, he could be replaced or overthrown.
- Mnemonics:
    - MAD: Mauryan administration with departments and assemblies limiting power
    - BGD: Benevolent deity of the Guptas wielding greater power
    - COT: Chola king, the owner with vast autocratic powers
