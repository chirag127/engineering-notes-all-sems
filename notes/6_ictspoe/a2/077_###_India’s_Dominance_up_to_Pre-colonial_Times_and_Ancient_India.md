### India’s Dominance up to Pre-colonial Times and Ancient India

- Ancient India was one of the most advanced and dominant civilizations from around 3000 BCE to 600 BCE.
- The Indus Valley Civilization (3300 BCE - 1300 BCE) had well-planned cities, developed irrigation systems, trade links with Mesopotamia, seals, weights, and bricks. Major cities were Harappa and Mohenjo-Daro.
- The Vedic Period (1500 BCE - 600 BCE) saw the rise of Hinduism and the Vedas (Rig, Yajur, Sama, Atharva). The caste system emerged. Iron, cattle, and agriculture gained importance. New crafts and arts flourished. The Upanishads and Brahmanas were also composed.
- The Mauryan Empire (320 BCE - 180 BCE) under Chandragupta Maurya and Ashoka was the first pan-India empire. Ashoka promoted Buddhism and dhamma. Administration was efficient with officials, espionage, and welfare measures.
- The Gupta Empire (320 CE - 550 CE) under Chandragupta II was a Golden Age. There was a thriving trade, flourishing arts, Sanskrit literature, and advances in science and technology. The decimal number system and zero were invented. Universities like Nalanda became renowned centers of learning.
- Kingdoms like Chola, Chera, Pandya, Pallava, Chalukya, and others dominated southern India. Temple architecture and sculpture flourished. Trade with Southeast Asia, Arabia, and China thrived.

Some mnemonics and learning tricks:

- Indus Valley cities: Think of the first two letters of the city names - Ha(rappa) and Mo(henjo)
- Vedas: ‘Cow is mother, Rig knows, Yajur acts, Sama sings, Atharva fights off evil’
- Mauryan pillars: ‘Asoka’ (lion capital) and ‘Dhamma’ (wheel)
- Southern kingdoms beginning with C: ‘Chola Chera Pallava’ (sea battle); ‘Chalukya’ (badminton)
