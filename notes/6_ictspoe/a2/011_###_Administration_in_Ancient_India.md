### Administration in Ancient India

- The ancient Indian subcontinent was divided into many Janapadas or Mahajanapadas, comprising various rulers and dynasties. The powers of the rulers were not absolute and there were assemblies like Sabha and Samiti that acted as checks.
- The concept of Rajdharma or duty of the rulers emphasized their moral and ethical responsibilities towards the subjects. The Arthashastra by Kautilya laid down rules and principles of administering a state efficiently. Key points included:
- Maintaining law and order through a well-structured police system and judiciary.
- Efficient tax collection and revenue system through taxes like Bali, Bhaga, Shulka, etc.
- Strong intelligence and espionage systems to detect conspiracies.
- Having a strong army and military capability for defense and offense.
- Promoting trade, agriculture, mining, etc. to boost the economy.
- Building reserves of money and military resources for times of crisis.
- The Mauryan empire under Chandragupta Maurya and Ashoka had an efficient centralized bureaucracy which was administered through a hierarchy of officers and committees. Important officials included Tirthas, Rajukas, and Yuthas. The empire was divided into provinces, districts, and villages with proper record-keeping.
- Tax rates were fixed and taxes collected in cash as well as kind. Revenue from taxes was used in the welfare of subjects through initiatives like building rest houses, planting trees, and digging wells.
- Formalized laws were administered through an independent judiciary. The code of laws was Dharmashastra which laid down rules of just conduct and penalties for offenses. The concept of equality before law and presumption of innocence until proven guilty was present.
- The Gupta empire also had a systematic administration divided into provinces, districts, villages, and towns. The Samanta system of feudal lords under the Gupta emperors helped administer far-flung regions. Taxes, law and order, and welfare were areas of focus.
- Revenue was through taxes on agriculture, trade, and professions. Viharas or monasteries were patronized and educational institutes like Nalanda flourished. Hindu temples also received grants showing the mingling of politics and religion.
