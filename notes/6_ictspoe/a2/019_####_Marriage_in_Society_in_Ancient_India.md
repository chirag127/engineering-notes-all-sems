#### Marriage in Society in Ancient India

- Marriage was considered a sacrament and not a contract in Ancient Indian society. It was one of the most important samskaras or sacraments that every person had to undergo.
- Child marriages were prevalent and marriage was almost universally compulsory. Widow remarriage was prohibited.
- Marriage alliances were arranged by parents in consultation with astrologers. The compatibility of the couple was ensured through matching of horoscopes.
- Polygamy was permitted though monogamy was the norm for the common people. The kings and the wealthy merchants used to have many wives.
- According to the Dharmashastras, marriage was necessary for having progeny for continuation of family lineage and performance of sacred rites. For women, marriage was the only means for attaining a respectable position in society.
- Some of the marriage types mentioned in the Dharmashastras were: Brahma, Daiva, Arsha, Prajapatya and Gandharva marriages. These were based on the categories of women known as Brahmana, Daiva, Arsha, Prajapatya and Gandharva women.
- Dowry was an important aspect of marriages. The Dharmashastras lay down the rules regarding the amount of dowry. Remarriage of widows and divorce were not permitted under normal circumstances.
