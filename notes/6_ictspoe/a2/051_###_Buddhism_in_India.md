### Buddhism in India

- Buddhism was founded in ancient India by Siddhartha Gautama, who is known as the Buddha, around 500 BCE.
- The Buddha was an ascetic who meditated on the nature of life and taught the Four Noble Truths, the Eightfold Path, and the concepts of karma and rebirth.
- The major branches of Buddhism that developed in India were Theravada, Mahayana, and Vajrayana.
- Theravada Buddhism focused on becoming an arhat by following the teachings of the Buddha to achieve enlightenment and release from samsara. Key teachings include the Three Marks of Existence and the Four Noble Truths.
- Mahayana Buddhism emphasized the bodhisattva ideal, the idea that one should practice compassion and delay entering nirvana to help others achieve enlightenment. New teachings included the concept of sunyata, or emptiness, and new sacred texts like the Prajnaparamita Sutras. Mahayana schools like Madhyamaka focused on the nature of reality.
- Vajrayana Buddhism, also known as Tibetan or Tantric Buddhism, incorporated esoteric rituals and magic as a path to enlightenment. Vajrayana developed new concepts like the Mandala and new practices like deity yoga. Tantric teachings are secret and passed down through initiations from teacher to student.
- Buddhism spread along trade routes from India to many other Asian countries. The decline of Buddhism in India occurred due to a variety of political and cultural factors as well as the resurgence of Hinduism. Many Buddhists fled to other countries, and India eventually became predominantly Hindu.

Mnemonics:
- Theravada: Follow the Buddha's teachings to achieve arhatship
- Mahayana: Become a bodhisattva and achieve sunyata
- Vajrayana: Incorporate magic and rituals to achieve enlightenment

Learning tricks:
- Compare the Three Marks of Existence to a flower (anicca is impermanence, dukkha is suffering, anatta is non-self)
- Compare the Eightfold Path to an eight-spoked wheel
- Remember that Mahayana incorporates new teachings like sunyata and new texts like the Prajnaparamita Sutras
- Remember that Vajrayana incorporates esoteric rituals and practices like deity yoga and mandalas
