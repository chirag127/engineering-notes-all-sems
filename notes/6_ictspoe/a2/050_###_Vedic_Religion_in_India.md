### Vedic Religion in India

- The Vedic religion refers to the religion of the Indo-Aryans that existed in ancient India. The Vedas are the earliest surviving scriptures of Hinduism containing spiritual knowledge and ideas.
- The Vedic religion revolved around fire sacrifices and worship of deities like Indra (the sky god and king of the gods), Agni (the fire god), and Soma (the god of the sacred drink). There were prayers and hymns recited by priests during rituals for appeasing the deities.
- The later Vedic period saw the rise of the Varnashrama Dharma system of four social classes based on occupation - Brahmins (priests), Kshatriyas (warriors), Vaishyas (traders), and Shudras (laborers). The system was justified based on the Purusha Sukta hymn of the Rig Veda which described the four classes as coming from the body of the primordial Purusha or cosmic man.
- The Upanishads represented a shift from ritual sacrifice to spiritual knowledge and philosophical reflection. They explored the nature of the soul (Atman) and its relationship with the ultimate reality (Brahman). The ideas of karma, samsara, and moksha were also discussed in the Upanishads. These ideas were influential on later Indian philosophies like Hinduism, Buddhism, and Jainism.
- Some key terms to remember:
- Vedas: the earliest Hindu scriptures containing mantras and hymns
- Brahmins: the priestly class in the Varna system
- Atman: the eternal self or soul
- Brahman: the ultimate reality or cosmic consciousness
- Karma: the law of cause and effect by which good or bad actions determine future outcomes
- Samsara: the cycle of rebirth
- Moksha: liberation from samsara
