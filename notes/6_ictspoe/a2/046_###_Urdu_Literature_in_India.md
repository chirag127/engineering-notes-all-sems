### Urdu Literature in India

- Urdu literature flourished in northern India, especially Lucknow and Hyderabad.
- The Mughal court patronized Urdu literature. Many poets and scholars flocked to the Mughal court. Prominent poets during Mughal rule include Muhammad Quli Qutb Shah, Wali Deccani, and Nazeer Akbarabadi.
- After the decline of Mughal rule, Lucknow and Hyderabad emerged as major centers of Urdu literature. Notable literary figures of this era include Mirza Ghalib, Muhammad Iqbal, and Mir Taqi Mir.
- Mirza Ghalib is regarded as one of the most prominent Urdu poets. His ghazals exhibit a range of emotions and address philosophical ideas. His divan or collection of Urdu poetry is iconic.
- Muhammad Iqbal, regarded as the national poet of Pakistan, wrote philosophical poetry that revived the spirit of Islam, especially through his poems Javed Nama and Bal-e-Jibril. His poem Saare Jahan Se Achcha is a popular patriotic song.
- Some prominent themes in Urdu literature include:
- Ishq (divine love) and Sufism as elaborated by poets like Rumi and Hafez
- The decline of Mughal rule and nostalgia for the past
- Nationalism and patriotism
- The challenges faced by the common man
- Some learning tricks:
- Remember key phrases or couplets from famous ghazals or poems to understand themes and tone
- Note rhyme schemes and meter (beher) of ghazals to understand form and structure
- Read translations alongside original texts to grasp meaning
