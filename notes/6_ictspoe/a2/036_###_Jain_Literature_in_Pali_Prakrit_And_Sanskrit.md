### Jain Literature in Pali,Prakrit And Sanskrit

The Jain literature comprises of religious texts pertaining to Jainism written in various languages. The Jain Agamas which are the canonical texts of Jainism were composed in Ardhamagadhi Prakrit. Later Jain literature was written in Sanskrit, Tamil, Marathi, Hindi and other Indian languages. Some key works and their languages are:

-   Agamas: Composed in Ardhamagadhi Prakrit. The primary authoritative texts of Jainism containing the teachings of the Jina. There are 45 Agamas which include 12 Angas, 12 Upangas, 4 Mulasutras, 6 Chedasutras, 2 Pavittasutras, 1 Nandisutra and 7 Anuyogas.
-   Tamil Jain Literature: Composed in Tamil from 3rd century CE onwards. Includes texts such as Naladiyar, Parijatham, Thamizharasanam and Thirukkural (by Jain author Thiruvalluvar). Focus on Jain ethics, virtues, yoga and liberation.
-   Prakrta Kahaaprakaara: Composed by Aachaarya Amitagati in c. 10th century CE in Prakrit. A comprehensive treatise on the various forms of conditioned origination according to the Jain philosophy.
-   Tattvartha Sutra: Composed by Umaswati in c. 2nd century CE in Sanskrit. One of the most authoritative Jain philosophical texts which systematizes and condenses the key principles of Jainism. It has gained widespread acceptance among all sects of Jainism.
-   Merutunga Suri's Prabandha-Chintamani: Composed by Merutunga Suri in 1304 CE in Prakrit. Consists of myths, legends, narratives and a brief history of famous Jain personages. Provides an insight into the medieval Jain traditions and customs.

-   The contents of key texts through abbreviations (e.g. Tattvartha Sutra contains Tattva or truths and Sutra or aphorisms).
-   The authors and time periods through rhymes or songs.
