#### Theories of origin of State in India

The major theories explaining the origin of the State in India are:

1. Divine origin theory: According to this theory, the State originated from divine sanction. The King derived his authority from the deity and he was regarded as the representative of God on earth. For example, in the ancient Indian texts like the Arthashastra and the Manusmriti, the King is regarded as the vice-regent of God.

2. Social contract theory: This theory states that the State originated from an agreement or contract entered into by the people in the primitive society to establish law and order. The people in the society surrendered some of their rights to a sovereign authority in return for security and justice. For example, the Buddhist texts talk about a mutual contract between the King and his people.

3. Historical origin: According to this theory, the origin of the State can be traced to powerful individuals or groups who acquired control over territory. They laid claim over the allegiance of the people and established administration. For example, most of the ancient Indian States seem to have originated from conquest by warlords or tribal chiefs.

4. Evolutionary origin: This theory views the State as originating from the patriarchal family and developing into small villages and tribal forms. Gradually, the tribal chief acquired sovereign powers and the State evolved. For example, the Arthashastra talks about the origin of the State from the family through the village and the district.

Some key mnemonics to remember the theories:

1. Divine sanction: God appoints the King
2. Social contract: People's agreement leads to rule
3. Historical origin: Powerful rulers acquire control
4. Evolutionary origin: Family to tribe to village to State

The advantages and disadvantages of each theory and detailed examples can also be discussed to learn the topic comprehensively. The theories can be compared in terms of their applicability to explain the origin of the State in ancient India.
