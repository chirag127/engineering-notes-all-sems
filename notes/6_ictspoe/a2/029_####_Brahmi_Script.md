#### Brahmi Script

- Brahmi is one of the oldest writing systems of India, developed in the 1st millennium BCE.
- It was developed in the 6th century BCE by the grammarian Panini.
- Brahmi script was the main means of writing Sanskrit and Prakrit.
- It is regarded as the ancestor of all modern Indian scripts such as Devanagari, Bengali, Tamil, etc.

Some key points about Brahmi Script:

- It was deciphered by James Prinsep in 1837 with the help of Brahmi inscriptions on edicts of Emperor Ashoka.
- Brahmi is written from left to right in horizontal lines.
- It has vowels and consonants and uses diacritical marks to indicate pitch and vowel length.
- The script is partly alphabetic and partly syllabic.
- Mnemonics to remember the Brahmi letters:
    - a i u e o ā ī ū ē ō k kh g gh ṅ
    - c ch j jh ñ ṭ ṭh ḍ ḍh ṇ t th d dh n
    - p ph b bh m y r l v ś ṣ s h

Advantages of Brahmi script:
- It is simple and easy to learn.
- It represents vowels and consonants distinctly.
- It indicates the length of vowels and pitch of letters.

Disadvantages:
- It does not indicate the place of articulation of consonants precisely.
- The short vowels are not represented in writing.

Applications:
- It was used to write Sanskrit and Prakrit languages.
- The Brahmi script developed into many other scripts such as Devanagari, Bengali, Tamil, etc. which are used to write many languages today.
- Brahmi inscriptions provide important information about ancient Indian history, society, and culture.
