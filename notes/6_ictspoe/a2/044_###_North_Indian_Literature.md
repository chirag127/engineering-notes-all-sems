### North Indian Literature

- The North Indian literature flourished in various Indo-Aryan languages like Sanskrit, Prakrit, Apabhramsha, etc. The earliest literary compositions were in Sanskrit and Prakrit.
- Sanskrit literature includes Vedas, Upanishads, Ramayana, Mahabharata, Puranas, etc. The Vedas are the earliest compositions dating back to 1500 BCE. The Upanishads explore philosophical questions about life, death, the soul, etc. The Ramayana and Mahabharata are the greatest epics in world literature. The Puranas contain stories and legends.
- The Gupta period (4th-6th century CE) is considered the Golden Age of Sanskrit literature. Kalidasa's works like Abhijnanashakuntala and Kumarasambhava representing the peak of Sanskrit literary achievement. Other eminent poets were Bhasa, Bharavi, and Banabhatta.
- Prakrit literature flourished from 3rd century BCE to 5th century CE. The earliest available texts are the inscriptions of Ashoka's edicts. The Sattasai and Gaha Sattasai are collections of Prakrit poems. Hâla's Sattasai is considered a high point of Prakrit literature.
- Apabhramsha literature emerged from 5th to 13th century CE. The earlier works like the Apabhramsha grammarian's work focus on poetry while later works covered a wider range of subjects. Some well-known works are Vikramapurana, Vindhyeshwari, and Prithviraj Raso.
- Some notable works were composed in languages like Maithili, Bengali, Punjabi, Sindhi, etc. during the medieval period. Amir Khusrau wrote poetry in Persian and Hindavi. The Bhakti movement led to the flourishing of devotional literature in vernacular languages.
- Learning tricks:
- Remember Vedas -> Upanishads -> Epics (Ramayana, Mahabharata) -> Puranas for chronology of Sanskrit literature.
- Think of the Golden Age of a king (Gupta period) for remembering Kalidasa and other eminent Sanskrit poets.
- Think of 'falling down' for Apabhramsha literature which emerged after Sanskrit and Prakrit.
