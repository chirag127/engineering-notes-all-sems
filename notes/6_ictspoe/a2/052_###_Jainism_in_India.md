### Jainism in India

Jainism is an ancient Indian religion that emphasizes complete non-violence and austerity. Followers of Jainism are called Jains, and they believe that liberation from worldly affairs is achievable through an ascetic life.

Some key points about Jainism in India:

1. Jainism arose in ancient India around the 6th century BCE. It was founded by Vardhamana Mahavira, the 24th and last Tirthankara (enlightened teacher) in Jainism.
2. Jains believe that the path to liberation involves freeing one's soul from matter by attaining a state of perfect perception, knowledge, bliss, and liberation. This is achieved by following the three main principles of Jainism: non-violence, non-attachment, and non-absolutism.
3. The Jains have a strong emphasis on asceticism and strict ethical codes of conduct. Jain monks and nuns follow very strict ascetic practices, fasting long periods, and meditating intensively. The laity also follow ethical precepts such as vegetarianism and non-violence towards all living beings.
4. Jainism has had a significant influence on other Indian religions such as Hinduism and Buddhism. There are about 4 million Jains worldwide, with most living in India. The majority of Jains reside in the states of Maharashtra, Rajasthan, Gujarat and Karnataka.
5. The Jains have a rich cultural heritage, with many ancient temples, pilgrimage sites, sculptures, and texts. The Jain emblem is the Swastika, and their religious festivals include Paryushana and Das Lakshana. Jain cuisine is strictly vegetarian and excludes garlic and onions.

The key learning tricks to remember about Jainism are:
1) Founded by Mahavira, based on non-violence, non-attachment and non-absolutism
2) Strict ascetic practices and ethical codes for monks/nuns and laity
3) Influenced other Indian religions, 4 million followers mostly in western/southern India
4) Rich cultural heritage with ancient temples, sculptures, texts; vegetarian diet
