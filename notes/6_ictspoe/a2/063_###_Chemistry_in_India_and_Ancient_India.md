### Chemistry in India and Ancient India

- Ancient Indian philosophers and texts contained theories and concepts of chemistry as part of natural philosophy and metaphysics.
- The Vedas (c. 1500 BCE - 500 BCE), especially the Sutras and Brahmanas, contained early speculations about the origin of the universe and the nature of matter. The Vedas mention basic ideas of astronomy, chemistry, mathematics, and physics.
- The Sushruta Samhita (~500 BCE) classifies chemical processes and reactions, acids, and alkalis, and chemical changes. It contains a list of sediments and salts; and recipes for extraction and purification of salts.
- Around 400 BCE, the Vaisheshika school of Indian philosophy proposed the existence of atoms and molecules.
- The Charaka Samhita (~200 BCE) classifies chemicals into organic and inorganic, with the latter broken down further into salts, oxides, and alkalis. The text describes various chemical operations like distillation, sublimation, and calcination; and recipes using alkalis, metals, and salts.
- Ancient Indian alchemists attempted the artificial production of precious metals and stones using chemical and physical methods based on esoteric beliefs and philosophies. Key alchemical concepts and theories included Rasavada (the theory of mercury), Shodhana (purification), and Marana (killing a substance by isolating its essence).
- From 600 CE, innovations in metallurgy, chemical techniques, and alchemy spread from India to the Middle East and China. From 800 CE, Arabic translations spread Indian chemistry to the Western world.
