### Telugu Literature

Telugu literature refers to the literature in the Telugu language. Telugu literature was initially an oral tradition, imbibed from Sanskrit literature. Telugu literature flourished under royal patronage during the Vijayanagara Empire. By the 18th century, new literary forms became popular in Telugu literature, including literary prose; drama; and kāvya poetry. Telugu literature encompasses a wide range of subjects, including philosophy, mythology, epics, histories, novels, short stories, poetry, and plays.

Some key points about Telugu literature:

- It is one of the most prolific literatures amongst the Indian languages with a vast body of literature spanning multiple genres over a thousand years. Telugu literature has been described by scholars as full, fertile, and abundant.
- The earliest literature in Telugu was poetry based on metric principles laid down in Sanskrit treaties. 'Nannaya's Telugu translation of the Mahabharata in the 11th century marks the beginning of Telugu literature.
- The Vijayanagara era was the golden age of Telugu literature under the patronage of the Vijayanagara rulers. Krishna Deva Raya's Amuktamalyada is considered a poetic classic of this era.
- Post Vijayanagara, new literary forms including prose and drama emerged. Modern Telugu literature includes influential works such as Gurazada Apparao's Kanyasulkam.
- Some notable Telugu poets include Kandukuri Veeresalingam, Gurajada Apparao, Molla, Kaasi Viswanatha Sastry, and Sri Sri. Notable Telugu writers include Arudra, Viswanatha Satyanarayana, and Yandamuri Veerendranath.
