### Trade in Ancient India

- Trade and commerce was an important activity in Ancient India. There were trade routes connecting India to other ancient civilizations like Mesopotamia, Egypt, Greece, and Rome.
- The major trading centers were the ports of the Indus Valley civilization like Lothal, Dholavira, and Harappa. These ports traded with Mesopotamia and Egypt.
- Other important trading centers were Ujjain, Varanasi, Vaishali, and Rajagriha. These inland towns were located on trade routes and traded goods like spices, textiles, metals, precious stones, etc.
- The main goods exported from India were spices like black pepper, cardamom, cloves, etc.; cotton and silk textiles; indigo dye; medicinal drugs; precious stones; and metals like iron and steel.
- The main imports were gold, silver, olive oil, wine, etc. from Europe and the Mediterranean region. Horses were imported from Central Asia.
- Internal trade within India was also prevalent. Goods were transported via roads and rivers. There were markets and bazaars in towns where goods were bought and sold.
- The trade was regulated through trade guilds and taxes were collected on the transport and sale of goods. Coins of metals like copper, silver, and gold facilitated monetary transactions.
- Some Mnemonics to remember - 'SPICES' to remember major exports; 'GOLD & STEED' to remember major imports (Gold, Silver, Horses). The inland river routes and trade centers can be remembered through a route map diagram.
