### Fairs and Festivals as Cultural Heritage of India

- India is a land of fairs and festivals. Fairs and festivals are an integral part of India's cultural heritage. They reflect the country's rich cultural diversity as well as religious and seasonal variations.
- Some popular religious festivals in India are:
- Diwali - Festival of Lights, celebrated by Hindus, Sikhs and Jains. Symbolizes the victory of light over darkness.
- Dussehra - Celebrates the victory of good over evil. Effigies of Ravana are burnt.
- Holi - Festival of Colors, celebrated as a spring festival. People play with colors and water.
- Eid-ul-Fitr - Marks the end of Ramadan month of fasting for Muslims.
- Christmas - Celebrated by Christians to mark the birth of Jesus Christ.
- Some popular harvest festivals are:
- Onam - Celebrated in Kerala to mark the homecoming of the legendary king Mahabali.
- Pongal - Celebrated in Tamil Nadu to give thanks for a successful harvest.
- Lohri - Celebrated in Punjab, people gather around bonfires, dance and eat sweets and rice.
- Some noted cultural fairs and festivals are:
- Khajuraho Dance Festival - Showcases various Indian classical dance forms.
- Taj Mahotsav - Celebrated near Taj Mahal in Agra, highlights culture of Braj region.
- Pushkar Camel Fair - One of the largest camel fairs in the world. Apart from camel trading, it has cultural programs and competitions.

Mnemonics and learning tricks:
- Remember acronym 'DDEF' for major religious festivals - Diwali, Dussehra, Eid, Christmas
- 'HOP' for harvest festivals - Holi, Onam, Pongal

Advantages:
- Preserve cultural heritage and traditions.
- Promote social bonding and unity.
- Boost tourism and economy.

Disadvantages:
- Can lead to wastage of resources and money.
- Can disrupt routine life and productivity.
- Some festivals promote gambling and alcohol consumption.
