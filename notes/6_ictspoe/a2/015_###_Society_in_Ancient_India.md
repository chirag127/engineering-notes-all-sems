### Society in Ancient India

- The ancient Indian society was divided into rigid social classes based on occupation and ethnicity. The Brahmins or priests were the highest ranking followed by Kshatriyas or warriors, Vaishyas or traders, and Shudras or laborers. 

- Outside the caste system were the Untouchables who performed menial tasks and lived in segregated communities. The rigid caste system determined a person's occupation, associations, and even marriage partners. 

- The family was the nucleus of ancient Indian society. People lived in extended families in the same household. Patriarchy and patrilineal customs were common with the eldest male as the head of the family. 

- Women had restricted rights and were expected to be devoted to their families and husbands. However, women had more freedom in ancient India compared to other ancient societies. They could inherit property, divorce their husbands, and conduct business. 

- Mnemonics to remember the social hierarchy: "Brahmins at the Top, Warriors Next, Then Traders and Lastly Labourers". The Untouchables were outside the caste system. 

- The paternal extended family and caste were the bases of social identity. Rigid rules governed most aspects of daily life including diet, occupation, and associations. However, women had more rights than in other comparable societies of the time.
