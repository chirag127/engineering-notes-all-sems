### Hindi Literature

- The Hindi literary tradition is one of the richest in the world. It spans more than a thousand years of continuous literary activity.
- The earliest works in Hindi were composed in dialects such as Apabhramsha and Braj Bhasha. The most famous Apabhramsha work is the Sindhi epic poem Kalhana's Rajatarangini or 'River of Kings' written in the 12th century.
- The classical period of Hindi literature began with the Muslim influence and Persian literary traditions in the 13th century. The influence of Sanskrit poetics stimulated new forms such as the religious epic the Ramacharitamanasa by Tulsidas in 1575 and the Rasikapriya, a pioneering work on erotics.
- The Bhakti movement from the 15th to 17th centuries produced a magnificent literature that fused devotional intensity with sensuous love of God. Key figures include Mirabai, Surdas, and Kabir.
- During the British colonial period from the mid-19th century, Hindi literature flourished in eloquent nationalism. Notable novelists include Premchand who pioneered the Urdu-Hindi novel, while poets such as Maithili Sharan Gupt, Ramdhari Singh 'Dinkar' and Mahadevi Varma invigorated Hindi poetry. Postcolonial writing has been enriched by feminist and Dalit perspectives.

Memorization techniques:
- Note the key time periods: Apabhramsha period, classical period under Persian/Sanskrit influence, Bhakti movement period, colonial/nationalist period. These provide a chronological structure to map the development of Hindi literature.
- Note key genres: epic poetry (Rajatarangini, Ramacharitamanasa), erotic poetry (Rasikapriya), devotional poetry (Mirabai, Kabir, Surdas), novels (Premchand), postcolonial perspectives. This categorization helps distinguish major forms and themes.
- Note the fusion of influences: Apabhramsha dialects, Persian/Islamic, Sanskrit poetics and aesthetics. This demonstrates how Hindi literature has absorbed and transformed diverse traditions.
