### Harappan Technologies in India and Ancient India

- The Harappan civilization flourished around 2600 BCE to 1900 BCE in the Indus Valley region. They were technologically advanced and had developed many technologies:
- Brick-making: They made bricks of a standard size using a ratio of mud and straw. These bricks were used to build houses, drainage systems, and other structures. Mnemonic: Think of the three little pigs and their houses of straw and bricks. The Harappans too made bricks but of a standardized size.
- Planned cities with drainage systems: The Harappan cities were well-planned with straight roads, houses with separate bathrooms, and covered drainages. They had a efficient system for disposal of sullage and sewage.
- Bead-making: They made beautiful beads of stones like carnelian, lapis lazuli, and steatite which were traded with other civilizations. Mnemonic: Think of a necklace with colorful beads. The Harappans too made colorful beads of stones and traded them.
- Pottery: They made pots and jars of excellent quality, some with black and red slip and baked in kilns. The pottery was wheel-made and of a standardized size.
- Seals: The Harappans made stone seals with animal motifs and script. These seals were possibly used for trade and official purposes.
- Cotton cultivation and textile production: They cultivated cotton and produced cotton textiles. Spindles and needles have been found during excavations.
- Metal technology: They knew the technique of bronze metallurgy and produced objects of copper, bronze, and tin.
- Trade: They had a flourishing trade with other civilizations in West Asia and Central Asia. The archaeological finds of lapis lazuli from Afghanistan and sea shells from the Arabian Sea coast are evidence of this.

The above points cover the major technological achievements of the Harappan civilization. The technologies show their advancement, efficiency, and standardization.
