#### Slavery in Society in Ancient India

- Slavery was prevalent in ancient Indian society and there were several ways in which people could become slaves:
- Prisoners of war were enslaved, and debtors unable to repay loans could also become slaves to creditors.
- The caste system determined occupations, and lower-caste individuals had limited opportunities, frequently leading to debt bondage.
- Children of slaves were also considered slaves. Manusmriti and other dharmasutras prescribed their duties and limited rights.
- However, ancient Indian society afforded more opportunities for slaves to earn freedom than did other societies of the time. If they converted to their owners' religion or exhibited good behavior, they could be emancipated. The king could also free slaves as an act of mercy on auspicious days.
- The rights and treatment of slaves varied based on caste and circumstances. Higher-caste slaves had greater privileges and more opportunities for freedom. The majority of slaves were drawn from tribal populations outside the caste system and faced poorer treatment.
- Slavery declined starting in the 7th-8th centuries, largely due to the spread of more egalitarian belief systems like Buddhism and Jainism, as well as the disruptions of foreign invasions. By around 1200 CE, slavery had largely disappeared from the major kingdoms of India.
