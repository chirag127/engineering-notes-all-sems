## Module 3- Indian Religion, Philosophy, and Practices

- Hinduism: Oldest religion of India. Follows concepts of rebirth, karma, and moksha. Lord Brahma, Vishnu and Shiva are main gods. Mahabharata and Ramayana are two major epics. Vedas and Upanishads are sacred texts. Yoga and meditation are core practices.
- Buddhism: Founded by Gautama Buddha. Teaches liberation from suffering by detaching from desires and following eightfold path. No concept of God. Four Noble Truths and Three Marks of Existence are core beliefs. Meditation and Nirvana are ultimate goals.
- Jainism: Founded by Mahavira. Based on principles of non-violence, non-absolute truth, and liberation of soul. Follows strict ethical code of conduct and austerity. Rejects the idea of a creator God. The goal is to attain enlightenment by ridding of karma through strict self-discipline.
- Sikhism: Founded by Guru Nanak. Monotheistic religion which rejects caste system and idolatry. Teaches devotion and remembrance of the name of the creator God. The Guru Granth Sahib is the holy text. The five Ks are five articles of faith. Community service and meditation are core practices.

Mnemonics:
- For Hinduism: Brahma, Vishnu, Shiva (Creator, Preserver, Destroyer)
- For Buddhism: Four Noble Truths (Life is suffering, Cause of suffering, End of suffering, Eightfold path)
- For Jainism: Three Jewels (Right perception, Right knowledge, Right conduct)
- For Sikhism: Five Ks (Five articles of faith starting with K)
