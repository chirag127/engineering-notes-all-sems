### Persian Literature in India

- Persian literature flourished in India during the rule of Muslim dynasties like the Delhi Sultanate and the Mughal Empire. Many Persian poets, writers and scholars migrated to India and contributed to Indo-Persian literature.
- Some important Persian poets who wrote in India include:
-- Amir Khusrau (1253-1325 CE): Known as the "Parrot of India", he mixed Persian, Arabic and Indian styles in his poetry. He is credited with pioneering the Indian musical forms of khayal and tarana. His poems include Ghurrat al-Kamal and Khamsa-e-Nizami.
-- Malik Muhammad Jayasi (1477-1542 CE): He wrote the famous Awadhi poem Padmavat which blends history, fiction and fantasy. It tells the story of Princess Padmavati of Singhala, her husband Ratansen and the attack by Alauddin Khilji.
-- Mirza Ghalib (1797-1869 CE): He is regarded as one of the most famous and influential poets of the Urdu, Persian and Hindi literary traditions. His poems include divans and ghazals like Aah ko chahiye, Umr kahan hai and Yaran di tolian.

- Some other significant works of Indo-Persian literature include: Babur-Nama (memoirs of Mughal emperor Babur), Darya-e-Latafat (Persian dictionary by Ali Sher Khan), Gulzar-e-Abrar (Sufi mystical poems), Shahnamah-e-Hind (epic by Firdausi), Zafarnama (chronicle of Timur's campaigns by Nizam al-Din Shami).
