### Modern religious practices in India

* Hinduism: Hinduism is the dominant religion of India. Some modern practices in Hinduism include:
- Celebration of festivals like Diwali, Holi, Ganesh Chaturthi, etc. with grandeur and fervor. These festivals are celebrated by worshipping deities, lighting up houses and streets, and distributing sweets.
- Performance of religious rituals like yajnas, havans, etc. These rituals are performed by priests chanting mantras and making offerings to the sacred fire for peace and prosperity.
- Visiting temples and offering prayers to the idols of deities. Devotees queue up to get a glimpse of the deity and offer flowers, coconut, incense sticks, etc.
- Going on pilgrimages to sacred destinations like Varanasi, Haridwar, Rishikesh, etc. which are considered holy cities.
- Chanting of mantras and practicing yoga and meditation as a part of spiritual development.

* Islam: Some modern practices in Islam include:
- Offering namaz or prayers five times a day facing towards Mecca. Devotees gather at mosques to offer Friday prayers or Jumma namaz.
- Following rituals during celebrations of Eid-al-Fitr and Eid-al-Adha like giving alms to the poor, sacrificing animals, and hosting feasts with friends and family.
- Fasting during the holy month of Ramadan from dawn to dusk. At dusk, the fast is broken with an evening meal called Iftar.
- Going on Hajj pilgrimage to Mecca, Saudi Arabia at least once in their lifetime by Muslims who can afford it.

* Christianity: Some modern practices in Christianity include:
- Attending church services on Sundays where hymns are sung, Bible is read, and sermons are delivered. Christmas and Easter are celebrated with special church services.
- Baptism of newborn infants and converts to symbolize purification from sin.
- Taking part in activities like Bible study groups, community outreach, etc. organized by the Church.
- Following practices during Lent like fasting, abstinence from certain foods, and holding prayer services. The 40-day period of Lent culminates in Easter celebrations.
