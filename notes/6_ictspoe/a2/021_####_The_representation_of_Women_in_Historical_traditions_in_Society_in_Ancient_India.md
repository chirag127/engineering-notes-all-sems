#### The representation of Women in Historical traditions in Society in Ancient India

- In ancient Indian society, women were given a respectable position. They were considered as equal partners to men in family and social affairs. However, their roles and positions varied in different regions and communities of ancient India.
- In the Vedic period, women enjoyed a respectable position in the society. They participated in social gatherings and public sacrificial rituals. They composed hymns and were also teachers. However, their primary duty was seen as child-bearing and household management.
- The Epics Ramayana and Mahabharata portray women in both positive and stereotypical ways. Goddesses like Sita and Draupadi are icons of wifely devotion, while other female characters exhibit traits such as passion, anger and independence.
- The Dharmashastras contain contradictory statements regarding women. Some verses praise women while others emphasize on their subordination to men and limit their freedom. The Manusmriti specifies the roles and duties of women which are secondary to men. It sanctions strict control of women's sexuality and mobility.
- In Southern India, women from elite households were educated and composed poetry in Tamil. The devadasis or temple dancers occupied a distinct position in the society. In some communities, the inheritance of property by women was recognized. However, the overarching patriarchal system still limited women's freedom.
- Thus, while ancient Indian traditions reveal a complex perception of women with both positive and negative elements, the patriarchal system resulted in the subordination of women and their secondary status in the society. Their representations were tied to their traditional roles as wives, mothers and homemakers.
