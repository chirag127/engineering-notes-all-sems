#### Purusārtha in Society in Ancient India

The ancient Indian society emphasized on four main goals of human life known as Purusārtha:

1. Dharma: Performing one's duty ethically, morality and righteousness. Following the order and harmony of the universe.
Mnemonic: Dharmo Rakshati Rakshita (Dharma protects those who follow it)

2. Artha: Material prosperity, wealth and power. Economic development and political administration.
Mnemonic: Artha is required to sustain life (easy to remember as artha refers to material means required to sustain)

3. Kāma: Fulfillment of desires and pleasure. Enjoyment of aesthetic and sensual pleasures.
Mnemonic: Kāma comes from the heart (as desires and emotions come from the heart)

4. Moksha: Liberation, freedom and self-realization. Release from the cycle of birth and death. Achieving unity with the Brahman (Supreme Reality).
Mnemonic: Moksha leads to immortality (as it leads to liberation from mortal life)

The Purusārtha depicts a holistic way of life where equal importance is given to morality (dharma), wealth (artha) and pleasure (kāma) along with the ultimate goal of liberation (moksha). It reflects the pluralistic nature of the ancient Indian society which valued both the material and spiritual aspects of life. The Purusārtha continues to be relevant in the present times as an guiding principle for sustainable living and well-being.
