### The Upanishads

The Upanishads are a collection of ancient Indian texts which contain some of the central philosophical concepts and ideas of Hinduism, such as:

- Brahman: The Upanishads describe Brahman as the eternal, infinite, omnipresent, omniscient, omnipotent foundation of all of reality. Brahman is the ineffable, transcendent power beyond all else.
- Atman: The Upanishads state that our true self or essence is the eternal, infinite spirit known as Atman, which is one with Brahman. The goal of Upanishadic philosophy is to realize that Atman (individual self) and Brahman (ultimate reality) are one. This is known as moksha or liberation.
- Samsara and Karma: The Upanishads recognize samsara, the cycle of death and rebirth, determined by one's karma or actions and intentions. Release from samsara comes from the realization of the identity of Atman and Brahman.
- Meditation and Yoga: To achieve liberation, the Upanishads prescribe meditation, self-knowledge, and yoga as the means to realize the identity of Atman and Brahman.

Some useful mnemonics for remembering the major Upanishads and their primary themes:

- Isha, Kena, Katha: Deal with the nature of Brahman and the path to knowledge of Brahman.
- Prasna, Mundaka: Focus on meditation and yoga as paths to realizing Brahman.
- Mandukya: Focuses on the four states of consciousness and the nature of the Atman.
- Taittiriya: Focuses on the relationship between the individual, the cosmic order, and Brahman.

The Upanishads contain some of the most concise yet profound summaries of the core beliefs of Hinduism. They have had a massive influence on Indian philosophy, as well as on the subsequent development of philosophy throughout the world.
