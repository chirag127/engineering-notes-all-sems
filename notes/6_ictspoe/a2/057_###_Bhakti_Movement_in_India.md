### Bhakti Movement in India

The Bhakti Movement was a revivalist movement in medieval India that emphasized religious devotion and worship of a personal God, instead of indulging in ritual sacrifices and worship of deities. It originated in South India in the 7th-8th century CE and spread to North India in the 15th-16th century CE.

Some key points about the Bhakti Movement:

- It was initiated by saints from varied social backgrounds like cobblers, weavers, peasants, etc. who expressed their devotion to God in vernacular languages, instead of Sanskrit. For example, Namdev was a tailor, Kabir was a weaver, Mirabai was a princess, etc.
- The saints rejected the caste system and the dominance of Brahmins as religious intermediaries. They promoted the path of devotion and direct worship of a personal God, without ritual sacrifices or priests.
- The Movement was centered around the idea that true liberation can only be attained by unconditional, selfless devotion and constant remembrance of God. Singing, dancing, and other forms of worship were popular ways to express affection for God.
- Key principles of the Bhakti saints included religious tolerance, equality, and brotherhood of all humankind. Their teachings had a lasting impact on Indian society and the status of women also improved in some Bhakti communities.
- Popular deities of worship were Vishnu and Shiva. The verses and hymns of the Bhakti saints had a profound influence on Indian music, poetry, and literature. For example, the poems of Mirabai and Surdas are still widely read today.

Some Mnemonics/learning tricks:

- Remember the Bhakti saints and their devotional expressions through catchy songs or poems. For example, "Saint Kabir das, weaver by caste, in Benaras made his abode, Guru Nanak's Japu, And Mira's soulful ode"
- Think of the Bhakti Movement as the "people's movement" where devotion was expressed in vernacular languages rather than Sanskrit, and people from all social backgrounds were welcomed to pursue the path of devotion. This made it more accessible to the common folk.
- The key principles of Bhakti like religious tolerance, equality, and brotherhood can be remembered through the phrase "Love for one, and one for all". The Bhakti saints promoted seeing the same God in everyone and universal brotherhood.
