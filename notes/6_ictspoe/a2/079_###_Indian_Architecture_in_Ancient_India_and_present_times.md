### Indian Architecture in Ancient India and present times

- Ancient Indian architecture can be traced back to the Indus Valley civilization with planned cities and dockyards. The Buddhist stupas at Sanchi and rock-cut temples at Ajanta Caves and Ellora Caves are excellent examples of ancient Indian architecture.
- The Mauryan period saw the building of the Grand Trunk Road and the monumental palace-city of Pataliputra with massive wooden structures. The highlight was the edict pillars of Ashoka with fine carvings and inscriptions.
- The Gupta period produced the magnificent temples of Khajuraho, Modhera Sun Temple, and the rock-cut Udayagiri and Khandagiri Caves. The temples exhibit intricate carvings and sculptural detailing.
- The Islamic architecture reached its zenith with the Mughal period producing grand monumental buildings like the Taj Mahal, Red Fort, Fatehpur Sikri, and the Buland Darwaza. These buildings display a blend of Persian, Indian and Islamic styles with domes, arches, gardens and intricate carvings.
- The Vijayanagara and Hoysala empires in southern India produced splendid temple architecture at Hampi and Belur-Halebidu with dramatic sculptural detailing. The gopurams or entrance towers of the temples are impressive examples of Dravidian architecture.
- The modern architecture in India imbibes diverse regional influences along with contemporary styles. Notable examples are Chandigarh city designed by Le Corbusier, Lotus Temple in Delhi, Venkataraman Temple in Delhi, and Hiranandani complex in Mumbai.

Here are some learning tricks to remember:
- Indus Valley civilization: planned cities, dockyards
- Buddhist: stupas, rock-cut caves
- Mauryan: edict pillars of Ashoka, massive wooden structures
- Gupta: Khajuraho temples, intricate carvings
- Mughal: Taj Mahal, domes, arches, gardens, Fatehpur Sikri
- Vijayanagara: Hampi temples, gopurams or entrance towers
- Modern: Chandigarh city, Lotus Temple, regional influences
