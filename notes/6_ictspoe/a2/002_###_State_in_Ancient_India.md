### State in Ancient India

The ancient Indian state evolved from small tribal chiefdoms into large territorial monarchies between c. 600 BCE to 300 CE. Some key points about the evolution of the state in ancient India are:

- The rise of large territorial states: After 600 BCE, small tribal kingdoms started consolidating into large territorial states like that of Magadha, Kosala, Kalinga, etc. These large states were ruled by monarchs with centralized administrations and they levied taxes on people and resources within their territories.

- Growth of cities: Large states led to urbanization as capitals and trading centres grew into large cities. For e.g. Pataliputra was the capital of Magadha and Varanasi was an important trading centre. Cities became centres of craft specialization and trade.

- Development of bureaucracy: Large states required elaborate bureaucratic machinery to administer different areas and monitor taxation, law and order, etc. The Mauryan empire had an efficient bureaucracy with officers at various levels to administer the vast empire.

- Emergence of new political ideas: The growth of states exposed people to new political realities and led to new political ideas like the legitimacy of the state based on dharma ( duty ) rather than divine right, limits on the powers of rulers, etc. These were reflected in texts like the Arthashastra and dharmashastras.

Some Mnemonics for remembering key points:

- **C.O.P.S**.: Consolidation into large states, growth of cities, development of bureaucracy, new political ideas.
- **3 Ts of states**: Territories, Taxes, Centralized administration.

The evolution of the ancient Indian state led to diverse political experiments and also contributed to growth of urbanism and spread of new political ideas in the subcontinent.
