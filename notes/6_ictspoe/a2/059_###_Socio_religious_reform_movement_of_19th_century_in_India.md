### Socio religious reform movement of 19th century in India

- The socio religious reform movement arose in the 19th century in India as a response to the stagnation and blind beliefs that had crept into Indian society.
- The reformers criticized irrational customs, outdated practices and rigid social hierarchies based on caste that were prevalent.
- Some of the prominent reformers and their contributions:

* Raja Ram Mohan Roy (1772-1833): Founded Brahmo Samaj in 1828 which emphasized on monotheism and rejected idolatry. He fought against Sati system and child marriage.
* Ishwar Chandra Vidyasagar (1820-1891): campaigned for widow remarriage and worked for spreading women's education. He simplified and modernized Bengali language.
* Swami Dayananda Saraswati (1824-1883): Established Arya Samaj in 1875, emphasized on the infallibility of Vedas. He criticized the caste system and idolatry. He promoted equality, education and Swadeshi.
* Maharshi Karve (1858-1962): Championed widow remarriage and women's education. He started a widow's home in 1916 and worked for women's rights and removing oppression faced by them.

- The socio religious movements aimed to remove social evils, promote modern and scientific outlook and make Hinduism dynamic in consonance with the modern values of equality, justice and tolerance. They played an important role in generating awareness and awakening the Indian society.

- Mnemonics: Roy-Vidyasagar-Dayananda-Karve (to remember the reformers in sequence).
- The reform movements made Indian society ready for the upcoming nationalist movement by addressing social problems and inculcating a modern outlook.
