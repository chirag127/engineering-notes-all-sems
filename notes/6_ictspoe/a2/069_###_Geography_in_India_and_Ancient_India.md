### Geography in India and Ancient India

- The Indian subcontinent is separated from the rest of Asia by the Himalayas in the north, the Hindu Kush Mountains in the northwest and the Arakan Yoma mountains in the east. These geographical features isolated India and allowed a distinctive culture to develop.
- The Indus Valley Civilization (3300 BCE - 1300 BCE) was located in the northwestern region of the Indian subcontinent, in modern-day Pakistan. It was situated between two rivers - the Indus River and the Ghaggar-Hakra River. The major cities were Harappa and Mohenjo-Daro, which had a planned urban layout with structures made of baked bricks, as well as underground drainage system and grain storage silos.
- The Vedic period (1500 BCE - 600 BCE) saw the rise of the Vedic religion and culture in the Indian subcontinent. The Vedas were composed and compiled. The early Vedic society centered in Punjab and the Doab, shifting eastward during the later Vedic period. The Janapadas were the main political units during this period that the Mahajanapadas succeeded.

Advantages: Proximity to the Himalayas provided timber, medicinal plants and protection. Rivers like Indus provided water and fertility to the land allowing agriculture to flourish. The isolated geography allowed a unique culture to develop in ancient India.
Disadvantages: The isolation also meant India was protected from outside influences, both positive and negative. It made India vulnerable to invasions through mountain passes by foreigners.

Examples and applications: The discovery of the IVC sites gives insights into the urban planning and infrastructure of ancient Indian cities. The Vedic texts are the earliest writings in Indic languages and are sources of history, culture and philosophy of the Vedic period. They continue to influence Indian religions and culture today.
