### Kannada Literature

- Kannada literature refers to the literature composed in the Kannada language, encompassing over a thousand years of writings. The earliest literary works in Kannada are the Kadambari by Banabhatta and other poets of the 5th-7th centuries CE.
- The Golden age of Kannada literature occurred during the Vijayanagara Empire and Kannada language writers, along with Telugu and Sanskrit writers, produced classics in literature under the patronage of the Vijayanagara Empire.
- Notable literary figures of the Vijayanagara period include Kumara Vyasa, the renowned Kannada language translation of the epic Mahabharata, and the poet Lakshmisa.
- The medieval period saw Gajasimha, Ranna and Pampa Mahakavi developed the harmony between indigenous and Sanskrit poetry traditions. Ranna's Kannada-language version of the ancient Indian epic Ramayana, called the Adbhuta Ramayana, is particularly noteworthy.
- Do Consecration of 35 temples in the Dravidian style of architecture is an unrivaled historical record built by the Western Chalukya dynasty and Badami Chalukya clan from the sixth to the twelfth century CE.
- Kanaka Dasa (1509–1609) was a philosopher, mystic, composer and musician and is widely regarded as one of the most prominent composers of Haridasa Kannada literature. Purandara Dasa (1484–1564) is considered the "Kannada Pitamaha" (father of Kannada literature) for his contributions to Kannada music.
- The Vijayanagara ruler Krishnadevaraya (1509–1529), himself a Kannada writer, patronized Kannada literature. The period between the 15th and 19th centuries saw the flourishing of several genres such as luxurious poetry, biography, panchadashi metre, devotional literature and erotica.
- Modern Kannada literature begins with the Kannada–English Dictionary written by Rev. Ferdinand Kittel in 1894.
