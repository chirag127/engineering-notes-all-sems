### Seals as Cultural Heritage of India

Seals are engraved stamps made of metal, clay or ivory which were used to stamp clay tags attached to goods and merchandise. They served as a hallmark of authentication and identification. The ancient Indian seals provide valuable insights into the political, economic and cultural fabric of the Indus Valley Civilisation and other subsequent cultures:

- The Indus valley seals were generally square or rectangular in shape with figures of animals such as the humped bull, unicorn, tiger and elephant engraved on them. The seals indicate the people's familiarity with local fauna and their significance in trade and religious practices. The seals also had the Indus script etched on them which is yet to be deciphered.
- The terracotta seals found at sites associated with the Black and Red Ware culture depict motifs indicative of rural life such as cattle, plough, trees etc. They provide clues regarding agrarian practices and the use of seals for marking ownership of agricultural produce.
- The rouletted ware seals from sites associated with the Painted Grey Ware culture feature geometric motifs and designs. The rouletting technique involves impressing patterns using a cogwheel. These seals shed light on changing artistic traditions and new techniques adopted by craftsmen.
- The seals found in various post-Harappan sites reflect a continuity of the sealing tradition as well as new influences. The seals exhibit diversification in their shapes and motifs including religious symbols, Devanagari scripts, riders on horses etc. These show the assimilation of new ideas through interactions with other contemporary cultures.

Advantages of studying seals:
- They are one of the most common artefacts found at archaeological sites, so studying them provides a holistic understanding of the associated culture.
- They reflect the artistic sensibilities, technological skills and economic systems of the people.
- They can contain inscriptions or symbols which help in deciphering scripts and languages.
- They provide evidence for long distance trade, cultural exchanges and the spread of ideas between contemporary civilisations.

Thus, seals are an important cultural heritage of India as they encapsulate the legacy of the diverse array of civilisations that flourished in the region. A systematic study of seals can unlock valuable insights into India's ancient past.
