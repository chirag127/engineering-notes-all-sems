#### Force Theory of origin of State

According to the Force Theory, the State came into existence as a result of the superiority of some individuals or a group over others. The dominant person or group established their control over the others through the use of physical force. The State thus originated from the exertion of force by the more powerful individuals or groups.

- The strong dominated the weak: According to this theory, in the primitive society the physically strong imposed their will on the weak. The chieftains and tribal leaders emerged as the most powerful persons who established their control over others through force and coercion. This resulted in the origin of the State.
- The theory emphasizes 'might is right': The theory emphasizes that the State originated from the principle of 'might is right'. The emergence of the State was the result of the domination of the stronger and more powerful persons or groups over the weaker ones. The supremacy of force determined the structure of the primitive society which later evolved into the State.
- Criticism: The theory is one-sided as it ignores the role of other factors in the origin of the State. It takes force as the only factor responsible for the rise of the State. The theory is thus inadequate and incomplete. The mutual cooperation and social contracts also played an important role in the development of the State. The theory presents a primitive picture of the society and the origin of the State.

Mnemonics:
MIGHT - Might Is Right (according to the force theory)

Advantages: The theory emphasizes the role of powerful individuals and groups in the origin of the State. It shows how physical force led to the domination of some over others resulting in the rise of the State.

Disadvantages: The theory is one-sided and incomplete. It ignores the role of other social, economic and political factors in the development of the State. It presents a primitive view of society.
