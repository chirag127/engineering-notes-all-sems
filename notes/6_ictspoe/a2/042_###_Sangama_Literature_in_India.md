### Sangama Literature in India

- Sangama literature refers to the corpus of Tamil poetry composed by elite poets and poetesses from southern India during the Sangam period from roughly the 2nd century BCE to the 4th century CE.
- The Sangam literature collection includes 2381 poems composed by 473 poets, including many works by anonymous authors.
- The poems were composed in the genre of the akam (love) and the puram (heroic) poetry traditions.
- The akam tradition focuses on personal or interior experiences including love and emotions. The puram tradition focuses on external experiences or heroic deeds including war.
- Some examples of Sangam literature works include:

- Tolkāppiyam - a treatise on grammar and poetics meant to establish standards for Tamil literary expression.
- Naṟṟiṇai - an anthology of 400 love poems following the akam tradition.
- Kuṟiñcippāṭṭu - an anthology of short heroic poems following the puram tradition describing scenes of battle and victory.
- Pattuppāṭṭu - an anthology of 10 long songs in the puram tradition celebrating heroic deeds and battles.

Advantages:
- Provides insight into ancient Tamil cultural and social life.
- Illuminates the poetic conventions and literary practices of the Sangam period.
- Demonstrates the sophisticated poetic traditions of classical Tamil.

Disadvantages:
- Difficult to interpret due to lack of context for many references and allusions.
- Challenge to translate from classical Tamil to modern languages while retaining poetic effect.

Mnemonics:
- Akam: Personal, interior = A(personal)k(interior)am
- Puram: External, heroic = P(external)ur(heroic)am
