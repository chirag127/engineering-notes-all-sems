### Theatre as Cultural Heritage of India

Theatre has been an integral part of Indian culture since ancient times. Indian theatre is a complex amalgam of various art forms, with roots in folk performance as well as classical Indian dance and music. The major forms of Indian theatre are:

1. Natya: Classical Sanskrit theatre based on Natyashastra and featuring fine arts like music, dance and poetry. Styles like Bharatanatyam and Kathakali originated from Natya.
2. Rangaprabhat: Devotional plays depicting stories of Hindu deities, performed in temples.
3. Yakshagana: Folk theatre from Karnataka combining dance, music, dialogue and costume. Stories from Hindu epics and Puranas are enacted.
4. Nautanki: Popular folk theatre from Uttar Pradesh and Bihar. Lavish costumes and exaggerated gestures and expressions are used.
5. Tamasha: Folk theatre from Maharashtra with singing and dancing, using instruments like dholki and harmonium. Satirical and comical performances on contemporary issues.

Mnemonics:
- Natya: Classical, based on Natyashastra. Styles like Bharatanatyam originated from it.
- Rangaprabhat: Devotional plays in temples.
- Yakshagana: Folk, from Karnataka. Dance, music and stories from Hindu epics.
- Nautanki: Folk, from UP and Bihar. Lavish and exaggerated.
- Tamasha: Folk, from Maharashtra. Singing, dancing and satire on contemporary issues.

Advantages: Preserves Indian art forms and cultural heritage. Provides entertainment and awareness.
Disadvantages: Some styles like Nautanki have become commercialised and lost authenticity.
