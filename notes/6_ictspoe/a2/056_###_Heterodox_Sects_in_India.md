### Heterodox Sects in India

The heterodox sects or nāstika schools of Indian philosophy rejected the authority of the Vedas and emphasized rationality and logic over revelation. The major heterodox schools are:

-   Cārvāka: Also known as Lokāyata, it was a materialist school that rejected the existence of the soul and an afterlife. It stated that perception was the only reliable source of knowledge and rejected inferences and the Vedas. It has been almost entirely lost except for fragments cited by other schools.
-   Jainism: Founded by Mahāvīra, Jainism believes in strict non-violence or ahiṃsā and rejects the Vedas and the caste system. Liberation is achieved by removing karma through ascetic practices and non-attachment to desires. The two major branches are Śvetāmbara and Digambara.
-   Buddhism: Founded by Gautama Buddha, Buddhism teaches that liberation from suffering is achieved by following the Eightfold Path and detaching oneself from desires. It rejects the Vedas and caste system and emphasizes meditation, moral conduct, and wisdom. There are many branches including Theravāda, Mahāyāna, and Vajrayāna.

Mnemonics/learning tricks:
- Cārvāka: Rejection of soul and afterlife; perception is only knowledge; materialism
- Jainism: Non-violence; reject Vedas and caste; remove karma through asceticism
- Buddhism: Follow Eightfold Path; end suffering by ending desires; reject Vedas and caste; emphasize wisdom, morality, and meditation
