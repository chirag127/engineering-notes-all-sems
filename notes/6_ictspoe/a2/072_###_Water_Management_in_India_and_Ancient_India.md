### Water Management in India and Ancient India

- Water management in India dates back to ancient times. In ancient India, water management systems were highly advanced and sustainable.
- Some important water management practices in ancient India:
    - Construction of tanks, wells, ponds and dams. Large dams were built across rivers for irrigation and water storage. For example, the Grand Anicut dam across Kaveri river was built in 2nd century CE.
    - Construction of canals for irrigation. For example, Kalinga had an extensive network of canals for irrigation.
    - Use of water lifts and pulleys to draw water from wells.
    - Rainwater harvesting techniques were widely practiced. For example, Bawdi system in Rajasthan.
    - Planned townships had proper drainage and sewage systems, for example Harappa and Mohenjo-Daro civilization.
- Important benefits of ancient Indian water management systems:
    - Ensured water security and reduced water scarcity.
    - Increased agricultural output through irrigation. This led to surplus food production and growth of civilization.
    - Proper sanitation systems improved public health.
    - Integrated approach towards water, considering all aspects - storage, drainage, sanitation, irrigation etc.
- However, many of these systems were destroyed due to neglect and natural calamities over time. Today we can learn from the sustainable practices of ancient water management and adopt/modify them as per current requirements.
