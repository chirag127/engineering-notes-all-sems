### Pottery as Cultural Heritage of India

- Pottery is one of the oldest crafts in India with a cultural heritage spanning over 5000 years. The traditional pottery of India is closely linked to the cultural traditions of the various regions of the country.
- The different pottery traditions of India can be broadly classified into three types:
    - Hand-built pottery - Made using hand-building techniques without the use of a potter's wheel. Examples include tribal pottery of central India and terracotta temple sculptures of South India.
    - Wheel-thrown functional pottery - Made on a potter's wheel and includes kitchenware and water pots. Examples include the famous blue pottery of Jaipur and the red pottery of Uttar Pradesh.
    - Decorative glazed pottery - Includes artistic glazed ceramic wares. Examples include the multicolored pottery of Varanasi and the white with blue patterns pottery of Delhi.
- Distinctive regional styles of Indian pottery include the terracotta temple sculptures of South India, hand-painted tiles and plates of Rajasthan, the black pottery of Odisha, the red and black pottery of Gujarat and the aforementioned blue pottery of Jaipur and multicolored pottery of Varanasi.
- The ancient Indus Valley civilization and the various dynasties that ruled India including the Mauryas, Guptas and Cholas contributed significantly to the development of pottery traditions in the country. Pottery has strong religious significance in Indian culture and is an important part of festivals and ceremonies.
- The wide variety of Indian pottery and its sustainability over millennia bears testimony to the rich cultural heritage of India. However, many traditional pottery styles are under threat due to competition from cheaper mass-produced alternatives and decline in patronage. Conservation efforts are underway to preserve this cultural heritage.
