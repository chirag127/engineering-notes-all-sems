### Astronomy in India and Ancient India

- Ancient Indian astronomy flowered in the Vedic period of Indian history. The Vedas contain references to astronomical events and cosmological time cycles. The earliest textual evidence of Indian astronomy is found in the Vedanga Jyotisha, a text dated to the middle of the first millennium BCE.
- The Magha or Vanaaka calendar is employed in the fifth text called Vedanga Jyotisha for ritual timing already references the nakshatras. The Vedanga Jyotisha describes rules to determine the times of Vedanga divasa with reference to the lunar nakshatras and solar months.
- The Vedas contain thought-provoking speculations about the nature and origin of the cosmos. Prominent Vedic astronomers include Lagadha and Vishnucharya. Post-Vedic Indian astronomy is represented by scholars such as Aryabhata, Brahmagupta, Bhaskara I and II, and Varahamihira. Important treatises by Aryabhata (499 CE) and Brahmagupta (598 CE) formulated the basis of theoretical Indian astronomy
- Aryabhata correctly insisted that the earth was spherical and rotated on its axis, and he calculated the length of the year and the circumference and diameter of the earth with reasonable accuracy. He also gave the correct explanation for lunar and solar eclipses and the first system for computing the motions of the planets.
- Indian astronomy flowered in the 5th-6th century, with Aryabhata, Brahmagupta and other astronomers making important advances and contributing to the foundation of astronomy. Their texts were translated to Arabic and then to Latin, which were used in Europe until the time of Isaac Newton.
- Some key topics and concepts covered in Indian astronomy texts include planetary models, lunar and solar eclipses, planetary motions (including retrogradation), the mean motions of the planets, and the calculation of the relative distances of the planets from Earth using eclipse data.
