### Writing Technology in India and Ancient India

- The Indus script is the earliest writing system developed in the Indian subcontinent. The Indus script was used by the Indus Valley Civilization from about 3300 BCE. However, the Indus script remains undeciphered.
- The earliest deciphered writing system of India is the Brahmi script. It was developed in the 6th-5th century BCE and was used to write several languages of the Indian subcontinent including Sanskrit, Prakrit, and Dravidian languages. The Brahmi script evolved into many regional scripts such as Gupta script, Pallava script, Kadamba script, etc.
- Other ancient scripts of India include Kharoshthi which was used in northwestern India between the 4th century BCE and 4th century CE, and Tamil Brahmi which was used to write the Tamil language in southern India.
- Ancient Indians used palm leaves, tree barks, leather, and metals like copper and stone/rock surfaces as writing materials. The palm leaf was the most popular and durable writing material. They used sharpened reeds or sticks as pens to write on these materials. Ink was prepared from lampblack or charcoal, gum and water.
- Some mnemonics and learning tricks to remember:
- Think of palm leaves as ancient Indian notepads.
- Picture ancient Indians using sharpened reeds as pens dipping in ink and writing on palm leaves and other materials.
- The undeciphered Indus script and the evolution of Brahmi script into regional scripts can be remembered as the development of writing technology in ancient India.
