### Engineering and Architecture as Cultural Heritage of India

The engineering and architectural feats achieved by ancient Indians are a testimony to the advanced scientific knowledge and skills that prevailed in the Indian subcontinent. Some of the notable engineering and architectural marvels of ancient India are:

- Harappan Civilization (3300 BCE - 1300 BCE): The Harappans exhibited excellent town planning skills. Their cities were well-structured with wide roads, underground drainage system, and citadels. The Great Bath at Mohenjo-Daro is a remarkable example of their hydraulic engineering capabilities.
- Mauryan Architecture (322 BCE - 185 BCE): The Mauryan empire under emperor Ashoka made use of polished stones and rock-cut architecture in the construction of pillars, stupas, and monasteries. The Ashoka Pillar with its polished surface and fine balance is an engineering marvel. The stupas of Sanchi are magnificent examples of Mauryan architecture.
- Gupta Architecture (320 CE - 550 CE): The Gupta period witnessed a boom in Hindu temple architecture. The Dashavatara temple at Deogarh is an excellent example of the Nagara style of architecture with a curvilinear tower and intricate carvings. The Hindu temples were symmetrical and harmonic in design following principles prescribed in ancient Indian texts like Shilpa Shastras.
- Vijayanagara Architecture (1336 CE - 1646 CE): The capital city of the Vijayanagara empire - Hampi, is a UNESCO world heritage site famous for its Dravidian architecture. The Vitthala temple with its ornate pillared halls, massive gateways and the stone chariot is an engineering marvel. The pillars of the Kalyana mandapa produce musical notes when struck, demonstrating the advanced skills of temple architects and sculptors.
- Traditional Water Harvesting Systems: From the Harappans to the people of the semi-arid regions of India, indigenous water harvesting systems were constructed that demonstrate remarkable engineering skills. Step wells, tanks, and network of canals and channels were designed to collect and store water efficiently for use during dry seasons. The Rani ki Vav stepwell in Gujarat is a famous example of such an architectural marvel.

The above points are some salient features of the engineering and architectural feats of ancient India. The techniques and skills required to build these marvels were passed down generations through practice and oral traditions. Sadly, many of the ancient Indian engineering and architectural wonders have either been destroyed or lie in ruins today. Preserving the ones that remain is crucial to understand the scientific temper and skills of our ancestors.
