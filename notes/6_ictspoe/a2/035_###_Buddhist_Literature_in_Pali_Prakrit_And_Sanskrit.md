### Buddhist Literature in Pali,Prakrit And Sanskrit

Buddhist literature refers to the corpus of scriptures and texts composed in various languages including Pali, Prakrit and Sanskrit which form the textual foundation of Buddhism. The early Buddhist texts were transmitted either orally or written on perishable materials, and preserved in the Buddhist monastic tradition.

The Pali canon refers to the standard collection of scriptures in the Theravada Buddhist tradition, preserved in the Pali language. The Vinaya Pitaka contains rules for monks and nuns. The Sutta Pitaka contains discourses and teachings of the Buddha on various topics. The Abhidhamma Pitaka contains philosophical, psychological teachings and classifications.

In Mahayana Buddhism, many sutras preserved in Sanskrit such as the Heart Sutra, Diamond Sutra, Lotus Sutra form an important part of the scriptural canon. The Prajnaparamita sutras emphasize the emptiness (Shunyata) doctrine. The Tathagatagarbha sutras focus on Buddha Nature.

Buddhist Hybrid Sanskrit and Middle Indo-Aryan languages including Prakrit were also used to compose texts. For example, the poems in the Therigatha and Theragatha collections in Prakrit describe the early masters and their awakening experiences.

- Mnemonics: Pitaka refers to "basket" (of teachings); Vinaya relates to discipline; Sutta refers to discourses; Abhidhamma refers to special/higher teachings.
- The three pitakas provide a comprehensive framework to learn and practice the Buddha's teachings. They form the Tripitaka or "Three Baskets" in Pali.
- Sanskrit sutras contain teachings of Mahayana philosophy and meditation. Understanding Emptiness and Buddha Nature are key to progress on the path.
- Poems in Prakrit capture the spiritual journeys of early followers. They provide inspiration through descriptions of struggles and victories over defilements.
