### Evolution of languages in India

The evolution of languages in India has a long history and complex patterns:

1. The Indus Valley civilization spoke an undeciphered Dravidian language. The earliest linguistic records of India are in the form of the Indus script dating back to ~3,500 BCE.

2. The early Vedic Sanskrit emerged in the 2nd millennium BCE. The Vedas are among the earliest compositions in Vedic Sanskrit and include elements from Indus Valley languages.

3. The Middle Indo-Aryan languages like Prakrit and Pali emerged around the 6th century BCE. They are descended from Vedic Sanskrit and were spoken in Northern India. Lord Buddha's teachings were recorded in Pali.

4. Classical Sanskrit developed from Vedic Sanskrit starting the 3rd century BCE. Panini's Ashtadhyayi is the earliest known work on Sanskrit grammar. Classical Sanskrit was used in high literary works and prayers.

5. The Prakrit languages diversified and evolved into Apabhra?a languages in the 1st millennium CE. Apabhra?a gave rise to languages like Hindi, Punjabi, Bengali, etc. in the succeeding centuries.

6. The Dravidian languages evolved earlier in southern India into languages like Tamil, Telugu, Kannada and Malayalam. Tamil is the oldest surviving Dravidian language with records dating to ~300 BCE.

7. The Austroasiatic Munda languages like Santali were spoken in eastern India. The Tibeto-Burman languages emerged in northeast India. Sino-Tibetan languages like Meitei and Tangkhul also originated in northeast India.

8. Persian/Arabic vocabulary influences came with Islamic rule starting the 13th century. The Persian script (modified as Urdu script) was used to write some languages like Urdu. English influences came with British colonization starting the 17th century.

Thus, the evolution of languages in India has been influenced by substratum languages of ancient India, as well as later immigrations and historical events. The diverse geography, long history and complex patterns of migration in India have resulted in a rich linguistic diversity.
