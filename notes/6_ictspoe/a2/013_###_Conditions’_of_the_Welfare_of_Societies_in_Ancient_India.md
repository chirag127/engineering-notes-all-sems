### Conditions’ of the Welfare of Societies in Ancient India

The ancient Indian thinkers laid down certain conditions for the welfare and prosperity of societies:

1. **Dharma:** The observance of moral and ethical codes of conduct. Dharma referred to the performance of duties and obligations towards oneself, family, society, and humanity. Following the path of righteousness was considered essential for individual and social well-being.

2. **Artha:** The pursuit of material well-being and economic prosperity. The ancient Indian thinkers recognized the importance of Artha or material prosperity for the overall development of society. However, they emphasized that the pursuit of Artha should be regulated by Dharma.

3. **Kama:** The satisfaction of desires and pleasures. While recognizing the importance of Kama or desire, the ancient Indian thinkers stressed that it should be pursued in a regulated manner. Over-indulgence in material pleasures and sensual gratification was discouraged.

4. **Moksha:** The attainment of liberation from the cycle of birth and death. The ancient Indian thinkers considered Moksha or emancipation as the ultimate goal of human life. However, they emphasized that the three preliminary goals of Dharma, Artha, and Kama must be achieved before one can attain Moksha.

**Mnemonics:**

*Dharma, Artha, Kama, Moksha*

*Righteousness, Wealth, Desire, Liberation*

The four goals that lead societies to prosper,

When pursued in harmony, without excess or measure.

**Advantages:** This framework recognized all aspects necessary for individual and societal welfare - moral, material, pleasurable, and spiritual. It encouraged a balanced and holistic approach to life.
**Disadvantages:** In practice, it was difficult to achieve a balance between the four goals. There were tendencies to over-emphasize certain goals like Artha or Kama over others like Dharma or Moksha. This led to a lopsided development of societies.
