#### Four-class Classification of Society in Ancient India

The ancient Indian society was divided into four classes based on occupation and social status:

1. Brahmins: The priests and teachers. They were considered the highest in the social hierarchy. They did not indulge in any economic activity and were dependent on the society for their livelihood.

2. Kshatriyas: The warriors and rulers. They were the protectors of the society. Examples are kings, administrators, and soldiers.

3. Vaishyas: The traders and merchants. They were engaged in economic activity and trade. Examples are businessmen and merchants.

4. Shudras: The laborers and service providers. They performed menial jobs and provided services to the other three classes. Examples are farmers, artisans, and servants.

* Mnemonic: The social hierarchy can be remembered as 'Brahmin priests did kirats our vassals as shudras'.
* The classification was based on birth and hereditary with limited mobility. The classes were rigid and segregated from each other.
* The classification reflected the division of labor and roles in the ancient Indian society. However, it led to inequality and social discrimination which still persists today.
