### drama as Cultural Heritage of India

- Indian drama and theatre has a long and rich tradition spanning over 2000 years. It encompasses a wide range of traditional performing arts from folk theatre to classical Sanskrit and regional language plays.
- The earliest known form of Indian drama was the Sanskrit drama. It flourished between the 1st century BC and the 10th century AD. The most well-known Sanskrit playwrights include Bhasa, Kalidasa, and Vishakhadatta. Famous Sanskrit plays include Shakuntala, Mrcchakatika, and Vikramorvasiyam. These plays were typically performed in royal courts and dealt with themes from mythology and history.
- Folk theatre became popular from around the 10th century. There are many forms of Indian folk theatre which are indigenous to different regions of India, based on local traditions and cultural practices. Some major forms are Bhand Pather from Kashmir, Nautanki from Uttar Pradesh, Tamasha from Maharashtra, Yakshagana from Karnataka, Therukoothu from Tamil Nadu, etc. These forms typically consist of music, songs, and dance along with dialogue. The performances involve stories from Indian epics and mythology using colorful costumes and makeup.
- Modern Indian theatre developed in the late 19th century as a blend of realism and folk forms. Today, Indian drama incorporates elements of European drama as well and deals with realistic as well as contemporary social issues. Contemporary Indian playwrights include Manjula Padmanabhan, Mahesh Dattani, and Habib Tanvir.
- Some key characteristics of traditional Indian drama are the representation of emotions through symbolic gestures and actions rather than dialogue, use of Sanskrit poetry and verse, parts played by male actors, and themes from mythology and legends. Indian drama is a rich cultural heritage which represents the long dramatic tradition in the country with a diverse range of art forms across multiple regions.
