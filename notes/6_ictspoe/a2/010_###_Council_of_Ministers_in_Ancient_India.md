### Council of Ministers in Ancient India

- The council of ministers in ancient India was known as the Mantri Parishad or Mantri Mandal.
- The king would appoint ministers based on their merit, wisdom, and loyalty to advice and assist him in administration.
- The number of ministers varied across kingdoms and dynasties. Some of the major ministers and their portfolios were:

- Prime Minister or Chief Minister - Held the highest position below the king. Assisted the king in overall administration and was the chief advisor.
- Minister of War - Handled the military and defense of the kingdom.
- Minister of Finance - Looked after the revenue, taxation, and financial matters of the kingdom.
- Minister of Justice - Deputy to the king in judicial matters and disputes. Appointed judges and ensured proper dispensation of justice.
- Minister of Public Works - Responsible for constructing infrastructure such as roads, canals, forts, palaces, etc.
- Minister of Religion - Looked after religious affairs, temples, propagation of the Vedas and other scriptures. Also looked after the education system.

- The king was not bound by the advice of the council of ministers. However, the ministers played an important role through their wisdom and experience in assisting the king in the efficient governance of the state. The institution declined with the fall of dynasties and the rise of imperialism.
