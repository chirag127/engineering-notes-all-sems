### Political Ideals in Ancient India and their relevance in modern times

- Ancient India had various schools of political thought and ideals:
   - **Artha** (prosperity/material well-being): Advocated by ancient texts like Arthashastra which dealt with statecraft, economy, and politics. Focused on _prakriti_ (nature/environment) and _purusha_ (human agency). Relevant today in advocating utilization of resources for public good and welfare.
   - **Dharma** (righteous duty): Concept of righteous duty of the ruler and citizens. Concept of _swadharma_ (individual duty) and _rajdharma_ (duties of ruler). Relevant today in advocating constitutionally defined duties of citizens and leaders.
   - **Kama** (desire/pleasure): Associated with hedonistic philosophies which were rejected and instead duty/virtue based philosophies were emphasized. Not relevant today as unsustainable.
   - **Moksha** (liberation): Associated with renunciation of worldly affairs and achieving spiritual liberation. Not relevant for statecraft but relevant for individuals in advocating equilibrium between material and spiritual welfare.
- Edicts of Ashoka emphasized dhamma (duty/virtue/piety), tolerance, non-violence, and public welfare. Relevant today in promoting constitutional values and public welfare.
- Kautilya's Arthashastra emphasized efficiency, pragmatism, and realpolitik in statecraft. Focused on power and prosperity of the state. Relevant today in aspects of defense, foreign policy, and utilitarian governance but its harsher prescriptions are debatable.
- Various schools like Buddhism, Jainism, etc. emerged emphasizing non-violence, equality, and liberation. Relevant today in promoting compassion, tolerance and individual welfare.

The political ideals of ancient India thus focused on duty, virtue, material well-being as well as spiritual welfare. They emphasize a balanced approach which is relevant even today in advocating constitutional values, public welfare, and sustainable development. The specifics of various philosophies are debatable but the overarching themes highlight the importance of dharma, artha, kama, and moksha - which can inspire and inform governance and policymaking even in the modern context.
