#### Contract Theory of origin of State

According to Contract Theory, the state originated as a result of an implied social contract between individuals. In the state of nature, individuals lived in a condition of anarchy, uncertainty and insecurity. To overcome this miserable condition, individuals came together and entered into a contract by which they agreed to surrender a portion of their natural rights in exchange for social order and security. The state is the result of this contract. The proponents of this theory are Thomas Hobbes, John Locke and Jean Jacques Rousseau.

- *Thomas Hobbes* advocated an absolute sovereign with supreme and undivided power to maintain order. According to him, in the state of nature life was "solitary, poor, nasty, brutish and short". So individuals contracted to establish a Leviathan with absolute power.
- *John Locke* argued for a limited state based on the consent of the governed. He believed that individuals in the state of nature had natural rights to life, liberty and property. They contracted to establish a state to protect these rights, and if it failed to do so, they had the right to overthrow it.
- *Jean Jacques Rousseau* argued that in the state of nature individuals were free, but the introduction of private property led to inequality and social conflict. So individuals contracted to establish a state based on the general will to achieve liberty and equality.

**Advantages:** Explains the origin of the state as a result of the consent and agreement of individuals. Emphasizes that the authority of the state is based on the consent of the people.
**Disadvantages:** The existence of a pre-political state of nature is doubtful. The social contract is a historical fiction. There is no evidence that individuals explicitly consented to establish the state.
**Mnemonics:** *Hobbes* - *Leviathan with absolute power;*
*Locke* - *Limited state to protect natural rights;*
*Rousseau* - *General will to achieve liberty and equality.*
