#### Āshrama or the Stages of Life in Society in Ancient India

The ancient Indian society divided a person's life into four stages or āshramas:

1. Brahmacharya āshrama: The student stage
- Spent in celibacy and learning; at the guru's āshrama
- Boys till age 24 devoted to education
- Brahmins spent this stage learning the Vedas; Kshatriyas and Vaishyas learned skills and warfare

Mnemonic: Think Brahman (ultimate reality) + charya (conduct) = leading life pursuing sacred knowledge

2. Grihastha āshrama: The householder stage
- Begins at marriage; dedicated to family and profession
- Considered the most important stage as it contributed to society
- responsible for income, duties, and procreation for continuation of lineage

3. Vānaprastha āshrama: The forest dweller stage
- Around age 60, person hands over responsibilities to next generation and retires to forest
- Leads an austere life focussing on meditation and penance; prepares for the final stage of life

4. Sannyāsa āshrama: The renounced stage
- After vānaprastha stage; person renounces all worldly possessions and duties
- Spends time in meditation focussing on achieving union with Brahman (liberation/moksha)
- Begs for alms for bare survival and roams without attachment
