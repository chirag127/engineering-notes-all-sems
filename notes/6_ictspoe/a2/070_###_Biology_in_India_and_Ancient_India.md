### Biology in India and Ancient India

- Ancient Indian scholars made significant contributions to the field of biology, particularly in the areas of botany, zoology, and genetics. Many scientific discoveries and advances were made, particularly during the ancient times and Vedic period of Indian history.
- The Charaka Samhita and Sushruta Samhita were important medical treatises written around 500 BCE which described concepts in biology and human anatomy. Charaka's treatise included sections on botany and pharmacology, describing 700 medicinal plants. Sushruta performed surgeries and described procedures for various medical conditions.
- The Vedas contain one of the earliest records of classification of plants and animals. Plants were classified into vrikshas (trees), osadhis (shrubs), vallis (creepers), and trnaani (grasses). The Manusmriti classified animals into five categories: those that dwell in water, in air, in trees, on land and in holes.
- The Vedas discussed topics in genetics, including inheritance of traits from parents. They described parental lineages without mentioning sex of parents, and mention that mother and father dispositions were borne by offspring in equal amounts. The Vedas also described multiple births from a single conception, embryonic development, intra-uterine growth, precocious pregnancy, and superfetation.
- The first botanical garden was developed in ancient India around 3000 BCE in which plants were arranged according to medicinal use. Important plants and their properties and uses were described in texts such as Agnivesha's Charaka Samhita and Sushruta's Sushruta Samhita (around 1000 BCE). Plants were grouped into families based on morphological features.
