### Mathematics in India and Ancient India

- Ancient Indian mathematics flourished in the Indian subcontinent from 1200 BCE until the end of the 18th century.
- The Indus Valley civilization (3300 BCE - 1300 BCE) of ancient India produced early geometrical constructions. Evidence of an early numerical system with a base of 10 has also been found.
- From around the 4th century BCE, Indian mathematicians made important contributions to mathematics in topics such as:
-- Decimal number system and the concept of 0. Aryabhata used decimal places and approximated Pi to 3.1416.
-- Algebra: Brahmagupta discovered solutions to quadratic equations and formulated the quadratic formula.
-- Geometry: Authors including Apastamba, Manava, and others described geometric shapes, their measurements, and solutions to geometric problems.
-- Trigonometry: Aryabhata calculated the value of Pi and the trigonometric functions of sine, cosine, and inverse sine.
-- Calculus: Kerala school of astronomy and mathematics between 1300 CE and 1600 CE anticipated infinitesimal and infinite numbers.

**Mnemonics**:
- Think of numbers in decimal system as powers of 10 - units, tens, hundreds, etc.
- Aryabhata approximated Pi to "3 and 1 quarter" (easy to remember).
- Think of Brahmagupta's formula as "minus B plus/minus square root B squared minus 4AC all over 2A".

**Advantages**: The decimal number system has simplified arithmetic calculations. Concepts from ancient Indian mathematics have applications in modern geometry, trigonometry, calculus, and astronomy.

**Disadvantages**: Ancient Indian mathematics was orally transmitted and not formally documented. Lack of standard mathematical notation led to ambiguity in interpreting concepts. Many mathematical insights were recorded in verse, which can be difficult to understand.
