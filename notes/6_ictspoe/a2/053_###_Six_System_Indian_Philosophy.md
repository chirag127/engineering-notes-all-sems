### Six System Indian Philosophy

The six orthodox systems or darśanas of Indian philosophy are:

1. Nyāya - focuses on logic and epistemology. Mnemonic: 'Nitī nigamana nāma nyāyaḥ' which means 'Nyāya is the science of right reasoning'. It examines sources of knowledge and criteria for correct thinking.
2. Vaiśeṣika - focuses on categorization and metaphysics. Mnemonic: 'padārtha paricchedanām vaiśeṣikam' which means 'Vaiśeṣika is the differentiation of categories'. It proposes atomism and divides all objects in the universe into six categories.
3. Sāṃkhya - focuses on cosmology and spiritual liberation. Mnemonic: 'saṃkhyaṃ khyāti vistaraḥ' which means 'Sāṃkhya is the detailed explanation of creation'. It proposes a dualistic worldview of puruṣa (consciousness) and prakṛti (primordial matter). Liberation occurs through discrimination of the two.
4. Yoga - focuses on meditation and spiritual practices. Mnemonic: 'yogash chitta vṛitti nirodhah' which means 'Yoga is the cessation of the modifications of the mind'. It elaborates on the discipline to still one's thoughts and attain liberation.
5. Mīmāṃsā - focuses on exegesis and ritual analysis. Mnemonic: 'mīmāṃsātattvavivecanam' which means 'Mīmāṃsā examines the nature of reality'. It examines the Vedas as a source of dharma and emphasizes performance of rituals.
6. Vedānta - focuses on the study of the Upaniṣads. Mnemonic: 'antaḥ prajñānam brahma' which means 'The innermost consciousness is Brahman'. It examines the nature of ultimate reality and the relation between Brahman, the world, and the individual self or ātman. Advaita, Viśiṣṭādvaita and Dvaita are sub-schools of Vedānta.
