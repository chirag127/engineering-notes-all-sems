### Puranas

The Puranas are ancient Hindu religious texts that contain legends, mythology, cosmology, and genealogies of kings. There are 18 major Puranas and 18 minor Puranas for a total of 36.

Some key points about the Puranas:

- They expand on the Vedas with stories and legends, making the philosophy and ideas more accessible to the common person.
- They contain explanations of rituals, cosmology, genealogy, and myths related to various deities.
- The Puranas are written in poetic verse, unlike the prose of the Vedas. This made them easier to memorize and transmit orally before being written down.
- Different Puranas focus on the legends and stories of different deities - for example, the Bhagavata Purana focuses on Vishnu and Krishna, the Shiva Purana focuses on Shiva, and the Devi-Bhagavata Purana focuses on Devi or the Goddess.
- The Puranas were compiled after the Vedas and Upanishads, likely between 400 CE to 1000 CE. They represent a shift to more personalized forms of religion and bhakti or devotional worship.
- They provide explanations for the contradictions in the Vedas and Smritis and demonstrate how the various deities are ultimately aspects of one supreme reality.
- Some of the most studied and popular Puranas today are the Bhagavata Purana, Vishnu Purana, Shiva Purana, Padma Purana, and Devi Bhagavata Purana.

Mnemonics/memory tricks: The Puranas can be remembered through the acronym "Vishnu Shiv Dev Padma Bhag" for the 5 most popular Puranas. The 18 major Puranas can be remembered as the Maha Puranas. The later composition and devotional focus of the Puranas can be remembered as they expand on and "purify" the Vedas, making them more accessible to the "commoner".
