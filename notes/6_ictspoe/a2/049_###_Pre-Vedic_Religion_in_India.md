### Pre-Vedic Religion in India

The Pre-Vedic religion in India refers to the religious beliefs and practices of the Indus Valley civilization. The following points highlight the key aspects of the Pre-Vedic religion:

1. Worship of Mother Goddess: The people of the Indus Valley civilization worshipped a Mother Goddess who is believed to be a symbol of fertility. The famous Indus Valley seal depicting a figure in a yogic posture surrounded by animals is considered to be a prototype of Shakti or the Mother Goddess.

2. Pashupati Seal: The Pashupati seal depicts a figure sitting in a yogic posture surrounded by animals. It is believed to be a prototype of the Hindu god Shiva as Pashupati (lord of animals). This indicates that the people of the Indus Valley civilization may have worshipped a deity analogous to the Vedic god Shiva.

3. Phallic worship: The prevalence of phallic symbols or Lingams in the ruins of the Indus Valley civilization suggests that the people may have practised some form of phallic worship. The Shiva lingam is a symbol of this in present-day Hinduism.

4. Tree and animal worship: Some Indus Valley seals depict trees and animals like the pipal tree, tiger, rhinoceros, etc. This indicates that the people may have worshipped these trees and animals, like in present-day Hinduism.

5. Fire worship: The presence of fire altars in the ruins suggests that the people may have practised fire worship or Homa, analogous to the fire rituals in present-day Hinduism.

The above points highlight some of the key features of the Pre-Vedic religion in India. The traces of worship of Mother Goddess, Pashupati, phallic symbols, trees/animals, and fire suggest that some elements of the Pre-Vedic religion were incorporated into later Vedic Hinduism. However, the Pre-Vedic religion was distinct from the Vedic religion which emphasised the worship of deities like Indra, Agni, etc. through rituals and sacrifices.
