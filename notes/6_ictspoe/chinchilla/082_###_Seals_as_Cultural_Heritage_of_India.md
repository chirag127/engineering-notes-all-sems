### Seals as Cultural Heritage of India

Seals are one of the most significant archaeological findings in India, which provide us with valuable insights into the historical, cultural, and social aspects of ancient India. Seals are small, usually square-shaped, engraved objects made of clay, stone, or metal, and were used for various purposes, including communication, trade, and identification. Here are some important points to understand the significance of seals as cultural heritage of India:

1. Indus Valley Civilization: Seals were first discovered in the Indus Valley Civilization, which is considered one of the oldest civilizations in the world. These seals were made of steatite and had engravings of animals, humans, and other objects, which provide us with valuable information about the social and cultural life of the Indus Valley people.

2. Trade and Commerce: Seals were used for identifying goods and marking ownership, which facilitated trade and commerce in ancient India. The seals found in the Indus Valley Civilization depict various animals, indicating that trade in animals and animal products was prevalent during that time.

3. Language: Some seals have inscriptions in the ancient Indus script, which is still undeciphered. These inscriptions provide us with valuable clues about the language and writing system used by the Indus Valley people.

4. Religion and Mythology: Seals found in India depict various deities and mythological scenes, indicating the importance of religion and mythology in ancient Indian society. For example, the famous Pashupati seal depicts a human-like figure sitting in a yogic posture surrounded by animals, which is believed to be an early representation of Lord Shiva.

5. Artistic Value: Seals are also significant for their artistic value, as they are finely crafted and engraved with intricate designs. These designs provide us with valuable insights into the artistic and aesthetic sensibilities of ancient Indian society.

2. "PARI" - P for Pashupati seal, A for Animals, R for Religion and Mythology, I for Inscriptions.

In conclusion, seals are an important cultural heritage of India, providing us with valuable insights into the history, culture, and social life of ancient India. Their significance lies not only in their historical and archaeological value but also in their artistic and aesthetic value, making them an important part of India's cultural heritage.
