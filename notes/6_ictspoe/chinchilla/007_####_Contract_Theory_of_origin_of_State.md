#### Contract Theory of origin of State

The Contract Theory of origin of State is a political theory that explains the origin and purpose of the state. According to this theory, the state is a social contract between individuals who have come together to form a society. The theory suggests that individuals willingly give up some of their rights and freedoms in exchange for protection and security provided by the state.

Here are some key points to understand the Contract Theory of origin of State:

1. Social Contract: The Contract Theory of origin of State is based on the concept of a social contract. This social contract is an agreement between individuals to give up some of their natural rights in exchange for protection and security provided by the state.

2. Natural Rights: The natural rights are the inherent rights that every individual possesses, such as the right to life, liberty, and property. According to the Contract Theory, individuals give up some of their natural rights to the state in exchange for protection and security.

3. Purpose of the State: The purpose of the state is to provide protection and security to the citizens. The state is responsible for maintaining law and order, protecting the citizens from external threats, and providing public services.

4. Consent of the Governed: The Contract Theory suggests that the state derives its legitimacy from the consent of the governed. In other words, individuals willingly give up some of their rights and freedoms to the state because they believe that the state will protect them.

5. John Locke's Theory: John Locke is one of the most prominent thinkers associated with the Contract Theory of origin of State. According to Locke, individuals have the right to life, liberty, and property. However, these rights are not absolute, and individuals must give up some of their rights to the state in exchange for protection and security.

6. Jean Jacques Rousseau's Theory: Another prominent thinker associated with the Contract Theory of origin of State is Jean Jacques Rousseau. According to Rousseau, the state is a social contract between individuals who have come together to form a society. In this social contract, individuals give up their individual rights and freedoms to the state in exchange for the general will of the society.
