### Six System Indian Philosophy

Indian Philosophy is a vast field of study that encompasses various schools of thought. The Six System Indian Philosophy, also known as Shad-Darsana, is a collection of six major schools of Indian philosophy that originated in ancient India. These six schools of thought are:

1. Samkhya - The Samkhya system was founded by Sage Kapila. It is based on the principle of dualism, where the universe is composed of two eternal realities - Purusha (consciousness) and Prakriti (matter). The Samkhya system focuses on the study of nature, evolution, and the human mind.

2. Yoga - The Yoga system was founded by Sage Patanjali. It is based on the principle of unity, where the individual self unites with the universal self. The Yoga system focuses on the practice of physical postures, breathing exercises, and meditation to attain a state of tranquility and spiritual enlightenment.

3. Nyaya - The Nyaya system was founded by Sage Gautama. It is based on the principle of logic and reasoning. The Nyaya system focuses on the study of epistemology (theory of knowledge), metaphysics (nature of reality), and ethics (moral principles).

4. Vaisheshika - The Vaisheshika system was founded by Sage Kanada. It is based on the principle of atomic theory, where the universe is composed of atoms that combine to form different objects. The Vaisheshika system focuses on the study of physics, metaphysics, and ethics.

5. Purva Mimamsa - The Purva Mimamsa system was founded by Sage Jaimini. It is based on the principle of ritualism, where the Vedas are the ultimate authority for all knowledge. The Purva Mimamsa system focuses on the study of the Vedas, their interpretation, and the performance of rituals.

6. Uttara Mimamsa or Vedanta - The Uttara Mimamsa system was founded by Sage Vyasa. It is based on the principle of monism, where the individual self and the universal self are one and the same. The Uttara Mimamsa system focuses on the study of the Upanishads, the Brahma Sutras, and the Bhagavad Gita.

Mnemonics and Learning Tricks:

- To remember the six systems of Indian philosophy, one can use the acronym "SUN VIP" where SUN stands for Samkhya, Yoga, Nyaya and VIP stands for Vaisheshika, Purva Mimamsa, and Uttara Mimamsa (Vedanta).

- Another trick to remember the six systems of Indian philosophy is to use the phrase "Some Yoga Nuts Vow Peaceful Unity" where the first letter of each word represents the first letter of each system - Samkhya, Yoga, Nyaya, Vaisheshika, Purva Mimamsa, and Uttara Mimamsa.

Advantages and Disadvantages:

- The Six System Indian Philosophy provides a comprehensive understanding of the various schools of thought that originated in ancient India.

- Each system has its own unique approach to understanding the nature of reality, the human mind, and spiritual enlightenment.

- The systems can be studied individually or in combination to gain a holistic understanding of Indian philosophy.

- The disadvantage of studying these systems is that they can be complex and difficult to comprehend, especially for beginners.

Examples and Applications:

- The Six System Indian Philosophy has influenced various fields such as metaphysics, ethics, psychology, and spirituality.

- The Yoga system has gained worldwide popularity for its focus on physical and mental well-being.

- The Nyaya system is used in modern Indian law to understand the principles of logic and reasoning.

- The Vedanta system has influenced various spiritual movements in India and abroad.

In conclusion, the Six System Indian Philosophy provides a deep understanding of the various schools of thought that originated in ancient India. By studying these systems, one can gain insights into the nature of reality, the human mind, and spiritual enlightenment.
