### Agriculture in India and Ancient India

Agriculture has been the backbone of the Indian economy since ancient times. In ancient India, agriculture was the main occupation of the people, and the agricultural practices were highly advanced. This article discusses the agriculture practices in ancient India and the current scenario of agriculture in India.

#### Agriculture in Ancient India

In ancient India, agriculture was practiced using traditional methods such as plowing the fields using oxen, sowing seeds, and harvesting crops. The ancient Indian farmers used techniques such as crop rotation, intercropping, and mixed farming to increase the productivity of their land.

**Mnemonics:** 
* "Plow with oxen, sow the seed, harvest the crop, and make them feed" 
* "Crop rotation, intercropping, mixed farming - ancient Indian techniques for high yield farming"

#### Current Scenario of Agriculture in India

India is still an agrarian economy with agriculture being the primary source of livelihood for a significant portion of the population. The agriculture sector in India has evolved significantly over the years, with the adoption of modern farming techniques, use of hybrid seeds, and improved irrigation practices.

**Advantages of modern farming techniques:**
* Increased crop yield
* Reduced labor costs
* Efficient use of resources
* Better quality crops

**Disadvantages of modern farming techniques:**
* Dependence on chemical fertilizers and pesticides
* Environmental pollution
* Loss of biodiversity

#### Major Crops in India

India is a diverse country, and the agriculture practices vary across different regions. Some of the major crops grown in India are:

* Rice
* Wheat
* Maize
* Millets
* Pulses
* Oilseeds
* Sugarcane
* Cotton
* Jute

**Mnemonics:**
* "Ravi was making millet porridge and adding sugarcane juice to it" 
* "Wheat and rice on one side, cotton and jute on the other"

#### Government Policies and Schemes

The Indian government has implemented several policies and schemes to support the agriculture sector, such as:

* Pradhan Mantri Fasal Bima Yojana (PMFBY)
* Pradhan Mantri Krishi Sinchai Yojana (PMKSY)
* National Agriculture Market (e-NAM)
* Soil Health Card Scheme
* Paramparagat Krishi Vikas Yojana (PKVY)

These policies aim to increase crop productivity, reduce crop losses, and improve the income of farmers.

**Mnemonics:**
* "PMFBY, PMKSY, e-NAM - Govt. schemes for agri-farmers"
* "Soil Health Card, PKVY - for healthy crops and happy farmers"

In conclusion, Agriculture has been an integral part of India's economy and culture since ancient times. The traditional methods used by farmers in ancient India are still relevant today, and the adoption of modern techniques has further improved the productivity of the agricultural sector. The government's policies and schemes have also played a crucial role in supporting the agriculture sector and improving the lives of farmers.
