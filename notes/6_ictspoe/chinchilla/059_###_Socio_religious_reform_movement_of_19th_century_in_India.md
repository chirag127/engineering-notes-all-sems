### Socio Religious Reform Movement of 19th Century in India

The 19th century in India was marked by various socio-religious reform movements that aimed to create a more progressive and modern society. These movements were led by prominent social and religious leaders who sought to address the various social evils prevalent in Indian society, such as caste discrimination, child marriage, and the practice of sati.

Here are some important points to remember about the socio-religious reform movement in 19th century India:

1. The reform movements were initiated by prominent social and religious leaders, such as Raja Ram Mohan Roy, Swami Vivekananda, and Dayanand Saraswati.

2. Raja Ram Mohan Roy, who is considered the father of the Indian Renaissance, founded the Brahmo Samaj in 1828. The Brahmo Samaj aimed to promote monotheism and reject idol worship.

3. Swami Vivekananda, a disciple of Ramakrishna, founded the Ramakrishna Mission in 1897. The mission aimed to promote the principles of Vedanta and the teachings of Ramakrishna.

4. Dayanand Saraswati founded the Arya Samaj in 1875. The Arya Samaj aimed to promote Vedic teachings and reject the caste system and other social evils.

5. The reform movements also aimed to promote education, especially for women. Many schools and colleges were established during this period, such as Bethune College in Kolkata and Aligarh Muslim University.

6. The movements also aimed to promote social equality and justice. They advocated for the abolition of caste discrimination, child marriage, and the practice of sati.

7. The reform movements also had a significant impact on the Indian independence movement, as many of the leaders involved in the reform movements also played a key role in the struggle for independence.
