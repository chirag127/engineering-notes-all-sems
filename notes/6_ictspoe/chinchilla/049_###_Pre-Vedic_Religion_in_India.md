### Pre-Vedic Religion in India

Pre-Vedic religion in India refers to the religious practices and beliefs that existed in the Indian subcontinent before the arrival of the Aryans. This period is believed to have lasted from around 7000 BCE to 1500 BCE. The religion during this period was predominantly nature worship, and the deities were associated with various aspects of nature.

Some of the key features of the Pre-Vedic religion in India are as follows:

1. Nature worship: The Pre-Vedic religion was primarily nature worship. The deities were associated with various natural phenomena such as the sun, moon, earth, and water.

2. Animal worship: Animals were also worshipped during this period. Some of the popular animals that were worshipped included the cow, horse, and snake.

3. Ancestor worship: Ancestor worship was also prevalent during this period. The ancestors were believed to have the power to influence the lives of the living, and hence, they were worshipped.

4. Rituals and sacrifices: Rituals and sacrifices were an important aspect of the Pre-Vedic religion. The rituals were performed to appease the deities and seek their blessings.

5. Lack of a formal priesthood: There was no formal priesthood during this period. The rituals were performed by the head of the family or the village elder.
