### Seals as Cultural Heritage of India

Seals are small objects made of stone, terracotta, or metal that were used in ancient times to mark property or to authenticate documents. In India, seals have been an important part of the country's cultural heritage for thousands of years. Here are some important points about seals as cultural heritage of India:

1. The earliest known use of seals in India dates back to the Indus Valley Civilization, which existed from around 3300 BCE to 1300 BCE. Seals were used to mark goods and to authenticate trade transactions.

2. The seals of the Indus Valley Civilization were made of steatite, a type of soft stone that was easy to carve. They were usually square or rectangular in shape, and featured images of animals, plants, and human figures.

3. Many of the seals from the Indus Valley Civilization also featured a script that has yet to be deciphered. This script is known as the Indus script, and its meaning remains a mystery.

4. Seals continued to be used in India throughout the centuries that followed. In the Mauryan Empire, which existed from around 322 BCE to 185 BCE, seals were used to mark official documents and to authenticate royal decrees.

5. The seals of the Mauryan Empire were made of terracotta, and featured images of animals, plants, and deities. They were also inscribed with the name of the ruler and the date of issuance.

6. During the Gupta Empire, which existed from around 320 CE to 550 CE, seals were used as a means of identification. Merchants and traders would carry seals with their names or initials on them, which they would press into wax or clay to mark their goods.

7. The seals of the Gupta Empire were made of gold, silver, or copper, and featured images of deities, animals, and human figures. They were often inscribed with the owner's name or title.
