### Six System Indian Philosophy

The Six System Indian Philosophy, also known as Shad-Darsana, is a set of six major schools of Hindu philosophy that date back to ancient times. Each school has its own unique set of beliefs, principles, and practices, which have had a significant impact on Indian culture and spirituality. Here are the six schools of Indian philosophy along with a brief overview of their teachings:

1. Samkhya: Samkhya is one of the oldest schools of Indian philosophy and is based on the concept of duality. It believes that the universe is made up of two distinct entities - purusha (consciousness) and prakriti (matter). Samkhya also teaches that liberation can be achieved by detaching oneself from the material world and realizing the true nature of the self.

2. Yoga: Yoga is a school of philosophy that focuses on the practice of physical and mental discipline. It teaches that the path to liberation can be achieved through meditation, self-discipline, and the cultivation of wisdom. Yoga also emphasizes the importance of ethical behavior and the control of one's thoughts and emotions.

3. Nyaya: Nyaya is a school of philosophy that is concerned with logic and reasoning. It teaches that knowledge can be attained through a process of inference, analogy, and testimony. Nyaya also emphasizes the importance of ethical behavior and the pursuit of truth.

4. Vaisheshika: Vaisheshika is a school of philosophy that is focused on the nature of reality. It teaches that the universe is made up of nine distinct substances, including earth, water, air, and space. Vaisheshika also emphasizes the importance of ethical behavior and the pursuit of knowledge.

5. Mimamsa: Mimamsa is a school of philosophy that is concerned with the interpretation of the Vedas. It teaches that the Vedas are the ultimate source of knowledge and that they should be studied and interpreted in order to attain liberation. Mimamsa also emphasizes the importance of ethical behavior and the performance of rituals.

6. Vedanta: Vedanta is a school of philosophy that is based on the teachings of the Upanishads. It teaches that the ultimate reality is Brahman, and that the individual self (atman) is identical to Brahman. Vedanta also emphasizes the importance of ethical behavior and the pursuit of knowledge.
