#### Marriage in Society in Ancient India

Marriage was considered to be a sacred institution in ancient India. It was not just a union between two individuals but a union between two families. Here are some important points about marriage in society in ancient India:

1. Types of Marriages:
   - Brahma Marriage: This is the most superior of all types of marriages. It is performed when the parents of the bride and groom are of the same caste and social status. The groom pays a dowry to the bride's family.
   - Daiva Marriage: In this type of marriage, the bride's father gives her away as a gift to a priest as part of a religious ceremony.
   - Arsha Marriage: This type of marriage is performed when the groom pays a bride price to the bride's father. The bride's family is usually poor.
   - Prajapatya Marriage: This type of marriage is similar to the Brahma marriage, but the bride's family does not receive a dowry.
   - Gandharva Marriage: This is a love marriage where the bride and groom choose each other without any societal pressure or influence.

2. Age of Marriage:
   - Boys were allowed to marry after they had completed their education and had attained the age of 16. 
   - Girls were married off before they reached puberty, usually by the age of 10 or 11.

3. Role of Parents:
   - Parents played a significant role in the selection of the spouse for their children.
   - They looked for families of the same social status and caste.
   - The bride's family paid a dowry to the groom's family, which was used to support the couple and their future children.

4. Divorce:
   - Divorce was allowed, but only under certain circumstances such as infidelity, cruelty or impotence.
   - Women were allowed to initiate divorce proceedings.
