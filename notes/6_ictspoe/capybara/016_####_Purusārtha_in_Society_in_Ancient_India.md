#### Purusārtha in Society in Ancient India

Purusārtha is a Sanskrit term that means the four goals or aims of human life. The four purusārthas are:

1. Dharma: It means righteousness, duty, and morality. It is the first and foremost goal of human life. It includes fulfilling one's social, religious, and moral obligations.

2. Artha: It means material wealth and prosperity. It is the second goal of human life. It includes earning money, acquiring property, and fulfilling material desires.

3. Kama: It means pleasure and enjoyment. It is the third goal of human life. It includes experiencing pleasure, enjoying life, and fulfilling desires.

4. Moksha: It means liberation from the cycle of birth and death. It is the ultimate goal of human life. It includes attaining spiritual knowledge, realizing the true nature of the self, and achieving ultimate liberation.

Some Mnemonics and learning tricks for remembering the four purusārthas are:

- DAMK: Dharma, Artha, Moksha, Kama.
- MADS: Moksha, Artha, Dharma, Kama.
- DAKM: Dharma, Artha, Kama, Moksha.

In ancient India, these four purusārthas formed the basis of the society. The varna system, also known as the caste system, was based on the idea of fulfilling these purusārthas. The Brahmins were responsible for fulfilling the goal of dharma, the Kshatriyas were responsible for fulfilling the goal of artha, the Vaishyas were responsible for fulfilling the goal of kama, and the Shudras were responsible for serving the other three varnas.

The purusārthas also influenced the social and cultural practices of ancient India. For example, the practice of arranged marriages was based on the idea of fulfilling the purusārthas. The parents of the bride and groom would consider factors such as social status, financial stability, and compatibility to ensure that the marriage would fulfill the goals of artha, kama, and dharma.

In conclusion, the four purusārthas played a significant role in shaping the society and culture of ancient India. They provided a framework for individuals to fulfill their duties, achieve material prosperity, experience pleasure, and attain spiritual liberation.
