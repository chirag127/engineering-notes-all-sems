## Module 3- Indian Religion, Philosophy, and Practices

This module is all about Indian religion, philosophy, and practices. It covers a vast range of topics related to the Indian culture and tradition, which are deeply rooted in spirituality and philosophy. Here are the key points that you need to know about this module:

### Indian Religion

- India is known for its religious diversity, and it is home to many religions such as Hinduism, Buddhism, Jainism, Sikhism, and Islam.
- Hinduism is the oldest religion in India, and it is based on the Vedas, which are the oldest scriptures of Hinduism.
- Buddhism and Jainism emerged as a reaction to the rigid caste system of Hinduism.
- Sikhism was founded by Guru Nanak Dev in the 15th century.
- Islam came to India with the invasion of the Mughals in the 12th century.

### Indian Philosophy

- Indian philosophy is a rich and diverse field, and it covers various aspects of life such as metaphysics, ethics, epistemology, and logic.
- The six orthodox schools of Indian philosophy are Samkhya, Yoga, Nyaya, Vaisheshika, Mimamsa, and Vedanta.
- The four noble truths and the eightfold path are the core teachings of Buddhism.
- Jainism emphasizes non-violence, non-attachment, and the importance of self-control.
- Sikhism emphasizes the importance of meditation, selfless service, and the belief in one God.

### Indian Practices

- India is known for its various practices such as yoga, meditation, Ayurveda, and astrology.
- Yoga is an ancient practice that involves physical postures, breathing techniques, and meditation.
- Ayurveda is a system of traditional medicine that originated in India and is based on the belief that health and wellness depend on a delicate balance between the mind, body, and spirit.
- Astrology is a practice that involves the study of celestial bodies and their influence on human affairs.

## Mnemonics and Learning Tricks

- To remember the six orthodox schools of Indian philosophy, you can use the acronym "SNVMMV," which stands for Samkhya, Nyaya, Vaisheshika, Mimamsa, and Vedanta.
- To remember the core teachings of Buddhism, you can use the mnemonic "FBOG," which stands for the four noble truths and the eightfold path.
- To remember the core teachings of Jainism, you can use the mnemonic "NAV," which stands for non-violence, non-attachment, and self-control.
