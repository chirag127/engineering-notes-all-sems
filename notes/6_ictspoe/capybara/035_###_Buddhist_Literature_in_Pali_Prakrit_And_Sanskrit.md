### Buddhist Literature in Pali, Prakrit and Sanskrit

Buddhism is a religion that originated in ancient India and spread throughout Asia. The Buddhist literature is vast and diverse, written in various languages such as Pali, Prakrit and Sanskrit. In this section, we will discuss the Buddhist literature in Pali, Prakrit and Sanskrit.

#### Pali Literature

Pali is the language of the Theravada Buddhist tradition, which is prevalent in Sri Lanka, Myanmar and Thailand. Pali literature includes the Tipitaka, which is the collection of Buddhist scriptures. The Tipitaka is divided into three parts, the Vinaya Pitaka, the Sutta Pitaka, and the Abhidhamma Pitaka.

The Vinaya Pitaka contains rules and regulations for monks and nuns. The Sutta Pitaka consists of discourses given by the Buddha and his disciples. The Abhidhamma Pitaka contains philosophical and psychological teachings of Buddhism.

#### Prakrit Literature

Prakrit is a group of languages used in ancient India, which were predecessors to modern Indian languages. Prakrit literature is mostly oral and was transmitted through generations. The Jaina and Buddhist traditions both have Prakrit literature.

The Jaina texts were written in Ardhamagadhi, a Prakrit language. The Buddhist texts were written in various Prakrit languages such as Pali, Gandhari, and Ardhamagadhi. The Prakrit literature includes the Suttanipata, Therigatha, and Theragatha.

#### Sanskrit Literature

Sanskrit is an ancient Indo-Aryan language, which was widely used in ancient India. Sanskrit literature includes the Mahayana Buddhist texts, which are the sutras and shastras. These texts were written in Sanskrit and were influential in the development of Mahayana Buddhism.

The Mahayana texts include the Prajnaparamita Sutras, the Lotus Sutra, the Vimalakirti Sutra, and the Avatamsaka Sutra. These texts are highly philosophical and discuss the nature of reality, the nature of the self, and the nature of the Buddha.

#### Learning Tricks

To remember the different types of literature in Buddhism, one can use the mnemonic "PAS" which stands for Pali, Ardhamagadhi, and Sanskrit. Another trick is to remember the three parts of the Tipitaka by using the mnemonic "VAS" which stands for Vinaya, Sutta, and Abhidhamma.

In conclusion, the Buddhist literature in Pali, Prakrit and Sanskrit is vast and diverse. It includes the Tipitaka, Jaina texts, Mahayana texts, and many other scriptures. By using the learning tricks mentioned above, one can easily remember the different types of literature in Buddhism.
