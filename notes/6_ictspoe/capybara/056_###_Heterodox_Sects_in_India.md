### Heterodox Sects in India

Heterodox sects refer to those religious movements that have emerged in India, which do not conform to the mainstream Hindu traditions. These movements are usually associated with social reform, and they often challenge the orthodox Brahminical practices. Some of the notable heterodox sects in India are:

1. Buddhism: It is one of the major heterodox sects that emerged in India around the 6th century BCE. The founder of Buddhism was Gautama Buddha, who preached the Four Noble Truths and the Eightfold Path. The core teachings of Buddhism are based on the concept of impermanence, suffering, and the attainment of Nirvana.

2. Jainism: It is another major heterodox sect that originated in India around the 6th century BCE. The founder of Jainism was Mahavira, who preached the concept of non-violence, non-attachment, and the importance of self-discipline. Jains follow a strict vegetarian diet and practice asceticism.

3. Sikhism: It is a heterodox sect that emerged in India during the 15th century CE. The founder of Sikhism was Guru Nanak, who preached the concept of oneness of God, equality, and social justice. Sikhs follow the teachings of the Guru Granth Sahib and practice the five Ks, which include Kesh (uncut hair), Kangha (comb), Kara (steel bracelet), Kaccha (a type of undergarment), and Kirpan (a ceremonial sword).

4. Kabir Panth: It is a heterodox sect that originated in India during the 15th century CE. The founder of Kabir Panth was the poet-saint Kabir, who preached the concept of oneness of God, and the importance of love and devotion. The followers of Kabir Panth worship one God and do not believe in idol worship.

5. Nath Sampradaya: It is a heterodox sect that emerged in India during the 8th century CE. The founder of Nath Sampradaya was Gorakshanath, who preached the concept of Hatha Yoga, and the importance of physical and mental discipline. The followers of Nath Sampradaya follow a strict vegetarian diet, practice meditation, and believe in the power of inner transformation.
