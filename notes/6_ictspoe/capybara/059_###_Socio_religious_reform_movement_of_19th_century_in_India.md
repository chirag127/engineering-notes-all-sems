### Socio-Religious Reform Movement of 19th Century in India

The socio-religious reform movement of the 19th century in India was a significant period of social and cultural awakening in the country. The movement was aimed at bringing about a change in the social and religious traditions of India, which were considered backward and regressive.

Some of the key features of the socio-religious reform movement of the 19th century in India are:

1. The movement was led by a group of enlightened individuals who were educated and had a modern outlook towards life.

2. The movement was aimed at bringing about a change in the social and religious practices of India, which were considered backward and regressive.

3. The movement aimed at promoting social and religious reforms, such as the abolition of sati, child marriage, and the caste system.

4. The movement also aimed at promoting education, especially for women, and the spread of scientific knowledge.

5. The movement was not limited to one particular religion but was open to people of all faiths.

Some of the prominent leaders of the socio-religious reform movement of the 19th century in India were:

1. Raja Ram Mohan Roy - He was a social reformer who worked towards the abolition of sati and the promotion of women's education.

2. Swami Vivekananda - He was a spiritual leader who spread the message of Vedanta and worked towards the upliftment of the poor and downtrodden.

3. Dayanand Saraswati - He was the founder of the Arya Samaj, which aimed at promoting the principles of Vedas and worked towards the abolition of caste system.

4. Jyotirao Phule - He was a social reformer who worked towards the upliftment of the lower castes and women.
