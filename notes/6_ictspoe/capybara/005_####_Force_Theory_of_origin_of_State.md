#### Force Theory of Origin of State

The Force Theory of Origin of State is a political theory that explains the origin of the state as the result of the conquest and subjugation of weaker communities by a stronger one. It is also known as the "might makes right" theory of the origin of the state.

According to this theory, the state is formed when a group of people, possessing superior military power, conquer and subjugate another group of people. This conquering group then establishes a government, which is responsible for maintaining law and order, and protecting the interests of the ruling group.

Mnemonics and Learning Tricks:

There are no easy-to-remember mnemonics or learning tricks for the Force Theory of Origin of State. However, the following tips may help in understanding and remembering the theory:

- Think about the relationship between a conqueror and a conquered people. The conqueror is the stronger party, while the conquered people are weaker. The conqueror establishes its dominance over the conquered people, and becomes the ruling power.
- Consider historical examples where the Force Theory of Origin of State can be applied. For instance, the colonization of the Americas by European powers, or the conquest of India by the British Empire.

Advantages:

- The Force Theory of Origin of State provides a simple and straightforward explanation for the origin of the state.
- It highlights the role of power and dominance in the formation of the state.

Disadvantages:

- The theory overlooks other factors that may have contributed to the formation of the state, such as economic, social, and cultural factors.
- It suggests that the state is founded on violence and conquest, which may not be an acceptable moral or ethical basis for the state.

Examples:

- The conquest of the Aztec Empire by the Spanish in the 16th century.
- The colonization of Africa by European powers in the 19th century.

Applications:

- The Force Theory of Origin of State can be used to explain the formation of states in history, as well as modern conflicts and power dynamics between nations.
