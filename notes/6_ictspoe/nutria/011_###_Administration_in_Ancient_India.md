
### Administration in Ancient India

* Ancient India had a complex administrative system, with a number of levels of governance. 
* At the top of the hierarchy were the kings, who were responsible for the overall administration of the empire. 
* Under the kings were the various provincial governors, who were responsible for the day-to-day administration of their respective provinces. 
* At the local level, the district administrators were responsible for the administration of the various districts. 
* The village headmen were responsible for the administration of the villages. 
* The village headmen had the authority to collect taxes and maintain law and order in their respective villages.

* The administration in Ancient India was based on the principles of dharma, which was the moral and ethical code of conduct. 
* Dharma was based on the Vedas, which were the ancient Hindu scriptures. 
* Dharma was used to guide the kings and their officials in their duties. 
* The kings and their officials were expected to uphold the principles of dharma in their administration. 

* The administration in Ancient India was highly centralized and the king had absolute control over the entire empire. 
* The king had the authority to appoint and dismiss provincial governors and district administrators. 
* The king had the power to levy taxes and to maintain law and order. 
* The king was also responsible for the defense of the empire.

* To facilitate the administration, the kings of Ancient India developed a number of administrative systems. 
* One of the most important administrative systems was the Mauryan system of administration. 
* The Mauryan system of administration was based on the principles of decentralization and delegation of authority. 
* This system allowed the king to delegate authority to his provincial governors and district administrators. 

* Another important administrative system was the Gupta system of administration. 
* The Gupta system of administration was based on the principles of centralization and uniformity. 
* This system allowed the king to maintain control over the entire empire and to ensure uniformity in the administration. 

* To facilitate the administration, the kings of Ancient India also developed a number of mnemonics and learning tricks. 
* One of the most important mnemonics was the ‘six fold division’, which was used to classify the various administrative functions. 
* This mnemonic divided the administrative functions into six categories: military, revenue, judicial, religious, public works and miscellaneous. 
* This mnemonic was used to help the administrators remember and understand the various administrative functions. 

* Another important mnemonic was the ‘eight fold division’, which was used to classify the various administrative departments. 
* This mnemonic divided the administrative departments into eight categories: military, revenue, judicial, religious, public works, finance, intelligence and miscellaneous. 
* This mnemonic was used to help the administrators remember and understand the various administrative departments.
