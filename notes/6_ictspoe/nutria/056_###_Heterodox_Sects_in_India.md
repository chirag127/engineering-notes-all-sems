
### Heterodox Sects in India

- Hinduism is a religion with a wide variety of sects and beliefs. Among the many sects, there are six major heterodox sects in India. These sects are Jainism, Buddhism, Charvaka, Ajivika, Sikhism and Lingayatism.

- Jainism is an ancient Indian religion that emphasizes non-violence and spiritual liberation. It is based on the teachings of Mahavira, a contemporary of the Buddha. Jains believe in the existence of multiple souls and the need to practice non-violence and austerity to attain liberation.

- Buddhism is a religion based on the teachings of the Buddha. It is a path of spiritual development and enlightenment through the practice of meditation and ethical living. Buddhists believe in the Four Noble Truths and the Eightfold Path as a way to end suffering and attain enlightenment.

- Charvaka is an atheistic school of thought that believes in the denial of the existence of God and the afterlife. It is based on the teachings of the ancient Hindu philosopher, Brihaspati. Charvaka believes in the pursuit of pleasure and the avoidance of pain as the ultimate goal of life.

- Ajivika is a sect of Indian philosophy that believes in the concept of karma and reincarnation. It is based on the teachings of the ancient Indian philosopher, Makkhali Gosala. Ajivikas believe that life is predetermined by fate and that it is impossible to alter one's destiny.

- Sikhism is a monotheistic religion founded by Guru Nanak in the 15th century in the Punjab region of India. It is based on the teachings of the ten Sikh gurus and the holy scripture, the Guru Granth Sahib. Sikhism emphasizes the importance of living a life of service and devotion to God.

- Lingayatism is an independent religious movement founded by Basava in the 12th century in the Karnataka region of India. It is based on the teachings of the five Lingayat gurus and the holy scripture, the Vachana Sahitya. Lingayatism emphasizes the importance of living a life of devotion to the divine.
