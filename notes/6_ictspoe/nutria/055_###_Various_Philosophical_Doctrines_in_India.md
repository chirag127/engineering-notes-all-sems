
### Various Philosophical Doctrines in India 

1. **Vedanta**: This is a school of thought that is based on the Upanishads, a set of ancient Hindu scriptures. It is one of the six major orthodox schools of Hindu philosophy, and it focuses on the concept of Brahman (the ultimate reality) and its relationship to Atman (the individual soul). The main goal of Vedanta is to attain Moksha (liberation). 

2. **Samkhya**: This is one of the oldest philosophical systems in India, and it is based on the dualism of Purusha (the conscious self) and Prakriti (the material world). It is a non-theistic system, and it focuses on the concept of evolution and the liberation of the individual.

3. **Yoga**: This is a spiritual discipline that focuses on the physical, mental, and spiritual aspects of life. It is based on the teachings of Patanjali, and it focuses on the practice of meditation and the attainment of enlightenment.

4. **Vaisheshika**: This is a school of thought that is based on the teachings of Kanada. It is an atomistic system, and it focuses on the concept of causation and the relationship between the elements and the physical world.

5. **Nyaya**: This is a school of thought that is based on the teachings of Gautama. It is a rationalistic system, and it focuses on the concept of inference and the relationship between the cause and the effect.

6. **Mimamsa**: This is a school of thought that is based on the teachings of Jaimini. It is a ritualistic system, and it focuses on the performance of rituals and the attainment of spiritual liberation.

7. **Vishishtadvaita**: This is a school of thought that is based on the teachings of Ramanuja. It is a qualified monism system, and it focuses on the concept of unity and the relationship between the individual soul and the ultimate reality.
