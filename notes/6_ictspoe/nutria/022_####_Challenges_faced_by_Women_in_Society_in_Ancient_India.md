
### Challenges Faced by Women in Society in Ancient India

1. Limited Access to Education: In ancient India, women were not allowed to attend school or receive any kind of formal education. This severely limited their career prospects, as they were not allowed to pursue higher education or be employed in certain professions. 
2. Limited Mobility: Women had limited mobility, as they were not allowed to leave their homes without permission from their husbands or male guardians. This meant that they were not able to travel freely and had to depend on their male counterparts for transportation.
3. Lack of Legal Rights: Women in ancient India had limited legal rights and were often not allowed to own property or make decisions about their own lives. They were also not allowed to testify in court, which meant that they were unable to seek justice if they were wronged.
4. Patriarchal Society: Women were expected to obey their husbands and fathers and were not allowed to make their own decisions or have any kind of autonomy. This created a patriarchal society in which women were seen as inferior to men and were not given any kind of respect or recognition.
5. Discrimination: Women were often discriminated against and treated as second-class citizens. This meant that they were not given the same opportunities as men and were not allowed to participate in politics or other areas of public life.
