
### The Seven Limbs of the State in Ancient India
The seven limbs of the state in ancient India were the seven components of a state or kingdom, as described by the ancient Indian scholar Kautilya in his famous work, the Arthashastra. The seven limbs of the state are:

1. **King**: The king was the head of the state and was responsible for the protection of his people and the administration of justice.
2. **Ministers**: The ministers were the king's advisors and were responsible for advising the king on matters of state.
3. **Army**: The army was responsible for defending the kingdom and protecting its citizens.
4. **Treasury**: The treasury was responsible for the collection of taxes and other revenues, as well as the management of the state's finances.
5. **Law**: The law was responsible for the administration of justice and the enforcement of laws.
6. **Adjudicators**: The adjudicators were responsible for settling disputes between citizens and ensuring that justice was served.
7. **People**: The people were the citizens of the state and were responsible for upholding the law and paying taxes.

To remember these seven limbs of the state, one can use the mnemonic "KMTALP", which stands for King, Ministers, Treasury, Army, Law, Adjudicators and People.
