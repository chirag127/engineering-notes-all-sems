
#### Āshrama or the Stages of Life in Society in Ancient India

The āshrama system is an ancient Indian social system that outlines the stages of life in Hindu society. It consists of four stages: Brahmacharya (studenthood), Grihastha (householder), Vanaprastha (retirement) and Sannyasa (renunciation of worldly life). 

1. **Brahmacharya (Studenthood)**: This is the first stage of life, where the individual is expected to be a student and pursue knowledge. During this period, the individual is expected to be celibate and live in a gurukul (school) environment.

2. **Grihastha (Householder)**: This is the second stage of life, where the individual is expected to marry and start a family. During this period, the individual is expected to be a responsible head of the household and fulfill his/her duties towards the family.

3. **Vanaprastha (Retirement)**: This is the third stage of life, where the individual is expected to retire from the household and move to the forest to pursue spiritual knowledge. During this period, the individual is expected to live a simple life and devote more time to spiritual pursuits.

4. **Sannyasa (Renunciation of Worldly Life)**: This is the fourth and final stage of life, where the individual is expected to renounce all worldly attachments and pursue a life of spiritual contemplation. During this period, the individual is expected to live a life of complete detachment from the world and devote all his/her time to spiritual pursuits.

Mnemonic for the four stages of life:

B- Be a student
G- Get married
V- Venture into the forest
S- Seek spiritual knowledge
