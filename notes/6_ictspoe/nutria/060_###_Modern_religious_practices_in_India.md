
### Modern religious practices in India

Modern religious practices in India are varied and reflect the diversity of Indian culture. Hinduism is the dominant religion in the country, with over 80% of the population identifying as Hindus. Buddhism, Jainism, Islam, Christianity, Sikhism and other indigenous faiths are also practiced in India. 

The modern practice of Hinduism is largely based on the Vedic scriptures, which are the oldest scriptures in the world. The Vedic scriptures include the Upanishads, the Bhagavad Gita, and the Vedas. These scriptures contain the teachings of the ancient sages and are the basis for many of the modern practices of Hinduism.

The modern practice of Buddhism is largely based on the teachings of the historical Buddha, Siddhartha Gautama. Buddhism is based on the Four Noble Truths and the Eightfold Path, which are the core teachings of the Buddha. Buddhism also emphasizes the importance of meditation, mindfulness, and compassion.

Jainism is an ancient Indian religion that emphasizes non-violence and the pursuit of spiritual liberation. The main teachings of Jainism are the five vows of non-violence, truthfulness, non-stealing, celibacy, and non-attachment. Jainism also emphasizes the importance of ahimsa (non-violence) and the practice of meditation.

Islam is the second-largest religion in India and is based on the teachings of the prophet Muhammad. The core teachings of Islam are the Five Pillars, which include belief in one God, prayer, charity, fasting, and pilgrimage to Mecca.

Christianity is the third-largest religion in India and is based on the teachings of Jesus Christ. The core teachings of Christianity are the Ten Commandments and the Sermon on the Mount.

Sikhism is a monotheistic religion founded in the 15th century in India. The main teachings of Sikhism are the Five Ks, which include keeping uncut hair, carrying a small sword, wearing a bracelet, carrying a comb, and wearing a special turban. 

In addition to the major religions, there are many indigenous faiths practiced in India. These include tribal faiths, animism, and shamanism. Each of these faiths has its own unique set of beliefs and practices. 

In general, religious practices in India are very diverse and reflect the rich cultural heritage of the country. Many of the modern religious practices are based on ancient traditions and beliefs, and are still practiced today.
