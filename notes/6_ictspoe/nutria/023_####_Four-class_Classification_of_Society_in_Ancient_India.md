
#### Four-class Classification of Society in Ancient India

The four-class classification of society in ancient India is a system of social stratification that was prevalent in the Indian subcontinent during the Vedic period. This system was based on the occupation of the people and divided the society into four classes: Brahmin, Kshatriya, Vaishya, and Shudra.

* **Brahmin**: The Brahmin class was the highest class in the four-class system and included priests and teachers. The Brahmin class was responsible for performing religious ceremonies and teaching the Vedas.

* **Kshatriya**: The Kshatriya class was the second highest class in the four-class system and included warriors and rulers. The Kshatriya class was responsible for protecting the kingdom and leading the army into battle.

* **Vaishya**: The Vaishya class was the third highest class in the four-class system and included merchants and farmers. The Vaishya class was responsible for trading goods and providing food for the kingdom.

* **Shudra**: The Shudra class was the lowest class in the four-class system and included laborers and servants. The Shudra class was responsible for providing manual labor and services to the other classes.

Mnemonic: B-K-V-S (Brahmin, Kshatriya, Vaishya, Shudra)
