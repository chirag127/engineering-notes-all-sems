
## Module 3- Indian Religion, Philosophy, and Practices

1. Hinduism: Hinduism is the oldest religion in India and is based on Vedic literature. It is polytheistic and believes in the existence of multiple gods and goddesses, as well as reincarnation and karma.
2. Buddhism: Buddhism was founded by Siddhartha Gautama and is based on the Four Noble Truths and the Eightfold Path. It is a non-theistic religion that emphasizes the importance of meditation and mindfulness.
3. Jainism: Jainism is an ancient Indian religion that focuses on non-violence and self-control. It is based on the teachings of Mahavira and emphasizes the importance of ahimsa (non-violence) and aparigraha (non-possession).
4. Sikhism: Sikhism is a monotheistic religion that was founded in the 15th century by Guru Nanak. It is based on the teachings of the ten Sikh Gurus and emphasizes the importance of selfless service and equality.
5. Zoroastrianism: Zoroastrianism is an ancient religion that is based on the teachings of the prophet Zoroaster. It is a monotheistic religion that believes in the existence of one God and emphasizes the importance of good thoughts, good words, and good deeds.
6. Animism: Animism is the belief that all living things have a spirit or soul. It is an ancient belief system that is still practiced in some parts of India.
7. Sufism: Sufism is an Islamic mystical tradition that emphasizes the importance of inner spiritual growth. It is based on the teachings of the Sufi saints and emphasizes the importance of love, devotion, and self-reflection.
8. Tribal Religions: Tribal religions are practiced by many tribal communities in India and are based on animistic beliefs. They emphasize the importance of nature and the spiritual connection between humans and the environment.
