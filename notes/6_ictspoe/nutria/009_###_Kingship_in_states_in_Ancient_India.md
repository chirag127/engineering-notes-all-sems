
### Kingship in states in Ancient India

1. The kingship in ancient India was based on the concept of the divine right of kings. This was the belief that the king was chosen by the gods and was therefore divinely appointed to rule. 
2. The king was seen as the protector of his people and was responsible for their welfare. He had the power to declare war, make laws, and administer justice. 
3. The king was also responsible for maintaining order and ensuring the safety of his kingdom. He was expected to uphold the laws of the land and protect his people from harm. 
4. The kingship in ancient India was based on the caste system. The king was the highest caste and all other castes were subordinate to him. 
5. The king was responsible for the maintenance of the social order and the administration of justice. He had the power to punish those who disobeyed his laws and to reward those who followed them. 
6. The kingship in ancient India was also based on the concept of dharma. Dharma is the law of righteousness and justice, and the king was responsible for upholding it. 
7. The kingship in ancient India was also based on the concept of karma. Karma is the law of cause and effect, and the king was responsible for ensuring that justice was served. 
8. The kingship in ancient India was also based on the concept of moksha. Moksha is the liberation from the cycle of life and death, and the king was responsible for helping his people achieve this. 
9. The kingship in ancient India was also based on the concept of dharma-raja. Dharma-raja is the king who rules according to the laws of dharma. 
10. The kingship in ancient India was also based on the concept of raja-dharma. Raja-dharma is the duty of the king to protect his people and to ensure justice and fairness.
