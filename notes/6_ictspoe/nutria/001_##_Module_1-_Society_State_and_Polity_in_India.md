

## Module 1 - Society State and Polity in India

* India is a union of 29 states and seven union territories, each with its own distinct culture, language, and history. 
* The Indian Constitution, which came into effect on 26 January 1950, is the supreme law of India. It lays down the framework defining fundamental political principles, establishes the structure, procedures, powers, and duties of government institutions, and sets out fundamental rights, directive principles, and the duties of citizens. 
* The Indian Constitution is the longest written constitution in the world, containing 444 articles in 22 parts, 12 schedules, and 118 amendments. 
* The Indian Constitution defines the power of the central government and the state governments. The central government has exclusive power to make laws on subjects listed in the Union List, while the state governments have exclusive power to make laws on subjects listed in the State List. 
* The Indian Parliament is bicameral, consisting of the Lok Sabha (the lower house) and the Rajya Sabha (the upper house). The Lok Sabha is composed of representatives of the people chosen by direct election on the basis of universal adult suffrage. 
* The Rajya Sabha is composed of representatives of the states chosen by indirect election. The President of India is the head of state and the Prime Minister is the head of government. 
* India is a federal republic, with a parliamentary system of government. The executive branch is headed by the President, who is elected by the electoral college for a five-year term. 
* The judicial branch is headed by the Supreme Court, which is the highest court of appeal in India. The Supreme Court has the power to declare any law or executive action unconstitutional. 
* India is a secular state, with no state religion. The Constitution guarantees freedom of religion to its citizens and prohibits discrimination on the basis of religion. 
* The Indian government is divided into three branches: executive, legislative, and judicial. The executive branch is headed by the President, who is elected by the electoral college for a five-year term. The legislative branch is composed of the Lok Sabha and the Rajya Sabha. The judicial branch is headed by the Supreme Court. 
* The Indian Constitution guarantees fundamental rights to its citizens, including the right to equality, freedom of speech and expression, freedom of assembly, freedom of religion, and the right to constitutional remedies. 
* The Indian Constitution also provides for directive principles of state policy, which are meant to guide the government in formulating laws and policies. 
* In addition, the Indian Constitution also provides for the protection of certain fundamental rights, such as the right to property, the right to education, and the right to life and personal liberty. 
* The Indian Constitution also provides for the establishment of a federal structure of government, with the central government having exclusive power to make laws on subjects listed in the Union List, and the state governments having exclusive power to make laws on subjects listed in the State List. 
* The Indian Constitution also provides for the establishment of a three-tier system of government, with the Union government at the centre, the state governments at the state level, and the local governments at the district and village levels. 
* The Indian Constitution also provides for the establishment of a system of checks and balances, whereby the powers of the central and state governments are limited and balanced against each other. 
* Finally, the Indian Constitution also provides for the establishment of a system of judicial review, whereby the Supreme Court has the power to declare any law or executive action unconstitutional.
