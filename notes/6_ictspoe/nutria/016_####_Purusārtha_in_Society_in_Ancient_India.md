

#### Purusārtha in Society in Ancient India

Purusārtha is a Sanskrit term meaning “aims of human life”. It is a concept found in Hinduism and refers to the four goals of life that one should strive to achieve in order to lead a fulfilling existence. These four goals are: 

1. Dharma (righteousness, morality, and duty)
2. Artha (prosperity, wealth, and security)
3. Kama (pleasure, desire, and passion)
4. Moksha (liberation, freedom, and self-realization).

In ancient Indian society, these four goals were believed to be essential for a person to lead a meaningful life. Dharma was seen as the most important of the four goals, as it was believed to provide the foundation for the other three. Dharma was seen as the path of righteousness, morality, and duty, and it was believed that the performance of one's duties would lead to the fulfillment of the other three goals. 

Artha was seen as the goal of achieving prosperity, wealth, and security. It was believed that by achieving financial stability and security, a person would be able to pursue the other three goals. Kama was seen as the goal of pursuing pleasure, desire, and passion. It was believed that by fulfilling one's desires, a person could lead a more fulfilling life. Moksha was seen as the goal of achieving liberation, freedom, and self-realization. It was believed that by achieving spiritual freedom, a person could lead a life of true happiness and fulfillment.

A useful mnemonic for remembering the four Purusārthas is "D-A-K-M", which stands for Dharma, Artha, Kama, and Moksha. This mnemonic is easy to remember and can be a helpful tool for recalling the four goals of life.
