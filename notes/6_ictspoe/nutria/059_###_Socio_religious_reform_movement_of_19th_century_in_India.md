
### Socio religious reform movement of 19th century in India

The 19th century in India saw a number of socio-religious reform movements, which sought to reform the existing religious, social, and political systems. The main aim of these movements was to bring about a more equitable society and to eliminate the oppressive practices of the caste system.

Some of the prominent reformers of the 19th century include Raja Ram Mohan Roy, Swami Vivekananda, Jyotirao Phule, and Ishwar Chandra Vidyasagar.

**Raja Ram Mohan Roy**

Raja Ram Mohan Roy (1772-1833) was a social reformer, philosopher, and religious leader who is often regarded as the "Father of the Indian Renaissance". He was a strong advocate of monotheism and was a proponent of the idea that all religions should be respected. He was also a strong advocate of women's rights and of the elimination of the practice of sati.

**Swami Vivekananda**

Swami Vivekananda (1863-1902) was a spiritual leader and a proponent of Vedanta philosophy. He was a strong advocate of social and religious reform and was a major influence on the Indian independence movement. He was also an advocate of education and was a proponent of the idea that education should be accessible to all.

**Jyotirao Phule**

Jyotirao Phule (1827-1890) was a social reformer and educator who is regarded as the "Father of Indian Social Revolution". He was a strong advocate of women's rights and was a proponent of the idea that all people, regardless of their caste or gender, should have access to education. He was also a strong critic of the caste system and sought to eliminate the oppressive practices associated with it.

**Ishwar Chandra Vidyasagar**

Ishwar Chandra Vidyasagar (1820-1891) was a social reformer, educator, and philanthropist who is regarded as the "Father of Bengali Renaissance". He was a strong advocate of women's rights and was a proponent of the idea that all people, regardless of their caste or gender, should have access to education. He was also a strong critic of the practice of child marriage and sought to eliminate it.

These reformers sought to bring about social, religious, and political reform in India. They believed in the equality of all people and sought to eliminate oppressive practices such as the caste system and child marriage. They also sought to bring about greater access to education for all people and to promote the idea of a more equitable society.
