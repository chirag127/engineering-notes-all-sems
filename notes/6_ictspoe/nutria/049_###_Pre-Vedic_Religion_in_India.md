
### Pre-Vedic Religion in India

- Pre-Vedic religion in India refers to the religious beliefs and practices of the people who inhabited India prior to the arrival of the Vedic culture. 
- Pre-Vedic religion is believed to have been polytheistic, with gods and goddesses associated with natural phenomena such as the sun, moon, and rivers. 
- The main deities of the Pre-Vedic religion were Indra, Agni, Varuna, and Ushas. 
- There was also a belief in the power of magic and ritual. 
- Animal sacrifice was a common practice, and sacrifices of food and drink were made to the gods. 
- The Pre-Vedic people also believed in the afterlife, and in the transmigration of the soul. 
- They also believed in the concept of karma, which stated that the actions of a person in this life would determine their fate in the next. 
- The Pre-Vedic religion had no formal structure, and there were no written scriptures or rituals. 
- Instead, the beliefs and practices were passed down orally from generation to generation. 
- Pre-Vedic religion was replaced by the Vedic religion, which was brought to India by the Aryans in the second millennium BCE.
