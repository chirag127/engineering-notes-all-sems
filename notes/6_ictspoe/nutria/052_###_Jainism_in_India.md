
### Jainism in India

1. Jainism is an ancient Indian religion that has been practiced in India since the 6th century BCE. It is based on the teachings of 24 spiritual leaders, known as Tirthankaras. 
2. Jains believe in the concept of ahimsa (non-violence) and emphasize non-violence towards all living beings.
3. Jains practice asceticism, which involves fasting, meditation, and other forms of self-discipline. 
4. Jainism is a transtheistic religion, meaning that it does not have a single God. Instead, Jains believe in multiple gods and goddesses. 
5. Jains are divided into two major sects: Digambara and Shvetambara. Digambara Jains believe that the soul is liberated from the body after death and that the body should not be harmed. Shvetambara Jains believe that the soul can be liberated from the body during life. 
6. Jains practice five major vows: ahimsa (non-violence), satya (truthfulness), asteya (non-stealing), brahmacharya (celibacy or chastity), and aparigraha (non-possessiveness). 
7. Jains believe in karma and the cycle of birth and death. They believe that one’s actions in life will determine their fate in their next life. 
8. Jains have a strong commitment to vegetarianism, as they believe that all living beings should be respected and not harmed. 
9. Jains have a strong emphasis on education and learning. They value knowledge and learning and believe that it is essential for spiritual growth. 
10. Jainism is practiced by about 4 million people in India, mainly in the states of Gujarat, Rajasthan, and Karnataka.
