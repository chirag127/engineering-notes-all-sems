
### Shankaracharya and Advaita Vedanta

Advaita Vedanta is a school of Hindu philosophy that was founded by the 8th-century Indian philosopher Adi Shankaracharya. It is based on the Upanishads and the Brahma Sutras, and is one of the most influential systems of thought in Hinduism. Advaita Vedanta teaches that the true nature of reality is non-dualism, and that the only way to achieve liberation from suffering is to realize this truth.

Adi Shankaracharya was born in Kerala in the 8th century CE. He is credited with reviving the Advaita Vedanta school of thought and spreading its teachings throughout India. He wrote numerous commentaries on the Upanishads and Brahma Sutras, and is considered one of the most influential philosophers in Hinduism.

The main teachings of Advaita Vedanta are that the ultimate reality is non-dual, and that the individual soul (Atman) is one with the Supreme Soul (Brahman). The goal of Advaita Vedanta is to achieve liberation from suffering through the realization of this truth.

Advaita Vedanta is often studied using mnemonic devices and learning tricks. For example, the Sanskrit term for non-duality is "Advaita," which can be broken down into "a" (not) + "dvaita" (dual). This helps to remember that Advaita Vedanta teaches that the ultimate reality is non-dual. Other mnemonic devices can also be used to remember the various teachings of Advaita Vedanta.
