### Virtual environments

- A virtual environment is a networked application that allows a user to interact with both the computing environment and the work of other users .
- A virtual environment can be used for communication, collaboration, education, entertainment, simulation, and training purposes.
- A virtual environment can be classified into two types: immersive and non-immersive.
  - Immersive virtual environments use specialized hardware and software to create a sense of presence and immersion for the user, such as head-mounted displays, gloves, trackers, and haptic devices.
  - Non-immersive virtual environments use conventional devices such as desktop computers, monitors, keyboards, and mice to present a virtual world to the user, such as web browsers, email, and chat applications.
- A virtual environment can also be distinguished by the degree of realism, interactivity, and social presence it offers.
  - Realism refers to how closely the virtual environment resembles the real world in terms of visual, auditory, tactile, and olfactory cues.
  - Interactivity refers to how responsive the virtual environment is to the user's actions and inputs, such as navigation, manipulation, and feedback.
  - Social presence refers to how the virtual environment supports social interaction and communication among users, such as avatars, gestures, and voice chat.
- A virtual environment can be created using various technologies and tools, such as virtualization, rendering, modeling, animation, and scripting.
  - Virtualization creates a simulated, or virtual, computing environment as opposed to a physical environment, such as virtual machines, containers, and hypervisors.
  - Rendering generates realistic images and sounds from a virtual environment, such as ray tracing, rasterization, and audio synthesis.
  - Modeling defines the shape, structure, and appearance of the objects and scenes in a virtual environment, such as polygons, textures, and materials.
  - Animation creates the motion and behavior of the objects and characters in a virtual environment, such as keyframes, physics, and artificial intelligence.
  - Scripting controls the logic and events in a virtual environment, such as variables, functions, and loops.
