# Wireless Displays in Educational Augmented Reality Applications

- Wireless displays are output devices that can receive and display wirelessly streamed AR content in real time.
- Wireless displays can be stereoscopic head mounted displays (HMDs) or TFT display modules that have been adapted to act as wireless receivers.
- Wireless displays have advantages for educational AR applications, such as:
  - Enabling collaboration and communication among multiple users who can share or access personalized views or context sensitive information.
  - Reducing the number of cables and wires that can pose usability problems, such as tangling, stumbling, restricting movement and interaction, and limiting the number of displays/users.
  - Increasing the mobility and flexibility of users who can move freely and interact with the AR environment without being tethered to a single PC or power source.
- Two examples of educational AR applications that use wireless displays are:
  - Construct3D: a collaborative, constructive geometry application that allows users to create and manipulate 3D geometric objects in a shared AR space using HMDs and a tracked stylus.
  - ARMath: a collaborative, arithmetic application that allows users to solve math problems in a shared AR space using HMDs and a tracked pen.
- Some teaching scenarios that benefit from the use of wireless displays are:
  - Teachers can monitor and guide students' progress and performance by switching between different views of the AR environment on their wireless display.
  - Students can work in groups or pairs and exchange their views or information by swapping or sharing their wireless displays.
  - Students can explore and interact with the AR environment from different perspectives and locations by moving around with their wireless displays.
  - Teachers can demonstrate and explain concepts or procedures by projecting their wireless display onto a large screen or wall for the whole class to see.
