### Evaluating Wayfinding Aids for 3D Interaction Techniques

- Wayfinding aids (WATs) are tools or techniques that help users navigate in 3D virtual environments (VEs) by providing spatial information or guidance.
- Evaluating WATs is important to understand their impact on user performance, experience, and preference in 3D VEs.
- Some common methods for evaluating WATs are:
  - Experimental studies: Comparing different WATs or WATs with no aid in terms of objective measures (e.g., task completion time, accuracy, errors) and subjective measures (e.g., user satisfaction, preference, workload) in controlled settings.
  - User studies: Collecting user feedback, opinions, or ratings on WATs using questionnaires, interviews, or focus groups in naturalistic settings.
  - Usability testing: Observing and analyzing user behavior, interaction, and problems with WATs using methods such as think-aloud, video recording, or eye tracking in realistic settings.
- Some common WATs for 3D VEs are:
  - Maps: Providing an overview of the VE layout, landmarks, or routes on a separate or embedded window.
  - Compasses: Indicating the direction of the user's orientation, destination, or landmarks on a separate or embedded window.
  - Landmarks: Using distinctive or salient objects or features in the VE to facilitate orientation, recognition, or memory.
  - Signs: Using textual or graphical labels or arrows in the VE to indicate directions, distances, or names of locations or objects.
- Some common factors that affect the effectiveness, efficiency, and satisfaction of WATs are:
  - User characteristics: Such as spatial ability, prior knowledge, or preference.
  - Task characteristics: Such as complexity, difficulty, or goal.
  - VE characteristics: Such as size, structure, or realism.
  - WAT characteristics: Such as design, presentation, or interaction.
