### Haptic Displays

Haptic displays are interfaces that provide users with a sense of touch by stimulating somatic receptors in the skin or muscles. Haptic displays can enhance the realism and interactivity of virtual environments, as well as provide feedback for remote control of machines and devices.

There are two main types of haptic feedback: cutaneous and kinesthetic. Cutaneous feedback refers to the stimulation of the skin receptors, such as pressure, vibration, temperature, and pain. Kinesthetic feedback refers to the stimulation of the muscle receptors, such as force, position, and movement.

Haptic displays can be classified by the type of output they generate, and the type of receptors they stimulate. Some examples of haptic displays are:

- Force feedback devices: These devices apply forces to the user's hand or body, such as joysticks, gloves, exoskeletons, or haptic vests. They can render kinesthetic feedback based on computational models, remote interaction, recordings, or data-driven approaches.
- Tactile displays: These devices stimulate the skin receptors of the user, such as pins, vibrators, or thermal elements. They can render cutaneous feedback based on textures, shapes, or patterns.
- Holographic haptic displays: These devices use ultrasound waves to create a sensation of touch in mid-air, without the need for a physical device or interface. They can render both cutaneous and kinesthetic feedback based on virtual objects or gestures.

Haptic displays can be used for various applications, such as:

- Education and training: Haptic displays can provide realistic and immersive learning experiences, such as simulating surgical procedures, exploring molecular structures, or practicing musical instruments.
- Entertainment and gaming: Haptic displays can enhance the enjoyment and engagement of users, such as feeling the recoil of a gun, the impact of a punch, or the texture of a virtual object.
- Communication and social interaction: Haptic displays can convey emotions and expressions, such as hugging, shaking hands, or kissing.
- Accessibility and assistance: Haptic displays can assist users with visual or auditory impairments, such as reading Braille, navigating environments, or recognizing sounds.
