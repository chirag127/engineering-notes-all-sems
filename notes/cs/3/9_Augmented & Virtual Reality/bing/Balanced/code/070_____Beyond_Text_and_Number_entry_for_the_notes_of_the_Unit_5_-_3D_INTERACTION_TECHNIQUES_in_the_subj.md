### Beyond Text and Number Entry for 3D Interaction Techniques

- Text and number entry are common tasks in many interactive applications, but they pose challenges in 3D environments, such as virtual reality (VR) and augmented reality (AR).
- Traditional input devices, such as keyboards and mice, are not well suited for 3D interaction, as they require a physical surface and constrain the user's movement and orientation.
- Alternative input methods, such as speech, gestures, and eye tracking, have been explored for 3D interaction, but they have limitations in terms of accuracy, efficiency, privacy, and social acceptability.
- Therefore, researchers have proposed and evaluated various 3D interaction techniques for text and number entry, which can be classified into four categories:
  - Surface-based techniques: These techniques use a virtual or physical surface, such as a tablet, a wall, or a floor, to display a keyboard or a keypad, and allow the user to select characters using a pointer, a finger, or a hand.
  - Tangible techniques: These techniques use a physical object, such as a cube, a ring, or a glove, to represent a keyboard or a keypad, and allow the user to manipulate the object or press buttons to enter text or numbers.
  - Gesture-based techniques: These techniques use hand or finger gestures, such as swiping, tapping, or drawing, to enter text or numbers, either in mid-air or on a virtual surface.
  - Eye-tracking techniques: These techniques use eye movements, such as gaze, blinks, or saccades, to enter text or numbers, either by selecting characters on a virtual keyboard or by spelling words using a gaze-based alphabet.
- Each category of techniques has its own advantages and disadvantages, depending on the context, the task, and the user preferences. Some factors that affect the performance and usability of these techniques are :
  - The size and layout of the keyboard or keypad: Larger and more familiar layouts tend to improve accuracy and speed, but they may also increase fatigue and occlusion.
  - The feedback and feedforward mechanisms: Auditory, visual, and haptic feedback and feedforward can help the user to confirm and anticipate their actions, but they may also introduce noise and distraction.
  - The selection and confirmation methods: Different methods, such as dwell time, button press, or gesture, can affect the ease and accuracy of selecting characters, but they may also introduce errors and delays.
  - The error correction and prediction methods: Methods such as backspace, undo, or word completion can help the user to correct and speed up their text or number entry, but they may also introduce confusion and frustration.
- In summary, text and number entry in 3D environments is a challenging and active research area, which requires careful consideration of the design space and evaluation methods. Future research directions may include exploring novel input modalities, such as brain-computer interfaces, combining multiple input methods, such as speech and gesture, and adapting the input methods to the user's context, task, and preferences.
