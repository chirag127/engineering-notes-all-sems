### Hardware Controls for Augmented and Virtual Reality

- Hardware controls are devices or components that enable users to interact with augmented and virtual reality applications.
- Hardware controls can be classified into three categories: input, output, and tracking.
- Input controls are devices that allow users to provide commands, gestures, or actions to the application. Examples of input controls are:
  - Controllers: handheld devices that have buttons, triggers, joysticks, or touchpads. Controllers can also have motion sensors or haptic feedback. Examples of controllers are the Oculus Touch, the HTC Vive Wand, and the PlayStation Move.
  - Gloves: wearable devices that track the movements and gestures of the user's hands and fingers. Gloves can also provide haptic feedback or tactile sensations. Examples of gloves are the Manus VR, the HaptX, and the Sensoryx VRfree.
  - Keyboards, mice, and gamepads: traditional input devices that can be used for some VR and AR applications, especially on PC or mobile platforms. These devices are usually less immersive and intuitive than controllers or gloves.
- Output controls are devices that display or project the VR or AR content to the user. Examples of output controls are:
  - Head-mounted displays (HMDs): wearable devices that cover the user's eyes and show stereoscopic images or videos. HMDs can also have built-in speakers, microphones, or cameras. Examples of HMDs are the Oculus Rift, the HTC Vive, and the PlayStation VR.
  - Smart glasses: wearable devices that look like regular glasses but have transparent lenses that overlay digital information or graphics on the user's view of the real world. Smart glasses can also have cameras, speakers, or microphones. Examples of smart glasses are the Google Glass, the Microsoft HoloLens, and the Magic Leap One.
  - Smartphones and tablets: mobile devices that can run VR or AR applications using their screens, cameras, speakers, or sensors. These devices can also be used with accessories such as cardboard viewers, lenses, or controllers. Examples of smartphones and tablets are the iPhone, the Samsung Galaxy, and the iPad.
- Tracking controls are devices or systems that measure the position, orientation, or movement of the user or the output device in the VR or AR environment. Tracking controls can be based on different technologies, such as optical, inertial, magnetic, or ultrasonic. Examples of tracking controls are:
  - Base stations: external devices that emit or detect signals, such as infrared light or radio waves, to determine the location and orientation of the output device or the user. Base stations can be mounted on walls, ceilings, or tripods. Examples of base stations are the HTC Vive Lighthouse and the Oculus Rift Constellation.
  - Cameras: external or internal devices that capture images or videos of the output device, the user, or the surroundings, and use computer vision algorithms to extract the relevant information. Cameras can be integrated into the output device, such as the Oculus Rift or the Microsoft HoloLens, or placed separately, such as the PlayStation Camera or the Kinect.
  - Sensors: internal devices that measure physical properties, such as acceleration, rotation, or magnetic field, and use them to estimate the motion or orientation of the output device or the user. Sensors can be embedded into the output device, such as the Oculus Rift or the Google Cardboard, or attached to the user, such as the Leap Motion or the Oculus Quest.
