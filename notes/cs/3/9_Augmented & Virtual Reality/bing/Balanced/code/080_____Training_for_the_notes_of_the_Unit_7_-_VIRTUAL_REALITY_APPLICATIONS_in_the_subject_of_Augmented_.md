# Training for the notes of the Unit 7 - VIRTUAL REALITY APPLICATIONS in the subject of Augmented & Virtual Reality

- Virtual reality (VR) is the use of computer modeling and simulation that enables a person to interact with an artificial three-dimensional (3D) visual or other sensory environment.
- VR applications immerse the user in a computer-generated environment that simulates reality through the use of interactive devices, such as goggles, headsets, gloves, or body suits.
- VR applications can be classified into two main categories: immersive and non-immersive.
  - Immersive VR applications provide a high degree of realism and presence, where the user feels as if they are actually in the virtual environment. Immersive VR applications typically require specialized hardware, such as head-mounted displays (HMDs), motion trackers, and haptic devices.
  - Non-immersive VR applications are less realistic and less engaging, where the user views the virtual environment on a screen or a projector. Non-immersive VR applications usually do not require special hardware, and can be accessed with a personal computer, a smartphone, or a tablet.
- VR applications have various uses and benefits in different domains, such as education, entertainment, health care, military, engineering, and tourism. Some examples of VR applications are:
  - Google Expeditions: a VR app that allows users to explore landmarks, museums, and natural wonders in 360° environments.
  - Beat Saber: a VR game that challenges users to slash musical blocks with lightsabers in sync with the rhythm.
  - VRChat: a VR social platform that enables users to create and join virtual worlds, chat with other users, and customize their avatars.
  - The Body VR: a VR app that lets users travel inside the human body and learn about its anatomy and functions.
  - Flight Simulator: a VR app that simulates the experience of flying a plane in realistic scenarios and weather conditions.
- VR applications face some challenges and limitations, such as technical issues, ethical concerns, and health risks. Some of these are:
  - Cost: VR applications often require expensive and sophisticated hardware and software, which may not be affordable or accessible for many users.
  - Quality: VR applications may suffer from low resolution, latency, or glitches, which can affect the user's immersion and satisfaction.
  - Privacy: VR applications may collect and store personal data from the users, such as their location, preferences, or biometrics, which can pose a threat to their privacy and security.
  - Ethics: VR applications may expose the users to inappropriate, violent, or harmful content, which can influence their behavior and values.
  - Health: VR applications may cause physical or psychological discomfort, such as nausea, dizziness, eye strain, or addiction, which can harm the user's health and well-being.
