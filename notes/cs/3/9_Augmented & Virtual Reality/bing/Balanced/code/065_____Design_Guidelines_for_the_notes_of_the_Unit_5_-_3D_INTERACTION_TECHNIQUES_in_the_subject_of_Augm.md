### Design Guidelines for the notes of the Unit 5 - 3D INTERACTION TECHNIQUES in the subject of Augmented & Virtual Reality

- 3D interaction techniques are methods of manipulating and interacting with 3D objects and environments in virtual reality (VR) and augmented reality (AR) applications.
- 3D interaction techniques can be classified into four categories: selection, manipulation, navigation, and system control.
- Selection techniques are methods of specifying one or more 3D objects as the target of an interaction, such as pointing, ray-casting, or gaze-based selection  .
- Manipulation techniques are methods of changing the position, orientation, size, shape, or appearance of 3D objects, such as direct manipulation, virtual hand, or 3D widgets   .
- Navigation techniques are methods of changing the viewpoint or location of the user or the virtual camera in the 3D environment, such as walking, flying, or teleporting   .
- System control techniques are methods of accessing and modifying the system parameters, modes, or functions, such as menus, buttons, or voice commands   .
- 3D interaction techniques should follow some design guidelines to ensure usability, efficiency, and effectiveness, such as   :
  - Match the interaction technique to the task and the user's goals.
  - Provide feedback and feedforward to inform the user of the system state and the possible actions.
  - Minimize the cognitive and physical workload of the user by reducing the number of steps, gestures, or commands required.
  - Support natural and intuitive interaction by leveraging the user's prior knowledge and expectations.
  - Avoid occlusion, clutter, and visual fatigue by using appropriate visual cues, depth cues, and stereoscopic rendering.
  - Consider the trade-offs between accuracy, speed, and comfort of the interaction technique.
  - Evaluate the interaction technique using appropriate metrics, such as task completion time, error rate, user satisfaction, and presence.
