### Requirements for VR

- VR is the simulation of a three-dimensional environment that can be interacted with by a user through a VR device such as a headset, controller, or gloves.
- VR aims to create a sense of presence, immersion, and realism for the user, as if they are actually in the virtual environment.
- To achieve this, VR requires the following components:

  - A VR device that can display stereoscopic images, track the user's head and body movements, and provide audio and haptic feedback.
  - A VR application that can generate and render the virtual environment, respond to the user's actions and inputs, and provide realistic physics and interactions.
  - A VR platform that can support the VR device and application, provide the necessary hardware and software resources, and ensure compatibility and performance.

- Some of the challenges and limitations of VR are:

  - The trade-off between quality and performance, as higher resolution, frame rate, and complexity of the virtual environment require more computational power and bandwidth.
  - The risk of cybersickness, which is a form of motion sickness caused by the mismatch between the visual and vestibular cues in VR.
  - The ethical and social implications of VR, such as the potential for addiction, isolation, deception, and manipulation.
