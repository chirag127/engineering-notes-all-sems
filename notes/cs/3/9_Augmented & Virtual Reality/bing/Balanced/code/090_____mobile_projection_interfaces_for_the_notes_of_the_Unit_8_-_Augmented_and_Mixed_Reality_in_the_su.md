# Mobile Projection Interfaces for Augmented Reality

- Mobile projection interfaces are devices that combine a camera and a projector to create interactive and immersive augmented reality (AR) applications.
- Mobile projection interfaces can be classified into three types, based on how they adapt to the object and the context of the projection :
  - Object-adaptive applications: These applications project information or graphics that are relevant to the object that is being captured by the camera. For example, projecting a recipe on a kitchen counter, or projecting a map on a wall.
  - Context-adaptive applications: These applications project information or graphics that are relevant to the environment or the situation of the user. For example, projecting a navigation guide on the road, or projecting a game on a table.
  - Camera-controlled applications: These applications use the camera as an input device to control the projection or interact with the projected content. For example, using gestures or face recognition to manipulate the projection, or using the camera as a pointer or a scanner.
- Mobile projection interfaces have several advantages over other types of AR displays, such as head-mounted displays or handheld displays  :
  - They can create large-scale and high-resolution AR experiences, without being limited by the size or the resolution of the screen.
  - They can project on any surface, without requiring special markers or trackers, and adapt to the shape and the texture of the surface.
  - They can support collaborative and social AR applications, by allowing multiple users to share and interact with the same projection.
  - They can enhance the user's sense of presence and immersion, by creating a seamless and natural integration between the physical and the virtual world.
- Mobile projection interfaces also have some challenges and limitations, such as :
  - The need for sufficient ambient light and contrast to ensure the visibility and the quality of the projection.
  - The need for accurate and robust calibration and alignment between the camera and the projector, to avoid distortion and misalignment of the projection.
  - The need for efficient and reliable power supply and cooling system, to avoid overheating and battery drain of the device.
  - The need for user-friendly and intuitive interaction techniques, to avoid occlusion and interference of the projection by the user's hands or body.
