### Gestrual Commands for the notes of the Unit 5 - 3D INTERACTION TECHNIQUES in the subject of Augmented & Virtual Reality

- Gestural commands are a type of system control technique that use the hand or body movements to issue commands to a 3D user interface.
- Gestural commands have significant appeal for system control in 3D UIs because of their important role in our day-to-day lives.
- Gestural commands can be classified as either postures or gestures :
  - Posture: a static configuration of the hand, such as a fist, an open palm, or a pointing finger .
  - Gesture: a movement of the hand, perhaps while it is in a certain posture, such as a swipe, a pinch, or a wave .
- Gestural commands can be used to interact with virtual content by directly grasping visible objects, or through invisible gestural commands that are associated with corresponding features in the immersive 3D space .
- The design of gestural commands for 3D UIs remains complex and challenging, as they need to consider several factors, such as :
  - The naturalness and intuitiveness of the gestures
  - The recognition accuracy and feedback of the gestures
  - The ergonomic and social aspects of the gestures
  - The consistency and compatibility of the gestures with other interaction techniques
- Some examples of gestural commands for 3D UIs are :
  - The pinch gesture: a posture where the thumb and index finger touch each other, which can be used to select, grab, or manipulate objects .
  - The swipe gesture: a gesture where the hand moves horizontally or vertically, which can be used to scroll, pan, or switch between menus or views .
  - The pointing gesture: a posture where the index finger is extended and the other fingers are curled, which can be used to indicate a direction, a location, or a target .
  - The fist gesture: a posture where the hand is clenched, which can be used to activate a mode, a function, or a tool .
  - The wave gesture: a gesture where the hand moves back and forth, which can be used to greet, acknowledge, or dismiss .
