### Content

#### What is virtual reality?

Virtual reality (VR) is a technology that creates immersive and interactive simulations of real or imagined environments. VR can be classified into three types, depending on the degree of immersion and realism:

- Non-immersive VR: The user views the virtual environment on a screen or a monitor, and interacts with it using a mouse, a keyboard, or a joystick. The user is aware of the real environment and can easily switch between the two. An example of non-immersive VR is a video game or a flight simulator.
- Semi-immersive VR: The user views the virtual environment on a large screen or a projection system, and interacts with it using more advanced devices, such as a head tracker, a motion controller, or a data glove. The user is partially immersed in the virtual environment, but still has some awareness of the real environment. An example of semi-immersive VR is a CAVE system or a dome projection.
- Fully immersive VR: The user views the virtual environment through a head-mounted display (HMD) or a VR headset, and interacts with it using body movements, gestures, and speech. The user is fully immersed in the virtual environment, and has no awareness of the real environment. An example of fully immersive VR is the Oculus Rift or the HTC Vive.

The main components of a VR system are:

- The display device: The device that presents the virtual environment to the user, such as a screen, a monitor, a projector, or a HMD.
- The tracking device: The device that tracks the user's position, orientation, and movement in the real and virtual environments, such as a camera, a sensor, or a GPS.
- The input device: The device that enables the user to interact with the virtual environment, such as a mouse, a keyboard, a joystick, a controller, a glove, or a microphone.
- The output device: The device that provides feedback to the user from the virtual environment, such as a speaker, a headphone, a vibrator, or a fan.

#### Why use VR for education?

VR can offer several benefits for education, such as:

- Enhancing motivation and engagement: VR can create immersive and realistic experiences that capture the user's attention and interest, and stimulate their curiosity and imagination.
- Facilitating learning by doing: VR can provide users with opportunities to practice and apply their knowledge and skills in authentic and meaningful contexts, and receive immediate and personalized feedback.
- Supporting collaboration and communication: VR can enable users to interact and cooperate with other users or agents in the virtual environment, and share their perspectives and ideas.
- Enabling accessibility and inclusion: VR can overcome the physical, temporal, and financial barriers that limit the access and participation of some learners to certain educational resources and activities, and accommodate their diverse needs and preferences.

However, VR also poses some challenges for education, such as:

- Requiring high technical and financial resources: VR can be expensive and complex to develop, implement, and maintain, and may require specialized hardware, software, and infrastructure.
- Causing physical and psychological discomfort: VR can induce negative effects on the user's health and well-being, such as nausea, dizziness, headache, eye strain, fatigue, anxiety, or isolation.
- Raising ethical and social issues: VR can raise questions and concerns about the privacy, security, and ownership of the user's data and identity, and the impact of VR on the user's behavior, values, and morals.
