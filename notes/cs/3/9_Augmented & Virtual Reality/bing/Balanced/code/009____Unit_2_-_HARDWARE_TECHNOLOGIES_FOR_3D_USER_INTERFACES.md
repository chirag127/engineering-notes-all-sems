## Unit 2 - HARDWARE TECHNOLOGIES FOR 3D USER INTERFACES

- This unit covers the main hardware components and technologies that enable 3D user interfaces, such as displays, sensors, trackers, haptics, and audio.
- The objectives of this unit are to:
  - Understand the basic principles and characteristics of different types of 3D displays, such as stereoscopic, autostereoscopic, volumetric, and holographic displays.
  - Learn about the advantages and disadvantages of various display parameters, such as resolution, field of view, refresh rate, latency, and brightness.
  - Compare and contrast different methods of tracking the position and orientation of the user and the objects in the 3D environment, such as optical, magnetic, inertial, and ultrasonic tracking.
  - Recognize the importance of calibration, registration, and error correction in tracking systems.
  - Explore the concepts and applications of haptic feedback, which provides tactile and force sensations to the user in 3D interaction.
  - Identify the types and components of haptic devices, such as gloves, exoskeletons, and force feedback joysticks.
  - Discuss the challenges and limitations of haptic rendering, such as stability, transparency, and latency.
  - Appreciate the role of audio in 3D user interfaces, which can enhance the realism, immersion, and awareness of the 3D environment.
  - Distinguish between different types of audio sources, such as monaural, stereo, binaural, and spatial audio.
  - Understand the basic principles and techniques of audio synthesis, processing, and rendering, such as sound synthesis, filtering, reverberation, and spatialization.
