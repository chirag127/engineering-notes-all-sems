### 3D Manipulation Tasks

3D manipulation tasks are the tasks of physically handling objects in a 3D environment, such as selecting, positioning, rotating, and scaling them. These tasks are essential for creating and modifying 3D scenes, as well as interacting with 3D objects and applications.

Some of the main 3D manipulation tasks are:

- **Selection**: Selection is the task of acquiring or identifying a particular object or subset of objects from the entire set of objects available. Selection can be done by pointing, clicking, touching, or using voice commands. Selection can also be based on attributes, such as color, shape, or name. Selection is often the first step for other manipulation tasks, such as positioning or rotation  .

- **Positioning**: Positioning is the task of changing the 3D position of an object. Positioning can be done by dragging, dropping, snapping, or using constraints. Positioning can also be done relative to other objects, such as aligning, grouping, or distributing them. Positioning is important for arranging objects in a 3D scene, as well as for navigation and exploration  .

- **Rotation**: Rotation is the task of changing the orientation of an object. Rotation can be done by twisting, turning, or using handles. Rotation can also be done relative to other objects, such as matching, orienting, or mirroring them. Rotation is important for adjusting the view of objects, as well as for creating complex shapes and structures  .

- **Scaling**: Scaling is the task of changing the size of an object. Scaling can be done by stretching, shrinking, or using sliders. Scaling can also be done relative to other objects, such as fitting, resizing, or scaling proportionally. Scaling is important for modifying the appearance of objects, as well as for creating realistic and immersive scenes  .

These 3D manipulation tasks can be performed using different techniques and input devices, depending on the number and integration of control dimensions, the type of feedback, and the user's preferences and skills. Some examples of techniques and devices are:

- **Direct manipulation**: Direct manipulation is a technique that allows the user to manipulate objects by directly touching or grabbing them with their hands or fingers. Direct manipulation can provide natural and intuitive interaction, as well as high accuracy and precision. Direct manipulation can be done using devices such as gloves, trackers, or haptic devices  .

- **Indirect manipulation**: Indirect manipulation is a technique that allows the user to manipulate objects by using a proxy or a cursor that represents their position and orientation. Indirect manipulation can provide flexible and efficient interaction, as well as low fatigue and high comfort. Indirect manipulation can be done using devices such as mice, keyboards, joysticks, or gamepads  .

- **Gesture-based manipulation**: Gesture-based manipulation is a technique that allows the user to manipulate objects by performing gestures with their hands, fingers, or body. Gesture-based manipulation can provide expressive and natural interaction, as well as low cognitive load and high engagement. Gesture-based manipulation can be done using devices such as cameras, sensors, or motion controllers  .

- **Voice-based manipulation**: Voice-based manipulation is a technique that allows the user to manipulate objects by using voice commands or speech recognition. Voice-based manipulation can provide convenient and hands-free interaction, as well as high accessibility and multimodality. Voice-based manipulation can be done using devices such as microphones, speakers, or headphones  .

These are some of the 3D manipulation tasks and techniques that can be used for 3D interaction in augmented and virtual reality. They can be combined and customized to suit different scenarios and applications, as well as different user needs and preferences.
