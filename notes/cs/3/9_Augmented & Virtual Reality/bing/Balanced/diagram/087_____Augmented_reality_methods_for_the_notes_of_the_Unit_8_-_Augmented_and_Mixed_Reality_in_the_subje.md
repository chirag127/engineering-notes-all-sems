### Augmented reality methods

Augmented reality (AR) is a technology that enhances the real-world environment by adding digital elements to it. AR can be used for various purposes, such as entertainment, education, marketing, gaming, and navigation. AR can be delivered through different devices, such as smartphones, tablets, smart glasses, and head-mounted displays (HMDs).

There are different types of AR methods that are based on how the digital content is triggered and positioned in the real-world environment. Some of the common AR methods are:

- **Marker-based AR**: This method uses a special visual object, such as a QR code, a logo, or a symbol, as a marker to initiate the digital content. The AR device scans the marker and calculates its orientation and position to overlay the digital content on top of it. For example, a marker-based mobile app can show how a furniture item would look in the user's room by scanning a marker on a catalog.

- **Marker-less AR**: This method does not require any marker to trigger the digital content. Instead, it uses the location and orientation data from the device's sensors, such as GPS, accelerometer, and gyroscope, to position the digital content in the real-world environment. For example, a marker-less mobile app can show the directions and landmarks on the user's screen by using the device's location and orientation data.

- **Projection-based AR**: This method uses a projector to project the digital content onto a physical surface, such as a wall, a floor, or a table. The projector can also sense the user's interaction with the projected content and respond accordingly. For example, a projection-based AR system can project a virtual keyboard on a table and detect the user's keystrokes.

- **Superimposition-based AR**: This method replaces or enhances a part or the whole of the real-world environment with the digital content. The AR device recognizes the object in the real-world environment and replaces it with the digital content. For example, a superimposition-based AR app can show how a user would look with different hairstyles or makeup by replacing the user's face with the digital content.

These are some of the common AR methods that are used to create immersive and interactive experiences for the users. AR methods can also be combined or customized to suit different applications and scenarios. AR is a rapidly evolving technology that has many potential benefits and challenges for the future.
