### Gestrual Commands for the notes of the Unit 5 - 3D INTERACTION TECHNIQUES in the subject of Augmented & Virtual Reality

- Gestural commands are a type of system control technique that use the hand or body movements to issue commands to a 3D user interface.
- Gestural commands have significant appeal for system control in 3D UIs because of their important role in our day-to-day lives.
- Gestural commands can be classified as either postures or gestures :
  - Posture: a static configuration of the hand, such as a fist, a point, or a thumbs-up .
  - Gesture: a movement of the hand, perhaps while it is in a certain posture, such as a swipe, a circle, or a pinch .
- Gestural commands can be used to interact with virtual content by directly grasping visible objects, or through invisible gestural commands that are associated with corresponding features in the immersive 3D space .
- The design of gestural commands for 3D UIs remains complex and challenging, as they need to consider the following aspects :
  - Recognition: how to accurately and robustly detect and interpret the user's gestures and postures .
  - Feedback: how to provide appropriate and timely feedback to the user about the recognition and execution of their gestural commands .
  - Mapping: how to assign meaningful and intuitive gestural commands to the desired system functions or actions .
  - Learnability: how to help the user learn and remember the available gestural commands and their effects .
- Some examples of gestural commands for 3D UIs are :
  - Pointing: using the index finger or a ray to select or manipulate objects in the 3D space .
  - Pinching: using the thumb and index finger to grab or scale objects in the 3D space .
  - Swiping: using the hand or a device to move or rotate objects in the 3D space .
  - Circling: using the hand or a device to activate a menu or a mode in the 3D UI .
  - Tapping: using the hand or a device to confirm or cancel a selection or an action in the 3D UI .
