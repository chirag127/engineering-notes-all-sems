### Virtual environments

- A virtual environment is a networked application that allows a user to interact with both the computing environment and the work of other users .
- Examples of virtual environments are email, chat, and web-based document sharing applications .
- Virtual environments can be classified into two types: immersive and non-immersive.
  - Immersive virtual environments are those that create a sense of presence and immersion for the user, such as virtual reality headsets, gloves, and haptic devices.
  - Non-immersive virtual environments are those that do not require special hardware or devices, and can be accessed through a standard computer screen, keyboard, and mouse, such as web browsers, video games, and online social platforms.
- Virtual environments can have various benefits and applications, such as education, entertainment, training, simulation, collaboration, and communication .
  - Education: Virtual environments can provide interactive and engaging learning experiences for students, such as virtual field trips, laboratories, and museums.
  - Entertainment: Virtual environments can offer immersive and realistic entertainment options for users, such as virtual reality games, movies, and concerts.
  - Training: Virtual environments can enable effective and safe training scenarios for various professions, such as military, medical, and aviation.
  - Simulation: Virtual environments can replicate complex and dynamic systems and phenomena, such as weather, traffic, and physics.
  - Collaboration: Virtual environments can facilitate teamwork and cooperation among geographically distributed users, such as online meetings, conferences, and workshops.
  - Communication: Virtual environments can enhance social interaction and expression among users, such as online chat, avatars, and emoticons.
