## Unit 6 - DESIGNING AND DEVELOPING 3D USER INTERFACES

3D user interfaces (3D UIs) are interfaces that allow users to interact with 3D data or environments using various input and output devices. 3D UIs can be used for various applications, such as virtual reality, augmented reality, gaming, simulation, education, and entertainment. 3D UIs can enhance the user's engagement, immersion, and performance, but also pose many challenges and trade-offs for design and development.

Some of the topics covered in this unit are:

- **3D UI design principles and guidelines**: These are general rules or recommendations that can help designers create effective and usable 3D UIs. Some of the sources of these principles and guidelines are human factors, cognitive psychology, ergonomics, and existing literature on 2D and 3D UI design  . Some examples of 3D UI design principles and guidelines are:

  - Match the interaction technique to the task and the user's goals.
  - Provide appropriate feedback and feedforward to the user.
  - Avoid visual clutter and occlusion in 3D scenes.
  - Support multiple levels of immersion and realism.
  - Consider the user's physical and mental workload and comfort.

- **3D UI metaphors and styles**: These are conceptual models or frameworks that define how the user interacts with the 3D data or environment. They can be based on real-world analogies, such as desktop, room, or world metaphors, or on abstract concepts, such as fish tank, magic lens, or portal metaphors. Some of the factors that influence the choice of 3D UI metaphors and styles are:

  - The type and dimensionality of the 3D data or environment.
  - The level of immersion and realism desired by the user and the application.
  - The availability and suitability of the input and output devices.
  - The trade-offs between naturalness, efficiency, and ease of use.

- **3D UI interaction techniques and technologies**: These are the methods and tools that enable the user to perform various actions and tasks in 3D UIs, such as selection, manipulation, navigation, and system control. They can be based on direct or indirect input, gesture or voice recognition, hand-held or wearable devices, or tracking or sensing systems . Some of the challenges and opportunities of 3D UI interaction techniques and technologies are:

  - Providing intuitive and consistent mappings between the user's input and the system's output.
  - Supporting multiple degrees of freedom and modes of interaction.
  - Leveraging the user's spatial and motor skills and abilities.
  - Adapting to the user's preferences, context, and environment.

- **3D UI evaluation methods and metrics**: These are the approaches and measures that can help designers and developers assess the usability and user experience of 3D UIs. They can be based on quantitative or qualitative data, subjective or objective measures, or laboratory or field experiments . Some of the aspects and dimensions of 3D UI evaluation methods and metrics are:

  - The user's performance, such as accuracy, speed, and error rate.
  - The user's satisfaction, such as enjoyment, engagement, and immersion.
  - The user's workload, such as physical, mental, and emotional effort.
  - The user's behavior, such as gaze, gesture, and posture.
