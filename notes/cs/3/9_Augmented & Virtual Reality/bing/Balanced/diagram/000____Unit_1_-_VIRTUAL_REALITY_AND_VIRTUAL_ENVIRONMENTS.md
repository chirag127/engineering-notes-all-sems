## Unit 1 - VIRTUAL REALITY AND VIRTUAL ENVIRONMENTS

- Virtual reality (VR) is a technology that creates an immersive and interactive simulation of a three-dimensional environment, using computer-generated graphics, sounds, and other sensory inputs.
- Virtual environments (VEs) are the specific settings or scenarios that are simulated in VR, such as a virtual classroom, a virtual museum, or a virtual battlefield.
- VR and VEs can be used for various purposes, such as education, entertainment, training, therapy, and research.
- VR and VEs can be classified into different types, based on the degree of immersion, realism, and interactivity they provide.
- Immersion is the extent to which the user feels present and engaged in the VE, and loses awareness of the real world.
- Realism is the degree to which the VE resembles the real world, in terms of visual, auditory, haptic, and other cues.
- Interactivity is the level of control and feedback the user has over the VE, and the responsiveness of the VE to the user's actions and inputs.
- Some examples of VR and VE types are:

  - Non-immersive VR: The user views the VE on a desktop monitor or a large screen, and interacts with it using a mouse, keyboard, or joystick. The user is aware of the real world and has limited immersion and realism. An example is a flight simulator game.
  - Semi-immersive VR: The user views the VE on a head-mounted display (HMD) or a projection screen, and interacts with it using a handheld controller, a glove, or a body suit. The user is partially immersed and has moderate realism. An example is a virtual roller coaster ride.
  - Fully immersive VR: The user views the VE on a HMD or a projection screen, and interacts with it using a full-body motion capture system, a haptic device, or a brain-computer interface. The user is fully immersed and has high realism. An example is a virtual surgery training system.
