## Unit 8 - Augmented and Mixed Reality

- Augmented reality (AR) and mixed reality (MR) are both immersive technologies that enhance the perception of reality by adding virtual elements to the real world.
- AR and MR differ in the degree of interaction and integration between the real and virtual elements.
- AR overlays virtual objects on the real-world environment, such as adding a digital clock to a wall or a virtual character to a video game. AR does not change the physical properties of the real world, but rather enhances it with additional information or entertainment.
- MR not only overlays but also anchors virtual objects to the real world, such as placing a hologram on a table or a virtual wall in a room. MR allows the virtual objects to interact with the real world, such as occluding, colliding, or responding to physical forces. MR also enables the user to manipulate the virtual objects with gestures or voice commands.
- AR and MR require different types of devices and technologies to create and display the virtual elements. AR devices include smartphones, tablets, smart glasses, and head-mounted displays (HMDs) that have cameras, sensors, and displays to capture and project the virtual objects onto the real world. MR devices include HMDs that have cameras, sensors, displays, and spatial audio to capture and render the virtual objects in relation to the real world.
- AR and MR have various applications and benefits in different domains, such as education, entertainment, health care, tourism, gaming, and industry. AR and MR can provide immersive and interactive learning experiences, enhance the enjoyment and engagement of media and games, improve the diagnosis and treatment of medical conditions, enrich the exploration and discovery of places and cultures, and increase the efficiency and safety of industrial processes and operations.
