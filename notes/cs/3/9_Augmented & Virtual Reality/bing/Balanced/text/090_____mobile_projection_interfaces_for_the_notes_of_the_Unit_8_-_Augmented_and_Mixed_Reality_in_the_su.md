### Mobile Projection Interfaces for Augmented Reality

- Mobile projection interfaces are a type of augmented reality (AR) interfaces that use a mobile device with a built-in or attached projector to display digital information on physical surfaces.
- Mobile projection interfaces can overcome some of the limitations of handheld display devices, such as small screen size, limited field of view, and occlusion issues.
- Mobile projection interfaces can also enable new forms of interaction and collaboration, such as projecting on objects, walls, floors, or other people, and using gestures, touch, or voice to control the projection.
- Mobile projection interfaces can be classified into different application classes, depending on how they adapt to the object and context of projection, and how they use the camera of the mobile device. Some examples of these classes are:

  - Object-adaptive applications: These applications use the camera to detect the shape, size, color, or texture of the object of projection, and adjust the projection accordingly. For example, projecting a virtual keyboard on a table, or projecting a game on a book cover.
  - Context-adaptive applications: These applications use the camera to sense the environment and the user's location, orientation, or movement, and change the projection accordingly. For example, projecting a map on the ground, or projecting a guide on a wall.
  - Camera-controlled applications: These applications use the camera as an input device to capture the user's gestures, touch, or voice, and use them to manipulate the projection. For example, projecting a menu on the palm, or projecting a drawing tool on the finger.

- Mobile projection interfaces pose some challenges and opportunities for design and development, such as:

  - Projector quality: The brightness, resolution, contrast, and focus of the projector affect the visibility and clarity of the projection, especially in outdoor or bright environments.
  - Projection surface: The shape, size, color, texture, and reflectivity of the surface affect the quality and stability of the projection, and may require calibration or correction techniques.
  - Interaction techniques: The projection may not support direct touch or feedback, and may require alternative methods of interaction, such as gestures, voice, or physical buttons.
  - User experience: The projection may create a sense of presence, immersion, or engagement, but may also cause privacy, security, or social issues, depending on the context and audience of the projection.
