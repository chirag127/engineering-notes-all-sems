### Computer Graphics for the notes of the Unit 1 - VIRTUAL REALITY AND VIRTUAL ENVIRONMENTS

- Virtual reality (VR) is the use of computer graphics systems in combination with various display and interface devices to provide the effect of immersion in the interactive 3D computer-generated environment . We call such an environment a virtual environment (VE).
- A virtual environment can simulate a real or an imaginary world, and can be designed for various purposes, such as entertainment, education, training, or research .
- A virtual environment typically consists of the following components:
  - A computer system that generates the graphics, sound, and other sensory information of the virtual world.
  - A display device that presents the virtual world to the user, such as a head-mounted display (HMD), a projection screen, or a monitor.
  - An input device that allows the user to interact with the virtual world, such as a joystick, a mouse, a glove, or a tracker.
  - A feedback device that provides the user with sensory feedback from the virtual world, such as a speaker, a haptic device, or a vest.
- A virtual environment can be classified into different types according to the degree of immersion, realism, and interactivity:
  - Immersion refers to the extent to which the user feels present in the virtual world, and is influenced by factors such as the field of view, the resolution, the latency, and the stereoscopy of the display device.
  - Realism refers to the degree of correspondence between the virtual world and the real world, and is influenced by factors such as the quality, accuracy, and consistency of the graphics, sound, and other sensory information.
  - Interactivity refers to the degree of control and feedback that the user has over the virtual world, and is influenced by factors such as the responsiveness, the flexibility, and the naturalness of the input and feedback devices.
- Some examples of virtual environments are :
  - Virtual tourism, where the user can explore a virtual representation of a real or a fictional place, such as a museum, a city, or a planet.
  - Virtual training, where the user can learn a skill or a procedure in a virtual scenario, such as a flight simulator, a surgical simulator, or a fire drill.
  - Virtual gaming, where the user can play a game or a sport in a virtual setting, such as a first-person shooter, a racing game, or a golf game.
  - Virtual art, where the user can create or experience a virtual artwork, such as a painting, a sculpture, or a performance.
