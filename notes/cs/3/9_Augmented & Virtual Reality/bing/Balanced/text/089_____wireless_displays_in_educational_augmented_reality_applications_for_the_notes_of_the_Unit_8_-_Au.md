### Wireless Displays in Educational Augmented Reality Applications

- Wireless displays are output devices that can receive and display wirelessly streamed AR content in real time.
- Wireless displays can be stereoscopic head mounted displays (HMDs) or TFT display modules that have been adapted to act as wireless receivers.
- Wireless displays have advantages for educational AR applications, such as:
  - Enabling collaboration and interaction among multiple users who can share or access personalized views or context sensitive information .
  - Eliminating the problems of cables that can restrict movement, cause safety hazards, and limit the number of displays/users .
  - Enhancing the immersion and engagement of users in the AR environment.
- Two examples of educational AR applications that use wireless displays are:
  - The AR Geometry application, which allows students to explore and manipulate geometric shapes and concepts in 3D space using a wireless HMD.
  - The AR Solar System application, which allows students to learn about the planets and their orbits using a wireless TFT display module.
- Some teaching scenarios that can benefit from wireless displays are:
  - A teacher can use a wireless display to monitor and guide the students' progress and activities in the AR application.
  - A student can use a wireless display to present his or her work to the class or to collaborate with other students in a group project.
  - A student can use a wireless display to access additional information or feedback that is not visible to other users.
- The evaluation of wireless displays in educational AR applications has shown positive results, such as:
  - Improved usability and user satisfaction .
  - Increased learning outcomes and motivation .
  - Reduced cognitive load and mental effort.
