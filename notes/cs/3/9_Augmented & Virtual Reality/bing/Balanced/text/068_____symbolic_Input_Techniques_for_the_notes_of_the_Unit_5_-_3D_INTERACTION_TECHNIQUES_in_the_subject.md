### Symbolic Input Techniques

Symbolic input is the task in which users communicate symbolic information (text, numbers, and other symbols or marks) to the system . Symbolic input is often used for tasks such as entering commands, passwords, annotations, labels, or queries. Symbolic input can be challenging in 3D interaction, especially in immersive virtual environments, because of the lack of conventional input devices such as keyboards or mice. Therefore, various techniques have been proposed to enable symbolic input in 3D interaction, which can be classified into four categories:

- Keyboard-based techniques: These techniques use physical or virtual keyboards to input text or symbols. Physical keyboards can be miniature, wearable, or handheld, and can provide tactile feedback and familiarity to the users. Virtual keyboards can be projected on a surface, displayed on a screen, or rendered in 3D space, and can be operated by pointing, touching, or gesturing. Keyboard-based techniques are efficient and accurate, but they can also be cumbersome, intrusive, or occluding in 3D interaction.

- Pen-based techniques: These techniques use a pen or stylus to input text or symbols by writing or drawing on a surface or in mid-air. Pen-based techniques can leverage existing handwriting recognition algorithms or use custom symbol sets. Pen-based techniques are natural and intuitive, but they can also be slow, error-prone, or tiring in 3D interaction.

- Gesture-based techniques: These techniques use hand or body gestures to input text or symbols by performing predefined or user-defined motions. Gesture-based techniques can use vision-based, glove-based, or device-based tracking systems to capture the gestures. Gesture-based techniques are expressive and flexible, but they can also be ambiguous, inconsistent, or hard to remember in 3D interaction.

- Speech-based techniques: These techniques use voice or speech to input text or symbols by speaking or dictating to the system. Speech-based techniques can use existing speech recognition algorithms or use custom vocabularies. Speech-based techniques are fast and easy, but they can also be noisy, unreliable, or socially awkward in 3D interaction.

Each of these techniques has its own advantages and disadvantages, and the choice of the most suitable technique depends on the application domain, the user preferences, and the environmental constraints. Some applications may also combine or switch between different techniques to provide more options or flexibility to the users. For example, a user may use speech to enter a query, and then use gestures to refine or modify the query. The design of symbolic input techniques for 3D interaction should consider the trade-offs between efficiency, accuracy, naturalness, and usability.
