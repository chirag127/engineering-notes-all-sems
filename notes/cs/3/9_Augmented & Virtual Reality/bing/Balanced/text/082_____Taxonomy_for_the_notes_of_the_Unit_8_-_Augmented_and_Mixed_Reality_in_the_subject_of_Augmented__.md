### Taxonomy for the notes of the Unit 8 - Augmented and Mixed Reality

- Taxonomy is a system of classification that organizes and categorizes things based on their similarities and differences.
- Augmented and mixed reality are two types of immersive technologies that combine the real and virtual worlds in different ways.
- Augmented reality (AR) is a technology that overlays digital information or objects on the real world, enhancing the user's perception and interaction with the environment. AR can be experienced through wearable devices (such as glasses or headsets) or non-wearable devices (such as smartphones or tablets).
- Mixed reality (MR) is a technology that merges the real and virtual worlds, creating a hybrid environment where physical and digital objects coexist and interact. MR can be experienced through wearable devices (such as headsets or haptic gloves) or non-wearable devices (such as projectors or cameras).
- A taxonomy of augmented and mixed reality can be based on the following facets:
  - The degree of immersion, which refers to how much the user feels present and engaged in the virtual environment. Immersion can be influenced by factors such as field of view, resolution, latency, interactivity, and realism.
  - The type of display, which refers to how the virtual information or objects are presented to the user. Display types can be categorized as optical (such as see-through glasses or holograms), video (such as camera-based glasses or monitors), or projection (such as spatial or surface projection).
  - The type of tracking, which refers to how the system detects and tracks the user's position, orientation, and movement in the real world. Tracking types can be categorized as marker-based (such as QR codes or fiducial markers), markerless (such as image or feature recognition), or hybrid (such as inertial or magnetic sensors).
  - The type of interaction, which refers to how the user manipulates and communicates with the virtual information or objects. Interaction types can be categorized as gesture-based (such as hand or body gestures), voice-based (such as speech or sound commands), touch-based (such as touchscreens or haptic feedback), or device-based (such as controllers or keyboards).
  - The type of content, which refers to what kind of information or objects are displayed and how they are related to the real world. Content types can be categorized as informational (such as text or graphics), experiential (such as games or simulations), or contextual (such as location or situation-based).
  - The type of application, which refers to what purpose or goal the system serves for the user. Application types can be categorized as educational (such as learning or training), entertainment (such as gaming or art), or professional (such as medical or engineering).
- A taxonomy of augmented and mixed reality can help to understand the characteristics, advantages, and challenges of different systems, as well as to identify the best practices and design principles for developing and evaluating them.
