## Unit 2 - Hardware Technologies for 3D User Interfaces

- Hardware technologies for 3D user interfaces are the devices and systems that enable the communication between a user (or users) and a 3D system.
- Hardware technologies can be classified into two main categories: input and output devices.
- Input devices allow the user to provide information, commands, or actions to the system, such as keyboards, mice, touchscreens, joysticks, trackers, gloves, etc.
- Output devices allow the system to provide information, feedback, or responses to the user, such as monitors, speakers, headphones, projectors, haptic devices, etc.
- Hardware technologies can also be categorized by the sensory modality they target, such as visual, auditory, or haptic.
- Visual devices display 3D graphics or images to the user, such as LCD screens, head-mounted displays, stereoscopic glasses, etc.
- Auditory devices produce 3D sounds or speech to the user, such as speakers, headphones, microphones, etc.
- Haptic devices provide 3D tactile or force feedback to the user, such as vibration motors, force-feedback joysticks, exoskeletons, etc.
- Hardware technologies can also be characterized by the degree of immersion they provide, such as non-immersive, semi-immersive, or fully immersive.
- Non-immersive devices are those that do not isolate the user from the real world, such as desktop monitors, keyboards, mice, etc.
- Semi-immersive devices are those that partially isolate the user from the real world, such as large screens, head-mounted displays, surround sound, etc.
- Fully immersive devices are those that completely isolate the user from the real world, such as virtual reality headsets, gloves, suits, etc.
- Hardware technologies can also be evaluated by the quality and performance they offer, such as resolution, refresh rate, latency, accuracy, etc.
- Resolution is the number of pixels or dots that a device can display or capture, such as 1920x1080 for a monitor or 640x480 for a camera.
- Refresh rate is the frequency at which a device can update its display or capture, such as 60 Hz for a monitor or 30 Hz for a camera.
- Latency is the delay between the user's action and the system's response, such as 10 ms for a keyboard or 100 ms for a tracker.
- Accuracy is the degree of error or deviation between the user's action and the system's response, such as 1 mm for a mouse or 10 cm for a tracker.
