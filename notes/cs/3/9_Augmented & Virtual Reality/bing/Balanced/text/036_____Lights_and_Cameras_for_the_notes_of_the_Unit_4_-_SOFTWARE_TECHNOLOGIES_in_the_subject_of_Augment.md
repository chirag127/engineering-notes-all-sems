### Lights and Cameras for the notes of the Unit 4 - SOFTWARE TECHNOLOGIES in the subject of Augmented & Virtual Reality

- Lights and cameras are essential components of augmented and virtual reality (AR/VR) systems, as they enable the creation and manipulation of realistic 3D scenes and objects that blend with the real world or immerse the user in a virtual environment.
- Lights and cameras can be classified into two categories: real and virtual. Real lights and cameras are the physical devices that capture and emit light, such as the LiDAR scanner or the camera on an iPhone or iPad. Virtual lights and cameras are the software representations of light sources and viewpoints that are used to render 3D graphics on a display, such as the directional light or the main camera in a Unity scene.
- Real lights and cameras provide information about the real world, such as the geometry, texture, color, and illumination of the environment and the objects in it. This information can be used to enhance the realism and interactivity of AR/VR applications, such as by creating realistic shadows, reflections, occlusions, and physics effects. Real lights and cameras can also be used to track the position and orientation of the user and the device, as well as to recognize and interact with real-world objects and markers.
- Virtual lights and cameras provide control over the appearance and perspective of the virtual world, such as by adjusting the brightness, color, direction, and angle of the light sources and the camera. Virtual lights and cameras can also be used to create special effects, such as by changing the field of view, depth of field, or lens distortion of the camera, or by adding post-processing effects, such as bloom, motion blur, or chromatic aberration. Virtual lights and cameras can also be animated or scripted to create dynamic and interactive scenes and objects.
- Lights and cameras in AR/VR systems require careful calibration and synchronization to ensure a consistent and seamless experience for the user. For example, the virtual camera should match the real camera in terms of position, orientation, and parameters, such as focal length and resolution. The virtual lights should also match the real lights in terms of intensity, color, and direction, or at least be estimated from the real lights using techniques such as light estimation or light probes. Additionally, the lights and cameras should be updated in real time to reflect the changes in the real and virtual worlds, such as the user's movement, the environment's lighting, or the virtual object's animation.
