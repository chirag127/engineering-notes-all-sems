### Gestural Commands for the notes of the Unit 5 - 3D INTERACTION TECHNIQUES in the subject of Augmented & Virtual Reality

- Gestural commands are a type of system control technique that use the hand or body movements to issue commands to a 3D user interface.
- Gestural commands have significant appeal for 3D interaction because they are natural, intuitive, and expressive.
- Gestural commands can be classified as either postures or gestures :
  - Posture: a static configuration of the hand, such as a fist, a point, or a thumbs-up.
  - Gesture: a movement of the hand, perhaps while it is in a certain posture, such as a swipe, a pinch, or a wave.
- Gestural commands can be used to directly manipulate visible objects in the 3D scene, or to invoke invisible commands that are associated with features in the immersive 3D space .
- Gestural commands can be designed based on the following criteria:
  - Naturalness: how well the gesture matches the intended action or command.
  - Ease of use: how easy the gesture is to perform and remember.
  - Recognition: how accurately the gesture can be detected and interpreted by the system.
  - Feedback: how the system provides information about the gesture and its effect.
  - Consistency: how the gesture is used across different contexts and applications.
- Gestural commands can be combined with other modalities, such as speech, gaze, or haptics, to create multimodal interfaces that enhance the user experience and performance.
