# Entertainment

Entertainment is one of the most popular and diverse applications of virtual reality (VR). VR can create immersive and interactive experiences that appeal to various senses and preferences of the users. VR can also provide access to places and events that are otherwise difficult or impossible to visit in real life. Some of the examples of VR applications in entertainment are:

- **Video games**: VR gaming is where a person can experience being in a three-dimensional virtual environment and interact with it during a game. VR gaming can enhance the realism, immersion and interactivity of the gameplay, as well as provide new challenges and opportunities for the players. VR gaming can be done using various devices, such as head-mounted displays (HMDs), controllers, gloves, treadmills, etc. Some of the popular VR games are Half-Life: Alyx, Beat Saber, Resident Evil 7, etc. 

- **Virtual museums**: VR can be used to create virtual replicas of museums and historical sites, where the users can explore and learn about the exhibits and artifacts without physically being there. VR can also enhance the educational value of the museums by providing additional information, narration, animation, etc. VR can also enable the preservation and restoration of the cultural heritage that may be damaged or lost in real life. Some of the examples of virtual museums are the British Museum, the Smithsonian Institution, the Louvre, etc. 

- **Galleries**: VR can be used to create virtual galleries where the users can view and appreciate various forms of art, such as paintings, sculptures, installations, etc. VR can also allow the users to create and share their own artworks in a virtual space, as well as interact with other artists and viewers. Some of the examples of virtual galleries are the Google Arts & Culture, the Museum of Other Realities, the VR Museum of Fine Art, etc. 

- **Theatre**: VR can be used to create virtual theatre performances, where the users can watch and participate in the drama, comedy, musical, etc. VR can also enable the users to experience the theatre from different perspectives, such as the stage, the audience, the backstage, etc. VR can also allow the users to create and perform their own theatre shows in a virtual space, as well as collaborate with other actors and directors. Some of the examples of virtual theatre are the National Theatre Immersive Storytelling Studio, the Royal Shakespeare Company, the VR Theatre, etc. 

- **Virtual theme parks**: VR can be used to create virtual theme parks, where the users can enjoy various rides, attractions, shows, etc. VR can also enhance the thrill and excitement of the theme parks by providing realistic and immersive sensations, such as motion, sound, wind, etc. VR can also allow the users to customize and design their own theme parks in a virtual space, as well as share and visit other users' theme parks. Some of the examples of virtual theme parks are the Disney VR, the Universal Studios VR, the RollerCoaster Tycoon VR, etc. 

- **Music VR experience**: VR can be used to create virtual music experiences, where the users can listen and watch various genres and styles of music, such as rock, pop, classical, etc. VR can also enable the users to experience the music from different angles, such as the stage, the crowd, the backstage, etc. VR can also allow the users to create and play their own music in a virtual space, as well as interact with other musicians and fans. Some of the examples of music VR experiences are the TheWaveVR, the MelodyVR, the Electronauts, etc.
