# Symbolic Input Techniques

Symbolic input is the task in which users communicate symbolic information (text, numbers, and other symbols or marks) to the system. Symbolic input is often used for tasks such as entering commands, passwords, names, annotations, or queries. Symbolic input can be challenging in 3D interaction, especially in immersive virtual environments, where traditional input devices such as keyboards or mice may not be available or suitable.

Some of the symbolic input techniques for 3D interaction are:

- Keyboard-based techniques: These techniques use physical or virtual keyboards to enter text or symbols. Physical keyboards can be attached to the user's body or the environment, or tracked by the system. Virtual keyboards can be displayed on a 2D or 3D surface, and the user can select the keys using a 3D cursor, a ray-casting technique, or a gesture. Keyboard-based techniques are familiar and efficient, but they may require additional hardware or visual space, and they may not support natural or expressive input.

- Pen-based techniques: These techniques use a pen or a stylus to write or draw symbols on a 2D or 3D surface. The surface can be a physical or virtual tablet, a wall, or a handheld device. Pen-based techniques can support natural and expressive input, but they may require handwriting recognition, which can be error-prone or slow, and they may not be suitable for 3D spatial input.

- Gesture-based techniques: These techniques use hand or body gestures to perform symbolic input. The gestures can be predefined or user-defined, and they can be recognized by the system using sensors, cameras, or gloves. Gesture-based techniques can support natural and expressive input, and they can be suitable for 3D spatial input, but they may require gesture learning, memorization, and feedback, and they may suffer from recognition errors or fatigue.

- Speech-based techniques: These techniques use voice commands or speech recognition to perform symbolic input. The user can speak words or sentences to the system, and the system can convert them to text or symbols. Speech-based techniques can support natural and expressive input, and they can be hands-free and eyes-free, but they may require speech recognition, which can be error-prone or slow, and they may not be suitable for noisy or public environments, or for multilingual or non-verbal input.
