# Cullers and Occluders for the notes of the Unit 4 - SOFTWARE TECHNOLOGIES in the subject of Augmented & Virtual Reality

- Cullers are algorithms or techniques that eliminate objects or parts of objects that are not visible to the viewer in a virtual or augmented reality scene. Culling can improve the performance and efficiency of rendering by reducing the number of polygons or pixels that need to be processed.
- Occluders are objects or surfaces that block the view of other objects in a virtual or augmented reality scene. Occlusion can enhance the realism and immersion of the scene by creating a sense of depth and spatial relationships between the virtual and real objects.
- Occlusion culling is a specific type of culling that uses occluders to determine which objects are hidden from the viewer and can be culled. Occlusion culling can be challenging in augmented reality, where the real environment is dynamic and complex, and the virtual objects need to be registered accurately with the real objects.
- Some of the methods or techniques for occlusion culling in augmented reality are:
  - Depth sensors: These are devices that can measure the distance or depth of objects in the real environment, such as LiDAR, stereo cameras, or time-of-flight cameras. Depth sensors can provide a depth map or a point cloud that can be used to identify occluders and occluded objects in the scene. However, depth sensors can be slow, limited in range, and low in resolution, and may not work well in some lighting conditions or with reflective or transparent surfaces.
  - Environment meshes: These are 3D models or representations of the real environment that can be pre-built or generated on the fly using techniques such as simultaneous localization and mapping (SLAM) or structure from motion (SfM). Environment meshes can be used to perform occlusion tests or queries between the virtual and real objects in the scene. However, environment meshes can be inaccurate, incomplete, or outdated, and may not capture the dynamic changes or movements in the real environment.
  - Image-based methods: These are methods that use the images or video frames captured by the camera or display to perform occlusion culling. Image-based methods can be based on pixel-level operations, such as alpha blending, depth peeling, or stencil buffering, or on feature-level operations, such as edge detection, segmentation, or optical flow. Image-based methods can be fast, simple, and robust, but may not handle complex occlusion scenarios or occluders with fine details or textures.
