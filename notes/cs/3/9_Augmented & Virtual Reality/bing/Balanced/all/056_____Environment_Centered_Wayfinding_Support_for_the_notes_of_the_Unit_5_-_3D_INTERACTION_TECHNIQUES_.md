# Environment Centered Wayfinding Support

- Wayfinding is the cognitive process of defining a path through an environment, using and acquiring spatial knowledge, and being aided by natural and artificial cues .
- Wayfinding is important because it helps us better understand our environment, instead of being overwhelmed by a lot of small details.
- Environment centered wayfinding support is the design of a virtual environment that supports wayfinding by providing cues, landmarks, maps, and other features that help the user navigate and orient themselves.
- Some examples of environment centered wayfinding support are:
  - Using consistent and recognizable colors, shapes, and symbols to mark different areas or functions of the environment.
  - Providing a clear and visible hierarchy of information, such as signs, labels, and directions, that guide the user from general to specific locations.
  - Creating a coherent and logical layout of the environment, such as a grid, a network, or a tree, that reduces complexity and ambiguity.
  - Incorporating natural and artificial landmarks, such as buildings, statues, or artworks, that help the user identify their position and orientation .
  - Offering maps, compasses, or other tools that show the user their location, destination, and route in relation to the environment .
  - Providing feedback, such as sounds, vibrations, or visual effects, that indicate the user's progress, status, and errors in wayfinding .
- Environment centered wayfinding support can enhance the user's experience, performance, and learning in a virtual environment, as well as their transfer of spatial knowledge to the real world.
