### Real-time computer graphics
- Real-time computer graphics is a sub-field of computer graphics that focuses on producing and analyzing images in real time .
- Real-time rendering is the process of generating every image of an animation as it is being displayed, without pre-computing or storing the images .
- Real-time rendering is interactive and delivers an immersive experience to the viewer, as the images respond to the user's input or changes in the environment .
- Real-time rendering is used for applications such as video games, simulations, virtual reality, augmented reality, mixed reality, and graphical user interfaces .
- Real-time rendering requires a high-performance graphics processing unit (GPU) that can handle complex computations and large amounts of data in parallel .
- Real-time rendering also involves various techniques and algorithms to optimize the quality and speed of the images, such as shading, lighting, texturing, culling, clipping, level of detail, occlusion, anti-aliasing, and post-processing .
- Real-time rendering can also support autostereoscopy, which is a method of displaying stereoscopic images without the need for special glasses or headsets. This can enhance the sense of depth and realism in the images.
