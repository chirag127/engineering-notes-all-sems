### Design Guidelines for the notes of the Unit 5 - 3D INTERACTION TECHNIQUES in the subject of Augmented & Virtual Reality

- 3D interaction techniques are methods that allow users to manipulate and navigate 3D objects and environments in virtual and augmented reality systems.
- 3D interaction techniques can be classified into four categories: selection, manipulation, navigation, and system control.
- Selection techniques are methods that allow users to specify one or more 3D objects or regions of interest in the virtual or augmented environment.
- Manipulation techniques are methods that allow users to change the position, orientation, size, shape, or appearance of 3D objects or regions of interest in the virtual or augmented environment.
- Navigation techniques are methods that allow users to change their viewpoint or location in the virtual or augmented environment.
- System control techniques are methods that allow users to access and modify system parameters, modes, menus, or commands in the virtual or augmented environment.
- Some design guidelines for 3D interaction techniques are:

  - Use natural and intuitive mappings between user actions and system responses, such as direct manipulation, gesture recognition, or speech input.
  - Provide feedback to the user about the state and outcome of the interaction, such as highlighting, snapping, or sound effects.
  - Avoid occlusion and clutter of 3D objects and regions of interest, such as using transparency, depth cues, or layers.
  - Support multiple levels of detail and scale for 3D objects and regions of interest, such as using zooming, fisheye, or focus+context techniques.
  - Consider the trade-offs between accuracy and efficiency of the interaction, such as using snapping, constraints, or guides.
  - Adapt the interaction techniques to the characteristics and capabilities of the user, the device, and the environment, such as using user profiles, device sensors, or context awareness.
