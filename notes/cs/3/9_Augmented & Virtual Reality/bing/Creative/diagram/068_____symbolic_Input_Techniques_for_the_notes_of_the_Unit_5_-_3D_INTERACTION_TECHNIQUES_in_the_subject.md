### Symbolic Input Techniques

- Symbolic input is the task in which users communicate symbolic information (text, numbers, and other symbols or marks) to the system .
- Symbolic input is often used for system control, data entry, annotation, communication, and search in 3D applications .
- Symbolic input can be challenging in 3D environments due to the lack of standard input devices, the difficulty of text entry and recognition, and the trade-off between immersion and usability .
- Symbolic input techniques can be classified into four categories based on the input device and modality: keyboard-based, pen-based, gesture-based, and speech-based  .

#### Keyboard-based Techniques

- Keyboard-based techniques use a physical or virtual keyboard to enter text or symbols in 3D environments  .
- Physical keyboards are familiar, efficient, and accurate, but they require the user to switch attention and hands between the keyboard and the 3D input device, and they may reduce the sense of immersion  .
- Virtual keyboards are graphical representations of keyboards that can be displayed in 3D space and manipulated by 3D input devices, such as a mouse, a stylus, or a hand  .
- Virtual keyboards can be integrated into the 3D environment, but they may suffer from occlusion, distortion, and low resolution, and they may be slower and less accurate than physical keyboards  .
- Examples of virtual keyboards are:

  - Miniature keyboards: small keyboards that can be attached to the user's hand or wrist, or embedded in a 3D input device, such as a glove or a wand  .
  - Floating keyboards: keyboards that can be positioned and oriented in 3D space by the user, and interacted with by pointing or touching  .
  - Fixed keyboards: keyboards that are fixed in a specific location and orientation in the 3D environment, and interacted with by moving the user's viewpoint or body  .
  - Projection keyboards: keyboards that are projected onto a physical surface, such as a table or a wall, and interacted with by touching or pointing  .

#### Pen-based Techniques

- Pen-based techniques use a stylus or a finger to write or draw symbols on a physical or virtual surface in 3D environments  .
- Pen-based techniques are natural, intuitive, and expressive, but they require the system to recognize the symbols, which may be prone to errors and ambiguities  .
- Pen-based techniques can be used for text entry, sketching, annotation, and gesture input  .
- Examples of pen-based techniques are:

  - Handwriting recognition: the system recognizes the handwritten text or symbols and converts them into digital form  .
  - Graffiti: the user writes simplified symbols or strokes that correspond to letters or commands, and the system recognizes them  .
  - Sketching: the user draws freeform shapes or diagrams, and the system interprets them as objects or concepts  .
  - Annotation: the user writes or draws on top of existing objects or scenes, and the system stores or displays them as metadata or feedback  .

#### Gesture-based Techniques

- Gesture-based techniques use hand or body movements to convey symbols or commands in 3D environments  .
