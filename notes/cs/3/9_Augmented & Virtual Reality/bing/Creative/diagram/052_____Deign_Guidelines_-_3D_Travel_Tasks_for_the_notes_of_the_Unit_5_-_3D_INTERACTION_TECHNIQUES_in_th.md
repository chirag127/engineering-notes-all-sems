### Design Guidelines - 3D Travel Tasks

- 3D travel tasks are the tasks that involve moving the user's viewpoint or body in a 3D virtual environment.
- 3D travel tasks can be classified into different types based on the following characteristics :
  - The goal of the task: whether the user has a specific destination or direction to reach, or is exploring or searching the environment.
  - The distance to be traveled: whether the user needs to move over short, medium, or long distances.
  - The amount of curvature or number of turns in the path: whether the user needs to follow a straight, curved, or complex path.
  - The visibility of the target from the starting location: whether the user can see the destination or direction from the beginning, or needs to discover it along the way.
  - The number of degrees of freedom (DOF) required for the movement: whether the user needs to move in one, two, or three dimensions, and whether the user needs to change the orientation or scale of the viewpoint or body.
  - The required accuracy of the movement: whether the user needs to reach the destination or direction precisely, or can tolerate some error or deviation.
  - The other primary tasks that take place during travel: whether the user needs to perform other tasks such as navigation, manipulation, or communication while traveling, and how they affect the travel task.
- Based on these characteristics, different 3D travel techniques can be designed to support different 3D travel tasks in the same application .
- Some general design guidelines for 3D travel techniques are :
  - The most common travel tasks should require a minimum amount of effort from the user.
  - Use high-speed transitional motions, not instant teleportation, if overall environment context is important.
  - Make simple travel tasks easier by using target-based techniques for goal-oriented travel and steering techniques for exploration and search.
  - Use a physical locomotion technique if user exertion or naturalism is required.
  - Provide feedback and guidance to the user during travel, such as visual, auditory, or haptic cues, to indicate the direction, speed, distance, or progress of the movement.
  - Avoid or minimize the negative effects of travel, such as motion sickness, disorientation, or fatigue, by using appropriate techniques, such as reducing the field of view, stabilizing the horizon, or providing a reference frame.
  - Consider the trade-offs between realism, efficiency, and usability when designing 3D travel techniques, and balance them according to the application requirements and user preferences.
