## Unit 5 - 3D INTERACTION TECHNIQUES

3D interaction techniques are methods for interacting with 3D environments and objects using 3D input devices and modalities. They enable users to perform various tasks such as navigation, selection, manipulation, and system control in 3D user interfaces (3D UIs). 3D interaction techniques can be classified into four categories:

- **Navigation**: The process of moving the user's viewpoint or the virtual camera in the 3D environment. Navigation techniques can be further divided into travel (changing the position of the viewpoint) and wayfinding (determining the direction and route of the travel). Examples of navigation techniques are steering, flying, walking, teleporting, and map-based navigation .
- **Selection**: The process of identifying and indicating one or more objects of interest in the 3D environment. Selection techniques can be further divided into pointing (directing a ray or a cursor at the target object) and specifying (confirming the selection of the target object). Examples of selection techniques are ray-casting, virtual hand, image plane, and spotlight .
- **Manipulation**: The process of changing the position, orientation, size, shape, or other attributes of the selected objects in the 3D environment. Manipulation techniques can be further divided into translation (moving the object along one or more axes), rotation (rotating the object around one or more axes), scaling (changing the size of the object), and deformation (changing the shape of the object). Examples of manipulation techniques are virtual hand, HOMER, 3D widgets, and bimanual manipulation .
- **System Control**: The process of accessing and modifying the system parameters, modes, and functions in the 3D environment. System control techniques can be further divided into menus (providing a list of options or commands), widgets (providing graphical elements for input or output), and gestures (providing predefined or user-defined motions for commands). Examples of system control techniques are pie menus, 3D buttons, speech recognition, and hand gestures .

3D interaction techniques can be designed and developed using various strategies, such as adapting 2D interaction techniques to 3D, using "magic" or "natural" metaphors, combining multiple modalities (e.g., vision, sound, touch, etc.), and exploiting two-handed or whole-body interaction. 3D interaction techniques can be evaluated using various criteria, such as performance, usability, user satisfaction, presence, immersion, and engagement. 3D interaction techniques can be applied to various domains and applications, such as virtual reality, augmented reality, gaming, education, training, visualization, and design   .
