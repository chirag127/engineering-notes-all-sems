### Marker-less tracking for augmented reality

- Marker-less tracking is a technique that allows augmented reality (AR) applications to display virtual content without the need for predefined markers or targets in the real environment .
- Marker-less tracking can use different methods to estimate the pose (position and orientation) of the camera or the device relative to the real scene, such as feature-based, model-based, or learning-based methods .
- Feature-based methods use local or global features extracted from the images to match them with a database of known features and find the corresponding camera pose. Examples of feature-based methods are ORB, SIFT, SURF, FREAK, etc.
- Model-based methods use a 3D model of the scene or the object to align it with the image and find the camera pose. Examples of model-based methods are edge-based, point-based, or region-based methods.
- Learning-based methods use machine learning techniques to train a classifier or a regressor that can predict the camera pose from the image. Examples of learning-based methods are neural networks, support vector machines, random forests, etc.
- Marker-less tracking can offer more flexibility and realism for AR applications, as it can work with any scene or object and does not require any prior preparation or calibration .
- Marker-less tracking can also face some challenges, such as occlusions, illumination changes, dynamic scenes, scale ambiguity, etc .
- Marker-less tracking can be used for various AR applications, such as gaming, education, entertainment, navigation, tourism, etc .
