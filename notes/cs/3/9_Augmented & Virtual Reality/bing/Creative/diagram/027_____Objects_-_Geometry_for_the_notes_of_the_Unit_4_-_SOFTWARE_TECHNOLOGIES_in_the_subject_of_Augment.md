### Objects - Geometry

- Objects are the basic building blocks of any 3D scene in augmented and virtual reality applications. They can be static or dynamic, simple or complex, realistic or abstract, depending on the design and purpose of the application.
- Geometry is the branch of mathematics that deals with the properties, measurements, and relationships of points, lines, angles, surfaces, and solids in space. Geometry is essential for creating and manipulating objects in 3D applications, as well as for rendering, lighting, collision detection, and physics simulation.
- There are different types of geometry that can be used to represent objects in 3D applications, such as:

  - Primitives: These are the simplest and most common types of geometry, such as spheres, cubes, cylinders, cones, and planes. They are usually defined by a few parameters, such as radius, height, width, length, and orientation. Primitives are useful for creating basic shapes and structures, as well as for testing and prototyping.
  - Meshes: These are more complex and flexible types of geometry, composed of vertices, edges, and faces. Vertices are the points in space that define the shape of the object, edges are the lines that connect the vertices, and faces are the polygons that fill the gaps between the edges. Meshes can be created manually using 3D modeling software, or automatically using algorithms or scanners. Meshes can represent any shape and level of detail, but they also require more memory and processing power than primitives.
  - Curves and Surfaces: These are types of geometry that use mathematical functions to define smooth and continuous shapes, such as circles, ellipses, parabolas, splines, NURBS, and Bézier curves and surfaces. Curves and surfaces can be used to create organic and natural-looking objects, such as plants, animals, and human faces. However, they are also more difficult to manipulate and render than meshes and primitives.

- The geometry of an object can be modified by applying various transformations, such as translation, rotation, scaling, shearing, and reflection. These transformations can change the position, orientation, size, shape, and symmetry of the object, without altering its intrinsic properties. Transformations can be applied to the whole object, or to a part of it, such as a vertex, edge, face, or group of elements.
- The geometry of an object can also be affected by the coordinate system that is used to define and measure its location and orientation in space. There are different types of coordinate systems, such as:

  - Cartesian: This is the most common and intuitive type of coordinate system, based on three perpendicular axes: x, y, and z. The origin of the system is the point where the three axes intersect, and the direction and length of each axis define the unit vectors of the system. A point in space can be represented by a triplet of numbers, called coordinates, that indicate its distance and direction from the origin along each axis. For example, the point (2, 3, 4) is located 2 units along the x-axis, 3 units along the y-axis, and 4 units along the z-axis from the origin.
  - Spherical: This is a type of coordinate system that is based on a sphere, with the origin at the center of the sphere. A point in space can be represented by a triplet of numbers, called coordinates, that indicate its distance from the origin, called radius, and its angle from the reference plane and the reference axis, called polar angle and azimuthal angle, respectively. For example, the point (5, 30, 60) is located 5 units away from the origin, 30 degrees above the xy-plane, and 60 degrees counterclockwise from the x-axis.
  - Cylindrical: This is a type of coordinate system that is based on a cylinder, with the origin at the center of the base of the cylinder. A point in space can be represented by a triplet of numbers, called coordinates, that indicate its distance from the origin along the axis of the cylinder, called height, its distance from the axis of the cylinder, called radius, and its angle from the reference axis, called azimuthal angle. For example, the point (3, 4, 45) is located 3 units above the base of the cylinder, 4 units away from the axis of the cylinder, and 45 degrees counterclockwise from the x-axis.
