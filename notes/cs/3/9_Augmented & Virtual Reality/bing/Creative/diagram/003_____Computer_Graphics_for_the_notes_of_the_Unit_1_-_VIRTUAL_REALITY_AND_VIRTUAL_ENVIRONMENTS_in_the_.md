### Computer Graphics for Virtual Reality and Virtual Environments

- Computer graphics is the field of computer science that deals with creating and manipulating images, models, animations, and other visual elements using computers and software.
- Virtual reality (VR) is the use of computer graphics systems in combination with various display and interface devices to provide the effect of immersion in the interactive 3D computer-generated environment. We call such an environment a virtual environment (VE).
- Virtual environments can be used for various purposes, such as entertainment, education, training, simulation, visualization, and communication. They can also create experiences that are impossible, dangerous, or impractical in the real world.
- Some examples of virtual environments are:
  - Virtual tours of museums, historical sites, or natural wonders.
  - Virtual games, sports, or adventures.
  - Virtual simulations of physical phenomena, such as gravity, fluid dynamics, or quantum mechanics.
  - Virtual representations of abstract concepts, such as data, networks, or algorithms.
  - Virtual social spaces, where people can interact with each other or with virtual characters.
- To create a virtual environment, we need to use computer graphics techniques, such as:
  - Modeling: creating 3D geometric shapes and objects that represent the virtual world and its elements.
  - Rendering: generating 2D images of the 3D models using lighting, shading, texturing, and other effects.
  - Animation: changing the position, orientation, shape, or appearance of the 3D models over time to create motion and dynamics.
  - Interaction: enabling the user to manipulate, navigate, or explore the virtual environment using input devices, such as keyboards, mice, joysticks, trackers, gloves, or headsets.
  - Display: presenting the rendered images to the user using output devices, such as monitors, projectors, or head-mounted displays (HMDs).
- To achieve a realistic and immersive virtual environment, we need to consider some factors, such as:
  - Fidelity: the degree of accuracy and detail of the virtual environment compared to the real world or the intended scenario.
  - Presence: the sense of being in the virtual environment rather than in the real world.
  - Immersion: the extent to which the virtual environment blocks out or replaces the sensory inputs from the real world.
  - Performance: the speed and quality of the computer graphics system in generating and displaying the virtual environment.
  - Usability: the ease and comfort of using the virtual environment and its interface devices.
  - Affordance: the possibilities and limitations of the virtual environment and its elements for the user's actions and goals.
