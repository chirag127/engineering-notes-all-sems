### Augmented reality methods

Augmented reality (AR) is a method by which we can alter our real world by adding some digital elements to it. This is done by superimposing a digital image on the person’s current view thus it enhances the experience of reality.

Some of the methods of augmented reality are:

- **Marker-based AR**: A marker, which is a special visual object like a special sign or anything, and a camera are used to initiate the 3D digital animations. The system will calculate the orientation and position of the market to position the content effectively. For example, a marker-based mobile-based AR furnishing app.
- **Marker-less AR**: This method does not require any marker or special object to trigger the digital content. Instead, it uses the location and orientation of the device to display the relevant information. For example, a marker-less AR navigation app.
- **Projection-based AR**: This method projects a digital image onto a physical surface, creating an illusion of interactivity. The projected image can be static or dynamic, and can respond to touch or gesture inputs. For example, a projection-based AR keyboard.
- **Superimposition-based AR**: This method replaces the original view of an object with an augmented view, either partially or fully. The system needs to recognize the object and its shape to overlay the digital content accurately. For example, a superimposition-based AR medical app that shows the internal organs of a patient.
- **Display systems**: These are devices that are worn on the human body, such as head-mounted displays (HMDs), smart glasses, or contact lenses, that provide a direct or indirect view of the real world with augmented elements. The display systems can use different technologies, such as optical projection, monitors, or holograms, to render the digital content. For example, a display system-based AR gaming device.
