### Virtual Reality

- Virtual reality (VR) is the use of computer modeling and simulation that enables a person to interact with an artificial three-dimensional (3D) visual or other sensory environment.
- VR applications immerse the user in a computer-generated environment that simulates reality through the use of interactive devices, such as headsets, controllers, gloves, and trackers.
- VR can create realistic and immersive experiences that can enhance learning, entertainment, training, and therapy.

### Virtual Reality Applications in Engineering

- VR can be used in engineering to model and design new hardware technologies, such as engines, robots, bridges, and buildings.
- VR can enable engineers to view their project in 3D and gain a greater understanding of how it works, as well as spot any flaws or potential risks before implementation .
- VR can also be used to conduct experiments and tests in a virtual environment, without the need for physical prototypes or costly equipment.
- VR can facilitate collaboration and communication among engineers, as well as with clients and stakeholders, by allowing them to share and review their designs in a virtual space.
- VR can also provide training and education for engineers, by exposing them to realistic scenarios and challenges that they may encounter in their field.
