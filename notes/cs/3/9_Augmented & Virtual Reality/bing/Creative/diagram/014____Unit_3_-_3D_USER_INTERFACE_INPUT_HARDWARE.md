## Unit 3 - 3D User Interface Input Hardware

- 3D user interface input hardware are devices that capture and interpret the actions performed by the user in a 3D environment.
- The main characteristics of 3D user interface input hardware are:
  - Degrees of freedom (DOF): the number of independent ways that a body can move in space. For example, a 3D mouse can have six DOF: three for translation (x, y, z) and three for rotation (pitch, yaw, roll).
  - Input type/frequency of data: the nature and rate of the data generated by the device. For example, a discrete input device produces a single data value, such as a Boolean value from a button. A continuous input device generates multiple data values, such as pixel coordinates from a 2D mouse.
  - Physical interaction: the amount and type of physical effort required by the user to operate the device. For example, a passive device does not require any external power or force, such as a glove. An active device requires some external power or force, such as a joystick.
- Some examples of 3D user interface input hardware are:
  - Keyboards: a set of keys that can be used to enter text, commands, or shortcuts. They are typically used in desktop 3D UIs and have low DOF, discrete input, and passive interaction.
  - 2D mice and trackballs: devices that can sense the movement of a user's hand on a flat surface or a ball. They are typically used in desktop 3D UIs and have two or three DOF, continuous input, and passive interaction.
  - Pen- and touch-based tablets: devices that can sense the position and pressure of a user's finger or stylus on a flat surface. They are typically used in desktop 3D UIs and have two or three DOF, continuous input, and passive interaction.
  - Joysticks: devices that can sense the direction and magnitude of a user's hand movement on a stick. They are typically used in immersive 3D UIs and have two or three DOF, continuous input, and active interaction.
  - Gloves: devices that can sense the position and orientation of a user's hand and fingers. They are typically used in immersive 3D UIs and have six or more DOF, continuous input, and passive interaction.
  - Trackers: devices that can sense the position and orientation of a user's head, body, or limbs. They are typically used in immersive 3D UIs and have six or more DOF, continuous input, and passive interaction.
