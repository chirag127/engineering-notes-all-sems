- 3D user interfaces (3D UIs) are interfaces that allow users to interact with 3D environments and objects using various input and output devices, such as head-mounted displays, controllers, gloves, trackers, etc.
- 3D UIs pose different challenges and opportunities than traditional 2D UIs, such as spatial awareness, depth perception, navigation, manipulation, system control, etc.
- To design and develop effective 3D UIs, it is important to consider the following aspects:
  - The goals, tasks, and context of the application and the users
  - The characteristics and limitations of the 3D devices and techniques
  - The principles and guidelines of 3D UI design, such as naturalness, consistency, feedback, affordance, etc.
  - The trade-offs and trade-offs between different design choices, such as realism, performance, usability, etc.
- To evaluate 3D UIs, it is important to use appropriate methods and metrics that can capture the user experience and the usability of the 3D UIs, such as:
  - Cognitive walkthrough: a method that involves stepping through common tasks that a user would perform and evaluating the interface’s ability to support each step 
  - Heuristic evaluation: a method that involves several usability experts separately evaluating a UI design by applying a set of design guidelines 
  - Formative evaluation: a method that involves an observational, empirical evaluation that identifies usability problems by iteratively placing representative users in task-based scenarios 
  - Summative evaluation: a method that involves a comparative, empirical evaluation that measures the effectiveness, efficiency, and satisfaction of the 3D UIs by placing representative users in realistic scenarios 
  - The metrics that can be used to evaluate 3D UIs include objective measures, such as task completion time, error rate, accuracy, etc., and subjective measures, such as user satisfaction, preference, workload, presence, etc.
