### Beyond Text and Number Entry for 3D Interaction Techniques

- Text and number entry are common tasks in many applications, but they can be challenging in 3D environments, especially in virtual reality (VR), where traditional input devices such as keyboards and mice are not available or suitable.
- There are different approaches to enable text and number entry in 3D interaction techniques, such as:
  - Selection-based text entry: The user selects characters from a virtual keyboard using a pointing device, such as a controller, a hand gesture, or a head-gaze.
  - Speech-based text entry: The user speaks the text or numbers and the system recognizes them using speech recognition technology.
  - Handwriting recognition: The user writes the text or numbers using a stylus or a finger on a touch-sensitive surface, such as a tablet or a virtual pad, and the system recognizes them using handwriting recognition technology.
  - Gesture-based text entry: The user performs gestures that correspond to characters or numbers, such as drawing them in the air or tapping on a virtual keypad, and the system recognizes them using gesture recognition technology.
- The choice of text and number entry method depends on several factors, such as:
  - The type and amount of text or numbers to be entered: For example, speech-based text entry may be faster and more natural for long sentences, while selection-based text entry may be more accurate and convenient for short words or passwords.
  - The context and environment of the user: For example, speech-based text entry may not be suitable for noisy or public settings, while gesture-based text entry may require more physical space and effort.
  - The availability and compatibility of input devices: For example, handwriting recognition may require a stylus or a touch-sensitive surface, while gesture-based text entry may require a camera or a sensor to capture the gestures.
  - The user preferences and abilities: For example, some users may prefer speech-based text entry for its ease and familiarity, while others may prefer selection-based text entry for its privacy and control.
- Text and number entry are not the only tasks that require input in 3D interaction techniques. There are also other tasks, such as menu selection, object manipulation, navigation, and command execution, that can be performed using different input modalities, such as:
  - Pointing: The user points at a target using a device, such as a controller, a hand gesture, or a head-gaze, and selects it by pressing a button, making a gesture, or dwelling on it.
  - Ray-casting: The user casts a ray from a device, such as a controller, a hand gesture, or a head-gaze, and selects the target that intersects with the ray by pressing a button, making a gesture, or dwelling on it.
  - Direct manipulation: The user grabs, moves, rotates, scales, or releases an object using a device, such as a controller, a hand gesture, or a head-gaze, and performs the action by pressing a button, making a gesture, or dwelling on it.
  - Natural object interaction: The user interacts with an object using its natural affordances, such as picking up a cup by its handle, opening a door by its knob, or turning a dial by its edge.
  - Gesture commands: The user performs gestures that correspond to commands, such as swiping, pinching, or tapping, and the system recognizes them using gesture recognition technology.
  - Speech commands: The user speaks commands and the system recognizes them using speech recognition technology.
