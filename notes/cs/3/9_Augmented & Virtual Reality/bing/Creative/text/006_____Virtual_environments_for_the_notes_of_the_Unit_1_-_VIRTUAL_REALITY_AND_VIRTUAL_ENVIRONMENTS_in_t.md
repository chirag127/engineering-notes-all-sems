### Virtual environments

- A virtual environment is a networked application that allows a user to interact with both the computing environment and the work of other users .
- Virtual environments can be used for various purposes, such as communication, collaboration, education, entertainment, simulation, training, and visualization.
- Virtual environments can be classified into different types based on the degree of immersion, realism, interactivity, and social presence they provide.
- Some examples of virtual environments are email, chat, web-based document sharing, virtual reality, augmented reality, mixed reality, and 3D virtual worlds  .
- Virtual environments can be created and accessed using various hardware and software technologies, such as head-mounted displays, gloves, trackers, sensors, cameras, projectors, speakers, microphones, keyboards, mice, graphics cards, processors, operating systems, browsers, applications, and protocols .
- Virtual environments can have various benefits and challenges for users, such as enhancing learning outcomes, increasing motivation, improving collaboration, reducing costs, overcoming physical limitations, providing feedback, creating presence, inducing cybersickness, raising ethical issues, and requiring technical skills.
