### Architecture for the notes of the Unit 7 - VIRTUAL REALITY APPLICATIONS in the subject of Augmented & Virtual Reality

- Virtual reality (VR) is a technology that creates immersive and interactive simulations of real or imagined environments, using computer-generated graphics, sounds, and other sensory inputs.
- VR applications for architecture are software tools that allow architects, designers, engineers, and clients to visualize, explore, and modify architectural projects in VR, using devices such as headsets, controllers, and trackers.
- VR applications for architecture can have various benefits, such as:
  - Enhancing the design process by enabling rapid prototyping, testing, and iteration of ideas in a realistic and immersive way.
  - Improving the communication and collaboration among stakeholders by allowing them to share and review the same virtual model, regardless of their physical location.
  - Increasing the engagement and satisfaction of clients by allowing them to experience and interact with the design before it is built, and to provide feedback and suggestions.
  - Reducing the cost and time of construction by identifying and resolving potential issues and errors in the design stage, and by optimizing the use of materials and resources.
- VR applications for architecture can have different levels of detail (LOD), ranging from low-fidelity sketches and wireframes to high-fidelity photorealistic renderings, depending on the purpose and stage of the design process.
- VR applications for architecture can also have different modes of interaction, such as:
  - Passive mode, where the user can only observe and navigate the virtual environment, without changing or manipulating it.
  - Active mode, where the user can modify and manipulate the virtual environment, such as changing the materials, colors, lighting, furniture, etc.
  - Collaborative mode, where multiple users can interact and collaborate with each other in the same virtual environment, using voice chat, gestures, annotations, etc.
- Some examples of VR applications for architecture are:
  - ARki, a mixed reality application that allows users to superimpose their 3D models on the real world, using their smartphone or tablet camera.
  - Storyboard VR, a free prototyping and visualization tool that allows users to create and edit VR scenes using 2D images, sketches, and text.
  - Pair, a VR application that allows users to place and arrange furniture and objects in their virtual or real spaces, using their smartphone or headset.
  - SmartReality, a VR application that allows users to view and interact with their 3D models in VR, using their smartphone and a cardboard viewer.
  - Fuzor, a VR application that allows users to import and synchronize their 3D models from various software platforms, such as Revit, SketchUp, Rhino, etc., and to explore and modify them in VR, using a headset and controllers.
