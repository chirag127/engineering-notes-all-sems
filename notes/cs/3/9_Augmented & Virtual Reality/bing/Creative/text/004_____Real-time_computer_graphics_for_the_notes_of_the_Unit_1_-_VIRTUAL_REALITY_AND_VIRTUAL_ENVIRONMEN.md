### Real-time computer graphics

- Real-time computer graphics is the sub-field of computer graphics focused on producing and analyzing images in real time .
- Real-time rendering is the process of generating each image of an animation as it is being displayed, without pre-computing or storing the images .
- Real-time rendering is interactive and delivers an immersive experience to the viewer, as the images respond to the user's input and actions .
- Real-time rendering is used for applications such as video games, simulations, virtual reality, augmented reality, and mixed reality .
- Real-time rendering requires a high-performance graphics processing unit (GPU) to handle the complex computations and data transfers involved in generating realistic images at high frame rates .
- Real-time rendering engines are software systems that provide the tools and libraries for creating and displaying real-time graphics. Some examples of real-time rendering engines are Unreal Engine, Unity, Notch, and Blender.
- Real-time rendering engines can adapt to different display technologies, such as autostereoscopy, which creates a 3D effect without the need for special glasses.
