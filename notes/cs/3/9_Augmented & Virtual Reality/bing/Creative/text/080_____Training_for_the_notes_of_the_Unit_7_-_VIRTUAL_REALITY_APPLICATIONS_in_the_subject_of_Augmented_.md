### Training for the notes of the Unit 7 - VIRTUAL REALITY APPLICATIONS in the subject of Augmented & Virtual Reality

- Virtual reality applications are applications that make use of virtual reality (VR), an immersive sensory experience that digitally simulates a virtual environment.
- VR applications require software and hardware to create and display the virtual environment, and to enable the user to interact with it through various devices, such as goggles, headsets, gloves, or body suits .
- VR applications can be classified into different categories based on their purpose, content, and level of immersion. Some of the common categories are:
  - **Entertainment**: VR applications that provide fun and engaging experiences for the user, such as games, movies, concerts, or sports. Examples: Allumette, Colosse, Kingspray Graffiti VR, Ocean Rift, Tilt Brush.
  - **Education**: VR applications that facilitate learning and training in various domains, such as science, history, art, or medicine. Examples: Google Expeditions, Titans of Space Plus, The Foo Show.
  - **Business**: VR applications that support various aspects of business operations, such as communication, collaboration, marketing, or design. Examples: Google Earth VR, Littlstar, Virtual Desktop, Within.
  - **Healthcare**: VR applications that assist in diagnosis, treatment, or rehabilitation of various medical conditions, such as phobias, pain, or stroke. Examples: SnowWorld, Bravemind, MindMotion Pro.
  - **Social**: VR applications that enable social interaction and networking among users in a virtual space, such as chat rooms, avatars, or virtual communities. Examples: Facebook Spaces, VRChat, Rec Room.
- VR applications have various benefits and challenges for the users and developers. Some of the benefits are:
  - **Enhanced realism**: VR applications can create realistic and immersive environments that stimulate the user's senses and emotions, and provide a sense of presence and agency.
  - **Increased engagement**: VR applications can capture the user's attention and interest, and motivate them to explore and learn more about the virtual world.
  - **Improved performance**: VR applications can improve the user's skills and abilities, such as spatial awareness, memory, creativity, or problem-solving, by providing feedback and guidance.
  - **Reduced costs and risks**: VR applications can reduce the costs and risks associated with real-world activities, such as travel, equipment, or safety, by simulating them in a virtual environment.
- Some of the challenges are:
  - **Technical limitations**: VR applications require high-performance hardware and software, and reliable network connectivity, to ensure smooth and realistic rendering and interaction of the virtual environment.
  - **User discomfort**: VR applications can cause physical and psychological discomfort for the user, such as nausea, dizziness, eye strain, or anxiety, due to the mismatch between the visual and vestibular cues, or the lack of control over the virtual environment.
  - **Ethical and social issues**: VR applications can raise ethical and social issues, such as privacy, security, ownership, or responsibility, regarding the use and impact of the virtual environment on the user and others.
