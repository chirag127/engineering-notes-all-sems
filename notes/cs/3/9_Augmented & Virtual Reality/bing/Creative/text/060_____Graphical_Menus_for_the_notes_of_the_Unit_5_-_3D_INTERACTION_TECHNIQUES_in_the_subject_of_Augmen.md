### Graphical Menus

- Graphical menus are an integral and essential component of user interfaces and have been widely used for 2D desktop applications .
- Graphical menus for 3D UIs are the 3D equivalent of the 2D menus that have proven to be a successful system control technique in desktop UIs .
- Graphical menus in 3D UIs can be subdivided into three categories: adapted 2D menus, 1-DOF menus, and 3D widgets .
- Adapted 2D menus are the simplest form of graphical menus in 3D UIs. They are basically 2D menus that are embedded in a 3D environment, either on a flat panel display or on a virtual surface .
- 1-DOF menus are graphical menus that use one degree of freedom (DOF) for navigation and selection. They can be either linear or circular, and can be oriented in any direction in 3D space .
- 3D widgets are graphical menus that take advantage of the extra DOF available in a 3D environment to enable more complex menu structures or better visual affordances for menu entries. They can be either 3D objects or 3D shapes that represent menu options .
- Graphical menus in 3D UIs face several challenges, such as positioning of graphical user interface elements in space, choosing an appropriate selection technique, representation of the menu and its overall structure, and providing feedback and feedforward .
- Graphical menus in 3D UIs can be evaluated based on several criteria, such as usability, performance, user satisfaction, user preference, and user experience .
- Graphical menus in 3D UIs can be used for various applications, such as virtual reality, augmented reality, mixed reality, and 3D games  .
