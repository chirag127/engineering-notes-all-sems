### Wireless Displays in Educational Augmented Reality Applications

- Wireless displays are output devices that can receive and display wirelessly streamed content, such as stereoscopic video signals, in real time.
- Wireless displays can be used for educational augmented reality (AR) applications, which are applications that combine real and virtual elements to enhance learning and teaching.
- Wireless displays can offer several advantages for educational AR applications, such as:
  - Enabling personalization and privacy of content for each user.
  - Reducing the number of cables and wires that can hinder movement and interaction.
  - Increasing the scalability and flexibility of the system by allowing more users and displays to be connected.
  - Supporting collaborative and cooperative learning scenarios by allowing users to share and exchange views and information.
- Examples of educational AR applications that use wireless displays are:
  - Construct3D: a collaborative 3D geometry construction tool that allows users to create and manipulate 3D objects using a wireless stereoscopic head mounted display (HMD) and a wireless pen.
  - ARMath: a math learning application that uses a wireless TFT display module to show interactive 3D visualizations of mathematical concepts and problems.
- Wireless displays can be implemented using various wireless technologies, such as:
  - Wireless USB: a short-range wireless technology that can transmit uncompressed video signals at high data rates and low latency.
  - Wi-Fi: a medium-range wireless technology that can transmit compressed video signals at lower data rates and higher latency.
  - Bluetooth: a short-range wireless technology that can transmit audio signals and control commands at low data rates and low latency.
- Wireless displays can also be integrated with other devices, such as smartphones, tablets, or laptops, to provide additional input and output capabilities, such as touch, gesture, or voice control, or web access.
