### Scientific landmarks for the notes of the Unit 1 - VIRTUAL REALITY AND VIRTUAL ENVIRONMENTS

- Scientific landmarks are significant achievements or discoveries that advance the field of virtual reality and its applications.
- Some examples of scientific landmarks in virtual reality are:

  - The first head-mounted display (HMD) developed by Ivan Sutherland in 1968, which allowed users to experience a computer-generated 3D environment.
  - The first immersive virtual environment system (IVES) created by Myron Krueger in 1974, which used video cameras and projectors to create interactive projections on walls and floors.
  - The first commercial virtual reality system, the Virtuality arcade machine, launched by W Industries in 1991, which offered stereoscopic graphics, head tracking, and haptic feedback.
  - The first virtual reality web browser, WebVR, introduced by Mark Pesce and Tony Parisi in 1994, which enabled users to navigate 3D websites using VRML (Virtual Reality Modeling Language).
  - The first virtual reality social network, Second Life, launched by Linden Lab in 2003, which allowed users to create and explore virtual worlds, interact with other users, and exchange virtual goods and services.
  - The first consumer-grade VR headset, the Oculus Rift, released by Oculus VR in 2016, which offered high-resolution displays, low-latency head tracking, and positional tracking.
  - The first virtual reality platform for scientists, Caltech's Virtual Reality Center for Data Science, established in 2019, which provided researchers with tools to visualize and analyze complex data sets in immersive environments.
  - The first virtual reality project for cultural heritage preservation, Open Heritage, launched by Google Arts & Culture and CyArk in 2018, which used 3D scanning and photogrammetry to create digital replicas of endangered historical sites.

- Scientific landmarks in virtual reality can also be related to specific domains or applications, such as:

  - Medical virtual reality, which uses VR to simulate surgical procedures, train medical students, diagnose and treat patients, and provide therapy and rehabilitation.
  - Educational virtual reality, which uses VR to enhance learning outcomes, engage students, and provide immersive and interactive experiences.
  - Scientific visualization, which uses VR to display and manipulate large and complex data sets, such as molecular models, astronomical simulations, or statistical results.
  - Entertainment virtual reality, which uses VR to create immersive and realistic games, movies, and simulations, such as roller coasters, sports, or horror.
  - Social virtual reality, which uses VR to facilitate communication, collaboration, and social interaction among users, such as online meetings, chat rooms, or virtual concerts.

- Scientific landmarks in virtual reality can also be related to specific concepts or techniques, such as:

  - Presence, which is the sense of being in a virtual environment and the degree of immersion and realism it provides.
  - Interaction, which is the ability to manipulate and control objects and events in a virtual environment using various input devices, such as controllers, gloves, or voice.
  - Feedback, which is the response from a virtual environment to the user's actions and inputs, such as visual, auditory, or haptic cues.
  - Navigation, which is the ability to move and orient oneself in a virtual environment using various methods, such as walking, flying, or teleporting.
  - Spatial cognition, which is the ability to acquire and use spatial knowledge and skills in a virtual environment, such as landmarks, routes, or maps.
