### Requirements for VR

- VR is the simulation of a three-dimensional environment that can be interacted with and explored by a user.
- VR requires the following components to create a realistic and immersive experience:
  - A display device that can present stereoscopic images to the user's eyes, such as a head-mounted display (HMD), a projection screen, or a cave automatic virtual environment (CAVE).
  - A tracking system that can measure the user's head and body movements and update the virtual scene accordingly, such as optical, magnetic, inertial, or hybrid sensors.
  - A rendering system that can generate the virtual scene in real time and adjust it to the user's perspective, such as a computer, a graphics card, or a cloud server.
  - An audio system that can provide spatial sound to the user's ears, such as headphones, speakers, or bone conduction devices.
  - An input device that can enable the user to interact with the virtual environment, such as a controller, a glove, a wand, or a gesture recognition system.
  - A feedback system that can provide haptic, tactile, or olfactory sensations to the user, such as a vest, a suit, a fan, or a scent dispenser.
- VR also requires the following factors to ensure the user's comfort and safety:
  - A high frame rate and low latency to avoid motion sickness and simulator sickness, which are caused by the mismatch between the user's visual and vestibular systems.
  - A wide field of view and high resolution to enhance the sense of presence and immersion, which are the feelings of being in and belonging to the virtual environment.
  - A realistic and consistent physics and lighting model to maintain the user's suspension of disbelief and avoid breaking the illusion of reality.
  - A clear and intuitive user interface and interaction design to facilitate the user's navigation and manipulation of the virtual environment.
  - A suitable and engaging content and narrative to capture the user's attention and motivation and provide a meaningful and enjoyable experience.
