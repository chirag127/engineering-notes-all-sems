### Direct Human Input for 3D User Interface Input Hardware

- Direct human input refers to the use of the human body or parts of it as input devices for 3D user interfaces (3D UIs).
- Direct human input can provide natural and intuitive interaction with 3D environments, as well as high degrees of freedom (DOF) and accuracy.
- Direct human input can be classified into two categories: gesture-based and touch-based.
- Gesture-based input uses the motion or posture of the hands, fingers, arms, head, or body to control 3D UIs. Examples of gesture-based input devices are:
  - Data gloves: wearable devices that sense the position and orientation of the hand and fingers, and sometimes provide haptic feedback.
  - Hand trackers: devices that track the position and orientation of the hand in 3D space, without requiring the user to wear anything on the hand.
  - Motion capture systems: devices that capture the movement of the whole body or parts of it, using cameras, markers, or sensors.
  - Eye trackers: devices that track the direction of the user's gaze, which can be used as a pointing or selection method.
- Touch-based input uses the contact or proximity of the fingers or hands with a surface or an object to control 3D UIs. Examples of touch-based input devices are:
  - Touchscreens: devices that sense the location and pressure of the finger or stylus on a 2D surface, and sometimes provide haptic feedback.
  - Touchpads: devices that sense the relative motion of the finger on a 2D surface, and sometimes provide haptic feedback.
  - Touch tables: devices that sense the location and pressure of multiple fingers or objects on a large 2D surface, and sometimes provide haptic feedback.
  - Tangible user interfaces: devices that use physical objects as input and output for 3D UIs, by sensing their position, orientation, or state.
