### Evaluating Wayfinding Aids for 3D Interaction Techniques

- Wayfinding aids (WATs) are tools or techniques that help users to navigate and orient themselves in 3D environments, such as virtual reality (VR) or augmented reality (AR).
- WATs can be classified into four categories: maps, compasses, landmarks and signs .
- Maps provide an overview of the environment and show the user's location and orientation. They can be either static or dynamic, and either embedded in the environment or displayed in a separate window.
- Compasses indicate the direction of a goal or a reference point, such as north. They can be either graphical or textual, and either fixed or movable.
- Landmarks are distinctive features or objects in the environment that help users to recognize their position and direction. They can be either natural or artificial, and either permanent or temporary.
- Signs are symbols or texts that provide information or instructions for navigation. They can be either directional or informational, and either explicit or implicit.
- Evaluating WATs is important to understand their effectiveness, efficiency and satisfaction for different tasks and users in 3D environments.
- Effectiveness measures how well the WATs help users to achieve their navigation goals, such as finding a target or completing a route. It can be assessed by metrics such as accuracy, error rate, completion rate, or task performance.
- Efficiency measures how much time, effort and resources the WATs require from users to achieve their navigation goals. It can be assessed by metrics such as time, distance, speed, or workload.
- Satisfaction measures how users feel about using the WATs, such as their preference, enjoyment, confidence, or frustration. It can be assessed by metrics such as subjective ratings, questionnaires, interviews, or observations.
- Evaluating WATs can be done by using different methods, such as experiments, surveys, simulations, or analytics.
- Experiments are controlled studies that compare different WATs or conditions in terms of their effects on users and tasks. They can be either laboratory-based or field-based, and either between-subjects or within-subjects.
- Surveys are methods that collect users' opinions, feedback, or experiences about using WATs. They can be either quantitative or qualitative, and either online or offline.
- Simulations are methods that model the behavior and performance of users and WATs in 3D environments. They can be either analytical or empirical, and either deterministic or stochastic.
- Analytics are methods that analyze the data generated by users and WATs in 3D environments. They can be either descriptive or predictive, and either online or offline.
- Evaluating WATs can help to design and improve 3D interaction techniques that support navigation and orientation in 3D environments. It can also help to identify the strengths and weaknesses of different WATs, and to provide guidelines and recommendations for their use.
