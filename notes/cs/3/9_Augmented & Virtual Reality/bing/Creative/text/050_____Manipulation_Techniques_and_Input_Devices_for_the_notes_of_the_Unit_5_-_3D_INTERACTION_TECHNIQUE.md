### Manipulation Techniques and Input Devices for 3D Interaction

- Manipulation of three dimensional (3D) data includes rotation, translation and scaling (RTS) tasks. Positioning of a virtual object can be subdivided into an initial selection, a coarse, large movement and a final, precise movement.
- The efficiency and adequacy of a manipulation technique depends strongly on the task. Different input and output devices, such as mouse, touchscreen, gestures, 2D-based monitors or 3D-based head mounted displays (HMDs), may require different manipulation techniques to suit the user's needs and preferences.
- The most prominent types of 3D user interfaces (UIs) involve a physical 3D spatial context, used for input. The user provides input to the system by making movements in physical 3D space or manipulating tools, sensors, or devices in 3D space, without regard for what this input is used to do or control.
- Some examples of manipulation techniques and input devices for 3D interaction are:

  - **Ray-casting**: The user points a ray from a hand-held device or a finger at a virtual object and selects it by pressing a button or making a gesture. The user can then move the object along the ray by moving the device or the finger, or rotate the object by rotating the device or the finger.
  - **Virtual hand**: The user wears a glove or a device that tracks the position and orientation of the hand and the fingers. The user can then grab and manipulate virtual objects as if they were real, using natural hand gestures.
  - **Haptic feedback**: The user wears a device that provides force feedback to the hand or the fingers, simulating the physical properties of the virtual objects, such as weight, shape, texture, and friction. The user can then feel the object and manipulate it more realistically.
  - **Multi-touch**: The user touches a 2D surface, such as a touchscreen or a table, with one or more fingers, and performs gestures to manipulate 3D objects displayed on the surface or on a separate screen. The user can use different gestures to perform different RTS tasks, such as pinching, spreading, rotating, or dragging.
  - **Device movement**: The user holds a device, such as a smartphone or a tablet, and moves it in 3D space to manipulate 3D objects displayed on the device's screen. The device's position and orientation are tracked by sensors, such as accelerometers, gyroscopes, or cameras.
  - **Combination of multi-touch and device movement**: The user holds a device with a touchscreen and performs multi-touch gestures on the screen, while also moving the device in 3D space, to manipulate 3D objects displayed on the screen. This technique combines the advantages of both multi-touch and device movement, such as high accuracy, efficiency, and intuitiveness.
