### Design Guidelines for the notes of the Unit 5 - 3D INTERACTION TECHNIQUES in the subject of Augmented & Virtual Reality

- 3D interaction techniques are methods for manipulating and interacting with 3D objects and environments in virtual reality (VR) and augmented reality (AR) applications.
- 3D interaction techniques can be classified into four categories: selection, manipulation, navigation, and system control.
- Selection techniques are methods for indicating or acquiring a target object or location in 3D space. Examples of selection techniques are ray-casting, cone-casting, image plane, and go-go.
- Manipulation techniques are methods for changing the position, orientation, size, or shape of a selected object in 3D space. Examples of manipulation techniques are direct manipulation, handle-based manipulation, gesture-based manipulation, and voice-based manipulation.
- Navigation techniques are methods for changing the viewpoint or location of the user in 3D space. Examples of navigation techniques are walking, flying, steering, and teleporting.
- System control techniques are methods for accessing menus, commands, or options in 3D applications. Examples of system control techniques are 2D widgets, 3D widgets, speech recognition, and gaze-based selection.
- 3D interaction techniques should be designed according to the following principles:
  - Affordance: the technique should suggest its functionality and usage by its appearance and feedback.
  - Consistency: the technique should behave in a predictable and coherent way across different contexts and applications.
  - Efficiency: the technique should minimize the physical and mental effort required by the user to perform the task.
  - Effectiveness: the technique should achieve the desired outcome and avoid errors and failures.
  - Intuitiveness: the technique should be easy to learn and use without prior knowledge or training.
  - Immersion: the technique should enhance the sense of presence and realism of the user in the 3D environment.
  - Satisfaction: the technique should be enjoyable and satisfying to use and match the user's preferences and expectations.
- 3D interaction techniques should be evaluated using both quantitative and qualitative methods, such as task performance, user feedback, usability testing, and user studies.
