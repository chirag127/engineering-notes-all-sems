# Visual Displays for 3D User Interfaces

- Visual displays are the output devices that present the 3D graphics and virtual environments to the user's eyes.
- Visual displays can be classified into two categories: fully-immersive and semi-immersive.
  - Fully-immersive displays occlude the real world and provide a high degree of presence and immersion to the user. Examples are head-mounted displays, arm-mounted displays, and virtual retinal displays.
  - Semi-immersive displays allow the user to see the real world and the virtual environment simultaneously or alternately. Examples are monitors, surround-screen displays, workbenches, hemispherical displays, and autostereoscopic displays.
- Visual displays can be characterized by several parameters, such as:
  - Field of regard (FOR): the total angular extent of the display system that can be seen by the user.
  - Field of view (FOV): the angular extent of the display that is currently visible to the user.
  - Spatial resolution: the number of pixels per unit area or angle that determine the visual quality and detail of the display.
  - Refresh rate: the frequency at which the display updates the image, measured in hertz (Hz).
  - Color depth: the number of bits used to represent the color of each pixel, which affects the range and accuracy of colors that can be displayed.
  - Stereoscopy: the technique of creating the illusion of depth by presenting slightly different images to each eye, which requires special hardware and software.
  - Latency: the delay between the user's input and the corresponding change in the display, which affects the responsiveness and realism of the interaction.
- Visual displays have different advantages and disadvantages depending on the application and the user's preferences. Some factors to consider are:
  - Cost: the price of the display hardware and software, as well as the maintenance and operation costs.
  - Portability: the size, weight, and power requirements of the display, as well as the ease of setup and transportation.
  - Comfort: the physical and psychological effects of the display on the user, such as eye strain, fatigue, nausea, and cybersickness.
  - Compatibility: the ability of the display to work with other devices and systems, such as input devices, tracking systems, and network protocols.
  - Usability: the ease of use and learnability of the display, as well as the user satisfaction and performance.
