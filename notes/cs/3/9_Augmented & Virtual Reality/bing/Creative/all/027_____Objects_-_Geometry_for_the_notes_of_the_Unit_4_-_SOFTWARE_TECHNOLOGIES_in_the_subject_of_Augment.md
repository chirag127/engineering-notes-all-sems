# Objects - Geometry

- Objects are the basic building blocks of 3D graphics. They are composed of vertices, edges, and faces that define their shape and appearance.
- Geometry is the branch of mathematics that studies the properties and relationships of shapes, such as points, lines, planes, curves, and solids.
- In 3D graphics, geometry is used to create and manipulate objects, as well as to define the coordinate system and the perspective of the scene.
- Some common types of geometry are:

  - Primitives: simple shapes such as spheres, cubes, cylinders, cones, and tori that can be created with a few parameters.
  - Meshes: complex shapes that consist of a large number of vertices, edges, and faces that form a polygonal surface.
  - Curves: smooth shapes that are defined by a series of control points and a mathematical function, such as Bezier curves, B-splines, and NURBS.
  - Surfaces: smooth shapes that are defined by a grid of control points and a mathematical function, such as Bezier surfaces, B-spline surfaces, and NURBS surfaces.
  - Volumes: solid shapes that are defined by a function that assigns a value to each point in space, such as implicit surfaces, voxel grids, and distance fields.

- Some common operations on geometry are:

  - Transformation: changing the position, orientation, or size of an object by applying a matrix or a function to its vertices.
  - Projection: mapping a 3D object onto a 2D plane, such as the screen or a texture, by applying a matrix or a function to its vertices.
  - Clipping: removing the parts of an object that are outside a given region, such as the view frustum or a clipping plane, by intersecting its edges or faces with the region boundary.
  - Tessellation: subdividing an object into smaller pieces, such as triangles or patches, by adding new vertices, edges, or faces to its geometry.
  - Shading: assigning colors, textures, or other attributes to the vertices, edges, or faces of an object, based on a lighting model or a function.
  - Culling: discarding the parts of an object that are not visible, such as the back faces or the occluded faces, by testing their orientation or depth against the view direction or the depth buffer.
