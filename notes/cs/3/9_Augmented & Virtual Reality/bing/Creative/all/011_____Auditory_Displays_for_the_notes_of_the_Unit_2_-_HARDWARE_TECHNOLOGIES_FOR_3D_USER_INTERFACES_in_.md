# Auditory Displays for 3D User Interfaces

- Auditory displays are devices that present information through the human auditory system, which is especially useful when supplying location and spatial information to the users.
- Auditory displays can complement or substitute visual displays, depending on the context and the task.
- Auditory displays can be classified into three main types: localization, generation of 3D sound, and sonification.
  - Localization refers to the ability to perceive the direction and distance of a sound source in a 3D space.
  - Generation of 3D sound involves creating realistic and immersive sound effects that match the visual scene and the user's actions.
  - Sonification is the process of transforming certain types of information into sound, such as data, events, or states.
- Auditory displays can enhance 3D user interfaces for virtual environments by providing feedback, guidance, awareness, and engagement.
- Auditory displays can also support collaborative 3D user interfaces by facilitating communication, coordination, and awareness among multiple users.
- Auditory displays require careful design and evaluation to ensure their effectiveness, usability, and user satisfaction.
- Auditory displays can involve art, design, science, and research, and can benefit from interdisciplinary and creative approaches.
