# Special Purpose Input Devices for 3D User Interfaces

- Special purpose input devices are designed to capture user input in physical 3D space or manipulate tools, sensors, or devices in 3D space for specific tasks or applications.
- Some examples of special purpose input devices are:
  - **3D trackers**: These devices measure the position and orientation of an object or a user in 3D space, using various technologies such as magnetic, optical, acoustic, or inertial sensors. They can be used to track the user's head, hand, or body movements, or to track the location and orientation of a handheld device or a tool. 3D trackers can enable natural and intuitive interaction with 3D environments or objects, such as navigation, selection, manipulation, or system control  .
  - **3D mice and spaceballs**: These devices are similar to 2D mice and trackballs, but they allow the user to control six degrees of freedom (DOF) of a cursor or an object in 3D space, by moving or rotating the device. They can be used to manipulate 3D models, navigate 3D scenes, or perform 3D gestures  .
  - **Data gloves**: These devices are worn on the user's hand and measure the finger bending and hand orientation, using sensors such as flex sensors, potentiometers, or optical fibers. They can be used to capture the user's hand gestures, such as pointing, grabbing, or pinching, or to control virtual objects or menus with natural hand movements  .
  - **Haptic devices**: These devices provide force feedback or tactile feedback to the user, by applying forces or vibrations to the user's hand, arm, or body, using actuators such as motors, solenoids, or pneumatic systems. They can be used to simulate the physical properties or interactions of virtual objects, such as shape, texture, weight, or collision, or to enhance the realism and immersion of 3D environments or applications  .
  - **Biosignal devices**: These devices measure the user's physiological signals, such as brain waves, eye movements, heart rate, or skin conductance, using electrodes, cameras, or sensors. They can be used to infer the user's mental state, attention, emotion, or intention, or to provide direct input to the system without requiring physical movement or speech  .
