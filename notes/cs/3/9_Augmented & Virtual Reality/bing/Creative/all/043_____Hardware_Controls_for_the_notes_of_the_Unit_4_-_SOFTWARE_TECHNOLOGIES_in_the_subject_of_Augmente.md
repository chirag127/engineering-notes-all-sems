# Hardware Controls for Augmented and Virtual Reality

- Hardware controls are the devices and components that enable users to interact with augmented and virtual reality applications and environments.
- Hardware controls can be classified into three main categories: input, output, and tracking.
- Input controls are the devices that allow users to provide commands, gestures, and feedback to the system. Examples of input controls are keyboards, mice, gamepads, touchscreens, voice recognition, eye tracking, and hand tracking.
- Output controls are the devices that display the augmented and virtual reality content to the users. Examples of output controls are head-mounted displays (HMDs), smart glasses, projectors, monitors, and speakers.
- Tracking controls are the devices that measure the position, orientation, and movement of the users and the environment. Examples of tracking controls are cameras, sensors, GPS, inertial measurement units (IMUs), and optical markers.
- Hardware controls can vary in their complexity, cost, performance, and compatibility. Some hardware controls are designed specifically for augmented and virtual reality, while others are adapted from existing technologies.
- Hardware controls can also differ in their level of immersion, which is the degree to which the users feel present and engaged in the augmented and virtual reality experience. Immersion can be influenced by factors such as field of view, resolution, latency, refresh rate, and interactivity.
