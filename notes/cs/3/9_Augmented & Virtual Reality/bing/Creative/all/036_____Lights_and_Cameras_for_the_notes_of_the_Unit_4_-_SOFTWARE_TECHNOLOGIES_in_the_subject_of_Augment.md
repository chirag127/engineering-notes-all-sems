# Lights and Cameras for the notes of the Unit 4 - SOFTWARE TECHNOLOGIES in the subject of Augmented & Virtual Reality

- Lights and cameras are essential components of augmented and virtual reality (AR/VR) systems, as they enable the creation and manipulation of realistic 3D scenes and objects that blend with the real world or immerse the user in a virtual environment.
- Lights and cameras can be classified into two types: real and virtual. Real lights and cameras are the physical devices that capture and emit light, such as the smartphone camera or the LiDAR scanner. Virtual lights and cameras are the software representations of light sources and viewpoints, such as the directional light or the main camera in a game engine.
- The main challenges and techniques of using lights and cameras in AR/VR are:

  - **Lighting estimation and matching**: This is the process of estimating the real-world lighting conditions and applying them to the virtual objects and scenes, so that they appear consistent and believable. Lighting estimation can be done using various sensors, such as ToF cameras, LiDAR, binocular depth sensing, or structured-light sensors. Lighting matching can be done using various methods, such as spherical harmonics, environmental HDR, or physically based rendering.
  - **Shadows and occlusions**: These are the effects of light blocking and interaction between real and virtual objects, such as the shadow cast by a virtual chair on the floor or the occlusion of a virtual character by a real wall. Shadows and occlusions can enhance the realism and depth perception of AR/VR scenes, but they are also computationally expensive and challenging to implement. Some techniques for generating shadows and occlusions are shadow mapping, shadow volumes, depth testing, and stencil buffer.
  - **3D displays and rendering**: These are the technologies and algorithms that enable the creation and presentation of 3D scenes and objects on various devices, such as head-mounted displays, smartphones, or projectors. 3D displays and rendering can provide vivid and immersive visual experiences, but they also pose some limitations and challenges, such as resolution, latency, field of view, vergence-accommodation conflict, and motion sickness. Some techniques for improving 3D displays and rendering are stereoscopic vision, light field displays, holographic displays, and AI-based super-resolution .
