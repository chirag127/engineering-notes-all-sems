# Design Guidelines for the notes of the Unit 5 - 3D INTERACTION TECHNIQUES in the subject of Augmented & Virtual Reality

- 3D interaction techniques are methods for manipulating and interacting with 3D objects and environments in virtual reality (VR) and augmented reality (AR) applications.
- 3D interaction techniques can be classified into four categories: selection, manipulation, navigation, and system control.
- Selection techniques are methods for indicating or acquiring a target object or location in 3D space. Examples of selection techniques are ray-casting, cone-casting, and image plane selection.
- Manipulation techniques are methods for changing the position, orientation, size, or shape of a selected object or region in 3D space. Examples of manipulation techniques are direct manipulation, handle-based manipulation, and gesture-based manipulation.
- Navigation techniques are methods for changing the viewpoint or location of the user or the virtual camera in 3D space. Examples of navigation techniques are walking, flying, teleporting, and steering.
- System control techniques are methods for accessing menus, commands, or options in the application. Examples of system control techniques are voice commands, gaze-based menus, and hand-held menus.
- The design of 3D interaction techniques should consider the following factors: the task, the user, the device, the environment, and the feedback.
- The task is the goal or objective of the user in the application, such as selecting an object, manipulating an object, navigating to a location, or controlling the system. The task determines the type and complexity of the interaction technique required.
- The user is the person who performs the task in the application, such as a student, a teacher, a gamer, a designer, or a researcher. The user's characteristics, preferences, skills, and expectations influence the usability and effectiveness of the interaction technique.
- The device is the hardware and software that enable the interaction technique, such as a head-mounted display (HMD), a hand-held controller, a camera, a sensor, or a computer. The device's capabilities, limitations, and affordances affect the feasibility and performance of the interaction technique.
- The environment is the physical and virtual context in which the interaction technique is used, such as a classroom, a laboratory, a living room, or a virtual world. The environment's constraints, opportunities, and distractions influence the suitability and safety of the interaction technique.
- The feedback is the information that the interaction technique provides to the user, such as visual, auditory, haptic, or olfactory cues. The feedback's quality, quantity, and timing affect the user's awareness, understanding, and satisfaction of the interaction technique.
- The design of 3D interaction techniques should follow the principles of usability, efficiency, effectiveness, learnability, and satisfaction.
- Usability is the degree to which the interaction technique is easy to use and understand by the user. Usability can be measured by the number of errors, the time to complete the task, and the user's subjective rating.
- Efficiency is the degree to which the interaction technique minimizes the physical and mental effort required by the user. Efficiency can be measured by the number of steps, the distance traveled, and the user's fatigue level.
- Effectiveness is the degree to which the interaction technique achieves the desired outcome or result by the user. Effectiveness can be measured by the accuracy, the precision, and the user's confidence level.
- Learnability is the degree to which the interaction technique is easy to learn and remember by the user. Learnability can be measured by the time to learn, the retention rate, and the user's recall ability.
- Satisfaction is the degree to which the interaction technique is enjoyable and satisfying by the user. Satisfaction can be measured by the user's mood, attitude, and preference.
