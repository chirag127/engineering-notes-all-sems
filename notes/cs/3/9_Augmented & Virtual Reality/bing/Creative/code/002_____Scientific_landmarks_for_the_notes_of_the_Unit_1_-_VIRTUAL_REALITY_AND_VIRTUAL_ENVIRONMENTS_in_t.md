### Scientific landmarks for the notes of the Unit 1 - VIRTUAL REALITY AND VIRTUAL ENVIRONMENTS

- Scientific landmarks are significant achievements or discoveries that advance the field of virtual reality and its applications.
- Some examples of scientific landmarks in virtual reality are:

  - The first head-mounted display (HMD) developed by Ivan Sutherland in 1968, which allowed users to see computer-generated 3D graphics in a stereoscopic view.
  - The first immersive virtual environment (IVE) created by Myron Krueger in 1974, which used video cameras and projectors to track the user's movements and display them on the walls of a room.
  - The first commercial virtual reality system, the Virtuality arcade machine, launched by W Industries in 1991, which offered multiplayer games and simulations with HMDs and gloves.
  - The first virtual reality web browser, VRML (Virtual Reality Modeling Language), introduced by Mark Pesce and Tony Parisi in 1994, which enabled users to create and explore 3D worlds on the internet.
  - The first virtual reality social network, Second Life, launched by Linden Lab in 2003, which allowed users to create and customize their own avatars, interact with other users, and build virtual communities.
  - The first virtual reality headset for smartphones, Google Cardboard, released by Google in 2014, which used a simple cardboard box and lenses to turn any smartphone into a VR device.
  - The first virtual reality platform for scientists, Caltech's Virtual Reality Center, established by Professor Chiara Daraio in 2019, which used a custom-built VR system to visualize and manipulate complex data sets and models.
  - The first virtual reality heritage project, Open Heritage, launched by Google Arts & Culture and CyArk in 2018, which used 3D scanning and photogrammetry to create digital replicas of 26 cultural sites around the world.

- Scientific landmarks in virtual reality can help scientists to:

  - Demonstrate a method or convey complex ideas in a visual format.
  - Explore and analyze large and multidimensional data sets in an intuitive way.
  - Collaborate and communicate with other researchers across disciplines and locations.
  - Educate and engage the public and students in scientific topics and issues.
  - Preserve and protect the cultural and natural heritage of humanity.
