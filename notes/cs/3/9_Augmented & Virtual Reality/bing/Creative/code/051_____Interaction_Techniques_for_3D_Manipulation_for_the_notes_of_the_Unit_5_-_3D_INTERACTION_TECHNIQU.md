# Interaction Techniques for 3D Manipulation

- Interaction techniques for 3D manipulation are methods that allow users to control and modify 3D objects in virtual or augmented environments.
- 3D manipulation techniques can be classified into three main categories: device-based, mid-air gestures, and volume-based.
- Device-based techniques use the physical attributes of a handheld device, such as rotation, tilt, skew, or movement, to manipulate 3D objects. For example, users can rotate a device to rotate an object, or move a device closer or farther to scale an object.
- Mid-air gestures techniques use the tracking of bare hands or finger gestures to manipulate 3D objects. For example, users can pinch, swipe, or tap in the air to select, move, or rotate an object.
- Volume-based techniques use the projection of a 3D volume or a 2D plane onto the 3D scene to manipulate 3D objects. For example, users can use a flashlight to select objects within a cone-shaped volume, or use an aperture to select objects within a circular area .
- Some common tasks that require 3D manipulation techniques are selection, translation, rotation, scaling, and deformation.
- Selection is the process of identifying and highlighting an object or a group of objects for further manipulation. Some examples of selection techniques are ray casting, fishing reel, flashlight, and aperture .
- Translation is the process of moving an object or a group of objects along one or more axes. Some examples of translation techniques are drag-and-drop, go-go, HOMER, and image plane .
- Rotation is the process of changing the orientation of an object or a group of objects around one or more axes. Some examples of rotation techniques are virtual sphere, arcball, handlebar, and torque .
- Scaling is the process of changing the size of an object or a group of objects along one or more axes. Some examples of scaling techniques are uniform scaling, non-uniform scaling, and bimanual scaling .
- Deformation is the process of changing the shape of an object or a group of objects by applying forces or constraints. Some examples of deformation techniques are free-form deformation, sketch-based deformation, and physics-based deformation .
- Some challenges and trade-offs for designing and evaluating 3D manipulation techniques are accuracy, efficiency, ease of use, learnability, feedback, consistency, and naturalness .
