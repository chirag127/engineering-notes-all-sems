### 3D Manipulation Tasks

3D manipulation tasks are the tasks of physically handling objects in a 3D environment with one or two hands. They are essential for interacting with virtual objects and creating immersive and realistic experiences. 3D manipulation tasks can be divided into three basic categories: selection, positioning, and rotation  .

- Selection is the task of acquiring or identifying a particular object or subset of objects from the entire set of objects available. Selection can be done by pointing, ray-casting, touching, or using a cursor   .
- Positioning is the task of changing the 3D position of an object. Positioning can be done by direct manipulation, indirect manipulation, or using a 3D widget  .
- Rotation is the task of changing the orientation of an object. Rotation can be done by direct manipulation, indirect manipulation, or using a 3D widget  .

3D manipulation tasks can be performed using different input devices and techniques, such as mouse, keyboard, joystick, glove, tracker, or gesture. The choice of input device and technique depends on the number and integration of control dimensions, the force or position control, and the feedback and feedforward mechanisms  .

Some examples of 3D manipulation techniques are:

- Virtual hand: a technique that uses a tracked hand or glove to directly manipulate objects in a 3D environment .
- Go-go technique: a technique that extends the reach of the virtual hand by scaling the mapping between the physical and virtual hand positions based on the distance from the body .
- HOMER technique: a technique that combines ray-casting and virtual hand techniques to allow selection and manipulation of distant objects .
- 3D widgets: graphical user interface elements that provide visual and interactive cues for manipulating objects in 3D, such as translation, rotation, or scaling widgets .
- 3D cursor: a technique that uses a 2D input device, such as a mouse, to control a 3D cursor that can select and manipulate objects in 3D .
