### Symbolic Input Tasks

- Symbolic input is the task in which users communicate symbolic information (text, numbers, and other symbols or marks) to the system  .
- Symbolic input is common in 2D interfaces, such as writing email messages, entering numbers into a spreadsheet, composing documents, etc .
- Symbolic input is challenging in 3D interfaces, because of the lack of standard input devices, the difficulty of text entry and recognition, the complexity of 3D layout and rendering, and the trade-off between immersion and usability  .
- Symbolic input can be classified into two categories: discrete and continuous .
  - Discrete symbolic input is the input of individual symbols or characters, such as typing on a keyboard, clicking on a virtual keyboard, or using speech recognition .
  - Continuous symbolic input is the input of continuous streams of symbols or marks, such as handwriting, sketching, or gesturing .
- Symbolic input can be performed using various techniques, such as:
  - Physical keyboards, which are familiar and efficient, but may break the immersion and require extra hardware .
  - Virtual keyboards, which are integrated into the 3D environment, but may be slow, inaccurate, and occlude the scene .
  - Speech input, which is natural and hands-free, but may suffer from recognition errors, noise, and privacy issues .
  - Handwriting input, which is intuitive and expressive, but may require special input devices, recognition algorithms, and feedback mechanisms .
  - Sketching input, which is similar to handwriting, but allows for more complex shapes and annotations .
  - Gesture input, which is fast and direct, but may require training, memorization, and recognition algorithms .
- Symbolic input can be evaluated using various criteria, such as:
  - Speed, which measures how fast the user can enter the symbols or marks .
  - Accuracy, which measures how correctly the system can recognize the symbols or marks .
  - Ease of use, which measures how comfortable and intuitive the user feels with the technique .
  - Immersion, which measures how well the technique preserves the sense of presence and engagement in the 3D environment .
  - Compatibility, which measures how well the technique fits with the 3D application and the user's task .
