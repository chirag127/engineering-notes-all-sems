# Travel Techniques

Travel techniques are methods for moving the user's viewpoint in a 3D virtual environment. Travel is the motor component of navigation, which also involves wayfinding (the cognitive process of determining and following a route). Travel is the most basic and common 3D interaction technique, used in almost any large-scale virtual environment.

Travel techniques can be classified by the metaphor they use, such as:

- Walking: the user moves the viewpoint by simulating the natural motion of walking, either by using physical input devices (such as treadmills, pedals, or gesture sensors) or by using non-physical input devices (such as keyboards, mice, or joysticks). Walking techniques aim to provide a high sense of presence and spatial awareness, but they may also cause fatigue, motion sickness, or spatial disorientation .
- Steering: the user moves the viewpoint by controlling the direction and speed of a virtual vehicle, such as a car, a plane, or a spaceship. Steering techniques use non-physical input devices, such as keyboards, mice, joysticks, or head trackers. Steering techniques are suitable for exploring large and complex environments, but they may also cause loss of control, motion sickness, or cognitive overload .
- Selection-based travel: the user moves the viewpoint by selecting a target location or direction in the virtual environment, either by pointing, clicking, or gazing. Selection-based travel techniques use non-physical input devices, such as mice, touchscreens, or eye trackers. Selection-based travel techniques are simple and efficient, but they may also cause spatial discontinuity, loss of context, or lack of feedback .
- Manipulation-based travel: the user moves the viewpoint by manipulating the virtual environment, such as grabbing, dragging, or scaling. Manipulation-based travel techniques use non-physical input devices, such as mice, touchscreens, or gesture sensors. Manipulation-based travel techniques are intuitive and flexible, but they may also cause distortion, occlusion, or inconsistency .

Some other aspects of travel techniques are:

- Input devices: the devices used to provide input for travel techniques, such as keyboards, mice, joysticks, head trackers, eye trackers, gesture sensors, treadmills, pedals, etc. Input devices affect the usability, performance, and realism of travel techniques .
- Output devices: the devices used to display the virtual environment, such as monitors, projectors, head-mounted displays, or augmented reality glasses. Output devices affect the immersion, presence, and spatial awareness of travel techniques .
- Feedback: the information provided to the user about the travel state, such as speed, direction, distance, or orientation. Feedback can be visual, auditory, or haptic. Feedback affects the accuracy, efficiency, and satisfaction of travel techniques .
- Constraints: the limitations or restrictions imposed on the travel techniques, such as physical boundaries, collision detection, or navigation aids. Constraints affect the safety, realism, and complexity of travel techniques .
- Perspectives: the viewpoints or cameras used to display the virtual environment, such as first-person, third-person, or bird's-eye. Perspectives affect the visibility, orientation, and scale of travel techniques   .
