# Taxonomy for the notes of the Unit 8 - Augmented and Mixed Reality

- Taxonomy is a systematic way of classifying and organizing concepts or objects based on their characteristics, relationships, and purposes.
- Augmented reality (AR) is a technology that enhances the perception of the real world by overlaying virtual elements on it, such as images, text, sounds, or haptic feedback.
- Mixed reality (MR) is a technology that combines the real and virtual worlds in a seamless way, allowing interaction and manipulation of both physical and digital objects.
- A taxonomy of AR and MR applications can help to understand the different types, features, and goals of these technologies, as well as to identify the challenges and opportunities for their development and use.
- Several taxonomies of AR and MR applications have been proposed in the literature, based on different criteria and perspectives, such as educational objectives, visualization techniques, interaction design, medical purposes, and visual displays.
- One possible taxonomy of AR and MR applications is based on the following facets :

  - **Educational purpose**: the intended learning outcome or objective of the application, such as knowledge, comprehension, application, analysis, synthesis, or evaluation.
  - **Application domain**: the specific field or area of knowledge or practice that the application supports, such as science, engineering, arts, humanities, or health.
  - **Application type**: the general category or genre of the application, such as simulation, game, tutorial, exploration, or collaboration.
  - **Output technology**: the hardware and software components that enable the generation and presentation of the virtual elements, such as head-mounted displays, handheld devices, projection systems, or spatial audio systems.
  - **Visual guidance**: the methods and techniques that help the user to perceive and understand the virtual elements and their relation to the real world, such as registration, occlusion, depth cues, annotations, or metaphors.
  - **Interaction technique**: the methods and techniques that enable the user to interact and manipulate the virtual and physical elements, such as gestures, voice commands, touch, or tangible interfaces.

- An example of an AR application based on this taxonomy is:

  - **Educational purpose**: application (applying learned concepts or skills to new situations or problems)
  - **Application domain**: engineering (designing and testing mechanical systems or structures)
  - **Application type**: simulation (creating and manipulating a virtual model of a real system or phenomenon)
  - **Output technology**: handheld device (using a smartphone or tablet to view the virtual model on the screen)
  - **Visual guidance**: registration (aligning the virtual model with the real environment using markers or sensors)
  - **Interaction technique**: touch (using the touchscreen to select, move, rotate, or scale the virtual model)

- An example of an MR application based on this taxonomy is:

  - **Educational purpose**: synthesis (creating or designing a new product or process based on existing knowledge or information)
  - **Application domain**: arts (creating and exhibiting artistic expressions or works)
  - **Application type**: exploration (discovering and experiencing a virtual environment or scenario)
  - **Output technology**: head-mounted display (using a headset or glasses to see the virtual environment in 3D)
  - **Visual guidance**: occlusion (making the virtual elements appear behind or in front of the real elements depending on their distance)
  - **Interaction technique**: gesture (using hand or body movements to navigate, select, or manipulate the virtual elements)

- This taxonomy is not exhaustive or definitive, but rather a possible way of organizing and describing AR and MR applications. Other taxonomies may use different facets or categories, depending on the focus and scope of the analysis. The taxonomy can be used as a tool for designing, developing, evaluating, or comparing AR and MR applications, as well as for identifying gaps and opportunities for future research and innovation.
