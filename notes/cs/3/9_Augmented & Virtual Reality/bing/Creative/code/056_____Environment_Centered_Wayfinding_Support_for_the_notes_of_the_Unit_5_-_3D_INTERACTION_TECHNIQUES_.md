### Environment Centered Wayfinding Support

- Environment centered wayfinding support refers to the use of cues and landmarks in the virtual environment to facilitate navigation and orientation.
- Environment centered wayfinding support can be classified into two types: natural and artificial.
- Natural wayfinding support relies on the inherent features of the environment, such as terrain, buildings, vegetation, etc. Natural wayfinding support can be enhanced by using realistic graphics, lighting, shadows, textures, etc.
- Artificial wayfinding support involves adding extra elements to the environment, such as signs, maps, arrows, trails, etc. Artificial wayfinding support can be static or dynamic, depending on whether they change according to the user's position and direction.
- Environment centered wayfinding support can be evaluated by measuring the user's performance, behavior, and subjective experience in the virtual environment. Some common metrics are: time, distance, errors, revisits, landmarks recognition, cognitive map accuracy, presence, satisfaction, etc.
- Environment centered wayfinding support can be designed by following some general guidelines, such as: providing multiple cues and landmarks, using consistent and meaningful symbols, avoiding clutter and occlusion, providing feedback and feedforward, etc.
