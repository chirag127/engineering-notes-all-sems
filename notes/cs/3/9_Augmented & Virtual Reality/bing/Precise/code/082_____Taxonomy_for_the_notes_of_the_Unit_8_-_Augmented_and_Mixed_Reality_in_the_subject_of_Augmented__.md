### Taxonomy for the notes of the Unit 8 - Augmented and Mixed Reality in the subject of Augmented & Virtual Reality

1. **Definition**: Taxonomy is the practice and science of classification. In the context of Augmented and Mixed Reality, taxonomy refers to the classification of different types of AR and MR experiences and technologies.

2. **Purpose**: The purpose of taxonomy in AR and MR is to provide a common language and framework for discussing, comparing, and evaluating different AR and MR experiences and technologies.

3. **Classification Criteria**: AR and MR experiences and technologies can be classified based on various criteria, such as the level of immersion, the type of display technology used, the type of interaction, and the type of content.

4. **Examples of Taxonomies**: Some examples of taxonomies used in AR and MR include the Reality-Virtuality Continuum, which classifies experiences based on the level of immersion, and the Milgram and Kishino taxonomy, which classifies experiences based on the type of display technology used.

5. **Applications**: Taxonomy in AR and MR can be used in various applications, such as in the design and development of AR and MR experiences, in the evaluation of AR and MR technologies, and in the communication and collaboration among researchers and practitioners in the field of AR and MR.

6. **Challenges**: One of the challenges in developing a taxonomy for AR and MR is the rapidly evolving nature of the field, which can make it difficult to establish a stable and widely accepted classification system. Another challenge is the need to balance the desire for a comprehensive and detailed taxonomy with the need for a simple and easy-to-use classification system.

7. **Conclusion**: Taxonomy is an important tool for the study and advancement of Augmented and Mixed Reality. By providing a common language and framework for discussing, comparing, and evaluating different AR and MR experiences and technologies, taxonomy can help to facilitate communication and collaboration among researchers and practitioners in the field. However, developing a widely accepted and useful taxonomy for AR and MR is an ongoing challenge, due to the rapidly evolving nature of the field and the need to balance comprehensiveness with simplicity.
