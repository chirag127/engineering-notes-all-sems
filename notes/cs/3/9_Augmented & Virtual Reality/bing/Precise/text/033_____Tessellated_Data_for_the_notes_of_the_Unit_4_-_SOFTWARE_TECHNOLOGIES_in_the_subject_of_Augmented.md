### Tessellated Data

- Tessellated data refers to the process of dividing a surface into a series of smaller, non-overlapping polygons, typically triangles, to create a mesh.
- This process is commonly used in computer graphics, particularly in the field of 3D modeling and rendering, to represent complex surfaces and shapes.
- Tessellation can improve the visual quality of 3D models by increasing the level of detail and smoothness of the surface.
- In the context of Augmented and Virtual Reality, tessellated data can be used to create more realistic and immersive virtual environments.
- Tessellation can be performed in real-time, allowing for dynamic changes to the level of detail based on the user's perspective and distance from the object.
- There are various algorithms and techniques for tessellating data, including Delaunay triangulation and Voronoi tessellation.
- Tessellated data can also be used for collision detection and physics simulations in virtual environments.
- The use of tessellated data can increase the computational demands of rendering, so it is important to balance the level of detail with performance considerations.
