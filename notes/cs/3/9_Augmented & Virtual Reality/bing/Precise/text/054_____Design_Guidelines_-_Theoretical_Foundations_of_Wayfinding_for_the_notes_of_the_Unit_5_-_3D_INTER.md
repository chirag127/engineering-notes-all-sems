### Design Guidelines - Theoretical Foundations of Wayfinding

1. Wayfinding is the process of navigating through an environment to reach a destination.
2. It involves the use of spatial information, such as landmarks, signs, and maps, to guide the user.
3. Theoretical foundations of wayfinding include cognitive psychology, environmental psychology, and spatial cognition.
4. Cognitive psychology focuses on how people process and use information to make decisions and solve problems.
5. Environmental psychology studies the relationship between people and their physical surroundings, including how the environment affects behavior and cognition.
6. Spatial cognition is the study of how people perceive, represent, and manipulate spatial information.
7. These theoretical foundations inform the design of effective wayfinding systems by providing insights into how people navigate and process spatial information.
8. Design guidelines for wayfinding systems include providing clear and consistent signage, using landmarks and other visual cues, and providing maps and other navigational aids.
9. Wayfinding systems should also be designed to be intuitive and easy to use, taking into account the needs and abilities of the user.
10. Effective wayfinding systems can improve the user experience by reducing confusion and frustration, and increasing confidence and efficiency in navigation.
