### Taxonomy for the notes of the Unit 8 - Augmented and Mixed Reality in the subject of Augmented & Virtual Reality

1. **Definition**: Taxonomy is the practice and science of classification. In the context of Augmented and Mixed Reality, taxonomy refers to the classification of different types of AR and MR experiences and technologies.
2. **Purpose**: The purpose of taxonomy in AR and MR is to provide a common language and framework for discussing and comparing different AR and MR experiences and technologies. This can help in the development and evaluation of AR and MR applications.
3. **Types of Taxonomies**: There are several taxonomies that have been proposed for AR and MR. Some common ones include:
    - **Milgram's Reality-Virtuality Continuum**: This taxonomy classifies AR and MR experiences based on the degree to which they mix real and virtual elements.
    - **Azuma's AR Taxonomy**: This taxonomy classifies AR experiences based on the type of display used (e.g. head-mounted, handheld, spatial), the type of tracking used (e.g. optical, inertial, magnetic), and the type of interaction used (e.g. gesture, voice, touch).
    - **Billinghurst's MR Taxonomy**: This taxonomy classifies MR experiences based on the type of display used (e.g. head-mounted, handheld, projection), the type of environment (e.g. indoors, outdoors, hybrid), and the level of immersion (e.g. low, medium, high).
4. **Applications**: Taxonomies can be used in various ways in the development and evaluation of AR and MR applications. For example, they can be used to:
    - **Compare and contrast** different AR and MR experiences and technologies.
    - **Identify gaps** in the current state of AR and MR technology and research.
    - **Guide the design** of new AR and MR experiences and technologies.
    - **Evaluate** the effectiveness of AR and MR experiences and technologies.
