### Bounding Volume

- A bounding volume is a shape that completely encloses an object or a group of objects.
- It is used in computer graphics to optimize collision detection and rendering.
- Bounding volumes can be of different shapes, such as spheres, boxes, cylinders, or convex hulls.
- The choice of the shape of the bounding volume depends on the application and the trade-off between accuracy and computational efficiency.
- Bounding volumes can be organized in a hierarchy, where the parent volume encloses all its children volumes.
- This hierarchy can be used to speed up collision detection and rendering by culling objects that are not visible or not in contact with other objects.
- Bounding volumes can also be used to accelerate ray tracing and other geometric queries.
- In the context of Augmented & Virtual Reality, bounding volumes can be used to improve the performance of the application by reducing the number of objects that need to be processed in real-time.
