# Augmented Reality Methods

Augmented reality (AR) is a technology that enhances the user's perception of the real world by overlaying digital information onto it. There are several methods used to achieve this, including:

1. **Marker-based AR:** This method uses a physical marker, such as a QR code or image, to trigger the display of digital information. The device's camera is used to recognize the marker, and the digital content is then overlaid onto the real world in the correct position relative to the marker.

2. **Markerless AR:** This method does not rely on a physical marker, but instead uses the device's sensors, such as the accelerometer and gyroscope, to determine the position and orientation of the device. The digital content is then overlaid onto the real world based on this information.

3. **Projection-based AR:** This method involves projecting digital content onto a physical surface, such as a wall or table. The digital content is then visible to the user without the need for a device.

4. **Superimposition-based AR:** This method replaces the view of the real world with a digitally augmented view. The device's camera is used to capture the real world, and the digital content is then overlaid onto the image, replacing the original view.

These are some of the most common methods used in augmented reality, each with its own advantages and limitations. It is important to choose the right method for the specific application to achieve the best results.
