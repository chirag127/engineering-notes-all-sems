### Marker-less Tracking for Augmented Reality

Marker-less tracking is a technique used in augmented reality (AR) applications to map environments and display AR content without the need for target images or objects (markers). This technique is also known as SLAM (Simultaneous Localization and Mapping) marker-less AR tracking.

There are several algorithms and classifiers used for marker-less tracking in AR. For example, the ORB algorithm is used for feature detection and the FREAK algorithm is used for feature description. The classifiers used for the tracking task include KNN, Random Forest, Extremely Randomized Trees, SVM, and Bayes classifier.

Visual marker-less pose trackers mainly rely on natural feature points (often also called interest points or key points) visible in the user’s environment. To allow for an accurate pose determination, such natural feature points must meet several requirements, including fast computational time.

In summary, marker-less tracking is a technique used in AR applications to map environments and display AR content without the need for markers. Several algorithms and classifiers are used for this task, and natural feature points play a key role in the accuracy of the pose determination.
