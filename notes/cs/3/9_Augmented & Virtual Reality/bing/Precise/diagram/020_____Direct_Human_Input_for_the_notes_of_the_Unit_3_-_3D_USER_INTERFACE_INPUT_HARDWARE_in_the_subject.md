### Direct Human Input

Direct human input refers to the use of natural human movements and actions to interact with and control a 3D user interface. This type of input is commonly used in augmented and virtual reality environments, where users can interact with virtual objects and environments using their own body movements.

Some common examples of direct human input include:
- Hand gestures: Users can use hand gestures to interact with virtual objects, such as pinching to zoom in or out, or swiping to navigate through menus.
- Body movements: Users can use their body movements to control their virtual avatar, such as walking in place to move forward or turning their head to look around.
- Voice commands: Users can use their voice to issue commands or interact with virtual assistants, such as saying "open menu" to access a menu or "search for restaurants" to find nearby dining options.

Direct human input allows for a more natural and intuitive interaction with virtual environments, as users can use their own movements and actions to control the interface. This can improve the overall user experience and make it easier for users to navigate and interact with virtual environments. However, it can also present challenges, such as accurately tracking and interpreting user movements and gestures, and ensuring that the interface is responsive and easy to use.
