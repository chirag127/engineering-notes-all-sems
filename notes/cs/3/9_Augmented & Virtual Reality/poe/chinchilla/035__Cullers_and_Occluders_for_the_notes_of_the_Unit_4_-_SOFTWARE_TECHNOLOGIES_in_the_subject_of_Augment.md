### Cullers and Occluders

Cullers and occluders are critical components of augmented and virtual reality software technologies. They are used to optimize the rendering process and improve the overall performance of AR and VR applications. In this section, we will discuss cullers and occluders in detail.

#### Cullers

Culling is a technique used in computer graphics to eliminate objects that are not visible from the camera's viewpoint. It is an essential process to improve the rendering performance of AR and VR applications. A culler is a software component that is responsible for culling out the invisible objects from the scene.

Some of the common types of cullers used in AR and VR applications are:

- View Frustum Cullers: This type of culler is used to eliminate objects that are outside the camera's frustum. It is an efficient technique to cull out objects that are not visible from the camera's viewpoint.

- Occlusion Cullers: This type of culler is used to eliminate objects that are occluded by other objects in the scene. It is an effective technique to optimize the rendering process by eliminating the hidden objects from the scene.

#### Occluders

Occluders are objects that are used to hide other objects in the scene. They are used to optimize the rendering process by reducing the number of objects that need to be rendered. Occluders are typically used in conjunction with occlusion cullers to improve the performance of AR and VR applications.

Some of the common types of occluders used in AR and VR applications are:

- Static Occluders: These are static objects that are placed in the scene to hide other objects. They are typically used in indoor environments where the occlusion geometry is known in advance.

- Dynamic Occluders: These are dynamic objects that are used to hide other objects in the scene. They are typically used in outdoor environments where the occlusion geometry is not known in advance.

In conclusion, cullers and occluders are critical components of AR and VR software technologies. They are used to optimize the rendering process and improve the overall performance of AR and VR applications. By using cullers and occluders, developers can create more immersive and engaging AR and VR experiences for their users.
