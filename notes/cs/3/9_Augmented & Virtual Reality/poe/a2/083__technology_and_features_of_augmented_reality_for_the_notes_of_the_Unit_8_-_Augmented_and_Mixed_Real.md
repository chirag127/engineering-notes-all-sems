### Technology and features of augmented reality

1. Marker-based AR: Uses special markers or codes to trigger the AR experience. The device camera detects the marker and displays the AR elements. The markers can be images, QR codes, etc. The AR elements are aligned with the marker and placed in the real world.
2. Location-based AR: Leverages GPS, digital compasses, and accelerometers to overlay AR elements based on the user's location and orientation. The AR elements are tied to GPS coordinates so that the user experiences them at specific locations.
3. Projection-based AR: Projects AR elements onto surfaces in the real world instead of displaying them on a device screen. Uses projectors and depth sensors to detect and track surfaces. The projections can serve as interfaces or to create illusion or ambiance.
4. Outlining AR: Uses edge detection techniques to identify the outlines or contours of objects in the real world and then augment digital information over these outlines. The real-world objects are not marked or tracked, only their outlines are used as a reference to overlay AR elements.
5. Superimposition-based AR: Uses computer vision techniques to detect and track features, surfaces, or objects in the real world and overlay digital AR elements on them as if superimposed in the scene. No special markers are required but the core technology must be capable of robust detection and tracking of real-world features for accurate superimposition and alignment of AR elements.
