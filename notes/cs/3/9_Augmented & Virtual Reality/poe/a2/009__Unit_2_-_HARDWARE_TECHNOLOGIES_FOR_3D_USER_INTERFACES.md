## Unit 2 - HARDWARE TECHNOLOGIES FOR 3D USER INTERFACES

1. Tracking Technologies
- Magnetic tracking: Uses magnetic fields to track the position and orientation of objects or body parts. Magnetic trackers are robust to occlusion but susceptible to magnetic interference.
- Optical tracking: Uses cameras to track the position and orientation of objects or body parts. Optical trackers do not suffer from magnetic interference but can have issues with occlusion.
- Ultrasonic tracking: Uses ultrasonic signals to measure distances and compute the position and orientation of objects. Ultrasonic trackers are typically less accurate than magnetic or optical trackers but are inexpensive and do not require a line of sight.

2. Input Technologies
- Data gloves: Fabric gloves with sensors that capture finger motions and bending.
- Exoskeletal gloves: Rigid gloves with joints and sensors that capture complex hand poses and motions.
- Motion controllers: Hand-held controllers with buttons and tracking capabilities that allow for 3D input.
- Gesture recognition: Vision-based techniques to recognize body poses and motions as input.

3. Display Technologies
- Stereo displays: Present separate images to the left and right eyes to create an illusion of depth.
- Head-mounted displays: Displays worn on the head that present immersive 3D environments.
- Volumetric displays: Display 3D content using lightfields or rotating 2D slices.
- Augmented reality displays: Combine digital content with the real world via see-through displays or projection onto real-world surfaces.
