### Position / Orientation

- Position refers to the location of an object in a 3D space. It is defined by X, Y and Z coordinates.
- Orientation refers to the rotational configuration of an object in 3D space. It is defined by yaw, pitch and roll angles.
- Tracking position and orientation of objects is crucial for AR/VR applications. It enables the overlay of virtual objects onto real world scenes in accurate locations and orientations.
- Position and orientation tracking can be done using various sensors and technologies:
- Inertial Measurement Units (IMUs) containing accelerometers, gyroscopes and magnetometers to track accelerations and rotations.
- Optical tracking using cameras to track the positions of fiducial markers or feature points.
- Depth sensors like time-of-flight cameras or structured light depth cameras to capture depth data and 3D positions.
- Sensor fusion combines measurements from multiple sensors to achieve robust and accurate tracking. Kalman filters and complementary filters are commonly used for this.
- Tracking latency and accuracy are important for good user experience in AR/VR. Higher latency and lower accuracy can lead to jitter, lag and registration errors.
