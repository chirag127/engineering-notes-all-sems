## Unit 8 - Augmented and Mixed Reality

1. Augmented Reality (AR) - Augmented reality is the integration of digital information with the user's environment in real time. It is used to enhance one's current perception of reality, sometimes also called mediated reality. Information about the surrounding real world is supplemented (augmented) with computer-generated data such as graphics and sounds.

2. Mixed Reality (MR) - Mixed reality (MR) is the merging of real and virtual worlds to produce new environments and visualizations where physical and digital objects co-exist and interact in real time. Mixed reality takes place not only in the physical world or the virtual world, but is a mix of reality and virtual reality, encompassing both augmented reality and augmented virtuality via immersive technology.

3. Differences between AR and MR - While augmented reality adds digital elements to a live view of the real world via a device, mixed reality blends the real world and digital elements into a hybrid reality where users cannot easily distinguish between the physical and the virtual. In mixed reality, digital elements are not just layered on top of the real world, but are embedded within the user's perception of the real world, often using head-mounted displays. Mixed reality is a type of augmented reality which is far more immersive than typical augmented reality.

4. Use cases - Augmented and mixed reality have a wide range of applications and use cases. Some examples are:

- In education, AR and MR can enhance learning experiences. Students can explore 3D models of molecules or historical sites.
- In business, AR and MR enable virtual prototyping, remote collaboration, and interactive data visualization.
- In healthcare, AR and MR allow surgeons to visualize patient scans while operating and provide enhanced rehabilitation experiences.
- In entertainment and gaming, AR and MR enable immersive experiences. Players can battle AR creatures in their living room or go on MR adventures.
