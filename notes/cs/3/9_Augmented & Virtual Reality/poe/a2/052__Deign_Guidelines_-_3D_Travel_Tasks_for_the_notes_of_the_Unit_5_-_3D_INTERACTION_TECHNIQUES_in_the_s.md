### Design Guidelines - 3D Travel Tasks

1. Provide a clear destination or goal: Having a clear end point or target for the travel task gives the user purpose and direction. Without a clear destination, the user may feel lost or unsure of what to do.

2. Keep the path straightforward: The travel path should be simple and intuitive to follow. Complex paths with many branches or forks can be confusing and frustrating for the user. Minimize the cognitive load by keeping the route as straightforward as possible.

3. Use landmarks or markers: Include memorable landmarks, markers, or other reference points along the path. These help give the user a sense of progress and orientation. However, be careful not to include too many reference points, which can also lead to confusion.

4. Control the speed: Give the user control over the speed and pace of the travel. Forcing the user to move at a fixed speed that is too fast or too slow can create a poor user experience. Allow the user to navigate at their own comfortable speed.

5. Limit distractions: Minimize additional objects, information, or tasks that could distract the user from the core travel task. Too many distractions can disrupt the user's focus and make the experience feel cluttered or disjointed. Keep the travel task as the central focus.

6. Provide feedback: Continually provide feedback to the user on their progress, location, and orientation. Feedback helps the user understand where they are in the journey and builds confidence that they are navigating correctly. Feedback can include markers of distance traveled, proximity to the destination, and more.
