### The Vedas for the notes of the Module 2- Indian Literature, Culture, Tradition, and Practices in the subject of INDIAN TRADITIONS, CULTURAL AND SOCIETY

The Vedas are a collection of sacred texts that form the oldest scriptures of Hinduism. They are considered to be the foundation of Hindu religious thought and practice. The word “Veda” means “knowledge” in Sanskrit, and the Vedas are believed to contain divine knowledge that was revealed to ancient rishis or seers.

There are four main Vedas: the Rigveda, Yajurveda, Samaveda, and Atharvaveda. The Rigveda is the oldest and most revered of the Vedas, and it contains hymns and prayers to various deities. The Yajurveda contains instructions for performing yajnas or sacrifices, and the Samaveda is a collection of hymns and songs used in the performance of these sacrifices. The Atharvaveda is a collection of spells, incantations, and hymns for various purposes, including healing and protection.

In addition to the four main Vedas, there are also a number of supplementary texts known as the Vedangas, which provide additional information on various aspects of Hindu religious life, such as pronunciation, grammar, and astronomy.

The Vedas are considered to be apauruṣeya, meaning “not of human origin.” They are believed to have been revealed to the rishis through divine inspiration, and they are considered to contain timeless truths that are applicable to all people, regardless of time or place.

The Vedas have been passed down orally from generation to generation, and they have been memorized and recited by Hindu priests for thousands of years. They are considered to be the foundation of Hinduism, and they continue to play a central role in Hindu religious life and practice.

In conclusion, the Vedas are a collection of sacred texts that form the foundation of Hinduism, and they are considered to contain divine knowledge and timeless truths. They have been passed down orally for thousands of years and continue to play a central role in Hindu religious life and practice.
