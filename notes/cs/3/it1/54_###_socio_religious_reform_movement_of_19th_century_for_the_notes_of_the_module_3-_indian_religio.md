### Socio religious reform movement of 19th century for the notes of the Module 3- Indian Religion, Philosophy, and Practices in the subject of INDIAN TRADITIONS, CULTURAL AND SOCIETY

The 19th century in India saw a series of socio-religious reform movements that aimed to modernize and revitalize Hinduism. These movements were a response to the challenges posed by colonialism, the impact of Western ideas and values, and the need to preserve Hindu cultural and religious traditions.

Some of the most prominent socio-religious reform movements of the 19th century include:

1. Brahmo Samaj: The Brahmo Samaj was founded in 1828 by Raja Ram Mohan Roy and aimed to promote monotheism and the rejection of idol worship and caste-based discrimination.

2. Prarthana Samaj: The Prarthana Samaj was founded in 1867 by Atmaram Pandurang and aimed to promote the idea of a universal God and to reject caste-based discrimination.

3. Arya Samaj: The Arya Samaj was founded in 1875 by Dayananda Saraswati and aimed to promote the revival of ancient Hindu scriptures and to reject caste-based discrimination and idol worship.

4. Ramakrishna Mission: The Ramakrishna Mission was founded in 1897 by Swami Vivekananda and aimed to promote the idea of unity of all religions and to provide social and educational services to the poor and marginalized.

These socio-religious reform movements had a profound impact on Indian society and culture, and they continue to be an important part of the religious and cultural landscape of India. They challenged traditional Hindu practices and beliefs, and sought to modernize and revitalize Hinduism in the face of colonialism and Westernization.

In conclusion, the socio-religious reform movements of the 19th century in India played a crucial role in shaping the religious and cultural landscape of the country, and they continue to be an important part of India's religious and cultural heritage.
