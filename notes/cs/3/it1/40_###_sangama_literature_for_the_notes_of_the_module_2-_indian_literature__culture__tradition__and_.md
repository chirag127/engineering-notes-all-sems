### Sangama Literature for the notes of the Module 2- Indian Literature, Culture, Tradition, and Practices in the subject of INDIAN TRADITIONS, CULTURAL AND SOCIETY

Sangam Literature refers to the classical Tamil literature that was produced between the 3rd century BCE and the 3rd century CE in the region of present-day Tamil Nadu, India. The term "Sangam" means "gathering" or "assembly" in Tamil, and refers to the gatherings of poets and scholars who composed and compiled this literature.

Sangam Literature is considered to be one of the oldest and most important works of Tamil literature, as well as one of the earliest examples of Indian epic poetry. It consists of two main collections: the Eighteen Major Anthologies (Ettuthokai) and the Ten Idylls (Pathuppattu). The former contains 2,381 poems, while the latter contains 400 poems.

The poems in Sangam Literature are primarily love poems or nature poems, and they often depict the beauty of the Tamil land and its people. They also contain elements of mythology, folklore, and religious beliefs, and they provide valuable insight into the social, cultural, and religious practices of ancient Tamil society.

Sangam Literature is also known for its use of elaborate metaphors and similes, as well as its complex and sophisticated poetic forms. The poems are written in a language that is considered to be the oldest form of the Tamil language, and they are considered to be masterpieces of Tamil literature.

In conclusion, Sangam Literature is a rich and diverse collection of classical Tamil poems that provides valuable insight into ancient Tamil society, culture, and traditions. It is an important part of Indian literary heritage and continues to be widely studied and appreciated by scholars and literary enthusiasts alike.
