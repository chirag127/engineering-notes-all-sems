# Slavery in India

- Slavery in India continued through the 18th and 19th centuries. During the colonial era, Indians were taken into different parts of the world as slaves by various European merchant companies as part of the Indian Ocean slave trade .
- Slavery in India escalated during the Muslim domination of northern India after the 11th century, when Muslim rulers re-introduced slavery to the Indian subcontinent .
- The Fatawa-i Alamgiri, a compilation of Islamic law, established the right of Muslims to purchase and own slaves, a Muslim man's right to have sex with a captive slave girl he owns or a slave girl owned by another Muslim, and established no inheritance rights for slaves. The testimony of all slaves was inadmissible in a court of law .
- Slavery is still present in India today, with an estimated 14 to 18 million people living in slavery. Slavery is a lucrative business, netting traffickers around $150 billion a year, with one-third of the profits produced in developed countries .
- Slavery can be found in brick kilns, rice mills, embroidery factories, quarries, farms, roadside brothels, and private homes .
