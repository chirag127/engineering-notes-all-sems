# Puppetry in India

Puppetry, popularly known as Kathputli, is an ancient performance art form in India that involves storytelling through the manipulation of puppets. Puppeteers travel from place to place to entertain people by performing mythological stories, historical romances, and other tales .

India is said to be the home of puppets, with almost all types of puppets found in the country. Puppetry has held an important place in traditional entertainment throughout the ages. Like traditional theatre, themes for puppet theatre are mostly based on epics and legends .

The origin of puppetry in India cannot be precisely dated, but mythology provides at least two stories that attest to the importance of this art form. According to one legend, the creator Brahma gave life to the adi, the first nat puppeteer, and created the first puppet for the entertainment of his wife Saraswati .

Puppetry in India is a family business. Children begin their apprenticeship by observing the work of their elders. A puppeteer's work includes not only the manufacture and operation of the puppets but also the memorization of texts. Family troupes pass down puppets from generation to generation as a family treasure, sometimes even used as a bride's dowry .

Kathputli, a string puppet theatre native to Rajasthan, is the most popular form of Indian puppetry. Being a string marionette, it is controlled by a single string that passes from the top of the puppet over the puppeteers .

Puppetry has philosophical significance in Indian culture, in addition to being an art form. In the Bhagavad Geeta, God is characterized as a puppeteer, directing the cosmos with three strings: Satta, Raja, and Tama. In Indian theatre, the storyteller is known as Sutradhar, which means "string bearer" .
