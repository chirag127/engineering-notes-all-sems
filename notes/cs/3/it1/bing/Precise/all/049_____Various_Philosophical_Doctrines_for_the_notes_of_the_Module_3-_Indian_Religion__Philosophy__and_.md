# Various Philosophical Doctrines

## Module 3- Indian Religion, Philosophy, and Practices

### Indian Traditions, Culture, and Society

1. **Samkhya Philosophy**: This is one of the six orthodox schools of Hindu philosophy. It is a dualistic philosophy that posits the existence of two fundamental principles: Purusha (consciousness) and Prakriti (matter).
2. **Yoga Philosophy**: This is another orthodox school of Hindu philosophy that is closely related to Samkhya philosophy. It emphasizes the practice of meditation and self-discipline to achieve spiritual liberation.
3. **Nyaya Philosophy**: This is another orthodox school of Hindu philosophy that focuses on logic and epistemology. It is concerned with the means of acquiring knowledge and the criteria for determining what is true and what is not.
4. **Vaisheshika Philosophy**: This is another orthodox school of Hindu philosophy that is closely related to Nyaya philosophy. It is concerned with the nature of reality and the classification of objects and experiences.
5. **Mimamsa Philosophy**: This is another orthodox school of Hindu philosophy that focuses on the interpretation of the Vedas, the ancient Hindu scriptures. It is concerned with the nature of dharma (duty) and the proper performance of rituals.
6. **Vedanta Philosophy**: This is another orthodox school of Hindu philosophy that is based on the Upanishads, the philosophical portions of the Vedas. It is concerned with the nature of reality and the relationship between the individual self and the ultimate reality.

These are some of the major philosophical doctrines in Indian religion, philosophy, and practices. Each of these schools of thought has its own unique perspective and approach to understanding the nature of reality and the human condition. They provide a rich and diverse tradition of philosophical inquiry and reflection.
