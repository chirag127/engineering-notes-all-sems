# Trade in Ancient India

- Trade has existed in Ancient India since time immemorial.
- Routes such as the Silk Route, Amber Road, and the Spice Route were used to trade materials from India.
- Maritime trade also flourished.
- India has thrived in trade since 2600 BC.
- During Harappan civilization, gold was the mainly traded good in the country.
- Ancient India saw trade since the Harappan civilization.
- The new cities that came up during the ancient times included Pataliputra, Ujjain, Puhar, and Mathura.
- Trade in the country during early years involved gold and then textiles, spices, as well as precious metals.
- Trade was an important source of commerce and it is thought that ancient Mesopotamian texts which mention Magan and Meluhha refer to India generally or, perhaps, Mohenjo-Daro specifically.
- Artifacts from the Indus Valley region have been found at sites in Mesopotamia though their precise point of origin in India is not always clear.
- Much of the significant data for long-distance and maritime trade across South Asia in the ancient period comes from archaeological sources.
- Textual sources too may have some things interesting and significant to say about material culture and trade in the ancient world.
