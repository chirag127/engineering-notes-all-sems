### Coins - Module 5: Cultural Heritage and Performing Arts in the subject of Indian Traditions, Culture, and Society

1. Coins have been used as a medium of exchange in India for centuries.
2. The earliest known coins in India were punch-marked coins, which were made of silver and copper and were used from the 6th century BCE.
3. The Mauryan Empire (321-185 BCE) introduced the first standardized coinage in India, which was made of silver and copper and featured images of animals and symbols.
4. During the Gupta period (320-550 CE), gold coins were introduced and became the primary medium of exchange. These coins featured images of rulers and deities and were known for their artistic excellence.
5. The Mughal Empire (1526-1857) introduced a new coinage system, which included the use of rupees, dam, and paisa. These coins were made of silver and copper and featured calligraphic inscriptions in Persian.
6. The British East India Company introduced their own coinage in the 17th century, which was made of silver and copper and featured the image of the British monarch.
7. After India gained independence in 1947, the new government introduced a new coinage system, which included the use of rupees and paise. These coins were made of various metals, including nickel, copper, and steel, and featured images of national symbols and leaders.
8. Today, coins continue to be an important part of Indian culture and heritage, and are used as a medium of exchange as well as for artistic and commemorative purposes.
