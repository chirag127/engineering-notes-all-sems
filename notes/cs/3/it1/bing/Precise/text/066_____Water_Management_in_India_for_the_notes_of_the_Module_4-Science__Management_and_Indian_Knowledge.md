### Water Management in India

Water management in India is a complex issue due to the country's large population, rapid urbanization, and changing lifestyles. Here are some key points to consider when studying water management in India:

1. **Providing Drinking Water to Rural Areas**: The government has historically been responsible for providing drinking water to 833 million rural people in some 640,000 villages across the country .

2. **Groundwater**: Over the past few decades, groundwater extraction has risen exponentially across India. India is now the world's largest user of groundwater .

3. **Improving access to water on dry rainfed lands**: This is an important aspect of water management in India, as over 50% of agriculture is still rain-fed .

4. **Cleaning the Ganga**: The Ganga river is considered sacred by many in India, and efforts are being made to clean it .

5. **Managing Floods and Droughts**: India is prone to both floods and droughts, and managing these natural disasters is an important aspect of water management .

6. **Enhancing Dam Safety in India**: Dam safety is an important issue in India, as many dams are old and in need of repair .

7. **Community-led groundwater management**: The Atal Bhujal Yojana is India’s largest community-led groundwater management program, which is helping improve rural livelihoods and build resilience in 7 Indian states which have the highest rates of groundwater depletion .

Water management in India must be holistic in nature, where the Centre, the States, and the local bodies (governance institutions) must ensure access to a minimum quantity of potable water for essential health and hygiene to all its citizens, available within easy reach of the household .
