### Varnāshrama System for the notes of the Module 1- Society State and Polity in India in the subject of INDIAN TRADITIONS, CULTURAL AND SOCIETY

- The Varnāshrama system is a social structure in Hinduism that divides society into four varnas or classes.
- The four varnas are Brahmins (priests and teachers), Kshatriyas (warriors and rulers), Vaishyas (merchants and farmers), and Shudras (laborers and artisans).
- The system is based on the principle of division of labor and is meant to ensure the smooth functioning of society.
- The Varnāshrama system also includes the concept of ashramas or stages of life, which are Brahmacharya (student life), Grihastha (householder life), Vanaprastha (retired life), and Sannyasa (renounced life).
- The system has been criticized for perpetuating social inequality and discrimination, particularly against the lower castes.
- Despite its flaws, the Varnāshrama system has played a significant role in shaping Indian society and culture.
