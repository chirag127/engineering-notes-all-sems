## Module 3- Indian Religion, Philosophy, and Practices

1. India is home to several major religions, including Hinduism, Buddhism, Jainism, and Sikhism.
2. Hinduism is the oldest and most widely practiced religion in India, with over 80% of the population identifying as Hindu.
3. Hinduism is a complex religion with many deities, beliefs, and practices, but it is generally characterized by a belief in reincarnation, karma, and the pursuit of dharma (righteousness).
4. Buddhism, founded by Siddhartha Gautama (the Buddha) in the 5th century BCE, teaches the Four Noble Truths and the Eightfold Path as a means to end suffering and attain enlightenment.
5. Jainism, founded by Mahavira in the 6th century BCE, emphasizes non-violence, truthfulness, and non-attachment as the path to liberation.
6. Sikhism, founded by Guru Nanak in the 15th century, teaches the belief in one God and the importance of living a virtuous life through service to others.
7. Indian philosophy includes several schools of thought, including Vedanta, Yoga, and Nyaya, which explore the nature of reality, consciousness, and the self.
8. Indian religious practices include rituals, festivals, and pilgrimages, as well as daily practices such as meditation, prayer, and yoga.
9. These religions and philosophies have had a profound impact on Indian culture, shaping its art, literature, and social customs.
