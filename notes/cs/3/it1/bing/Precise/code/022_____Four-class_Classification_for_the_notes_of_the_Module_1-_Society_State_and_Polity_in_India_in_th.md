### Four-class Classification for the notes of the Module 1- Society State and Polity in India in the subject of INDIAN TRADITIONS, CULTURAL AND SOCIETY

1. The four-class classification, also known as the Varna system, is a social hierarchy in Hinduism that divides society into four classes or castes.
2. The four classes are Brahmins (priests and scholars), Kshatriyas (warriors and rulers), Vaishyas (merchants and farmers), and Shudras (laborers and artisans).
3. This system is based on the principle of division of labor and is believed to have originated from the Purusha Sukta hymn in the Rigveda.
4. The Varna system has been criticized for perpetuating social inequality and discrimination, particularly against the lower castes and those outside the system, known as Dalits or untouchables.
5. Despite efforts to eradicate the caste system, it continues to influence social and economic relations in India to this day.
