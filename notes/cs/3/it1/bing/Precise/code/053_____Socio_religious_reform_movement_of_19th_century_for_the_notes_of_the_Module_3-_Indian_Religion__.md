### Socio-religious reform movement of 19th century

- The socio-religious reform movements of the 19th century provided the soil for growth of Indian Nationalism.
- The main reason for the emergence of these reform movements was the spread of western education and liberal ideas.
- These reforms started in Bengal and soon spread to all parts of India.
- There was a wave of reforming activities in India from the thirties of the 19th century, which influenced the minds of her people through the wholesome activities of different samajas, societies, and other organizations such as Brahmo Samaj, Arya Samaj, Prarthana Samaj, Aligarh movement, etc.
- The Aligarh movement was another crucial socio-religious reform movement in the 19th century, founded by Sayyid Ahmed Khan in 1875 at Mohammedan Anglo-Oriental College.
- The Indian society in the first half of the 19th century was caste-ridden, decadent, and rigid. It followed certain practices that were not in keeping with humanitarian feelings or values but were still being followed in the name of religion.
- The social and religious reform in North India was spearheaded by Swami Dayanand Saraswati (1824-1883) who founded the Arya Samaj in 1875. This society strove against idolatry, polytheism, rituals, priesthood, animal sacrifice, child marriage, and the caste system. It also encouraged the dissemination of western scientific knowledge.
- Contemporary groups, collectively termed Hindu reform movements, reform Hinduism, Neo-Hinduism, or Hindu revivalism, strive to introduce regeneration and reform to Hinduism, both in a religious or spiritual and in a societal sense. The movements started appearing during the Bengali Renaissance.
