### Four-class Classification for the notes of the Module 1- Society State and Polity in India in the subject of INDIAN TRADITIONS, CULTURAL AND SOCIETY

1. The four-class classification, also known as the Varna system, is a social hierarchy in Hinduism that divides society into four classes or Varnas.
2. The four Varnas are Brahmins (priests and teachers), Kshatriyas (warriors and rulers), Vaishyas (merchants and farmers), and Shudras (laborers and service providers).
3. This system is believed to have originated from the Purusha Sukta hymn in the Rigveda, which describes the creation of the four Varnas from the body of the cosmic being Purusha.
4. The Varna system is often criticized for perpetuating social inequality and discrimination, particularly against the Shudras and those considered to be outside the Varna system, such as the Dalits or "untouchables".
5. Despite its controversial nature, the Varna system has played a significant role in shaping the social and political structure of Indian society.
6. In modern India, the Varna system is not legally recognized, and discrimination based on caste is prohibited by the Constitution. However, the caste system continues to influence social and economic relations in many parts of the country.
