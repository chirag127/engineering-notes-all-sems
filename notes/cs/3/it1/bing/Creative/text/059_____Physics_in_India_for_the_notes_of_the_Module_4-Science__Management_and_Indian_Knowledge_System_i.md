### Physics in India

- Physics is the branch of science that deals with the nature and properties of matter and energy, and the interactions between them.
- Physics in India has a long and rich history, dating back to the ancient times when the Indian philosophers and sages developed various concepts and theories related to the physical world.
- Some of the notable contributions of Indian physics are:

  - The concept of atomism, which was proposed by the Vaisheshika school of philosophy, founded by Kanada in the 6th century BCE. According to this school, the universe is composed of indivisible and eternal atoms, which combine in different ways to form complex substances. The Vaisheshika school also developed the concepts of motion, force, gravity, and magnetism, and classified the elements into nine categories: earth, water, fire, air, ether, time, space, mind, and soul.
  - The concept of zero, which was invented by the Indian mathematicians around the 5th century CE. The symbol for zero, which is a dot or a circle, was first used by Brahmagupta in his treatise Brahmasphutasiddhanta. The concept of zero was crucial for the development of the decimal number system, algebra, and calculus, which are essential tools for physics.
  - The concept of infinity, which was explored by the Indian mathematicians and philosophers, such as Bhaskara II, Madhava of Sangamagrama, and Srinivasa Ramanujan. They developed various methods and formulas to calculate the values of infinite series, such as the value of pi, the area of a circle, and the circumference of an ellipse. They also introduced the concept of irrational numbers, such as the square root of two, and transcendental numbers, such as e and pi.
  - The concept of relativity, which was hinted at by the Indian philosopher Nagarjuna in the 2nd century CE. He argued that the reality is dependent on the perspective of the observer, and that there is no absolute truth or essence in the world. He also suggested that time and space are not fixed or absolute, but relative and interdependent. His philosophy influenced the development of the Buddhist school of Madhyamaka, which is considered as one of the precursors of modern physics.
  - The concept of quantum mechanics, which was inspired by the Indian philosophy of Vedanta, which holds that the ultimate reality is Brahman, the universal consciousness, and that the individual self, or Atman, is identical with Brahman. This philosophy implies that the observer and the observed are not separate, but one, and that the reality is not deterministic, but probabilistic. Some of the pioneers of quantum mechanics, such as Erwin Schrödinger, Werner Heisenberg, and Niels Bohr, were influenced by the Vedantic philosophy and acknowledged its similarity with the quantum theory.

- Physics in India also has a modern and contemporary history, marked by the achievements of many eminent physicists and institutions. Some of the notable examples are:

  - C.V. Raman, who won the Nobel Prize in Physics in 1930 for his discovery of the Raman effect, which is the scattering of light by molecules. He also established the Department of Physics at the Indian Institute of Science in Bangalore, which is one of the leading centers for advanced research and education in physics in India .
  - S.N. Bose, who collaborated with Albert Einstein to develop the Bose-Einstein statistics, which describe the behavior of particles that obey the Pauli exclusion principle, such as photons and electrons. He also proposed the concept of Bose-Einstein condensate, which is a state of matter in which a large number of particles occupy the same quantum state. He founded the Department of Physics at the University of Calcutta, which is one of the oldest and most prestigious physics departments in India.
  - Homi Bhabha, who is considered as the father of the Indian nuclear program. He established the Tata Institute of Fundamental Research (TIFR) in Mumbai, which is one of the premier institutes for research in physics and other fields of science in India. He also initiated the Atomic Energy Commission of India, which oversees the development and use of nuclear energy for peaceful and military purposes.
