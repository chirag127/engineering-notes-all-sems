### Harappan Technologies

- The Harappan civilization, also known as the Indus Valley Civilization, was a Bronze Age civilization that flourished in the northwest of the Indian subcontinent from about 2500 BCE to 1900 BCE.
- The Harappans developed various technologies and crafts, such as metallurgy, seal carving, pottery, jewelry, weights and measures, urban planning, drainage systems, and writing.
- Some of the notable features of Harappan technologies are:

  - Metallurgy: The Harappans used copper, bronze, lead, and tin to make tools, weapons, vessels, and ornaments. They knew how to alloy copper with tin to make bronze, which is harder and more durable than copper. They also knew how to cast metal by the lost-wax process, which involves making a wax model of the desired object, covering it with clay, heating it to melt the wax, and then pouring molten metal into the hollow mold.
  - Seal carving: The Harappans carved distinctive seals, usually made of steatite, a soft stone, with various motifs and inscriptions. The seals were used to stamp clay on trade goods, to identify property, and possibly to serve as amulets or tokens of authority. The motifs on the seals include animals, such as bulls, elephants, rhinoceroses, tigers, and unicorns, as well as human and divine figures. The inscriptions are in a script that has not been deciphered yet.
  - Pottery: The Harappans produced a variety of pottery, both plain and painted, for domestic and ritual purposes. The pottery was made of fine clay, well-fired, and often decorated with geometric patterns, floral designs, and animal motifs. The pottery shapes include dishes, bowls, jars, vases, and goblets. Some of the pottery was glazed with a green or blue coating.
  - Jewelry: The Harappans were skilled in making jewelry from materials such as gold, silver, copper, bronze, ivory, shell, bone, and beads of stone, glass, and faience. They made necklaces, bracelets, earrings, rings, pendants, and brooches, often with elaborate designs and inlays. The jewelry was worn by both men and women, and reflected the wealth and status of the wearer.
  - Weights and measures: The Harappans developed a system of standardized weights and measures, based on a decimal system, that was used for trade and administration. The weights were made of stone or metal, and ranged from 0.05 grams to 500 grams. The measures were based on a unit of length called the Indus inch, which was about 1.32 cm. The Harappans also used scales and balances to weigh goods and measure quantities.
  - Urban planning: The Harappans built well-planned cities, with a grid layout of streets, rectangular blocks of houses, and public buildings. The cities had fortifications, granaries, warehouses, baths, and assembly halls. The houses were made of baked bricks, and had multiple rooms, courtyards, wells, and bathrooms. The Harappans also developed a sophisticated drainage system, with underground pipes, covered drains, and cesspits, to dispose of wastewater and sewage.
