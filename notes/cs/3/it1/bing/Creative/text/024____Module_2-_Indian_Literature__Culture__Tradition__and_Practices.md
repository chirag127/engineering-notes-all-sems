## Module 2- Indian Literature, Culture, Tradition, and Practices

- Indian literature is one of the oldest and richest in the world, spanning from the ancient Vedic texts to the modern novels and poetry. Indian literature reflects the diversity of languages, religions, regions, and genres in the country. Some of the prominent literary traditions in India are Sanskrit, Tamil, Hindi, Urdu, Bengali, Marathi, Gujarati, Malayalam, Kannada, and English. Indian literature has influenced and been influenced by other cultures, such as Persian, Arabic, Greek, Chinese, and European. Some of the famous Indian writers and poets are Kalidasa, Valmiki, Vyasa, Tulsidas, Kabir, Mirza Ghalib, Rabindranath Tagore, Munshi Premchand, R.K. Narayan, Salman Rushdie, Arundhati Roy, and Amitav Ghosh .
- Indian culture is a complex and diverse phenomenon, shaped by various factors such as geography, history, religion, philosophy, art, and social norms. Indian culture is known for its unity in diversity, as it accommodates and respects different beliefs, values, customs, and practices. Some of the salient features of Indian culture are greetings, religious customs, festivals, family structure, marriage, symbols, cuisine, clothing, dances, epics, mythology, martial arts, and languages .
- Indian tradition is the collective wisdom and practices of the people of India, passed down from generation to generation. Indian tradition is based on the principles of dharma (duty), karma (action), and moksha (liberation). Indian tradition is also influenced by the teachings of various sages, saints, and gurus, such as Buddha, Mahavira, Guru Nanak, Swami Vivekananda, Mahatma Gandhi, and others. Indian tradition is manifested in various aspects of life, such as rituals, ceremonies, rites of passage, festivals, arts, crafts, music, literature, architecture, and medicine .
- Indian practices are the specific ways of doing things in India, based on the culture and tradition of the people. Indian practices vary from region to region, community to community, and individual to individual, depending on their preferences, beliefs, and circumstances. Some of the common Indian practices are yoga, meditation, ayurveda, astrology, vegetarianism, cow worship, caste system, joint family, arranged marriage, dowry, hospitality, respect for elders, and non-violence  .
