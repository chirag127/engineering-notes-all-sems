### Bhakti Movement

- The Bhakti movement was a significant religious movement in medieval Hinduism that sought to bring religious reforms to all strata of society by adopting the method of devotion to achieve salvation.
- The term bhakti means "devotion" in Sanskrit and refers to the intense emotional attachment and love of a devotee toward a personal god and of the god for the devotee.
- The Bhakti movement preached against the caste system, ritualism, idolatry, and social inequalities and used the local languages to convey the message of universal love and harmony to the masses.
- The Bhakti movement originated in South India in the 7th-8th centuries CE with the Alvars (Vaishnavite saints) and the Nayanars (Shaivite saints) who composed devotional hymns in Tamil and visited various sacred shrines.
- The Bhakti movement spread to North India in the 12th-13th centuries CE with the influence of the Nathpanthis, the Sufis, and the Ramanandis. Some of the prominent Bhakti saints of North India were Ramananda, Kabir, Nanak, Surdas, Tulsidas, Mirabai, and Chaitanya.
- The Bhakti movement emphasized the personal and emotional relationship between the devotee and the god, rather than the formal and ritualistic one. It also advocated the equality of all human beings regardless of their caste, gender, or status.
- The Bhakti movement contributed to the development of regional languages, literature, music, art, and culture in India. It also fostered a spirit of tolerance, compassion, and social reform among the people.
- The Bhakti movement is considered to be one of the paths of yoga, known as Bhakti yoga, which is the yoga of devotion and love. Bhakti yoga involves expressing one's love for the god through various practices such as chanting, singing, praying, meditating, serving, and surrendering.
