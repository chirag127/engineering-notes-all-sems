## Module 3- Indian Religion, Philosophy, and Practices

- India is a diverse and pluralistic country with many religions and philosophies that have influenced its culture and society.
- The major religions that originated in India are Hinduism, Buddhism, Jainism, and Sikhism. These religions share some common concepts, such as karma, dharma, reincarnation, and liberation, but also have distinctive doctrines, rituals, and ethical codes.
- Hinduism is the oldest and largest religion in India, with about 80% of the population identifying as Hindus. Hinduism is a complex and diverse tradition that encompasses many sects, schools, and movements. Hinduism is based on the Vedas, the ancient scriptures that contain hymns, rituals, and philosophical teachings. Hinduism also recognizes many gods and goddesses, who are manifestations of the supreme reality, Brahman. Some of the major Hindu deities are Vishnu, Shiva, Ganesha, Lakshmi, and Durga. Hinduism also has various paths of spiritual practice, such as bhakti (devotion), jnana (knowledge), karma (action), and yoga (discipline).
- Buddhism is a religion and philosophy that was founded by Siddhartha Gautama, also known as the Buddha, in the 6th century BCE. Buddhism teaches the Four Noble Truths, which are the truth of suffering, the truth of the cause of suffering, the truth of the end of suffering, and the truth of the path that leads to the end of suffering. The path that leads to the end of suffering is the Noble Eightfold Path, which consists of right view, right intention, right speech, right action, right livelihood, right effort, right mindfulness, and right concentration. Buddhism also teaches the concept of anatman, which means that there is no permanent self or soul, and that all phenomena are impermanent, unsatisfactory, and interdependent. The goal of Buddhism is to attain nirvana, which is the state of liberation from the cycle of rebirth.
- Jainism is a religion and philosophy that was founded by Mahavira, also known as the Jina, in the 6th century BCE. Jainism teaches the principle of ahimsa, which means non-violence towards all living beings. Jainism also teaches the concept of anekantavada, which means that reality has multiple aspects and perspectives, and that one should respect the views of others. Jainism also teaches the concept of aparigraha, which means non-attachment to worldly possessions and desires. The goal of Jainism is to attain moksha, which is the state of liberation from the cycle of rebirth.
- Sikhism is a religion and philosophy that was founded by Guru Nanak in the 15th century CE. Sikhism teaches the concept of monotheism, which means that there is only one God, who is called Waheguru. Sikhism also teaches the concept of nam, which means the divine name or word of God, and the concept of shabad, which means the sacred scripture or sound of God. Sikhism also teaches the concept of seva, which means selfless service to God and humanity. The goal of Sikhism is to attain union with God, which is achieved by following the teachings of the ten Gurus and the Guru Granth Sahib, the holy book of Sikhism.
