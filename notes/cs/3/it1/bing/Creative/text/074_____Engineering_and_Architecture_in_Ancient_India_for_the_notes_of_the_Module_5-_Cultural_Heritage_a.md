### Engineering and Architecture in Ancient India

- Engineering and architecture in ancient India was known as **Sthapatya-Kala**, which means the art of constructing something.
- Ancient Indian architects and engineers derived principles of construction and architecture based on experience, observation of natural phenomena and considering social and cultural aspects of India.
- Ancient India witnessed two urban revolutions: the first one in the Indus Valley Civilization (around 2500 BCE) and the second one in the Mauryan and Kushan periods (around 6th century BCE to 3rd century CE).
- The Indus Valley Civilization was remarkable for its urban planning, drainage systems, granaries, docks, and monumental structures such as the Great Bath and the Great Stupa.
- The Mauryan and Kushan periods saw the development of stone architecture, sculpture, and monumental pillars, such as the Ashoka pillars and the Sanchi Stupa.
- Ancient India also developed various styles of temple architecture, such as the Nagara style in North India and the Dravida style in South India, which incorporated distinctive features such as the shikhara (spire) and the vimana (tower).
- Some of the engineering marvels of ancient India include the Kailasa Temple at Ellora, which was carved out of a single rock; the stepwells of Gujarat and Rajasthan, which were designed to store water and provide relief from the heat; and the iron pillar of Delhi, which has resisted corrosion for over 1600 years .
