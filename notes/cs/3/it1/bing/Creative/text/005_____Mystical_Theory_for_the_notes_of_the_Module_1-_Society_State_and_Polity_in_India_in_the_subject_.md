### Mystical Theory for the notes of the Module 1- Society State and Polity in India in the subject of INDIAN TRADITIONS, CULTURAL AND SOCIETY

- Mystical theory is one of the theories that attempts to explain the origin of the state in ancient India.
- According to this theory, the state is a divine institution that is created by God or gods for the welfare of human beings.
- The king is the representative of God or gods on earth and has a sacred duty to uphold the moral and social order.
- The king is also the protector of the dharma, the cosmic law that governs the universe and the human society.
- The king derives his authority and legitimacy from the divine sanction and the consent of the people.
- The king is bound by the dharma and the shastras, the sacred scriptures that prescribe the duties and responsibilities of the ruler and the ruled.
- The king is also guided by the advice of the brahmins, the priestly class that has the knowledge of the dharma and the shastras.
- The king is expected to rule with justice, benevolence, and compassion, and to ensure the welfare and happiness of his subjects.
- The king is also responsible for performing various rituals and sacrifices to please the gods and to maintain the harmony and balance of the cosmos.
- The king is accountable to the gods and the people for his actions and can be removed or punished if he fails to fulfill his obligations or becomes tyrannical or corrupt.
- Some examples of the mystical theory are the concept of the chakravartin, the universal ruler who is endowed with the divine wheel of sovereignty, the concept of the rajarshi, the king who is also a sage and a saint, and the concept of the ramrajya, the ideal state of peace and prosperity established by Lord Rama.
