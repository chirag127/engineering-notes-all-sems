### Purusārtha

- Purusārtha is a Sanskrit term that means "the object or aim of human life" .
- It is a key concept in Hinduism that refers to the four proper goals or aims of a human life .
- The four purusārthas are: dharma, artha, kāma, and mokṣa .
- Dharma means "righteousness, duty, or moral order" . It is the ethical and spiritual foundation of human life . It guides one's actions and choices in accordance with one's nature, role, and stage of life .
- Artha means "wealth, prosperity, or material values" . It is the pursuit of economic and social well-being . It enables one to fulfill one's needs and desires, as well as to contribute to the welfare of others .
- Kāma means "pleasure, enjoyment, or sensual values" . It is the pursuit of happiness and satisfaction in life . It encompasses the aesthetic, emotional, and erotic aspects of human experience .
- Mokṣa means "liberation, freedom, or spiritual values" . It is the ultimate goal of human life . It is the realization of one's true self and the transcendence of the cycle of birth and death .
- The four purusārthas are interrelated and interdependent . They are not mutually exclusive or contradictory, but rather complementary and harmonious . They balance and support each other in the quest for a fulfilling and meaningful life .
- The four purusārthas are also dynamic and flexible . They vary according to one's individual nature, circumstances, and stage of life . They are not fixed or rigid, but rather adaptable and evolving .
- The four purusārthas are the inherent values of the universe . They are the blueprint for human fulfillment . Working with them helps one create a satisfyingly balanced, meaningful life at the deepest and most holistic level . They offer a way for evaluating one's life and making good decisions . Knowing one's goals brings meaning to one's spiritual practice .
