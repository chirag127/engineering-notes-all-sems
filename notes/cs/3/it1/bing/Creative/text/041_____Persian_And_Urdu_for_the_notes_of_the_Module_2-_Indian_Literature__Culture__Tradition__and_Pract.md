### Persian and Urdu

- Persian and Urdu are two languages that have influenced the literary and cultural traditions of India, especially in the northern and central regions.
- Persian was the official language of the Mughal Empire, which ruled over most of India from the 16th to the 18th century. Persian was also the language of court, administration, literature, and education in many parts of India until the 19th century.
- Persian literature in India includes poetry, prose, history, biography, travelogue, and mysticism. Some of the famous Persian poets and writers in India are Amir Khusrau, Abdur Rahim Khan-e-Khanan, Mirza Ghalib, and Sir Syed Ahmad Khan.
- Persian also influenced the development of other languages in India, such as Hindi, Urdu, Punjabi, Sindhi, Gujarati, Marathi, and Bengali. Many words, phrases, idioms, and expressions in these languages are derived from Persian.
- Urdu is a language that emerged from the interaction of Persian, Arabic, and local languages in India, especially in the Delhi region. Urdu is also known as Hindustani or Rekhta.
- Urdu literature in India includes poetry, prose, drama, and fiction. Some of the famous Urdu poets and writers in India are Mir Taqi Mir, Mirza Ghalib, Mir Anees, Allama Iqbal, Munshi Premchand, Saadat Hasan Manto, and Ismat Chughtai.
- Urdu also influenced the development of other languages in India, such as Hindi, Punjabi, Sindhi, and Kashmiri. Urdu is also the language of Bollywood, the Indian film industry, and has contributed to the popular culture of India.
