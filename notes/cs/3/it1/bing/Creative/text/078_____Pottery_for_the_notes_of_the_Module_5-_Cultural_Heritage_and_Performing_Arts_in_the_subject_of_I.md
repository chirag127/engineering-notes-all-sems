### Pottery

Pottery is one of the most ancient and enduring forms of artistic expression in India. It is a cultural art that reflects the diversity and richness of Indian traditions, regions, and history. Pottery is also a functional craft that serves various purposes in everyday life, such as cooking, storing, and decorating.

Some of the main points to note about pottery in India are:

- Pottery in India has a long history, dating back to the prehistoric times. Evidence of pottery has been found in the early settlements of Lahuradewa and later the Indus Valley Civilisation . Pottery was also used by the Vedic people, the Mauryan empire, the Gupta period, and the medieval kingdoms.
- Pottery in India is made from various materials, such as clay, terracotta, stoneware, porcelain, and metal. Different types of pottery are distinguished by their shapes, sizes, colours, designs, and techniques. Some of the common techniques are hand-molding, wheel-throwing, slip-casting, and glazing.
- Pottery in India is influenced by various factors, such as geography, climate, culture, religion, and trade. Different regions of India have their own distinctive styles and forms of pottery, such as the blue pottery of Jaipur, the black pottery of Nizamabad, the red pottery of Khurja, and the bamboo pottery of Manipur  . Pottery in India also reflects the religious and cultural beliefs of the people, such as the use of symbols, motifs, and patterns. Pottery in India also shows the impact of foreign influences, such as the Persian, Chinese, and European styles.
- Pottery in India is a living craft tradition that is still practiced extensively by the potters, or kumhars, who belong to various castes and communities. Pottery is a source of livelihood and identity for many potters, who inherit the skill and knowledge from their ancestors. Pottery is also a form of creative expression and innovation, as the potters experiment with new materials, designs, and markets.
- Pottery in India is a valuable cultural heritage that needs to be preserved and promoted. Pottery is a part of the intangible cultural heritage of India, as it embodies the oral traditions, social practices, and artistic skills of the people. Pottery is also a part of the tangible cultural heritage of India, as it represents the material culture, history, and aesthetics of the country. Pottery in India faces many challenges, such as the loss of traditional knowledge, the competition from modern products, and the lack of recognition and support. Pottery in India needs to be supported by various stakeholders, such as the government, the NGOs, the media, and the consumers, to ensure its survival and growth.
