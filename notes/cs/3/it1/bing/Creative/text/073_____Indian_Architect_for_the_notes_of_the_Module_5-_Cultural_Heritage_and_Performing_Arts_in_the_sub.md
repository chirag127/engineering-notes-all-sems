### Indian Architecture

- Indian architecture is rooted in the history, culture, and religion of India. It reflects the diversity and richness of Indian civilization and its interactions with other cultures over time.
- Indian architecture can be classified into several styles and traditions, such as Hindu temple architecture, Indo-Islamic architecture, South Indian architecture, Indo-Saracenic architecture, and modern architecture.
- Hindu temple architecture is characterized by the use of stone, brick, or wood to create elaborate structures with towers, domes, halls, and sanctuaries. The temples are often decorated with sculptures, carvings, and paintings depicting gods, goddesses, and mythological scenes. The temples are also aligned with the cardinal directions and follow the principles of vastu shastra, the ancient science of architecture.
- Indo-Islamic architecture is the result of the fusion of Islamic and Hindu elements, especially during the medieval period. It includes the styles of Rajput architecture, Mughal architecture, and Deccan architecture. Rajput architecture is known for its forts, palaces, and temples with intricate ornamentation and geometric patterns. Mughal architecture is famous for its monumental tombs, mosques, and gardens with domes, arches, and minarets. Deccan architecture is influenced by Persian and Turkish styles and features domed pavilions, cusped arches, and glazed tiles.
- South Indian architecture is distinct from the north Indian styles and is influenced by the Dravidian, Chola, Vijayanagara, and Nayaka cultures. It features rock-cut temples, gopurams (towering gateways), mandapas (pillared halls), and vimanas (pyramidal roofs). The temples are often built on raised platforms and surrounded by walls and moats. The sculptures and paintings depict scenes from Hindu epics and legends.
- Indo-Saracenic architecture is a hybrid style that emerged during the British colonial rule in India. It combines elements of Islamic, Hindu, Gothic, and Victorian architecture. It is characterized by domes, arches, spires, turrets, and balconies. Some examples of this style are the Victoria Memorial in Kolkata, the Gateway of India in Mumbai, and the Rashtrapati Bhavan in New Delhi.
- Modern architecture in India is influenced by the global trends and movements of the 20th and 21st centuries, such as Art Deco, Bauhaus, Brutalism, and Postmodernism. It also incorporates local materials, techniques, and traditions to create innovative and sustainable designs. Some of the prominent modern architects in India are Le Corbusier, Charles Correa, B.V. Doshi, Raj Rewal, and Balkrishna Doshi.
