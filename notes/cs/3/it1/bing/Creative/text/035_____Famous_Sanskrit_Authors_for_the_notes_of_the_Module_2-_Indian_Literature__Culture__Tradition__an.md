### Famous Sanskrit Authors

- Sanskrit is one of the oldest and most influential languages of India. It is the language of the Vedas, the Upanishads, the Epics, the Puranas, the dramas, the poetry and the philosophy of ancient India. Sanskrit literature is vast and diverse, covering various genres and themes. Here are some of the famous Sanskrit authors and their works:

- **Valmiki**: He is regarded as the first poet (Adi Kavi) and the author of the Ramayana, the epic that narrates the life and adventures of Rama, the ideal king and hero. The Ramayana is one of the two major epics of India, along with the Mahabharata. Valmiki is also credited with composing some of the hymns in the Rig Veda, the oldest of the four Vedas.  

- **Vyasa**: He is the legendary sage and author of the Mahabharata, the other major epic of India, which depicts the Kurukshetra war between the Pandavas and the Kauravas, and the moral and spiritual teachings of Krishna, the supreme incarnation of Vishnu. Vyasa is also considered to be the compiler of the Vedas, the Upanishads, the Puranas, and the Vedanta Sutras. He is also known as Veda Vyasa, Krishna Dvaipayana, and Badarayana.  

- **Kalidasa**: He is the most celebrated poet and dramatist of classical Sanskrit literature. He is known for his exquisite style, imagery, and similes. His works include three plays: Abhijnana Shakuntalam (The Recognition of Shakuntala), Vikramorvashiya (Urvashi Won by Valour), and Malavikagnimitram (Malavika and Agnimitra); two epic poems: Kumarasambhava (The Birth of Kumara) and Raghuvamsha (The Dynasty of Raghu); and three lyric poems: Meghaduta (The Cloud Messenger), Ritusamhara (The Seasons), and Shyamala Dandakam (A Hymn to Shyamala).  

- **Bharavi**: He is the author of Kiratarjuniya (The Arjuna and the Mountain Man), an epic poem that describes the encounter between Arjuna, the Pandava hero, and Shiva, disguised as a hunter, in the forest. The poem is known for its vivid descriptions of nature, its complex and ornate style, and its use of various figures of speech.  

- **Magha**: He is the author of Shishupala Vadha (The Slaying of Shishupala), an epic poem that narrates the killing of Shishupala, the king of Chedi, by Krishna, at the sacrifice of Yudhishthira, the eldest Pandava. The poem is famous for its elaborate and intricate style, its use of alliteration, rhyme, and puns, and its display of erudition and wit.  

- **Harsha**: He is the author of Naishadhiya Charitam (The Story of Nala and Damayanti), an epic poem that tells the love story of Nala, the king of Nishadha, and Damayanti, the princess of Vidarbha, and their separation and reunion after many trials and tribulations. The poem is praised for its elegance, sweetness, and pathos.  

- **Bhasa**: He is one of the earliest and most prolific Sanskrit dramatists, who wrote about thirteen plays, of which only nine have survived. His plays are based on the stories from the epics and the Puranas, and are characterized by their simplicity, realism, and humor. Some of his famous plays are Svapnavasavadatta (The Dream of Vasavadatta), Pratijnayaugandharayana (The Vow of Augandharayana), and Madhyamavyayoga (The Middle One).
