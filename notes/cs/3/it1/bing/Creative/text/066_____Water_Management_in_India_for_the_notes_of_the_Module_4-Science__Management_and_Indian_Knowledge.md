### Water Management in India

Water management in India refers to the planning, development, allocation, utilization, conservation and protection of water resources in the country. Water management is essential for ensuring water security, health, agriculture, industry, environment and socio-economic development in India. Some of the key aspects of water management in India are:

- Providing drinking water to rural areas: The government has been responsible for providing drinking water to 833 million rural people in some 640,000 villages across the country. However, due to various challenges such as declining groundwater levels, contamination, poor operation and maintenance, and lack of community participation, many rural areas face water scarcity and quality issues. The government has launched various schemes and programs to improve rural water supply, such as the Jal Jeevan Mission, which aims to provide piped water supply to every rural household by 2024.
- Groundwater management: Over the past few decades, groundwater extraction has risen exponentially across India. India is now the world's largest user of groundwater, accounting for about 25% of the global total. Groundwater is the main source of irrigation and domestic water supply in India, but it is also facing severe depletion and degradation due to overexploitation, pollution, climate change and lack of regulation. The government has taken several measures to promote sustainable groundwater management, such as the Atal Bhujal Yojana, which is India's largest community-led groundwater management program, helping improve rural livelihoods and build resilience in seven Indian states that have the highest rates of groundwater depletion .
- Improving access to water on dry rainfed lands: About 60% of India's net sown area is rainfed, which means it depends on rainfall for irrigation. Rainfed agriculture contributes to about 40% of the country's food production, but it is also vulnerable to droughts, floods, soil erosion and low productivity. The government has initiated various initiatives to enhance water availability and efficiency on rainfed lands, such as the Pradhan Mantri Krishi Sinchayee Yojana, which aims to provide irrigation to every farm, and the Watershed Development Program, which supports integrated management of natural resources in watershed areas.
- Cleaning the Ganga: The Ganga is India's most sacred and longest river, supporting the livelihoods and culture of more than 500 million people. However, the river is also severely polluted by domestic, industrial and agricultural waste, affecting its ecological and social value. The government has launched the Namami Gange Program, which is a comprehensive and integrated approach to clean and rejuvenate the Ganga and its tributaries, involving various interventions such as sewage treatment, riverfront development, biodiversity conservation, public awareness and community participation.
- Managing floods and droughts: India is prone to frequent and severe floods and droughts, which cause huge losses of life, property and infrastructure. Floods and droughts are also exacerbated by climate change, urbanization, deforestation and poor water management practices. The government has established various institutions and mechanisms to deal with floods and droughts, such as the Central Water Commission, the National Disaster Management Authority, the National Hydrology Project, the National Flood Forecasting and Warning System, and the National Drought Management Plan .
- Enhancing dam safety in India: India has about 5,200 large dams, which provide multiple benefits such as irrigation, hydropower, flood control and water supply. However, many of these dams are old, poorly maintained and pose potential risks to downstream communities and ecosystems. The government has initiated the Dam Rehabilitation and Improvement Project, which aims to improve the safety and operational performance of selected dams across the country, and to strengthen the institutional and legal framework for dam safety management in India .
