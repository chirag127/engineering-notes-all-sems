### The Vedas

- The Vedas are the oldest and most authoritative scriptures of Hinduism, the religion of India. They are composed in Vedic Sanskrit, a language that is no longer spoken but is the ancestor of many modern Indian languages. The word Veda means "knowledge" or "vision" and refers to the divine wisdom revealed to the ancient sages or rishis .
- The Vedas are divided into four collections: the Rigveda, the Yajurveda, the Samaveda and the Atharvaveda. Each collection consists of four parts: the Samhitas, the Brahmanas, the Aranyakas and the Upanishads. The Samhitas are the hymns and prayers to various gods and goddesses. The Brahmanas are the ritual manuals and explanations of the sacrifices. The Aranyakas are the forest texts that deal with the mystical and philosophical aspects of the rituals. The Upanishads are the philosophical texts that explore the nature of the self, the supreme reality and the relationship between them  .
- The Vedas are considered to be shruti, or "that which is heard", meaning that they are the direct revelation of the supreme truth to the rishis, who transmitted them orally to their disciples for generations. The Vedas are not the work of any human author, but are eternal and uncreated. They are the source of dharma, or the cosmic order and moral duty, that governs the life of Hindus. The Vedas contain the knowledge of all aspects of existence, from the material to the spiritual, and prescribe the rituals, ethics, values and practices that lead to the attainment of moksha, or liberation from the cycle of rebirth  .
- The Vedas are the foundation of the Vedic culture and civilization, which flourished in India from around 1500 BCE to 500 BCE. The Vedic culture was characterized by a complex social hierarchy, a sophisticated system of sacrifices, a rich mythology and cosmology, a diverse pantheon of gods and goddesses, and a profound spiritual vision. The Vedic culture influenced the development of later Hindu traditions, such as the six schools of philosophy, the bhakti movement, the epics and the puranas, as well as other religions, such as Buddhism, Jainism and Sikhism  .
