### Varnāshrama System

- The Varnāshrama system is a concept of social and spiritual organization in Hinduism that divides the society into four classes (varnas) and four stages of life (ashramas).
- The four varnas are: Brahmanas (priests and teachers), Kshatriyas (warriors and rulers), Vaishyas (merchants and farmers), and Sudras (servants and laborers).
- The four ashramas are: Brahmacharya (student), Grihastha (householder), Vanaprastha (forest dweller), and Sannyasa (renunciate).
- The Varnāshrama system is based on the idea of dharma, which means the duty or moral law of an individual according to his or her varna and ashrama.
- The Varnāshrama system aims to provide a framework for the harmonious functioning of the society and the spiritual progress of the individual.
- The Varnāshrama system is not rigid or hereditary, but flexible and based on the natural qualities and abilities of each person.
- The Varnāshrama system is not a caste system, which is a later and distorted form of social stratification that is based on birth and discrimination.
- The Varnāshrama system is a dynamic and evolving concept that has changed over time and across regions in India.
