### Six System Indian Philosophy

The six systems of Indian philosophy are Nyaya, Vaisheshika, Samkhya, Yoga, Purva Mimamsa and Vedanta. They are also known as shad-darshana, meaning six views or perspectives. Each system has its own founder, texts, concepts and methods of inquiry. They represent different aspects of the intellectual and spiritual traditions of Hinduism.

- Nyaya: This system was founded by Akshapada Gautama in the 2nd century BCE. It deals with logic and epistemology, or the theory of knowledge. It aims to establish the validity of sources of knowledge, such as perception, inference, analogy and testimony. It also develops rules of reasoning and debate to establish the truth or falsity of propositions. Nyaya is considered the foundation of Hindu philosophy, as it provides the tools for rational inquiry and argumentation .
- Vaisheshika: This system was founded by Kanada Kashyapa in the 6th century BCE. It is also known as the atomistic school of Indian philosophy, as it proposes that the universe is composed of indivisible, eternal and qualitatively distinct atoms of different elements. It also identifies six categories of reality: substance, quality, action, generality, particularity and inherence. Vaisheshika is concerned with the analysis of the nature of reality and the classification of its constituents .
- Samkhya: This system was founded by Kapila Muni in the 6th century BCE. It is one of the oldest and most influential schools of Indian philosophy. It is a dualistic system that posits two fundamental principles: purusha (consciousness or spirit) and prakriti (matter or nature). It also enumerates 25 tattvas (principles or categories) that emerge from the interaction of purusha and prakriti. Samkhya is a system of metaphysics and cosmology that explains the origin and evolution of the universe and the diversity of life forms .
- Yoga: This system was founded by Patanjali in the 2nd century BCE. It is closely related to Samkhya, as it accepts its metaphysical and cosmological framework. However, it adds a practical dimension of ethics, discipline and meditation to achieve the liberation of purusha from prakriti. Yoga is a system of psychology and spirituality that aims to control the fluctuations of the mind and attain a state of union with the supreme reality .
- Purva Mimamsa: This system was founded by Jaimini in the 3rd century BCE. It is also known as the Karma Mimamsa, as it focuses on the interpretation and application of the ritual and ethical injunctions of the Vedas, the sacred scriptures of Hinduism. It assumes the authority and validity of the Vedas and seeks to establish the means and ends of human action. Purva Mimamsa is a system of hermeneutics and ethics that defends the value and necessity of performing the prescribed duties and sacrifices .
- Vedanta: This system was founded by Badarayana in the 2nd century BCE. It is also known as the Uttara Mimamsa, as it focuses on the philosophical and mystical teachings of the Upanishads, the concluding part of the Vedas. It questions the authority and validity of the ritual and ethical injunctions of the Vedas and seeks to realize the ultimate reality that underlies the phenomenal world. Vedanta is a system of ontology and theology that expounds the doctrine of Brahman (the absolute) and Atman (the self) and their identity or difference .
