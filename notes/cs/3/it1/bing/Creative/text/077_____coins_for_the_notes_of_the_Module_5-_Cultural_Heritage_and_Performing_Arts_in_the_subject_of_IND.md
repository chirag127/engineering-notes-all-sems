### Coins

Coins are metal objects that are used as a medium of exchange or as a store of value. Coins have been used in India since ancient times, and have reflected the cultural, political, and economic changes of the Indian subcontinent. Coins can also be considered as sources of historical information, as they often bear inscriptions, symbols, images, or legends that reveal the identity, beliefs, or achievements of their issuers.

Some of the main points to note about the coinage of India are:

- The earliest coins of India were **punch-marked coins**, which were made of silver or copper and had various symbols or marks punched on them. These coins were used from the 6th century BCE to the 1st century CE, and were issued by different kingdoms or republics, such as the Mahajanapadas, the Mauryan Empire, the Indo-Greeks, the Sakas, the Kushans, and the Satavahanas   .
- The first coins to bear the names or portraits of rulers were issued by the **Indo-Greeks**, who ruled parts of northwestern India from the 2nd century BCE to the 1st century CE. These coins were influenced by the Hellenistic style and culture, and showed the Greek gods, goddesses, or legends on one side, and the Indian deities, symbols, or scripts on the other side  .
- The **Kushan Empire**, which extended from Central Asia to northern India from the 1st to the 3rd century CE, issued gold, silver, and copper coins that depicted the Kushan kings, their titles, their religious affiliations, and their conquests. The Kushan coins also showed a variety of Indian and foreign gods and goddesses, such as Shiva, Buddha, Kartikeya, Mithra, and Oesho  .
- The **Gupta Empire**, which ruled most of northern and central India from the 4th to the 6th century CE, is considered as the golden age of Indian coinage, as the Gupta coins were of high artistic quality and sophistication. The Gupta coins were mainly made of gold, and showed the Gupta kings, their attributes, their achievements, and their patronage of arts, sciences, and religions. The Gupta coins also introduced the use of Sanskrit legends and the Brahmi script on coins  .
- The **Medieval Period** (from the 7th to the 15th century CE) saw the emergence of various regional kingdoms and dynasties, such as the Chalukyas, the Pallavas, the Rashtrakutas, the Cholas, the Pandyas, the Pratiharas, the Palas, the Senas, the Chauhans, the Delhi Sultanate, the Vijayanagara Empire, and the Bahmani Sultanate. These kingdoms and dynasties issued coins of different metals, shapes, sizes, and designs, reflecting their diverse cultures, languages, religions, and political aspirations. Some of the innovations in coinage during this period were the introduction of **bilingual coins**, which had legends in two languages, such as Arabic and Sanskrit, or Persian and Telugu; the use of **calligraphy** and **floral motifs** on coins; and the minting of **fractional coins**, which were smaller denominations of larger coins  .
- The **Mughal Empire**, which ruled most of India from the 16th to the 18th century CE, established a uniform and standardized system of coinage, which was based on the **silver rupee**. The Mughal coins were inscribed with the names, titles, and dates of the Mughal emperors, their religious slogans, and their mint names. The Mughal coins also showed the influence of the Persian and Islamic art and culture, and used the Persian script and the Islamic calendar on coins  .
