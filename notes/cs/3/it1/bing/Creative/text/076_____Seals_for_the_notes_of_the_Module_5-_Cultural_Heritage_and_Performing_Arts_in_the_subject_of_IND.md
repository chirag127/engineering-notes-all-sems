### Seals for the notes of the Module 5- Cultural Heritage and Performing Arts in the subject of INDIAN TRADITIONS, CULTURAL AND SOCIETY

- Seals are small objects that have an engraved design on one side and are used to make an impression on a soft material, such as clay or wax, to authenticate documents, seal containers, or indicate ownership.
- Seals have been used in various cultures around the world for thousands of years, and they reflect the artistic, religious, and social aspects of those cultures.
- In India, seals have been found from the ancient Indus Valley Civilization (c. 2500-1900 BCE), which was one of the earliest urban civilizations in the world and spanned across present-day Pakistan and northwest India.
- The Indus seals are mostly made of steatite, a soft stone that can be easily carved, and they are usually rectangular, circular, or cylindrical in shape. They measure about 2-5 cm in length and have a hole on one end for attaching a cord or a stick.
- The Indus seals have two main elements: an image and a script. The image is usually of a single animal, such as a buffalo, a rhinoceros, an elephant, or a unicorn (a mythical creature with a single horn). The animal is shown in profile and sometimes accompanied by other symbols, such as a tree, a feeding trough, or a multiple-headed creature. The image may represent a deity, a clan, a trade, or a personal emblem.
- The script is composed of a series of symbols that are arranged in a horizontal line above or below the image. The script is still undeciphered, and its meaning and language are unknown. The script may contain the name of the owner, the purpose of the seal, or a religious invocation.
- The Indus seals were used for various purposes, such as sealing trade goods, marking ownership of property, identifying officials, or expressing religious beliefs. The seals were pressed into clay or wax to leave an impression, which could be verified by the original seal. The seals were also worn as amulets or ornaments, or deposited in sacred places as offerings.
- The Indus seals are valuable sources of information about the Indus Valley Civilization, which had a complex and sophisticated culture that remains largely mysterious. The seals reveal the artistic skill, the religious diversity, the trade networks, and the social organization of the Indus people. The seals also show the influence of other cultures, such as Mesopotamia and Central Asia, on the Indus Valley Civilization.
- The Indus seals are part of the cultural heritage and performing arts of India, as they reflect the ancient roots of Indian civilization and the continuity of artistic and religious traditions. The seals are also objects of curiosity and fascination, as they challenge us to decipher their secrets and understand their messages. The seals are displayed in museums and galleries around the world, and they inspire modern artists and scholars to create new works and interpretations.
