### Sufi movement

- Sufism is a mystical form of Islam that emphasizes the personal experience of God and the spiritual purification of the soul.
- Sufism originated in the Middle East in the 8th and 9th centuries and spread to various regions of the Muslim world, including India.
- Sufis are organized into orders or silsilas, each with a spiritual leader or pir who guides the disciples or murids in their quest for God.
- Sufis practice various methods of devotion, such as meditation, chanting, music, poetry, dance, and service to humanity.
- Sufism in India began in the 13th century with the arrival of various Sufi saints from Central Asia and Persia, who settled in different parts of the country and attracted followers from different backgrounds and faiths.
- Some of the prominent Sufi orders in India are Chishti, Suhrawardi, Qadiri, Naqshbandi, and Shattari.
- Sufism had a significant impact on the religious, cultural, and social life of India. It promoted the values of tolerance, harmony, love, and peace among different communities. It also influenced the development of literature, art, music, and architecture in India.
- Some of the famous Sufi saints of India are Khwaja Moinuddin Chishti, Nizamuddin Auliya, Amir Khusrau, Baba Farid, Sheikh Hamiduddin Nagauri, and Makhdoom Ashraf Jahangir Simnani.
