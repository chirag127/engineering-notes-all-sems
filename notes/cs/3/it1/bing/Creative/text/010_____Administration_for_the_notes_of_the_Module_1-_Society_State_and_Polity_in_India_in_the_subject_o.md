### Administration

- Administration is the process of managing the affairs of an organization or a state by implementing policies, rules and regulations.
- Administration in India has a long and diverse history, influenced by various factors such as geography, culture, religion, ideology, colonialism and democracy.
- Administration in India can be broadly classified into two categories: traditional and modern.
- Traditional administration refers to the systems and practices of governance that existed in India before the advent of British colonial rule in the 18th century.
- Modern administration refers to the systems and practices of governance that emerged in India after the British colonial rule and the subsequent independence and formation of the Indian republic in the 20th century.
- Some of the features of traditional administration in India are:

  - It was based on the principles of dharma (duty), nyaya (justice) and raja dharma (king's duty).
  - It was hierarchical, centralized and autocratic, with the king as the supreme authority and the source of law.
  - It was influenced by various religious and philosophical traditions such as Hinduism, Buddhism, Jainism, Islam and Sikhism.
  - It was characterized by the division of society into four varnas (castes) and numerous jatis (sub-castes), each with its own duties and rights.
  - It was supported by various institutions such as the sabha (assembly), the samiti (council), the panchayat (village council), the nyayalaya (court) and the danda (punishment).
  - It was flexible, adaptive and diverse, reflecting the regional and local variations in culture, geography and resources.

- Some of the features of modern administration in India are:

  - It is based on the principles of democracy, secularism, socialism and federalism.
  - It is democratic, decentralized and participatory, with the people as the sovereign and the source of law.
  - It is influenced by various political and ideological movements such as nationalism, liberalism, socialism, communism and feminism.
  - It is characterized by the equality of all citizens before the law and the protection of their fundamental rights and freedoms.
  - It is supported by various institutions such as the parliament, the executive, the judiciary, the election commission, the comptroller and auditor general and the lokpal (ombudsman).
  - It is complex, dynamic and evolving, reflecting the challenges and opportunities of globalization, development and diversity.
