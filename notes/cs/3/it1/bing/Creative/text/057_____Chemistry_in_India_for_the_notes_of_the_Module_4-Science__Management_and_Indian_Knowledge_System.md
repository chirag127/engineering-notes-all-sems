### Chemistry in India

Chemistry is the branch of science that deals with the study of matter, its composition, structure, properties, and reactions. Chemistry has a wide scope and applications in various fields, such as medicine, agriculture, industry, environment, and education. Chemistry is also an integral part of the Indian culture and heritage, as it has a long and rich history of development and achievements in this domain.

Some of the main points to note about chemistry in India are:

- Chemistry in India can be traced back to the ancient times, when the Indus Valley or Harappan civilization (3rd millennium BCE) used various chemical techniques, such as metallurgy, dyeing, pottery, and cosmetics.
- The Vedic period (2nd millennium BCE to 6th century BCE) witnessed the emergence of Ayurveda, the traditional system of medicine that used various minerals, metals, plants, and animal products for healing and wellness. Ayurveda also played an important role in the development of chemistry, as it involved the preparation, purification, and analysis of various substances.
- The post-Vedic period (6th century BCE to 5th century CE) saw the growth of various schools of philosophy, such as Samkhya, Nyaya, Vaisheshika, and Buddhism, that contributed to the understanding of the nature and classification of matter, atoms, and elements. The Vaisheshika school, in particular, proposed a theory of atomism that was remarkably similar to the modern atomic theory.
- The classical period (5th century CE to 12th century CE) was marked by the flourishing of various branches of science, such as astronomy, mathematics, medicine, and alchemy. Alchemy, or Rasayan Shastra, was the older form of chemistry that aimed at the transmutation of metals, the discovery of the elixir of life, and the attainment of spiritual enlightenment. Alchemists developed various methods and devices for chemical experiments, such as distillation, sublimation, calcination, and extraction. They also discovered and used many chemical substances, such as mercury, sulfur, arsenic, zinc, and iron .
- The medieval period (12th century CE to 18th century CE) witnessed the invasion and influence of various foreign cultures, such as the Arabs, Turks, Mongols, and Europeans, on the Indian science and technology. Chemistry in India was influenced by the Arabic alchemy, which introduced new concepts, terms, and techniques, such as the classification of metals into base and noble, the use of alembic and retort, and the synthesis of various compounds, such as sal ammoniac, nitric acid, and sulfuric acid. Chemistry in India also influenced the European alchemy, which adopted many Indian alchemical ideas, such as the use of mercury and sulfur, the concept of the philosopher's stone, and the search for the universal medicine.
- The modern period (18th century CE onwards) saw the decline and revival of chemistry in India, as it faced the challenges and opportunities of the colonial rule, the independence movement, and the post-independence era. Chemistry in India was influenced by the Western science, which introduced new theories, methods, and discoveries, such as the periodic table, the atomic structure, the chemical bonding, and the organic chemistry. Chemistry in India also contributed to the Western science, by producing many eminent chemists, such as Acharya Prafulla Chandra Ray, Sir C.V. Raman, Dr. Homi Bhabha, and Dr. A.P.J. Abdul Kalam, who made significant contributions to various fields, such as inorganic chemistry, spectroscopy, nuclear chemistry, and space chemistry .
- Chemistry in India today is a vibrant and dynamic field, that has a wide scope and impact on various sectors, such as education, research, industry, and society. Chemistry in India is also integrated with the Indian traditions, culture, and values, as it draws inspiration and guidance from the ancient wisdom and knowledge systems, such as Ayurveda, Yoga, and Vedanta. Chemistry in India is also committed to the global challenges and opportunities, such as the sustainable development, the environmental protection, and the innovation and excellence .
