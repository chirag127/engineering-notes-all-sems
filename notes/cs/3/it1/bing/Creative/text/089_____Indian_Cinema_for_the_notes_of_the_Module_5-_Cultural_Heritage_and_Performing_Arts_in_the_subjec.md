### Indian Cinema

- Indian cinema is one of the oldest and largest film industries in the world, producing films in various languages and genres.
- The history of Indian cinema can be divided into four phases: silent films, talkies, regional films, and new wave cinema.
- Silent films (1890s-1920s): The first public screening of a film in India took place in 1896, when the Lumiere brothers' films were shown in Bombay. The first Indian filmmaker was Dadasaheb Phalke, who made the first full-length feature film, Raja Harishchandra, in 1913. The silent era saw the emergence of genres such as mythological films, historical films, social dramas, and comedies.
- Talkies (1930s-1940s): The advent of sound in cinema revolutionized the industry and gave rise to new stars, directors, and composers. The first Indian talkie was Alam Ara, made by Ardeshir Irani in 1931. The talkie era saw the development of genres such as musicals, romance, action, and melodrama. Some of the prominent filmmakers of this era were V. Shantaram, Mehboob Khan, Bimal Roy, and Raj Kapoor.
- Regional films (1950s-1960s): The post-independence period witnessed the growth of regional film industries, such as Bengali, Tamil, Telugu, Malayalam, and Kannada. These industries produced films that reflected the cultural, social, and political realities of their regions, as well as experimented with new forms and styles of cinema. Some of the influential filmmakers of this era were Satyajit Ray, Ritwik Ghatak, Guru Dutt, K. Balachander, Adoor Gopalakrishnan, and Mrinal Sen.
- New wave cinema (1970s-1980s): The emergence of a new generation of filmmakers, who were influenced by the global cinema movements and the socio-political changes in India, led to the birth of a new wave of cinema, also known as parallel cinema or art cinema. These filmmakers challenged the conventions of mainstream cinema and explored themes such as realism, alienation, oppression, sexuality, and violence. Some of the notable filmmakers of this era were Shyam Benegal, Govind Nihalani, Mani Kaul, Kumar Shahani, and Ketan Mehta.
