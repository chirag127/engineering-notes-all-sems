### Marriage

Marriage is a social institution that legally and culturally binds two or more individuals as partners. Marriage is considered to be a sacred and lifelong bond in many religions and cultures. Marriage also has various legal, social and economic implications for the individuals and the society.

Some of the main points to note about marriage in India are:

- **Weddings in India vary according to the region, the religion, the community and the personal preferences of the bride and groom.** There are different rituals, ceremonies, customs and traditions that are followed by different groups of people. Some of the common types of weddings in India are Hindu, Muslim, Christian, Sikh, Jain, Buddhist, Parsi, etc. Each of these weddings has its own set of rules and regulations that govern the eligibility, consent, dowry, divorce, inheritance, etc. of the spouses.
- **The average age of marriage for women in India has increased to 21 years, according to the 2011 Census of India.** In 2009, about 7% of women got married before the age of 18. Arranged marriages have long been the norm in Indian society, where the parents or elders of the families select the prospective partners for their children, based on various criteria such as caste, religion, education, income, horoscope, etc. However, in recent times, there has been a growing trend for some women to choose their own partners or to not marry at all, due to various factors such as education, career, personal preferences, etc. Technology is also replacing traditional methods of matchmaking, as many young Indians and their parents are turning to online marriage sites to find a partner. 
- **In India, the apex court has provided the right to life and liberty under Article 21 of the Constitution. This includes the right to get married.** However, in order to be married legally, both the bride and groom must complete certain requirements that are laid down by the laws. There are different laws that govern marriage in India, depending on the religion of the parties. Some of the major laws are the Hindu Marriage Act, 1955, the Muslim Personal Law (Shariat) Application Act, 1937, the Indian Christian Marriage Act, 1872, the Special Marriage Act, 1954, etc. These laws specify the conditions for a valid marriage, such as age, consent, mental capacity, prohibited degrees of relationship, etc. They also provide for the registration, dissolution, maintenance, custody, etc. of the marriage. 
- **There are few inter-caste or interfaith marriages in India.** Marrying within your caste remains an essential feature of marriage in India. In a 2014 survey of more than 70,000 people, fewer than 5% of marriages were inter-caste and fewer than 2% were interfaith. Caste and religion are seen as markers of identity, status and compatibility in the Indian society. Inter-caste and interfaith marriages often face opposition, discrimination and violence from the families and the communities of the spouses. The government has introduced various schemes and incentives to promote inter-caste and interfaith marriages, such as the Dr. Ambedkar Scheme for Social Integration through Inter-Caste Marriages, the Scheme for Inter-Caste Marriage with Dalits, etc. 
- **Getting married in India requires a lot of planning, preparation and paperwork.** At a minimum, you’ll need the following documents in advance of your wedding day: Proof of Residency and Address in India for at least 30 days prior to the application, for at least one of the parties; A valid passport for both parties; Original birth certificate for both parties; Visa or Ration Card; Affidavit stating the date of birth, marital status and nationality of both parties; No Objection Certificate from the respective embassy or consulate, if one or both parties are foreign nationals; Divorce decree or death certificate, if one or both parties are previously married; Notice of Intended Marriage, to be filed at the local marriage registrar office; Marriage Certificate, to be issued by the marriage registrar after the wedding ceremony. 

These are some of the key points to understand about marriage in India. Marriage is a complex and diverse phenomenon that reflects the social, cultural, religious and legal aspects of the Indian society. Marriage is also a dynamic and evolving institution that is influenced by the changing needs, aspirations and challenges of the people.
