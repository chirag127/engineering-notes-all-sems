### Music for the notes of the Module 5- Cultural Heritage and Performing Arts in the subject of INDIAN TRADITIONS, CULTURAL AND SOCIETY

- Music is one of the oldest and most diverse forms of performing arts in India. It has a long and rich history that spans across various regions, languages, religions and genres.
- Music in India can be broadly classified into two main traditions: Carnatic and Hindustani. Carnatic music is the classical music of South India, while Hindustani music is the classical music of North India. Both traditions share some common elements, such as the use of raga and tala, but also have distinct features, such as the instruments, styles, forms and compositions.
- Raga is the melodic framework of Indian music. It is a set of notes that defines the mood, emotion and character of a musical piece. Each raga has its own rules of ascending and descending, as well as specific ornaments and phrases. There are hundreds of ragas in Indian music, each with its own name and personality.
- Tala is the rhythmic framework of Indian music. It is a cycle of beats that repeats throughout a musical piece. Each tala has a fixed number of beats, divided into smaller units called matras. The first beat of a tala is called sam, and it is the most important and accentuated beat. There are many types of talas in Indian music, each with its own name and structure.
- Indian music is mainly based on oral tradition and improvisation. The musicians learn from their gurus (teachers) and practice for years to master the nuances and subtleties of their art. They also improvise within the boundaries of the raga and tala, creating spontaneous and expressive musical expressions. The main forms of improvisation in Indian music are alap, jor, jhala, tan and bol.
- Alap is the introductory section of a musical piece, where the musician explores the raga without any rhythm or tempo. It is a slow and meditative exposition of the raga, where the musician establishes the mood and the tonal center of the piece.
- Jor is the second section of a musical piece, where the musician introduces a pulse or a tempo, but still without any fixed rhythm. It is a transitional section, where the musician gradually increases the speed and intensity of the music, leading to the next section.
- Jhala is the third section of a musical piece, where the musician plays fast and rhythmic patterns, usually on the drone strings of the instrument. It is a climactic section, where the musician showcases their virtuosity and skill, creating a contrast with the previous sections.
- Tan is a type of improvisation, where the musician plays fast and melodic phrases, usually in ascending or descending order. It is a way of embellishing and ornamenting the raga, adding variety and excitement to the music.
- Bol is a type of improvisation, where the musician sings or recites the syllables of the tala, or the names of the notes of the raga. It is a way of demonstrating the rhythmic and melodic structure of the music, as well as creating a dialogue with the accompanying musicians.
- Indian music has a rich and diverse repertoire of compositions, ranging from ancient hymns and chants, to medieval devotional songs and poems, to modern classical and popular songs and film music. Some of the main types of compositions in Indian music are kriti, dhrupad, khayal, thumri, bhajan, ghazal and filmi.
- Kriti is a type of composition in Carnatic music, consisting of three parts: pallavi, anupallavi and charanam. It is a devotional song, usually in praise of a deity or a guru. It is composed by a renowned musician or composer, and follows a specific raga and tala.
- Dhrupad is a type of composition in Hindustani music, consisting of four parts: sthayi, antara, sanchari and abhog. It is a sacred song, usually in praise of a deity or a king. It is composed by a revered poet or saint, and follows a specific raga and tala.
- Khayal is a type of composition in Hindustani music, consisting of two parts: vilambit and drut. It is a romantic or expressive song, usually in praise of a beloved or a mood. It is composed by a talented musician or poet, and follows a flexible raga and tala.
