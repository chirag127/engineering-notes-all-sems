### Contract Theory for the notes of the Module 1- Society State and Polity in India in the subject of INDIAN TRADITIONS, CULTURAL AND SOCIETY

- Contract theory is the view that the moral and/or political obligations of individuals are dependent upon a contract or agreement among them to form a society.
- Contract theory can be traced back to ancient and medieval thinkers such as Plato, Aristotle, Cicero, Aquinas, Hobbes, Locke, Rousseau, Kant, and Rawls.
- Contract theory can be divided into two main types: social contract theory and legal contract theory.
- Social contract theory is the view that the legitimacy of the state and its authority over the individuals is derived from a hypothetical or actual agreement among the people to form a political community.
- Legal contract theory is the view that the rights and obligations of the parties to a contract are determined by the terms and conditions of the agreement, which are enforceable by law.
- In the Indian context, contract theory can be applied to both the constitutional and the contractual aspects of the society and the state. 
- The Constitution of India can be seen as a social contract between the people and the state, which defines the basic structure, principles, and values of the political system and the fundamental rights and duties of the citizens.
- The Indian Contract Act, 1872 can be seen as a legal contract framework, which regulates the formation, performance, and enforcement of contracts in India, and provides remedies for breach of contract.
- The interpretation of contracts in India is guided by the principles of natural justice, good faith, and reasonableness, and the courts seek to ascertain the intention of the parties from the language and the context of the contract. 
- The courts in India also imply certain terms into contracts, such as the duty of care, the duty to cooperate, and the duty to mitigate loss, to supplement the express terms and to ensure fairness and efficiency.
