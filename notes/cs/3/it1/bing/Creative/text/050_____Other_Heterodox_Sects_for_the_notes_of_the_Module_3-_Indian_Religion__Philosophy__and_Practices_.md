### Other Heterodox Sects

- Heterodox sects are those that do not accept the authority of the Vedas and the Brahmanical tradition.
- They emerged in the 6th century BCE as a reaction to the social, economic and religious changes in India.
- They challenged the caste system, the ritual sacrifices, the priestly domination and the metaphysical speculation of the orthodox schools.
- They offered alternative paths of salvation based on ethical conduct, rational inquiry and personal experience.
- Some of the heterodox sects are:

  - **Ajivika**: A fatalistic sect that believed in the doctrine of niyati (determinism) and denied the role of free will, karma and rebirth. They followed a strict ascetic lifestyle and practiced austerities to exhaust their predetermined destiny. They were founded by Makkhali Gosala, a contemporary of Mahavira and Buddha .
  - **Unchedvadi**: A materialistic sect that rejected the existence of soul, God, afterlife and moral values. They advocated the pursuit of sensual pleasures and worldly happiness. They were also known as Lokayata or Charvaka. They were influenced by the teachings of Brihaspati, a mythical sage.
  - **Nityavadi**: An eternalist sect that believed in the permanence of all things and denied the possibility of change and transformation. They were opposed to the doctrine of anitya (impermanence) taught by Buddhism and Jainism. They were followers of Purana Kassapa, another contemporary of Mahavira and Buddha.
  - **Sandehvadi**: An agnostic sect that suspended judgment on all metaphysical and religious questions. They argued that the truth is unknowable and uncertain. They were followers of Sanjaya Belatthiputta, who was also a contemporary of Mahavira and Buddha.
  - **Akriyavadi**: An amoralist sect that denied the existence of karma and its consequences. They claimed that actions have no moral or causal effects. They were followers of Ajita Kesakambali, who was also a contemporary of Mahavira and Buddha.
