### Puppetry

Puppetry is an ancient art form of storytelling that involves manipulating puppets to perform various actions and dialogues. Puppetry is a part of the cultural heritage and performing arts of India, as it reflects the social, religious, and historical aspects of the country. Puppetry in India can be classified into four main types, based on the materials and techniques used to create and operate the puppets. These are:

- **String puppets**: These are puppets that are controlled by strings attached to their limbs and head. The strings are manipulated by the puppeteer from above, using a wooden device called a kathputli. String puppets are the most popular form of puppetry in India, and are found in many states such as Rajasthan, Orissa, Karnataka, Tamil Nadu, and Maharashtra. Some of the famous string puppet forms are Kathputli (Rajasthan), Kundhei (Orissa), Gombeyatta (Karnataka), Bommalattam (Tamil Nadu), and Kalasutri Bahulya (Maharashtra). String puppets are usually made of wood, cloth, leather, or paper, and are decorated with bright colors and costumes. String puppets perform stories from epics, legends, folk tales, and social issues.  

- **Shadow puppets**: These are puppets that are made of translucent materials such as leather, paper, or cloth, and are cut into various shapes and figures. The puppets are held against a screen that is lit from behind, creating shadows that are visible to the audience. The puppeteer manipulates the puppets by moving them closer or farther from the light source, and also provides the voice and narration. Shadow puppets are found in many states such as Kerala, Andhra Pradesh, Karnataka, Maharashtra, and Tamil Nadu. Some of the famous shadow puppet forms are Tholpavakoothu (Kerala), Tholu Bommalata (Andhra Pradesh), Togalu Gombeyatta (Karnataka), Chamadyache Bahulya (Maharashtra), and Tolubommalata (Tamil Nadu). Shadow puppets are usually made of leather, and are painted with vegetable dyes and perforated with intricate designs. Shadow puppets perform stories from epics, legends, folk tales, and local myths.  

- **Rod puppets**: These are puppets that are controlled by rods attached to their limbs and head. The rods are manipulated by the puppeteer from below, using his or her hands or a wooden device. Rod puppets are found in many states such as West Bengal, Bihar, Orissa, and Assam. Some of the famous rod puppet forms are Putul Nautch (West Bengal), Yampuri (Bihar), Kathi Kandhe (Orissa), and Putala Nach (Assam). Rod puppets are usually made of wood, cloth, leather, or paper, and are decorated with colorful costumes and accessories. Rod puppets perform stories from epics, legends, folk tales, and social issues.  

- **Glove puppets**: These are puppets that are worn on the hands of the puppeteer, who uses his or her fingers to move the puppets' limbs and head. The puppeteer also provides the voice and narration for the puppets. Glove puppets are found in many states such as Uttar Pradesh, Kerala, Orissa, and West Bengal. Some of the famous glove puppet forms are Pavakoothu (Uttar Pradesh), Pavakathakali (Kerala), Sakhi Kundhei (Orissa), and Benir Putul (West Bengal). Glove puppets are usually made of cloth, leather, or paper, and are decorated with colorful costumes and accessories. Glove puppets perform stories from epics, legends, folk tales, and comic situations.  

Puppetry in India is a family business, and the puppeteers inherit the puppets and the skills from their ancestors. Puppetry is also a way of preserving and transmitting the cultural and religious values of the society. Puppetry is a versatile and expressive art form that can entertain, educate, and inspire the audience.
