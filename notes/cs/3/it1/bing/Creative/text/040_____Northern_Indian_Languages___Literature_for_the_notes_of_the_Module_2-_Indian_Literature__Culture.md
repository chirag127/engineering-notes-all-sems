### Northern Indian Languages and Literature

- Northern India is a diverse region with many languages and dialects that have evolved from the ancient Indo-Aryan languages and the medieval Apabhramsha languages.
- Some of the major languages of northern India are Hindi, Urdu, Punjabi, Kashmiri, Rajasthani, Gujarati, Sindhi, Marathi, Nepali, and Bhojpuri.
- These languages have developed rich and varied literature in poetry, prose, drama, and folk genres, reflecting the culture, history, and traditions of the people of northern India.

  - Kalidasa, who wrote classic Sanskrit plays and poems in the 4th-5th century CE, such as Abhijnanasakuntalam, Raghuvamsa, and Meghaduta .

  - Guru Nanak, who founded Sikhism and wrote hymns and songs in Punjabi and other languages in the 15th-16th century CE, such as Japji Sahib, Asa di Var, and Sidh Gosht.
  - Mirabai, who wrote bhakti poetry and songs in Rajasthani and other languages in the 16th century CE, such as Mira Bai ki Padavali, Chittor ki Rani, and Hari Tum Haro.
  - Tulsidas, who wrote epic poetry and devotional songs in Awadhi and other languages in the 16th-17th century CE, such as Ramcharitmanas, Vinay Patrika, and Hanuman Chalisa.
  - Surdas, who wrote lyrical poetry and songs in Braj Bhasha and other languages in the 16th-17th century CE, such as Sur Sagar, Sur Saravali, and Sahitya Lahari.
