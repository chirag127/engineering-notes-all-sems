### Shankaracharya

- Shankaracharya is a title given to the heads of monastic institutions (mathas) in the Advaita Vedanta tradition of Hinduism .
- The title derives from the name of Adi Shankara, an 8th-century philosopher and theologian who is considered the founder of the Advaita Vedanta school .
- Adi Shankara was born in Kaladi, Kerala, and traveled across India to propagate his teachings and establish mathas .
- He wrote commentaries on the Upanishads, the Brahma Sutras, and the Bhagavad Gita, as well as original works such as the Vivekachudamani and the Upadesasahasri .
- He taught that the ultimate reality (Brahman) is one, without attributes, and identical to the individual self (Atman) .
- He advocated the path of knowledge (Jnana) as the means to attain liberation from the cycle of birth and death (Samsara) .
- He also defended the authority of the Vedas and the importance of the four stages of life (Ashramas) and the four goals of life (Purusharthas) .
- He is credited with reviving the Hindu tradition and influencing the development of various schools of thought and movements within Hinduism .
- He is revered as a saint and a teacher by his followers and by many other Hindu traditions .
- He is also associated with the worship of Shiva and the six-faced deity Subrahmanya .
