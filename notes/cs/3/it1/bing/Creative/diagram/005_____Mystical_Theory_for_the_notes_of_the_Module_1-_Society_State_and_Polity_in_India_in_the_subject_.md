### Mystical Theory for the notes of the Module 1- Society State and Polity in India in the subject of INDIAN TRADITIONS, CULTURAL AND SOCIETY

- Mystical theory is one of the theories that attempts to explain the origin of the state in ancient India.
- According to this theory, the state is a divine institution that is established by God or his representative on earth.
- The king is the embodiment of God or his chosen one, who rules by divine right and grace.
- The king is the source of law and justice, and his authority is absolute and unquestionable.
- The king is also the protector and benefactor of his subjects, who owe him loyalty and obedience.
- The king is expected to uphold the dharma (righteousness) and perform sacrifices and rituals for the welfare of the state and the society.
- The king is assisted by a council of ministers, who are also appointed by God or the king himself.
- The mystical theory is based on the ancient scriptures and texts, such as the Vedas, the Upanishads, the Mahabharata, the Ramayana, the Manusmriti, etc.
- The mystical theory reflects the religious and spiritual worldview of the ancient Indians, who believed in the supremacy of God and the cosmic order.
- The mystical theory also justifies the hierarchical and patriarchal structure of the society, where the king is at the top and the Brahmins are the highest caste.
