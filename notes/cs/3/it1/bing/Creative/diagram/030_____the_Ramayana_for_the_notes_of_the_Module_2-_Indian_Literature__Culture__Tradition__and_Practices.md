### The Ramayana

The Ramayana is one of the two major Hindu epics, along with the Mahabharata, that narrates the life and adventures of Rama, the seventh incarnation of the god Vishnu. The Ramayana consists of 24,000 verses in seven books (kandas) and 500 cantos (sargas). It was composed by the sage Valmiki, who is considered the first poet in Sanskrit literature. The Ramayana is a story of dharma (righteousness), karma (action and consequence), and bhakti (devotion).

The main points of the Ramayana are:

- Rama is the eldest son of King Dasaratha of Ayodhya, who is chosen to succeed his father as the king. He is married to Sita, the daughter of King Janaka of Mithila, who is the incarnation of the goddess Lakshmi. Rama is an ideal man, who is virtuous, brave, loyal, and compassionate.
- Rama's stepmother, Kaikeyi, is influenced by her maid, Manthara, to ask Dasaratha to exile Rama for 14 years and make her son, Bharata, the king instead. Dasaratha reluctantly agrees, but dies of grief soon after. Rama accepts his fate and leaves for the forest with Sita and his brother, Lakshmana. Bharata refuses to take the throne and goes to the forest to persuade Rama to return, but Rama insists on fulfilling his father's promise. Bharata takes Rama's sandals and places them on the throne as a symbol of Rama's authority.
- Rama, Sita, and Lakshmana wander in the forest and meet many sages, allies, and enemies. They encounter the demoness Surpanakha, who tries to seduce Rama and Lakshmana, but is rejected and mutilated by them. She complains to her brother, Ravana, the king of Lanka, who is a powerful and evil demon with ten heads and twenty arms. Ravana decides to abduct Sita and avenge his sister. He sends his uncle, Maricha, to disguise as a golden deer and lure Rama and Lakshmana away from Sita. Sita is then kidnapped by Ravana, who flies away in his chariot. Rama and Lakshmana chase after him, but are unable to catch him. They meet the vulture, Jatayu, who tries to stop Ravana, but is fatally wounded. Jatayu tells Rama and Lakshmana the direction of Ravana's flight before dying.
- Rama and Lakshmana search for Sita and meet the monkey king, Sugriva, and his minister, Hanuman, who are also enemies of Ravana. Sugriva promises to help Rama in exchange for Rama's help in defeating his brother, Vali, who has usurped his kingdom and wife. Rama kills Vali and restores Sugriva's throne. Sugriva then mobilizes his army of monkeys and bears to find Sita. Hanuman, who is the son of the wind god, Vayu, and a devotee of Rama, is sent as a scout to Lanka. He finds Sita in Ravana's garden, where she is guarded by demonesses and tormented by Ravana. Hanuman gives Sita Rama's ring as a token of his identity and assures her of Rama's arrival. He also sets fire to Lanka and kills many demons, but is captured and brought before Ravana. Ravana orders to burn Hanuman's tail, but Hanuman escapes and returns to Rama with the news of Sita's location and condition.
- Rama and his allies build a bridge across the ocean to reach Lanka. They engage in a fierce battle with Ravana and his army of demons. Many heroes and villains are killed or wounded in the war. Rama kills Ravana's brother, Kumbhakarna, who is a giant and a formidable warrior. He also kills Ravana's son, Indrajit, who is a master of magic and weapons. Finally, Rama faces Ravana in a one-on-one duel and slays him with the help of the god Brahma's arrow. Rama rescues Sita and crowns Ravana's brother, Vibhishana, as the new king of Lanka.
