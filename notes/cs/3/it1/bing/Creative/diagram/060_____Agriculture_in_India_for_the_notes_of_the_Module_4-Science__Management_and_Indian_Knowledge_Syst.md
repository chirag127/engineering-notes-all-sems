### Agriculture in India

- Agriculture is one of the major sectors of the Indian economy, contributing about 16% of the gross domestic product (GDP) and employing about 58% of the population.
- India has a diverse agro-climatic zone, ranging from tropical to temperate, and produces a variety of crops, such as cereals, pulses, oilseeds, fruits, vegetables, spices, sugarcane, cotton, jute, tea, coffee, rubber, etc.
- India is the world's largest producer of many fresh fruits, such as banana, mango, guava, papaya, lemon, and vegetables, such as chickpea, okra, and milk, major spices, such as chili pepper, ginger, fibrous crops, such as jute, staples, such as millets, and castor oilseed.
- India is also the world's second-largest producer of rice, wheat, sugarcane, cotton, and groundnut, and the third-largest producer of tobacco and coconut.
- India has the world's largest cattle herd (buffaloes), and is the largest producer of milk, with an annual production of 198 million tonnes in 2020-21.
- India's agriculture sector is facing several challenges, such as low productivity, declining soil fertility, water scarcity, climate change, pests and diseases, market volatility, and policy constraints.
- India's agriculture sector is also undergoing structural changes, such as diversification towards high-value crops, increasing mechanization, integration with global markets, and adoption of new technologies, such as biotechnology, precision farming, and digital agriculture.
- India's agriculture sector is supported by various policies and programs of the central and state governments, such as the Minimum Support Price (MSP), the Pradhan Mantri Fasal Bima Yojana (PMFBY), the Pradhan Mantri Krishi Sinchayee Yojana (PMKSY), the National Food Security Mission (NFSM), the Rashtriya Krishi Vikas Yojana (RKVY), the National Horticulture Mission (NHM), the National Mission for Sustainable Agriculture (NMSA), etc.
- India's agriculture sector is also influenced by various social and cultural factors, such as the role of women, caste, religion, and indigenous knowledge systems, in shaping the farming practices, resource management, and livelihood outcomes.
- India's agriculture sector is facing new opportunities and challenges in the context of the COVID-19 pandemic, the farm laws, and the global environmental and trade scenarios, which require innovative and inclusive solutions to ensure food security, income stability, and sustainability.
