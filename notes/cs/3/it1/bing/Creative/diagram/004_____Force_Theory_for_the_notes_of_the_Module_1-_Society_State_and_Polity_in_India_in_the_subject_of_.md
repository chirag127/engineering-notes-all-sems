### Force Theory of State Origin

- The force theory of state origin is a theory that explains the emergence of the state as a result of the conquest and domination of one group of people over another by the use of physical force  .
- According to this theory, the state originated due to the need for security, order and protection from external threats, as well as the desire for power, glory and wealth by the conquerors  .
- The theory assumes that the state is a product of violence, coercion and subjugation, and that the state is based on the principle of might is right  .
- The theory also implies that the state is a natural and inevitable phenomenon, and that the state is the highest form of human organization.
- Some examples of the force theory of state origin are the ancient empires of Egypt, Babylon, Assyria, Persia, Greece and Rome, as well as the modern colonial empires of Britain, France, Spain and Portugal  .
- The force theory of state origin has been criticized for being simplistic, unrealistic, ethnocentric and ahistorical. It ignores the role of consent, cooperation, culture, religion, law and morality in the formation and development of the state   .
- The force theory of state origin also fails to account for the diversity and complexity of the state, as well as the variations and changes in the state over time and space   .
