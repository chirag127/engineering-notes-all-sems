# Sangama Literature

Sangama literature is the term used to refer to the ancient Tamil literature that was composed in the region of Tamilakam, which covers the present-day states of Tamil Nadu, Kerala, parts of Andhra Pradesh, Karnataka and Sri Lanka. The word Sangama means a confluence or an assembly, and it denotes the gatherings of Tamil poets and scholars who patronized by various kings and chieftains and produced literary works of high quality and originality.

The Sangama literature is divided into three periods, according to the tradition of the Tamil scholars. The first Sangama lasted for 4,400 years and was presided over by the sage Agastya at Then Madurai, a mythical city that was destroyed by a great flood. The second Sangama lasted for 3,700 years and was presided over by the sage Tolkappiyar at Kapalapuram, another mythical city that was also submerged by the sea. The third Sangama lasted for 1,850 years and was presided over by the poet Nakkirar at Madurai, the capital of the Pandya kingdom. The works of the third Sangama are the only ones that have survived and are available today.

The Sangama literature consists of two main genres: akam and puram. Akam poems deal with the theme of love, both in its various aspects and stages, and in relation to the five landscapes or tinai, which are the mountain, the forest, the pasture, the seashore and the desert. Each landscape has its own flora, fauna, seasons, deities, moods and types of lovers. The akam poems are highly lyrical, rich in imagery and symbolism, and express the emotions and sentiments of the lovers in a refined and subtle manner.

Puram poems deal with the theme of heroism, both in war and in peace, and in relation to the four types of kingship or velir, which are the righteous, the generous, the valiant and the victorious. The puram poems praise the kings and their deeds, their ancestors and their allies, their enemies and their battles, their wealth and their generosity, their virtues and their vices. The puram poems are also historical, political, social and religious, and reflect the culture and values of the ancient Tamils.

The Sangama literature comprises of eight anthologies of akam and puram poems, called Ettuthokai, and ten long poems, called Pattuppattu, which are also divided into akam and puram. The most famous of these are the Kuruntokai, a collection of 400 akam poems, the Purananuru, a collection of 400 puram poems, and the Silappatikaram, an epic poem that narrates the story of a chaste woman named Kannagi and her husband Kovalan, who is falsely accused of stealing a queen's anklet and executed by the king.

The Sangama literature is considered to be one of the oldest and finest examples of Indian literature, and has influenced many later works in Tamil and other languages. The Sangama literature is also a valuable source of information about the history, geography, society, religion, art, science and philosophy of the ancient Tamils. The Sangama literature is a testimony to the creativity, wisdom and culture of the Tamil people.
