### Four-class Classification

- The four-class classification or varna system was a social hierarchy based on the division of labor and the qualities of individuals in ancient India .
- The four classes or varnas were: Brahmins (priests and teachers), Kshatriyas (warriors and rulers), Vaishyas (merchants and farmers), and Shudras (servants and laborers) .
- The varna system was based on the concept of dharma or duty, which prescribed the roles and responsibilities of each class according to their nature and abilities .
- The varna system was also influenced by the doctrine of karma or action, which determined the status and rebirth of individuals according to their deeds and moral conduct .
- The varna system was not rigid or fixed, but flexible and dynamic, allowing for mobility and change among the classes based on merit and performance .
- The varna system was not a caste system, but a broad and general classification of society that did not account for the diversity and complexity of the subgroups and occupations within each class .
- The varna system was not a source of oppression or discrimination, but a way of organizing and harmonizing society according to the principles of justice and order .
