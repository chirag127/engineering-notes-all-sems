### Indian Architecture

- Indian architecture is rooted in the history, culture, and religion of India. It reflects the diversity and richness of Indian civilization and its interactions with other cultures.
- Indian architecture can be classified into several styles and traditions, such as Hindu temple architecture, Indo-Islamic architecture, South Indian architecture, Indo-Saracenic architecture, and modern architecture.
- Hindu temple architecture is characterized by the use of stone, brick, or wood to create elaborate structures with towers, domes, halls, and sanctuaries. The temples are often decorated with sculptures, carvings, and paintings depicting gods, goddesses, and mythological scenes.
- Indo-Islamic architecture is the result of the fusion of Islamic and Indian elements, especially during the medieval period. It includes features such as arches, domes, minarets, courtyards, and geometric patterns. Some examples of Indo-Islamic architecture are the Taj Mahal, the Qutub Minar, and the Red Fort.
- South Indian architecture is influenced by the Dravidian and Pallava cultures, as well as the Chola, Vijayanagara, and Nayaka dynasties. It is known for its massive gopurams (gateway towers), mandapas (pillared halls), and vimanas (pyramidal roofs). Some examples of South Indian architecture are the Brihadeeswarar Temple, the Meenakshi Temple, and the Hampi ruins.
- Indo-Saracenic architecture is a style that emerged during the British colonial rule in India. It combines elements of Indian, Islamic, and Gothic architecture, creating a distinctive and eclectic aesthetic. Some examples of Indo-Saracenic architecture are the Victoria Memorial, the Gateway of India, and the Chhatrapati Shivaji Terminus.
- Modern architecture in India is influenced by various global and local trends, such as the Bauhaus, the International Style, the Postmodernism, and the Regionalism. It also incorporates aspects of sustainability, social responsibility, and cultural identity. Some examples of modern architecture in India are the Lotus Temple, the Chandigarh Capitol Complex, and the IIM Ahmedabad.
