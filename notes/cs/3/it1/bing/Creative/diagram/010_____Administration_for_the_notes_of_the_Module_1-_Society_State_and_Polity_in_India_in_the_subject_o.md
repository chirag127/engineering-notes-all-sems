### Administration

- Administration is the process of managing the affairs of an organization or a state by implementing policies, rules and regulations.
- Administration in India has a long and diverse history, influenced by various factors such as geography, culture, religion, ideology, colonialism and democracy.
- Administration in India can be broadly divided into four phases: ancient, medieval, colonial and post-colonial.

#### Ancient administration

- Ancient administration in India refers to the period from the Indus Valley Civilization (c. 2500-1500 BCE) to the end of the Gupta Empire (c. 550 CE).
- Ancient administration in India was characterized by the following features:
  - The emergence of urban centers, trade networks, social stratification and political organization in the Indus Valley Civilization.
  - The development of the Vedic culture, which laid the foundation of Hinduism, the caste system, the concept of dharma (duty) and the four stages of life (ashramas).
  - The rise and fall of various kingdoms and empires, such as the Mauryan Empire (c. 321-185 BCE), the Kushan Empire (c. 30-375 CE), the Satavahana Empire (c. 230 BCE-220 CE) and the Gupta Empire (c. 320-550 CE), which established centralized and decentralized forms of administration, taxation, law and order, and public welfare.
  - The influence of Buddhism and Jainism, which challenged the authority of the Brahmins and the caste system, and promoted non-violence, equality and renunciation.
  - The interaction with foreign invaders and traders, such as the Greeks, the Scythians, the Parthians, the Sakas, the Huns and the Romans, who brought new ideas, technologies, arts and cultures to India.
