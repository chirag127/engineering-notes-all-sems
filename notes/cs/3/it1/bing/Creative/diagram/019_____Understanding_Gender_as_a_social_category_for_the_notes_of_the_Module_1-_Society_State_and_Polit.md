### Understanding Gender as a Social Category

- Gender is a social category that refers to the social meanings, expectations, and roles associated with being male or female in a given society .
- Gender is not the same as sex, which is a biological classification based on body parts. Sex is determined by chromosomes, hormones, and genitalia, while gender is influenced by culture, identity, behavior, and interaction.
- Gender is a social construct, which means that it is created and maintained by social institutions, norms, and practices. Gender is not fixed or natural, but rather it can change over time and across contexts .
- Gender is also a social relation, which means that it shapes how people interact with each other and how they are positioned in the social hierarchy. Gender affects access to resources, opportunities, and power, as well as experiences of discrimination, oppression, and violence .
- Gender is not a binary or a dichotomy, but rather a spectrum or a continuum. There are more than two genders, and some people may identify with multiple or no gender at all. Gender diversity is a natural and normal variation of human expression .
- Gender is one of the major axes of social difference and inequality, along with other categories such as race, class, caste, religion, ethnicity, sexuality, and disability. These categories intersect and overlap, creating complex and unique identities and experiences for individuals and groups .
