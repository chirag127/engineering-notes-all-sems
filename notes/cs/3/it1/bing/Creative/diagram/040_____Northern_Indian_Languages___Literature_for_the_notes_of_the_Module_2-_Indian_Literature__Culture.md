### Northern Indian Languages & Literature

- Northern Indian languages are derived from the Indo-Aryan branch of the Indo-European language family, which evolved from the ancient Sanskrit and Prakrit languages .
- The main northern Indian languages are Hindi, Urdu, Punjabi, Kashmiri, Sindhi, Gujarati, Marathi, Bengali, Assamese, Oriya, Nepali, and Bihari .
- These languages have developed their own scripts, vocabularies, grammars, and literary traditions over the centuries, influenced by various cultural, religious, and political factors  .
- Some of the common features of northern Indian languages are the use of retroflex consonants, the distinction between aspirated and unaspirated stops, the presence of gender and number agreement, and the use of postpositions .
- Northern Indian literature is rich and diverse, reflecting the diversity of the regions, communities, and genres of the speakers  .
- Some of the earliest forms of northern Indian literature are the epics, the Puranas, and the Bhakti poetry, which are based on the Sanskrit sources and express the religious and cultural values of the people  .
- Some of the prominent writers and poets of northern Indian literature are Kalidasa, Kabir, Tulsidas, Amir Khusrau, Guru Nanak, Mirabai, Surdas, Jayadeva, Vidyapati, Rabindranath Tagore, Premchand, Sarat Chandra Chattopadhyay, and Bankim Chandra Chatterjee    .
- Some of the genres and forms of northern Indian literature are the drama, the lyric, the epic, the novel, the short story, the essay, the biography, the autobiography, the travelogue, the satire, the allegory, the fable, the folk tale, and the song   .
- Northern Indian literature has contributed to the national and regional identity, the social and political movements, the cultural and linguistic diversity, and the artistic and aesthetic expression of the people of India    .
