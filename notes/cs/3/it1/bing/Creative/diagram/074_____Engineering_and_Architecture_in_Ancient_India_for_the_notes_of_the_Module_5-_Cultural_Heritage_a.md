# Engineering and Architecture in Ancient India

- Engineering and architecture in ancient India was known as **Sthapatya-Kala**, which means the art of constructing something.
- Ancient Indian engineers and architects derived principles of construction and architecture based on experience, observation of natural phenomena and social and cultural aspects of India.
- Ancient India witnessed two urban revolutions: the first one in the Harappan civilization (2500-1900 BCE) and the second one in the Mauryan and Kushan empires (6th-3rd centuries BCE).
- The Harappan civilization is known for its urban planning, drainage systems, granaries, baths, docks and standardized weights and measures.
- The Mauryan and Kushan empires are known for their stone pillars, rock-cut caves, stupas, chaityas, viharas and temples.
- Ancient India also developed various styles of temple architecture, such as the Nagara style in North India and the Dravida style in South India.
- The Nagara style features a shikhara (spire) over the sanctum, while the Dravida style features a vimana (tower) over the sanctum.
- Some of the famous examples of ancient Indian temple architecture are the Kailasa Temple at Ellora, the Khajuraho Temples, the Brihadeeswara Temple at Thanjavur and the Sun Temple at Konark .
- Ancient India also produced engineering marvels such as the stepwells, which are wells or ponds with a series of steps leading down to the water level.
- Stepwells were used for water storage, irrigation, bathing, socializing and religious ceremonies.
- Some of the famous examples of stepwells are the Rani ki Vav at Patan, the Chand Baori at Abhaneri and the Adalaj Vav at Ahmedabad.
