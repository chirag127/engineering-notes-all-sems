### Astronomy in India

- Astronomy has a long history in the Indian subcontinent, stretching from pre-historic to modern times. Some of the earliest roots of Indian astronomy can be dated to the period of Indus Valley civilization or earlier.
- Astronomy later developed as a discipline of Vedanga, or one of the "auxiliary disciplines" associated with the study of the Vedas, the ancient scriptures of Hinduism. The Vedanga Jyotisha, one of the earliest texts on astronomy and astrology, is attributed to Lagadha, who lived around the 14th century BCE.
- Indian astronomy was influenced by Greek astronomy beginning in the 4th century BCE and through the early centuries of the Common Era, for example by the Yavanajataka and the Romaka Siddhanta, Sanskrit translations of Greek texts disseminated from the 2nd century. Indian astronomers adopted the zodiac signs, the seven planets, and the concept of epicycles from the Greeks .
- Indian astronomy flourished during the classical period, from the 5th to the 11th centuries CE, with the contributions of many scholars such as Aryabhata, Varahamihira, Brahmagupta, Bhaskara, Lalla, and Sripati. They developed mathematical methods for calculating planetary positions, eclipses, and the duration of the day and night. They also proposed heliocentric models, elliptical orbits, and the rotation of the earth on its axis .
- Indian astronomy continued to develop during the medieval period, from the 12th to the 18th centuries CE, with the works of Bhaskara II, Madhava, Nilakantha, Jyesthadeva, Munisvara, and others. They refined the methods of trigonometry, algebra, and calculus for astronomical computations. They also made observations of comets, supernovae, and lunar and solar eclipses .
- Indian astronomy was influenced by Islamic astronomy from the 13th century onwards, as a result of the cultural exchange between the Muslim and Hindu kingdoms. Indian astronomers adopted the use of the astrolabe, the sine quadrant, and the Arabic numerals from the Muslims. They also translated Arabic texts into Sanskrit and wrote commentaries on them .
- Indian astronomy was introduced to Europe by the Jesuit missionaries and the East India Company in the 16th and 17th centuries. The Surya Siddhanta, the most popular work on astronomy in North India, was translated into Latin by John Zephaniah Holwell in 1767. The Kerala school of astronomy and mathematics, which developed a series of infinite series expansions for trigonometric functions, was studied by Charles Whish in 1835  .
- Indian astronomy entered a modern phase in the 19th and 20th centuries, with the establishment of observatories, institutes, and universities for astronomical research and education. Some of the notable figures in this period include Radhanath Sikdar, who measured the height of Mount Everest in 1852, C.V. Raman, who won the Nobel Prize in Physics in 1930 for his discovery of the Raman effect, and Meghnad Saha, who developed the Saha ionization equation for stellar spectra in 1920 .
- Indian astronomy has made significant contributions to the fields of cosmology, astrophysics, planetary science, and space exploration in the 21st century. Some of the achievements include the launch of the Aryabhata satellite in 1975, the first Indian satellite, the discovery of water on the moon by the Chandrayaan-1 mission in 2008, the Mars Orbiter Mission (Mangalyaan) in 2013, the first interplanetary mission by an Asian country, and the AstroSat in 2015, the first Indian space observatory .
