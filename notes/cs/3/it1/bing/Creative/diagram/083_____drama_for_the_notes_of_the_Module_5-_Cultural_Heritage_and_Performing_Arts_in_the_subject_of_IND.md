### Drama

- Drama is a form of performing arts, where stories are enacted by the use of dialogue, music, gesture and dance.
- Drama in the Indian subcontinent has a long and rich history, dating back to the Vedic period (1200-1500 BCE), when hymns, dialogues and scenes were composed in Sanskrit.
- Drama in India has been influenced by various religious, cultural and social factors, such as Hinduism, Buddhism, Jainism, Islam, colonialism, nationalism and modernity .
- Drama in India can be classified into two broad categories: classical and folk .
- Classical drama refers to the drama that follows the rules and conventions laid down by the Nātyaśāstra, a treatise on dramaturgy attributed to Bharata Muni, a sage and scholar who lived around the 3rd-4th century BCE .
- The Nātyaśāstra defines drama as a representation of the three worlds: the divine, the human and the demonic, and prescribes the elements of plot, character, dialogue, music, dance, gesture, costume, stage and audience .
- The Nātyaśāstra also classifies drama into ten types, based on the dominant sentiment (rasa) and the number of acts. The ten types are: nāṭaka (heroic), prakaraṇa (social), bhāṇa (monologue), prahasana (farce), ḍima (satire), vyāyoga (minor heroic), samavakāra (mixed), īhāmṛga (fantastic), anka (episodic) and vīthi (street play) .
- Some of the famous examples of classical drama in India are: Abhijñānaśākuntalam (The Recognition of Shakuntala) by Kālidāsa, Mṛcchakaṭika (The Little Clay Cart) by Śūdraka, Mālavikāgnimitram (Mālavikā and Agnimitra) by Kālidāsa, Mudrārākṣasa (The Signet of the Minister) by Viśākhadatta, Ratnāvalī (The Jewel Garland) by Harṣa, and Uttararāmacarita (The Later Deeds of Rama) by Bhavabhūti .
- Folk drama refers to the drama that is performed by the common people, often in rural areas, and reflects their local traditions, customs, beliefs and values .
- Folk drama in India is very diverse and varies from region to region, community to community, and language to language .
- Folk drama in India often incorporates elements of music, dance, ritual, myth, legend, humour, satire, social criticism and political commentary .
- Folk drama in India also adapts to the changing times and contexts, and incorporates new themes, forms and techniques .
- Some of the famous types of folk drama in India are: Yatra/Jatra (Bengal and Odisha), Ramleela and Raasleela (Uttar Pradesh and other parts of North India), Swang and Nautanki (Haryana, Punjab and Uttar Pradesh), Dashavatar (Maharashtra and Goa), Kariyala and Bhagat (Himachal Pradesh), Khyal and Tamasha (Rajasthan and Maharashtra), Bhavai and Rasdhari (Gujarat), Yakshagana and Bayalata (Karnataka), Therukoothu and Kattaikkuttu (Tamil Nadu), and Kathakali and Koodiyattam (Kerala)  .
