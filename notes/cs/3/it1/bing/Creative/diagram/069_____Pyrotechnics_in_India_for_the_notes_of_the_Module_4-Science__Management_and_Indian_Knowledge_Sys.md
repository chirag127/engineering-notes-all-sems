### Pyrotechnics in India

- Pyrotechnics is the science and art of creating and using fireworks and other explosive devices for entertainment, military, or industrial purposes.
- Fireworks and pyrotechnic shows existed as a form of royal entertainment in many medieval Indian kingdoms during festivals, events and special occasions like weddings.
- Manufacturing formulas for fireworks describing pyrotechnic mixtures are found within Kautukachintamani, a Sanskrit volume by Gajapati Prataparudradeva (1497-1539), a reputed king of the Suryavamsi Gajapati dynasty of Odisha.
- Fireworks were also used for religious and cultural celebrations, such as Diwali, the festival of lights, which symbolizes the victory of good over evil and the return of Lord Rama to Ayodhya after defeating Ravana.
- Fireworks are also associated with other festivals, such as Holi, the festival of colors, Dussehra, the festival of the victory of good over evil, and Ganesh Chaturthi, the festival of the elephant-headed god.
- Fireworks are also used for sporting events, such as cricket matches, and for political rallies and public gatherings.
- India is one of the largest producers and consumers of fireworks in the world, with an estimated annual turnover of over Rs. 20,000 crore and employing over 8 lakh people, mostly in the southern state of Tamil Nadu.
- However, fireworks also pose a serious threat to the environment and human health, as they release harmful pollutants, such as particulate matter, carbon monoxide, sulfur dioxide, nitrogen oxides, heavy metals, and volatile organic compounds, into the air.
- Fireworks also cause noise pollution, which can affect the hearing and mental well-being of people and animals, and fire accidents, which can result in injuries and deaths.
- Facing expanding evidence that India’s air pollution has become a public health disaster, the government has dramatically rewritten rules on fireworks sales, stringently enforcing a Supreme Court ban on most pyrotechnics and allowing only a handful of “green crackers” to be sold nationwide.
- Green crackers are fireworks that have reduced emission levels and noise levels, and are made with eco-friendly materials, such as paper, cardboard, and biodegradable substances.
- The government has also launched a campaign to promote awareness and encourage people to celebrate festivals in a green and safe manner, by using alternatives, such as candles, lamps, and electric lights, and avoiding bursting crackers.
