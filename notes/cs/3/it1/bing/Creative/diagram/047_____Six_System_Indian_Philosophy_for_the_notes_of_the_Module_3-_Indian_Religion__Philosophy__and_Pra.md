### Six Systems of Indian Philosophy

- The six systems of Indian philosophy are Nyaya, Vaisheshika, Samkhya, Yoga, Purva Mimamsa and Uttara Mimamsa or Vedanta  .
- These systems are based on the Vedas, the ancient scriptures of India, and explore various aspects of reality, knowledge, ethics and liberation.
- Each system has its own founder, texts, concepts, methods and goals.

#### Nyaya
- Nyaya is the school of logic and epistemology, founded by Akshapada Gautama in the 2nd century BCE .
- Nyaya analyzes the sources and validity of knowledge, such as perception, inference, analogy and testimony .
- Nyaya also provides a detailed account of the categories of reality, such as substance, quality, action, relation, universality, negation and inherence .
- Nyaya aims to attain liberation from suffering by eliminating false beliefs and attaining valid knowledge of the self and God .

#### Vaisheshika
- Vaisheshika is the school of atomism and naturalism, founded by Kanada Kashyapa in the 6th century BCE .
- Vaisheshika postulates that all reality is composed of indivisible atoms of earth, water, fire, air and ether, as well as mind, soul and time .
- Vaisheshika also classifies the properties and relations of these entities, such as number, size, shape, motion, contact, causation and inherence .
- Vaisheshika aims to attain liberation from the cycle of rebirth by understanding the nature of reality and detaching from worldly desires .

#### Samkhya
- Samkhya is the school of dualism and analysis, founded by Kapila Muni in the 6th century BCE .
- Samkhya distinguishes between two fundamental principles of reality: purusha (consciousness) and prakriti (matter-energy)^1^ .
- Samkhya explains the evolution of prakriti into various elements, senses, mind and intellect, as well as the bondage and liberation of purusha due to ignorance and knowledge .
- Samkhya aims to attain liberation from suffering by realizing the distinction between purusha and prakriti and attaining self-knowledge .

#### Yoga
- Yoga is the school of discipline and meditation, founded by Patanjali in the 2nd century BCE .
- Yoga is based on the Samkhya philosophy, but adds the concept of Ishvara (God) as the supreme purusha and the source of guidance .
- Yoga prescribes an eightfold path of ethical conduct, physical postures, breath control, sensory withdrawal, concentration, meditation and absorption .
- Yoga aims to attain liberation from the fluctuations of the mind and the identification with the body by uniting with Ishvara or the true self .

#### Purva Mimamsa
- Purva Mimamsa is the school of ritual exegesis and hermeneutics, founded by Jaimini in the 3rd century BCE .
- Purva Mimamsa focuses on the interpretation and application of the Vedic rituals and injunctions, which are considered the source of valid knowledge and duty .
- Purva Mimamsa also defends the authority and eternity of the Vedas, the theory of karma and rebirth, and the concept of dharma (righteousness) as the supreme goal .
- Purva Mimamsa aims to attain liberation from the cycle of rebirth by performing the Vedic rituals and fulfilling one's dharma .
