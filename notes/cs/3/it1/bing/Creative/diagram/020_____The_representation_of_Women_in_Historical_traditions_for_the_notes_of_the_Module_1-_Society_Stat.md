### The representation of Women in Historical traditions

- The position of women in ancient India was not equal to men. They were mainly recognised as wives and mothers, and had to follow the norms and duties prescribed by the scriptures and the society.
- Some women in ancient India, however, had access to education, religious rites, and economic freedom. They also participated in public life and influenced men on social and religious matters . Some examples of such women are Gargi, Maitreyi, Sulabha, and Sita.
- The medieval period saw the decline of women's status due to the invasions, wars, and social changes. Women faced restrictions on their mobility, education, and rights. They also suffered from practices like sati, child marriage, purdah, and polygamy .
- The modern period witnessed the emergence of various social reform movements that challenged the oppressive customs and laws against women. Women also participated in the nationalist struggle and the social movements for equality, education, and empowerment .
- The contemporary period has seen the rise of women in various fields of politics, education, science, arts, and sports. Women have also gained legal and constitutional rights and protections. However, women still face challenges such as gender discrimination, violence, poverty, and lack of representation .
