### Purusārtha

- Purusārtha is a Sanskrit word that means "object (ive) of men" or "human endeavour".
- It is a key concept in Hinduism, and refers to the four proper goals or aims of a human life.
- The four purusārthas are: Dharma, Artha, Kāma, and Moksha. 
- Dharma means "righteousness", "duty", or "ethics". It is the moral law that guides one's actions and responsibilities in accordance with one's nature and role in society. 
- Artha means "wealth", "prosperity", or "economic values". It is the pursuit of material well-being and security that enables one to fulfill one's dharma and kāma. 
- Kāma means "desire", "pleasure", or "love". It is the enjoyment of sensual and aesthetic experiences that enrich one's life and happiness. 
- Moksha means "liberation", "freedom", or "salvation". It is the ultimate goal of human life, which is to attain release from the cycle of birth and death (samsāra) and union with the supreme reality (Brahman). 
- The purusārthas are the inherent values of the universe and the blueprint for human fulfillment. They offer a way for evaluating one's life and making good decisions. They also help one to create a satisfyingly balanced, meaningful life at the deepest and most holistic level.
- The purusārthas are not mutually exclusive or sequential, but interdependent and complementary. They are to be pursued in harmony and moderation, without neglecting or violating any of them. They are also contextual and relative, depending on one's stage of life, social status, and personal circumstances.  
- The purusārthas are derived from the Vedas, the sacred scriptures of Hinduism, and elaborated by various schools of thought and traditions within Hinduism. They reflect the diversity and complexity of Hindu thought and practice, as well as the universal quest for human happiness and transcendence.
