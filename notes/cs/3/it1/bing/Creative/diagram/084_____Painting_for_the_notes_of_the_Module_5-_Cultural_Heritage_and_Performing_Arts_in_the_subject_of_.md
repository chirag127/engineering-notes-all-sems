### Painting

- Painting is one of the oldest and most diverse forms of artistic expression in Indian culture.
- Indian paintings can be broadly classified as murals, miniatures and paintings on cloth.
- Murals are large works executed on the walls of solid structures, such as temples, caves and palaces. Some of the most famous examples of murals are the Ajanta Caves and the Kailashnath temple in Maharashtra, the Brihadeshwara temple in Tamil Nadu, and the Lepakshi temple in Andhra Pradesh.
- Miniatures are small-scale paintings done on paper, palm leaf, ivory, wood or metal. They are usually made for books, albums, manuscripts or as independent works. Some of the most prominent styles of miniatures are the Mughal, Rajput, Pahari, Deccani and Bengal schools.
- Paintings on cloth are done on various types of fabrics, such as cotton, silk, jute or wool. They are often used as wall hangings, banners, scrolls or ritual objects. Some of the most popular styles of paintings on cloth are the Pattachitra of Odisha, the Kalamkari of Andhra Pradesh, the Madhubani of Bihar, the Warli of Maharashtra, the Phad of Rajasthan, and the Tanjore of Tamil Nadu.
- Indian paintings reflect the rich diversity of regional, religious, historical and aesthetic influences that have shaped the Indian civilization. They also showcase the skill, creativity and innovation of the Indian artists who have used various techniques, materials, colors and motifs to create masterpieces of art.
