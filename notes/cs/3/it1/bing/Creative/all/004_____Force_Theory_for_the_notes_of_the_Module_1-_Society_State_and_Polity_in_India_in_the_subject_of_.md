# Force Theory

- Force theory is a view on the origin of the state that argues that nations were created through violence and domination.
- According to this theory, the state originated due to force exerted by the strong over the weak, who then became their subjects and obeyed their commands.
- The theory is based on the old saying that "war begets the king", implying that the state is a product of war and conquest .
- The theory is supported by some historical examples of states that emerged from military conquests, such as the Roman Empire, the Mongol Empire, and the British Empire.
- The theory is criticized for being too simplistic and ignoring other factors that contribute to the formation of the state, such as social contract, divine right, natural law, and consent of the governed.
- The theory also fails to explain the existence of states that are not based on force, such as Switzerland, Iceland, and Costa Rica.
- The theory is mainly of historical interest and does not reflect the modern understanding of the state as a complex and dynamic institution that requires legitimacy, sovereignty, and public welfare.
