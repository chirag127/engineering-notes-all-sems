# Sangama Literature

Sangama literature is the term used to refer to the ancient Tamil literature that was composed in the Tamilakam region of South India. The word Sangama means a confluence or a gathering, and it is derived from the tradition that there were three Sangamas or academies of poets and scholars that flourished at different periods of time and produced the Sangama literature. The Sangama literature is divided into two main categories: akam (love) and puram (heroism).

## Akam Literature

Akam literature consists of poems that deal with various aspects of love, such as courtship, separation, union, and infidelity. The poems are set in different landscapes or tinai, such as the mountains, the forests, the seashore, the desert, and the fields, which symbolize the different stages and emotions of love. The poems also use various natural objects and animals as metaphors and symbols to convey the feelings and situations of the lovers. The akam literature comprises five collections of poems, namely:

- Kuruntokai: A collection of 400 short poems on various aspects of love, attributed to 205 poets.
- Natrinai: A collection of 400 poems on love and friendship, attributed to 245 poets.
- Ainkurunuru: A collection of 500 poems on love in five different landscapes, attributed to five poets.
- Kalittokai: A collection of 150 poems on love in the desert landscape, attributed to 11 poets.
- Paripatal: A collection of 70 poems on love and devotion, attributed to nine poets.

## Puram Literature

Puram literature consists of poems that deal with the themes of heroism, war, valor, generosity, and praise of kings and their deeds. The poems also reflect the social and political conditions of the ancient Tamil kingdoms and their relations with other regions and powers. The puram literature comprises two collections of poems, namely:

- Patirruppattu: A collection of 100 poems on 10 ancient Tamil kings, attributed to 10 poets.
- Purananuru: A collection of 400 poems on various aspects of heroism, attributed to 157 poets.

## Other Works

Apart from the akam and puram collections, the Sangama literature also includes some other works, such as:

- Tirumurukarrupatai: A poem on the praise of the god Murugan, attributed to the poet Nakkirar.
- Tolkappiyam: A treatise on Tamil grammar, phonology, and poetics, attributed to the sage Tolkappiyar.
- Silappatikaram: An epic on the story of Kannagi, a chaste woman who avenges the injustice done to her husband, attributed to the poet Ilango Adigal.
- Manimekalai: An epic on the story of Manimekalai, the daughter of a courtesan who becomes a Buddhist nun, attributed to the poet Sattanar.
- Tirukkural: A collection of 1330 couplets on ethics, politics, and love, attributed to the sage Tiruvalluvar.
