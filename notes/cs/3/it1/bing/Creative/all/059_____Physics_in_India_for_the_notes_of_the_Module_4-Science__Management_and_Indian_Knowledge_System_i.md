# Physics in India

Physics is the branch of science that deals with the nature and properties of matter and energy. Physics in India has a long and rich history, dating back to the ancient times. Here are some points to note about physics in India:

- The Nyaya and Vaisheshika schools of philosophy, which emerged around the 6th century BCE, developed concepts of atomism, causation, inference, logic, and mechanics. They also proposed theories of light, sound, gravity, and motion. These aspects are dealt with in the present day physics, through the laws of motion, gravitation, thermodynamics, wave mechanics, hydrostatics, classical and quantum mechanics, nuclear and atomic physics, and electromagnetic theory.
- The famous mathematician and astronomer Aryabhata, who lived in the 5th century CE, wrote the Aryabhatiya, a treatise on astronomy and mathematics. He gave the value of pi, calculated the circumference of the earth, explained the causes of eclipses, and proposed a heliocentric model of the solar system. He also introduced the concept of zero and the decimal system in India. His work influenced many later Indian and Islamic scholars.
- Another influential astronomer and mathematician was Brahmagupta, who lived in the 7th century CE. He wrote the Brahmasphutasiddhanta, which contains many important results in algebra, geometry, trigonometry, and astronomy. He gave the rules for arithmetic operations with zero and negative numbers, solved quadratic and linear equations, and derived formulas for the area and volume of various geometric figures. He also gave a method for finding the sine of an angle and the inverse sine of a ratio.
- In the 11th century CE, Bhaskara II, also known as Bhaskaracharya, wrote the Siddhanta Shiromani, a comprehensive text on astronomy and mathematics. He gave the correct formula for the length of the year, the diameter of the earth, and the distance between the earth and the moon. He also gave the solutions to various types of indeterminate equations, and developed the concepts of differential calculus and infinitesimals. He also discussed the concepts of gravity, force, acceleration, and free fall.
- In the 14th century CE, Madhava of Sangamagrama, the founder of the Kerala school of astronomy and mathematics, made significant contributions to the fields of calculus, trigonometry, and infinite series. He gave the power series expansions for sine, cosine, tangent, and arctangent functions, and derived the value of pi to 11 decimal places. He also gave the infinite series for the inverse tangent function, which is now known as the Gregory-Leibniz series or the Madhava-Leibniz series. He also gave methods for finding the approximate values of various transcendental numbers, such as pi, e, and square roots.
- In the 16th century CE, Nilakantha Somayaji, a disciple of Madhava, wrote the Tantrasangraha, a treatise on astronomy and mathematics. He improved the heliocentric model of Aryabhata, and gave a correct explanation of the retrograde motion of the planets. He also gave the correct formula for the elliptical orbits of the planets, and derived the value of the astronomical unit, the distance between the earth and the sun. He also gave the infinite series for the sine and cosine functions, and the arctangent function.
- In the 19th century CE, Srinivasa Ramanujan, a self-taught genius, made remarkable contributions to the fields of number theory, algebra, analysis, and combinatorics. He discovered thousands of new formulas and theorems, many of which are still being proved and explored by mathematicians. He also discovered the Ramanujan theta function, the Ramanujan prime, the Ramanujan tau function, the Ramanujan conjecture, the Ramanujan-Soldner constant, the Ramanujan modular equations, and the Ramanujan-Sato series. He also gave a formula for calculating the number of partitions of a positive integer, and a method for finding the approximate value of pi.
- In the 20th century CE, India produced many eminent physicists, such as C.V. Raman, S.N. Bose, Homi Bhabha, Meghnad Saha, Jagadish Chandra Bose, Raja Ramanna, Subrahmanyan Chandrasekhar, and Abdus Salam. They made significant contributions to the fields of atomic physics
