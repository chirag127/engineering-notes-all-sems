# India’s Dominance up to Pre-colonial Times

- Pre-colonial India refers to the period before the British occupation of India, which started in 1757 with the Battle of Plassey.
- India has a long and rich history that dates back to 2500 BC, when the Indus Valley Civilization emerged as one of the earliest urban civilizations in the world .
- India witnessed the rise and fall of many empires and dynasties, such as the Mauryans, Guptas, Kushans, Cholas, Delhi Sultanate, Vijayanagara, Mughals, Marathas, Sikhs and others, that contributed to the political, cultural, religious and economic development of the subcontinent  .
- India was also a hub of trade, commerce, science, technology, art, literature, philosophy and spirituality, attracting travelers, merchants, scholars and invaders from various regions of Asia, Europe, Africa and the Middle East   .
- India had a diverse and pluralistic society, with multiple languages, religions, castes, sects, cultures and traditions coexisting and influencing each other  .
- India was a self-sufficient and prosperous economy, with a large share of the world's GDP, trade and wealth, and a high level of agricultural and industrial production .
- India's dominance up to pre-colonial times was challenged by the colonial policies of the British, who exploited the resources, suppressed the native industries, imposed taxes and tariffs, disrupted the social and political structures, and introduced changes in the education, administration and legal systems of India .
