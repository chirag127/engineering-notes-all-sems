# Political Ideals in Ancient India

- Political ideals in ancient India were based on the concepts of **dharma**, **raja**, **samaj** and **artha**. Dharma was the moral and religious duty of the king and the people, raja was the sovereign authority of the king, samaj was the social order and artha was the material and economic well-being of the state.
- The ancient Indian political thought was influenced by the **Vedic** literature, which reflected the democratic principles and ideals of the early Indo-Aryan society. The Rig Veda, for example, described democracy as a deity called **Samjnana**, which meant the collective consciousness of the people. The Vedic society was divided into four **varnas** or classes, each with its own duties and rights. The Vedic assemblies, such as the **sabha**, **samiti**, **vidatha** and **gana**, were the institutions of participatory governance, where the people could voice their opinions and elect their leaders.
- The ancient Indian political thought also developed under the influence of the **monarchical** states of northern India, which emerged after the sixth century BCE. The monarchical states were based on the concept of **dandaniti** or the science of politics, which was derived from the **Itihasa** or the fifth Veda. The Itihasa consisted of the epics, such as the **Ramayana** and the **Mahabharata**, which narrated the stories of ideal kings and heroes, such as **Rama** and **Krishna**, who upheld the principles of dharma and justice. The monarchical states also adopted the concept of **chakravartin** or the universal ruler, who was supposed to conquer the four quarters of the world and establish a righteous and prosperous empire.
- The ancient Indian political thought reached its peak with the works of **Kautilya**, who wrote the **Arthashastra**, a treatise on statecraft and administration, around the fourth century BCE. Kautilya was the prime minister of **Chandragupta Maurya**, the founder of the Mauryan Empire, which was the first pan-Indian empire in history. Kautilya's Arthashastra was based on the concept of **artha**, which meant the material and economic well-being of the state. Kautilya advocated a pragmatic and realistic approach to politics, where the king had to use various means, such as **sama**, **dana**, **bheda** and **danda**, or conciliation, gift, division and punishment, to achieve his goals. Kautilya also laid down the principles of **foreign policy**, **warfare**, **espionage**, **taxation**, **law and order**, **welfare** and **trade** in his Arthashastra.
- The ancient Indian political thought was not static or conservative, but rather dynamic and adaptable to the changing times and circumstances. The ancient Indian political thinkers also recognized the diversity and plurality of the Indian society and culture, and tried to accommodate different views and interests. The ancient Indian political thought was also influenced by various **philosophical** and **religious** schools, such as **Buddhism**, **Jainism**, **Samkhya**, **Yoga**, **Nyaya**, **Vaisheshika**, **Mimamsa** and **Vedanta**, which offered different perspectives on the nature of reality, the role of human beings and the purpose of life.
