# Astronomy in India

- Astronomy has a long history in the Indian subcontinent, stretching from pre-historic to modern times.
- Some of the earliest roots of Indian astronomy can be dated to the period of Indus Valley civilization or earlier, when people observed the celestial phenomena and developed calendars based on lunar and solar cycles.
- Astronomy later developed as a discipline of Vedanga, or one of the "auxiliary disciplines" associated with the study of the Vedas, the ancient scriptures of Hinduism.
- The Vedanga Jyotisha, attributed to Lagadha, is one of the earliest texts on astronomy in India, dating from around the 6th century BCE. It contains rules for calculating the positions of the sun, moon, and planets, as well as the length of the day, the seasons, and the lunar and solar eclipses.
- Indian astronomy was influenced by Greek astronomy beginning in the 4th century BCE and through the early centuries of the Common Era, for example by the Yavanajataka and the Romaka Siddhanta, Sanskrit translations of Greek texts disseminated from the 2nd century CE.
- The Siddhantas, or astronomical treatises, were composed by various authors from the 5th to the 15th centuries CE, and covered topics such as planetary motions, eclipses, cosmology, and mathematical methods.
- Some of the notable Siddhanta authors include Aryabhata, Varahamihira, Brahmagupta, Bhaskara, and Nilakantha Somayaji.
- Indian astronomers made significant contributions to the fields of trigonometry, algebra, geometry, and arithmetic, and developed methods for solving various astronomical problems.
- They also proposed heliocentric models of the solar system, estimated the size and distance of the earth and the moon, and discovered the precession of the equinoxes.
- Astronomy in India was closely related to astrology, calendar making, and religious rituals, and was patronized by various rulers and institutions.
- The Suryasiddhanta, the most popular work on astronomy in North India, is attributed to divine authorship but seems to have been composed around 800 CE. It adopts the midnight epoch and certain elements from the old Saurasiddhanta, but differs from it in other respects.
- The Indian National Calendar, adopted in 1957, is based on the Saka era and the Suryasiddhanta.
- Astronomy in India continues to thrive in the modern era, with the establishment of various observatories, institutes, and organizations, such as the Indian Institute of Astrophysics, the Indian Space Research Organization, and the Astronomical Society of India.
- Indian astronomers are involved in various research areas, such as solar physics, stellar astronomy, galactic astronomy, extragalactic astronomy, cosmology, and astrobiology.
- India has also participated in several international collaborations and missions, such as the Chandrayaan lunar probes, the Mars Orbiter Mission, the Astrosat satellite, and the Thirty Meter Telescope project.
