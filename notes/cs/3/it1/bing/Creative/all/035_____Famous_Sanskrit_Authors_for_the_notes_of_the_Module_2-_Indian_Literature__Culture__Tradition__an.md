# Famous Sanskrit Authors

- Sanskrit is one of the oldest and most influential languages of India and the world. It has a rich and diverse literature that spans various genres, periods and regions.
- Some of the famous Sanskrit authors and their works are:

  - **Valmiki**: He is regarded as the first poet (Adi Kavi) and the author of the epic Ramayana, which narrates the life and deeds of Rama, the ideal king and hero. He also composed the Yoga Vasistha, a philosophical text that teaches the principles of Vedanta. 
  - **Vyasa**: He is the legendary sage and compiler of the Vedas, the four sacred scriptures of Hinduism. He is also the author of the Mahabharata, the longest epic poem in the world, which depicts the Kurukshetra war between the Pandavas and the Kauravas. He also wrote the Puranas, the Bhagavad Gita, the Brahma Sutras and many other works. 
  - **Kalidasa**: He is considered to be the greatest poet and dramatist of classical Sanskrit literature. He wrote three plays: Abhijnana Shakuntalam (The Recognition of Shakuntala), Vikramorvashiya (Urvashi Won by Valour) and Malavikagnimitram (Malavika and Agnimitra); two epics: Kumarasambhava (The Birth of Kumara) and Raghuvamsha (The Dynasty of Raghu); and several lyric poems, such as Meghaduta (The Cloud Messenger) and Ritusamhara (The Seasons). He is known for his mastery of language, imagery, simile, metaphor and romance.  
  - **Bharavi**: He is the author of Kiratarjuniya (The Arjuna and the Mountain Man), an epic poem that describes the encounter between Arjuna and Lord Shiva in the guise of a hunter. He is praised for his skill in alankara (ornamentation), shlesha (double meaning) and artha (meaning).  
  - **Magha**: He is the author of Shishupala Vadha (The Slaying of Shishupala), an epic poem that narrates the killing of Shishupala, a wicked king and a cousin of Krishna, by the latter at the Rajasuya sacrifice of Yudhishthira. He is admired for his use of chitra kavya (picture poetry), shabda alankara (sound ornamentation) and rasa (sentiment).  
  - **Harsha**: He is the author of Naishadhiya Charitam (The Story of Nala and Damayanti), an epic poem that tells the love story of Nala, the king of Nishadha, and Damayanti, the princess of Vidarbha, and their separation and reunion. He is renowned for his elegance, sweetness and depth of expression.  
  - **Bhasa**: He is one of the earliest and most prolific Sanskrit dramatists, who wrote about thirteen plays, such as Svapnavasavadatta (The Dream of Vasavadatta), Pratijnayaugandharayana (The Pledge of Augandharayana) and Urubhanga (The Shattered Thigh). He is noted for his realism, characterization, humor and tragedy.  
  - **Asvaghosa**: He is a Buddhist poet and philosopher, who composed the Buddhacarita (The Acts of the Buddha), the first Sanskrit biography of the Buddha, and the Saundarananda (The Handsome Nanda), a kavya that depicts the conversion of Nanda, the Buddha's half-brother, to Buddhism. He is also credited with writing two plays: Sariputraprakarana (The Play of Sariputra) and Samajaparikatha (The Discourse on the Assembly). He is acclaimed for his eloquence, wisdom and devotion. 
  - **Bhartrihari**: He is a grammarian, philosopher and poet, who wrote the Vakyapadiya (The Treatise on Sentences and Words), a seminal work on the philosophy of language
