# The Ramayana

The Ramayana is a Hindu epic that narrates the life and adventures of Rama, the prince of Kosala, and his wife Sita. It is one of the two major Sanskrit epics of ancient India, along with the Mahabharata. The Ramayana consists of seven books and 24,000 verses, and is attributed to the sage Valmiki.

Some of the main points of the Ramayana are:

- Rama is the eldest son of King Dasaratha of Kosala, and is the incarnation of the god Vishnu. He is virtuous, brave, and loyal, and is destined to be the king of Kosala.
- Sita is the daughter of King Janaka of Mithila, and is the incarnation of the goddess Lakshmi. She is beautiful, pure, and devoted, and is the ideal wife of Rama.
- Rama and Sita get married after Rama breaks the bow of Shiva, a feat that no one else could accomplish. They are very happy and loved by the people of Kosala.
- Rama's stepmother Kaikeyi, influenced by her maid Manthara, asks Dasaratha to grant her two boons: to banish Rama to the forest for 14 years, and to make her son Bharata the king instead. Dasaratha reluctantly agrees, as he had promised to fulfill her wishes long ago.
- Rama accepts his fate and leaves for the forest, accompanied by Sita and his brother Lakshmana. Bharata refuses to take the throne and tries to persuade Rama to return, but Rama insists on obeying his father's command. Bharata takes Rama's sandals and places them on the throne as a symbol of his authority.
- Rama, Sita, and Lakshmana wander in the forest, encountering sages, ascetics, and various creatures. They also face many dangers and challenges, such as the demoness Surpanakha, who tries to seduce Rama and attacks Sita, and the demon king Khara, who leads an army of 14,000 demons against Rama.
- The main antagonist of the Ramayana is Ravana, the king of Lanka, who has ten heads and twenty arms. He is a powerful and arrogant ruler, who has conquered many worlds and defied the gods. He is also a great devotee of Shiva, and has obtained many boons from him, making him invincible to most beings.
- Ravana hears of Sita's beauty and decides to abduct her. He sends his uncle Maricha to disguise himself as a golden deer and lure Rama and Lakshmana away from Sita. Sita, thinking that Rama is in danger, asks Lakshmana to go after him. Lakshmana reluctantly leaves, drawing a protective circle around Sita and warning her not to cross it. Ravana then appears as a mendicant and asks Sita for alms. Sita, out of compassion, steps out of the circle and is seized by Ravana, who flies away with her in his chariot.
- Rama and Lakshmana return and realize that Sita has been kidnapped. They search for her, aided by the vulture Jatayu, who tries to stop Ravana but is fatally wounded. They also meet the monkey king Sugriva, who has been exiled by his brother Vali. Rama helps Sugriva to regain his kingdom and his wife by killing Vali. In return, Sugriva promises to help Rama find Sita.
- Sugriva sends his monkey army in all directions to look for Sita. The most important of them is Hanuman, the son of the wind god Vayu, who is a loyal and courageous friend of Rama. Hanuman flies across the ocean and reaches Lanka, where he finds Sita in a grove, guarded by demonesses. He gives her Rama's ring as a token of his identity, and assures her that Rama will come to rescue her. He also sets fire to Lanka, causing havoc and angering Ravana.
