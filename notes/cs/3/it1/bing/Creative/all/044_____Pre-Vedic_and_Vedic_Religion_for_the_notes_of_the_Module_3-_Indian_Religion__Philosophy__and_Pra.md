# Pre-Vedic and Vedic Religion

- Pre-Vedic religion refers to the religious beliefs and practices of the people of the Indus Valley Civilization (IVC), which flourished from around 2500 BCE to 1500 BCE in the northwest of the Indian subcontinent.
- Vedic religion refers to the religious beliefs and practices of the Indo-Aryan peoples who entered India from the northwest around 1500 BCE and composed the four Vedas, the oldest and most authoritative scriptures of Hinduism.
- Some of the main features of Pre-Vedic and Vedic religion are:

## Pre-Vedic Religion

- The Pre-Vedic religion was based on the worship of natural forces and fertility symbols, such as the Mother Goddess, the bull, the pipal tree, and the swastika.
- The Pre-Vedic religion also involved the use of seals, amulets, terracotta figurines, and fire altars for ritual purposes.
- The Pre-Vedic religion did not have a clear concept of a supreme deity, but some scholars have suggested that there may have been a proto-Shiva or a proto-Vishnu among the IVC pantheon.
- The Pre-Vedic religion did not have a priestly class, but some evidence suggests that there may have been a class of ritual specialists or magicians who performed ceremonies and divination.
- The Pre-Vedic religion did not have a written language, but some scholars have proposed that the Indus script may have been a form of pictographic or logo-syllabic writing that encoded religious messages.
- The Pre-Vedic religion did not have a clear concept of an afterlife, but some evidence suggests that the IVC people practiced burial and cremation and believed in the existence of spirits and ghosts.

## Vedic Religion

- The Vedic religion was based on the worship of a pantheon of gods and goddesses, who represented various aspects of nature, such as Indra (thunder and rain), Agni (fire), Surya (sun), and Ushas (dawn).
- The Vedic religion also involved the performance of sacrifices (yajnas), which were elaborate rituals that involved the offering of animals, plants, and soma (a sacred drink) to the gods through the mediation of priests (brahmins).
- The Vedic religion had a clear concept of a supreme deity, who was variously identified as Prajapati (the creator), Vishvakarman (the architect), or Purusha (the cosmic man).
- The Vedic religion had a priestly class, who were the custodians of the Vedas and the ritual knowledge. They were divided into four categories: hotri (reciter), adhvaryu (performer), udgatri (singer), and brahman (supervisor).
- The Vedic religion had a written language, which was the Sanskrit language, derived from the Indo-European language family. The Vedas were composed in a form of Sanskrit known as Vedic Sanskrit, which was different from the classical Sanskrit of later times.
- The Vedic religion had a clear concept of an afterlife, which was based on the doctrine of karma (action) and samsara (rebirth). The Vedic people believed that the soul (atman) transmigrated from one body to another according to its deeds and that the ultimate goal was to attain liberation (moksha) from the cycle of birth and death.
