# Council of Ministers

- The Council of Ministers is the principal executive organ of the Government of India, which functions as the senior decision making body of the executive branch.
- It is chaired by the Prime Minister and consists of the heads of each of the executive government ministries.
- The President of India appoints the Council of Ministers on the advice of the Prime Minister.
- The Council of Ministers is collectively responsible to the Lok Sabha, the lower house of the Parliament of India.
- The Council of Ministers is made up of five categories of ministers, in descending order of rank:
  - Prime Minister: Leader of the executive of the Government of India.
  - Deputy Prime Minister (if any): Presides as Prime Minister in his absence or as the senior most cabinet minister.
  - Cabinet Ministers: Heads of the important ministries, such as Defence, Home, Finance, External Affairs, etc. They are the core decision makers of the government.
  - Ministers of State (with independent charge): Heads of the ministries that are not assigned to any cabinet minister, such as Civil Aviation, Statistics and Programme Implementation, etc. They have the authority to take independent decisions on their portfolios.
  - Ministers of State: Junior ministers who assist the cabinet ministers or the ministers of state (with independent charge) in their respective ministries. They do not have the authority to take independent decisions on their portfolios.
  - Deputy Ministers: Junior ministers who assist the ministers of state in their respective ministries. They do not have the authority to take independent decisions on their portfolios.
- The current Council of Ministers was formed on 7 July 2021, after a major reshuffle and expansion of the previous council.
- The current Council of Ministers has 77 members, including the Prime Minister, 30 cabinet ministers, 45 ministers of state, and one deputy minister.
