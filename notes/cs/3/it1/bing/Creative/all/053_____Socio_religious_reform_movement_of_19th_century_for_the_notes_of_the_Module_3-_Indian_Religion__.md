# Socio-religious reform movement of 19th century

- The socio-religious reform movement of 19th century was a wave of reforming activities that aimed to remove the social evils and religious superstitions from the Indian society and to revive the ancient glory of Hinduism.
- The main causes of the emergence of the reform movement were:
  - The spread of western education and liberal ideas that exposed the Indians to the rational and scientific outlook of the West and made them question the existing social and religious practices.
  - The contact with the Christian missionaries and the British administration that challenged the Hindu faith and culture and provoked a defensive and reformist response from the Hindus.
  - The influence of the Indian Renaissance that fostered a spirit of nationalism and cultural revival among the Indians and inspired them to reform their society and religion.
  - The social and economic changes that resulted from the British rule, such as the growth of urbanization, industrialization, commerce, transport and communication, that created new classes of educated and progressive Indians who demanded social and religious reforms.
- The main types of the reform movement were:
  - The Brahmo Samaj, founded by Raja Ram Mohan Roy in 1828, that advocated monotheism, abolition of idolatry, caste system, sati, child marriage and polygamy, and promotion of women's education, widow remarriage and social service.
  - The Arya Samaj, founded by Swami Dayanand Saraswati in 1875, that propagated the doctrine of "Back to the Vedas" and rejected the later accretions of Hinduism, such as idolatry, polytheism, rituals, priesthood, animal sacrifice, caste system and untouchability. It also encouraged the dissemination of western scientific knowledge and social reforms, such as women's education, widow remarriage and inter-caste marriage.
  - The Prarthana Samaj, founded by Atmaram Pandurang in 1867, that followed the teachings of Raja Ram Mohan Roy and advocated monotheism, abolition of caste system, child marriage and widowhood, and promotion of women's education, widow remarriage and social service. It also supported the reform of Hindu rituals and ceremonies and the establishment of educational and charitable institutions.
  - The Ramakrishna Mission, founded by Swami Vivekananda in 1897, that propagated the philosophy of Vedanta and the universal religion of humanity. It emphasized the harmony of religions, the service of mankind as the service of God, and the spiritual and moral upliftment of the masses. It also engaged in various social and educational activities, such as running schools, colleges, hospitals, orphanages and relief camps.
  - The Theosophical Society, founded by Madame Blavatsky and Colonel Olcott in 1875, that aimed to promote the study of comparative religion, philosophy and science, and to investigate the hidden mysteries of nature and human potential. It also sought to revive the ancient wisdom of Hinduism and Buddhism and to foster a sense of brotherhood among all people.
  - The Aligarh Movement, founded by Sir Syed Ahmed Khan in 1866, that aimed to reform the Muslim society and religion and to reconcile Islam with modern science and western education. It also advocated the loyalty of the Muslims to the British rule and the promotion of Hindu-Muslim unity. It established the Muhammadan Anglo-Oriental College, later known as the Aligarh Muslim University, as the center of its educational and cultural activities.
