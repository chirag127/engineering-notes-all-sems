# Six System Indian Philosophy

The six systems of Indian philosophy are Nyaya, Vaisheshika, Samkhya, Yoga, Purva Mimamsa and Uttara Mimamsa or Vedanta. These are also known as the shad-darshana, or the six views of reality. Each of these systems differs in one way or the other in terms of its concepts, phenomena, laws and dogmas. Each system has its own founder as well. Here is a brief overview of each system:

- **Nyaya**: The founder of Nyaya is Akshapada Gautama and it dated back to 2nd Century BCE. It is a school that deals with logic and epistemology. It aims to provide a method of reasoning to attain valid knowledge and liberation from suffering. It accepts four sources of knowledge: perception, inference, comparison and testimony. It also analyzes the categories of being, such as substance, quality, action, relation, etc. It defends the existence of a self, a God and a moral order.  

- **Vaisheshika**: The founder of Vaisheshika is Kanada and it dated back to 6th Century BCE. It is a school that deals with metaphysics and ontology. It proposes that reality is composed of six categories: substance, quality, action, generality, particularity and inherence. It also postulates that the ultimate constituents of matter are atoms, which combine to form molecules, and that the self and God are distinct substances.  

- **Samkhya**: The founder of Samkhya is Kapila and it dated back to 6th Century BCE. It is a school that deals with cosmology and psychology. It proposes a dualistic theory of reality, consisting of two principles: purusha (consciousness) and prakriti (matter). It explains the evolution of the universe and the diversity of beings from the interaction of these two principles. It also describes the process of liberation from the bondage of prakriti through the discrimination of purusha.  

- **Yoga**: The founder of Yoga is Patanjali and it dated back to 2nd Century BCE. It is a school that deals with ethics and spirituality. It is closely related to Samkhya, but differs in accepting the existence of a personal God, Ishvara, as the highest purusha. It prescribes an eightfold path of discipline and meditation to attain the union of the individual self with the supreme self. It also discusses the various powers and states of consciousness that result from the practice of yoga.  

- **Purva Mimamsa**: The founder of Purva Mimamsa is Jaimini and it dated back to 3rd Century BCE. It is a school that deals with hermeneutics and ritual. It focuses on the interpretation and application of the Vedas, the sacred scriptures of Hinduism. It argues that the performance of the Vedic rituals is the means to attain the highest good, which is heaven. It also analyzes the nature and validity of language, meaning and inference.  

- **Uttara Mimamsa or Vedanta**: The founder of Vedanta is Badarayana and it dated back to 2nd Century BCE. It is a school that deals with theology and philosophy. It is based on the Upanishads, the philosophical portions of the Vedas. It expounds the doctrine of Brahman, the absolute reality, and its identity or relation with the individual self, or atman. It also discusses the nature and means of liberation, or moksha, from the cycle of rebirth, or samsara. There are various sub-schools of Vedanta, such as Advaita, Vishishtadvaita, Dvaita, etc., that differ in their interpretation of the Upanishadic teachings.
