# Dance

Dance is an ancient and celebrated cultural tradition in India. It is a form of expression, communication, and devotion. Dance can be classified into two main categories: classical and folk.

## Classical Dances

Classical dances are those that have a codified set of rules, techniques, and aesthetics. They are usually performed on stage, in temples, or in courts. They are based on the ancient texts of Natya Shastra and Abhinaya Darpana, which describe the theory and practice of dance, drama, and music. There are six recognized classical dances in India:

- **Bharatanatyam**: Originated in Tamil Nadu, it is one of the oldest and most popular dance forms in India. It is characterized by graceful movements, intricate footwork, expressive gestures, and elaborate costumes and jewelry. It depicts the stories and themes from Hindu mythology, especially of Lord Shiva and his consort Parvati.
- **Kathak**: Originated in North India, it is a dance form that combines storytelling, music, and rhythmic patterns. It is influenced by the Mughal and Persian cultures, and has three distinct styles: Lucknow, Jaipur, and Banaras. It is known for its fast spins, complex footwork, and subtle facial expressions. It often portrays the love stories of Radha and Krishna, or the epics of Ramayana and Mahabharata.
- **Kathakali**: Originated in Kerala, it is a dance-drama form that combines dance, music, acting, and makeup. It is distinguished by its elaborate costumes, masks, and facial paint, which represent different characters and emotions. It depicts the stories from the epics, Puranas, and local legends, with emphasis on the conflicts between good and evil.
- **Manipuri**: Originated in Manipur, it is a dance form that is influenced by the Vaishnava tradition of Hinduism. It is characterized by soft and graceful movements, fluidity, and harmony. It portrays the love and devotion of Radha and Krishna, as well as the Ras Lila, the cosmic dance of the divine couple.
- **Kuchipudi**: Originated in Andhra Pradesh, it is a dance form that is similar to Bharatanatyam, but with more emphasis on drama and narration. It is performed by both male and female dancers, who play different roles and switch between them. It depicts the stories and characters from Hindu mythology, such as Shiva, Vishnu, Rama, and Sita.
- **Odissi**: Originated in Odisha, it is a dance form that is inspired by the sculptures and temples of the region. It is characterized by its curved and graceful postures, intricate footwork, and expressive gestures. It depicts the themes of love, devotion, and beauty, especially of Lord Jagannath, the presiding deity of Odisha.

## Folk Dances

Folk dances are those that are performed by the common people, usually in groups, for social, religious, or festive occasions. They are influenced by the local culture, geography, and history of the region. They are spontaneous, lively, and colorful. They reflect the diversity and richness of India's cultural heritage. Some examples of folk dances are:

- **Bhangra**: Originated in Punjab, it is a dance form that is performed during the harvest season, especially during the festival of Baisakhi. It is a vigorous and energetic dance, accompanied by the beats of the dhol, a drum. It involves jumping, kicking, and swinging of the arms and legs.
- **Garba**: Originated in Gujarat, it is a dance form that is performed during the nine nights of Navratri, a festival dedicated to the goddess Durga. It is a circular dance, in which the dancers move around a lamp or an idol, clapping their hands or using sticks. It symbolizes the cycle of life and death, and the power of the goddess.
- **Dandiya**: Originated in Gujarat, it is a dance form that is also performed during Navratri, but with a different style and rhythm. It involves the use of colorful sticks, called dandiya, which the dancers strike with each other in a coordinated manner. It represents the mock fight between the goddess Durga and the demon Mahishasura.
