# Modern religious practices in India

- India is a secular country with a diverse and pluralistic religious landscape. According to the 2011 census, 79.8% of the population of India practices Hinduism, 14.2% adheres to Islam, 2.3% adheres to Christianity, 1.7% adheres to Sikhism, 0.7% adheres to Buddhism and 0.4% adheres to Jainism. Zoroastrianism, Sanamahism and Judaism also have an ancient history in India, and each has several thousands of Indian adherents.
- Religion has historically influenced Indian society on a political, cultural and economic level. There is a sense of pride associated with the country’s rich religious history as the traditions of Hinduism, Buddhism, Sikhism and Jainism all emerged out of India.
- India’s religious groups share several religious practices and beliefs. After living side by side for generations, India’s minority groups often engage in practices or hold beliefs that are more closely associated with Hindu traditions than with their own. For example, most Indians believe in karma, the concept of reincarnation and the importance of respecting elders.
- Some of the common religious practices in India include:

  - Visiting houses of worship: The survey conducted by Pew Research Center in 2019-2020 found that 77% of Indians say they visit a temple, mosque, church, gurdwara or other place of worship at least once a month, and 40% say they do so at least once a week. The frequency of visiting houses of worship varies by region, with southern Indians being the least likely to say religion is very important in their life.
  - Giving to charitable causes: The same survey found that 77% of Indians say they gave money, food or other goods to the poor or needy in the past year, and 64% say they gave money or goods to a religious organization. Giving to charity is considered a religious duty in many faiths, such as zakat in Islam, daan in Hinduism, and dasvandh in Sikhism.
  - Celebrating festivals: India is known for its vibrant and colorful festivals that celebrate various religious and cultural occasions. Some of the major festivals in India include Diwali, the festival of lights; Holi, the festival of colors; Eid al-Fitr, the festival of breaking the fast; Christmas, the festival of the birth of Jesus Christ; Guru Nanak Jayanti, the birthday of the founder of Sikhism; and Buddha Purnima, the birthday of the founder of Buddhism. These festivals often involve rituals, prayers, offerings, decorations, food, music, dance, and social gatherings.
  - Performing rites of passage: Rites of passage are ceremonies that mark important transitions in a person's life, such as birth, puberty, marriage, and death. Different religions have different rites of passage, but some of them are common across faiths, such as naming ceremonies, circumcision, weddings, and funerals. These rites of passage often involve family, community, and religious leaders, and are seen as a way of expressing one's identity, faith, and values.
