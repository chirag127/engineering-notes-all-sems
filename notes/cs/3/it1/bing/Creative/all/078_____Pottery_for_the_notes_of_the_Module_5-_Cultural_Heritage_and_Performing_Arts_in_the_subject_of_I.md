# Pottery

Pottery is one of the oldest and most widespread forms of art in India. It is a cultural heritage that reflects the diversity and creativity of the Indian people. Pottery is also a useful source of information for archaeologists and historians to study the past civilizations and cultures of India.

Some of the main points to note about pottery in India are:

- Pottery in India has an ancient history and is one of the most tangible and iconic elements of Indian art  .
- Evidence of pottery has been found in the early settlements of Lahuradewa and later the Indus Valley Civilisation  . The Indus Valley pottery was mainly wheel-made, red or black in colour, and decorated with geometric patterns, animal motifs, and plant designs .
- Pottery in India has evolved over time and has been influenced by various cultural, religious, and artistic traditions. Some of the major types of pottery in India are:

  - Terracotta pottery: This is the most common and basic form of pottery, made from clay and baked in a kiln or sun-dried. Terracotta pottery is usually unglazed and has a reddish-brown colour. Terracotta pottery is used for making pots, bowls, figurines, toys, lamps, and other items. Terracotta pottery is found in almost every region of India and has a rich variety of styles and forms  .
  - Glazed pottery: This is a form of pottery that has a shiny coating of glass-like material, usually applied after firing. Glazed pottery is more durable and resistant to water and stains than terracotta pottery. Glazed pottery is often decorated with colours, patterns, and designs. Glazed pottery is mainly found in northern and western India, especially in Rajasthan, Gujarat, Delhi, and Uttar Pradesh. Some of the famous forms of glazed pottery are:

    - Jaipur blue pottery: This is a form of glazed pottery that originated from the Turko-Persian style and is known for its distinctive blue colour and floral motifs. Jaipur blue pottery is made from quartz and not clay, and is fired at a low temperature. Jaipur blue pottery is used for making vases, plates, tiles, jars, and other items .
    - Khurja pottery: This is a form of glazed pottery that originated from Khurja, a town in Uttar Pradesh. Khurja pottery is known for its bright colours, such as yellow, green, and brown, and its intricate designs, such as birds, flowers, and geometric shapes. Khurja pottery is used for making cups, saucers, bowls, jugs, and other items .
    - Nizamabad pottery: This is a form of glazed pottery that originated from Nizamabad, a town in Uttar Pradesh. Nizamabad pottery is known for its black colour and silver patterns, created by applying a solution of clay and vegetable matter and then polishing it with a metal piece. Nizamabad pottery is used for making pots, lamps, vases, and other items .

  - Painted pottery: This is a form of pottery that is painted with colours and designs, usually before firing. Painted pottery is often used for artistic and decorative purposes, rather than functional ones. Painted pottery is found in various regions of India, such as Madhya Pradesh, Bihar, West Bengal, and Odisha. Some of the famous forms of painted pottery are:

    - Gond pottery: This is a form of painted pottery that is made by the Gond tribe of Madhya Pradesh. Gond pottery is known for its vibrant colours, such as red, yellow, green, and white, and its folk motifs, such as animals, birds, trees, and humans. Gond pottery is used for making pots, bowls, plates, and other items .
    - Madhubani pottery: This is a form of painted pottery that is made by the women of Madhubani, a district in Bihar. Madhubani pottery is known for its intricate and colourful designs, inspired by the Madhubani painting style. Madhubani pottery is used for making pots, jars, mugs, and other items .
