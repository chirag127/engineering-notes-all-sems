# Engineering and Architecture in Ancient India

Engineering and architecture are the arts of designing and constructing buildings, structures, and systems that serve various purposes. In ancient India, engineering and architecture were known as Sthapatya-Kala, which means the art of constructing something. Ancient Indians developed various principles and techniques of engineering and architecture based on their experience, observation of natural phenomena, and consideration of social and cultural aspects of India. Some of the main features and achievements of engineering and architecture in ancient India are:

- **Harappan civilization**: The Harappan civilization, which flourished from 2500 BCE to 1900 BCE, is one of the earliest urban civilizations in the world. The Harappans built well-planned cities with sophisticated drainage systems, brick houses, granaries, public baths, and fortifications. They also constructed monumental structures such as the Great Bath at Mohenjo-daro, the Dockyard at Lothal, and the Citadel at Dholavira. The Harappans used standard units of measurement, geometrical shapes, and advanced tools for their engineering and architectural works.
- **Vedic period**: The Vedic period, which lasted from 1500 BCE to 600 BCE, is the period of the oldest scriptures of Hinduism, the Vedas. The Vedic texts contain references to various types of buildings, such as houses, palaces, temples, altars, and fireplaces. The Vedic people also constructed sacrificial arenas, called yajna-shalas, where they performed elaborate rituals. The Vedic architecture was mainly based on the principles of vastu-shastra, which is the science of orientation and layout of buildings according to the cosmic forces and natural elements.
- **Mauryan empire**: The Mauryan empire, which ruled from 322 BCE to 185 BCE, was one of the largest and most powerful empires in ancient India. The Mauryans built impressive structures, such as the Pillars of Ashoka, the Sanchi Stupa, the Barabar Caves, and the Pataliputra Palace. The Mauryans also developed irrigation systems, roads, bridges, and fortifications. The Mauryan architecture was influenced by the Persian and Greek styles, as well as the indigenous styles of the regions they conquered.
- **Buddhist and Jain architecture**: Buddhism and Jainism are two ancient religions that originated in India and spread to other parts of Asia. The followers of these religions built various types of structures, such as stupas, chaityas, viharas, and temples, to worship and commemorate their teachers and saints. The Buddhist and Jain architecture was characterized by the use of stone, brick, wood, and plaster, as well as the incorporation of sculptures, paintings, and carvings. Some of the famous examples of Buddhist and Jain architecture are the Ajanta and Ellora Caves, the Karla and Bhaja Caves, the Mahabodhi Temple, and the Dilwara Temples.
- **Gupta period**: The Gupta period, which spanned from 320 CE to 550 CE, is considered the golden age of ancient India. The Guptas patronized art, literature, science, and culture, and also built magnificent structures, such as the Dashavatara Temple, the Udayagiri Caves, the Vishnu Temple at Deogarh, and the Iron Pillar of Delhi. The Gupta architecture was marked by the refinement and elegance of the forms, the use of symbolism and iconography, and the development of the Nagara style of temple architecture, which featured a curved tower, called shikhara, over the sanctum.
- **South Indian temple architecture**: South India is known for its rich and diverse temple architecture, which developed from the 6th century CE onwards. The South Indian temples were built by various dynasties, such as the Pallavas, the Cholas, the Chalukyas, the Rashtrakutas, the Hoysalas, and the Vijayanagara. The South Indian temples were also adorned with elaborate sculptures, paintings, and carvings, depicting various gods, goddesses, and mythological scenes.
