# Buddhist and Jain Literature in Pali, Prakrit and Sanskrit

- Pali and Prakrit are two languages that were used to write the religious literature of Buddhism and Jainism, respectively. Pali is an old form of Prakrit that combines several dialects, while Prakrit is a term that refers to any language other than the standard one, Sanskrit.
- The earliest Buddhist works were written in Pali, which was spoken in Magadha and South Bihar. These works include the Tripitaka or the Three Baskets, which consist of the Sutta Pitaka (discourses of the Buddha), the Vinaya Pitaka (rules of monastic discipline), and the Abhidhamma Pitaka (philosophical and psychological analysis of the doctrine).
- The Pali literature also contains commentaries, chronicles, biographies, and other genres. Some of the famous Pali works are the Dhammapada (a collection of verses on ethics and wisdom), the Jataka (stories of the Buddha's previous lives), the Milindapanha (a dialogue between King Milinda and a Buddhist monk), and the Mahavamsa (a historical chronicle of Sri Lanka).
- The Buddhist literature in Sanskrit and Prakrit includes the Mahayana sutras, which expound the doctrines of the Great Vehicle, such as emptiness, compassion, and bodhisattva ideal. Some of the well-known Mahayana sutras are the Prajnaparamita Sutras (on the perfection of wisdom), the Lotus Sutra (on the universal salvation of all beings), the Avatamsaka Sutra (on the interpenetration of all phenomena), and the Lankavatara Sutra (on the nature of mind and reality).
- The Jain literature in Prakrit and Sanskrit comprises the canonical texts or the Agamas, which are divided into two main groups: the Angas (limbs) and the Angabahyas (external to the limbs). The Angas consist of 12 texts that deal with the teachings of Mahavira, the 24th and last Tirthankara (ford-maker) of Jainism, and his immediate disciples. The Angabahyas consist of 34 texts that deal with various topics such as cosmology, ethics, logic, grammar, and biography.
- The Jain literature also includes non-canonical texts, such as commentaries, treatises, stories, and poems. Some of the prominent Jain authors are Haribhadra (8th century CE), who wrote on logic, yoga, and ethics; Hemacandra (12th century CE), who wrote on grammar, history, and philosophy; and Jinasena (9th century CE), who wrote the Adipurana, a biography of the first two Tirthankaras.
