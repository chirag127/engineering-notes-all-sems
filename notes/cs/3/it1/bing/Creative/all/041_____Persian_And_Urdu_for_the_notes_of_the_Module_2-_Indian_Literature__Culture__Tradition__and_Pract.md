# Persian and Urdu

- Persian and Urdu are two languages that have influenced and enriched the literary and cultural heritage of India, especially during the medieval period.
- Persian was the language of administration, literature, and culture in the Mughal Empire, which ruled most of India from the 16th to the 18th century. Persian was also the language of the Delhi Sultanate, the predecessor of the Mughals, and the language of many Sufi saints and poets who spread Islam and mysticism in India .
- Urdu emerged as a common language of communication for people speaking different dialects in the Mughal Empire, especially in the northern and central regions. Urdu was a mixture of Persian, Arabic, Turkish, and local Indian languages, such as Hindi, Punjabi, and Sindhi. Urdu was also influenced by the Sanskrit and Prakrit languages of ancient India .
- Persian and Urdu literature developed in parallel and often interacted with each other. Many Persian poets wrote in both languages, such as Amir Khusrau, Mir Taqi Mir, and Ghalib. Many Urdu poets were inspired by the Persian poetic traditions, such as the ghazal, the masnavi, and the qasida. Persian and Urdu literature also reflected the social, political, and religious aspects of the times, such as the Mughal decline, the British colonialism, the Hindu-Muslim relations, and the Sufi and Bhakti movements  .
- Persian and Urdu are still spoken and written in India today, although their status and influence have declined since the independence and partition of India in 1947. Persian is mainly used by the Parsi community and some scholars, while Urdu is mainly used by the Muslim community and some regions, such as Kashmir, Hyderabad, and Delhi. Persian and Urdu have also contributed to the development of other Indian languages, such as Hindi, Bengali, Gujarati, and Marathi.
