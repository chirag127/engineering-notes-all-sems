# Council of Ministers

- The Council of Ministers is the principal executive organ of the Government of India, which functions as the senior decision making body of the executive branch.
- It is chaired by the Prime Minister and consists of the heads of each of the executive government ministries.
- The President of India appoints the Prime Minister and the other ministers on the advice of the Prime Minister.
- The Council of Ministers is collectively responsible to the Lok Sabha, the lower house of the Parliament of India.
- The Council of Ministers exercises executive authority in India and implements the policies and laws enacted by the Parliament.

## Types of Ministers

- There are five categories of the Council of Ministers as given below, in descending order of rank:

  - Prime Minister: Leader of the executive of the Government of India.
  - Deputy Prime Minister (if any): Presides as Prime Minister in his absence or as the senior most cabinet minister.
  - Cabinet Ministers: Heads of the important ministries and members of the Cabinet Committee, which decides the policies and affairs of the government.
  - Ministers of State (with independent charge): Heads of the ministries that are not attached to any cabinet minister and have independent charge of their portfolios.
  - Ministers of State: Junior ministers who assist the cabinet ministers or the ministers of state (with independent charge) in their respective ministries.

## Functions and Powers of the Council of Ministers

- The Council of Ministers performs the following functions and powers:

  - It formulates the policies and programmes of the government and presents them before the Parliament for approval.
  - It implements the laws and resolutions passed by the Parliament and ensures their enforcement.
  - It advises the President on various matters such as appointment of judges, governors, ambassadors, etc.
  - It coordinates the activities of various ministries and departments and resolves any inter-ministerial disputes.
  - It deals with the external affairs of the country and maintains relations with foreign countries and international organizations.
  - It handles the defence and security matters of the country and oversees the armed forces and intelligence agencies.
  - It manages the financial resources of the country and prepares the annual budget and economic plans.
  - It responds to the public grievances and demands and ensures the welfare of the people.
  - It handles the emergencies and crises that may arise in the country and takes necessary measures to restore normalcy.
  - It exercises the ordinance-making power of the President when the Parliament is not in session.
