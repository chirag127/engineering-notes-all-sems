### Stages of State Formation in Ancient India

- State formation in ancient India is linked with the growth and spread of agriculture, trade, industry and urbanisation along the river valleys.
- The first states (Janapadas) emerged in the Indo-Gangetic plains around the 6th century BCE, as a result of agricultural surplus, population growth, social stratification and political centralisation  .
- The Janapadas were either monarchies or republics, with varying degrees of autonomy and power. Some of the prominent Janapadas were Kuru, Panchala, Kosala, Magadha, Vatsa, Avanti, etc .
- The Janapadas were often engaged in wars and alliances with each other, leading to the rise and fall of different dynasties and kingdoms. The most powerful and influential Janapada was Magadha, which expanded its territory and influence under the Nanda, Maurya, Sunga, Kanva and Gupta dynasties from the 4th century BCE to the 6th century CE .
- The Mauryan Empire (321-185 BCE) was the first pan-Indian empire, which unified most of the Indian subcontinent under a centralised administration and a common culture. The Mauryan Empire was founded by Chandragupta Maurya, who was assisted by his mentor and minister Kautilya (also known as Chanakya), the author of the Arthashastra, a treatise on statecraft and governance .
- The Mauryan Empire reached its zenith under Ashoka (268-232 BCE), who is regarded as one of the greatest rulers in world history. Ashoka embraced Buddhism after the bloody Kalinga war, and propagated the principles of dhamma (righteousness) and ahimsa (non-violence) throughout his empire and beyond. He also erected pillars, edicts and stupas to spread his message and commemorate his achievements .
- The Mauryan Empire declined after Ashoka's death, and was replaced by several regional kingdoms, such as the Sunga, Kanva, Satavahana, Kushan, Shaka, etc. These kingdoms contributed to the cultural, religious and artistic diversity and development of India. They also facilitated trade and contact with other regions, such as Central Asia, West Asia, Southeast Asia, etc .
- The Gupta Empire (320-550 CE) is considered as the golden age of ancient India, as it witnessed a remarkable revival of political unity, economic prosperity, social harmony and cultural creativity. The Gupta Empire was founded by Chandragupta I, who consolidated his power by marrying the Licchavi princess Kumaradevi. His successors, Samudragupta, Chandragupta II, Kumaragupta and Skandagupta, expanded and defended the empire against various invaders and rebels .
- The Gupta Empire is known for its achievements in the fields of literature, art, architecture, science, mathematics, astronomy, medicine, etc. Some of the eminent personalities of this period were Kalidasa, Vishnusharma, Aryabhata, Varahamihira, Charaka, Sushruta, etc. The Gupta Empire also patronised Buddhism, Jainism and Hinduism, and promoted the worship of Vishnu, Shiva and Shakti .
- The Gupta Empire collapsed due to the invasions of the Huns, who were nomadic tribes from Central Asia. The Huns were eventually defeated by the Indian kings, such as Yasodharman of Malwa and Narasimhagupta of Gupta dynasty. However, the political fragmentation and instability continued, and India entered into the medieval period .
