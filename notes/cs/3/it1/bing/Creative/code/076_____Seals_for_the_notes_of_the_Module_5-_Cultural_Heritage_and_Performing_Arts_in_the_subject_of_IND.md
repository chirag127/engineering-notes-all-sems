### Seals

- Seals are small objects that have an engraved or carved design on one side and are used to make impressions on another surface, such as clay, wax, or paper.
- Seals have been used in various cultures and civilizations for different purposes, such as trade, administration, identification, authentication, communication, and ritual.
- In India, seals have a long history and reflect the diversity and richness of its cultural heritage and performing arts.
- Some of the types and examples of seals in Indian culture are:

  - **Indus Valley seals**: These are the oldest known seals in India, dating back to the third millennium BCE. They are mostly made of steatite, a soft stone that can be easily carved. They are rectangular, circular, or cylindrical in shape and have a perforated boss on the back for holding. They depict various animals, such as buffaloes, rhinoceroses, elephants, and unicorns, as well as trees and composite creatures. They also have a script that has not been deciphered yet. These seals are believed to have been used for trade and commerce, as they have been found in various sites along the Indus River and beyond .
  - **Mauryan seals**: These are the seals that were used during the Mauryan Empire, which ruled most of India from the fourth to the second century BCE. They are made of terracotta, copper, silver, or ivory and have a circular or oval shape. They have the image of an animal, such as a bull, a lion, or an elephant, or a symbol, such as a wheel, a lotus, or a swastika, on one side and an inscription in Brahmi script on the other. These seals are thought to have been used for administrative and official purposes, as they bear the names of rulers, officials, or departments.
  - **Buddhist seals**: These are the seals that were used by the followers of Buddhism, one of the major religions in India. They are made of metal, stone, clay, or wood and have a square, rectangular, or round shape. They have the image of a Buddha, a Bodhisattva, a stupa, a lotus, or a dharmachakra, or a mantra, such as "Om mani padme hum", on one side and an inscription in various scripts, such as Brahmi, Kharosthi, Gupta, or Tibetan, on the other. These seals are believed to have been used for religious and devotional purposes, such as sealing sacred texts, offerings, or relics, or as amulets or talismans.
