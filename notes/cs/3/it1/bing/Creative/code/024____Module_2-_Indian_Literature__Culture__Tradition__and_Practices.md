## Module 2- Indian Literature, Culture, Tradition, and Practices

- Indian literature is the literature produced in the Indian subcontinent and its diaspora. It includes works in many languages, such as Sanskrit, Hindi, Urdu, Bengali, Tamil, Telugu, Malayalam, Kannada, Marathi, Gujarati, Punjabi, and English. Indian literature reflects the rich and diverse cultural heritage of India, as well as its history, philosophy, spirituality, and social issues.
- Indian culture is the collective expression of the beliefs, values, customs, arts, crafts, music, dance, architecture, cuisine, and festivals of the people of India. It is influenced by various factors, such as geography, climate, religion, ethnicity, foreign invasions, and colonialism. Indian culture is known for its diversity, unity, tolerance, and resilience.
- Indian tradition is the set of practices and rituals that are followed by the people of India, either as part of their religious duties or as part of their social norms. Some of the common traditions are greeting elders with respect, wearing ethnic clothes on festivals, celebrating various occasions with family and friends, performing rites of passage, and respecting the environment and animals.
- Indian practices are the ways of living and working that are adopted by the people of India, either as part of their culture or as part of their personal choices. Some of the common practices are yoga, meditation, ayurveda, vegetarianism, astrology, and handicrafts. These practices are aimed at enhancing the physical, mental, and spiritual well-being of the individuals and the society.
