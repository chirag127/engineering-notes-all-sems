### Dance

Dance is an ancient and celebrated cultural tradition in India. It is a form of expression, communication, devotion, and entertainment. Dance in India comprises numerous styles of dances, generally classified as classical or folk. Classical dances are based on ancient texts and codified rules, while folk dances are more spontaneous and regional. There are six forms of classical dances recognised by India on a national level. They are:

- **Bharatnatyam**: Originated in Tamil Nadu, it is one of the oldest and most popular forms of classical dance. It is characterized by graceful movements, intricate footwork, expressive gestures, and elaborate costumes and jewellery. It is performed to Carnatic music and depicts themes from Hindu mythology and literature.  
- **Kathak**: Originated in northern India, it is a dance form that narrates stories through rhythmic footwork, swift spins, and expressive facial expressions. It is performed to Hindustani music and incorporates elements of Persian and Mughal culture. It has three major schools or gharanas: Jaipur, Lucknow, and Banaras.  
- **Kathakali**: Originated in Kerala, it is a dance-drama form that combines dance, music, theatre, and costume. It is known for its elaborate make-up, masks, and costumes that represent various characters from Hindu epics and Puranas. It is performed to a percussion-based music and uses a complex language of gestures and facial expressions.  
- **Manipuri**: Originated in Manipur, it is a dance form that is influenced by the Vaishnavite tradition and the folk dances of the region. It is characterized by graceful and fluid movements, delicate footwork, and subtle expressions. It is performed to devotional songs and depicts themes of love and devotion, especially between Radha and Krishna.  
- **Kuchipudi**: Originated in Andhra Pradesh, it is a dance form that combines dance, music, and drama. It is similar to Bharatnatyam in terms of movements and gestures, but differs in the use of speech and acting. It is performed to Carnatic music and depicts stories from Hindu scriptures and literature.  
- **Odissi**: Originated in Odisha, it is a dance form that is inspired by the sculptures of the temples and the devadasi tradition. It is characterized by curved postures, intricate footwork, and expressive gestures. It is performed to Odissi music and depicts themes of love and devotion, especially between Shiva and Parvati.  

The folk dances of India are much more than mere body movements, they reflect the diversity and richness of the Indian culture and society. They are performed on various occasions, such as festivals, weddings, harvests, and celebrations. Some of the popular folk dances of India are:

- **Bhangra**: Originated in Punjab, it is a lively and energetic dance form that is performed by men and women to the beats of the dhol, a drum. It is associated with the harvest festival of Baisakhi and expresses joy and happiness. 
- **Garba**: Originated in Gujarat, it is a circular dance form that is performed by women in colorful costumes and jewellery. It is performed during the nine nights of Navratri, a festival dedicated to the goddess Durga. It involves clapping and moving in sync with the music. 
- **Dandiya**: Originated in Gujarat, it is a dance form that is performed by men and women in pairs using sticks called dandiya. It is also performed during Navratri and involves striking the sticks in rhythm with the music. It is a fast-paced and energetic dance form. 
- **Lavani**: Originated in Maharashtra, it is a dance form that is performed by women in traditional sarees and jewellery. It is a combination of dance and song that deals with various themes, such as love, romance, social issues, and politics. It is performed to the accompaniment of the dholki, a drum, and the tabla, a percussion instrument. 
- **Ghoomar**: Originated in Rajasthan, it is a dance form that is performed by women in swirling skirts and veils. It is performed during festivals and weddings and involves spinning and moving gracefully.
