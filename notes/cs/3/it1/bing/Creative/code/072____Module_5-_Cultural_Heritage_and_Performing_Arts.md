## Module 5- Cultural Heritage and Performing Arts

- Cultural heritage is the legacy of physical and intangible attributes of a group or society that are inherited from past generations, maintained in the present and bestowed for the benefit of future generations.
- Cultural heritage includes tangible culture (such as buildings, monuments, landscapes, books, works of art, and artefacts), intangible culture (such as folklore, traditions, language, and knowledge), and natural heritage (such as culturally significant landscapes, and biodiversity).
- Performing arts are forms of art that use the body, voice, or objects to convey artistic expression, such as music, dance, theatre, opera, mime, puppetry, circus arts, and performance art.
- Performing arts are often performed in front of an audience, but may also be performed for a specific purpose, such as ritual, education, or therapy.
- Performing arts are part of the intangible cultural heritage of many cultures and play a vital role in preserving and transmitting cultural values, identity, and diversity.
- Performing arts can also be a source of creativity, innovation, and social cohesion, as well as a means of promoting intercultural dialogue and understanding.
- Some examples of performing arts that are recognized by UNESCO as intangible cultural heritage are:

  - Urtiin Duu, traditional folk long song from Mongolia, which is characterized by an abundance of ornamentation, a wide vocal range, and a free compositional form.
  - Wayang puppet theatre from Indonesia, which is a form of storytelling that uses flat leather puppets, accompanied by a gamelan orchestra, to enact scenes from Hindu epics or local legends.
  - Flamenco from Spain, which is a musical and dance expression that combines singing, guitar playing, hand clapping, finger snapping, and dancing, influenced by various cultures such as Gypsy, Arab, Jewish, and Andalusian.
  - Kabuki from Japan, which is a classical theatre form that combines drama, music, dance, and elaborate costumes and make-up, to portray historical events, moral conflicts, and love stories.
  - Tango from Argentina and Uruguay, which is a social dance and musical genre that originated in the late 19th century among the lower classes of Buenos Aires and Montevideo, and reflects the influences of African, European, and indigenous cultures.
