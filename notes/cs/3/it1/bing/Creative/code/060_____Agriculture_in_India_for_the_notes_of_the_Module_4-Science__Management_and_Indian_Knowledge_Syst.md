### Agriculture in India

Agriculture is one of the most important sectors of the Indian economy, contributing about 16% of the GDP and employing about 58% of the population. India has a diverse agro-climatic zone, ranging from tropical to temperate, and produces a variety of crops, such as cereals, pulses, oilseeds, fruits, vegetables, spices, sugarcane, cotton, jute, tea, coffee, and rubber. India is also the world's largest producer and consumer of milk, and has a large livestock population, including cattle, buffaloes, goats, sheep, poultry, and fish. India is also rich in biodiversity, with over 45,000 plant and 91,000 animal species recorded.

Some of the major challenges faced by the Indian agriculture sector are:

- Low productivity and yield: Despite having the largest cultivated area in the world, India's crop productivity is lower than the global average, due to factors such as poor soil health, inadequate irrigation, lack of mechanization, pest and disease infestation, climate change, and post-harvest losses .
- Fragmented landholding: The average size of landholding in India is 1.08 hectares, and about 86% of the farmers are small and marginal, with less than 2 hectares of land. This limits their access to credit, technology, inputs, markets, and extension services, and reduces their bargaining power and profitability .
- Market inefficiencies: The Indian agricultural market is characterized by a long and complex supply chain, involving multiple intermediaries, high transaction costs, information asymmetry, and lack of transparency. The farmers often receive a low share of the consumer price, and face price volatility and uncertainty .
- Policy and institutional constraints: The Indian agricultural sector is governed by a plethora of laws, regulations, and schemes, which are often outdated, inconsistent, and overlapping. The sector also suffers from inadequate public investment, poor infrastructure, weak research and extension, and low institutional capacity .

Some of the major initiatives and reforms undertaken by the Indian government to address these challenges are:

- Pradhan Mantri Fasal Bima Yojana (PMFBY): A crop insurance scheme launched in 2016, which provides comprehensive risk coverage to farmers from pre-sowing to post-harvest stages, at a low premium rate of 1.5% to 5% .
- Pradhan Mantri Krishi Sinchayee Yojana (PMKSY): A flagship scheme launched in 2015, which aims to enhance water use efficiency and irrigation coverage, through convergence of various sub-schemes, such as Accelerated Irrigation Benefit Programme (AIBP), Har Khet Ko Pani (HKKP), Per Drop More Crop (PDMC), and Watershed Development (WD) .
- Pradhan Mantri Kisan Samman Nidhi (PM-KISAN): A direct income support scheme launched in 2019, which provides Rs. 6,000 per year to all eligible farmer families, in three equal installments of Rs. 2,000 each .
- Pradhan Mantri Kisan Maan Dhan Yojana (PM-KMY): A pension scheme launched in 2019, which provides a monthly pension of Rs. 3,000 to all eligible small and marginal farmers, after attaining the age of 60 years .
- Agriculture Infrastructure Fund (AIF): A fund of Rs. 1 lakh crore launched in 2020, which provides loans to farmers, FPOs, cooperatives, and agri-entrepreneurs, for creating post-harvest infrastructure, such as cold storage, warehouses, grading and sorting units, processing units, etc. .
- Farm Acts 2020: Three acts passed by the Parliament in 2020, which aim to liberalize the agricultural market, by allowing farmers to sell their produce outside the regulated mandis, enter into contract farming agreements, and access electronic trading platforms .
