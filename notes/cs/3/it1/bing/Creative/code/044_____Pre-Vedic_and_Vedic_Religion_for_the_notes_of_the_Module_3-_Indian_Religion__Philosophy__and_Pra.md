### Pre-Vedic and Vedic Religion

- Pre-Vedic religion refers to the religious beliefs and practices of the people who lived in the Indus Valley Civilization (IVC) from around 2500 BCE to 1500 BCE.
- Vedic religion refers to the religious beliefs and practices of the Indo-Aryan peoples who entered northwestern India from Persia around 1500 BCE and composed the four Vedas, the oldest sacred texts of Hinduism .

#### Pre-Vedic Religion

- The IVC was a highly urbanized and sophisticated civilization that had a complex social and economic organization, writing system, art and architecture, and trade networks.
- The IVC people revered both the male and female aspects of nature and fertility, as evidenced by the numerous figurines, seals, and terracotta objects depicting animals, plants, and human forms.
- The IVC people also worshipped a horned deity, possibly a proto-Shiva, who was associated with animals, water, and the underworld.
- The IVC people may have practiced some form of fire worship, as fire altars have been found in some sites.
- The IVC people may have believed in an afterlife, as they buried their dead with pottery, ornaments, and other personal belongings.
- The IVC religion was likely polytheistic, animistic, and shamanistic, with no clear evidence of a priestly class or a centralized authority.

#### Vedic Religion

- The Indo-Aryan peoples who migrated to India brought with them their own religious beliefs and practices, which were influenced by the Indo-Iranian and Indo-European traditions.
- The Indo-Aryan peoples composed the four Vedas, namely the Rigveda, the Yajurveda, the Samaveda, and the Atharvaveda, which contain hymns, rituals, and philosophical speculations on various aspects of life and the cosmos.
- The Indo-Aryan peoples worshipped many gods, such as Indra, the king of gods and the lord of thunder; Varuna, the guardian of the cosmic order; Agni, the fire god and the mediator between humans and gods; Surya, the sun god; and Rudra, the storm god and the precursor of Shiva .
- The Indo-Aryan peoples also revered some goddesses, such as Ushas, the dawn goddess; Aditi, the mother of gods; and Saraswati, the goddess of rivers and learning.
- The Indo-Aryan peoples performed elaborate rituals, such as the yajna, the fire sacrifice, in which they offered various substances, such as ghee, grains, and animals, to the gods in exchange for their blessings and protection.
- The Indo-Aryan peoples believed in the concept of rta, the cosmic order and law that governs the natural and moral realms, and the concept of karma, the principle of cause and effect that determines the fate of one's actions.
- The Indo-Aryan peoples also developed the idea of samsara, the cycle of birth and death, and the idea of moksha, the liberation from samsara, which were later elaborated in the Upanishads, the philosophical texts that form the basis of Hinduism.
- The Vedic religion was polytheistic, ritualistic, and hierarchical, with a priestly class, the Brahmins, who had the authority and knowledge to perform the rituals and interpret the Vedas .
