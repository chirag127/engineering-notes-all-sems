### Puranas

- Puranas are a vast genre of Indian literature that deal with a wide range of topics, such as legends, myths, history, cosmology, ethics, rituals, and philosophy. 
- Puranas are considered as one of the most ancient and important sources of Hindu culture and tradition. They are also regarded as the fifth Veda, after the four Vedas and the Upanishads.
- Puranas are composed in Sanskrit verse, mostly in the form of narrative couplets.  They date from the 4th century BCE to about 1000 CE, and vary greatly in terms of origin, authorship, and content. 
- Puranas are classified into two main categories: Mahapuranas (major Puranas) and Upapuranas (minor Puranas). There are 18 Mahapuranas and 18 Upapuranas, although some sources mention more or less. 
- Puranas are also categorized according to the three aspects of the supreme deity: Brahma (the creator), Vishnu (the preserver), and Shiva (the destroyer). Each Purana focuses on one of these aspects and narrates the stories and teachings related to them. 
- Puranas are not meant to be taken literally, but rather as symbolic and allegorical expressions of spiritual truths. They are meant to inspire devotion, morality, and wisdom among the readers and listeners. 
- Puranas are a rich source of information on various aspects of Indian culture, such as geography, astronomy, astrology, art, architecture, music, dance, literature, festivals, customs, and practices.  
- Puranas are also influential in shaping the popular Hindu beliefs and practices, such as the worship of various gods and goddesses, the concept of avatar (incarnation), the doctrine of karma (action and consequence), the cycle of rebirth, and the paths of liberation.
