### Chemistry in India

- Chemistry is the study of the composition, structure, properties and reactions of matter.
- India has a rich and ancient tradition of chemistry, dating back to the Vedic period (1500-500 BCE).
- Some of the main contributions of Indian chemistry are:

  - The concept of five elements (panchabhuta) as the basic constituents of matter, namely earth (prithvi), water (apas), fire (tejas), air (vayu) and ether (akasha).
  - The development of alchemy (rasayana shastra), which aimed at the transmutation of metals, the preparation of elixirs of immortality and the purification of the body and mind.
  - The discovery of various metals, alloys, minerals, salts, acids, bases, dyes, perfumes, cosmetics, medicines and poisons, and their extraction, purification and application methods.
  - The invention of various chemical apparatus, such as crucibles, furnaces, retorts, alembics, stills, filters, distillation units, etc.
  - The formulation of various chemical theories, such as the atomic theory (anu-vada), the theory of chemical affinity (samyoga-vada), the theory of chemical equilibrium (samya-vada), the theory of oxidation and reduction (dhatu-vada), etc.
  - The classification of substances into various categories, such as metals (loha), non-metals (amla), organic substances (jiva), inorganic substances (ajiva), etc.
  - The development of various branches of chemistry, such as metallurgy (dhatu-vijnana), mineralogy (khanija-vijnana), pharmacy (bhaishajya-vijnana), dyeing (ranjana-vijnana), etc.

- Some of the notable Indian chemists are:

  - Kanada (6th century BCE), who propounded the atomic theory and the concept of anu (atom) and paramanu (subatomic particle).
  - Nagarjuna (8th century CE), who wrote the Rasaratnakara, a treatise on alchemy, metallurgy, mineralogy and pharmacy, and discovered various metals and alloys, such as zinc, brass, wootz steel, etc.
  - Sushruta (6th century BCE), who wrote the Sushruta Samhita, a treatise on surgery, medicine and pharmacology, and described various methods of preparing and using drugs, such as fermentation, extraction, distillation, etc.
  - Charaka (2nd century BCE), who wrote the Charaka Samhita, a treatise on medicine and pharmacology, and classified drugs into various groups, such as plant, animal, mineral, etc.
  - Patanjali (2nd century BCE), who wrote the Yoga Sutras, a treatise on yoga and psychology, and described various methods of purifying the body and mind, such as pranayama, asana, dhyana, etc.
  - Varahamihira (6th century CE), who wrote the Brihat Samhita, a treatise on astronomy, astrology, geography and meteorology, and described various phenomena, such as eclipses, comets, rainbows, etc.
  - Aryabhata (5th century CE), who wrote the Aryabhatiya, a treatise on mathematics, astronomy and cosmology, and calculated the value of pi, the circumference of the earth, the length of the year, etc.
  - Bhaskara II (12th century CE), who wrote the Siddhanta Shiromani, a treatise on mathematics, astronomy and astrology, and solved various problems, such as quadratic equations, indeterminate equations, trigonometry, etc.
