### Administration

- Administration is the process of managing the affairs of an organization or a state by implementing policies, rules and regulations.
- Administration in India has a long and diverse history, influenced by various factors such as geography, culture, religion, ideology, colonialism and democracy.
- Administration in India can be broadly divided into four phases: ancient, medieval, colonial and post-colonial.

#### Ancient administration

- Ancient administration in India refers to the period from the Indus Valley Civilization (c. 2500-1500 BCE) to the end of the Gupta Empire (c. 550 CE).
- Ancient administration in India was characterized by the following features:
  - The emergence of urban centers, trade networks, writing systems and social stratification in the Indus Valley Civilization.
  - The development of political institutions such as monarchy, republics, oligarchies and confederations in the Vedic and post-Vedic periods.
  - The codification of laws, ethics and duties in the Dharmashastras and the Arthashastra.
  - The expansion of empires, such as the Mauryan, Kushan, Satavahana and Gupta, and the establishment of centralized administration, taxation, diplomacy and welfare schemes.
  - The influence of Buddhism, Jainism and Hinduism on the administration, culture and society of India.
  - The interaction with foreign invaders, such as the Greeks, Scythians, Parthians and Huns, and the adoption of their administrative practices and innovations.

#### Medieval administration

- Medieval administration in India refers to the period from the decline of the Gupta Empire (c. 550 CE) to the advent of the British rule (c. 1757 CE).
- Medieval administration in India was characterized by the following features:
  - The fragmentation of political authority and the emergence of regional kingdoms, such as the Chalukyas, Pallavas, Rashtrakutas, Pratiharas, Cholas, Pandyas, Chahamanas, Delhi Sultanate and Vijayanagara Empire.
  - The introduction of feudalism, land grants, military service and revenue collection systems in the administration.
  - The consolidation of Islamic rule and culture in northern India by the Delhi Sultanate and the Mughal Empire, and the establishment of a centralized, bureaucratic and merit-based administration.
  - The development of art, architecture, literature, music, science and technology under the patronage of various rulers and dynasties.
  - The resistance and coexistence of Hindu, Muslim and Sikh communities and their impact on the administration, culture and society of India.
  - The encounter with European traders, missionaries and colonizers, such as the Portuguese, Dutch, French and British, and the emergence of new forms of administration, commerce and diplomacy.

#### Colonial administration

- Colonial administration in India refers to the period from the establishment of the British East India Company (c. 1757 CE) to the end of the British Raj (c. 1947 CE).
- Colonial administration in India was characterized by the following features:
  - The expansion of the British East India Company's political and economic control over India through wars, treaties, alliances and annexations.
  - The introduction of the dual system of administration, the doctrine of lapse, the subsidiary alliance and the paramountcy in the governance of India.
  - The imposition of the British legal system, education system, civil service, railways, telegraph, postal service and currency in India.
  - The exploitation of India's natural and human resources, and the creation of a colonial economy based on trade, taxation, land revenue and opium.
  - The emergence of social, religious and political reforms, movements and leaders, such as the Bengal Renaissance, the Brahmo Samaj, the Arya Samaj, the Indian National Congress, the Muslim League and Mahatma Gandhi, and their demand for self-rule, democracy and independence.
  - The participation of India in the First and Second World Wars, and the impact of the events such as the Partition of Bengal, the Jallianwala Bagh Massacre, the Non-Cooperation Movement, the Civil Disobedience Movement, the Quit India Movement and the Indian National Army on the administration, culture and society of India.
