## Module 3- Indian Religion, Philosophy, and Practices

- India is a diverse and pluralistic country with many religions and philosophies that have influenced its culture, society, and history.
- The major religions in India are Hinduism, Buddhism, Jainism, Sikhism, Islam, and Christianity. There are also smaller groups of followers of Judaism, Zoroastrianism, and Baha'i Faith.
- Hinduism is the oldest and largest religion in India, with about 80% of the population identifying as Hindus. Hinduism is a complex and diverse tradition that encompasses many schools of thought, sects, and practices. Hinduism is based on the Vedas, the ancient scriptures that contain hymns, rituals, and philosophy. Hinduism believes in the concepts of karma, dharma, reincarnation, and moksha (liberation from the cycle of birth and death).
- Buddhism originated in India in the 6th century BCE, when Siddhartha Gautama, known as the Buddha, attained enlightenment and taught the Four Noble Truths and the Eightfold Path to end suffering and achieve nirvana. Buddhism spread across Asia and developed into various branches, such as Theravada, Mahayana, and Vajrayana. Buddhism is followed by about 0.7% of the Indian population, mainly in the states of Ladakh, Sikkim, Arunachal Pradesh, and Maharashtra.
- Jainism is an ancient religion that teaches the principles of non-violence, non-attachment, and non-absolutism. Jainism believes in the existence of soul in every living being and the possibility of attaining liberation by following the three jewels of right knowledge, right conduct, and right faith. Jainism is followed by about 0.4% of the Indian population, mostly in the states of Gujarat, Rajasthan, Maharashtra, and Karnataka.
- Sikhism is a monotheistic religion that was founded by Guru Nanak in the 15th century CE, and developed by his nine successors. Sikhism emphasizes the unity of God, the equality of all people, the importance of service, and the practice of the five Ks (kesh, kanga, kara, kachera, and kirpan). Sikhism is followed by about 1.7% of the Indian population, mainly in the state of Punjab and the national capital region of Delhi.
- Islam is the second-largest religion in India, with about 14.2% of the population identifying as Muslims. Islam is a monotheistic religion that is based on the teachings of Prophet Muhammad, as revealed in the Quran, the holy book of Muslims. Islam prescribes the five pillars of faith, which are the declaration of faith, prayer, charity, fasting, and pilgrimage. Islam has two major sects, Sunni and Shia, and several sub-sects, such as Sufism, Ahmadiyya, and Barelvi.
- Christianity is the third-largest religion in India, with about 2.3% of the population identifying as Christians. Christianity is a monotheistic religion that is based on the life and teachings of Jesus Christ, as recorded in the Bible, the holy book of Christians. Christianity has three major branches, which are Roman Catholic, Protestant, and Orthodox, and several denominations, such as Anglican, Baptist, Methodist, and Pentecostal. Christianity is mainly concentrated in the states of Kerala, Goa, Mizoram, Nagaland, and Meghalaya.
