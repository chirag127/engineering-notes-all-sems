### Indian’s Cultural Contribution to the World

India is a country of diverse and rich cultural heritage, which has influenced many aspects of the world civilization. Some of the major contributions of India to the world are:

- **Education**: India gave the world its first university, Takshashila University, which was established around 700 BCE and had more than 10,000 students from different countries . India also had other ancient centers of learning, such as Nalanda, Vikramashila, and Taxila, which attracted scholars from various parts of Asia.
- **Mathematics**: India gave the world the numeral system, which is the basis of modern arithmetic and algebra. The concept of zero, decimal system, place value system, and the use of symbols for numbers were developed in India by mathematicians such as Aryabhata, Brahmagupta, and Bhaskara . India also contributed to the fields of geometry, trigonometry, calculus, and astronomy.
- **Games**: India gave the world the game of chess, which originated as chaturanga, a four-player strategy game, in the 6th century CE . India also inspired the game of snakes and ladders, which was derived from an ancient Indian game called mokshapat, which taught moral lessons. India is also the birthplace of playing cards, which were called ganjifa and had different suits and symbols.
- **Art and Literature**: India gave the world some of the finest examples of art and literature, such as the Ajanta and Ellora caves, which are UNESCO World Heritage Sites and showcase the Buddhist, Hindu, and Jain paintings and sculptures. India also gave the world the epic poems of Ramayana and Mahabharata, which are among the longest and oldest literary works in the world and have influenced many cultures and religions. India also gave the world the Sanskrit language, which is one of the oldest and most sophisticated languages in the world and has influenced many other languages, such as Greek, Latin, Persian, and English.
- **Religion and Philosophy**: India gave the world some of the major religions and philosophies, such as Hinduism, Buddhism, Jainism, and Sikhism, which have millions of followers across the world and have shaped the moral and spiritual values of many people. India also gave the world the concepts of yoga, meditation, karma, dharma, and reincarnation, which are widely practiced and accepted by many people as ways of achieving physical, mental, and spiritual well-being.
- **Science and Technology**: India gave the world some of the earliest and most advanced inventions and discoveries in the fields of science and technology, such as the iron pillar of Delhi, which is a 7-meter-high pillar made of rust-resistant iron and dates back to the 4th century CE. India also gave the world the art of metallurgy, which involved the extraction and purification of metals, such as zinc, copper, tin, and steel. India also gave the world the art of surgery, which was practiced by ancient Indian physicians, such as Sushruta and Charaka, who performed various operations, such as cataract, rhinoplasty, and caesarean.
- **Culture and Lifestyle**: India gave the world some of the most unique and diverse aspects of culture and lifestyle, such as the use of spices, herbs, and ayurveda, which are part of the Indian cuisine and medicine and have many health benefits. India also gave the world the use of cotton, silk, and indigo, which are part of the Indian textile and dyeing industry and have influenced the fashion and trade of many countries. India also gave the world the use of shampoo, which originated as a herbal mixture of soapberries and other ingredients and was used to cleanse the hair and body. India also gave the world the use of henna, which is a natural dye made from the leaves of a plant and is used to decorate the skin and hair.
