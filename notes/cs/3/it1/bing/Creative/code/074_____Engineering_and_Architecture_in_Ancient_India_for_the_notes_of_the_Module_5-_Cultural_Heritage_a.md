# Engineering and Architecture in Ancient India

- Engineering and architecture in ancient India was known as **Sthapatya-Kala**, which means the art of constructing something.
- Ancient Indian architects and engineers derived principles of construction and architecture based on experience, observation of natural phenomena and considering social and cultural aspects of India.
- Ancient India witnessed two urban revolutions: the first one in the Indus Valley Civilization (around 2500 BCE) and the second one in the Mauryan and Kushan periods (around 6th century BCE to 3rd century CE).
- The Indus Valley Civilization was remarkable for its urban planning, drainage systems, granaries, docks, and monumental structures like the Great Bath.
- The Mauryan and Kushan periods saw the development of stone architecture, rock-cut caves, pillars, stupas, and temples.
- The most famous examples of Mauryan architecture are the Ashoka pillars, which have polished sandstone shafts and animal capitals, and the Sanchi stupa, which has a hemispherical dome and a carved torana (gateway).
- The most famous examples of Kushan architecture are the Gandhara and Mathura schools of art, which blended Indian and Hellenistic influences, and the rock-cut chaityas (prayer halls) and viharas (monasteries) at Ajanta, Ellora, and Karle.
- Hindu temple architecture in the Indian subcontinent continued to develop in North India and South India. Nagara style developed in North India where a Hindu temple incorporated Shikhara (spire) as its predominant architectural element whereas in southern India Vimana (tower) was used instead.
- The most famous examples of Nagara style are the Khajuraho temples, which have erotic sculptures, and the Kandariya Mahadeva temple, which has a complex and ornate shikhara.
- The most famous examples of Vimana style are the Brihadeeswarar temple, which has a massive vimana and a Nandi bull statue, and the Rameshwaram temple, which has the longest corridor among all Hindu temples.
- Ancient India also produced engineering marvels such as the Kailasa temple at Ellora, which was carved out of a single rock, and the stepwells of Gujarat and Rajasthan, which were designed to store water and provide relief from the heat.
- Engineering and architecture in ancient India reflected the diversity, creativity, and spirituality of the Indian civilization.
