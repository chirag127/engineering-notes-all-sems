# Slavery in India

- Slavery in India is a historical and contemporary phenomenon that has existed in various forms and periods.
- Slavery in India can be broadly categorized into two types: domestic slavery and bonded labor.
- Domestic slavery refers to the practice of owning and exploiting household servants, often captured or purchased from vulnerable groups such as lower castes, tribals, or religious minorities.
- Bonded labor refers to the practice of forcing workers to repay a debt or obligation through labor, often under harsh and abusive conditions, with little or no control over their wages, working hours, or freedom of movement.
- Slavery in India has been influenced by various factors, such as political, economic, social, cultural, and religious factors.
- Slavery in India has also been affected by the interactions and invasions of foreign powers, such as the Muslim rulers, the British colonizers, and the Portuguese traders, who introduced new forms and dimensions of slavery to the Indian subcontinent.
- Slavery in India has had significant impacts on the lives and rights of the enslaved people, as well as on the society and culture of India.
- Slavery in India has also been resisted and challenged by various movements and individuals, such as the anti-slavery campaigns of the British abolitionists, the social reforms of the Indian nationalists, and the human rights activism of the contemporary civil society organizations.
