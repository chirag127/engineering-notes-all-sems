### India’s Dominance up to Pre-colonial Times

- India has a long and rich history that dates back to 2500 BC, when the Indus Valley civilization emerged as one of the earliest urban cultures in the world.
- India witnessed the rise and fall of many empires and dynasties, such as the Mauryans, Guptas, Kushans, Palas, Cholas, Delhi Sultanate, Vijayanagara, Mughals, Marathas, Sikhs and others, that contributed to its political, cultural, religious and economic diversity and development .
- India was a major center of trade, commerce, learning, arts, sciences, and spirituality, attracting travelers, merchants, scholars, and pilgrims from various regions of Asia, Africa, and Europe  .
- India had a self-sufficient and flourishing economy, producing and exporting various goods, such as textiles, spices, metals, gems, handicrafts, and agricultural products, to different parts of the world. India was also a net importer of gold and silver, which enhanced its wealth and prestige .
- India had a complex and diverse social structure, based on the caste system, which divided the population into four main varnas (classes) and numerous jatis (sub-castes), each with its own rules, customs, and occupations. India also had various religious and ethnic groups, such as Hindus, Muslims, Buddhists, Jains, Sikhs, Christians, Jews, Parsis, and others, that coexisted and interacted with each other .
- India faced several challenges and threats from external invaders, such as the Greeks, Scythians, Huns, Arabs, Turks, Mongols, Afghans, Persians, Portuguese, and others, who tried to conquer and exploit its resources and people. Some of them succeeded in establishing their rule, while others were repelled or assimilated by the native rulers and cultures .
- India's dominance in the pre-colonial period was gradually eroded by the expansion of the British East India Company, which gained political and economic control over most of the subcontinent by the mid-18th century, through a combination of diplomacy, warfare, and alliances with local rulers. The British colonial rule had a profound impact on India's society, economy, culture, and identity, which led to the emergence of the Indian nationalist movement in the 19th and 20th centuries .
