### Kingship in Ancient India

- Kingship in ancient India was a **sovereignty** over a territory by a **king** who functioned as its **protector**, a role which involved both **secular and religious power**.
- Ancient Indians believed that the kingship arose out of **military requirement**. A king must be a capable **general** whose leadership is recognized by all.
- The meaning and significance of kingship changed dramatically between the **Vedic and Later Vedic period**, and underwent further development under the times of the **Jain and Buddhist rulers**.
- Some of the famous kings and emperors of ancient India were **Ajatasatru**, **Ashoka**, **Chandragupta Maurya**, **Samudragupta**, **Harshavardhana**, **Vikramaditya**, **Rajaraja Chola**, **Krishnadevaraya**, **Akbar**, **Shivaji** and many more.
- There were different **theories of kingship** in ancient India, such as the **Brahmanic theory**, the **Kshatriya theory**, the **Shramanic theory**, the **Dharmashastra theory**, the **Arthashastra theory** and the **Tantric theory** .
- The Brahmanic theory held that the king was a **divine** or **semi-divine** being, who derived his authority from the **gods** and the **sacrifices** he performed.
- The Kshatriya theory asserted that the king was a **heroic** or **noble** warrior, who earned his right to rule by his **valor** and **conquest**.
- The Shramanic theory challenged the Brahmanic and Kshatriya theories, and advocated for a **republican** or **democratic** form of government, where the people had a say in the selection and removal of the ruler.
- The Dharmashastra theory emphasized that the king was a **guardian** or **upholder** of the **dharma** or the **moral law**, and that he had to follow the **scriptures** and the **traditions** of his ancestors.
- The Arthashastra theory proposed that the king was a **realist** or **pragmatist**, who had to use his **intelligence** and **strategy** to achieve his **political** and **economic** goals, and that he had to be **ruthless** and **cunning** when necessary.
- The Tantric theory suggested that the king was a **mystic** or **magician**, who had to harness the **cosmic** and **occult** forces to gain **power** and **domination**, and that he had to be **secretive** and **initiated** into the **esoteric** teachings.
