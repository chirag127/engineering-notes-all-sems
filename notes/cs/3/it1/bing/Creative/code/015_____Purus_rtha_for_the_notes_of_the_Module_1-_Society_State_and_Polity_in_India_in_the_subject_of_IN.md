### Purusārtha

- Purusārtha is a Sanskrit word that means "object (ive) of men" or "human endeavour".
- It is a key concept in Hinduism that refers to the four proper goals or aims of a human life.
- The four purusārthas are:
  - Dharma: the moral and ethical duty of a person according to their stage and role in life. It also means righteousness, justice, law, and virtue. Dharma is the foundation of the other three purusārthas.  
  - Artha: the material and economic well-being of a person. It includes wealth, security, power, and prosperity. Artha is necessary for fulfilling one's dharma and kāma, but it should not be pursued at the expense of them.  
  - Kāma: the sensual and emotional pleasure of a person. It includes love, desire, passion, and enjoyment. Kāma is a natural and legitimate goal of human life, but it should be regulated by dharma and artha.  
  - Mokṣa: the spiritual liberation of a person from the cycle of birth and death (saṃsāra). It is the ultimate and highest goal of human life. It is achieved by realizing one's true nature as the ātman (self) or Brahman (supreme reality). Mokṣa is the culmination of the other three purusārthas.  
- The purusārthas are the blueprint for human fulfillment. They offer a way of evaluating one's life and making good decisions. They also provide a framework for understanding the diversity and complexity of Hindu traditions.
