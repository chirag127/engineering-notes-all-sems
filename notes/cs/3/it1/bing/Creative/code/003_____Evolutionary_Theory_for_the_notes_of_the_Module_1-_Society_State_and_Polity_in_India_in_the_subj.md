### Evolutionary Theory

- Evolutionary theory is a perspective that explains the origin and development of human societies and cultures based on the principles of biological evolution.
- Evolutionary theory assumes that human societies and cultures are the products of natural selection, adaptation, and inheritance, and that they change over time in response to environmental and historical factors.
- Evolutionary theory also suggests that human societies and cultures can be classified into different stages or types according to their level of complexity, organization, and technology.
- Evolutionary theory was influenced by the works of Charles Darwin, Herbert Spencer, Lewis Henry Morgan, Edward Burnett Tylor, and others in the 19th and early 20th centuries.
- Evolutionary theory was criticized by later anthropologists and sociologists for being ethnocentric, deterministic, unilinear, and teleological, and for ignoring the diversity, agency, and contingency of human societies and cultures.
- Evolutionary theory was revived and modified by neo-evolutionists such as Leslie White, Julian Steward, Marshall Sahlins, and Elman Service in the mid-20th century, who introduced concepts such as cultural evolution, multilinear evolution, cultural ecology, and cultural materialism.
- Evolutionary theory is still relevant and influential in some fields of anthropology and sociology, such as evolutionary psychology, evolutionary sociology, cultural evolution, and sociobiology.
