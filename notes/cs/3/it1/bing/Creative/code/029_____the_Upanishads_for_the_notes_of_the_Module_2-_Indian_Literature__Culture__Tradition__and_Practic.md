# The Upanishads

- The Upanishads are late Vedic Sanskrit texts that supplied the basis of later Hindu philosophy.
- The word Upanishad means "to sit down closely" as one would to listen attentively to instruction by a teacher or other authority figure.
- The Upanishads are commonly referred to as Vedanta, which means "the end of the Veda" or "the highest purpose of the Veda".
- The aim of all Upanishads is to investigate the nature of Atman (self), and direct the enquirer toward it.
- There are more than 200 known Upanishads, but the most influential and widely studied are the 12 principal Upanishads, which date from the 8th to the 3rd century BCE .
- The Upanishads present a vision of an interconnected universe with a single, unifying principle behind the apparent diversity in the cosmos, any articulation of which is called Brahman.
- The Upanishads teach that Brahman resides in the Atman, the unchanging core of the human individual, and that the goal of life is to realize this identity and attain liberation from the cycle of rebirth.
- The Upanishads also introduce concepts such as karma (action and its consequences), dharma (duty or law), samsara (transmigration of souls), moksha (liberation), and yoga (discipline or practice) that are central to Hindu thought and practice .
- The Upanishads are considered to be the source of the highest wisdom and the essence of the Vedic teachings in Hinduism. They have also influenced other religious traditions such as Buddhism, Jainism, Sikhism, and even Western philosophy .
