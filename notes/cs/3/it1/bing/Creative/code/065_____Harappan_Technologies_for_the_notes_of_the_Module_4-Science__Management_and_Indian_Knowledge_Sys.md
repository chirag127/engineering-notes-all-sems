### Harappan Technologies

- The Harappan civilization, also known as the Indus Valley civilization, was a Bronze Age civilization that flourished in the northwest of the Indian subcontinent from about 2500 BCE to 1900 BCE.
- The Harappans developed various technologies and crafts, such as metallurgy, seal carving, pottery, jewelry, weights and measures, urban planning, drainage systems, and writing.
- Some of the notable features of Harappan technologies are:

  - **Metallurgy**: The Harappans used copper, bronze, lead, and tin to make tools, weapons, vessels, and ornaments. They knew how to alloy copper with tin to make bronze, which is harder and more durable than copper. They also knew how to cast metal objects using the lost-wax process, which involves making a wax model, covering it with clay, heating it to melt the wax, and then pouring molten metal into the hollow mold.
  - **Seal carving**: The Harappans carved distinctive seals, usually made of steatite, a soft stone, with various motifs and inscriptions. The seals were used to stamp clay on trade goods, to identify property, and possibly to serve as amulets or tokens of authority. The motifs on the seals include animals, such as bulls, elephants, rhinoceroses, tigers, and unicorns, as well as human figures, trees, and geometric patterns. The inscriptions are in a script that has not been deciphered yet.
  - **Pottery**: The Harappans produced fine pottery, mostly wheel-made, with various shapes, sizes, and decorations. The pottery was usually red or buff in color, with black or red painted designs. The designs include geometric patterns, floral motifs, animals, birds, and fish. Some of the pottery types include storage jars, cooking pots, dishes, bowls, cups, spouted vessels, and perforated jars.
  - **Jewelry**: The Harappans made jewelry from materials such as gold, silver, copper, bronze, ivory, shell, bone, terracotta, and stone. They used techniques such as hammering, soldering, wire-drawing, bead-making, and inlaying to create various ornaments, such as necklaces, bracelets, bangles, earrings, rings, pendants, and headbands. The jewelry was often adorned with semi-precious stones, such as carnelian, lapis lazuli, turquoise, and agate.
  - **Weights and measures**: The Harappans developed a system of standardized weights and measures, based on a decimal and binary system. The weights were made of chert, a type of stone, and were shaped like cubes, spheres, or cones. The smallest weight was about 0.8 grams, and the largest was about 12.5 kilograms. The weights were used for trade and taxation purposes. The measures were based on a unit of length called the Indus inch, which was about 1.6 centimeters. The measures were used for surveying, architecture, and engineering.
  - **Urban planning**: The Harappans built well-planned cities, with a grid layout, fortified walls, and gateways. The cities had public buildings, such as granaries, baths, citadels, and temples, as well as private houses, workshops, and markets. The houses were usually two or three stories high, made of baked bricks, and had flat roofs. The houses had rooms, courtyards, wells, bathrooms, and drains. The drains were connected to a sophisticated sewage system, which carried the waste water to the main drains and then to the rivers or the sea.
  - **Writing**: The Harappans developed a system of writing, which is one of the oldest in the world. The writing consisted of about 400 signs, which were mostly pictographic, representing objects, animals, plants, and abstract concepts. The writing was written from right to left, and sometimes boustrophedon, which means alternating the direction of writing in each line. The writing was used for various purposes, such as recording transactions, labeling goods, expressing religious beliefs, and communicating messages. The writing was inscribed on seals, tablets, pottery, copper plates, and other materials.
