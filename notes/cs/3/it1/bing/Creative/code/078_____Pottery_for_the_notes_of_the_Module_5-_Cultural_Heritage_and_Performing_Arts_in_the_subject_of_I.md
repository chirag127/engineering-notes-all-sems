# Pottery

Pottery is one of the most ancient and enduring forms of artistic expression in India. It is a cultural art that reflects the diversity and richness of Indian traditions, regions, and history. Here are some points to note about pottery in India:

- Pottery is the process of forming vessels and other objects with clay and other ceramic materials, which are fired at high temperatures to give them a hard, durable form.
- Pottery in India has a long history, dating back to the prehistoric times. Evidence of pottery has been found in the early settlements of Lahuradewa and later the Indus Valley Civilisation .
- Pottery in India is not only a utilitarian craft, but also a medium of artistic expression. Pottery is used to create various objects such as pots, bowls, plates, cups, lamps, toys, figurines, idols, and decorative items.
- Pottery in India is influenced by various factors such as geography, climate, culture, religion, and trade. Different regions of India have developed their own distinctive styles, techniques, and motifs of pottery over time.
- Some of the well-known types of pottery in India are:

  - Terracotta pottery: This is the most common and basic form of pottery, made from unglazed red or brown clay. Terracotta pottery is widely used for domestic and ritual purposes, as well as for making sculptures and architectural elements. Terracotta pottery is found in almost every part of India, with some notable examples being Bishnupur, Bankura, and Panchmura in West Bengal, Nizamabad in Uttar Pradesh, and Molela in Rajasthan .
  - Glazed pottery: This is a form of pottery that has a layer of glass-like coating on the surface, which gives it a shiny and colorful appearance. Glazed pottery is mainly used for decorative and ornamental purposes, as well as for making tiles and tableware. Glazed pottery is believed to have been introduced in India by the Persian and Arab invaders, and later influenced by the Mughal and British styles. Some of the famous centers of glazed pottery in India are Khurja and Rampur in Uttar Pradesh, Jaipur and Sanganer in Rajasthan, and Delhi .
  - Blue pottery: This is a form of glazed pottery that has a distinctive blue color, derived from the cobalt oxide used in the glaze. Blue pottery is one of the most exquisite and unique forms of pottery in India, and is considered a specialty of Jaipur. Blue pottery is mainly used for making vases, jars, plates, tiles, and other decorative items. Blue pottery is said to have originated in Persia, and was brought to India by the Mongol and Turk invaders. It was later patronized by the Mughal and Rajput rulers, and developed into a fine art form by the master potter Sardar Gurcharan Singh and his son Mansimran Singh  .
  - Black pottery: This is a form of pottery that has a dark black color, achieved by reducing the oxygen supply during the firing process. Black pottery is known for its smooth and glossy texture, and its intricate designs made with cane or bamboo. Black pottery is mainly used for making pots, bowls, cups, and other utensils. Black pottery is found in some regions of India, such as Nizamabad in Uttar Pradesh, Longpi in Manipur, and Khavda in Gujarat  .
  - Stoneware pottery: This is a form of pottery that is made from high-quality clay, and fired at very high temperatures, making it hard, dense, and durable. Stoneware pottery is resistant to water, heat, and scratches, and is suitable for making cookware, bakeware, and tableware. Stoneware pottery is a relatively modern form of pottery in India, and is influenced by the Western and Japanese styles. Some of the prominent centers of stoneware pottery in India are Andretta in Himachal Pradesh, Auroville in Tamil Nadu, and Pondicherry .

- Pottery in India is not only a craft, but also a cultural heritage and a performing art. Pottery is often associated with various festivals, rituals, and folklores, and is a source of livelihood and identity for many communities.
