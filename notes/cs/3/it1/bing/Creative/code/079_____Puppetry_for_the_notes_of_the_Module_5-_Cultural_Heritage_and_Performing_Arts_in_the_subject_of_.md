### Puppetry

Puppetry is an ancient art form that involves storytelling through manipulating puppets. Puppets are figures that resemble humans, animals, or other objects that can be moved by strings, rods, wires, hands, or other means. Puppetry is a form of performing arts that can convey various themes, emotions, and messages.

Some of the main points to know about puppetry in India are:

- India is said to be the home of puppets, as almost all types of puppets are found in India.
- Puppetry in India dates back thousands of years, and many folk tales, ballads, and songs have references to it.
- Puppetry in India is a family business, and children learn the craft from their elders. The puppets are a family treasure, and sometimes used as a bride's dowry.
- Puppetry in India has philosophical significance, as it reflects the concept of God as a puppeteer who controls the cosmos with three strings: Satta, Raja, and Tama. The storyteller in Indian theatre is called Sutradhar, which means "string bearer".
- Puppetry in India is influenced by the regional culture, history, and traditions of the different states. The puppets vary in size, shape, material, and technique according to the local style.
- Puppetry in India can be classified into four major types: string puppets, rod puppets, glove puppets, and shadow puppets.
- String puppets are controlled by strings attached to different parts of the puppet's body. The most popular form of string puppetry in India is Kathputli, which is native to Rajasthan. Kathputli means "wooden doll" in Hindi, and the puppets are made of wood and cloth.
- Rod puppets are controlled by rods attached to the puppet's limbs or body. The puppeteer holds the rods and moves them to animate the puppet. The most famous form of rod puppetry in India is Yampuri, which is practiced in West Bengal. Yampuri means "wooden horse" in Bengali, and the puppets are made of wood and bamboo.
- Glove puppets are controlled by the puppeteer's hand, which fits inside the puppet's body. The puppeteer uses the fingers to move the puppet's head and arms. The most well-known form of glove puppetry in India is Pavakoothu, which is performed in Kerala. Pavakoothu means "puppet play" in Malayalam, and the puppets are made of leather and cloth.
- Shadow puppets are flat figures that are held against a screen or a wall, and illuminated by a light source. The puppeteer moves the puppets to create shadows that tell the story. The most prominent form of shadow puppetry in India is Tholu Bommalata, which is prevalent in Andhra Pradesh. Tholu Bommalata means "leather puppet dance" in Telugu, and the puppets are made of leather and painted with bright colors.

Puppetry in India is a rich and diverse art form that reflects the cultural heritage and performing arts of the country. Puppetry in India is not only a source of entertainment, but also a medium of education, social awareness, and cultural preservation. Puppetry in India is a living tradition that continues to evolve and adapt to the changing times and audiences.
