### Harappan Script

- The Harappan script, also known as the Indus script, is a corpus of symbols produced by the Indus Valley Civilisation, which flourished in the northwest of the Indian subcontinent from c. 2500 to 1900 BCE.
- The script is found on various objects such as seals, tablets, pottery, copper plates, tools, and jewellery, mostly from the urban centres of Harappa and Mohenjo-Daro .
- The script consists of about 400 signs, some of which are pictograms (representing objects or concepts) and some of which are phonograms (representing sounds or syllables) .
- The script has not been deciphered satisfactorily, and therefore the language or languages it represents remain unknown . Some scholars have suggested that the script may encode a Dravidian, Indo-Aryan, Sumerian, or Munda language, but there is no consensus on this issue .
- The script is written from right to left, sometimes in a boustrophedon style (alternating direction of successive lines) . The signs are usually arranged in groups of varying length, separated by spaces or lines .
- The script may have been used for various purposes, such as recording trade transactions, religious rituals, personal names, titles, or messages  . However, the exact function and meaning of the script are still unclear and debated .
- The script is one of the oldest known writing systems in the world, and reflects the cultural and technological achievements of the Indus Valley Civilisation  . It is also a source of mystery and fascination for scholars and enthusiasts who hope to unlock its secrets  .
