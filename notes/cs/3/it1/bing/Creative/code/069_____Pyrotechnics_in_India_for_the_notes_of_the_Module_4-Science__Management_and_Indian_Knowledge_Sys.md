# Pyrotechnics in India

Pyrotechnics is the science and art of creating and using fireworks and other explosive devices for entertainment, military, or industrial purposes. Pyrotechnics has a long and rich history in India, dating back to ancient times. Here are some points to note about pyrotechnics in India:

- According to some sources, fireworks were invented in India by the alchemists of the Gupta dynasty (320-550 CE), who used them for religious and festive occasions. They also used them for warfare, such as throwing fireballs and rockets at enemies.
- Fireworks and pyrotechnic shows existed as a form of royal entertainment in many medieval Indian kingdoms during festivals, events and special occasions like weddings. Manufacturing formulas for fireworks describing pyrotechnic mixtures are found within Kautukachintamani, a Sanskrit volume by Gajapati Prataparudradeva (1497-1539), a reputed king of the Suryavamsa dynasty of Orissa.
- One of the earliest notes of pyrotechnical shows in India is made by Abdur Razzaq, the ambassador of the Timurid Sultan Shahrukh to the court of the Vijayanagar king Devaraya II in 1443. He describes a grand display of fireworks at the king's palace, which lasted for several hours and amazed the spectators.
- Fireworks became a popular and integral part of Diwali, the festival of lights, in India. Diwali celebrates the victory of good over evil, and the fireworks symbolize the light that dispels the darkness. Fireworks are also used for other festivals and occasions, such as Holi, Dussehra, Eid, weddings, birthdays, and New Year's Eve.
- India is one of the largest producers and consumers of fireworks in the world. The fireworks industry in India is mainly concentrated in Sivakasi, Tamil Nadu, which accounts for about 90% of the domestic production and 80% of the exports. Sivakasi is known as the "fireworks capital of India" and employs about 250,000 people directly and indirectly.
- However, the use of fireworks in India has also raised environmental and health concerns, especially in urban areas. Fireworks emit harmful pollutants, such as particulate matter, carbon monoxide, sulfur dioxide, nitrogen oxides, and heavy metals, which contribute to air pollution and respiratory diseases. Fireworks also cause noise pollution, which can affect the hearing and well-being of humans and animals. Fireworks also pose a risk of accidents, injuries, and fires, especially when handled by children or untrained persons.
- In recent years, the government and the judiciary have taken steps to regulate and reduce the use of fireworks in India, in order to protect the environment and public health. In 2018, the Supreme Court of India banned the sale and use of most pyrotechnics and allowed only a handful of "green crackers", which are supposed to have less noise and smoke. The court also restricted the timing of fireworks to two hours during Diwali and other festivals. The court also directed the government to set up a regulatory framework for the fireworks industry and to promote public awareness and education on the harmful effects of fireworks.
- Despite these measures, the implementation and enforcement of the ban and the regulations have been challenging and inconsistent, due to the lack of adequate infrastructure, manpower, and coordination among the authorities. The demand and supply of fireworks also remain high, as many people consider them as an essential part of their culture and tradition. The fireworks industry also faces economic and social challenges, as it provides livelihood to thousands of workers and artisans, who have been affected by the restrictions and the pandemic.
- Therefore, there is a need for a balanced and sustainable approach to pyrotechnics in India, which can address the environmental, health, and safety issues, while also respecting the cultural and economic aspects. Some possible solutions include:

  - Developing and promoting more eco-friendly and safer alternatives to conventional fireworks, such as LED lights, laser shows, soundless crackers, and biodegradable materials.
  - Improving the quality and standards of the fireworks industry, by introducing better technology, innovation, and quality control, and by ensuring compliance with the regulations and the guidelines.
  - Enhancing the awareness and education of the public, especially the children and the youth, on the harmful effects of fireworks and the benefits of green crackers, and by encouraging them to celebrate the festivals in a more responsible and respectful manner
