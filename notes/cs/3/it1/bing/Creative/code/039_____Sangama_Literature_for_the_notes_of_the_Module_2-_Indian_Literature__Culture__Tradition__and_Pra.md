### Sangama Literature

- Sangama Literature is the name given to the collection of Tamil literary works produced during the Sangam period, which lasted from the 3rd century BCE to the 3rd century CE.
- The Sangam period is considered the golden age of Tamil literature and culture, as it witnessed the flourishing of various aspects of Tamil civilization, such as language, grammar, poetry, music, art, architecture, religion, philosophy, ethics, politics, and trade.
- The Sangam literature consists of two main categories: Ettuthokai (Eight Anthologies) and Pattuppattu (Ten Idylls). The Ettuthokai contains eight anthologies of poems on various themes, such as love, war, heroism, ethics, and nature. The Pattuppattu contains ten long poems that narrate the legends and achievements of ancient Tamil kings and heroes.
- The Sangam literature also includes two important works on Tamil grammar and poetics: Tolkappiyam and Iraiyanar Akapporul. The Tolkappiyam is the oldest extant work of Tamil literature, and it deals with the phonology, morphology, syntax, and semantics of the Tamil language. The Iraiyanar Akapporul is a treatise on the principles and techniques of Tamil poetry, and it explains the various types of poetic meters, figures of speech, and genres.
- The Sangam literature is characterized by its realism, naturalism, humanism, and secularism. The poems depict the life and culture of the ancient Tamils in vivid and authentic details, without any exaggeration or fantasy. The poems also express the emotions and aspirations of the common people, as well as the values and ideals of the society. The poems are mostly secular in nature, and they do not focus on any particular religion or doctrine, but rather on the universal aspects of human experience.
- The Sangam literature is a valuable source of information and insight into the history, geography, sociology, and psychology of the ancient Tamils. The poems provide various references and clues to the political, economic, and cultural aspects of the Sangam era, such as the names and locations of the kingdoms, cities, rivers, mountains, forests, and seas, the types and modes of trade and commerce, the customs and traditions of the people, the festivals and ceremonies, the arts and crafts, the music and dance, the food and clothing, the wars and alliances, the laws and justice, the education and learning, the morals and ethics, and the beliefs and practices of the various religions and sects.

The poems display a high degree of creativity, originality, and sophistication in the use of language, imagery, symbolism, metaphor, and allegory. The poems also exhibit a remarkable variety and versatility in the choice and treatment of themes, genres, and styles.
