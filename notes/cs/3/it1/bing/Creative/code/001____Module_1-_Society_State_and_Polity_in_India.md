# Module 1- Society State and Polity in India

This module aims to introduce the concepts of society, state and polity and their interrelations in the context of India. It will also explore the historical and contemporary issues and challenges that shape the Indian society and polity.

## Learning objectives

- To understand the basic concepts of society, state and polity and their differences and similarities.
- To trace the evolution of the Indian society and polity from ancient to modern times and the factors that influenced them.
- To analyze the diversity and complexity of the Indian society and polity and the role of identity, culture, religion, caste, class, gender, region, language, etc. in shaping them.
- To examine the constitutional and institutional framework of the Indian polity and the principles and values that underpin it.
- To evaluate the achievements and challenges of the Indian polity in terms of democracy, development, secularism, federalism, human rights, social justice, etc.
- To appreciate the role and contribution of various social and political actors and movements in the Indian society and polity.

## Topics

- Society, state and polity: definitions, dimensions and distinctions.
- Historical overview of the Indian society and polity: ancient, medieval and modern periods.
- Diversity and complexity of the Indian society and polity: identity, culture, religion, caste, class, gender, region, language, etc.
- Constitutional and institutional framework of the Indian polity: preamble, fundamental rights and duties, directive principles, parliament, executive, judiciary, etc.
- Principles and values of the Indian polity: democracy, development, secularism, federalism, human rights, social justice, etc.
- Achievements and challenges of the Indian polity: elections, representation, participation, accountability, governance, development, social movements, etc.
- Social and political actors and movements in the Indian society and polity: political parties, civil society, media, NGOs, social reformers, nationalists, etc.
