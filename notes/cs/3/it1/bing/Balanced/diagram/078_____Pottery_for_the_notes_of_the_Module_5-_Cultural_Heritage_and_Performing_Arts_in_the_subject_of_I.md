### Pottery

- Pottery is one of the most ancient and tangible forms of Indian art. It is a cultural art that reflects the diversity and richness of Indian traditions and society.
- Pottery is the process of forming vessels and other objects with clay and other ceramic materials, which are fired at high temperatures to give them a hard, durable form.
- Pottery has been found in the early settlements of Lahuradewa and Mehargarh, dating back to the Neolithic period. Later, the Indus Valley Civilization developed a sophisticated pottery culture, with various types of pottery such as plain, painted, perforated, and glazed.
- Pottery is still practiced extensively in India, especially in rural areas and pottery villages. Pottery plays an important role in the daily life, rituals, and economy of the people. Pottery is also used for artistic expression and decoration.
- Pottery in India has a variety of forms, styles, techniques, and materials, depending on the region, culture, and history of the place. Some of the well-known forms of pottery in India are:

  - Jaipur Blue Pottery: A glazed pottery from Rajasthan, with a distinctive blue color and floral motifs. It is influenced by the Persian and Mughal art and is made with quartz stone powder, glass, and multani mitti (fuller's earth).
  - Khurja Pottery: A traditional pottery from Uttar Pradesh, with a white background and colorful designs. It is made with red clay and is used for making tableware, tiles, and decorative items.
  - Longpi Pottery: A black pottery from Manipur, with a smooth and shiny texture. It is made with a mixture of serpentine stone and weathered rock, and is hand-moulded without using a potter's wheel.
  - Khavda Pottery: A terracotta pottery from Gujarat, with a red or brown color and geometric patterns. It is made with clay and decorated with white clay slip and geru (red ochre).
  - Nizamabad Pottery: A black pottery from Uttar Pradesh, with a metallic lustre and silver or golden designs. It is made with clay and polished with a vegetable or animal oil. It is decorated with incised or stamped patterns and sometimes with silver or gold foil.
