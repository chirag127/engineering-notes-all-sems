### Physics in India

- Physics is the branch of science that studies the nature and properties of matter and energy, and the interactions between them.
- Physics in India has a long and rich history, dating back to the ancient times, when the Indian philosophers and sages developed various concepts and theories related to physics, such as atomism, cosmology, optics, acoustics, astronomy, and mathematics.
- Some of the notable contributions of ancient Indian physics are:
  - The Nyaya and Vaisheshika schools of philosophy proposed the idea of paramanu (atoms) as the indivisible units of matter, and the concept of anu (molecules) as the combinations of atoms. They also described the five basic elements (earth, water, fire, air, and ether) and their properties, and the laws of motion and causation.
  - The Sankhya school of philosophy developed the concept of prakriti (nature) as the source of all material phenomena, and the concept of purusha (consciousness) as the source of all spiritual phenomena. They also classified the prakriti into three gunas (qualities): sattva (purity), rajas (activity), and tamas (inertia), and explained their effects on the creation and evolution of the universe.
  - The Yoga school of philosophy introduced the concept of chitta (mind) as the subtlest form of matter, and the concept of prana (life force) as the energy that animates all living beings. They also developed the techniques of pranayama (breath control) and meditation to regulate the flow of prana and achieve higher states of consciousness.
  - The Vedanta school of philosophy formulated the concept of brahman (the ultimate reality) as the source and essence of everything, and the concept of atman (the individual self) as the reflection of brahman in the human soul. They also expounded the doctrine of advaita (non-duality) as the realization of the identity of brahman and atman, and the concept of maya (illusion) as the cause of the apparent diversity and multiplicity of the world.
  - The Jaina school of philosophy developed the concept of jiva (soul) as the eternal and independent entity that possesses consciousness and free will, and the concept of ajiva (non-soul) as the inert and dependent entity that includes matter, space, time, and motion. They also proposed the theory of anekantavada (many-sidedness) as the recognition of the relative and partial nature of truth, and the principle of syadvada (conditional predication) as the expression of the multiple aspects and perspectives of reality.
  - The Buddhist school of philosophy proposed the concept of shunyata (emptiness) as the ultimate nature of reality, and the concept of pratityasamutpada (dependent origination) as the causal and conditional relation of all phenomena. They also developed the theory of skandhas (aggregates) as the five components of the human personality (form, sensation, perception, mental formation, and consciousness), and the concept of anatta (no-self) as the denial of the existence of a permanent and independent self.
  - The mathematician and astronomer Aryabhata (476-550 CE) wrote the Aryabhatiya, one of the earliest treatises on Indian astronomy and mathematics. He gave the value of pi as 3.1416, and calculated the circumference of the earth as 24,835 miles. He also proposed the heliocentric model of the solar system, and explained the causes of eclipses, the phases of the moon, and the motion of the planets.
  - The physicist and mathematician Brahmagupta (598-668 CE) wrote the Brahmasphutasiddhanta, one of the most influential works on Indian mathematics and astronomy. He introduced the concept of zero and negative numbers, and gave the rules for arithmetic operations involving them. He also gave the formula for the area of a cyclic quadrilateral, and solved various problems of geometry and algebra.
  - The physicist and astronomer Bhaskara II (1114-1185 CE) wrote the Siddhanta Shiromani, one of the most comprehensive works on Indian mathematics and astronomy. He gave the value of pi as 3.14159265359, and calculated the length of the solar year as 365.258756484
