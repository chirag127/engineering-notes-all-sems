## Module 2- Indian Literature, Culture, Tradition, and Practices

- Indian literature is the expression of the diverse and rich cultural heritage of India, which includes oral traditions, folklores, epics, myths, poetry, drama, and prose in various languages and scripts.
- Indian culture is the result of the synthesis of various influences, such as indigenous, foreign, ancient, and modern, that have shaped the values, beliefs, customs, and rituals of the people of India over thousands of years.
- Indian tradition is the transmission of the accumulated wisdom, knowledge, and experience of the ancestors, which guides the social, moral, and spiritual life of the present and future generations.
- Indian practices are the ways of living, working, celebrating, worshiping, and interacting that reflect the diversity, creativity, and adaptability of the Indian people in different regions, religions, castes, and communities.

Some of the main aspects of Indian literature, culture, tradition, and practices are:

- Greetings: Indians greet each other with a namaste, which means "I bow to the divine in you". It is done by folding the palms together and placing them near the chest or forehead, while bowing slightly. This gesture shows respect, humility, and goodwill.
- Religious customs: India is home to many religions, such as Hinduism, Buddhism, Jainism, Sikhism, Islam, Christianity, Zoroastrianism, and Judaism. Each religion has its own rituals, festivals, scriptures, and places of worship. Some of the common religious customs in India are:
  - Offering prayers, flowers, fruits, incense, and lamps to various deities and saints in temples, mosques, churches, gurudwaras, and other shrines.
  - Performing rites of passage, such as birth, naming, initiation, marriage, and death, according to the specific traditions of each community.
  - Observing fasts, vows, pilgrimages, and charity as a way of expressing devotion, gratitude, penance, or fulfillment of a wish.
  - Celebrating festivals, such as Diwali, Holi, Eid, Christmas, Guru Nanak Jayanti, and Buddha Purnima, with joy, enthusiasm, and harmony.
- Festivals of India: India has a rich and diverse calendar of festivals, which mark the seasons, harvests, historical events, legends, and myths. Some of the major festivals of India are:
  - Diwali: The festival of lights, which celebrates the victory of good over evil, and the return of Lord Rama to his kingdom after defeating the demon king Ravana. People light lamps, candles, and fireworks, exchange gifts and sweets, and worship Goddess Lakshmi, the goddess of wealth and prosperity.
  - Holi: The festival of colors, which celebrates the arrival of spring, and the triumph of love over hatred. People smear each other with colored powders and water, play music and dance, and share delicacies and drinks.
  - Eid: The festival of breaking the fast, which marks the end of the holy month of Ramadan, when Muslims abstain from food and drink from dawn to dusk. People offer prayers, give alms, visit relatives and friends, and feast on special dishes, such as biryani, kebabs, and seviyan.
  - Christmas: The festival of the birth of Jesus Christ, the son of God, and the savior of mankind. Christians decorate their homes and churches with lights, stars, and trees, attend midnight mass, sing carols, exchange gifts and cards, and enjoy cakes and puddings.
  - Guru Nanak Jayanti: The festival of the birth anniversary of Guru Nanak, the founder of Sikhism, and the first of the ten Sikh gurus. Sikhs recite the holy scripture, Guru Granth Sahib, take out processions, sing hymns, and serve free meals, called langar, to everyone.
  - Buddha Purnima: The festival of the full moon day, which commemorates the birth, enlightenment, and death of Gautama Buddha, the founder of Buddhism, and the teacher of the four noble truths and the eightfold path. Buddhists visit monasteries, offer prayers, meditate, and follow the five precepts of morality, such as abstaining from killing, stealing, lying, intoxication, and sexual misconduct.
