### Harappan Script

- Harappan script is the name given to the symbols found on the inscriptions and seals of the Indus Valley Civilisation, which flourished in the northwest of India and Pakistan from about 2500 to 1900 BCE .
- Harappan script is also known as Indus script, as it was first discovered in the ancient city of Harappa .
- Harappan script consists of about 400 signs, which vary in shape, size, and orientation . Some signs are pictorial, representing animals, plants, or objects, while others are abstract or geometric .
- Harappan script is written from right to left, sometimes with a boustrophedon (alternating direction) pattern . Most inscriptions are very short, consisting of only a few signs, and are found on pottery, seals, tablets, copper plates, and other artefacts  .
- Harappan script has not been deciphered yet, despite many attempts by various scholars and methods  . The main challenges are the lack of bilingual inscriptions, the unknown underlying language(s), and the brevity and variability of the texts  .
- Harappan script is one of the oldest writing systems in the world, and it reflects the culture and society of the Indus Valley Civilisation  . It may have been used for trade, administration, religion, or personal communication, but the exact purpose and meaning of the script remain a mystery   .
