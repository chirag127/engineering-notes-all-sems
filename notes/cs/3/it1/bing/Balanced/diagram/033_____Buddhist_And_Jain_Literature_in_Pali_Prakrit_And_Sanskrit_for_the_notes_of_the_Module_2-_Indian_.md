### Buddhist and Jain literature in Pali, Prakrit and Sanskrit

- Pali and Prakrit are two languages that were used to write the religious texts of Buddhism and Jainism, respectively. Pali is an old form of Prakrit that combines several dialects, while Prakrit is a term that refers to any language other than the standard one, Sanskrit.
- The earliest Buddhist works were written in Pali, which was spoken in Magadha and South Bihar. These works include the Tripitaka or the Three Baskets, which consist of the Vinaya Pitaka (rules of conduct for monks and nuns), the Sutta Pitaka (discourses of the Buddha and his disciples) and the Abhidhamma Pitaka (philosophical and psychological analysis of the teachings).
- The Pali literature also contains commentaries, chronicles, biographies, poems and stories. Some of the famous works are the Dhammapada (a collection of verses on ethics and wisdom), the Jataka (stories of the Buddha's previous lives), the Milindapanha (a dialogue between King Milinda and a Buddhist monk), the Dipavamsa and Mahavamsa (chronicles of Sri Lanka) and the Therigatha and Theragatha (poems of the elder nuns and monks).
- The Buddhist literature in Sanskrit and Prakrit developed later, as Buddhism spread to other regions and sects. The Mahayana and Vajrayana schools of Buddhism produced a large number of texts in Sanskrit, such as the Prajnaparamita (perfection of wisdom) sutras, the Avatamsaka (flower garland) sutra, the Lotus sutra, the Lankavatara (descent to Lanka) sutra, the Tantras (esoteric teachings) and the biographies of the Buddha and the Bodhisattvas.
- The Prakrit literature of Buddhism includes the works of the Sarvastivada and Mahasamghika schools, which were written in Gandhari, a dialect of Prakrit. These works include the Vinaya (rules of conduct), the Sutra (discourses), the Abhidharma (analysis of the teachings) and the Jataka (stories of the Buddha's previous lives). Some of the texts are preserved in fragments, while others are translated into Chinese and Tibetan.
- The Jain literature is written in various Prakrit languages, such as Ardhamagadhi, Sauraseni, Magadhi and Maharashtri. The canonical texts of the Jains are called the Agamas, which are divided into two groups: the Angas (limbs) and the Angabahyas (those outside the limbs). The Angas consist of 12 texts, which deal with the doctrines, ethics, cosmology, biography and history of Jainism. The Angabahyas consist of 34 texts, which include commentaries, stories, legends, poems and encyclopedias.
- The Jain literature also contains non-canonical works, which are written in Prakrit and Sanskrit. Some of the famous works are the Kalpasutra (a biography of Mahavira and the Jain Tirthankaras), the Uttaradhyayana (a collection of sermons and dialogues), the Niryavalika (a commentary on the Angas), the Kavyas (poems), the Puranas (mythological histories) and the Prakirnakas (miscellaneous works).
