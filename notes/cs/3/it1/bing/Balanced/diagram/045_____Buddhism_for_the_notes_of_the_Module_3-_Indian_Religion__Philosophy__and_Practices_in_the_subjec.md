### Buddhism

- Buddhism is an Indian religion or philosophy that developed from the teachings of the Buddha (Sanskrit: "Awakened One"), a teacher who lived in northern India between the mid-6th and mid-4th centuries BCE .
- Buddhism is also known as Buddha Dharma and Dharmavinaya, which means "doctrines and disciplines".
- Buddhists follow the Four Noble Truths, the Noble Eightfold Path, and the Three Jewels, which are the Buddha, the Dharma (the teachings), and the Sangha (the community).
- Buddhists believe in the concept of karma, which is the law of cause and effect, and the cycle of rebirth, which is the process of being born again and again until one attains nirvana, which is the state of liberation from suffering.
- Buddhism has many branches and schools, such as Theravada, Mahayana, Vajrayana, Zen, Pure Land, and Tibetan Buddhism, which differ in their doctrines, practices, and traditions.
- Buddhism has spread to many countries and regions, such as China, Japan, Korea, Southeast Asia, Tibet, Sri Lanka, and the West, and has influenced many aspects of culture, art, literature, ethics, and society.
