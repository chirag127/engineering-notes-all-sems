### Puppetry

Puppetry is an ancient art form that involves storytelling through manipulating puppets. Puppets are figures that resemble humans, animals, or mythical creatures, and are animated by strings, rods, wires, or hands. Puppetry is a form of performing arts that reflects the cultural heritage and diversity of India.

Some of the main points to know about puppetry in India are:

- India is said to be the home of puppets, as almost all types of puppets are found in India.
- Puppetry in India dates back thousands of years, and has references in folk tales, ballads, and songs.
- Puppetry in India is a family business, and the puppets are passed on from generation to generation as a family treasure.
- Puppetry in India has philosophical significance, as God is depicted as a puppeteer who controls the cosmos with three strings: Satta, Raja, and Tama. The storyteller in Indian theatre is called Sutradhar, which means "string bearer".
- Puppetry in India is based on themes from epics, legends, history, and social issues. Puppetry is also used for education, awareness, and entertainment purposes .
- Puppetry in India has four major types: string puppets, rod puppets, glove puppets, and shadow puppets. Each type has its own regional variations, styles, and techniques .
- Some of the famous forms of puppetry in India are: Kathputli from Rajasthan, which is a string puppet theatre ; Bommalattam from Tamil Nadu, which is a rod puppet theatre; Pavakoothu from Kerala, which is a shadow puppet theatre; and Putul Nautch from West Bengal, which is a glove puppet theatre.
- Puppetry in India is facing challenges due to the decline in patronage, competition from modern media, and lack of recognition and support. However, puppetry in India is also undergoing innovations and adaptations to suit the changing times and audiences.
