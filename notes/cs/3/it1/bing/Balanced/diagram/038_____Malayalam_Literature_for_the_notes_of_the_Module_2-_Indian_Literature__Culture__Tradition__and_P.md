### Malayalam Literature

- Malayalam literature is the body of writing in the Malayalam language, a South-Dravidian language spoken in the Indian state of Kerala .
- The earliest extant literary work in Malayalam is Ramacharitam, a narrative poem based on the Ramayana, composed in the late 12th or early 13th century .
- Malayalam literature has been influenced by two outside sources: Sanskrit, the language of scholarship and classical literature, and Tamil, the language of administration and popular culture for a long period.
- Malayalam literature can be divided into four main periods: the early period (up to 15th century), the medieval period (15th to 19th century), the modern period (19th to 20th century), and the post-modern period (20th to 21st century).
- The early period is characterized by the development of a popular song (pattu) literature, which is entirely indigenous, and a sophisticated poetry (kavya) literature, which is influenced by Sanskrit models .
- The medieval period is marked by the emergence of a new style of writing called Manipravalam, which is an admixture of Malayalam and Sanskrit, and is mainly used for erotic and devotional poetry .
- The modern period is distinguished by the rise of prose literature, especially novels, short stories, essays, and drama, which reflect the social and political changes brought by the British colonial rule, the Christian missionary activities, and the reform movements.
- The post-modern period is characterized by the experimentation with new forms and themes, such as surrealism, magic realism, feminism, Dalit literature, and environmental literature, which challenge the established norms and conventions of Malayalam literature.
- Some of the notable writers and works of Malayalam literature are:

  - Thunchaththu Ramanujan Ezhuthachan, the father of Malayalam literature, who wrote Adhyathmaramayanam, a Malayalam version of the Ramayana, and Mahabharatham, a Malayalam version of the Mahabharata.
  - Poonthanam Nambudiri, a devotional poet, who wrote Jnanappana, a collection of verses on the philosophy of Bhakti.
  - Cherusseri Namboothiri, a court poet, who wrote Krishnagatha, a poem on the life of Krishna.
  - Ezhuthachan Puraskaram, the highest literary honour in Malayalam literature, which is awarded annually by the Kerala Sahitya Akademi.
  - O. N. V. Kurup, a modern poet, who won the Jnanpith Award, the highest literary award in India, for his contributions to Malayalam poetry.
  - M. T. Vasudevan Nair, a novelist and screenwriter, who also won the Jnanpith Award for his novel Randamoozham, a retelling of the Mahabharata from the perspective of Bhima.
  - Kamala Surayya, a poet and memoirist, who wrote in both English and Malayalam, and is known for her controversial and confessional style of writing.
  - Vaikom Muhammad Basheer, a novelist and short story writer, who is regarded as one of the pioneers of modern Malayalam literature, and is known for his humorous and realistic portrayal of the common people.
  - Thakazhi Sivasankara Pillai, a novelist and short story writer, who won the Jnanpith Award for his novel Kayar, which depicts the life of a fishing community in Kerala.
  - M. Mukundan, a novelist and short story writer, who is known for his novels based on the history and culture of Mahe, a former French colony in Kerala.
  - K. Satchidanandan, a poet and critic, who is one of the leading figures of modern Malayalam poetry, and is known for his innovative and experimental style of writing.
  - N. S. Madhavan, a novelist and short story writer, who is known for his works that explore the themes of identity, migration, and globalization.
