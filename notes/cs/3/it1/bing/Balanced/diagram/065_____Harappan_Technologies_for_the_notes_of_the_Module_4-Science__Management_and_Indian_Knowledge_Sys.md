### Harappan Technologies

- The Harappan civilization, also known as the Indus Valley civilization, was a Bronze Age civilization that flourished in the northwest of the Indian subcontinent from about 2500 BCE to 1900 BCE.
- The Harappans developed various technologies and crafts, such as metallurgy, seal carving, pottery, jewelry, weights and measures, urban planning, drainage systems, and writing.
- Some of the notable features of Harappan technologies are:

  - Metallurgy: The Harappans used copper, bronze, lead, and tin to make tools, weapons, vessels, and ornaments. They knew how to alloy copper with tin to make bronze, which is harder and more durable than copper. They also knew how to cast metal by using the lost-wax process, which involves making a wax model of the desired object, covering it with clay, heating it to melt the wax, and then pouring molten metal into the hollow mold.
  - Seal carving: The Harappans carved distinctive seals, usually made of steatite, a soft stone, and sometimes of terracotta, metal, or ivory. The seals were used to stamp clay on trade goods, to identify property, and possibly to serve as amulets or tokens of authority. The seals often had animal motifs, such as the unicorn, the bull, the elephant, the tiger, and the rhinoceros, and sometimes human or divine figures. The seals also had a short inscription in a script that has not been deciphered yet.
  - Pottery: The Harappans made pottery of various shapes, sizes, and colors, using the wheel and the kiln. The pottery was mainly plain or painted with geometric designs, floral motifs, or animal figures. The pottery was used for domestic and ritual purposes, as well as for trade and storage. Some of the common types of pottery were dishes, bowls, jars, vases, goblets, and perforated vessels.
  - Jewelry: The Harappans made jewelry of gold, silver, copper, bronze, ivory, shell, bone, and beads of stone, glass, or faience. They used techniques such as soldering, wire drawing, granulation, and filigree to create intricate and elegant designs. The jewelry was worn by men and women, and included necklaces, bracelets, bangles, earrings, rings, pendants, and head ornaments.
  - Weights and measures: The Harappans developed a system of standardized weights and measures, based on a binary and decimal system. They used cubical or spherical weights of stone, chert, or metal, ranging from 0.05 grams to 500 grams, and corresponding to the ratio of 1:2:4:8:16:32:64. They also used linear measures of ivory, shell, or metal, marked with decimal subdivisions. The weights and measures were used for trade, taxation, and administration, and show a high degree of accuracy and uniformity.
  - Urban planning: The Harappans built well-planned cities, such as Harappa, Mohenjo-daro, Lothal, Kalibangan, and Dholavira, with a grid layout, fortified walls, and gateways. The cities had public buildings, such as granaries, warehouses, baths, and assembly halls, as well as private houses, shops, and workshops. The houses were made of baked bricks, and had flat roofs, courtyards, and bathrooms. The cities also had a sophisticated drainage system, with covered drains, manholes, and soak pits, to dispose of wastewater and sewage.
