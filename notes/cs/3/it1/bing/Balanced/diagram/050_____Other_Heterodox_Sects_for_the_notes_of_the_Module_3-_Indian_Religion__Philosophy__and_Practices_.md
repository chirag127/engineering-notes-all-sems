### Other Heterodox Sects

- Heterodox sects are those that do not accept the authority of the Vedas and the Brahmanical rituals and practices.
- They emerged in the 6th century BCE as a reaction to the rigid and complex Vedic religion, the social inequalities, and the need for new ethical and spiritual teachings.
- Some of the heterodox sects are:

  - **Ajivika**: Founded by Makkhali Gosala, this sect believed in fatalism, that everything is predetermined by fate and human actions have no effect on it . They also rejected the concepts of karma, rebirth, and liberation.
  - **Lokayata**: Also known as Charvaka, this sect advocated materialism, that only matter is real and there is no soul, god, or life after death. They also rejected the concepts of morality, duty, and virtue, and promoted hedonism, that pleasure is the only goal of life.
  - **Nityavadi**: This sect held the view that everything is eternal and unchanging, and that there is no creation or destruction. They also denied the existence of god, soul, and causation.
  - **Sandehavadi**: This sect expressed doubt or agnosticism about everything, and refused to accept any doctrine or authority. They also questioned the validity of perception, inference, and testimony as sources of knowledge.
  - **Akriyavadi**: This sect denied the efficacy of action, and argued that human actions have no consequences or rewards. They also rejected the concepts of karma, dharma, and moksha, and advocated indifference and inaction.
