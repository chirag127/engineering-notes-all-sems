- Indian architecture is rooted in the history, culture, and religion of India. It reflects the diversity and richness of Indian civilization and its interactions with other cultures.
- Indian architecture can be classified into several styles and traditions, such as Hindu temple architecture, Indo-Islamic architecture, South Indian architecture, Indo-Saracenic architecture, and modern architecture.
- Hindu temple architecture is characterized by the use of stone, brick, or wood to create elaborate structures with towers, domes, halls, and sanctuaries. The temples are often decorated with sculptures, carvings, and paintings depicting gods, goddesses, and mythological scenes. The temples are also aligned with the cardinal directions and follow the principles of vastu shastra, the ancient science of architecture.
- Indo-Islamic architecture is the result of the fusion of Islamic and Hindu elements, especially during the medieval period. It includes the styles of Rajput architecture, Mughal architecture, and Deccan architecture. Some of the features of Indo-Islamic architecture are the use of arches, domes, minarets, calligraphy, geometric patterns, and floral motifs. Some of the famous examples of Indo-Islamic architecture are the Taj Mahal, the Red Fort, and the Qutub Minar.
- South Indian architecture is influenced by the Dravidian culture and the Pallava, Chola, Chera, and Pandya dynasties. It is distinguished by the use of granite, sandstone, and soapstone to create massive temples with pyramidal towers, pillared halls, and sculpted walls. The temples are often surrounded by walls and moats and have elaborate gateways called gopurams. Some of the notable examples of South Indian architecture are the Brihadeeswarar Temple, the Meenakshi Temple, and the Hampi complex.
- Indo-Saracenic architecture is a hybrid style that combines elements of Indian, Islamic, and Gothic architecture. It emerged during the British colonial rule in India and was used to create public buildings, palaces, and monuments. Some of the characteristics of Indo-Saracenic architecture are the use of domes, arches, spires, turrets, balconies, and stained glass windows. Some of the prominent examples of Indo-Saracenic architecture are the Victoria Memorial, the Gateway of India, and the Chhatrapati Shivaji Terminus.
- Modern architecture in India is influenced by the global trends and movements of the 20th and 21st centuries, such as the Bauhaus, the International Style, the Brutalism, and the Postmodernism. It also incorporates local materials, techniques, and traditions to create innovative and sustainable designs. Some of the leading figures of modern architecture in India are Le Corbusier, Charles Correa, B.V. Doshi, and Raj Rewal. Some of the examples of modern architecture in India are the Chandigarh Capitol Complex, the Lotus Temple, and the IIM Ahmedabad.
