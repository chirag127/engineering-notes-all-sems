### Āshrama or the Stages of Life

- Āshrama (Sanskrit: आश्रम) is a system of stages of life discussed in Hindu texts of the ancient and medieval eras.
- The four āshramas are: Brahmacharya (student), Gṛhastha (householder), Vanaprastha (forest walker/forest dweller), and Sannyasa (renunciate)  .
- The Āshrama system is one facet of the Dharma concept in Hinduism  .
- The Āshrama system is believed to have been prevalent since the 5th century B.C.E. in Hindu society.
- The Āshrama system aims to guide the individual through four stages of spiritual development, each with its own duties, responsibilities, and goals  .
- The four āshramas are briefly described below:

  - Brahmacharya: This is the stage of the celibate student, marked by chastity, devotion, and obedience to one’s teacher. It lasts until around age 25, during which the student learns the Vedas, the sacred scriptures of Hinduism, and other subjects. The goal of this stage is to acquire knowledge and discipline .
  - Gṛhastha: This is the stage of the householder, requiring marriage, the begetting of children, sustaining one’s family and society, and performing rituals. It lasts until around age 50, during which the householder fulfills his or her worldly desires and duties. The goal of this stage is to enjoy and share the fruits of one’s actions .
  - Vanaprastha: This is the stage of the forest walker or forest dweller, marked by withdrawal from worldly attachments, renunciation of material possessions, and pursuit of spiritual goals. It lasts until around age 75, during which the forest dweller lives a simple and austere life in the wilderness or a hermitage, meditates, and studies the scriptures. The goal of this stage is to prepare for liberation from the cycle of rebirth .
  - Sannyasa: This is the stage of the renunciate, marked by complete detachment from worldly affairs, identification with the universal self, and realization of the ultimate truth. It lasts until death, during which the renunciate lives a life of wandering, begging, and teaching. The goal of this stage is to attain moksha, or liberation from the cycle of rebirth .

- The Āshrama system is not rigid or compulsory, but rather a flexible and optional framework for spiritual growth. Not everyone follows the four stages in the same order or duration, and some may skip or combine some stages. The Āshrama system is also influenced by factors such as caste, gender, and historical context .
- The Āshrama system is one of the distinctive features of Hinduism, and reflects its diversity and pluralism. It recognizes that different people have different needs and aspirations at different stages of life, and provides a way for them to harmonize their individual and social duties with their spiritual goals .
