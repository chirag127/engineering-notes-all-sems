### Martial Arts Traditions

- Martial arts are systems of codified practices and traditions of training for combat. They are used for self-defense, physical fitness, mental and spiritual development, entertainment, and cultural preservation.
- India has an ancient and diverse tradition of martial arts. Some of the most prominent and ancient martial art forms of India are:

  - **Kalaripayattu**: It is one of the oldest martial arts in the world, originating from Kerala. It involves the use of weapons, such as swords, daggers, spears, shields, and bows, as well as unarmed techniques, such as kicks, strikes, locks, and throws. It also incorporates elements of yoga, meditation, and healing. It is considered the mother of all martial arts, as it influenced the development of karate and kung-fu in China and Japan .
  - **Silambam**: It is a martial art that originated in Tamil Nadu, and has been mentioned in the Sangam literature. It involves the use of a long bamboo staff, called a silambam, as well as other weapons, such as knives, swords, and spears. It also includes unarmed techniques, such as punches, kicks, and blocks. It is known for its fluid and graceful movements, and its emphasis on speed, agility, and accuracy .
  - **Kushti**: It is a form of traditional wrestling that developed during the Mughal era, by combining the local sport of Malla Yuddha with the Persian sport of Varzesh-e-Bastani. It involves grappling, takedowns, pins, and submissions, as well as striking with the hands, feet, elbows, and knees. It is practiced on a circular earthen pit, called an akhara, and the wrestlers wear a loincloth, called a langot. It is a popular sport and cultural activity in many parts of India, especially in Maharashtra, Punjab, Haryana, and Uttar Pradesh .
  - **Gatka**: It is a martial art that originated in Punjab, and is associated with the Sikh warriors. It involves the use of weapons, such as swords, daggers, axes, and chakrams, as well as sticks, called soti, that simulate the movements of the sword. It also includes unarmed techniques, such as kicks, punches, and locks. It is performed as a ritual, a sport, and a self-defense method, and is often accompanied by music and chanting .
  - **Thang Ta**: It is a martial art that originated in Manipur, and is also known as Huyen Langlon. It involves the use of weapons, such as swords, spears, and shields, as well as unarmed techniques, such as kicks, strikes, and blocks. It is based on the ancient warfare and hunting traditions of the Meitei people, and is influenced by their animistic beliefs and rituals. It is practiced as a cultural and spiritual activity, as well as a sport and a self-defense method .
  - **Thoda**: It is a martial art that originated in Himachal Pradesh, and is also known as the dance of the arrows. It involves the use of bows and arrows, as well as wooden sticks, called thodas, that are used to deflect the arrows. It is based on the mythological battles between the gods and the demons, and is performed as a festival and a sport. It is a test of skill, accuracy, and courage, and is a symbol of harmony and brotherhood among the participants .

- These are some of the martial arts traditions that reflect the rich and diverse cultural heritage and performing arts of India. They are not only forms of physical exercise, but also expressions of identity, history, and values. They are sources of pride, joy, and inspiration for many Indians, and are worthy of preservation and promotion.
