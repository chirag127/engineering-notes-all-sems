## Module 4-Science, Management and Indian Knowledge System

This module covers the following topics:

- Astronomy in India: The ancient Indian astronomers made significant contributions to the fields of observational astronomy, mathematical astronomy, cosmology, and astrology. Some of the notable achievements include the discovery of the heliocentric model, the calculation of the sidereal year, the estimation of the diameter of the Earth, the concept of zero, and the use of decimal system .
- Chemistry in India: The ancient Indian chemists developed various techniques and methods for the extraction, purification, and synthesis of metals, minerals, dyes, medicines, perfumes, and cosmetics. They also invented the process of distillation, fermentation, and crystallization. Some of the important works in this field are Rasaratnasamuccaya, Rasendra Sara Sangraha, and Rasarnava .
- Mathematics in India: The ancient Indian mathematicians made remarkable contributions to the fields of arithmetic, algebra, geometry, trigonometry, calculus, and combinatorics. They invented the concepts of zero, negative numbers, fractions, decimals, and irrational numbers. They also developed theorems and formulas for solving various problems, such as the Pythagorean theorem, the value of pi, the area of a circle, the binomial theorem, and the Fibonacci sequence .
- Physics in India: The ancient Indian physicists explored the nature of matter, energy, force, motion, sound, light, and magnetism. They proposed theories and models for explaining various phenomena, such as the atomic theory, the theory of gravity, the theory of relativity, the wave theory of light, and the concept of ether. They also invented devices and instruments for measuring and experimenting, such as the balance, the sundial, the water clock, and the telescope .
- Agriculture in India: The ancient Indian agriculturists developed a sophisticated system of farming, irrigation, soil conservation, and crop rotation. They cultivated a variety of crops, such as rice, wheat, barley, millet, pulses, cotton, sugarcane, spices, and fruits. They also domesticated animals, such as cows, buffaloes, horses, elephants, camels, sheep, goats, and chickens. They also practiced animal husbandry, veterinary science, and dairy production .
- Medicine in India: The ancient Indian physicians and surgeons developed a holistic system of medicine, known as Ayurveda, which means the science of life. They classified the human body into three doshas (humors), seven dhatus (tissues), and three malas (wastes). They diagnosed diseases based on the symptoms, pulse, urine, and stool. They prescribed treatments based on the principles of diet, exercise, hygiene, and herbal remedies. They also performed various surgical procedures, such as cataract surgery, rhinoplasty, lithotomy, and caesarean section .
- Metallurgy in India: The ancient Indian metallurgists mastered the art and science of extracting, refining, and alloying metals, such as iron, copper, bronze, gold, silver, and zinc. They also developed methods for casting, forging, welding, and tempering metals. They produced various objects and artifacts, such as coins, jewelry, weapons, tools, statues, and pillars. They also invented the process of making steel, known as wootz or Damascus steel .
- Geography in India: The ancient Indian geographers studied the physical features, climate, flora, fauna, and resources of the Earth. They also mapped the continents, oceans, rivers, mountains, and islands. They divided the Earth into seven dvipas (islands) and nine khandas (regions). They also calculated the circumference and diameter of the Earth, the distance between the Earth and the Moon, and the length of the day and night .
- Biology in India: The ancient Indian biologists investigated the structure, function, and classification of living organisms. They identified and named various plants, animals, and microorganisms. They also studied the anatomy, physiology, embryology, and genetics of humans and animals. They also experimented with artificial insemination, hybridization, and cloning .
- Harappan Technologies: The Harappan civilization, also known as the Indus Valley civilization, was one of the earliest urban civilizations in the world.
