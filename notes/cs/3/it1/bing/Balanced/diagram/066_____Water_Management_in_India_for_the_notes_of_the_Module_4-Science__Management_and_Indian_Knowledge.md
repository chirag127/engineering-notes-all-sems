### Water Management in India

Water management is the planning, development, allocation, utilization, and conservation of water resources for various purposes such as drinking, irrigation, industry, energy, and environment. Water management is essential for ensuring water security, health, and economic growth in India, which faces multiple challenges such as water scarcity, pollution, overexploitation, floods, and droughts. Some of the key aspects of water management in India are:

- Providing drinking water to rural areas: The government has been responsible for providing drinking water to 833 million rural people in some 640,000 villages across the country. However, many rural areas still lack access to safe and adequate water supply due to poor infrastructure, low coverage, and poor quality. The World Bank has supported several projects to improve rural water supply and sanitation, such as the Swajal Project, the National Rural Drinking Water Program, and the Jal Jeevan Mission. These projects aim to increase the coverage, quality, and sustainability of rural water services, and to promote community participation, decentralization, and convergence with other schemes.

- Groundwater management: Over the past few decades, groundwater extraction has risen exponentially across India. India is now the world's largest user of groundwater, accounting for about 25% of the global groundwater use. Groundwater is the main source of irrigation and drinking water for most of the population, especially in the dry and semi-arid regions. However, excessive and unregulated pumping of groundwater has led to declining water tables, depletion of aquifers, deterioration of water quality, and increased energy consumption. The World Bank has supported several initiatives to improve groundwater management, such as the Hydrology Project, the National Hydrology Project, and the Atal Bhujal Yojana. These initiatives aim to strengthen the data and information systems, institutional and policy frameworks, and community-based management of groundwater resources .

- Improving access to water on dry rainfed lands: About 60% of India's net sown area is rainfed, which means it depends on rainfall for irrigation. Rainfed agriculture contributes to about 40% of the country's food production and supports the livelihoods of millions of small and marginal farmers. However, rainfed areas face low and erratic rainfall, poor soil quality, low productivity, and high vulnerability to climate change. The World Bank has supported several projects to enhance water availability and productivity on rainfed lands, such as the Andhra Pradesh Drought Adaptation Initiative, the Karnataka Watershed Development Project, and the Maharashtra Project on Climate Resilient Agriculture. These projects aim to promote watershed development, soil and water conservation, rainwater harvesting, micro-irrigation, crop diversification, and climate-smart agriculture.

- Cleaning the Ganga: The Ganga is the most sacred and the longest river in India, which supports the lives and livelihoods of about 500 million people. However, the river is severely polluted by domestic, industrial, and agricultural wastes, which pose serious threats to the health and environment of the people and the ecosystems. The World Bank has supported the National Ganga River Basin Project, which aims to improve the management and restoration of the Ganga river basin. The project focuses on strengthening the institutional and regulatory framework, increasing the sewage treatment capacity, promoting industrial pollution control, and enhancing public participation and awareness.

- Managing floods and droughts: India is prone to frequent and severe floods and droughts, which cause huge losses of lives, livelihoods, and assets. Floods affect about 30 million people and 8 million hectares of land every year, while droughts affect about 50 million people and 40 million hectares of land every year. The World Bank has supported several projects to improve the preparedness and response to floods and droughts, such as the National Cyclone Risk Mitigation Project, the Bihar Kosi Basin Development Project, and the Maharashtra Drought Resilience Project. These projects aim to strengthen the early warning systems, disaster risk management plans, flood protection infrastructure, and drought mitigation measures.
