### Trade in Ancient India

- Trade in ancient India was an important source of commerce and cultural exchange since the Harappan civilization (c. 2600-1900 BCE)  .
- The Harappans traded gold, copper, ivory, beads, textiles, spices, and other goods with Mesopotamia, Egypt, Persia, and Central Asia .
- The Harappan civilization declined around 1900 BCE, but trade continued with the rise of new urban centers such as Pataliputra, Ujjain, Puhar, and Mathura .
- These cities were connected by a network of roads, rivers, and ports that facilitated trade within India and with other regions .
- Trade in ancient India was regulated by the state, guilds, and merchants, who followed various laws, customs, and conventions .
- Trade in ancient India was also influenced by religion, as Buddhism, Jainism, and Hinduism promoted trade ethics, pilgrimage, and patronage .
- Trade in ancient India reached its peak during the Mauryan (c. 321-185 BCE) and Gupta (c. 320-550 CE) empires, when India became a major exporter of textiles, spices, gems, metals, and other products to the Roman Empire, China, Southeast Asia, and Africa   .
- Trade in ancient India declined after the Gupta period, due to political instability, foreign invasions, and competition from other regions  .
