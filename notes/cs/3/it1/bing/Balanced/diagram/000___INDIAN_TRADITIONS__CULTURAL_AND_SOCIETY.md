# Indian Traditions, Culture and Society

India is a diverse and ancient country with a rich and varied heritage. India has been influenced by many different civilizations, religions, languages, and cultures over the centuries, and has also contributed to the world in many ways. India is home to more than 1.3 billion people, who belong to different ethnic groups, speak different languages, follow different religions, and practice different customs and traditions. India is also known for its artistic, scientific, philosophical, and spiritual achievements, as well as its vibrant and colorful festivals, cuisine, and clothing.

Some of the main aspects of Indian traditions, culture and society are:

- **Arts and Language:** India has a long and rich tradition of literature, poetry, music, dance, theater, painting, sculpture, and architecture. India has 22 official languages and hundreds of regional languages and dialects. The ancient Indian classical language, Sanskrit, was one of the earliest Indo-European languages to be developed, and is the source of many words in modern languages. Sanskrit is also the language of many Hindu scriptures, such as the Vedas, the Upanishads, and the Bhagavad Gita. India has produced many famous writers, poets, musicians, dancers, actors, painters, sculptors, and architects, such as Kalidasa, Rabindranath Tagore, Ravi Shankar, Rukmini Devi Arundale, Amitabh Bachchan, M.F. Husain, Raja Ravi Varma, and Le Corbusier .

- **Philosophy and Religion:** India is the birthplace of four major world religions: Hinduism, Buddhism, Jainism, and Sikhism. Hinduism is the oldest and the most widely followed religion in India, with about 80% of the population identifying as Hindus. Hinduism is a diverse and complex faith, with many gods and goddesses, sects and schools, rituals and practices, and beliefs and values. Hinduism is based on the concept of dharma, or the cosmic order and moral duty, and the cycle of rebirth and karma, or the law of cause and effect. Hinduism also teaches the concepts of moksha, or liberation from the cycle of rebirth, and yoga, or the union of the individual soul with the supreme soul. Buddhism, Jainism, and Sikhism are also based on the teachings of their respective founders, who were born in India: Gautama Buddha, Mahavira, and Guru Nanak. Buddhism teaches the Four Noble Truths and the Eightfold Path to attain nirvana, or the cessation of suffering. Jainism teaches the principles of ahimsa, or non-violence, anekantavada, or multiple perspectives, and aparigraha, or non-attachment. Sikhism teaches the principles of monotheism, equality, service, and justice. India also has a significant population of Muslims, Christians, Parsis, Jews, and other religious minorities, who have contributed to the diversity and harmony of Indian society  .

- **Food and Drink:** The cuisine of India varies considerably across its various regions and ethnic communities. Indian food is known for its use of spices, herbs, grains, pulses, vegetables, fruits, dairy products, and meat. Some of the common ingredients in Indian cuisine are rice, wheat, lentils, chickpeas, potatoes, onions, garlic, ginger, turmeric, cumin, coriander, chili, cardamom, saffron, and ghee. Some of the popular dishes in Indian cuisine are biryani, curry, dal, roti, naan, dosa, idli, samosa, chaat, pakora, tandoori, kebab, korma, paneer, raita, lassi, and halwa. India also has a variety of beverages, such as tea, coffee, milk, buttermilk, juice, and alcoholic drinks. Some of the traditional drinks in India are masala chai, filter coffee, badam milk, nimbu pani, aam panna, and thandai. India also has a rich and diverse culture of sweets, such as jalebi, gulab jamun, rasgulla, kheer, barfi, ladoo, and peda  .
