### Kingship in ancient India

- Kingship was a sovereignty over a territory by a king who functioned as its protector, a role which involved both secular and religious power.
- The origin of kingship is traced to the Vedic period, when the king was elected by the people or the nobles, and was assisted by a council of elders or priests .
- The king was expected to be a capable general, a just ruler, a patron of arts and culture, and a defender of dharma (righteousness) .
- The king performed various rituals and sacrifices to ensure the prosperity and welfare of his subjects, and to legitimize his authority  .
- The king was also considered to be a representative of the gods, and sometimes identified with a particular deity, such as Indra, Vishnu, or Shiva  .
- The concept and significance of kingship changed over time, influenced by the ideas of Jainism and Buddhism, which challenged the brahmanical supremacy and the divine right of the king  .
- The king also faced various challenges and threats from internal and external enemies, such as rebellions, invasions, and wars .
- Some of the famous kings and emperors of ancient India were Ajatasatru, Chandragupta Maurya, Ashoka, Samudragupta, Harshavardhana, and Vikramaditya .
