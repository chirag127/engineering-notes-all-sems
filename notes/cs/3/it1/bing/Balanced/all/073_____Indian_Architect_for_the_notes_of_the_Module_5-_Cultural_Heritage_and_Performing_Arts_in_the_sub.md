# Indian Architecture

- Indian architecture is the expression of the history, culture, and religion of India through various styles and traditions of building design and construction.
- Indian architecture can be classified into several periods and regions, such as ancient, medieval, colonial, and modern; and north, south, east, and west.
- Some of the best-known examples of Indian architecture are:
  - The rock-cut temples of Ajanta and Ellora, which are UNESCO World Heritage Sites and showcase the artistic and religious traditions of Buddhism, Hinduism, and Jainism .
  - The Hindu temple architecture, which is characterized by the use of stone, elaborate carvings, and geometric patterns, and consists of various styles such as Nagara, Dravida, Vesara, and Kalinga.
  - The Indo-Islamic architecture, which is the result of the fusion of Islamic and Indian elements, and includes the Rajput, Mughal, Deccan, and Bengal styles.
  - The South Indian architecture, which is influenced by the Dravidian culture and features gopurams (towering gateways), mandapas (pillared halls), and vimanas (pyramidal roofs).
  - The Indo-Saracenic architecture, which is a hybrid of Indian, Islamic, and Gothic styles, and was developed by the British colonial rulers in the 19th and 20th centuries.
- Some of the notable architectural monuments in India are:
  - The Taj Mahal, which is a white marble mausoleum built by the Mughal emperor Shah Jahan for his wife Mumtaz Mahal, and is considered one of the seven wonders of the world.
  - The Sun Temple at Konark, which is a 13th-century temple dedicated to the sun god Surya, and is famous for its intricate stone sculptures and chariot-shaped design.
  - The Lotus Temple, which is a 20th-century Baháʼí House of Worship, and is shaped like a lotus flower with 27 petals made of marble.
  - The Qutub Minar, which is a 12th-century minaret and the tallest brick tower in the world, and is part of a complex of Islamic monuments in Delhi.
  - The Victoria Memorial, which is a 20th-century marble building and museum, and is a tribute to Queen Victoria and the British Raj in Kolkata.
- Indian architecture is constantly evolving and adapting to the changing needs and aspirations of the society, and reflects the diversity and richness of the Indian culture and heritage.
