# Brahmi Script

- Brahmi script is one of the oldest writing systems in the world, and the ancestor of many modern scripts in South and Southeast Asia.
- Brahmi script was used to write various languages, such as Sanskrit, Prakrit, Pali, Tamil, and others, from the 3rd century BCE to the 6th century CE.
- Brahmi script is composed of syllabic characters, each representing a consonant followed by an inherent vowel. Diacritical marks are used to modify the vowel sound or indicate the absence of a vowel.
- Brahmi script is written from left to right, and has a horizontal line at the top of the characters, called the headstroke or shirorekha.
- Brahmi script has several regional and chronological variants, such as Kharosthi, Gandhari, Ashokan, Mauryan, Gupta, and others, which differ in the shape and number of characters, as well as the direction of writing.
- Brahmi script is the source of many later scripts, such as Devanagari, Bengali, Gujarati, Gurmukhi, Tamil, Telugu, Kannada, Malayalam, Sinhala, Thai, Lao, Khmer, and others.
- Brahmi script is also related to the Aramaic script, which influenced its development and gave rise to the Kharosthi script.
- Brahmi script is named after the Hindu goddess of writing and learning, Saraswati, who is also called Brahmi.
- Brahmi script is considered a sacred script in Hinduism, Buddhism, and Jainism, as it was used to write the scriptures and teachings of these religions.
- Brahmi script is a valuable source of historical and cultural information, as it records the inscriptions, edicts, coins, seals, manuscripts, and other artifacts of ancient India and its neighboring regions.
