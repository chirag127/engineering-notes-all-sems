# Malayalam Literature

- Malayalam literature is the body of writing in the Malayalam language, a South-Dravidian language spoken in the Indian state of Kerala .
- The earliest extant literary work in Malayalam is Ramacharitam, a narrative poem composed in the late 12th or early 13th century .
- The subsequent period witnessed the emergence of two distinct styles of literature: pattu (song) and manipravalam (mixed language) .
- Pattu literature was mainly composed in pure Malayalam, using native meters and vocabulary, and was influenced by folk songs and ballads .
- Manipravalam literature was a blend of Malayalam and Sanskrit, and was used to write mainly erotic poetry and courtly dramas .
- Some of the notable works of pattu literature are Thirunizhalmala, Ramakathapattu, and Krishnagatha.
- Some of the notable works of manipravalam literature are Unnunilisandesam, Sitaswayamvaram, and Champu Ramayanam.
- The modern period of Malayalam literature began in the late 19th century, with the rise of social reform movements, print media, and western education.
- The modern period saw the emergence of various genres such as novels, short stories, essays, biographies, travelogues, and translations.
- Some of the prominent writers of the modern period are Kumaran Asan, Vallathol Narayana Menon, Ulloor S. Parameswara Iyer, Kesavadev, Thakazhi Sivasankara Pillai, Vaikom Muhammad Basheer, M.T. Vasudevan Nair, O.V. Vijayan, Kamala Surayya, and M. Mukundan.
- Malayalam literature reflects the rich cultural and linguistic diversity of Kerala, and has contributed to the development of Indian literature as a whole .
- Malayalam literature is also known for its experimentation and innovation, as seen in the works of contemporary writers such as K. Satchidanandan, Anand, Paul Zacharia, Sarah Joseph, and Benyamin .
