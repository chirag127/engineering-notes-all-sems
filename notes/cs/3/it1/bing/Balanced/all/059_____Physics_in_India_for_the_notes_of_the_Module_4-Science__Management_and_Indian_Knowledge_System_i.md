# Physics in India

- Physics is the branch of science that deals with the nature and properties of matter and energy.
- India has a rich and ancient tradition of physics, dating back to the Vedic period (1500-500 BCE).
- Some of the notable contributions of Indian physicists are:

  - The concept of atomism, which states that all matter is composed of indivisible and indestructible atoms, was developed by the Vaisheshika school of philosophy, founded by Kanada (6th century BCE).
  - The concept of zero, which is essential for mathematics and physics, was invented by the Indian mathematicians around the 5th century CE.
  - The concept of shunya (emptiness or void), which is related to the quantum vacuum and the zero-point energy, was explored by the Buddhist and Jain philosophers, such as Nagarjuna (2nd century CE) and Umasvati (5th century CE).
  - The concept of anekantavada (many-sidedness or relativity), which is similar to the principle of complementarity in quantum mechanics, was proposed by the Jain philosopher Mahavira (6th century BCE).
  - The concept of prana (life force or vital energy), which is analogous to the concept of field in physics, was studied by the Yoga and Ayurveda schools of thought, such as Patanjali (2nd century BCE) and Charaka (2nd century CE).
  - The concept of chakras (energy centers or vortexes), which are related to the concept of vortices in fluid dynamics, was developed by the Tantra school of philosophy, such as Abhinavagupta (10th century CE).
  - The concept of shabda (sound or vibration), which is connected to the concept of wave in physics, was analyzed by the Mimamsa and Nyaya schools of logic, such as Kumarila Bhatta (7th century CE) and Uddyotakara (7th century CE).
  - The concept of rasa (essence or taste), which is linked to the concept of phase in physics, was investigated by the Natya Shastra (the science of drama), attributed to Bharata Muni (2nd century BCE).
  - The concept of guna (quality or attribute), which is similar to the concept of charge in physics, was elaborated by the Samkhya school of philosophy, founded by Kapila (6th century BCE).
  - The concept of karma (action or causation), which is comparable to the concept of force in physics, was expounded by the Vedanta school of philosophy, such as Shankara (8th century CE) and Ramanuja (11th century CE).

- In the modern era, India has produced many eminent physicists, such as:

  - Jagadish Chandra Bose (1858-1937), who pioneered the study of radio waves and plant physiology, and invented the crescograph, a device to measure the growth of plants.
  - Satyendra Nath Bose (1894-1974), who discovered the Bose-Einstein statistics and the Bose-Einstein condensate, and collaborated with Albert Einstein on the theory of the photon gas.
  - Meghnad Saha (1893-1956), who formulated the Saha ionization equation, which explains the spectral lines of stars and the solar corona, and founded the Indian Institute of Astrophysics.
  - C. V. Raman (1888-1970), who discovered the Raman effect, the scattering of light by molecules, and won the Nobel Prize in Physics in 1930.
  - Homi J. Bhabha (1909-1966), who proposed the Bhabha scattering, the interaction of electrons and positrons, and established the Tata Institute of Fundamental Research and the Atomic Energy Commission of India.
  - Subrahmanyan Chandrasekhar (1910-1995), who discovered the Chandrasekhar limit, the maximum mass of a white dwarf star, and won the Nobel Prize in Physics in 1983.
  - Vikram Sarabhai (1919-1971), who initiated the Indian space program and founded the Indian Space Research Organization and the Physical Research Laboratory.
  - Abdus Salam (1926-1996), who developed the electroweak theory and the Salam-Weinberg model, and won the Nobel Prize in Physics in 1979.
  - Raja Ramanna (1925-2004), who led the first nuclear test of India in 1974, and served as the director of the Bhabha Atomic Research Centre and the chairman of the Atomic Energy Commission of India.
