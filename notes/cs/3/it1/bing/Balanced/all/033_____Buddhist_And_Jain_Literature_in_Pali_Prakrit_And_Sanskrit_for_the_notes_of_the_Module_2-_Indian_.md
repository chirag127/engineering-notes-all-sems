# Buddhist and Jain Literature in Pali, Prakrit and Sanskrit

- Pali, Prakrit and Sanskrit are three languages that were used to write the religious and philosophical texts of Buddhism and Jainism in ancient India.
- Pali is a term that refers to an archaic or old form of Prakrit that combines several existing dialects. It was spoken in Magadha and South Bihar, and was the language of the earliest Buddhist works.
- Prakrit is a term that refers to any language other than the standard one, Sanskrit. It was spoken by the common people and used for various literary genres such as drama, poetry, epics and inscriptions. It was also the language of the Jain scriptures, which were written in Ardhamagadhi, a Prakrit dialect .
- Sanskrit is the classical and literary language of India, derived from the Vedic language. It was used for writing the later Buddhist and Jain texts, as well as the Hindu scriptures, epics, and scientific works.

## Buddhist Literature

- The Buddhist literature can be divided into two main categories: the canonical literature and the non-canonical literature.
- The canonical literature consists of the Tripitaka or the Three Baskets, which are the collections of the teachings of the Buddha and his disciples. They are:
  - The Vinaya Pitaka, which contains the rules and regulations for the monastic order.
  - The Sutta Pitaka, which contains the discourses and dialogues of the Buddha and his followers on various topics such as ethics, meditation, philosophy, and cosmology.
  - The Abhidhamma Pitaka, which contains the systematic and analytical exposition of the Buddhist doctrine and psychology.
- The canonical literature was originally transmitted orally, and was written down in Pali in the first century BCE in Sri Lanka. Later, it was translated into various languages such as Sanskrit, Chinese, Tibetan, and others  .
- The non-canonical literature consists of the works that were written after the canonical literature, and are not considered as authoritative by all the Buddhist schools. They include:
  - The commentaries and sub-commentaries on the canonical texts, which explain and interpret the teachings of the Buddha and his disciples. They were written in Pali, Sanskrit, and other languages by various scholars and monks.
  - The Jataka tales, which are stories of the previous lives of the Buddha and his associates, illustrating the moral and spiritual lessons of the Buddhist path. They were written in Pali and other languages, and are popular among the lay followers of Buddhism.
  - The Mahayana sutras, which are the scriptures of the Mahayana or the Great Vehicle school of Buddhism, which emerged around the first century CE. They emphasize the concepts of bodhisattva, emptiness, compassion, and skillful means. They were written in Sanskrit and other languages, and are revered by the Mahayana and Vajrayana traditions of Buddhism  .

## Jain Literature

- The Jain literature can be divided into two main categories: the canonical literature and the non-canonical literature.
- The canonical literature consists of the Agamas or the Scriptures, which are the collections of the teachings of the Tirthankaras or the enlightened teachers of Jainism. They are:
  - The Angas, which are the twelve primary texts that contain the core doctrines and practices of Jainism, such as karma, rebirth, non-violence, and liberation.
  - The Upangas, which are the twelve secondary texts that supplement the Angas, and deal with various topics such as cosmology, ethics, logic, and history.
  - The Prakirnakas, which are the ten miscellaneous texts that cover various aspects of Jainism, such as conduct, rituals, legends, and stories.
  - The Chhedasutras, which are the six texts that prescribe the rules and regulations for the ascetics and the lay followers of Jainism.
  - The Mulasutras, which are the four texts that summarize the essential teachings of Jainism, such as the five vows, the three jewels, and the six substances.
  - The Culikasutras, which are the two texts that contain the appendices and the glossaries of the Jain terms and concepts.
