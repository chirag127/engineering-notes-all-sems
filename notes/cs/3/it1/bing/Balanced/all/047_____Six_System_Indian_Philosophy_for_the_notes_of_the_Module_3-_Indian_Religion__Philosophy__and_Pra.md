# Six System Indian Philosophy

The six systems of Indian philosophy are Nyaya, Vaisheshika, Samkhya, Yoga, Purva Mimamsa and Vedanta. They are also known as shad-darshana, meaning six views or perspectives. Each system has its own founder, texts, concepts and methods of inquiry. They represent different aspects of the intellectual and spiritual traditions of Hinduism.

## Nyaya
- Nyaya is the school of logic and epistemology, founded by Akshapada Gautama in the 2nd century BCE.
- Nyaya means rule or method, and it aims to provide a rational and systematic way of acquiring valid knowledge and avoiding error.
- Nyaya accepts four sources of knowledge: perception, inference, comparison and testimony.
- Nyaya also develops a theory of categories, causation, argumentation, fallacies, syllogism and debate.
- Nyaya is often paired with Vaisheshika, as they share many concepts and principles.

## Vaisheshika
- Vaisheshika is the school of atomism and naturalism, founded by Kanada Kashyapa in the 6th century BCE.
- Vaisheshika means distinction or particularity, and it aims to explain the nature and diversity of reality in terms of atoms and their combinations.
- Vaisheshika accepts six categories of existence: substance, quality, action, generality, particularity and inherence.
- Vaisheshika also proposes a theory of atomism, which holds that all physical objects are composed of indivisible and eternal atoms of earth, water, fire and air.
- Vaisheshika is often paired with Nyaya, as they share many concepts and principles.

## Samkhya
- Samkhya is the school of dualism and analysis, founded by Kapila Muni in the 6th century BCE.
- Samkhya means enumeration or discrimination, and it aims to provide a rational and empirical account of the origin and evolution of the universe and the human condition.
- Samkhya accepts two fundamental realities: purusha (consciousness) and prakriti (matter), which are eternal, independent and opposite in nature.
- Samkhya also proposes a theory of evolution, which holds that prakriti manifests itself into various forms of existence through the interaction of three qualities: sattva (purity), rajas (activity) and tamas (inertia).
- Samkhya is often paired with Yoga, as they share many concepts and principles.

## Yoga
- Yoga is the school of discipline and practice, founded by Patanjali in the 2nd century BCE.
- Yoga means union or connection, and it aims to provide a practical and experiential way of attaining liberation and self-realization.
- Yoga accepts the dualism of Samkhya, but adds a third reality: Ishvara (God), who is the supreme purusha and the source of all knowledge and guidance.
- Yoga also prescribes a system of eight limbs or steps of practice: yama (restraints), niyama (observances), asana (postures), pranayama (breath control), pratyahara (withdrawal of senses), dharana (concentration), dhyana (meditation) and samadhi (absorption).
- Yoga is often paired with Samkhya, as they share many concepts and principles.

## Purva Mimamsa
- Purva Mimamsa is the school of ritual and exegesis, founded by Jaimini in the 3rd century BCE.
- Purva Mimamsa means earlier or first investigation, and it aims to provide a systematic and authoritative interpretation of the Vedas, the sacred scriptures of Hinduism.
- Purva Mimamsa accepts the Vedas as the eternal and infallible source of knowledge and duty, and rejects any other authority or revelation.
- Purva Mimamsa also develops a theory of language, meaning, inference, ritual, ethics and dharma (righteousness).
- Purva Mimamsa is often contrasted with Vedanta, as they have different views on the nature and purpose of the Vedas.
