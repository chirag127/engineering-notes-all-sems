# Pottery

Pottery is one of the most ancient and enduring forms of Indian art and culture. It is the craft of making objects from clay or other ceramic materials by shaping, molding, or firing them at high temperatures. Pottery has been used for various purposes, such as storing food and water, cooking, serving, decorating, and expressing religious beliefs. Pottery also reflects the diversity and richness of India's geography, history, and traditions.

Some of the main points to note about pottery in India are:

- Pottery in India dates back to the prehistoric times, as evidenced by the findings of pottery shards and vessels in the early settlements of Lahuradewa and Mehrgarh, and later the Indus Valley Civilization .
- Pottery in India has been influenced by various factors, such as the availability of raw materials, the climate, the trade and cultural contacts, the religious and social customs, and the artistic and aesthetic preferences of different regions and communities .
- Pottery in India can be broadly classified into two types: earthenware and stoneware. Earthenware is pottery that is porous and low-fired, while stoneware is pottery that is vitrified and high-fired. Earthenware is more common and widely used in India, while stoneware is more refined and specialized .
- Pottery in India can also be categorized based on the techniques, forms, functions, and decorations of the pottery. Some of the common techniques are hand-building, wheel-throwing, slip-casting, and molding. Some of the common forms are pots, bowls, plates, cups, jars, lamps, toys, figurines, and tiles. Some of the common functions are domestic, ritual, architectural, and artistic. Some of the common decorations are incising, stamping, painting, glazing, and applique .
- Pottery in India has a rich and diverse regional and local variation, reflecting the unique identity and creativity of the potters and their communities. Some of the famous and distinctive pottery traditions in India are:

  - Khurja pottery from Uttar Pradesh, known for its white clay body and colorful floral and geometric designs.
  - Khavda pottery from Gujarat, known for its terracotta body and white slip and red and black motifs.
  - Longpi pottery from Manipur, known for its black clay body and cane and bamboo embellishments .
  - Jaipur blue pottery from Rajasthan, known for its blue and white glazed body and Persian and Mughal influences .
  - Nizamabad black pottery from Uttar Pradesh, known for its glossy black body and silver incised patterns.
  - Andretta pottery from Himachal Pradesh, known for its stoneware body and artistic and contemporary designs.

Pottery in India is a living and evolving craft tradition that is still practiced extensively by thousands of potters across the country. Pottery in India is also a valuable cultural heritage that showcases the skill, innovation, and diversity of Indian art and society.
