# Puranas

- Puranas are a vast genre of Indian literature that deal with various topics, especially legends and traditional lore .
- Puranas are considered as one of the most ancient and important sources of Hindu culture, religion, history, and cosmology .
- Puranas are classified as Smriti (remembered) texts, as opposed to Shruti (heard) texts like the Vedas .
- Puranas are composed in Sanskrit verse, mostly in the form of narrative couplets, similar to the epics Mahabharata and Ramayana .
- Puranas are divided into two categories: Mahapuranas (major Puranas) and Upapuranas (minor Puranas) .
- There are 18 Mahapuranas, each focusing on a different aspect or manifestation of the Supreme Being, such as Vishnu, Shiva, or Shakti .
- There are also 18 Upapuranas, which are usually shorter and less authoritative than the Mahapuranas, and deal with various topics such as sects, regions, festivals, or sacred places .
- Puranas are not considered as authoritative or binding as the Vedas, but they are widely read and revered by the Hindus for their spiritual and moral guidance, as well as their cultural and historical information .
- Puranas are also known for their symbolic and allegorical interpretations of the events and characters, which often have multiple layers of meaning and significance .
- Puranas are dynamic and evolving texts, which reflect the changes and developments in the Hindu society and thought over the centuries .
