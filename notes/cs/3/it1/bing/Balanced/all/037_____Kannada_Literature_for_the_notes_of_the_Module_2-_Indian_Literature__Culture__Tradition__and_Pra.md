# Kannada Literature

- Kannada literature is one of the oldest and richest in India. It has a history of more than 1500 years and has produced many eminent writers, poets, and scholars.
- Kannada literature can be broadly divided into three periods: classical, medieval, and modern.
- The classical period (9th to 14th century) is marked by the emergence of the champu style, which combines prose and verse, and the development of various genres such as kavya (poetry), prabandha (narrative), and shastra (treatise). Some of the notable works of this period are Pampa's Adipurana and Vikramarjuna Vijaya, Ranna's Gadayuddha and Ajitanatha Purana, and Janna's Yashodhara Charite.
- The medieval period (14th to 18th century) is characterized by the growth of bhakti (devotional) literature, which expressed the religious and social sentiments of the masses. The vachana (sayings) and kirtana (songs) of the Veerashaiva saints, such as Basavanna, Akka Mahadevi, and Allama Prabhu, and the dasara pada (songs) of the Haridasa saints, such as Purandara Dasa and Kanaka Dasa, are the major examples of this genre. The medieval period also witnessed the rise of the navya (new) style, which introduced innovations in form and content, and the emergence of the dalita (oppressed) literature, which voiced the concerns of the marginalized sections of the society. Some of the prominent works of this period are Kumara Vyasa's Bharata, Lakshmisha's Jaimini Bharata, Sarvajna's Tripadi, and Ratnakaravarni's Bharatesha Vaibhava.
- The modern period (19th century onwards) is marked by the influence of Western literature and the social reform movements. The novel, the short story, the essay, and the drama became the dominant forms of expression. The modern period also saw the development of various literary movements, such as navodaya (renaissance), navya (modernist), bandaya (protest), dalita (oppressed), and spandana (responsive). Some of the notable works of this period are B.M. Srikantaiah's Kannada Sahitya Charitre, Kuvempu's Shri Ramayana Darshanam, Masti Venkatesha Iyengar's Chikaveera Rajendra, Shivarama Karanth's Chomana Dudi, U.R. Ananthamurthy's Samskara, Girish Karnad's Tughlaq, and P. Lankesh's Mussanjeya Katha Prasanga.
