# Painting

Painting is one of the oldest and most diverse forms of artistic expression in India. It reflects the rich cultural heritage and diversity of the country, as well as the influences of various historical periods, regions, religions, and dynasties.

Some of the main features and types of Indian painting are:

- Indian paintings can be broadly classified as **murals, miniatures and paintings on cloth**.
- Murals are large works executed on the walls of solid structures, such as temples, caves, palaces, and forts. They are usually done in fresco technique, using natural pigments and water. Some of the most famous examples of murals are the **Ajanta Caves** and the **Kailashnath temple** in Maharashtra, the **Brihadeshwara temple** in Tamil Nadu, and the **Lepakshi temple** in Andhra Pradesh.
- Miniatures are small paintings done on paper, palm leaf, wood, ivory, or metal. They are often used to illustrate manuscripts, books, or albums, and depict scenes from religious texts, history, literature, or court life. Some of the most prominent styles of miniatures are the **Pala**, **Mughal**, **Rajput**, **Deccan**, **Pahari**, and **Company** schools.
- Paintings on cloth are artworks done on fabric, such as cotton, silk, or jute. They are usually created as scrolls, banners, or hangings, and depict religious or folk themes. Some of the most popular forms of paintings on cloth are the **Kalamkari**, **Madhubani**, **Pattachitra**, **Phad**, **Warli**, and **Thangka** paintings.
- Indian paintings are influenced by various factors, such as the **geographical location**, the **religious beliefs**, the **social customs**, the **political events**, and the **foreign contacts** of the artists and patrons. For example, the paintings of the north and south India differ in their styles, motifs, and colors, reflecting the regional variations in climate, culture, and architecture. Similarly, the paintings of the Hindu, Buddhist, Jain, Islamic, and Sikh traditions show different aspects of their respective faiths, such as the depiction of gods, goddesses, saints, prophets, and gurus.
- Indian paintings are also known for their **use of vibrant colors**, **intricate details**, **symbolic elements**, **perspective techniques**, and **aesthetic principles**. The colors are often derived from natural sources, such as minerals, plants, and insects, and have specific meanings and associations. The details are used to convey the mood, emotion, and personality of the characters and the setting. The symbols are used to represent abstract concepts, such as the cosmic order, the cycle of life, and the divine attributes. The perspective techniques are used to create a sense of depth, distance, and dimension, such as the use of overlapping, foreshortening, and shading. The aesthetic principles are based on the concepts of **rasa** (the essence or flavor of art), **shilpa** (the skill or craft of art), and **dhvani** (the suggestion or implication of art).
