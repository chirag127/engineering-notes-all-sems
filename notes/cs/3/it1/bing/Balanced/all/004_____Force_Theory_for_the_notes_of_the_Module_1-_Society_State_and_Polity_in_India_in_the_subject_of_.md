# Force Theory

- The force theory is one of the theories that explain the origin of the state.
- According to this theory, the state emerged as a result of the conquest and subjugation of one group of people by another.
- The victorious group imposed its will and authority on the vanquished group and established a political organization to maintain its domination.
- The state, therefore, is based on force, violence and coercion, and not on consent, agreement or contract.
- The force theory is supported by some historical examples, such as the formation of the Roman Empire, the Mongol Empire, the British Empire, etc.
- The force theory is criticized by some scholars, who argue that it is too simplistic and ignores the role of other factors, such as culture, religion, law, economy, etc., in the evolution of the state.
- The force theory also fails to account for the diversity and complexity of the modern states, which are not merely the products of military conquest, but also of social contract, popular sovereignty, nationalism, democracy, etc.
