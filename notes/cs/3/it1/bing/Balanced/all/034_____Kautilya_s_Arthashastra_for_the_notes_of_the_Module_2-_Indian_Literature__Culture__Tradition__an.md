# Kautilya's Arthashastra

- Kautilya's Arthashastra is an ancient Indian treatise on statecraft, economics, military strategy, and social organization .
- It is attributed to Kautilya, also known as Chanakya, Vishnugupta, or the Machiavelli of India, who was a scholar at Taxila University and the prime minister of the Mauryan Empire under Chandragupta Maurya  .
- The Arthashastra was written around the 4th century BCE, but was lost for centuries until it was rediscovered by Rudrapatna Shamasastry in 1905 CE .
- The Arthashastra consists of 15 books, 150 chapters, and 180 topics, covering various aspects of governance, administration, diplomacy, law, trade, agriculture, mining, taxation, welfare, defense, espionage, and war  .
- The Arthashastra is considered a masterpiece of political realism, pragmatism, and utilitarianism, as it advocates for the use of any means necessary to achieve the welfare and security of the state, without regard for ethical or moral considerations  .
- The Arthashastra also shows compassion for the poor, the slaves, and the women, and provides guidelines for the protection of the environment, the preservation of culture, and the promotion of education and science  .
- The Arthashastra is relevant for the modern world, as it offers insights into the principles and practices of statecraft, economics, and diplomacy, and provides a comprehensive and systematic analysis of the various factors that influence the success and failure of a state  .
