# Northern Indian Languages & Literature

- Northern Indian languages are derived from the Indo-Aryan branch of the Indo-European language family, which evolved from the ancient Sanskrit and Prakrit languages  .
- The major Northern Indian languages are Hindi, Urdu, Punjabi, Kashmiri, Sindhi, Gujarati, Marathi, Bengali, Assamese, Oriya, Nepali, and Bihari .
- The literature of these languages is influenced by the ancient Indian background, which includes the Sanskrit epics, the Puranas, the Bhakti movement, the Islamic culture, and the colonial period  .
- Some of the prominent features of Northern Indian literature are:
  - The use of regional dialects, folk songs, and oral traditions  .
  - The expression of religious devotion, social reform, nationalism, and humanism  .
  - The development of various genres, such as poetry, drama, novel, short story, essay, biography, and autobiography   .
  - The contribution of many eminent writers, such as Kabir, Tulsidas, Mirabai, Guru Nanak, Surdas, Amir Khusrau, Kalidasa, Bhartrihari, Jayadeva, Rabindranath Tagore, Premchand, Sarat Chandra Chattopadhyay, Bankim Chandra Chatterjee, and many others   .
- Northern Indian languages and literature reflect the rich and diverse cultural heritage of India, as well as the historical and contemporary issues faced by the people     .
