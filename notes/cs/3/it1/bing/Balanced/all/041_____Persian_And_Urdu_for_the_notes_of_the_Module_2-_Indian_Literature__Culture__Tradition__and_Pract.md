# Persian and Urdu

- Persian and Urdu are two languages that have influenced the literary and cultural traditions of India, especially in the northern and central regions.
- Persian was the official language of the Mughal Empire, which ruled over most of India from the 16th to the 18th centuries. Persian was also the language of court, administration, literature, and art in many regional kingdoms and sultanates that emerged after the decline of the Mughals.
- Urdu is a language that developed from the interaction of Persian, Arabic, and local Indian languages, such as Hindi, Punjabi, and Sindhi. Urdu is written in a modified form of the Arabic script, while Hindi is written in the Devanagari script. Urdu and Hindi are mutually intelligible in their spoken forms, but differ in their literary and religious vocabularies.
- Persian and Urdu have contributed to the richness and diversity of Indian literature, culture, tradition, and practices in various ways, such as:

  - Persian and Urdu poets, such as Amir Khusrau, Mirza Ghalib, Allama Iqbal, and Faiz Ahmed Faiz, have composed works of poetry that express the themes of love, mysticism, nationalism, and social justice. Their poetry has inspired generations of Indian writers and artists, and has been translated into many Indian and foreign languages.
  - Persian and Urdu have also influenced the genres and forms of Indian literature, such as the ghazal, the qawwali, the masnavi, the dastan, and the rekhta. These are forms of lyrical, musical, narrative, and hybrid expressions that combine elements of poetry, prose, and oral storytelling.
  - Persian and Urdu have also enriched the vocabulary and style of many Indian languages, such as Hindi, Gujarati, Marathi, Bengali, and Telugu. Many words of Persian and Urdu origin are commonly used in everyday speech, as well as in literary and academic contexts. Some examples are: adab (manners), afsana (story), azaadi (freedom), bazaar (market), darbar (court), fanaa (annihilation), ghazal (lyric poem), hukum (order), imaan (faith), jannat (paradise), khushboo (fragrance), mausam (season), nawaazish (kindness), qalam (pen), riyaz (practice), shayari (poetry), taraana (song), ummeed (hope), waqt (time), and zindagi (life).
  - Persian and Urdu have also influenced the cultural and artistic traditions of India, such as music, painting, architecture, calligraphy, cuisine, and clothing. Some examples are: the Hindustani classical music, which incorporates Persian and Urdu elements in its ragas, talas, and compositions; the Mughal and Deccani miniature paintings, which depict scenes from Persian and Urdu literature, history, and mythology; the Indo-Islamic architecture, which combines Persian and Indian styles in its domes, arches, minarets, and gardens; the Nastaliq and Naskh scripts, which are used for writing Persian and Urdu in elegant and artistic ways; the Mughlai and Awadhi cuisines, which blend Persian and Indian ingredients and techniques in their dishes, such as biryani, kebab, korma, and halwa; and the sherwani and salwar kameez, which are types of clothing that originated from Persian and Urdu influences.
