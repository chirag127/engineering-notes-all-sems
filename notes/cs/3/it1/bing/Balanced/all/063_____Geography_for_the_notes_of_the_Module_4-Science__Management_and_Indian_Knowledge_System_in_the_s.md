# Geography

- Geography is the study of the physical features of the earth and its atmosphere, and of human activity as it affects and is affected by these features.
- Geography has two main branches: physical geography and human geography.
- Physical geography deals with the natural phenomena of the earth, such as climate, landforms, water, soil, vegetation, and wildlife.
- Human geography deals with the spatial aspects of human society and culture, such as population, settlement, urbanization, agriculture, industry, trade, and geopolitics.
- Geography is an interdisciplinary subject that draws on various fields of knowledge, such as history, economics, sociology, anthropology, and environmental science.
- Geography is also a part of the Indian knowledge system, which has a long and rich tradition of observing, mapping, and understanding the earth and its resources.
- Some of the ancient Indian texts that deal with geography are the Vedas, the Puranas, the Ramayana, the Mahabharata, the Arthashastra, and the Brihat Samhita.
- Some of the prominent Indian geographers who contributed to the development of geography as a science are Varahamihira, Aryabhata, Brahmagupta, Bhaskara, Alberuni, and Raja Todar Mal.
- Some of the concepts and themes that are unique to the Indian geography are the following:
  - The idea of Bharatavarsha, or the land of Bharata, which is a cultural and spiritual entity that encompasses the Indian subcontinent and beyond.
  - The concept of Pancha Mahabhuta, or the five elements, which are earth, water, fire, air, and ether, and which form the basis of the Indian cosmology and ecology.
  - The notion of Chaturvarna, or the four-fold division of society, which is based on the principle of varna, or the innate quality and aptitude of a person, and which influences the spatial distribution and occupation of people.
  - The classification of regions according to the six seasons, or ritus, which are vasanta (spring), grishma (summer), varsha (monsoon), sharad (autumn), hemanta (winter), and shishira (pre-spring), and which reflect the diversity and richness of the Indian climate and culture.
  - The concept of sacred geography, which is the belief that certain places, such as mountains, rivers, forests, and temples, are endowed with divine power and significance, and which inspire reverence and pilgrimage among the people.
