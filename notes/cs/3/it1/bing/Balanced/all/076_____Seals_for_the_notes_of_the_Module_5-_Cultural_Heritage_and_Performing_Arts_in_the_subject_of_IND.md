# Seals for the notes of the Module 5- Cultural Heritage and Performing Arts in the subject of INDIAN TRADITIONS, CULTURAL AND SOCIETY

- Seals are engraved objects that are used to imprint a mark or a symbol on another surface, such as clay, wax, or paper.
- Seals have been used in various cultures and civilizations for different purposes, such as trade, identification, authentication, communication, and ritual.
- Seals are part of the cultural heritage and performing arts of India, as they reflect the artistic, religious, social, and economic aspects of the Indian people throughout history.
- Some of the types and examples of seals in Indian culture are:

  - **Indus seals**: These are small, square or rectangular seals made of steatite, a soft stone, that were used by the people of the Indus Valley Civilization (c. 2500-1900 BCE). They have an animal motif on one side and a script on the other, which is still undeciphered. The animals include buffaloes, rhinoceroses, elephants, unicorns, and composite creatures. The seals may have been used for trade, administration, or religious purposes  .
  - **Mauryan seals**: These are circular or oval seals made of terracotta, copper, or ivory that were used by the Mauryan Empire (c. 322-185 BCE). They have an inscription in Brahmi script on one side and a symbol or an image on the other, such as the lion capital, the bull, the elephant, or the peacock. The seals may have been used for royal authority, taxation, or diplomacy.
  - **Gupta seals**: These are round or rectangular seals made of copper, silver, or gold that were used by the Gupta Empire (c. 320-550 CE). They have an inscription in Sanskrit on one side and a portrait or a deity on the other, such as the king, the goddess Lakshmi, or the god Vishnu. The seals may have been used for political propaganda, religious patronage, or personal identification.
  - **Temple seals**: These are seals that are associated with Hindu temples and their rituals. They are usually made of metal, stone, or clay and have an image of a deity or a sacred symbol on one side and an inscription on the other. The seals may have been used for worship, donation, or protection.
  - **Islamic seals**: These are seals that are influenced by the Islamic culture and religion that came to India from the 12th century onwards. They are usually made of metal, stone, or glass and have an inscription in Arabic or Persian on one side and a floral or geometric design on the other. The seals may have been used for official documents, personal letters, or seals of ownership.
  - **British seals**: These are seals that are introduced by the British colonial rule in India from the 18th century onwards. They are usually made of metal, wax, or paper and have an inscription in English or a local language on one side and a coat of arms or a monogram on the other. The seals may have been used for legal, administrative, or commercial purposes.

- Seals are important sources of information and evidence for the study of Indian traditions, culture, and society, as they reveal the artistic styles, linguistic diversity, religious beliefs, political systems, economic activities, and social relations of the Indian people over time.
