# Engineering and Architecture in Ancient India

- Engineering and architecture in ancient India was known as **Sthapatya-Kala**, which means the art of constructing something.
- Ancient Indian architects and engineers derived principles of construction and architecture based on experience, observation of natural phenomena and considering social and cultural aspects of India.
- Ancient India witnessed two urban revolutions: the first one in the Harappan civilization (2500-1900 BCE) and the second one in the Mauryan and Kushan empires (6th-3rd centuries BCE).
- The Harappan civilization is known for its urban planning, drainage systems, granaries, baths, docks and monumental structures like the Great Bath and the Great Stupa.
- The Mauryan and Kushan empires are known for their stone pillars, rock-cut caves, stupas, chaityas, viharas and temples .
- Ancient India also developed various styles of temple architecture, such as the Nagara style in North India and the Dravida style in South India.
- The Nagara style features a shikhara (spire) as the main element, while the Dravida style features a vimana (tower) as the main element.
- Some of the famous examples of ancient Indian temple architecture are the Kailasa Temple at Ellora, the Khajuraho Temples, the Brihadeshwara Temple at Thanjavur and the Sun Temple at Konark .
- Ancient India also produced engineering marvels such as the stepwells, the iron pillar of Delhi, the Ajanta and Ellora caves, the Konark wheel and the Jantar Mantar observatories .
- These engineering and architectural achievements of ancient India reflect the scientific, artistic and cultural excellence of the ancient Indian civilization.
