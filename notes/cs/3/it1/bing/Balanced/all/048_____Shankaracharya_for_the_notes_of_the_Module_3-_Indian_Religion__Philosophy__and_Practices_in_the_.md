# Shankaracharya

- Shankaracharya is a title given to the heads of monastic institutions (mathas) in the Advaita Vedanta tradition of Hinduism. The title means "teacher of the way of Shankara", referring to the 8th-century philosopher and theologian Adi Shankara, who is considered the founder of the Advaita Vedanta school and the reviver of Hinduism in India.  
- Adi Shankara was born in a Brahmin family in Kaladi, Kerala, around the 8th century CE. He was a prodigy who mastered the Vedas and the Upanishads at a young age. He renounced the worldly life and became a wandering monk, seeking the ultimate truth of reality. He traveled across India, engaging in debates with scholars of various schools of thought, such as Buddhism, Jainism, Mimamsa, Nyaya, and Samkhya. He established his doctrine of Advaita Vedanta, which asserts that the supreme reality (Brahman) is one, without a second, and that the individual self (Atman) is identical with Brahman. He also taught that the phenomenal world of multiplicity and diversity is an illusion (Maya) caused by ignorance (Avidya) of the true nature of reality. He wrote commentaries on the Upanishads, the Brahma Sutras, and the Bhagavad Gita, as well as original works such as the Vivekachudamani and the Upadesasahasri. He also composed devotional hymns to various deities, such as Shiva, Vishnu, and Devi. 
- Adi Shankara established four mathas in the four corners of India, namely, Sringeri in the south, Dwarka in the west, Puri in the east, and Badrinath in the north. He appointed four of his disciples as the heads of these mathas, and gave them the authority to ordain new monks and teach the Advaita Vedanta doctrine. He also divided the Hindu scriptures into two categories: the Prasthanatrayi, consisting of the Upanishads, the Brahma Sutras, and the Bhagavad Gita, which are the authoritative sources of Vedanta; and the Smriti, consisting of the Puranas, the Itihasas, the Dharmashastras, and the Agamas, which are the supplementary sources of Hindu law and practice. He also organized the Hindu society into four varnas (castes) and four ashramas (stages of life), and prescribed the duties and rituals for each of them. 
- Adi Shankara is widely regarded as one of the greatest philosophers and teachers of Hinduism, and his influence is evident in various aspects of Hindu culture, such as art, literature, music, and spirituality. He is also revered as a saint and an incarnation of Shiva by his followers. His teachings have been interpreted and elaborated by various sub-schools and commentators of Advaita Vedanta, such as Mandana Mishra, Sureshvara, Padmapada, Prakasatman, Vachaspati Mishra, and Madhusudana Sarasvati. He is also respected and admired by other schools of Hindu philosophy, such as Vishishtadvaita, Dvaita, and Bhakti, as well as by other religions, such as Buddhism, Sikhism, and Jainism.
