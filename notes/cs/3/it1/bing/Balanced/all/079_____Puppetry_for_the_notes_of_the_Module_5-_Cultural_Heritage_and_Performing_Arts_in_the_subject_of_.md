# Puppetry

Puppetry is an ancient art form that involves storytelling through manipulating puppets. Puppets are figures that resemble humans, animals, or mythical creatures, and are animated by strings, rods, wires, or hands. Puppetry is a form of performing arts that reflects the cultural heritage and diversity of India.

## Types of Puppetry in India

There are four main types of puppetry in India, based on the way the puppets are controlled and manipulated. They are:

- **String puppets**: These are puppets that are attached to strings that are held by the puppeteer from above. The puppeteer can move the puppets by pulling and releasing the strings. String puppets are also known as marionettes or kathputli. They are the most popular form of puppetry in India, and are found in states like Rajasthan, Orissa, Karnataka, Tamil Nadu, and Kerala. Some examples of string puppets are kathputli of Rajasthan, gombeyatta of Karnataka, bommalattam of Tamil Nadu, and pavakoothu of Kerala.
- **Rod puppets**: These are puppets that are supported and moved by rods that are attached to their limbs or body. The puppeteer can manipulate the puppets by holding and moving the rods from below. Rod puppets are also known as stick puppets or danda kathputli. They are found in states like West Bengal, Bihar, Orissa, and Assam. Some examples of rod puppets are putul nautch of West Bengal, yampuri of Bihar, kundhei of Orissa, and putala nach of Assam.
- **Shadow puppets**: These are puppets that are made of leather or paper, and are cut out in various shapes and sizes. They are held against a screen that is lit from behind, creating shadows that are visible to the audience. The puppeteer can move the puppets by holding them with rods or wires. Shadow puppets are also known as chhaya kathputli or tolubommalata. They are found in states like Andhra Pradesh, Karnataka, Kerala, Maharashtra, Orissa, and Tamil Nadu. Some examples of shadow puppets are tholu bommalata of Andhra Pradesh, togalu gombeyatta of Karnataka, tholpavakoothu of Kerala, chamadyache bahulya of Maharashtra, ravanachhaya of Orissa, and tol pava koothu of Tamil Nadu.
- **Glove puppets**: These are puppets that are worn on the hands of the puppeteer, who can move the puppets by using their fingers. Glove puppets are also known as hand puppets or hasth kathputli. They are found in states like Uttar Pradesh, West Bengal, Orissa, and Kerala. Some examples of glove puppets are gopalila kundhei of Uttar Pradesh, benir putul of West Bengal, sakhi kundhei of Orissa, and pava kathakali of Kerala.

## Origin and Salient Features of Puppetry in India

- Puppetry in India is said to be the home of puppets, as it dates back to thousands of years. There are references to puppetry in ancient texts like the Rig Veda, the Ramayana, the Mahabharata, the Puranas, and the Natyashastra. Puppetry was also mentioned by Greek historians like Megasthenes and Arrian, who visited India in the 4th and 2nd centuries BCE, respectively.
- Puppetry in India is a family business, as the puppeteers inherit the skills and the puppets from their ancestors. The puppeteers belong to various castes and communities, and have their own traditions and styles of puppetry. The puppeteers travel from place to place to entertain the people, and also perform in festivals and fairs. The puppeteers are also known as sutradhars, which means "string bearers", as they narrate the stories through the puppets.
- Puppetry in India is a form of folk theatre, as it reflects the local culture, language, music, and costumes of the regions where it is performed. The themes of puppetry are mostly based on epics and legends, such as the Ramayana, the Mahabharata, the Puranas, and the folk tales of the regions.
