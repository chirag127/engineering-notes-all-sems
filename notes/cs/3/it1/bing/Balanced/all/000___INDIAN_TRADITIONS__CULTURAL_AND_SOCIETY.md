# Indian Traditions, Culture and Society

India is a diverse and ancient country with a rich and varied heritage. India has been influenced by many different civilizations, religions, languages, and cultures over the centuries, resulting in a unique and complex mosaic of traditions and customs. Some of the main aspects of Indian culture and society are:

- **Arts and Language**: India has a long and rich history of artistic expression, ranging from classical music and dance, to painting and sculpture, to literature and poetry. India is also home to many languages, with 22 official languages and hundreds of regional dialects. The most widely spoken languages are Hindi, English, Bengali, Telugu, Marathi, Tamil, Urdu, and Gujarati. The ancient Indian classical language, Sanskrit, was one of the earliest Indo-European languages to be developed and is still used for religious and scholarly purposes.

- **Philosophy and Religion**: India is the birthplace of four major world religions: Hinduism, Buddhism, Jainism, and Sikhism. Hinduism is the oldest and most dominant religion in India, with about 80% of the population identifying as Hindus. Hinduism is a diverse and polytheistic faith that encompasses various sects, beliefs, practices, and rituals. Buddhism, Jainism, and Sikhism are offshoots of Hinduism that emerged in the 6th and 5th centuries BCE, and emphasize non-violence, compassion, and liberation from the cycle of rebirth. India also has a significant Muslim population, about 14%, as well as smaller communities of Christians, Zoroastrians, Jews, and others. India is known for its religious tolerance and pluralism, as well as its philosophical contributions to ethics, logic, metaphysics, and epistemology.

- **Food and Drink**: The cuisine of India varies considerably across its various regions and ethnic communities. However, some common elements include the use of spices, herbs, grains, legumes, vegetables, fruits, dairy products, and meat (except for beef, which is forbidden for Hindus and Sikhs). Indian food is typically cooked in oil or ghee (clarified butter) and served with flatbreads, such as roti, naan, or paratha, or rice. Some of the most popular dishes include dal (lentil soup), curry (spicy stew), biryani (rice with meat or vegetables), tandoori (marinated and roasted meat or vegetables), samosa (fried pastry with savory filling), chaat (snack food), and sweets, such as laddu, jalebi, and halwa. India also has a rich and diverse tradition of beverages, such as tea, coffee, lassi (yogurt drink), nimbu pani (lemonade), and alcoholic drinks, such as toddy, feni, and bhang.

- **Society and Culture**: India is a multi-ethnic, multi-lingual, and multi-religious society, with a population of about 1.3 billion people. India is also a federal parliamentary republic, with a central government and 28 states and 8 union territories, each with its own legislature and administration. India has a complex and hierarchical social structure, based on the ancient system of varna (caste) and jati (sub-caste), which divides people into four main categories: Brahmins (priests and scholars), Kshatriyas (warriors and rulers), Vaishyas (merchants and farmers), and Shudras (servants and laborers). There is also a fifth category of people, known as Dalits or untouchables, who are considered outside the caste system and face discrimination and oppression. However, the caste system is gradually losing its relevance and influence in modern India, especially in urban areas and among the educated and affluent classes. India also has a strong and vibrant civil society, with many non-governmental organizations, social movements, media outlets, and cultural institutions that promote democracy, human rights, social justice, and diversity   .
