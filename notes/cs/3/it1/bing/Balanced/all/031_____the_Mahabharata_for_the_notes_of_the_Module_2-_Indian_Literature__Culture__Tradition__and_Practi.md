# The Mahabharata

The Mahabharata is one of the two major Sanskrit epics of ancient India, along with the Ramayana. It is a long and complex narrative that covers the history, culture, religion, and philosophy of the Indian subcontinent. It is also a story of a dynastic struggle for the throne of Hastinapura, a kingdom ruled by the Kuru clan.

The main plot of the Mahabharata can be summarized as follows:

- The Kuru clan is divided into two branches: the Kauravas, led by Dhritarashtra, the blind and eldest son of the previous king; and the Pandavas, led by Pandu, the younger brother of Dhritarashtra. Dhritarashtra has 100 sons, while Pandu has five sons, born from his two wives Kunti and Madri, with the help of various gods.
- Pandu dies after being cursed by a sage for killing a deer while hunting. His sons, known as the Pandavas, are raised by Kunti along with the Kauravas in Hastinapura. The Pandavas are Yudhishthira, the eldest and the most virtuous; Bhima, the strongest and the most loyal; Arjuna, the most skilled archer and warrior; Nakula and Sahadeva, the twins and the youngest.
- The Kauravas, especially the eldest Duryodhana, are jealous and hostile towards the Pandavas, and try to harm them in various ways. They plot to kill them by burning them alive in a house of lac, but the Pandavas escape with the help of their cousin Vidura and their uncle Krishna, who is an incarnation of the god Vishnu.
- The Pandavas go into exile and disguise themselves as Brahmins. They travel across India and have many adventures and encounters with various sages, kings, demons, and gods. They also participate in a swayamvara, a contest for the hand of a princess, where Arjuna wins the beautiful Draupadi as his wife. However, due to a misunderstanding, Draupadi becomes the common wife of all the five Pandavas, as per the custom of polyandry among the Panchalas, her father's kingdom.
- The Pandavas return to Hastinapura and claim their share of the kingdom. Dhritarashtra reluctantly agrees to give them half of the land, known as Khandavaprastha, which is barren and infested with snakes. However, with the help of Krishna, the Pandavas transform it into a prosperous and beautiful city, called Indraprastha. They also perform the Rajasuya sacrifice, a ritual that establishes their supremacy over other kings.
- Duryodhana, who is humiliated and enraged by the success and glory of the Pandavas, invites them to a game of dice, where he cheats with the help of his uncle Shakuni, a master of gambling. He wins everything from the Pandavas, including their wealth, kingdom, and Draupadi. He then orders his brother Dushasana to drag Draupadi to the court and disrobe her in front of everyone. However, Krishna miraculously saves her honor by providing her with endless garments. Draupadi vows to not tie her hair until she washes it with the blood of Dushasana and Duryodhana.
- The Pandavas are exiled for 13 years, with the condition that they must spend the last year in disguise, and if they are recognized, they must repeat the exile. They again wander across India and have more adventures and encounters. During this time, Arjuna obtains divine weapons from various gods, including Shiva, Indra, and Varuna. He also marries Subhadra, the sister of Krishna, and begets a son named Abhimanyu. The other Pandavas also have other wives and sons during their exile.
- After the 13 years are over, the Pandavas ask for their kingdom back, but Duryodhana refuses to give them even a needlepoint of land. A war becomes inevitable, and both sides gather their allies and armies. The war is fought on the plain of Kurukshetra, near Delhi, and lasts for 18 days. Krishna becomes the charioteer and advisor of Arjuna, and delivers the Bhagavad Gita, a sacred scripture that contains the essence of Hindu philosophy and ethics, before the war begins.
