# Module 4-Science, Management and Indian Knowledge System

This module covers the following topics:

- Astronomy in India: The ancient Indian astronomers made significant contributions to the fields of observational astronomy, mathematical astronomy, cosmology, and astrology. Some of the notable achievements include the discovery of the heliocentric model, the calculation of the sidereal year, the concept of zero, the use of decimal system, the development of trigonometry, and the formulation of the sine and cosine functions  .
- Chemistry in India: The ancient Indian chemists developed various techniques and methods for the extraction, purification, and synthesis of metals, alloys, minerals, dyes, perfumes, medicines, and cosmetics. They also invented the processes of distillation, calcination, sublimation, fermentation, and crystallization. Some of the important works in this field include the Rasashastra, the Rasaratnakara, and the Rasendrasara  .
- Mathematics in India: The ancient Indian mathematicians made remarkable contributions to the fields of arithmetic, algebra, geometry, calculus, and number theory. They discovered the concepts of zero, infinity, negative numbers, fractions, irrational numbers, and the place value system. They also developed the rules of arithmetic operations, the solutions of linear and quadratic equations, the Pythagorean theorem, the formula for the area of a triangle, the value of pi, the series expansions of trigonometric functions, and the binomial theorem  .
- Physics in India: The ancient Indian physicists explored the nature of matter, energy, force, motion, sound, light, heat, electricity, and magnetism. They proposed the atomic theory, the theory of relativity, the law of conservation of mass, the law of gravitation, the wave theory of sound, the reflection and refraction of light, the concept of latent heat, the generation of electricity by friction, and the properties of magnets  .
- Agriculture in India: The ancient Indian agriculturists developed various methods and practices for the cultivation, irrigation, fertilization, pest control, and harvesting of crops. They also domesticated various animals and plants, and produced various products such as cereals, pulses, oilseeds, spices, fruits, vegetables, milk, ghee, honey, silk, cotton, and jute  .
- Medicine in India: The ancient Indian physicians developed various systems and branches of medicine, such as Ayurveda, Siddha, Unani, and Yoga. They also discovered the concepts of anatomy, physiology, pathology, diagnosis, prognosis, treatment, surgery, pharmacology, and hygiene. Some of the famous works in this field include the Charaka Samhita, the Sushruta Samhita, and the Ashtanga Hridaya  .
- Metallurgy in India: The ancient Indian metallurgists mastered the art and science of producing various metals, alloys, and artifacts, such as iron, steel, copper, bronze, brass, gold, silver, and zinc. They also invented the processes of smelting, forging, casting, welding, soldering, and tempering. Some of the remarkable examples of their craftsmanship include the iron pillar of Delhi, the bronze statue of Nataraja, and the gold coins of Gupta dynasty  .
- Geography in India: The ancient Indian geographers studied the physical features, climate, flora, fauna, and human settlements of the earth. They also mapped the continents, oceans, rivers, mountains, and islands of the world. They also devised the concepts of latitude, longitude, equator, tropics, and meridians. Some of the notable works in this field include the Brihat Samhita, the Vishnu Purana, and the Ptolemy's Geography  .
- Biology in India: The ancient Indian biologists investigated the structure, function, classification, and evolution of living organisms. They also discovered the concepts of cell, tissue, organ, system, heredity, variation, adaptation, and natural selection. They also classified the animals and plants into various categories and subcategories, based on their morphology, anatomy, physiology, and behavior.
