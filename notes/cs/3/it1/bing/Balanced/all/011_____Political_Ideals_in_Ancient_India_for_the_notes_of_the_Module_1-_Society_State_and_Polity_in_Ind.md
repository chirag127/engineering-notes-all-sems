# Political Ideals in Ancient India

- Ancient India had a rich and diverse political culture that spanned from the Vedic period (c. 1500-500 BCE) to the medieval period (c. 1200-1700 CE).
- The political ideals of ancient India were based on various sources, such as the Vedas, the Upanishads, the epics, the Dharmashastras, the Arthashastra, the Buddhist and Jain texts, and the inscriptions and edicts of various rulers.
- Some of the common political ideals that emerged in ancient India were:
  - Dharma: The concept of dharma, or moral duty, was the foundation of political legitimacy and social order in ancient India. Dharma was seen as the cosmic law that governed the universe and the human society. Dharma also defined the roles and responsibilities of different classes and groups in the society, such as the varnas (castes), the ashramas (stages of life), and the jatis (sub-castes). Dharma also prescribed the ethical conduct and the duties of the rulers and the subjects. The rulers were expected to uphold and protect dharma by following the principles of rajadharma, or the dharma of kingship, which included justice, benevolence, protection, welfare, and sacrifice. The subjects were expected to obey and support the rulers who followed dharma, and to resist and overthrow the rulers who violated dharma.
  - Rta: The concept of rta, or cosmic order, was closely related to dharma. Rta was the principle that maintained the harmony and balance of the natural and the social world. Rta was also the source of truth, law, and justice. The rulers and the subjects had to follow rta to ensure the prosperity and stability of the society. Rta also implied the participation and consultation of the people in the political affairs. The Rig Veda, the oldest of the Vedas, mentions the concept of samjnana, or the collective consciousness of the people, as a deity and a source of political authority. The Rig Veda also mentions the assemblies of the people, such as the sabha and the samiti, where the people could voice their opinions and elect their leaders.
  - Artha: The concept of artha, or material wealth, was another important political ideal in ancient India. Artha was seen as the means to achieve the four goals of human life, namely dharma, artha, kama (pleasure), and moksha (liberation). Artha also referred to the science of politics, or the art of governance, that aimed at the acquisition and maintenance of power and wealth. The most influential treatise on artha was the Arthashastra, attributed to Kautilya, the minister of the Mauryan emperor Chandragupta Maurya (c. 321-297 BCE). The Arthashastra advocated a pragmatic and ruthless approach to politics, based on the principles of niti (policy), shakti (power), and yoga (skill). The Arthashastra also prescribed the methods of diplomacy, espionage, warfare, taxation, administration, and law and order, to ensure the security and prosperity of the state.
  - Ahimsa: The concept of ahimsa, or non-violence, was another prominent political ideal in ancient India, especially among the heterodox traditions of Buddhism and Jainism. Ahimsa was seen as the highest virtue and the supreme dharma, that transcended the distinctions of caste, creed, and nationality. Ahimsa also implied compassion, tolerance, and respect for all living beings. The proponents of ahimsa advocated the renunciation of violence, war, and animal sacrifice, and the promotion of peace, harmony, and welfare. Some of the rulers who embraced ahimsa were Ashoka (c. 268-232 BCE), the Mauryan emperor who converted to Buddhism after witnessing the horrors of the Kalinga war, and Harshavardhana (c. 606-647 CE), the king of Thanesar and Kannauj, who patronized both Buddhism and Jainism.
