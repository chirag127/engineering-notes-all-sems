# Chemistry in India

Chemistry is the branch of science that deals with the study of matter, its composition, structure, properties, and reactions. Chemistry has a wide scope and applications in various fields, such as medicine, agriculture, industry, environment, and education. Chemistry is also an integral part of the Indian culture and heritage, as it has a long and rich history of development and achievements in this domain.

Some of the main points to note about chemistry in India are:

- Chemistry in India can be traced back to the ancient times, when the Indus Valley or Harappan civilization (3rd millennium BCE) used various chemical techniques, such as metallurgy, dyeing, tanning, and pottery.
- The Vedic period (2nd millennium BCE to 6th century BCE) witnessed the emergence of Ayurveda, the system of medicine that used a variety of minerals, metals, plants, and animal products for healing and wellness. Ayurveda also played an important role in the development of chemistry, as it involved the preparation and purification of various substances, such as mercury, sulfur, iron, copper, gold, silver, and zinc.
- The post-Vedic period (6th century BCE to 5th century CE) saw the growth of various schools of philosophy, such as Nyaya, Vaisheshika, Sankhya, Yoga, Mimamsa, and Vedanta, that explored the nature and classification of matter, atoms, elements, and compounds. The Vaisheshika school, founded by Kanada, was particularly influential in the development of atomic theory and the concept of anu (atom) and paramanu (subatomic particle).
- The classical period (5th century CE to 12th century CE) was marked by the flourishing of various branches of science, such as astronomy, mathematics, medicine, and alchemy. Alchemy, or Rasayan Shastra, was the older form of chemistry that aimed at the transmutation of metals, the discovery of the elixir of life, and the attainment of spiritual enlightenment. Alchemy involved the use of various devices, instruments, apparatus, and processes, such as distillation, sublimation, calcination, fusion, extraction, and filtration, for conducting chemical experiments. Some of the prominent alchemists of this period were Nagarjuna, Nityanatha, Govinda Bhagavatpada, and Vagbhata .
- The medieval period (12th century CE to 18th century CE) witnessed the invasion and influence of various foreign powers, such as the Arabs, Turks, Mongols, Persians, and Europeans, on the Indian culture and science. This period also saw the decline of the indigenous scientific traditions and the emergence of new forms of knowledge and practices. However, some aspects of chemistry, such as metallurgy, dyeing, papermaking, glassmaking, and fireworks, continued to thrive and evolve in this period. Some of the notable chemists of this period were Hemadri, Madhava, Chakrapani Datta, and Sawai Jai Singh.
- The modern period (18th century CE onwards) was characterized by the colonial rule and the struggle for independence of India. This period also witnessed the revival and renaissance of the Indian science and culture, as well as the integration and interaction of the Indian and Western scientific traditions. Some of the pioneers of modern chemistry in India were Acharya Prafulla Chandra Ray, Sir C.V. Raman, Dr. Homi J. Bhabha, Dr. Shanti Swarup Bhatnagar, Dr. Vikram A. Sarabhai, and Dr. A.P.J. Abdul Kalam .
- Chemistry as a career option is immense in India. Candidates can pursue degree, diploma and certificate courses in chemistry at undergraduate (UG), post graduate (PG) and doctoral levels. Some of the popular chemistry courses are B.Sc. Chemistry, M.Sc. Chemistry, B.Tech. Chemical Engineering, M.Tech. Chemical Engineering, Ph.D. Chemistry, and Diploma in Chemical Technology.
