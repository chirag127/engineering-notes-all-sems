# Marriage

- Marriage is a social institution that regulates the sexual and reproductive behavior of individuals in a society.
- Marriage also establishes rights and obligations between the spouses and their kin, such as inheritance, property, residence, and child custody.
- Marriage can be classified into different types based on various criteria, such as the number of spouses, the mode of spouse selection, the degree of kinship, and the rules of residence and descent.
- Some of the common types of marriage are:

  - Monogamy: A marriage between one man and one woman.
  - Polygamy: A marriage involving more than one spouse of either sex.
    - Polygyny: A marriage in which a man has more than one wife.
    - Polyandry: A marriage in which a woman has more than one husband.
  - Endogamy: A marriage within a specific group, such as caste, clan, or tribe.
  - Exogamy: A marriage outside a specific group, such as caste, clan, or tribe.
  - Arranged marriage: A marriage in which the spouses are selected by their parents or elders, based on factors such as family, caste, class, education, and horoscope.
  - Love marriage: A marriage in which the spouses choose each other based on their personal preferences, such as attraction, compatibility, and affection.
  - Patrilocal: A marriage in which the wife moves to the husband's place of residence after marriage.
  - Matrilocal: A marriage in which the husband moves to the wife's place of residence after marriage.
  - Neolocal: A marriage in which the spouses establish a new place of residence after marriage.
  - Patrilineal: A marriage in which the children inherit the father's surname and lineage.
  - Matrilineal: A marriage in which the children inherit the mother's surname and lineage.
  - Bilateral: A marriage in which the children inherit both the parents' surnames and lineages.

- Marriage is influenced by various factors, such as religion, law, economy, culture, and ideology.
- Marriage has various functions and consequences for the individuals and the society, such as:

  - Providing companionship, intimacy, and emotional support to the spouses.
  - Regulating sexual behavior and preventing promiscuity, adultery, and incest.
  - Ensuring the continuity of the family and the society through procreation and socialization of children.
  - Creating and maintaining social bonds and networks among the kin and the community.
  - Distributing and transferring resources, wealth, and status among the spouses and their kin.
  - Enhancing or reducing the social mobility and the power of the spouses and their kin.
  - Promoting or challenging the social norms and values of the society.
  - Contributing or hindering the social change and development of the society.
