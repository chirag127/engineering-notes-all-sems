# Harappan Technologies

- The Harappan civilization, also known as the Indus Valley civilization, was a Bronze Age civilization that flourished in the northwest of the Indian subcontinent from about 2500 BCE to 1900 BCE.
- The Harappans developed various technologies and crafts, such as metallurgy, seal carving, pottery, jewelry, weights and measures, urban planning, drainage systems, and writing.
- Some of the notable features of Harappan technologies are:

  - Metallurgy: The Harappans used copper, bronze, lead, and tin to make tools, weapons, vessels, and ornaments. They knew how to alloy copper with tin to make bronze, which is harder and more durable than copper. They also knew how to cast metal using the lost-wax process, which involves making a wax model of the desired object, covering it with clay, heating it to melt the wax, and then pouring molten metal into the hollow mold.
  - Seal carving: The Harappans are known for their distinctive seals, which are small, carved objects used for stamping clay on trade goods or identifying property. The seals are usually made of steatite, a soft stone that can be easily engraved. The seals have various designs, such as animals, humans, symbols, and inscriptions in an undeciphered script. The seals reflect the artistic, religious, and commercial aspects of Harappan culture.
  - Pottery: The Harappans produced a variety of pottery, ranging from plain, utilitarian ware to fine, painted ware. The pottery is usually wheel-made, although some hand-made pottery is also found. The pottery is often decorated with geometric patterns, floral motifs, animals, and human figures. The pottery is fired at high temperatures, making it durable and water-resistant.
  - Jewelry: The Harappans were skilled in making jewelry from materials such as gold, silver, copper, bronze, ivory, shell, bone, and beads. They made necklaces, bracelets, earrings, rings, pendants, and amulets. The jewelry is often adorned with intricate designs, such as filigree, granulation, and inlay. The jewelry reflects the wealth, status, and aesthetic sense of the Harappans.
  - Weights and measures: The Harappans developed a system of standardized weights and measures, which facilitated trade and administration. The weights are usually made of chert, a hard stone, and are shaped like cubes, spheres, or cones. The weights follow a binary system, with ratios of 1:2, 1:4, 1:8, etc. The smallest weight is about 0.8 grams, and the largest is about 12.5 kilograms. The measures are usually made of bronze, and are used for measuring length, volume, and area. The measures are based on a unit of about 1.32 inches, which is subdivided into smaller units. The measures are accurate and consistent across different sites.
  - Urban planning: The Harappans built well-planned cities, with regular streets, brick houses, public buildings, and fortifications. The cities are divided into two parts: the citadel, which is a raised platform with administrative and religious structures, and the lower town, which is a residential and commercial area. The cities have sophisticated drainage systems, with covered channels, wells, baths, and toilets. The cities also have granaries, warehouses, workshops, and docks. The cities are designed to accommodate the needs and activities of the Harappans.
