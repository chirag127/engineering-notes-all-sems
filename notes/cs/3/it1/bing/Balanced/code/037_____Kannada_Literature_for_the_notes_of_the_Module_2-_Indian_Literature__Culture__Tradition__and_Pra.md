### Kannada Literature

- Kannada literature is the literature written in the Kannada language, one of the oldest Dravidian languages spoken mainly in the Indian state of Karnataka and some parts of neighboring states.
- Kannada literature has a rich and long history, dating back to the 5th century CE. It has produced many notable poets, writers, and scholars who have contributed to the development of Kannada language, culture, and society.
- Some of the major genres of Kannada literature are poetry, prose, drama, and folklore. Kannada literature also reflects the influence of various literary traditions, such as Sanskrit, Prakrit, Tamil, Telugu, Persian, Arabic, and English.
- Kannada literature can be broadly divided into three periods: Old Kannada (5th to 12th century CE), Middle Kannada (12th to 18th century CE), and Modern Kannada (19th century CE onwards).
- Some of the prominent features of Kannada literature are:

  - The use of various forms of meters, rhyme schemes, and figures of speech in poetry, such as tripadi, shatpadi, ragale, champu, vachana, kavya, and navodaya.
  - The development of various forms of prose, such as katha, akhyana, charita, purana, and gadhya.
  - The emergence of various forms of drama, such as yakshagana, bayalata, and prasanga.
  - The incorporation of various themes, such as religion, philosophy, ethics, social issues, history, romance, and humor.
  - The expression of various ideologies, such as Shaivism, Vaishnavism, Jainism, Buddhism, Veerashaivism, Bhakti, and Dalit.
  - The representation of various aspects of Kannada culture, such as festivals, customs, rituals, arts, crafts, music, dance, and cuisine.
