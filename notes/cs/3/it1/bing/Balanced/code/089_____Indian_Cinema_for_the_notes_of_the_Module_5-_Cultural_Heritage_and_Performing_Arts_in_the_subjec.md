### Indian Cinema

Indian cinema is one of the largest and most diverse film industries in the world, producing films in various languages, genres, and styles. Indian cinema has a rich and complex history that spans over a century, reflecting the social, cultural, and political changes of the nation. Here are some of the main points to know about Indian cinema:

- The beginning of Indian cinema dates back to 1896, when the first public screening of a movie by the Lumiere brothers took place in Bombay (now Mumbai) . The father of Indian cinema, Dadasaheb Phalke, released the first ever full-length feature film, Raja Harishchandra, in 1913. The silent film was a commercial success and inspired many filmmakers to follow suit .
- The advent of sound in cinema marked a new era for Indian cinema, as the first talkie, Alam Ara, by Ardeshir Irani, was screened in Bombay in 1931. The film was a musical drama that featured seven songs and attracted huge crowds. The talkies also gave rise to the growth of regional films, as different languages and dialects could be used to cater to different audiences .
- The 1930s and 1940s saw the emergence of some of the most influential filmmakers and actors of Indian cinema, such as V. Shantaram, Prithviraj Kapoor, Devika Rani, and K.L. Saigal. The films of this period dealt with various themes, such as social realism, nationalism, romance, and mythology. Some of the notable films of this era include Achhut Kanya (1936), Sant Tukaram (1936), Kismet (1943), and Mother India (1957) .
- The 1950s and 1960s are considered the golden age of Indian cinema, as some of the most acclaimed and popular films were made during this time. The films of this period showcased the artistic and technical excellence of Indian cinema, as well as the diversity of genres and styles. Some of the prominent filmmakers and actors of this era include Satyajit Ray, Guru Dutt, Raj Kapoor, Bimal Roy, Dilip Kumar, Nargis, Madhubala, and Meena Kumari. Some of the landmark films of this era include Pather Panchali (1955), Pyaasa (1957), Mughal-e-Azam (1960), and Guide (1965) .
- The 1970s and 1980s witnessed the rise of the masala film, a genre that combined action, comedy, romance, drama, and music in a formulaic and entertaining manner. The masala film was popularized by filmmakers such as Manmohan Desai, Prakash Mehra, and Ramesh Sippy, and actors such as Amitabh Bachchan, Dharmendra, Hema Malini, and Rekha. Some of the iconic films of this genre include Sholay (1975), Amar Akbar Anthony (1977), and Mr. India (1987) .
- The 1990s and 2000s saw the emergence of new trends and challenges in Indian cinema, as the industry faced competition from television, video, and foreign films. The films of this period explored new themes, such as urban life, globalization, diaspora, and terrorism. The films also experimented with new genres, such as parallel cinema, art cinema, and new wave cinema. Some of the notable filmmakers and actors of this era include Adoor Gopalakrishnan, Shyam Benegal, Aamir Khan, Shah Rukh Khan, Salman Khan, Madhuri Dixit, and Kajol. Some of the popular films of this era include Dilwale Dulhania Le Jayenge (1995), Lagaan (2001), Dil Chahta Hai (2001), and Rang De Basanti (2006) .
- The 2010s and 2020s have witnessed the expansion and diversification of Indian cinema, as the industry has embraced new technologies, platforms, and markets. The films of this period have explored various genres, such as biopic, thriller, horror, and sci-fi. The films have also addressed various issues, such as gender, caste, religion, and politics.
