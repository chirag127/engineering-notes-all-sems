### Sangama Literature

- Sangama Literature is the name given to the collection of Tamil literary works produced during the Sangam period, which lasted from the 3rd century BCE to the 3rd century CE.
- The Sangam period is considered the golden age of Tamil literature and culture, as it witnessed the flourishing of various genres of poetry, prose, drama, grammar, lexicography, and ethics.
- The Sangam literature is divided into two main groups: the Ettuthokai (Eight Anthologies) and the Pattuppattu (Ten Idylls). The Ettuthokai consists of eight anthologies of short poems on various themes, such as love, war, heroism, ethics, and nature. The Pattuppattu consists of ten long poems that narrate the legends and achievements of ancient Tamil kings and heroes.
- The Sangam literature also includes two important works of Tamil grammar and poetics: the Tolkappiyam and the Iraiyanar Akapporul. The Tolkappiyam is the oldest extant work of Tamil grammar and literature, attributed to the sage Tolkappiyar. It deals with the phonology, morphology, syntax, and semantics of the Tamil language, as well as the principles and conventions of Tamil poetry. The Iraiyanar Akapporul is a treatise on the nature and classification of love poetry, attributed to the poet Nakkirar. It explains the various types and stages of love, the characteristics and emotions of the lovers, and the role of the gods and the environment in love poetry.
- The Sangam literature reflects the social, political, religious, and cultural aspects of the ancient Tamil society. It provides valuable insights into the history, geography, ecology, economy, and customs of the Tamils and their interactions with other regions and peoples. It also showcases the richness, diversity, and creativity of the Tamil language and literature, and its influence on other Indian and world literatures.
