# Indian Architecture

- Indian architecture is rooted in the history, culture, and religion of India.
- It reflects the diversity and richness of Indian civilization and its interactions with other cultures.
- It encompasses a variety of styles and traditions, such as Hindu temple architecture, Indo-Islamic architecture, Rajput architecture, Mughal architecture, South Indian architecture, and Indo-Saracenic architecture.
- Some of the common features of Indian architecture are the use of stone, brick, wood, and metal; the emphasis on ornamentation, symbolism, and geometry; the integration of sculpture, painting, and calligraphy; and the adaptation to climate, topography, and function.

## Hindu Temple Architecture

- Hindu temple architecture is the design and construction of sacred structures for the worship of Hindu deities.
- It evolved from the ancient Vedic fire altars and the rock-cut cave temples to the elaborate and monumental temples of the medieval and modern periods.
- It is based on the principles of Vastu Shastra, a traditional system of architecture that prescribes the layout, orientation, and proportions of a temple.
- It consists of three main elements: the garbhagriha (sanctum sanctorum), the mandapa (hall), and the shikhara (tower).
- It is characterized by the use of intricate carvings, sculptures, and paintings depicting various gods, goddesses, and mythological scenes.
- It varies according to the region, sect, and style of the temple, such as Nagara, Dravida, Vesara, Kalinga, Hoysala, Chola, and Vijayanagara.

## Indo-Islamic Architecture

- Indo-Islamic architecture is the fusion of Islamic and Indian architectural traditions that emerged after the Muslim conquest of India.
- It reflects the cultural and religious diversity of India, as well as the political and artistic patronage of various Muslim dynasties.
- It is influenced by the Persian, Turkish, Arab, and Central Asian styles of Islamic architecture, as well as the indigenous styles of Hindu, Jain, and Buddhist architecture.
- It is distinguished by the use of arches, domes, minarets, and geometric patterns, as well as the incorporation of calligraphy, floral motifs, and animal figures.
- It includes mosques, tombs, palaces, forts, and gardens, such as the Qutub Minar, the Taj Mahal, the Red Fort, and the Humayun's Tomb.

## Rajput Architecture

- Rajput architecture is the style of architecture developed by the Rajput clans of northern India between the 11th and 19th centuries.
- It is influenced by the Hindu, Jain, and Islamic architectural traditions, as well as the local and regional cultures of Rajasthan, Gujarat, and Madhya Pradesh.
- It is known for its grandeur, elegance, and intricacy, as well as its defensive and symbolic features.
- It consists of forts, palaces, temples, havelis, and stepwells, such as the Chittorgarh Fort, the Amber Palace, the Dilwara Temples, the Patwon Ki Haveli, and the Rani Ki Vav.

## Mughal Architecture

- Mughal architecture is the style of architecture developed by the Mughal emperors and their successors in India between the 16th and 18th centuries.
- It is a synthesis of the Persian, Turkish, and Indian architectural traditions, as well as the personal tastes and preferences of the Mughal rulers.
- It is renowned for its elegance, symmetry, and harmony, as well as its use of white marble, red sandstone, and precious stones.
- It comprises of mosques, tombs, palaces, forts, and gardens, such as the Jama Masjid, the Taj Mahal, the Agra Fort, the Fatehpur Sikri, and the Shalimar Gardens.
