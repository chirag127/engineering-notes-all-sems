### Four-class Classification

- The four-class classification, also known as the varna system, is a social hierarchy that divides the society into four main categories: Brahmins, Kshatriyas, Vaishyas and Shudras.
- The varna system is based on the concept of dharma, or duty, that assigns different roles and responsibilities to each class according to their inherent qualities and abilities.
- The Brahmins are the highest class, who are responsible for performing rituals, teaching and preserving the sacred knowledge. They are considered to be pure and closest to the divine.
- The Kshatriyas are the second class, who are responsible for protecting the society, administering justice and maintaining order. They are considered to be brave and noble.
- The Vaishyas are the third class, who are responsible for engaging in trade, commerce and agriculture. They are considered to be industrious and prosperous.
- The Shudras are the lowest class, who are responsible for serving the other three classes and performing menial and unclean tasks. They are considered to be impure and dependent.
- The varna system is often criticized for being rigid, oppressive and discriminatory, as it creates a hierarchy of privilege and power that denies equal opportunities and rights to the lower classes.
- The varna system is also challenged by the existence of other social groups, such as the jatis, or castes, that are based on birth, occupation and region, and the dalits, or untouchables, that are excluded from the varna system and face extreme marginalization and violence.
