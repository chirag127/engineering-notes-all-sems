### Northern Indian Languages & Literature

- Northern India is a diverse region with many languages and dialects that belong to the Indo-Aryan branch of the Indo-European language family.
- The oldest form of Indo-Aryan language is Sanskrit, which is the language of the ancient Vedic scriptures and classical literature. Sanskrit influenced the development of many modern languages of northern India, such as Hindi, Urdu, Punjabi, Bengali, Gujarati, Marathi, etc.
- The medieval period saw the emergence of new languages from the old Apabhramsha, which was a vernacular form of Sanskrit. These languages were evolving at two levels: the spoken and the written language. The spoken languages were influenced by the local dialects, while the written languages were influenced by the scripts and literary traditions of Sanskrit, Persian, Arabic, and Turkish.
- Some of the prominent languages and literatures of northern India are:

  - Hindi: It is the most widely spoken language in northern India and the official language of the Indian government. It is written in the Devanagari script and has two main dialects: Khari Boli and Braj Bhasha. Hindi literature has a rich and diverse history, spanning from the Bhakti poetry of Kabir, Surdas, Tulsidas, etc. to the modern prose and poetry of Premchand, Harivansh Rai Bachchan, Mahadevi Varma, etc.
  - Urdu: It is the national language of Pakistan and one of the official languages of India. It is written in the Perso-Arabic script and has a strong influence of Persian and Arabic vocabulary and grammar. Urdu literature is known for its elegance and sophistication, especially in the genres of poetry, such as ghazal, nazm, qasida, etc. Some of the famous Urdu poets are Mir, Ghalib, Iqbal, Faiz, etc.
  - Punjabi: It is the language of the Punjab region, which is divided between India and Pakistan. It is written in the Gurmukhi script in India and the Shahmukhi script in Pakistan. Punjabi literature is influenced by the Sikh religion and the folk culture of the region. Some of the notable Punjabi writers are Guru Nanak, Bulleh Shah, Waris Shah, Amrita Pritam, etc.
  - Bengali: It is the language of the Bengal region, which is divided between India and Bangladesh. It is written in the Bengali script and has a rich literary tradition that dates back to the 11th century. Bengali literature is known for its creativity and innovation, especially in the fields of poetry, drama, and fiction. Some of the eminent Bengali writers are Rabindranath Tagore, Kazi Nazrul Islam, Bankim Chandra Chattopadhyay, Sarat Chandra Chattopadhyay, etc.
  - Gujarati: It is the language of the Gujarat state in western India. It is written in the Gujarati script and has a long and varied history of literature, ranging from the religious texts of the Jain and Vaishnava sects to the modern novels and short stories of Govardhanram Tripathi, Ramanlal Vasantlal Desai, Umashankar Joshi, etc.
  - Marathi: It is the language of the Maharashtra state in western India. It is written in the Devanagari script and has a rich and diverse literary heritage, starting from the saint-poets of the Bhakti movement, such as Dnyaneshwar, Tukaram, Namdev, etc. to the modern writers of social reform and nationalism, such as Mahatma Jyotiba Phule, Lokmanya Tilak, Bal Gangadhar Tilak, etc.
