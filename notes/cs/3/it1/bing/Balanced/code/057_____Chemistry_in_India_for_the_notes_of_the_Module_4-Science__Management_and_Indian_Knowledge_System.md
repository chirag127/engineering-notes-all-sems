### Chemistry in India

- Chemistry is the branch of science that deals with the study of matter, its composition, structure, properties and reactions.
- India has a rich and ancient tradition of chemistry, dating back to the Vedic period (1500-500 BCE).
- Some of the notable contributions of Indian chemists are:

  - The concept of five elements (panchabhuta) as the basic constituents of matter, namely earth (prithvi), water (apas), fire (tejas), air (vayu) and ether (akasha).
  - The discovery of various metals and alloys, such as iron (ayas), copper (tamra), bronze (kamsya), brass (pitala), zinc (yashada), tin (trapu), lead (sisa), mercury (rasa) and gold (hiranya).
  - The development of metallurgical techniques, such as smelting, casting, forging, welding, soldering, quenching, tempering, annealing, polishing, gilding and inlaying.
  - The invention of various chemical apparatus, such as crucibles, furnaces, bellows, retorts, alembics, stills, condensers, filters, flasks, beakers, test tubes, pipettes, burettes, balances and weights.
  - The preparation of various chemical substances, such as acids (amlas), bases (ksharas), salts (lavanas), alkalis (sudhas), minerals (khanijas), ores (ayaskas), gems (ratnas), dyes (ranjakas), perfumes (gandhas), cosmetics (sringaras), medicines (ausadhas), poisons (vishas) and explosives (agnis).
  - The formulation of various chemical theories, such as atomic theory (anu-vada), molecular theory (parmanu-vada), qualitative analysis (gunapariksha), quantitative analysis (parimanapariksha), chemical equilibrium (samya), chemical affinity (samyoga), chemical kinetics (gatipariksha), chemical thermodynamics (ushmapariksha) and chemical nomenclature (namakarana).
  - The application of chemistry in various fields, such as agriculture (krishi), medicine (ayurveda), metallurgy (dhatu-vidya), alchemy (rasa-vidya), astronomy (jyotisha), astrology (jyotishya), art (shilpa), architecture (vastu), warfare (dhanur-vidya) and religion (dharma).

- Some of the eminent Indian chemists are:

  - Kanada (6th century BCE), who propounded the atomic theory and classified atoms into nine types based on their properties and combinations.
  - Charaka (2nd century BCE), who wrote the Charaka Samhita, a comprehensive treatise on medicine, pharmacology and surgery, and described various chemical methods of diagnosis, treatment and prevention of diseases.
  - Nagarjuna (8th century CE), who wrote the Rasaratnakara, a seminal work on alchemy, metallurgy and mineralogy, and discovered various metals and alloys, such as zinc, brass, steel and mercury.
  - Sushruta (6th century CE), who wrote the Sushruta Samhita, a pioneering work on surgery, anatomy and physiology, and described various chemical methods of sterilization, anesthesia and wound healing.
  - Aryabhata (5th century CE), who wrote the Aryabhatiya, a remarkable work on mathematics, astronomy and astrology, and explained various chemical phenomena, such as eclipses, tides, seasons and planetary motions.
  - Varahamihira (6th century CE), who wrote the Brihat Samhita, a vast encyclopedia on various subjects, and discussed various chemical aspects of weather, climate, agriculture, art, architecture and warfare.
  - Patanjali (2nd century BCE), who wrote the Yoga Sutras, a classic text on yoga, and described various chemical effects of breathing, meditation and concentration on the mind and body.
  - Bharadvaja (6th century BCE), who wrote the Yantra Sarvasva, a treatise on mechanical engineering, and described various chemical devices, such as flying machines, submarines, robots, weapons and fireworks.
