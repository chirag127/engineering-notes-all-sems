## Module 3- Indian Religion, Philosophy, and Practices

- India is a diverse and pluralistic country with many religions, philosophies, and practices that have influenced its culture and history.
- The major religions in India are Hinduism, Islam, Christianity, Sikhism, Buddhism, and Jainism. Each of these religions has its own scriptures, doctrines, rituals, ethics, and social institutions.
- Hinduism is the oldest and most dominant religion in India, with about 80% of the population identifying as Hindus. Hinduism is a complex and diverse tradition that encompasses many sects, schools, and movements. Hinduism is based on the Vedas, the ancient scriptures that contain hymns, rituals, and philosophical teachings. Hinduism also recognizes other sources of authority, such as the Upanishads, the Bhagavad Gita, the Puranas, and the epics of Ramayana and Mahabharata.
- Hinduism has many gods and goddesses, who are manifestations of the supreme reality called Brahman. Some of the most popular deities are Vishnu, Shiva, Ganesha, Lakshmi, Durga, and Saraswati. Hindus worship these deities in temples, shrines, homes, and festivals. Hindus also believe in the concepts of karma, dharma, samsara, and moksha. Karma is the law of cause and effect that determines one's destiny. Dharma is the moral duty and righteous conduct that one should follow. Samsara is the cycle of birth, death, and rebirth that one undergoes due to karma. Moksha is the liberation from samsara and the attainment of union with Brahman.
- Hinduism has many philosophical schools that explore the nature of reality, knowledge, and ethics. Some of the major schools are Samkhya, Yoga, Nyaya, Vaisheshika, Mimamsa, and Vedanta. Each school has its own methods, arguments, and views on various topics, such as the existence of God, the nature of the self, the sources of knowledge, and the goals of life.
- Hinduism also has many practices that aim to purify the body, mind, and soul. Some of the common practices are meditation, yoga, puja, mantra, pranayama, fasting, pilgrimage, and charity. Hinduism also prescribes various rites of passage, such as birth, initiation, marriage, and death, that mark the different stages of life.
- Islam is the second largest religion in India, with about 14% of the population identifying as Muslims. Islam is a monotheistic religion that believes in one God, Allah, and his final messenger, Muhammad. Islam is based on the Quran, the holy book that contains the revelations of Allah to Muhammad, and the Sunnah, the traditions and practices of Muhammad and his companions. Islam also recognizes other prophets, such as Adam, Noah, Abraham, Moses, and Jesus, who preached the same message of submission to Allah.
- Islam has five pillars, or basic obligations, that every Muslim should follow. They are shahada, or the declaration of faith; salat, or the five daily prayers; zakat, or the almsgiving to the poor; sawm, or the fasting during the month of Ramadan; and hajj, or the pilgrimage to Mecca once in a lifetime. Islam also has a legal system, called sharia, that regulates the personal, social, and political aspects of life according to the Quran and the Sunnah.
- Islam has many sects and schools that differ in their interpretation and application of the Quran and the Sunnah. The major sects are Sunni and Shia, who disagree on the succession and leadership of the Muslim community after Muhammad's death. The major schools are Hanafi, Maliki, Shafi'i, and Hanbali, who differ in their methods and sources of deriving the sharia. Islam also has many mystical and reformist movements, such as Sufism, Wahhabism, and Ahmadiyya, that have influenced the religious and cultural diversity of Indian Muslims.
