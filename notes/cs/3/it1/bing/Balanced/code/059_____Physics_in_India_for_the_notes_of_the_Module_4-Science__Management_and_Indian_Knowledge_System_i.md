# Physics in India

- Physics is the branch of science that deals with the nature and properties of matter and energy.
- India has a rich and ancient tradition of physics, dating back to the Vedic period (1500-500 BCE).
- Some of the contributions of Indian physicists are:

  - The concept of atomism, which states that all matter is composed of indivisible and indestructible atoms, was developed by the Vaisheshika school of philosophy, founded by Kanada (6th century BCE).
  - The concept of zero, which is essential for mathematics and physics, was invented by the Indian mathematicians around the 5th century CE.
  - The concept of shunya (void or emptiness), which is related to the quantum vacuum, was explored by the Buddhist and Jain philosophers, such as Nagarjuna (2nd century CE) and Umasvati (5th century CE).
  - The concept of relativity, which states that the laws of physics are the same in all inertial frames of reference, was implied by the Sankhya school of philosophy, founded by Kapila (6th century BCE).
  - The concept of gravity, which states that all objects attract each other with a force proportional to their masses and inversely proportional to the square of their distance, was proposed by the astronomer and mathematician Bhaskara II (12th century CE).
  - The concept of heliocentrism, which states that the Sun is the center of the solar system and the planets revolve around it, was advocated by the astronomer and mathematician Aryabhata (5th century CE).
  - The concept of infinitesimal calculus, which is the foundation of modern physics, was developed by the mathematicians of the Kerala school, such as Madhava (14th century CE), Nilakantha (15th century CE), and Jyesthadeva (16th century CE).
  - The concept of trigonometry, which is the study of the relations between the sides and angles of triangles, was advanced by the mathematicians of the Siddhanta school, such as Brahmagupta (7th century CE), Varahamihira (6th century CE), and Surya Siddhanta (4th century CE).

- Some of the modern Indian physicists are:

  - Jagadish Chandra Bose (1858-1937), who was a pioneer of radio and microwave optics, and demonstrated the existence of electromagnetic waves and their effects on living and non-living matter.
  - Satyendra Nath Bose (1894-1974), who was a co-discoverer of the Bose-Einstein statistics and the Bose-Einstein condensate, and collaborated with Albert Einstein on the theory of quantum mechanics.
  - Meghnad Saha (1893-1956), who was a founder of the Saha ionization equation and the Saha-Nordheim theory of white dwarfs, and contributed to the fields of astrophysics, spectroscopy, and thermodynamics.
  - C.V. Raman (1888-1970), who was a Nobel laureate for his discovery of the Raman effect, which is the scattering of light by molecules, and a pioneer of the Raman spectroscopy, which is a technique for analyzing the molecular structure of substances.
  - Homi J. Bhabha (1909-1966), who was a founder of the Indian nuclear program and the Tata Institute of Fundamental Research, and a developer of the Bhabha scattering and the Bhabha-Heitler theory of cosmic rays.
  - Subrahmanyan Chandrasekhar (1910-1995), who was a Nobel laureate for his theory of the Chandrasekhar limit and the Chandrasekhar mass, which determine the fate of massive stars, and a leader of the fields of stellar dynamics, black holes, and general relativity.
  - Abdus Salam (1926-1996), who was a Nobel laureate for his contribution to the electroweak unification theory, which explains the relation between the electromagnetic and weak nuclear forces, and a founder of the International Centre for Theoretical Physics.
  - Raja Ramanna (1925-2004), who was a director of the Indian nuclear program and the Bhabha Atomic Research Centre, and a pioneer of the nuclear fusion and the peaceful uses of nuclear energy
