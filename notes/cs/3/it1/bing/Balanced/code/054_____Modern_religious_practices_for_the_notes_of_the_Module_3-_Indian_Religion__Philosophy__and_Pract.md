### Modern religious practices in India

- India is a diverse and pluralistic country, where different religions coexist and influence each other. According to the 2011 census, the major religions practiced in India are Hinduism (79.8%), Islam (14.2%), Christianity (2.3%), Sikhism (1.7%), Buddhism (0.7%), and Jainism (0.4%). 
- Hinduism is the oldest and most dominant religion in India, with a variety of sects, schools, and traditions. Hinduism is based on the Vedas, the ancient scriptures that contain the teachings of the sages and the gods. Hinduism is also known for its concepts of karma, dharma, reincarnation, and moksha (liberation). Hinduism has many rituals, festivals, and pilgrimage sites, such as the Kumbh Mela, Diwali, Holi, and Varanasi. 
- Islam is the second-largest religion in India, with a long and rich history. Islam was introduced to India by Arab traders, invaders, and Sufi saints, who spread the message of the Quran and the Prophet Muhammad. Islam is divided into two main sects, Sunni and Shia, and has several sub-sects, such as Ahmadiyya, Barelvi, Deobandi, and Bohra. Islam has five pillars of faith, which are the declaration of faith, prayer, charity, fasting, and pilgrimage. Islam also has many festivals, such as Eid al-Fitr, Eid al-Adha, and Muharram. 
- Christianity is the third-largest religion in India, with a diverse and vibrant community. Christianity was brought to India by various missionaries, such as Thomas the Apostle, Saint Francis Xavier, and Mother Teresa. Christianity is based on the teachings of Jesus Christ and the Bible. Christianity has three main branches, which are Catholic, Protestant, and Orthodox, and has several denominations, such as Anglican, Baptist, Methodist, and Pentecostal. Christianity has many rituals, such as baptism, communion, and confession, and festivals, such as Christmas, Easter, and Good Friday. 
- Sikhism is the fourth-largest religion in India, with a distinct and dynamic identity. Sikhism was founded by Guru Nanak and his nine successors, who taught the principles of monotheism, equality, service, and justice. Sikhism is based on the Guru Granth Sahib, the holy scripture that contains the hymns and teachings of the Gurus and other saints. Sikhism has five symbols of faith, which are the uncut hair, the comb, the steel bracelet, the sword, and the shorts. Sikhism has many rituals, such as the initiation ceremony, the daily prayers, and the langar (community kitchen), and festivals, such as Guru Nanak Jayanti, Baisakhi, and Guru Gobind Singh Jayanti. 
- Buddhism is the fifth-largest religion in India, with a long and influential legacy. Buddhism was founded by Siddhartha Gautama, who became the Buddha, the enlightened one, after attaining nirvana, the state of freedom from suffering. Buddhism is based on the Four Noble Truths, which are the truth of suffering, the truth of the cause of suffering, the truth of the end of suffering, and the truth of the path to the end of suffering. Buddhism has three main branches, which are Theravada, Mahayana, and Vajrayana, and has several schools, such as Zen, Tibetan, and Pure Land. Buddhism has many rituals, such as meditation, chanting, and offering, and festivals, such as Vesak, Asalha Puja, and Bodhi Day. 
- Jainism is the sixth-largest religion in India, with a ancient and revered tradition. Jainism was founded by Mahavira, the last of the 24 Tirthankaras, who are the spiritual teachers and leaders of Jainism. Jainism is based on the doctrine of ahimsa, which is the principle of non-violence towards all living beings. Jainism has two main sects, which are Digambara and Svetambara, and has several sub-sects, such as Terapanthi, Sthanakvasi, and Shrimad Rajchandra. Jainism has many rituals, such as fasting, worship, and pilgrimage
