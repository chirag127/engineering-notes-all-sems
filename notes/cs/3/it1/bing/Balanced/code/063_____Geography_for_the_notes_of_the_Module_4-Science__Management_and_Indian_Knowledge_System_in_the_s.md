### Geography

- Geography is the study of the physical features of the earth and its atmosphere, and of human activity as it affects and is affected by these features.
- Geography has two main branches: physical geography and human geography. Physical geography deals with the natural environment, such as climate, landforms, vegetation, soils, and water resources. Human geography deals with the spatial aspects of human society and culture, such as population, urbanization, agriculture, industry, transport, and geopolitics.
- Geography is an interdisciplinary subject that draws on various fields of knowledge, such as history, economics, sociology, anthropology, and environmental science. Geography also uses various methods and tools, such as maps, statistics, remote sensing, and geographic information systems (GIS).
- Geography has a long and rich tradition in India, dating back to the ancient times. Some of the earliest geographers in India were the Vedic sages, who composed hymns describing the natural phenomena and the regions of the earth. The Vedas, the Upanishads, the Puranas, and the epics such as the Ramayana and the Mahabharata contain many geographical references and descriptions.
- Some of the notable geographers in India were Varahamihira (6th century CE), who wrote the Brihat Samhita, a compendium of astronomy, astrology, and geography; Kalhana (12th century CE), who wrote the Rajatarangini, a historical chronicle of Kashmir; Alberuni (11th century CE), a Persian scholar who visited India and wrote the Kitab al-Hind, a comprehensive account of India's geography, culture, and society; and Raja Todar Mal (16th century CE), who was the revenue minister of Akbar and devised a scientific system of land measurement and taxation.
- Geography in India has evolved over time, reflecting the changing political, economic, and social contexts. During the colonial period, geography was influenced by the British imperial interests and perspectives, and focused on the mapping and exploration of India's resources and territories. After independence, geography in India became more diversified and critical, and addressed the issues of regional development, planning, and environmental management. Today, geography in India is a vibrant and dynamic discipline, that engages with the global and local challenges of the 21st century, such as climate change, urbanization, globalization, and sustainability.
