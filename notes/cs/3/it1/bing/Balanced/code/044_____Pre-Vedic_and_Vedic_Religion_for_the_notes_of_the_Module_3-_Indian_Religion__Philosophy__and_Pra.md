### Pre-Vedic and Vedic Religion

- Pre-Vedic religion refers to the religious beliefs and practices of the people who lived in the Indus Valley Civilization (IVC) from around 2500 to 1500 BCE.
- Vedic religion refers to the religious beliefs and practices of the Indo-Aryan peoples who entered northwestern India from Persia around 1500 BCE and composed the four Vedas, the oldest scriptures of Hinduism.
- Some of the main features of Pre-Vedic and Vedic religion are:

  - Pre-Vedic religion was based on the worship of nature and natural forces, such as the sun, the moon, the fire, the water, the earth, the animals and the plants. The IVC people also revered both male and female aspects of divinity, as evidenced by the seals and figurines depicting various gods and goddesses.
  - Vedic religion was based on the worship of many gods and goddesses, who represented different aspects of nature, such as Indra (the god of thunder and rain), Varuna (the god of water and cosmic order), Agni (the god of fire and sacrifice), Surya (the sun god), and Rudra (the god of storms and destruction). The Vedic people also believed in the concept of rita, the cosmic law that governed the natural and moral order of the universe.
  - Pre-Vedic religion did not have a priestly class or a centralized authority. The IVC people performed rituals and ceremonies in their homes or in public places, such as the Great Bath or the fire altars. They also used symbols and signs, such as the swastika, the pipal tree, and the bull, to express their religious ideas.
  - Vedic religion had a priestly class, called the Brahmins, who were responsible for conducting the sacrifices and reciting the hymns from the Vedas. The Vedic people performed rituals and ceremonies in open-air altars, called yajnas, where they offered various substances, such as ghee, milk, grains, and animals, to the gods. They also used mantras, or sacred sounds, to invoke the gods and seek their blessings.
  - Pre-Vedic religion did not have a clear concept of life after death or reincarnation. The IVC people buried or cremated their dead and placed various objects, such as pottery, jewelry, and toys, in their graves. They also believed in the existence of spirits and ghosts, who could be appeased or warded off by charms and amulets.
  - Vedic religion had a clear concept of life after death and reincarnation. The Vedic people believed that the soul, or atman, was immortal and transmigrated from one body to another according to the law of karma, or the consequences of one's actions. They also believed in the existence of heaven and hell, where the soul enjoyed or suffered according to its deeds. They also believed in the concept of moksha, or liberation from the cycle of birth and death, which could be attained by following the path of knowledge, action, or devotion.
