### Astronomy in India

- Astronomy is the oldest branch of science in India, dating back to the Vedic period (1500-500 BCE).
- The earliest astronomical texts in India are the Vedanga Jyotisha, which deal with the measurement of time and the calendar, and the Siddhantas, which are treatises on mathematical astronomy.
- Some of the notable Indian astronomers and their contributions are:

  - Aryabhata (476-550 CE): He wrote the Aryabhatiya, which contains the earliest known description of the heliocentric model of the solar system, the calculation of the value of pi, the approximation of the sine function, and the solution of linear and quadratic equations.
  - Varahamihira (505-587 CE): He wrote the Panchasiddhantika, which summarizes five earlier astronomical systems, and the Brihat Samhita, which covers topics such as astrology, meteorology, geography, and architecture.
  - Brahmagupta (598-668 CE): He wrote the Brahmasphutasiddhanta, which contains the rules for arithmetic operations with zero and negative numbers, the solution of indeterminate equations, and the derivation of the area of a cyclic quadrilateral.
  - Bhaskara I (629-680 CE): He wrote the Mahabhaskariya, which contains the earliest known use of the decimal system, the approximation of the square root of 2, and the derivation of the sine and cosine functions.
  - Bhaskara II (1114-1185 CE): He wrote the Siddhanta Shiromani, which contains the proof of the Pythagorean theorem, the calculation of the value of e, the solution of Pell's equation, and the concept of differential calculus.
  - Nilakantha Somayaji (1444-1544 CE): He wrote the Tantrasangraha, which contains the most accurate model of the planetary orbits in the pre-telescopic era, based on the Tychonic system, and the Aryabhatiya Bhashya, which contains the derivation of the formula for the volume of a frustum.
  - Jyesthadeva (1500-1575 CE): He wrote the Yuktibhasha, which contains the first exposition of the calculus techniques developed by the Kerala school of astronomy and mathematics, such as the infinite series expansions of trigonometric functions, the Taylor series, and the mean value theorem.
  - Sawai Jai Singh II (1688-1743 CE): He built the Jantar Mantar observatories in Delhi, Jaipur, Ujjain, Varanasi, and Mathura, which are large-scale instruments for measuring celestial phenomena, such as the position of the sun, the moon, and the planets, the time of day, and the altitude of stars.
