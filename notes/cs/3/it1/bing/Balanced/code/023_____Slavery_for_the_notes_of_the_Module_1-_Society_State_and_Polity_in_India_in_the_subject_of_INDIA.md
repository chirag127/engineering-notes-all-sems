### Slavery in India

- Slavery in India is a historical and contemporary phenomenon that has existed in various forms and periods, from ancient times to the present day.
- Slavery in India can be broadly classified into two categories: domestic slavery and bonded labor.
- Domestic slavery refers to the practice of owning, buying, selling, or inheriting human beings as property, who are forced to work and serve their masters or mistresses without any rights or freedom.
- Bonded labor refers to the practice of forcing people to work for little or no wages, often to repay a debt or a loan, or as a form of punishment or exploitation.
- Slavery in India was influenced by various factors, such as caste, religion, gender, ethnicity, colonialism, and globalization.
- Some of the main features and impacts of slavery in India are:

  - Slavery in India was prevalent in both rural and urban areas, and affected people from different social and economic backgrounds.
  - Slavery in India was often justified by religious, legal, and cultural norms, such as the Hindu concept of varna (social order), the Islamic law of sharia, and the British colonial policies and laws.
  - Slavery in India was a source of cheap and abundant labor for various sectors, such as agriculture, mining, manufacturing, domestic service, and sex work.
  - Slavery in India was a form of violence and oppression that violated the human dignity and rights of the enslaved people, who suffered from physical, mental, and sexual abuse, exploitation, discrimination, and deprivation.
  - Slavery in India was a factor that shaped the social, economic, and political history and development of the country, as well as its relations with other regions and countries.
  - Slavery in India was challenged and resisted by various movements and individuals, such as the anti-slavery campaigns of the British abolitionists, the Indian social reformers, the nationalist leaders, and the enslaved people themselves.
  - Slavery in India continues to exist in the form of modern slavery, which includes practices such as human trafficking, forced labor, debt bondage, child labor, forced marriage, and commercial sexual exploitation.
