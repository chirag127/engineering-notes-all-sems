# Engineering and Architecture in Ancient India

- Engineering and architecture in ancient India was known as **Sthapatya-Kala**, which means the art of constructing something.
- Ancient Indian architects and engineers derived principles of construction and architecture based on experience, observation of natural phenomena and social and cultural aspects of India.
- Ancient India witnessed two urban revolutions: the first one in the Indus Valley Civilization (around 2500 BCE) and the second one in the Mauryan and Kushan periods (around 6th century BCE to 3rd century CE).
- The Indus Valley Civilization was remarkable for its urban planning, sanitation, drainage, water supply, standardized weights and measures, and monumental structures such as the Great Bath and the granaries.
- The Mauryan and Kushan periods saw the development of stone architecture, sculpture, stupa, chaitya, vihara, and rock-cut caves. The most famous examples are the Ashoka pillars, the Sanchi stupa, the Ajanta and Ellora caves, and the Kailasa temple  .
- Ancient India also developed various styles of temple architecture, such as the Nagara style in North India and the Dravida style in South India. These styles were characterized by distinctive features such as the shikhara (spire) and the vimana (tower) respectively.
- Ancient Indian engineering and architecture also included other aspects such as irrigation, metallurgy, astronomy, mathematics, and medicine. Some of the notable achievements are the stepwells, the iron pillar of Delhi, the Jantar Mantar observatories, and the Ayurvedic system of medicine .
