### Administration

- Administration is the process of managing the affairs of an organization or a state by implementing policies, rules and regulations.
- Administration in India has a long and rich history, dating back to the ancient times when various kingdoms and empires ruled over different regions of the subcontinent.
- Some of the features of administration in ancient India were:

  - The king was the supreme authority and the head of the administration. He was assisted by a council of ministers, advisers and officials.
  - The king was responsible for maintaining law and order, protecting the people from external and internal enemies, promoting welfare and justice, and upholding dharma (righteousness).
  - The king was also expected to follow the principles of rajadharma (duty of the ruler), which included being virtuous, benevolent, impartial, generous, courageous, and respectful of the elders, sages and gods.
  - The administration was divided into various departments, such as revenue, military, judiciary, foreign affairs, education, religion, etc. Each department had its own hierarchy of officials and functionaries.
  - The administration was also decentralized into various units, such as provinces, districts, towns, villages, etc. Each unit had its own local administration, headed by a governor, a magistrate, a chief, a headman, etc.
  - The administration was based on the principles of varna (social class) and ashrama (stage of life), which determined the rights and duties of the people. The four varnas were brahmins (priests and scholars), kshatriyas (warriors and rulers), vaishyas (merchants and farmers), and shudras (servants and laborers). The four ashramas were brahmacharya (student), grihastha (householder), vanaprastha (retired), and sannyasa (renunciant).
  - The administration was also influenced by the religious and philosophical traditions of Hinduism, Buddhism, Jainism, etc. These traditions provided the ethical and moral values, the concepts of dharma (duty), karma (action), moksha (liberation), etc., and the ideals of ahimsa (non-violence), satya (truth), shanti (peace), etc.
  - The administration was also marked by the diversity and plurality of cultures, languages, regions, etc. that existed in India. The administration had to accommodate and respect the differences and similarities among the people, and foster a sense of unity and harmony.
