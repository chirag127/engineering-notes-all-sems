## Module 4-Science, Management and Indian Knowledge System

This module covers the following topics:

- Astronomy in India: The development of astronomy in India from the Vedic period to the modern era, including the contributions of Aryabhata, Brahmagupta, Varahamihira, Bhaskara, and others. The concepts of heliocentrism, lunar and solar eclipses, planetary motions, and cosmology in Indian astronomy. The astronomical instruments and observatories in India, such as the Jantar Mantar and the Aryabhatiya. The influence of Indian astronomy on other cultures and civilizations, such as the Arabs, the Greeks, and the Chinese  .

- Chemistry in India: The origin and evolution of chemistry in India from the ancient to the medieval period, including the concepts of alchemy, metallurgy, medicine, and dyeing. The achievements of Indian chemists, such as Nagarjuna, Kanada, Charaka, Sushruta, and others. The discovery and synthesis of various metals, alloys, minerals, salts, acids, bases, and organic compounds in India. The techniques and methods of distillation, calcination, sublimation, extraction, and purification in Indian chemistry. The applications of chemistry in various fields, such as medicine, agriculture, cosmetics, textiles, and warfare .

- Mathematics in India: The origin and development of mathematics in India from the Indus Valley Civilization to the colonial period, including the contributions of Baudhayana, Pingala, Panini, Katyayana, Apastamba, and others. The concepts of zero, decimal system, algebra, geometry, trigonometry, calculus, and combinatorics in Indian mathematics. The mathematical texts and treatises in India, such as the Sulba Sutras, the Chandas Shastra, the Shulba Sutra, the Aryabhatiya, the Brahmasphutasiddhanta, the Lilavati, and others. The influence of Indian mathematics on other cultures and civilizations, such as the Arabs, the Europeans, and the Chinese  .

- Physics in India: The origin and growth of physics in India from the Vedic period to the modern era, including the contributions of Kapila, Kanada, Gautama, Patanjali, Bharadvaja, and others. The concepts of atomism, motion, force, gravity, sound, light, heat, electricity, and magnetism in Indian physics. The physical theories and models in India, such as the Sankhya, the Vaisheshika, the Nyaya, the Yoga, and the Vedanta. The experiments and observations in Indian physics, such as the measurement of time, the determination of the speed of sound, the estimation of the circumference of the earth, and the invention of the iron pillar .

- Agriculture in India: The history and evolution of agriculture in India from the Neolithic period to the present day, including the domestication of plants and animals, the development of irrigation systems, the introduction of crops and techniques, and the impact of climate and geography. The achievements and innovations of Indian farmers, such as the cultivation of rice, wheat, cotton, sugarcane, spices, fruits, and vegetables, the use of fertilizers, pesticides, and biocontrol agents, the adoption of organic farming, and the improvement of crop yield and quality. The challenges and opportunities of agriculture in India, such as the issues of food security, sustainability, biodiversity, and globalization .

- Medicine in India: The origin and development of medicine in India from the ancient to the modern period, including the contributions of Charaka, Sushruta, Vagbhata, Madhava, and others. The concepts and principles of Ayurveda, the traditional system of medicine in India, such as the theory of tridosha, the classification of diseases, the diagnosis and treatment methods, and the pharmacology and surgery. The medical texts and treatises in India, such as the Charaka Samhita, the Sushruta Samhita, the Ashtanga Hridaya, and the Madhava Nidana. The influence and integration of Indian medicine with other systems of medicine, such as the Unani, the Siddha, the Tibetan, and the modern  .
