### Socio-religious reform movement of 19th century India

- Socio-religious reform movement refers to the efforts made by various individuals and organizations to reform the social and religious practices of Indian society in the 19th century.
- The main causes of the emergence of these movements were:
  - The spread of western education and liberal ideas among the educated Indians, which exposed them to the rational and scientific outlook of the Enlightenment and the French Revolution.
  - The contact with the British rule and culture, which made the Indians aware of the political, economic and social backwardness of their country and the need for modernization and progress.
  - The influence of Christian missionaries, who criticized the Hindu religion and society for their superstitions, idolatry, caste system, and oppression of women and lower castes.
  - The rise of nationalism and patriotism among the Indians, who felt the need to revive and reform their ancient culture and heritage and to resist the cultural imperialism of the British.
- The main types of these movements were:
  - The reformist movements, which aimed at reforming the existing social and religious institutions and practices within the framework of Hinduism. They advocated the principles of monotheism, rationalism, humanism, and social equality. Some examples of these movements are the Brahmo Samaj, the Prarthana Samaj, the Aligarh Movement, and the Ramakrishna Mission.
  - The revivalist movements, which aimed at reviving the ancient and pure form of Hinduism and rejecting the foreign influences and corruptions. They advocated the principles of Vedanta, Vedic authority, and Hindu nationalism. Some examples of these movements are the Arya Samaj, the Ram Rajya Parishad, and the Hindu Mahasabha.
  - The radical movements, which aimed at creating a new social and religious order by rejecting the existing Hinduism and its scriptures. They advocated the principles of atheism, materialism, and social revolution. Some examples of these movements are the Young Bengal Movement, the Brahmo Samaj of India, and the Satyashodhak Samaj.
