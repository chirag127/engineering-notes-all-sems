# Mathematics in India

- Mathematics in India is the discipline of mathematics as it developed in the Indian subcontinent from 1200 BCE until the end of the 18th century.
- Mathematics in India is characterized by a rich diversity of topics, methods, and styles, influenced by various cultural, religious, and philosophical traditions.
- Mathematics in India can be divided into four broad periods: the Vedic period (1200-500 BCE), the classical period (400-1200 CE), the medieval period (1200-1600 CE), and the modern period (1600-1800 CE).
- Some of the main achievements and contributions of Indian mathematics are:

  - The development of the decimal place-value system and the use of zero as a number, which are the basis of the modern number system .
  - The discovery of various algebraic identities, such as the Pythagorean theorem, the binomial theorem, and the quadratic formula.
  - The invention of trigonometry, including the sine, cosine, and tangent functions, and the calculation of accurate values of pi and other mathematical constants.
  - The formulation of the concept of infinity and the recognition of different levels of infinity, such as countable and uncountable infinities.
  - The advancement of geometry, including the study of conic sections, polygons, and circles, and the construction of geometric proofs and algorithms.
  - The creation of calculus, including the concepts of differentiation, integration, series, and limits, and the application of calculus to astronomy, physics, and engineering.
  - The exploration of combinatorics, number theory, and cryptography, including the study of permutations, combinations, partitions, prime numbers, and congruences.

- Some of the notable mathematicians of India are:

  - Aryabhata (476-550 CE), who wrote the Aryabhatiya, a treatise on astronomy and mathematics, which contains the earliest known use of zero, the approximation of pi, and the solution of linear and quadratic equations.
  - Brahmagupta (598-668 CE), who wrote the Brahmasphutasiddhanta, a treatise on arithmetic, algebra, and geometry, which contains the rules for zero, negative numbers, and fractions, and the solution of indeterminate equations.
  - Bhaskara II (1114-1185 CE), who wrote the Siddhanta Siromani, a comprehensive work on mathematics and astronomy, which contains the proof of the Pythagorean theorem, the derivation of the binomial theorem, and the development of calculus.
  - Varahamihira (505-587 CE), who wrote the Panchasiddhantika, a summary of five earlier astronomical works, which contains the trigonometric tables, the calculation of eclipses, and the measurement of the Earth's circumference.
  - Madhava of Sangamagrama (1350-1425 CE), who founded the Kerala school of mathematics and astronomy, which made significant contributions to calculus, series, and infinite sums, and calculated the value of pi up to 11 decimal places.

- Mathematics in India reflects the diversity and richness of the Indian culture and civilization, and has influenced the development of mathematics in other regions of the world, such as China, Arabia, and Europe .
