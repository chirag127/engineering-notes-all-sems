### Shankaracharya

- Shankaracharya is a title given to the heads of monastic institutions (mathas) in the Advaita Vedanta tradition of Hinduism. The title means "teacher of the way of Shankara", referring to the 8th-century philosopher and theologian Adi Shankara, who is considered the founder of the Advaita Vedanta school and the reviver of the Vedic tradition in India.
- Adi Shankara was born in a Brahmin family in Kaladi, Kerala, around the 8th century CE. He was a prodigy who mastered the Vedas, the Upanishads, and other scriptures at a young age. He renounced the worldly life and became a wandering monk, seeking the ultimate truth of reality.
- Adi Shankara's main contribution was his interpretation of the Vedanta philosophy, which is based on the Upanishads, the Brahma Sutras, and the Bhagavad Gita. He taught that the ultimate reality (Brahman) is one, without a second, and that the individual self (Atman) is identical with Brahman. He rejected the dualistic and pluralistic views of other schools of Hindu philosophy, and argued that the apparent diversity and multiplicity of the world is an illusion (Maya) caused by ignorance (Avidya) of the true nature of reality.
- Adi Shankara also established four mathas in the four corners of India, namely Sringeri in the south, Dwarka in the west, Puri in the east, and Badrinath in the north. He appointed four of his disciples as the heads of these mathas, and gave them the authority to propagate his teachings and ordain new monks. These four mathas are known as the amnaya mathas, and their heads are the Shankaracharyas of the respective regions.
- Adi Shankara also wrote many commentaries (bhashyas) on the Vedanta scriptures, as well as original works (prakaranas) on various topics of philosophy, ethics, devotion, and logic. Some of his famous works are the Vivekachudamani, the Upadesasahasri, the Brahma Sutra Bhashya, the Upanishad Bhashya, and the Bhagavad Gita Bhashya. He also composed many hymns (stotras) in praise of various deities, such as the Shivananda Lahari, the Saundaryalahari, and the Bhaja Govindam.
- Adi Shankara is widely regarded as one of the greatest thinkers and teachers of India, and his influence on Hindu thought and culture is immense. He is revered as a saint, a philosopher, a poet, and a reformer by millions of Hindus across the world. He is also respected by other traditions, such as Buddhism, Jainism, and Sikhism, for his intellectual acumen and spiritual insight.
