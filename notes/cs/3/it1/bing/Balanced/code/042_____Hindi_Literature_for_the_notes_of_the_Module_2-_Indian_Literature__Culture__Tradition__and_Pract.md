### Hindi Literature

Hindi literature is the literature written in the Hindi language, which includes various dialects and scripts. Hindi literature has a rich and diverse history, spanning from the medieval to the modern period. Some of the main features and topics of Hindi literature are:

- Hindi literature originated from the poetry of Apabhraṃśa, a group of languages derived from Prakrit, which were spoken in northern India from the 7th to the 13th century. Some of the prominent Apabhraṃśa poets were Vidyapati, Kabir, and Tulsidas.
- Hindi literature can be divided into three broad styles: prose (gadya), poetry (padya), and prosimetrum (campū). Prose includes genres such as novels, short stories, essays, and biographies. Poetry includes genres such as epics, lyrics, ballads, and bhakti (devotional) poems. Prosimetrum is a mixed form of prose and poetry, often used for satire, humor, and allegory.
- Hindi literature reflects the cultural, religious, social, and political aspects of India. Some of the common themes of Hindi literature are Hinduism, Islam, Buddhism, Jainism, Sikhism, nationalism, colonialism, feminism, caste, class, and gender. Some of the influential Hindi writers who explored these themes are Premchand, Jaishankar Prasad, Mahadevi Varma, Harivansh Rai Bachchan, Munshi Premchand, Bhisham Sahni, and Mahasweta Devi .
- Hindi literature has also been influenced by other Indian and foreign languages and literatures, such as Sanskrit, Persian, Urdu, English, Bengali, and Marathi. Some of the examples of such influences are the adaptation of Sanskrit epics like Ramayana and Mahabharata, the incorporation of Persian and Urdu words and styles, the translation of English and Bengali novels and poems, and the use of Marathi folk songs and stories.
