### Drama

Drama is a form of performing art that involves actors, dialogue, and a stage. Drama can be used to entertain, educate, or express ideas and emotions. Drama is also a reflection of the culture and society of the people who create and perform it.

Some of the main points to note about drama in Indian culture and society are:

- Drama in India has a long and rich history, dating back to the ancient times. According to the Natyashastra, a treatise on the art of performance, drama was a gift from the gods to the humans.
- Drama in India is influenced by various factors, such as religion, mythology, folklore, history, politics, and social issues. Drama in India also incorporates elements of music, dance, poetry, and spectacle.
- Drama in India is not a homogenous entity, but rather a diverse and dynamic phenomenon that varies across regions, languages, communities, and genres. There are many forms of traditional and folk theatre in India, such as Ramleela, Nautanki, Yakshagana, Kathakali, Chhau, Bhand Pather, Tamasha, and Jatra.
- Drama in India also underwent changes and innovations in the modern period, especially after the colonial rule and the independence movement. Many playwrights and theatre practitioners experimented with new themes, styles, and techniques, such as realism, naturalism, symbolism, expressionism, absurdism, and political theatre. Some of the prominent figures of modern Indian theatre are Rabindranath Tagore, Mohan Rakesh, Vijay Tendulkar, Girish Karnad, Badal Sircar, Mahesh Dattani, and Mahasweta Devi.
- Drama in India is also a medium of social commentary and critique, as it reflects the realities and challenges of the contemporary society. Drama in India addresses issues such as gender, caste, class, religion, violence, corruption, and globalization. Drama in India also celebrates the diversity and resilience of the Indian people and their culture.
