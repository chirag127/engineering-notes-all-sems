### Socio-religious reform movement of 19th century

- The socio-religious reform movement of 19th century was a wave of reforming activities that aimed to modernize and rationalize the Indian society and religion in the light of western education and liberal ideas .
- The movement started in Bengal and soon spread to other parts of India, such as Bombay, Madras, Punjab, and Aligarh .
- The movement was influenced by various factors, such as:
  - The impact of British colonial rule, which exposed the Indians to new political, economic, and social changes .
  - The contact with western culture and thought, which challenged the traditional beliefs and practices of the Indians .
  - The rise of a new class of educated and progressive Indians, who questioned the authority of the orthodox and conservative sections of the society .
  - The emergence of a nationalist sentiment, which inspired the Indians to revive their cultural and religious heritage and assert their identity .
- The movement had two main types of reformers, namely:
  - The revivalists, who sought to revive the ancient and pure form of Hinduism and reject the foreign influences and corruptions . They advocated the authority of the Vedas and the Upanishads, and opposed idolatry, polytheism, rituals, priesthood, caste system, and social evils . Some examples of revivalist movements are the Arya Samaj, the Ramakrishna Mission, and the Theosophical Society .
  - The reformists, who sought to reform the Hinduism and society by incorporating the western values of reason, humanism, and democracy . They advocated the principles of monotheism, universalism, social equality, and human rights . Some examples of reformist movements are the Brahmo Samaj, the Prarthana Samaj, and the Aligarh Movement .
- The movement had a significant impact on the Indian society and religion, such as:
  - It challenged the orthodoxy and superstition of the Hinduism and promoted a rational and scientific outlook .
  - It raised the status and condition of women and advocated their education, emancipation, and empowerment .
  - It opposed the social evils and practices such as child marriage, sati, polygamy, untouchability, and caste discrimination .
  - It fostered a sense of unity and solidarity among the Indians and prepared the ground for the growth of Indian nationalism .
  - It enriched the Indian culture and literature by producing many eminent thinkers, writers, and social reformers .
