### Six System Indian Philosophy

- The six systems of Indian philosophy are Nyaya, Vaisheshika, Samkhya, Yoga, Purva Mimamsa and Uttara Mimamsa or Vedanta.
- These systems represent the intellectual exploration of truth that originated in the Indian subcontinent over centuries.
- Each system has its own founder, concepts, phenomena, laws and dogmas that differ from the others.
- The six systems are briefly explained below:

  - **Nyaya:** Founded by Akshapada Gautama in the 2nd century BCE, this system deals with logic and epistemology. It analyzes the means of knowledge and the validity of inference. It also discusses topics such as perception, inference, analogy, testimony, self-awareness, negation, error, doubt, debate, fallacies, syllogism, causation, substance, quality, action, relation, number, eternity, God, liberation, etc.   
  - **Vaisheshika:** Founded by Kanada Kashyapa in the 6th century BCE, this system is known as the atomistic school of Indian philosophy. It postulates that all objects in the physical universe are reducible to a finite number of atoms. It also classifies the categories of being as substance, quality, action, generality, particularity and inherence. It also deals with topics such as perception, inference, comparison, verbal testimony, negation, non-existence, number, measure, contact, disjunction, priority, posteriority, cognition, pleasure, pain, desire, aversion, effort, merit, demerit, bondage, liberation, etc.  
  - **Samkhya:** Founded by Kapila Muni in the 6th century BCE, this system is an extremely complex school of philosophy that goes deep into the understanding of the creator and the creation. It is based on the dualism of purusha (spirit) and prakriti (matter). It also enumerates the 25 principles of reality, which include the five elements, the five senses, the five organs of action, the mind, the intellect, the ego, the unmanifested prakriti and the countless purushas. It also deals with topics such as evolution, involution, bondage, liberation, causation, manifestation, dissolution, etc.  
  - **Yoga:** Founded by Patanjali in the 2nd century BCE, this system is closely related to Samkhya and is also known as Ashtanga Yoga or the eight-limbed yoga. It is a practical discipline that aims at the union of the individual soul with the supreme soul through the control of the body, the senses, the mind and the intellect. It also prescribes the eight steps of yoga, which are yama (restraint), niyama (observance), asana (posture), pranayama (breath control), pratyahara (withdrawal of senses), dharana (concentration), dhyana (meditation) and samadhi (absorption). It also deals with topics such as the nature of the self, the obstacles to yoga, the powers of yoga, the stages of samadhi, the means of liberation, etc.  
  - **Purva Mimamsa:** Founded by Jaimini in the 3rd century BCE, this system is also known as the Karma Mimamsa or the ritualistic school of Indian philosophy. It is mainly concerned with the interpretation and application of the Vedic injunctions and the performance of sacrifices and rituals. It also analyzes the nature and validity of knowledge, the sources of knowledge, the means of knowledge, the objects of knowledge, the relation between language and reality, the nature of dharma, the role of action, the theory of karma, the concept of apurva, the doctrine of svatah pramana, the methods of exegesis, etc.
