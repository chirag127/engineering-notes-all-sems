### Modern religious practices in India

- India is a secular country with a diverse and pluralistic religious landscape. According to the 2011 census, the major religions practiced in India are Hinduism (79.8%), Islam (14.2%), Christianity (2.3%), Sikhism (1.7%), Buddhism (0.7%) and Jainism (0.4%). 
- Hinduism is the oldest and most dominant religion in India, with a variety of sects, schools, traditions and practices. Hinduism is based on the concept of dharma, or the cosmic order, and the belief in reincarnation and karma. Hinduism has many sacred texts, rituals, festivals, temples, gods and goddesses, and ethical values. Some of the common practices of Hinduism include worshiping idols, performing puja, celebrating Diwali, observing fasts, practicing yoga and meditation, and going on pilgrimages. 
- Islam is the second largest religion in India, with a majority of Sunni Muslims and a minority of Shia Muslims. Islam is based on the belief in one God, Allah, and the teachings of Prophet Muhammad, as revealed in the Quran and the Hadith. Islam has five pillars of faith, which are the declaration of faith, prayer, charity, fasting and pilgrimage. Some of the common practices of Islam include praying five times a day, giving zakat, observing Ramadan, celebrating Eid, wearing modest clothing, and avoiding alcohol and pork. 
- Christianity is the third largest religion in India, with a majority of Roman Catholics and a minority of Protestants, Orthodox and other denominations. Christianity is based on the belief in one God, the Trinity, and the teachings of Jesus Christ, as recorded in the Bible. Christianity has two sacraments, which are baptism and communion. Some of the common practices of Christianity include attending church, reading the Bible, celebrating Christmas and Easter, following the Ten Commandments, and doing good works. 
- Sikhism is the fourth largest religion in India, with a majority of Khalsa Sikhs and a minority of Sahajdhari Sikhs. Sikhism is based on the belief in one God, Waheguru, and the teachings of the ten Gurus, as compiled in the Guru Granth Sahib. Sikhism has five articles of faith, which are the kesh, kanga, kara, kachera and kirpan. Some of the common practices of Sikhism include reciting the Mul Mantar, wearing the turban, visiting the gurdwara, celebrating Vaisakhi, following the Rehat Maryada, and serving the community. 
- Buddhism is the fifth largest religion in India, with a majority of Mahayana Buddhists and a minority of Theravada and Vajrayana Buddhists. Buddhism is based on the belief in the Four Noble Truths and the Eightfold Path, as taught by the Buddha. Buddhism has three jewels, which are the Buddha, the Dharma and the Sangha. Some of the common practices of Buddhism include taking refuge, following the precepts, meditating, chanting, celebrating Vesak, and venerating the relics and statues of the Buddha. 
- Jainism is the sixth largest religion in India, with a majority of Digambara Jains and a minority of Svetambara Jains. Jainism is based on the belief in the principle of ahimsa, or non-violence, and the teachings of the 24 Tirthankaras, or spiritual teachers. Jainism has three jewels, which are the right faith, right knowledge and right conduct. Some of the common practices of Jainism include observing the five vows, practicing asceticism, fasting, celebrating Mahavir Jayanti, and performing rituals such as puja and samayika. 
- India's religious groups share several religious practices and beliefs, such as the belief in reincarnation, karma, dharma, ahimsa, and the concept of guru. They also engage in practices such as pilgrimage, charity, fasting, meditation, and worship. However, they also have distinct differences in their doctrines, rituals, festivals, scriptures, and symbols. India's religious diversity is a source of both harmony and conflict, as well as a reflection of its rich cultural heritage.
