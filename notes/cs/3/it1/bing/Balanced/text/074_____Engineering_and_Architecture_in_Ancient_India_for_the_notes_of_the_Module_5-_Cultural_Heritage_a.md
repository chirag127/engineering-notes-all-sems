### Engineering and Architecture in Ancient India

- Engineering and architecture in ancient India was known as **Sthapatya-Kala**, which means the art of constructing something.
- Ancient Indian architects and engineers derived principles of construction and architecture based on experience, observation of natural phenomena and social and cultural aspects of India.
- Ancient India witnessed two urban revolutions: the first one in the **Harappan civilization** (2500-1900 BCE) and the second one in the **Mauryan and Kushan empires** (6th century BCE-3rd century CE).
- The Harappan civilization is known for its **urban planning**, **drainage systems**, **granaries**, **dockyards**, **seals**, **weights and measures** and **script**.
- The Mauryan and Kushan empires are known for their **stone pillars**, **stupas**, **rock-cut caves**, **sculptures** and **temples** .
- Ancient India also developed two distinct styles of **Hindu temple architecture**: the **Nagara style** in North India and the **Dravida style** in South India.
- The Nagara style features a **shikhara** (spire) over the **garbhagriha** (sanctum) and a **mandapa** (hall) in front of it.
- The Dravida style features a **vimana** (tower) over the **garbhagriha** and a **gopuram** (gateway) at the entrance.
- Some of the most remarkable examples of ancient Indian engineering and architecture are the **Kailasa temple** at Ellora, the **Brihadeshwara temple** at Thanjavur, the **Konark Sun temple**, the **Khajuraho temples**, the **Ajanta and Ellora caves**, the **Sanchi stupa**, the **Qutub Minar**, the **Iron pillar of Delhi** and the **Stepwells of Gujarat and Rajasthan** .
