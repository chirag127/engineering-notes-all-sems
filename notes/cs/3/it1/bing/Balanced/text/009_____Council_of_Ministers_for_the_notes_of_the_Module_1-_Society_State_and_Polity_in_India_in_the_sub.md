### Council of Ministers

- The Council of Ministers is the principal executive organ of the Government of India, which functions as the senior decision making body of the executive branch.
- It is chaired by the Prime Minister, who is the leader of the executive of the Government of India .
- It consists of the heads of each of the executive government ministries, who are further classified as Cabinet Ministers, Ministers of State, and Ministers of State (Independent Charge) .
- The President of India appoints the Prime Minister and other ministers on the advice of the Prime Minister.
- The Council of Ministers is collectively responsible to the Lok Sabha, the lower house of the Parliament of India .
- The Council of Ministers exercises executive authority in India and implements the policies and laws enacted by the Parliament .
- The Council of Ministers also advises the President on various matters, such as appointment of judges, governors, ambassadors, etc.
- The Council of Ministers can be dissolved by the President on the advice of the Prime Minister or when the Prime Minister resigns or dies.
- The Council of Ministers is assisted by various secretaries, advisers, and other officials in the administration of the government.
- The current Council of Ministers was formed on 7 July 2021, after the reshuffle and expansion of the previous council by Prime Minister Narendra Modi .
