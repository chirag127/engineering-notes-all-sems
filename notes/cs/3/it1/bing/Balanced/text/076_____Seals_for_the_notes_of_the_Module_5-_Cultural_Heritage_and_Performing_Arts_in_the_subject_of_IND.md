### Seals for the notes of the Module 5- Cultural Heritage and Performing Arts in the subject of INDIAN TRADITIONS, CULTURAL AND SOCIETY

- Seals are small objects that have an engraved design on one side and are used to make an impression on a soft material, such as clay or wax, to authenticate documents, seal containers, or indicate ownership.
- Seals have been used in various cultures and civilizations across the world, including India, where they have a long and rich history.
- Seals of ancient India give us useful information about the civilization of the Indus Valley, which flourished from about 2500 to 1900 BCE in the northwest of the Indian subcontinent. Seals were used in ancient times for trade, administration, and ritual purposes.
- The seals of the Indus Valley are mostly rectangular, circular, or cylindrical in shape, and are made of steatite, a soft stone that can be easily carved. They have an engraved design on one side, usually featuring an animal, a plant, or a geometric pattern, and a short inscription in an undeciphered script on the other side .
- The animals depicted on the Indus seals include buffaloes, rhinoceroses, elephants, tigers, bulls, and unicorns (that is, animals with a single horn). Some of these animals are native to the region, while others may have been imported from other lands. The animals are always shown in profile and sometimes standing alongside feeding troughs. The animals may represent the power, wealth, or status of the seal owners, or may have a symbolic or religious meaning .
- The plants depicted on the Indus seals include trees, such as the pipal tree, which is considered sacred in Hinduism and Buddhism, and flowers, such as the lotus, which is a symbol of purity and enlightenment. The plants may also have a symbolic or religious meaning, or may indicate the fertility and prosperity of the land .
- The geometric patterns depicted on the Indus seals include circles, squares, triangles, and swastikas. The swastika is an ancient symbol of good luck and auspiciousness in many cultures, including India. The geometric patterns may have a mathematical or astronomical significance, or may represent abstract concepts or deities .
- The inscriptions on the Indus seals are written in a script that has not been deciphered yet, despite many attempts by scholars and linguists. The script consists of about 400 signs, some of which resemble pictograms, while others are more abstract. The inscriptions are usually very short, consisting of one to five signs, and are read from right to left. The inscriptions may indicate the name, title, or profession of the seal owners, or may have a religious or magical function .
- The Indus seals were used to make impressions on clay tablets, sealings, or tags, which were attached to goods, documents, or containers. The impressions served as a mark of identity, authority, or ownership, and also as a protection against tampering or theft. The seals may have also been used as amulets or talismans, to ward off evil or bring good fortune .
- The Indus seals are a valuable source of information about the culture, society, economy, and religion of the Indus Valley civilization, which remains largely mysterious and enigmatic. The seals reflect the artistic skill, technological innovation, and cultural diversity of the people who made and used them. The seals also raise many questions and challenges for researchers who try to decipher their meaning and significance .
