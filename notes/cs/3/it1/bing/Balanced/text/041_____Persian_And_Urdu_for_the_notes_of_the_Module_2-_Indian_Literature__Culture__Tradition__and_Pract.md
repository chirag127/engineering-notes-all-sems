### Persian And Urdu

- Persian and Urdu are two languages that have influenced the literary and cultural traditions of India, especially in the northern and central regions.
- Persian was the official language of the Mughal Empire, which ruled over most of India from the 16th to the 18th centuries. Persian was also the language of court, administration, literature, and art in many regional kingdoms and sultanates that emerged after the decline of the Mughals.
- Urdu is a language that developed from the interaction of Persian, Arabic, and local languages such as Hindi, Punjabi, and Sindhi. Urdu was initially a spoken language of the common people, but later became a literary language that expressed the cultural and religious diversity of India.
- Some of the features of Persian and Urdu that have enriched the Indian literary and cultural traditions are:

  - The use of poetic forms such as ghazal, qasida, masnavi, rubai, and nazm, which have a distinct rhyme, meter, and structure.
  - The use of metaphors, imagery, symbolism, and allegory to convey complex and subtle meanings and emotions.
  - The use of themes such as love, mysticism, spirituality, social justice, and patriotism, which reflect the values and aspirations of the Indian people.
  - The use of words and phrases that have become part of the common vocabulary of many Indian languages, such as khush, shukriya, adaab, salaam, jannat, firdaus, etc.
  - The use of scripts such as Nastaliq and Devanagari, which have a distinctive aesthetic and calligraphic appeal.
