### Sangama Literature

- Sangama literature is the oldest literature to have been composed in the Tamilakam, the ancient region of South India that corresponds to the modern states of Tamil Nadu and Kerala .
- The word Sangama means "assembly" or "academy" and refers to three legendary gatherings of Tamil poets and scholars that lasted for several centuries and produced a vast body of literary works  .
- The first Sangama was said to be held at Then Madurai, the second at Kapalapuram, and the third at Madurai. The exact dates and locations of these Sangamas are uncertain, but they are generally believed to span from the 3rd century BCE to the 3rd century CE .
- Sangama literature consists of poems, grammar, epics and Tirukkural, a collection of ethical maxims. The poems are divided into two main categories: akam (love) and puram (heroism). Akam poems deal with various aspects of love, such as courtship, separation, union, and infidelity. Puram poems deal with various aspects of heroism, such as war, valor, generosity, and praise of kings and their deeds  .
- Sangama literature is remarkable for its secular and realistic portrayal of human emotions, social life, and natural scenery. It also reflects the cultural diversity and richness of the Tamilakam, which was influenced by various religions, such as Hinduism, Buddhism, Jainism, and Greek and Roman mythology  .
- Sangama literature had fallen into oblivion for much of the second millennium of the common era, but was preserved by and rediscovered in the monasteries of Hinduism, particularly those related to Shaivism near Kumbakonam, by colonial-era scholars in the late nineteenth century. It is now regarded as one of the finest and oldest examples of classical Tamil literature and a valuable source of historical and cultural information .
