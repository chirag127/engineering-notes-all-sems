## Module 5- Cultural Heritage and Performing Arts

- Cultural heritage is the legacy of physical and intangible attributes of a group or society that are inherited from past generations, maintained in the present and bestowed for the benefit of future generations.
- Cultural heritage includes tangible culture (such as buildings, monuments, landscapes, books, works of art, and artefacts), intangible culture (such as folklore, traditions, language, and knowledge), and natural heritage (such as culturally significant landscapes, and biodiversity).
- Performing arts are forms of art that use the body, voice, or objects to convey artistic expression, such as music, dance, theatre, opera, mime, puppetry, circus arts, and performance art.
- Performing arts are often performed in front of an audience, but may also be performed for a specific purpose, such as ritual, education, or social change.
- Performing arts are part of the intangible cultural heritage of many cultures and civilizations, as they reflect the creativity, values, identity, and history of the people who practice them.
- Performing arts can also contribute to the safeguarding and transmission of cultural heritage, as they can help to preserve, document, and revitalize the oral traditions, languages, skills, and knowledge that are associated with them.
- Some examples of performing arts that are recognized by UNESCO as intangible cultural heritage are:

  - Urtiin Duu, traditional folk long song of Mongolia, which is sung in a specific vocal technique that requires great skill and endurance.
  - Kutiyattam, Sanskrit theatre of India, which is one of the oldest living theatrical traditions in the world, dating back to more than 2000 years.
  - Flamenco, an artistic expression fusing song, dance, and instrumental music, which is rooted in the cultural diversity of Andalusia, Spain.
  - Wayang puppet theatre, a form of storytelling that uses flat leather puppets, which is widely practiced in Indonesia and other parts of Southeast Asia.
  - Tango, a social dance and musical genre that originated in the Rio de la Plata basin, which is a symbol of cultural identity and social integration for Argentina and Uruguay.

- Cultural heritage research is the academic study of the various aspects of cultural heritage, such as its definition, preservation, management, interpretation, valorization, and impact.
- Cultural heritage research is an interdisciplinary field that draws from the humanities, social sciences, natural sciences, and engineering.
- Cultural heritage research aims to generate new knowledge, methods, and tools to address the challenges and opportunities posed by cultural heritage in the contemporary world.
- Some of the topics and questions that cultural heritage research explores are:

  - How can we identify, document, and protect the tangible and intangible cultural heritage of different communities and regions?
  - How can we enhance the accessibility, visibility, and understanding of cultural heritage for diverse audiences and stakeholders?
  - How can we foster the participation, engagement, and empowerment of local communities and groups in the management and governance of their cultural heritage?
  - How can we evaluate the social, economic, and environmental impacts and benefits of cultural heritage for sustainable development?
