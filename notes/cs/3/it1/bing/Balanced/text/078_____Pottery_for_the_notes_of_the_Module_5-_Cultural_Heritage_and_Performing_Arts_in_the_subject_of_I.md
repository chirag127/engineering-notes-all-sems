### Pottery

- Pottery is one of the most ancient and tangible forms of Indian art, with evidence of pottery making found in the early settlements of Lahuradewa and later the Indus Valley Civilization  .
- Pottery is a cultural art that reflects the diversity and richness of Indian traditions, as different regions have developed their own distinctive styles, techniques, and motifs of pottery over time .
- Pottery plays an important role in studying culture and reconstructing the past, as pottery artifacts can reveal information about the social, economic, religious, and artistic aspects of various historical periods.
- Some of the famous pottery traditions that have been practiced across India are:

  - Jaipur Blue Pottery, Rajasthan: This is a glazed pottery that has its origins derived from the Turko-Persian style. It is known for its vibrant blue color and intricate floral and geometric patterns.
  - Longpi Black Pottery, Manipur: This is a unique pottery that is made from a mixture of black serpentine stone and weathered rock. It is hand-molded and polished with a cane and a leaf. It is used for making cooking utensils, vases, and decorative items.
  - Khavda Pottery, Gujarat: This is a pottery that is made from red clay and decorated with white clay slip and geometric designs. It is fired in an open kiln and then coated with geru (red ochre) to give it a reddish hue. It is used for making water pots, lamps, and bowls.
  - Nizamabad Black Pottery, Uttar Pradesh: This is a pottery that is made from fine clay and coated with a black solution of clay, vegetable matter, and iron filings. It is then engraved with silver or golden motifs of flowers, birds, and animals. It is used for making hookahs, surahis, and lamps.
  - Metal Pottery, Karnataka: This is a pottery that is made from a mixture of zinc and copper, which gives it a metallic appearance. It is shaped on a wheel and then engraved with floral and geometric designs. It is used for making utensils, idols, and jewelry.
