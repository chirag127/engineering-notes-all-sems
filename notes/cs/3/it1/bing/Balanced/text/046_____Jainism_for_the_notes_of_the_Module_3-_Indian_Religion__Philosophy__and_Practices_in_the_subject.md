### Jainism

- Jainism is an ancient Indian religion that teaches a path to spiritual purity and enlightenment through disciplined nonviolence (ahimsa) to all living creatures .
- Jainism is also a transtheistic religion, meaning that it does not affirm or deny the existence of a supreme being, but rather considers the universe to be eternal and self-sustaining.
- The name Jainism derives from the Sanskrit verb ji, "to conquer", which refers to the ascetic battle that Jain renunciants (monks and nuns) must fight against the passions and bodily senses to gain omniscience and purity of soul.
- The followers of Jainism are called Jains, and they believe that every soul (jiva) is potentially divine and can attain liberation (moksha) from the cycle of rebirth (samsara) by following the teachings of the tirthankaras, or "ford-makers", who are the enlightened beings who have shown the way .
- The most recent and 24th tirthankara in this time cycle was Mahavira, who lived in the 6th century BCE and is considered the founder of Jainism as we know it today .
- The three main pillars of Jainism are ahimsa (non-violence), anekantavada (non-absolutism), and aparigraha (asceticism).
  - Ahimsa means avoiding harm to any living being, not only in action, but also in speech and thought. Jains practice strict vegetarianism, avoid killing insects, and wear masks or cloths over their mouths to prevent inhaling microorganisms .
  - Anekantavada means recognizing the multiplicity of perspectives and the relativity of truth. Jains avoid dogmatism and intolerance, and seek to understand different points of view through dialogue and debate .
  - Aparigraha means renouncing attachment to worldly possessions and pleasures, and leading a simple and contented life. Jains practice voluntary poverty, celibacy, fasting, and meditation, and donate to charitable causes .
- Jainism has two main sects: Digambara and Svetambara, which differ mainly in their monastic practices and doctrines. Digambara monks go naked, while Svetambara monks wear white robes. Digambara monks also do not accept that women can attain liberation, while Svetambara monks do .
- Jainism has a rich and diverse literature, consisting of scriptures, commentaries, stories, biographies, and philosophical works. Some of the most important texts are the Agamas, the canonical scriptures of Jainism, which contain the teachings of the tirthankaras and their disciples .
- Jainism has influenced and been influenced by other Indian religions, especially Hinduism and Buddhism. Jainism shares many concepts and practices with Hinduism, such as karma, dharma, and ahimsa, but rejects the authority of the Vedas, the caste system, and the worship of gods. Jainism also shares some similarities with Buddhism, such as the rejection of a creator god, the emphasis on nonviolence, and the goal of liberation, but differs in its view of the soul, the role of the tirthankaras, and the nature of reality .
- Jainism is a minority religion in India, with about 4.5 million adherents, or 0.4% of the population. Most Jains live in the states of Maharashtra, Gujarat, Rajasthan, and Karnataka. Jainism has also spread to other countries, such as the United States, Canada, the United Kingdom, and Kenya, through migration and conversion .
