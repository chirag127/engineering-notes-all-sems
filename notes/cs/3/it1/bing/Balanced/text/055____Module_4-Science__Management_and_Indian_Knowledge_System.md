## Module 4-Science, Management and Indian Knowledge System

- This module covers the contributions of Indian culture and civilization to various fields of science, technology and management.
- It aims to provide an overview of the Indian knowledge systems and their applications in different domains.
- It also highlights the relevance and significance of the Indian heritage and wisdom for the contemporary world.

### Astronomy in India
- Astronomy is one of the oldest branches of science in India, dating back to the Vedic period (1500-500 BCE).
- The Vedas, the ancient scriptures of Hinduism, contain references to astronomical phenomena such as eclipses, solstices, equinoxes, constellations, planets and stars.
- The Vedanga Jyotisha, a supplementary text to the Vedas, is the earliest treatise on astronomy and calendar making in India.
- The Siddhantas, or astronomical treatises, were composed by various scholars from the 5th century CE onwards, such as Aryabhata, Varahamihira, Brahmagupta, Bhaskara and Lalla.
- These works deal with topics such as planetary motions, eclipses, cosmology, spherical trigonometry, time measurement, celestial coordinates and mathematical astronomy.
- The Indian astronomers also developed sophisticated instruments for observation and calculation, such as the gnomon, the water clock, the armillary sphere, the astrolabe and the yantra.
- The Indian astronomy influenced and was influenced by the astronomy of other cultures, such as the Greeks, the Persians, the Arabs, the Chinese and the Europeans.

### Chemistry in India
- Chemistry in India has a long and rich history, spanning from the Indus Valley Civilization (2500-1900 BCE) to the modern era.
- The Indus Valley people were skilled in metallurgy, pottery, dyeing, tanning, perfumery and cosmetics.
- The Vedic texts mention the use of fire, water, earth, air and ether as the five basic elements of nature, and the concept of panchamahabhuta, or the five gross elements, as the basis of all matter.
- The Ayurveda, the ancient system of medicine, deals with the theory and practice of chemistry in relation to health and disease. It classifies substances into three categories: sattva (pure), rajas (active) and tamas (inert), and uses various methods of purification, extraction, distillation, calcination, sublimation and fermentation to prepare medicines.
- The Rasashastra, or the science of alchemy, emerged as a branch of Ayurveda around the 8th century CE. It aims to transform base metals into gold, and to achieve immortality and enlightenment through the use of mercury, sulphur and other minerals and metals.
- The Rasaratnakara, a text attributed to Nagarjuna, is one of the earliest and most influential works on alchemy in India. It describes the properties and processes of various substances, such as metals, minerals, gems, salts, acids, alkalis, oils, plants and animal products.
- The Indian chemistry also contributed to other fields, such as agriculture, textile, paper, glass, soap, gunpowder, fireworks and explosives.
