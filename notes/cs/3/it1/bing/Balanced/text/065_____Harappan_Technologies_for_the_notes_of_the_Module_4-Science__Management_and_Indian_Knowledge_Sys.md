### Harappan Technologies

- The Harappan civilization, also known as the Indus Valley Civilization, was a Bronze Age civilization that flourished in the northwest of the Indian subcontinent from about 2500 BCE to 1900 BCE.
- The Harappans developed various technologies and crafts, such as metallurgy, seal carving, pottery, jewelry, weights and measures, urban planning, drainage systems, and writing.
- Some of the notable features of Harappan technologies are:

  - Metallurgy: The Harappans used copper, bronze, lead, and tin to make tools, weapons, vessels, and ornaments. They knew how to alloy copper with tin to make bronze, which is harder and more durable than copper. They also knew how to cast metal by the lost-wax process, which involves making a wax model, covering it with clay, heating it to melt the wax, and then pouring molten metal into the mold.
  - Seal carving: The Harappans carved distinctive seals, usually made of steatite, a soft stone, with various motifs and inscriptions. The seals were used to stamp clay on trade goods, to identify property, and possibly to serve as amulets or tokens of authority. The motifs on the seals include animals, such as bulls, elephants, rhinoceroses, tigers, and unicorns, as well as human and divine figures. The inscriptions are in a script that has not been deciphered yet.
  - Pottery: The Harappans produced a variety of pottery, both plain and painted, for domestic and ritual purposes. The pottery was made of fine clay, well-fired, and often decorated with geometric patterns, floral motifs, or animal figures. The pottery shapes include dishes, bowls, jars, vases, goblets, and spouted vessels.
  - Jewelry: The Harappans crafted jewelry from materials such as gold, silver, copper, bronze, ivory, shell, bone, and beads of stone, glass, or faience. The jewelry items include necklaces, bracelets, bangles, earrings, rings, pendants, and amulets. The jewelry reflects the skill and aesthetic sense of the Harappan artisans, as well as the wealth and status of the wearers.
  - Weights and measures: The Harappans developed a system of standardized weights and measures, based on a decimal and binary system. The weights were made of stone or metal, and ranged from 0.05 grams to 500 grams. The measures were based on a unit of length of about 1.32 inches, which was subdivided into smaller units. The Harappans used these weights and measures for trade, taxation, and administration.
  - Urban planning: The Harappans built well-planned cities, with a grid layout, fortified walls, and gateways. The cities had a citadel, where the public buildings and religious structures were located, and a lower town, where the residential and commercial areas were situated. The cities had a sophisticated drainage system, with covered drains, manholes, and cesspits. The cities also had granaries, warehouses, baths, wells, and reservoirs.
