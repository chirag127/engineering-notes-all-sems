### Contract Theory

- Contract theory is a branch of political philosophy that studies the origin, nature, and legitimacy of the state and its authority over individuals.
- Contract theory assumes that individuals are rational, self-interested, and free to consent or dissent to any social arrangement.
- Contract theory proposes that the state is the result of a voluntary agreement or contract among individuals who give up some of their natural rights and liberties in exchange for the protection and benefits of a common authority.
- Contract theory can be divided into two main types: classical and modern.
- Classical contract theory is based on the idea of natural law and natural rights, which are derived from human reason and nature. Classical contract theorists include Thomas Hobbes, John Locke, and Jean-Jacques Rousseau.
- Modern contract theory is based on the idea of hypothetical consent and impartiality, which are derived from rational choice and moral reasoning. Modern contract theorists include John Rawls, Robert Nozick, and David Gauthier.
