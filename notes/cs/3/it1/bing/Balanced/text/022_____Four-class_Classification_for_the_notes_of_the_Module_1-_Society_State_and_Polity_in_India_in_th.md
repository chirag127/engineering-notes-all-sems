### Four-class Classification for the notes of the Module 1- Society State and Polity in India in the subject of INDIAN TRADITIONS, CULTURAL AND SOCIETY

- The four-class classification, also known as the varna system, is a social hierarchy that divides the Hindu society into four main categories: Brahmins (priests and scholars), Kshatriyas (warriors and rulers), Vaishyas (merchants and farmers), and Shudras (servants and laborers).
- The origin of the varna system is traced back to the Rig Veda, one of the oldest Hindu scriptures, where the four classes are said to have emerged from the body of the cosmic being Purusha: the Brahmins from his mouth, the Kshatriyas from his arms, the Vaishyas from his thighs, and the Shudras from his feet.
- The varna system is based on the concept of dharma, or duty, which assigns different roles and responsibilities to each class according to their nature and abilities. The Brahmins are expected to perform rituals, study the scriptures, and teach others. The Kshatriyas are expected to protect the society, uphold justice, and fight wars. The Vaishyas are expected to engage in trade, agriculture, and commerce. The Shudras are expected to serve the other three classes and perform menial tasks.
- The varna system is also linked to the concept of karma, or action, which determines the quality of one's life and rebirth. According to the Hindu belief in reincarnation, the soul transmigrates from one body to another after death, depending on the actions performed in the previous life. The varna system provides a framework for ethical conduct and spiritual progress, as one can attain a higher or lower status in the next life by following or violating one's dharma.
- The varna system has been criticized for creating social inequality, discrimination, and oppression, especially for the Shudras and the outcastes, who are considered to be outside the four-class system and are subjected to various forms of exclusion and stigma. The outcastes, also known as the untouchables or the Dalits, are those who perform the most polluting and degrading occupations, such as scavenging, leather work, and cremation. They are often denied access to education, public facilities, and religious places, and face violence and harassment from the upper classes.
- The varna system has also been challenged by various social movements, religious reformers, and political leaders, who have advocated for the abolition of caste discrimination and the promotion of social justice and human rights. Some of the prominent figures who have opposed the varna system are Buddha, Mahavira, Guru Nanak, Raja Ram Mohan Roy, Mahatma Gandhi, B.R. Ambedkar, and Dr. Martin Luther King Jr.
