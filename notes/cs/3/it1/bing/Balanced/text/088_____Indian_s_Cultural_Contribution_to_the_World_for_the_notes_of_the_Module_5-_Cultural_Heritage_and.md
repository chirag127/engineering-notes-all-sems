### Indian's Cultural Contribution to the World

- India is one of the oldest and most diverse civilizations in the world, with a rich and varied heritage of art, literature, philosophy, religion, science, and technology.
- India has given many gifts to the world that have influenced and enriched various aspects of human culture and civilization. Some of these contributions are:

  - India gave the world its first university, Takshashila University, which was established around the 6th century BCE and attracted scholars from all over Asia. It offered courses in various disciplines such as grammar, logic, medicine, astronomy, and mathematics .
  - India gave the world the numeral zero, which is essential for mathematics and science. The concept of zero and the decimal system were developed by Indian mathematicians such as Aryabhata, Brahmagupta, and Bhaskara in the 5th to 7th centuries CE .
  - India gave the world the game of chess, which originated from an ancient Indian game called chaturanga, meaning "four divisions". The game was played on an 8x8 board with pieces representing the king, the elephant, the horse, the chariot, and the foot soldiers .
  - India gave the world the use and invention of buttons, which were first used in India as early as 2000 BCE. Buttons were made of seashells, metal, ivory, wood, and bone, and were used to fasten clothes and ornaments .
  - India gave the world the shampoo, which originated from the Sanskrit word champu, meaning "to massage". Shampoo was a herbal mixture of soapberries, gooseberries, and other ingredients that was used to cleanse and condition the hair and scalp .
  - India gave the world the cure for leprosy, which was a chronic and disfiguring disease that affected the skin and nerves. The ancient Indian physician Sushruta described the symptoms and treatment of leprosy in his medical treatise Sushruta Samhita, which was written around the 6th century BCE. He prescribed the use of oil, alkaline, and surgical procedures to treat the disease .
  - India gave the world the game of snakes and ladders, which was inspired from an Indian game called mokshapat, meaning "the ladder to salvation". The game was a moral lesson that taught the effects of good and bad deeds on one's destiny. The ladders represented virtues and the snakes represented vices .
  - India gave the world the concept of non-violence, which was advocated by the Indian spiritual leader Mahatma Gandhi, who led the Indian independence movement against the British colonial rule. Gandhi's philosophy of non-violence, or ahimsa, inspired many other movements and leaders around the world, such as Martin Luther King Jr., Nelson Mandela, and Dalai Lama .
  - India gave the world the practice of yoga, which is a system of physical, mental, and spiritual exercises that originated in India around the 5th century BCE. Yoga is derived from the Sanskrit word yuj, meaning "to unite". Yoga aims to harmonize the body, mind, and soul, and to achieve a state of liberation or enlightenment .
  - India gave the world the art of meditation, which is a technique of focusing one's attention and awareness on a single object, thought, or sensation. Meditation is a part of many Indian traditions, such as Hinduism, Buddhism, Jainism, and Sikhism. Meditation has many benefits for the health and well-being of the practitioner, such as reducing stress, improving concentration, and enhancing creativity .
  - India gave the world the diversity of languages, which reflects the richness and complexity of Indian culture and history. India has 22 official languages and hundreds of regional and local languages, belonging to four major language families: Indo-Aryan, Dravidian, Austroasiatic, and Tibeto-Burman. India is also the birthplace of four major world religions: Hinduism, Buddhism, Jainism, and Sikhism, which have influenced the culture and philosophy of many other regions and countries .
  - India gave the world the literature of epics, which are long narrative poems that depict the heroic deeds and adventures
