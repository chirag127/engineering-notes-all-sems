### Pre-Vedic and Vedic Religion

- Pre-Vedic religion refers to the religious beliefs and practices of the people who lived in the Indus Valley or Harappan civilization, which flourished from about 2500 to 1500 BCE.
- Pre-Vedic religion was characterized by the worship of both male and female aspects of nature, such as trees, animals, rivers, mountains, and fertility symbols.
- Pre-Vedic religion also involved the use of seals, terracotta figurines, fire altars, and ritual baths, which suggest a complex and sophisticated culture.
- Vedic religion refers to the religion of the Indo-European-speaking peoples who entered northwestern India from Persia around 1500 BCE and composed the four Vedas, the oldest sacred texts of Hinduism.
- Vedic religion was based on the worship of many gods, such as Indra, Varuna, Agni, Surya, and Rudra, who represented natural forces and cosmic phenomena.
- Vedic religion also involved the performance of sacrifices, rituals, and hymns, which were meant to appease the gods and secure their blessings for prosperity, protection, and happiness.
- Vedic religion evolved over time and gave rise to various schools of thought, such as the Upanishads, the Brahmanas, the Aranyakas, and the Sutras, which explored the nature of reality, the self, and the supreme being.
