### Geography for the notes of the Module 4-Science, Management and Indian Knowledge System in the subject of INDIAN TRADITIONS, CULTURAL AND SOCIETY

- Geography is the study of the physical features of the earth and its atmosphere, and of human activity as it affects and is affected by these features.
- Geography has two main branches: physical geography and human geography. Physical geography deals with the natural environment, such as climate, landforms, water, soil, vegetation, and wildlife. Human geography deals with the spatial aspects of human society and culture, such as population, settlement, urbanization, agriculture, industry, trade, and geopolitics.
- Geography is an interdisciplinary subject that draws on various fields of knowledge, such as history, economics, sociology, anthropology, political science, and environmental science. Geography also uses various methods and tools, such as maps, statistics, remote sensing, geographic information systems (GIS), and fieldwork.
- Geography has a long and rich tradition in India, dating back to the ancient times. Some of the earliest geographers in India were the Vedic sages, who composed hymns and texts that described the geography of the Indian subcontinent and its surroundings. They also developed concepts such as the seven continents (sapta-dvipa), the four directions (dik), and the five elements (pancha-mahabhuta).
- Some of the prominent geographers in the classical period of India were Varahamihira, who wrote the Brihat-Samhita, a compendium of astronomy, astrology, geography, and meteorology; Kalidasa, who wrote the Raghuvamsa, an epic poem that narrates the history and geography of the Raghu dynasty; and Kautilya, who wrote the Arthashastra, a treatise on statecraft, economics, and geography.
- Some of the notable geographers in the medieval period of India were Al-Biruni, who wrote the Kitab al-Hind, a comprehensive account of the geography, history, culture, and religion of India; Amir Khusrau, who wrote the Khazain-ul-Futuh, a chronicle of the conquests and geography of the Delhi Sultanate; and Abul Fazl, who wrote the Ain-i-Akbari, a detailed description of the geography, administration, and society of the Mughal Empire.
- Some of the influential geographers in the modern period of India were Raja Rammohan Roy, who advocated for the introduction of Western geography and science in India; R.C. Dutt, who wrote the Economic History of India, a critical analysis of the geography and economy of colonial India; and Radhakamal Mukerjee, who wrote the Foundations of Indian Geography, a systematic exposition of the physical and human geography of India.
- Geography is an important subject for understanding the diversity and unity of India, as well as its challenges and opportunities in the global context. Geography can help us appreciate the natural and cultural heritage of India, as well as its role and responsibility in the world. Geography can also help us develop a scientific and rational outlook, as well as a sense of environmental awareness and social responsibility.
