### Buddhist and Jain Literature in Pali, Prakrit and Sanskrit

- Pali, Prakrit and Sanskrit are three languages that were used to write the religious and philosophical texts of Buddhism and Jainism in ancient India.
- Pali is a term that refers to an archaic or old form of Prakrit that combines several existing dialects. It was spoken in Magadha and South Bihar, and was the language of the earliest Buddhist works.
- Prakrit is a term that refers to any language other than the standard one, Sanskrit. It was spoken by the common people and was used to write various forms of literature, such as drama, poetry, epics, and inscriptions. It was also the language of the Jain scriptures, which were written in Ardhamagadhi, a Prakrit dialect .
- Sanskrit is the classical and literary language of India, derived from the Vedic language. It was used to write the Vedas, the Upanishads, the epics, the Puranas, and many other works of Hinduism. It was also used by some Buddhist and Jain scholars to write commentaries, treatises, and philosophical works.

- The Buddhist literature in Pali consists of three main collections: the Vinaya Pitaka, the Sutta Pitaka, and the Abhidhamma Pitaka. These are collectively known as the Tipitaka or the Pali Canon, and contain the teachings of the Buddha and his disciples  .
  - The Vinaya Pitaka contains the rules and regulations for the monastic order, as well as stories of the Buddha and his disciples.
  - The Sutta Pitaka contains the discourses and sermons of the Buddha and his disciples on various topics, such as ethics, meditation, wisdom, and cosmology. It is divided into five collections: the Digha Nikaya, the Majjhima Nikaya, the Samyutta Nikaya, the Anguttara Nikaya, and the Khuddaka Nikaya.
  - The Abhidhamma Pitaka contains the analysis and exposition of the Buddhist doctrine, such as the nature of mind, matter, causality, and liberation. It is divided into seven books: the Dhammasangani, the Vibhanga, the Dhatukatha, the Puggalapannatti, the Kathavatthu, the Yamaka, and the Patthana.
- The Buddhist literature in Sanskrit and Prakrit consists of various works that were written by later schools and sects of Buddhism, such as the Mahayana, the Vajrayana, and the Hinayana. These include the following :
  - The Mahayana Sutras, which are the scriptures of the Mahayana school, which emphasize the ideal of the bodhisattva, the compassionate being who strives for the liberation of all sentient beings. Some of the famous Mahayana Sutras are the Lotus Sutra, the Prajnaparamita Sutras, the Avatamsaka Sutra, and the Lankavatara Sutra.
  - The Tantras, which are the scriptures of the Vajrayana school, which emphasize the use of esoteric practices, such as mantras, mudras, mandalas, and rituals, to achieve enlightenment. Some of the famous Tantras are the Guhyasamaja Tantra, the Hevajra Tantra, the Kalachakra Tantra, and the Chakrasamvara Tantra.
  - The Hinayana Sutras, which are the scriptures of the Hinayana school, which follow the original teachings of the Buddha and the Pali Canon. Some of the Hinayana Sutras are the Dhammapada, the Udana, the Itivuttaka, and the Sutta Nipata.
