### Seals for the notes of the Module 5- Cultural Heritage and Performing Arts in the subject of INDIAN TRADITIONS, CULTURAL AND SOCIETY

Seals are small, usually circular, engraved or stamped objects made of metal, stone, or other materials that are used to make an impression or mark. In the context of Indian traditions, culture, and society, seals have played a significant role in the preservation of cultural heritage and the documentation of historical events and transactions.

Seals have been used in India for thousands of years, and have been found at archaeological sites dating back to the Indus Valley Civilization. These seals were used for a variety of purposes, including the marking of goods, the authentication of documents, and the representation of religious and political symbols.

In the medieval period, seals were used by royalty, nobles, and merchants to mark their property and to authenticate documents. These seals often featured intricate designs and symbols, and were made of precious materials such as gold, silver, and ivory.

In modern times, seals continue to play an important role in India, particularly in the fields of archaeology and numismatics. The study of seals and seal impressions provides valuable insight into the cultural, social, and political history of India, and helps to preserve the country's rich cultural heritage.

In conclusion, seals are small, engraved or stamped objects that have played a significant role in the preservation of cultural heritage and the documentation of historical events and transactions in India. From the Indus Valley Civilization to the present day, seals have provided valuable insight into the cultural, social, and political history of the country.
