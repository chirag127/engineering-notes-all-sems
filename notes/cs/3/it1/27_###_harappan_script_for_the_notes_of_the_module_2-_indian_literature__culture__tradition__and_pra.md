### Harappan Script for the notes of the Module 2- Indian Literature, Culture, Tradition, and Practices in the subject of INDIAN TRADITIONS, CULTURAL AND SOCIETY

The Harappan script is the script used by the Indus Valley Civilization, one of the world's earliest civilizations. This civilization flourished in the Indus Valley region of what is now Pakistan and northwest India from around 2600 BCE to 1900 BCE.

The Harappan script is considered to be one of the earliest forms of writing in the world, and is thought to have been used for a variety of purposes, including record-keeping, trade, and communication. Despite its importance, however, the script remains largely undeciphered, and its exact nature and purpose are still the subject of much debate among scholars.

The script consists of around 400 symbols, which have been found on seals, pottery, and other artifacts from the Indus Valley Civilization. The symbols are small and are often arranged in rows or columns, and they are thought to represent words, phrases, or even whole sentences.

One of the challenges of deciphering the Harappan script is the lack of written records or inscriptions that provide context or meaning for the symbols. Another challenge is the fact that the script has not been found in a continuous sequence, making it difficult to determine the order and meaning of the symbols.

Despite these challenges, researchers have made significant progress in deciphering the Harappan script in recent years. They have used a variety of methods, including comparative linguistics, statistical analysis, and computer modeling, to try to understand the nature and purpose of the script.

In conclusion, the Harappan script is an important part of the cultural heritage of India, and represents one of the earliest forms of writing in the world. Despite its importance, however, much remains to be learned about this script, and its exact nature and purpose continue to be the subject of much debate among scholars.
