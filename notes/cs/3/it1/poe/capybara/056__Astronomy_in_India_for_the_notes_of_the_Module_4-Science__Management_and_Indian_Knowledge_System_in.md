### Astronomy in India

Astronomy has been a significant part of Indian culture and history. Here are some key points about astronomy in India:

- The earliest records of astronomy in India date back to the Indus Valley Civilization, around 3000 BCE.
- The ancient Indian astronomers were known as the Siddhantas, and their work was based on observations of the sky and mathematical calculations.
- Aryabhata was one of the most famous Indian astronomers. He lived in the 5th century CE and is known for his work on the motion of planets and the determination of the Earth's circumference.
- Another famous Indian astronomer was Brahmagupta, who lived in the 7th century CE. He is known for his work on the movement of planets and the calculation of eclipses.
- Indian astronomy made significant contributions to the world, including the invention of the decimal system and the concept of zero.
- The ancient Indian astronomers also developed a system of 27 constellations, known as the nakshatras, which are still used in Indian astrology today.
- The Jantar Mantar observatories, built in the 18th century, are a testament to the advanced state of Indian astronomy at the time. These observatories were used to study the movement of celestial bodies and to create accurate calendars.
- In modern India, the Indian Space Research Organisation (ISRO) is responsible for the country's space program. ISRO has launched numerous satellites, including the Mars Orbiter Mission, which made India the first country to successfully send a spacecraft to Mars on its first attempt.

Overall, astronomy has played a significant role in Indian culture and history, and continues to be an important field of study and research in modern India.
