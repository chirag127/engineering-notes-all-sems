### Seals for the notes of the Module 5- Cultural Heritage and Performing Arts in the subject of INDIAN TRADITIONS, CULTURAL AND SOCIETY.

- Seals are a significant aspect of Indian cultural heritage and have been used since ancient times.
- The seals were used to authenticate and authorize important documents and were also used as a mark of ownership on various objects.
- The seals were usually made of stone, terracotta, or metal and had intricate designs and inscriptions engraved on them.
- The Harappan civilization is known for its use of seals, which were made of steatite, and had animal motifs and inscriptions in the Indus script.
- The Mauryan and Gupta dynasties also used seals extensively, with the Mauryan seals having the famous bull emblem and the Gupta seals featuring various deities and religious symbols.
- The Mughal empire also used seals, which were made of gold, silver, or jade and had the emperor's name and titles engraved on them.
- The use of seals declined during the British colonial period, but they still hold great cultural and historical significance in India.
- Today, seals are considered as artifacts and are displayed in museums and galleries, providing glimpses into India's rich cultural heritage.
