### Physics in India

- Ancient Indian scholars made notable contributions to physics, especially in mechanics, cosmology and astronomy.
- Some important concepts in physics like the idea of zero, infinity, and the decimal system were invented in India.
- Philosophical ideas like atomism were proposed in ancient India. The concept of atoms expounded in the Vaiseshika system of Indian philosophy is quite close to the modern scientific idea.
- Key discoveries in mechanics were also made, including an early law of gravitation by Brahmagupta in the 7th century CE.
- In astronomy, Aryabhata calculated the sidereal rotation (the rotation of the earth on its axis with respect to the stars) as 23 hours, 56 minutes and 4.1 seconds; which forms the basis of the modern value of 23:56:4.091.
- There were also advances in the field of optics, including the notions that light travels in straight lines, and that reflection and refraction are responsible for the formation of images in lenses and mirrors.
- The study of Physics along with other sciences declined in India after the 12th century CE with the rise of religious orthodoxy and decline of ancient universities like Nalanda.
- However, the modern study of Physics in India picked up again after India's independence in 1947 with the establishment of institutions like the Tata Institute of Fundamental Research. India has since produced notable scientists in the field of Physics like C. V. Raman and Chandrasekhara Venkata Raman who won the Nobel Prize in Physics.
