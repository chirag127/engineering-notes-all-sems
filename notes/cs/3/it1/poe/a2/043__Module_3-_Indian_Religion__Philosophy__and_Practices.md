## Module 3- Indian Religion, Philosophy, and Practices

1. Hinduism
- One of the oldest religions with majority of followers in India
- Based on ancient Vedic traditions and practices
- Follows concepts of samsara, karma, and moksha
- Sacred texts include Vedas, Upanishads, Ramayana, and Bhagavad Gita
- Diverse traditions like Vaishnavism, Shaivism, Shaktism, and Smartism
- Common beliefs in reincarnation and the worship of Brahma, Vishnu, and Shiva as main deities

2. Jainism
- An ancient Indian religion founded by Mahavira in 6th century BCE
- Based on principles of non-violence, non-attachment, and non-absolutism
- Goal is to liberate the soul from the cycles of rebirth by following strict ethical code and austerities
- Followers called Jains revere tirthankaras or 'ford-makers' who have attained liberation
- Main sects are Digambara and Svetambara with ascetics following strict codes of conduct

3. Buddhism
- Founded by Siddhartha Gautama (Buddha) in 6th century BCE
- Based on Four Noble Truths and Eightfold Path aimed at liberation from suffering
- Non-theistic philosophy that rejects Vedas and idea of Brahmans or eternal soul
- Spread through teachings of Buddha and missionary activities of monks across Asia
- Major branches are Theravada, Mahayana, and Vajrayana with diverse practices
