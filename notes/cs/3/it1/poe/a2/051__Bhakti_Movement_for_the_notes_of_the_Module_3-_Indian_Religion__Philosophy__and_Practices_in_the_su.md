### Bhakti Movement

- Bhakti movement refers to the movement of devotional worship towards a personal god that originated in South India in the 7th-8th centuries CE.
- It emphasized devotional worship of Vishnu or Shiva through love and devotion instead of ritual sacrifices and worship of Brahman as advocated in the Upanishads.
- The Bhakti saints came from all sections of society, including outcastes and women. They rejected the caste system and the dominance of Brahmins as religious intermediaries.
- They expressed their love and devotion to god in vernacular languages instead of Sanskrit. This enriched the local languages and made spiritual ideas more accessible to people.
- The important saints of this movement include Nayanars (devotees of Shiva) in South India and Alvars (devotees of Vishnu) in South India, as well as Kabir, Mirabai, Chaitanya and others in North India.
- The Bhakti movement weakened the rigidities of caste and ritual prescriptions of orthodox Hinduism. It also led to the composition of much-loved vernacular literature and songs in praise of god that remain popular to this day.
- The Bhakti tradition continues today with the huge following of Vaishnavism and Shaivism in South India and other devotional traditions like Sikhism in North India.
