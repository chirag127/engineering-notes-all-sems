### Buddhism

- Buddhism was founded in ancient India by Siddhartha Gautama (the Buddha) around 500 BCE.
- The Buddha was an ascetic who meditated on the nature of life and taught the Four Noble Truths, the Eightfold Path, and the concepts of karma and rebirth.
- The Four Noble Truths are (1) life is suffering, (2) the cause of suffering is desire, (3) the end of desire leads to the end of suffering, and (4) the Eightfold Path can end desire.
- The Eightfold Path includes guidelines for moral behavior, meditation, and wisdom to reach Nirvana.
- Karma is the idea that good and bad deeds produce merit and demerit that affect how a person will be reborn after death. Rebirth continues until Nirvana is reached.
- Nirvana is the ultimate goal of escaping desire and the cycle of rebirth to reach a state of enlightenment and peace. It is achieved by following the Buddha's teachings.
- Buddhism spread from India to many other Asian countries, including China, Japan, Thailand, Myanmar, Sri Lanka, Vietnam, and Tibet. The religion has evolved into many different regional forms and branches.
- The main branches of Buddhism are Theravada, Mahayana, and Vajrayana. They share common beliefs but have unique practices and texts. Most Buddhists follow one of the first two branches.
