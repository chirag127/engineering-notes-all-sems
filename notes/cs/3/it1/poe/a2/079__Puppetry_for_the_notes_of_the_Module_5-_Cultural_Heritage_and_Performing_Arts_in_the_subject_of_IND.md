### Puppetry

- Puppetry is a form of theatre or performance that involves the manipulation of puppets. It is a very ancient form of entertainment and was one of the earliest forms of animated performances.
- Puppets are inanimate objects, often resembling some type of human or animal figure, that are animated or manipulated by a puppeteer.
- The puppeteer uses movements of her or his hands, arms, or control devices such as rods or strings to move the puppet.
- The puppeteer often speaks in the voice of the character represented by the puppet, while simultaneously animating its mouth and facial expressions to correspond with the voice and speech of the character.
- Puppetry is often used as a medium to educate or entertain children, but it is also often enjoyed by adults.
- There are many different varieties of puppets, and they are made of a wide range of materials, including paper, wood, fabric, and even vegetables.
- The tradition of puppetry in India is very old. The references of puppet shows are found in ancient Indian literature like NatyaShastra.
- Puppetry was a popular entertainment form in Indian courts. String puppets, shadow puppets and glove puppets are main varieties of traditional Indian puppet theatre.
- String puppets or Kathputli are connected to strings and are manipulated by the puppeteer to create various movements. Shadow puppets are flat figures held against a screen with a light behind it. Glove puppets are worn like gloves by the puppeteer.
- Indian puppet theatre involves mythological stories and folk tales. Famous traditional puppet theatres of India are Puppet Theatre of Odisha, Kathputli of Rajasthan and Tolu Bommalata of Andhra Pradesh.
