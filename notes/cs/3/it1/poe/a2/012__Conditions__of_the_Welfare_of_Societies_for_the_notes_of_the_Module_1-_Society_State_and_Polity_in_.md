### Conditions’ of the Welfare of Societies

- Stability: A stable society where institutional structures and social practices do not frequently change is essential for the welfare of societies. Frequent changes and uncertainty create chaos and confusion which hampers progress.
- Unity: The members of a society must share a common bond of unity to work together towards mutual welfare. Unity in diversity is an ideal condition where different groups accept and respect each other despite differences.
- Cooperation: The members of a society must cooperate with each other to achieve common goals. Conflict and competition create hostility while cooperation fosters trust and teamwork.
- Adaptability: While stability is desirable, a society must also be capable of adapting to changes to handle challenges and innovations. Rigidity leads to stagnation while adaptability enables progress.
- Efficiency: The institutions and systems of a society must be efficient enough to utilize resources and address issues effectively. Inefficiency leads to wastage and inadequacy in meeting objectives.
- Justice: The distribution of resources and opportunities in a society must be based on the principles of justice, equality, and fairness. Inequality and discrimination are detrimental to welfare.
- Protection: The society must ensure the safety and security of all its members. The presence of law and order and protection from internal and external threats are necessary for well-being.
