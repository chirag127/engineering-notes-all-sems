### India’s Dominance up to Pre-colonial Times

- India was one of the largest economies of the world up to the 18th century. It had a flourishing trade with other parts of Asia and Europe.
- India had a very efficient system of agriculture and production. The handloom industry and village crafts were in abundance. This led to surplus produce and economic prosperity.
- The taxation system was quite efficient. The revenue was used for the welfare of the people in the form of public works, grants, salaries, infrastructure etc. This led to a high standard of living for the people.
- The Indian traders and merchant class were quite prosperous and traded with other countries in spices, textiles, metal works etc. The traders were organized into guilds. This led to growth of towns and urban settlements.
- The Indian rulers promoted trade and commerce. The roads, ports, rest houses etc. were maintained for easy conduct of trade. This promoted economic activities and revenue for the kingdom.
- The Indian society was divided into different classes based on occupation. The Brahmins, Kshatriyas, Vaishyas and Shudras had their defined roles and duties. This led to specialization of labour and efficient functioning of the society.
- The religious temples and institutions were quite wealthy due to grants and contributions. They promoted arts, crafts, dance, music etc. This led to employment for many and nurtured culture.
- Thus, India was a major economic power and a cradle of culture up to the pre-colonial times. The efficient systems led to prosperity, specialization and cultural growth. The decline started with the colonial rule.
