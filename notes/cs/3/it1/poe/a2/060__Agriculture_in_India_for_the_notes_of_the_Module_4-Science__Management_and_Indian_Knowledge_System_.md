### Agriculture in India

- Agriculture is the primary occupation in India and contributes significantly to the country's economy. About 70% of India's population depends on agriculture for their livelihood.
- The Indian subcontinent has a long history of agriculture that goes back more than 4000 years. Some important crops that were cultivated in ancient India include rice, wheat, millet, barley, etc.
- The Indus Valley civilization was an early civilization that flourished in parts of what is now Pakistan and northwestern India from 3300 BCE to 1300 BCE. The people of the Indus Valley civilization primarily cultivated wheat, barley, peas, sesame, and cotton.
- During the Gupta empire from 320 to 550 CE, there were significant advances in agriculture. New crops such as sugarcane, pineapple, and banana were introduced. There were also improvements in irrigation systems.
- After independence in 1947, the Government of India made efforts to increase food production through expansion of cultivated areas, introduction of high-yielding varieties of crops, and increased use of fertilizers and irrigation. This led to the Green Revolution in the 1960s and 70s and increased agricultural output.
- The main crops cultivated in India are rice, wheat, millet, barley, maize, oats, pulses, oilseeds, fibers, sugarcane, fruits, and vegetables. India is the world's largest producer of milk, pulses, jute, potato, sugarcane, and various spices.
- However, Indian agriculture still faces many challenges such as small land holdings, poor irrigation facilities, lack of warehousing and cold storage, and over-reliance on monsoons. More investment in agriculture and reforms are needed to boost productivity and farmer incomes.
