### Mystical Theory for the notes of the Module 1- Society State and Polity in India in the subject of INDIAN TRADITIONS, CULTURAL AND SOCIETY

- The mystical theory emphasizes the spiritual basis of the state and considers the king as the representative of the divine on earth.
- The king derives his authority from divine sanction and his primary duty is to maintain order (dharma) in society as per the cosmic laws through just and virtuous rule.
- The Arthashastra and Manusmriti consider the king as the pivot of the polity and his personal qualities and righteous conduct as crucial for the well-being of the state.
- The king is required to perform certain rituals and sacrifices to establish a link between himself and the divine. Ashvamedha (horse sacrifice) was one such important ritual.
- The Gupta empire emphasized the divine sanction theory of kingship and the complementarity of the spiritual and temporal powers (danda-niti and daiva-niti). The titles Paramabhattaraka and Parameshvara affirmed the semi-divine status of the Gupta monarchs.
- The Chola inscriptions describe the king as the 'Lord of the Three Worlds', 'One whose orders are obeyed by the guardian deities of the eight directions', etc. thereby emphasizing his exalted ritual status and closeness to the divine.
