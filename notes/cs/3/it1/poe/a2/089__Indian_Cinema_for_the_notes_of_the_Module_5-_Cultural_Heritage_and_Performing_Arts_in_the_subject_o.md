### Indian Cinema

- Indian cinema is one of the largest film producers in the world. It consists of films produced across India, including the cinematic cultures of Hindi, Tamil, Telugu, Punjabi, Bengali, Malayalam, Kannada, Marathi, Odia, Gujarati, and Assamese industries.
- The first Indian feature film was the Bengali silent film 'Raja Harishchandra' in 1913. The first Indian sound film was the Tamil film 'Kalidas' in 1931. After India attained independence in 1947, the films started addressing social issues related to the common masses. The 1950s and 1960s are regarded as the golden age of Indian cinema.
- Some of the most acclaimed and popular Indian films include -
-- Mother India (1957) - A Hindi epic drama dealing with rural poverty and women's struggles in Indian society.
-- Sholay (1975) - A highly successful 'Curry Western' action film featuring Amitabh Bachchan and Dharmendra.
-- Pather Panchali (1955) - A Bengali drama by legendary director Satyajit Ray, depicting the poverty of an Indian village family.
- Indian cinema has a number of classic film genres including -
-- Mythological films based on stories from Indian epics like Ramayana and Mahabharata.
-- Devotional films based on the lives of saints, gurus and deities.
-- Masala films which incorporate various genres like action, comedy, romance and drama.
- Bollywood and South Indian cinema are the major film producers in India with distinct sensibilities and cinematic styles. The New Indian Cinema emphasises realistic parallel cinema. Indian cinema has had a huge impact on the country's masses and popular culture over the decades.
