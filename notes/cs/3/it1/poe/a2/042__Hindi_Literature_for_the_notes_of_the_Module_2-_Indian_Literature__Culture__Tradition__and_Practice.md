### Hindi Literature

- Hindi literature dates back to around the 4th century. The earliest literary works in Hindi were by the poets of the Bhakti movement such as Kabir, Surdas, and Ravidass.
- The modern Hindi literary tradition emerged during the 19th century with the literary works of Bhartendu Harishchandra. In the 20th century, Hindi literature flourished with the works of Premchand, Maithili Sharan Gupt, Sumitranandan Pant, Mahadevi Varma, and others.
- Major literary traditions of Hindi include Khadi boli or bracket CFM, Brajbhasha, Awadhi, Bundeli, Marwari, Bagheli, and others. Modern Standard Hindi is based on the Khariboli dialect.
- Themes in Hindi literature include Indian philosophy, the Indian independence movement, and the greater Indian diaspora. Genres include poetry, novels, short stories, drama, and essay.
- Notable works includePremchand's Godaan, Sumitranandan Pant's Chidambara, Mahadevi Varma's Yama, and Bhartendu Harishchandra's plays such as Andher Nagari and Bharat-durdasha.
- The Sahitya Akademi Award is one of the most prestigious awards for Hindi literature given annually by the Sahitya Akademi, India's National Academy of Letters.
