### Political Ideals in Ancient India

1. Dharma: The ideal of dharmic principles ruled the ancient Indian polity. The king was expected to uphold dharma through just and fair rule. The Arthashastra outlines the dharma of the king which includes providing welfare and protection to the people.

2. Fourfold Purusharthas: The four goals of human existence - dharma, artha, kama and moksha were guiding principles of governance. The king was expected to create conditions for the attainment of purusharthas for all individuals in the society.

3. Theory of divine origin: The king was believed to have divine origins. The possession of divinity gave the king a superior position but also made him responsible for the welfare of the people. The people could withdraw support if the king did not uphold his dharma.

4. Council of ministers: The king was assisted by a council of ministers in governance. The council reflected the diverse sections of the society and advised the king. The ultimate decision making power rested with the king but he was expected to consult the council before taking important decisions.

5. No absolute power: Though the king was superior, his power was not absolute. He was subject to dharma and could be questioned if he did not uphold it. Also, he had to take the advice of the council of ministers. This principle of not vesting absolute power in one person was an important political ideal.
