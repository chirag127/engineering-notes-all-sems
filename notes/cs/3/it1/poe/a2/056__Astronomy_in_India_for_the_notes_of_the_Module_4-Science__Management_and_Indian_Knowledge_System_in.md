### Astronomy in India

- Indian astronomy has a long history stretching from pre-historic to modern times. Some of the earliest roots of Indian astronomy can be dated to the Indus Valley civilization, around 3000 BC.
- The Rig Veda contains Mandala 10 which refers to astronomy and positions of planets based on 27 constellations. This was further expanded in later Vedic texts, including detailed astronomical calculations to be used in ritualistic ceremonies.
- Aryabhata's Aryabhatiya, written in 499 AD, expands on planetary models and trigonometry using sinusoidal functions, and calculates the length of the sidereal year and the Earth's circumference.
- Brahmagupta's Brahmasphutasiddhanta written in 628 AD includes a description of planetary conjunctions, innovative planetary models, approaches to determine latitude and longitude, and methods to determine solstices and equinoxes.
- The astronomical tradition continued with prominent astronomers including Bhaskara I (7th century), Bhaskara II (12th century), Madhava of Sangamagrama (14th century), Nilakantha Somayaji (15th-16th century), and others. Many astronomical concepts and methods were incorporated into Pancha-siddhantika in the 5th century, which reflects remarkable continuity and enrichment of knowledge across centuries.
- There were important applications of Indian astronomy including time-keeping, computation of calendars based on solstices/equinoxes and lunar/solar cycles, prediction of planetary conjunctions and eclipses, and determination of latitudes and longitudes for navigation.
- Indian astronomy emphasised computation and algorithms, but did not stress theory of planetary motion. Planetary orbits were viewed using epicycles, but some astronomers used elliptical orbits. The lack of precise instruments led to some errors, but Indian astronomers made remarkable contributions given the limitations.
