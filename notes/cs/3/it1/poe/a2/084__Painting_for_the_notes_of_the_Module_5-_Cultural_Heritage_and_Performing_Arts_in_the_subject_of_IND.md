### Painting for the notes of the Module 5- Cultural Heritage and Performing Arts in the subject of INDIAN TRADITIONS, CULTURAL AND SOCIETY

- Indian painting has a long tradition and history in Indian art. The paintings were done on walls of temples and palaces.
- The paintings primarily consisted of religious figures and motifs. They represented mythologies and stories.
- The styles of paintings evolved from the Pre-historic rock paintings to the Mughal miniatures during different periods.
- The notable painting styles are:
    - Ajanta Frescoes from 2nd century BCE which depicted Jataka tales and scenes from the life of Buddha.
    - Tanjore style of painting from 9th century which was known for rich colours and ornamentation with semi-precious stones and glasses.
    - Mughal painting which evolved during Islamic invasions and flourished under Akbar's patronage. The miniatures depicted courtly scenes, daily life, plants and animals.
    - Rajput painting style from 16th century showed hunting scenes, festivals, animal and bird studies.
    - Pattachitra style from Odisha which was done on palm leaf using natural colours and depicted religious stories and figures.
    - Madhubani art from Bihar which was done by the women of the region using homes as canvas and depicted mythological scenes, nature and daily life.
- Thus, Indian painting evolved over centuries and reflected the prevalent religions, customs and societies through distinctive styles in different regions. The paintings are valuable artistic expressions of Indian culture and history.
