### The Ramayana

- The Ramayana is one of the two major Sanskrit epics of ancient India, the other being the Mahabharata.
- It was composed by the sage Valmiki around the 4th century BCE.
- The epic Ramayana narrates the story of Rama, the seventh incarnation of the god Vishnu.
- It follows his adventures and life as he rescues his wife Sita after she is kidnapped by the demon king Ravana.
- The Ramayana consists of 24,000 verses which are divided into seven books and approximately 500 cantos.
- The epic tale is very influential and popular in India and South East Asia. Many alternative versions of the epic have developed across South and Southeast Asia over centuries.
- The story of Ramayana serves as an important influential tradition within Hinduism and has helped shape cultural traditions in many South Asian societies.
- It emphasizes virtues like devotion, duty, honour, sacrifice and unity of all mankind. Rama is seen as the ideal man and role model.
- The Ramayana continues to be frequently portrayed in many diverse art forms including dance, puppetry, visual arts and literature across South Asia.
