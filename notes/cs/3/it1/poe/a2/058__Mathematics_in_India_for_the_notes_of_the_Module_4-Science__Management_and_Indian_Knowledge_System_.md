### Mathematics in India

- Indian mathematics emerged in the Indian subcontinent from 1200 BCE until the end of the 18th century.
- The Indus Valley civilization (around 3000 BCE) and ancient Vedic period (around 2000 BCE) of Indian mathematics are not well explored.
- The earliest extant mathematical documents from India are the Sulba Sutras (around 800 BCE-500 BCE).
- They focus on geometry, meant to construct accurate fire altars during sacred rituals.
- The Indus Valley civilization may have developed a "practical mathematics" comparable to ancient Egypt and Mesopotamia.
- The Śulba Sūtras consist of "rules" for the construction of altars and mention geometric shapes like the circle and square.
- Geometry made early inroads and Jaina and Buddhist scholars contributed to mathematics during the Mauryan Empire (322 BCE-185 BCE).
- Pingala in the 2nd century BCE in his Chandas Shastra discusses arithmetic, geometric, and harmonic progressions.
- Aryabhata (476 CE - 550 CE) wrote the Aryabhatiya covering many topics including trigonometry and algebra.
- Brahmagupta (598 CE - 668 CE) wrote Brahmasphutasiddhanta with rules for arithmetic operations and algorithms for square roots and cube roots.
- Bhaskara II (1114 CE - 1185 CE) in his Bijaganita expanded on quadratic equations and characterized different types of zeros of quadratic polynomials.
- The Kerala School of Astronomy and Mathematics (14th century to 16th century) focused on infinite series expansions and calculus.
- Madhava of Sangamagrama (1350 CE - 1425 CE) and Nilakantha Somayaji (1444 CE - 1544 CE) were prominent mathematicians of this school.
