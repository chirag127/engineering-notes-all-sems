### Marriage

- Marriage is a socially sanctioned union between two individuals that establishes certain rights and obligations between them, between them and their children, and between them and their in-laws.
- In most societies marriage links not just two individuals but also two families.
- The structure of marriage varies across cultures and has changed over time. Some of the common forms are:
- Monogamy: Marriage of two individuals.
- Polygamy: Marriage of one individual to two or more others. For example, polygyny means a male marrying two or more females.
- Arranged marriage: Marriage arranged by family members.
- Love marriage: Marriage based on love and consent of individuals.
- Endogamy: Marriage within a social group. For example, caste endogamy means marriage within one's caste.
- Exogamy: Marriage outside a social group. For example, clan exogamy means marriage outside one's clan.
- Marriage has socio-economic and political functions like the formation of alliances, division of labor along gender lines, regulation of sex, and reproduction and transfer of property.
- Marriage practices and rules vary across religions in India. While monogamy is the norm for Christianity and Islam, Hinduism allows polygamy and practices like levirate and sororate.
