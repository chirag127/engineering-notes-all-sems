
### Kingship in India

* Kingship in India is an ancient form of governance that dates back to the Vedic period (1500–500 BC).
* Kings were seen as divinely appointed rulers, and their authority was seen as coming from the gods.
* Kings were expected to uphold Dharma (righteousness) and were responsible for the welfare of their subjects.
* Kings were usually from the Kshatriya (warrior) caste and were expected to uphold the principles of courage, honour and loyalty.
* In the Mauryan period (322–185 BC), the kings adopted a more centralized form of government and established an elaborate bureaucracy.
* The Gupta period (320–550 AD) saw the rise of the imperial system, which was based on the principles of a centralized government and a hierarchical bureaucracy.
* During the Mughal period (1526–1857 AD), the Mughal emperors adopted the imperial system and established a powerful and centralized government.
* In modern India, the President is the head of state and the Prime Minister is the head of government.
