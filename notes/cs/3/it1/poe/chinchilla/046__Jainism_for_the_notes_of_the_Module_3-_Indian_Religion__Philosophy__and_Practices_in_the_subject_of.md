### Jainism

Jainism is an ancient Indian religion that emphasizes non-violence, self-control, and religious tolerance. It was founded by Lord Mahavira in the 6th century BCE.

#### Basic Beliefs

Jainism is based on three main principles:

1. Ahimsa - non-violence towards all living beings
2. Anekantavada - the idea that truth is multi-faceted and cannot be fully understood from any single perspective
3. Karma - the belief that one's actions in this life will determine one's fate in the next

#### Practices

Jains follow a strict code of conduct that includes:

1. Vegetarianism - Jains do not eat meat or any other products that involve the killing of animals
2. Fasting - Jains fast regularly as a form of self-discipline
3. Meditation - Jains practice meditation to achieve inner peace and self-awareness
4. Pilgrimage - Jains visit holy sites and temples as a form of spiritual practice

#### Scriptures

Jains follow a collection of sacred texts called the Agamas. These texts contain the teachings of Lord Mahavira and other Jain leaders.

#### Sects

Jainism is divided into two main sects:

1. Digambara - monks of this sect do not wear any clothes and believe that total nudity is necessary for spiritual liberation
2. Svetambara - monks of this sect wear white robes and believe that nudity is not necessary for spiritual liberation

#### Significant Figures

Some of the most significant figures in Jainism include:

1. Lord Mahavira - the founder of Jainism
2. Rishabhanatha - the first Tirthankara (spiritual leader) of Jainism
3. Parshvanatha - the 23rd Tirthankara of Jainism
4. Gautama Swami - one of Lord Mahavira's chief disciples

#### Influence

Jainism has had a significant influence on Indian culture and society. Its emphasis on non-violence has inspired movements for social justice and political change. Jain philosophy has also contributed to the development of Indian art, literature, and architecture.
