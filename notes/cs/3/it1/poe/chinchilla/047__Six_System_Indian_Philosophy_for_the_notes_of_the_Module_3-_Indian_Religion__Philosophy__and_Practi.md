### Six System Indian Philosophy

1. The Six System Indian Philosophy, also known as Shad-darshana, refers to the six major schools of philosophy in ancient India.

2. These schools of thought emerged between the 2nd century BCE to the 2nd century CE and were developed by various Indian philosophers and thinkers.

3. Each of these schools has its own unique perspective on the nature of reality, the purpose of life, and the means to achieve liberation.

4. The six systems of Indian philosophy are as follows:

   a) Samkhya: It is one of the oldest schools of Indian philosophy that explains the nature of the universe as consisting of two distinct realities - Purusha (consciousness) and Prakriti (matter).

   b) Yoga: Yoga philosophy emphasizes the practice of meditation and physical postures to achieve self-realization and liberation.

   c) Nyaya: It is a school of logic that emphasizes the study of epistemology (theory of knowledge) and metaphysics.

   d) Vaisheshika: It is a school of philosophy that focuses on the study of the nature of the universe and the classification of its elements.

   e) Mimamsa: It is a school of philosophy that focuses on the interpretation of the Vedic texts and the performance of Vedic rituals.

   f) Vedanta: Vedanta philosophy is based on the belief in the ultimate reality of Brahman and the goal of achieving liberation through the realization of this reality.

5. Each of these schools has its own unique way of understanding the nature of reality and the means to achieve liberation.

6. The study of the six systems of Indian philosophy is important in understanding the complexity and diversity of Indian thought and culture. It provides a deeper understanding of the Indian worldview and the ways in which Indian philosophy has influenced other cultures and traditions.
