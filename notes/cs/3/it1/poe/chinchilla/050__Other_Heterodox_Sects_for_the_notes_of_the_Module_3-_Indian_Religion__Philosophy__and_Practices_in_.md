### Other Heterodox Sects

Here are some of the other heterodox sects found in Indian religion:

- Ajivika: This sect was founded by Makkhali Gosala in the 5th century BCE. The Ajivikas believed in the concept of fate and that everything that happens in life is predetermined. They also believed in the transmigration of the soul.

- Charvaka: This sect was founded by Brihaspati in the 6th century BCE. The Charvakas believed in the principle of materialism and denied the existence of the soul, afterlife, and gods. They believed that the only reality is what can be perceived by the senses.

- Jainism: Jainism was founded by Mahavira in the 6th century BCE. Jains believe in the concept of karma and the cycle of birth and rebirth. They follow non-violence and believe in the idea of ahimsa or non-harming.

- Buddhism: Buddhism was founded by Siddhartha Gautama in the 5th century BCE. Buddhists believe in the Four Noble Truths and the Eightfold Path. They follow the idea of impermanence and the cessation of suffering.

- Sikhism: Sikhism was founded by Guru Nanak in the 16th century CE. Sikhs believe in the concept of one God and follow the teachings of the ten Sikh Gurus. They believe in the principles of equality and service to others.

- Kabir Panth: This sect was founded by the poet Kabir in the 15th century CE. The Kabir Panthis believe in the concept of one God and follow the teachings of Kabir. They believe in the principles of love, equality, and service to others.

- Nath Sampradaya: This sect was founded by Gorakshanath in the 11th century CE. The Nath Sampradayis believe in the concept of one God and the attainment of liberation through the practice of yoga and meditation.

- Dadu Panth: This sect was founded by Dadu Dayal in the 16th century CE. The Dadu Panthis believe in the concept of one God and the attainment of liberation through devotion and service to others.

These heterodox sects have their own unique beliefs and practices, which are different from the orthodox Hindu traditions. They have contributed to the diversity of Indian religion and philosophy.
