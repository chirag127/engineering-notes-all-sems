### Seals for the notes of the Module 5- Cultural Heritage and Performing Arts in the subject of INDIAN TRADITIONS, CULTURAL AND SOCIETY.

Seals have played a significant role in the cultural heritage of India. Here are some important points to take note of when studying the role of seals in Indian traditions, cultural and society:

- Seals have been used in India for thousands of years, with early examples dating back to the Indus Valley Civilization. These seals were made of materials such as steatite, faience, and ivory, and were used to mark goods and documents.
- Seals were often used to indicate ownership or authorship. For example, a seal might be used to mark a document as belonging to a particular individual or family. Seals were also used to mark goods as belonging to a particular trader or merchant.
- The use of seals was not limited to economic transactions. Seals were also used in religious and spiritual contexts. For example, seals were used to mark religious texts and artifacts, and were sometimes used in rituals and ceremonies.
- Seals were often highly decorated, with intricate designs and inscriptions. These designs often incorporated elements of the natural world, such as animals, plants, and geometric patterns. Some seals also featured inscriptions in ancient scripts, such as Brahmi and Kharosthi.
- The use of seals declined in India with the introduction of paper and the spread of literacy. However, the cultural significance of seals has endured, and they continue to be used in some traditional contexts today. For example, some Indian artisans still use seals to mark their work and indicate their authorship.

In summary, seals have played an important role in the cultural heritage of India, both as practical tools for marking goods and documents and as objects of cultural and spiritual significance. Understanding the history and significance of seals is an important aspect of studying Indian traditions, cultural and society.
