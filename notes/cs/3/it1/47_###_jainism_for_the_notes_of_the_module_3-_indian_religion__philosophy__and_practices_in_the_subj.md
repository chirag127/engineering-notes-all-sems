### Jainism for the notes of the Module 3- Indian Religion, Philosophy, and Practices in the subject of INDIAN TRADITIONS, CULTURAL AND SOCIETY

Jainism is an ancient Indian religion that originated in the Indian subcontinent around the 6th century BCE. It is one of the oldest religions in India and is based on the teachings of a series of 24 enlightened beings known as Tirthankaras, with the first and most important being Lord Mahavira.

Jainism is a non-theistic religion that emphasizes individual spiritual progress and the attainment of liberation from the cycle of birth and death. This liberation is achieved through a process of self-purification and the attainment of spiritual knowledge, known as kevala jnana.

Jains believe in the existence of a soul, which is believed to be eternal and infinite. They also believe in the existence of a number of non-physical entities, such as gods, demons, and other beings, which are believed to inhabit the universe.

Jainism places a strong emphasis on non-violence and compassion, and Jains are known for their strict adherence to a vegetarian diet and their commitment to avoiding harm to any living being. Jainism also places a strong emphasis on the importance of self-discipline and the practice of austerities, such as fasting, meditation, and other forms of asceticism.

Jainism has a rich tradition of literature, with a number of important texts, such as the Agamas, the Purvas, and the Tattvartha Sutra, playing a central role in the religion. Jainism also has a long and rich history of art and architecture, with a number of important Jain temples and monuments being found throughout India.

Jainism has a significant following in India, with a number of important centers of pilgrimage, such as Palitana in Gujarat and Shikharji in Jharkhand. It also has a growing number of followers in other countries, particularly in the West, where it has gained popularity in recent years.

In conclusion, Jainism is an ancient Indian religion that emphasizes individual spiritual progress, non-violence, and compassion. It is a rich and diverse tradition that has had a significant impact on Indian culture, religion, and society, and continues to be an important part of the religious landscape of India and beyond.
