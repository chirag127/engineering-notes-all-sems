### Six System Indian Philosophy for the notes of the Module 3- Indian Religion, Philosophy, and Practices in the subject of INDIAN TRADITIONS, CULTURAL AND SOCIETY

The Six Systems of Indian Philosophy, also known as the Shatdarshana, are a group of classical philosophical systems that have been influential in the development of Hindu thought and culture. The six systems are:

1. Samkhya: Samkhya is a dualistic philosophy that posits the existence of two ultimate realities, Purusha (consciousness) and Prakriti (matter). It is considered to be one of the oldest philosophical systems in India and is said to have been taught by the sage Kapila.

2. Yoga: Yoga is a philosophical and spiritual tradition that originated in India and is focused on the cultivation of inner peace and self-realization. It is based on the Samkhya philosophy and is considered to be one of the six systems of Indian philosophy.

3. Nyaya: Nyaya is a system of logic and epistemology that is focused on the nature of knowledge and the methods of valid inference. It was developed by the sage Gautama and is considered to be one of the six systems of Indian philosophy.

4. Vaisheshika: Vaisheshika is a system of metaphysics that is focused on the nature of reality and the relationship between objects and their properties. It was developed by the sage Kanada and is considered to be one of the six systems of Indian philosophy.

5. Mimamsa: Mimamsa is a system of philosophy that is focused on the interpretation of the Vedas, the sacred texts of Hinduism. It was developed by the sage Jaimini and is considered to be one of the six systems of Indian philosophy.

6. Vedanta: Vedanta is a system of philosophy that is based on the teachings of the Upanishads, the philosophical texts that are considered to be part of the Vedas. It is considered to be the most important of the six systems of Indian philosophy and is said to be the culmination of all the other systems.

These six systems of Indian philosophy have been influential in shaping the religious, philosophical, and cultural traditions of India, and they continue to be studied and debated by scholars and practitioners to this day. Whether through the dualistic philosophy of Samkhya, the spiritual practice of Yoga, the logical system of Nyaya, the metaphysics of Vaisheshika, the interpretation of the Vedas in Mimamsa, or the teachings of the Upanishads in Vedanta, the Six Systems of Indian Philosophy provide a rich and diverse framework for understanding the nature of reality, the nature of knowledge, and the path to self-realization.
