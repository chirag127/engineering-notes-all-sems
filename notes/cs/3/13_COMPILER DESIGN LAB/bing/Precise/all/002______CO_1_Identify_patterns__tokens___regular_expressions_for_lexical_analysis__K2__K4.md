#### CO 1 Identify patterns, tokens & regular expressions for lexical analysis. K2, K4

Lexical analysis is the process of converting a sequence of characters into a sequence of tokens. A token is a sequence of characters that represents a single logical entity, such as a keyword, identifier, or operator. The following points explain the process of identifying patterns, tokens, and regular expressions for lexical analysis:

1. **Patterns**: A pattern is a description of the form that the lexemes of a token may take. In lexical analysis, patterns are used to specify the rules for recognizing tokens. For example, the pattern for an identifier in many programming languages is a letter followed by zero or more letters or digits.

2. **Tokens**: Tokens are the smallest units of a program that have meaning to the compiler. They are generated by the lexical analyzer and passed to the parser for syntax analysis. Tokens can be classified into several categories, such as keywords, identifiers, operators, and literals.

3. **Regular expressions**: Regular expressions are a powerful tool for describing patterns. They are used in lexical analysis to specify the patterns for recognizing tokens. A regular expression is a string that describes a set of strings. It is composed of characters, operators, and constructs that denote sets of strings.

In summary, lexical analysis involves identifying patterns, tokens, and regular expressions to convert a sequence of characters into a sequence of tokens. This process is essential for the compiler to understand the structure and meaning of the program.
