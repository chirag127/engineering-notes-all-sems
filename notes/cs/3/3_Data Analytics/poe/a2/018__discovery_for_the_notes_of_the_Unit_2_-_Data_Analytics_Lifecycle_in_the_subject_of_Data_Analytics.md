### Discovery

- Define the business problem: Identify the key business problem or opportunity where data analytics can help. This could be increasing revenue, reducing costs, improving customer experience, etc.
- Determine key metrics: Identify the key metrics that would signify if the business problem is solved or opportunity is achieved. For example, increase in monthly recurring revenue, reduction in customer churn, increase in customer satisfaction scores, etc.
- Data inventory: Take an inventory of all the internal and external data sources that could be relevant to solve the business problem. This could include data from CRM systems, transaction databases, social media, industry reports, etc.
- Determine data gaps: Identify any gaps in the data that is needed to solve the business problem. The gaps could be missing data, incomplete data, or the need for additional data sources.
- Discuss feasibility: Evaluate the feasibility of collecting and analyzing the required data to solve the business problem. Consider timelines, costs, resource requirements, and regulatory requirements.
- Define the hypothesis: Formulate hypotheses on how analyzing the data might lead to insights and solutions to solve the business problem. The hypotheses would be validated or invalidated as the data is analyzed in the subsequent steps.
