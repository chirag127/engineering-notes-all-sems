### Interaction Techniques for Unit 6 - Frameworks and Visualization

1. Selection:
- Use highlight, lasso and brush selection tools to select data points or subsets of the data for filtering and emphasis.
- Selection can enable focus+context techniques where the selected data is highlighted and the unselected data is deemphasized.

2. Filtering:
- Filters enable users to reduce the number of visible data points based on attributes or categories.
- Range sliders, checkboxes, radio buttons or keywords can be used for filtering the data.
- Filtering reduces clutter and allows users to focus on relevant data points. However, it can lead to losing the overall context if not designed properly.

3. Linking & Brushing:
- Linking refers to connecting the visual encodings of multiple views such that selecting or hovering over data in one view highlights the corresponding data in the other view.
- Brushing refers to selecting data points in one view to highlight the corresponding points in other views. Linking and brushing enable exploration of relationships and patterns across multiple visualizations.

4. Zooming and Panning:
- Zooming changes the scale of the visualization allowing to see greater detail or more context.
- Panning moves the visible area of the visualization in horizontal or vertical directions to bring new data into view.
- Zooming and panning are common navigation techniques and can be used together to explore the data space. However, they can disorient the user if the overview context is not provided.
