### Sources and nature of data for the notes of the Unit 1 - Introduction to Data Analytics in the subject of Data Analytics

In the field of data analytics, it is essential to have a clear understanding of the sources and nature of data. This knowledge helps in the proper handling and analysis of data, which is crucial for making informed decisions. Below are some of the sources and nature of data that are essential to understand in the context of data analytics:

1. **Primary Data:** This refers to the data that is collected by the analyst or organization directly from the source. Primary data can be collected in various ways, such as surveys, interviews, experiments, and observations.

2. **Secondary Data:** This refers to data that is already available and has been collected by someone else. Secondary data can be obtained from various sources, such as government agencies, research organizations, and academic institutions.

3. **Big Data:** This refers to large and complex data sets that cannot be easily processed using traditional data processing methods. Big data often includes unstructured data such as texts, images, and videos.

4. **Structured Data:** This refers to data that is organized and can be easily analyzed using traditional methods. Structured data includes data in tables, spreadsheets, and databases.

5. **Unstructured Data:** This refers to data that is not organized and cannot be easily analyzed using traditional methods. Unstructured data includes data from social media, emails, and other text-based sources.

6. **Real-time Data:** This refers to data that is generated and processed at the same time. Real-time data is often used in applications such as traffic monitoring, stock trading, and weather forecasting.

7. **Historical Data:** This refers to data that has been collected over a period of time and can be used for trend analysis and forecasting.

8. **Public Data:** This refers to data that is available to the general public. Public data can be obtained from various sources such as government websites, social media, and other public databases.

In conclusion, understanding the sources and nature of data is a crucial step in the data analytics process. It is important to know the type of data being analyzed to ensure that the appropriate methods and tools are used for analysis.
