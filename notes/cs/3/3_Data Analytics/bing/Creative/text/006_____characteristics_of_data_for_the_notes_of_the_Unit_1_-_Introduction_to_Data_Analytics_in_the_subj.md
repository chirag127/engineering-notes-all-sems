### Characteristics of Data

Data is any form of information that has been gathered and organized in a meaningful format that can be processed further. Data can be used for various purposes, such as analysis, decision making, communication, etc. Data can be classified into different types, such as numerical, categorical, ordinal, etc. depending on the nature and level of measurement of the information.

To ensure that data is of good quality and useful, it should have some characteristics, such as :

- **Accuracy**: Data should be precise and contain accurate information. It should be free of errors, mistakes, or biases that may affect the validity or reliability of the data. Accuracy can be measured by comparing the data with the actual or expected values, or by using quality control methods.
- **Relevance**: Data should be relevant and according to the requirements of the user. It should be appropriate for the intended purpose and context of the data analysis. Relevance can be assessed by evaluating the usefulness, timeliness, and completeness of the data.
- **Consistency**: Data should be consistent and reliable across different sources, systems, or time periods. It should not have any contradictions, discrepancies, or anomalies that may affect the integrity or comparability of the data. Consistency can be ensured by following standard procedures, formats, and definitions for data collection, processing, and storage.
- **Completeness**: Data should be complete and cover all the aspects of the phenomenon or problem under study. It should not have any missing, incomplete, or irrelevant values that may affect the accuracy or representativeness of the data. Completeness can be improved by using appropriate sampling methods, data validation techniques, and data imputation methods.
- **Uniqueness**: Data should be unique and distinct from other data sets or records. It should not have any duplicates, redundancies, or overlaps that may affect the efficiency or clarity of the data. Uniqueness can be achieved by using unique identifiers, keys, or indexes for data entities, attributes, or values.

Data can also be described by its two main characteristics: central tendency and dispersion :

- **Central tendency**: Data tends to be centered around a point known as the average or the measure of central tendency. It indicates the typical or most common value of the data. There are different ways to measure the central tendency, such as mean, median, mode, etc. depending on the type and distribution of the data.
- **Dispersion**: Data tends to be spread out from the central point to different extents known as the measure of dispersion. It indicates the variability or diversity of the data. There are different ways to measure the dispersion, such as range, standard deviation, variance, etc. depending on the type and distribution of the data.
