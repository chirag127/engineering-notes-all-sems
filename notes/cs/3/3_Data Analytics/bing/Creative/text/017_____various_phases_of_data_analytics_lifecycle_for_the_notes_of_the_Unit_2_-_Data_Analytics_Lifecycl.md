### Various phases of data analytics lifecycle

The data analytics lifecycle is a framework that guides the process of transforming data into insights that can support business decisions. It consists of six phases, based on the CRISP-DM methodology :

- **Business understanding**: This phase involves defining the problem or opportunity, identifying the objectives and requirements, and establishing the scope and criteria for the data analytics project.
- **Data understanding**: This phase involves collecting, exploring, and describing the data that is relevant to the problem or opportunity. It also involves assessing the quality, completeness, and suitability of the data for the analysis.
- **Data preparation**: This phase involves cleaning, transforming, and integrating the data into a format that can be used for analysis. It also involves selecting, sampling, and partitioning the data for modeling and validation.
- **Modeling**: This phase involves applying various analytical techniques and algorithms to the data to discover patterns, relationships, and insights. It also involves selecting, testing, and comparing different models to find the best fit for the data and the problem.
- **Evaluation**: This phase involves assessing the performance, accuracy, and validity of the models and the insights they generate. It also involves reviewing the results and the assumptions, and identifying the limitations and risks of the analysis.
- **Deployment**: This phase involves communicating and presenting the results and the insights to the stakeholders, and implementing the solutions or recommendations based on the analysis. It also involves monitoring and updating the models and the data as needed.

These phases are not necessarily sequential or linear, and they may require iteration and feedback loops depending on the complexity and the context of the project. The data analytics lifecycle provides a structured and systematic way to manage data and to generate value from it.
