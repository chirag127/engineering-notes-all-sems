### Visualization before analysis

- Visualization is the process of creating and displaying graphical representations of data or information.
- Visualization can help to explore, understand, communicate, and present data or information in a meaningful and effective way.
- Visualization can also support the analysis of data or information by revealing patterns, trends, outliers, relationships, distributions, and other insights that may not be easily detected by numerical or textual methods.
- Visualization before analysis is the practice of creating and examining visualizations of data or information before applying any analytical techniques or methods.
- Visualization before analysis can have several benefits, such as:

  - It can help to identify the characteristics, quality, and structure of the data or information, such as the type, range, scale, dimensionality, granularity, completeness, accuracy, and consistency of the data or information.
  - It can help to formulate, refine, or validate the analytical questions, hypotheses, or goals, such as the purpose, scope, context, and audience of the analysis.
  - It can help to select, design, or evaluate the appropriate analytical techniques or methods, such as the type, level, and complexity of the analysis, and the assumptions, limitations, and trade-offs of the analytical techniques or methods.
  - It can help to interpret, explain, or communicate the results or findings of the analysis, such as the significance, validity, reliability, and implications of the results or findings.

- Visualization before analysis can be done using various tools, techniques, and principles, such as:

  - Tools: software applications, libraries, frameworks, or platforms that can create and display visualizations of data or information, such as Excel, Tableau, Power BI, R, Python, D3.js, etc.
  - Techniques: methods or procedures that can generate or manipulate visualizations of data or information, such as charts, graphs, maps, diagrams, tables, dashboards, etc.
  - Principles: guidelines or best practices that can enhance the quality or effectiveness of visualizations of data or information, such as data-ink ratio, chart junk, lie factor, visual encoding, visual perception, visual literacy, etc.

- Visualization before analysis can be applied to various types, sources, and domains of data or information, such as:

  - Types: numerical, categorical, temporal, spatial, relational, hierarchical, multivariate, etc.
  - Sources: databases, files, web, sensors, surveys, etc.
  - Domains: business, science, engineering, education, health, social, etc.
