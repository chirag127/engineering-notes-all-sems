### Exploratory Data Analysis

- Exploratory data analysis (EDA) is the process of examining and summarizing data sets using statistical and graphical methods.
- EDA aims to discover patterns, trends, outliers, anomalies, and relationships in the data, as well as to check the assumptions and quality of the data.
- EDA is an essential step before applying any formal modeling or inference techniques, as it can help to choose appropriate methods, identify potential problems, and generate hypotheses.
- EDA can be performed using various tools and frameworks, such as Python, R, Excel, Tableau, Power BI, etc.
- EDA typically involves the following steps:

  - **Data collection**: Obtaining the data from various sources, such as databases, files, APIs, web scraping, etc.
  - **Data cleaning**: Preparing the data for analysis by handling missing values, outliers, errors, duplicates, inconsistencies, etc.
  - **Data transformation**: Manipulating the data to create new variables, aggregate, filter, sort, group, join, etc.
  - **Data visualization**: Creating charts, graphs, maps, dashboards, etc. to explore the distribution, variation, correlation, and comparison of the data.
  - **Data interpretation**: Drawing conclusions and insights from the data, as well as identifying limitations and further questions.

- Some of the common types of data visualization techniques are:

  - **Histograms**: Showing the frequency distribution of a single numeric variable.
  - **Box plots**: Showing the summary statistics (such as median, quartiles, range, outliers) of a single numeric variable or across different groups.
  - **Scatter plots**: Showing the relationship between two numeric variables, as well as the possibility of a linear or nonlinear trend.
  - **Bar charts**: Showing the comparison of a categorical variable or a numeric variable across different categories.
  - **Pie charts**: Showing the proportion of a categorical variable or a numeric variable across different categories.
  - **Line charts**: Showing the change of a numeric variable over time or another continuous variable.
  - **Heat maps**: Showing the intensity of a numeric variable across two dimensions, such as a matrix or a geographic area.
  - **Tree maps**: Showing the hierarchical structure and proportion of a categorical variable or a numeric variable across different levels.
  - **Word clouds**: Showing the frequency of words or phrases in a text data set.
