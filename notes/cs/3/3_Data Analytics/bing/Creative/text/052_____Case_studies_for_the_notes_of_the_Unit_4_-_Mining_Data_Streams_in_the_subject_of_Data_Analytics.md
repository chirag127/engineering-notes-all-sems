### Case studies for the notes of the Unit 4 - Mining Data Streams in the subject of Data Analytics

- Mining data streams is the process of extracting knowledge structures, such as models and patterns, from continuous and unbounded streams of information.
- Data stream mining poses many challenges, such as high volume, high velocity, concept drift, limited memory, and real-time processing.
- Data stream mining algorithms need to be scalable, adaptive, incremental, and efficient.
- Some of the applications of data stream mining include network monitoring, sensor networks, web analytics, social media analysis, and fraud detection.
- Here are some case studies of data stream mining from different domains:

  - **A Case Study: Stream Data Mining Classification**
    - This case study presents a stream data mining classification system that uses a sliding window technique and a decision tree algorithm to classify streaming data.
    - The system is applied to a real-world dataset of network intrusion detection, which contains four types of attacks and normal traffic.
    - The system achieves high accuracy and low processing time, and can handle concept drift and missing values in the data stream.
  - **Improvised methods for tackling big data stream mining challenges: case study of electricity price prediction**
    - This case study proposes a holistic data stream mining approach that integrates different components of data stream mining, such as feature selection, ensemble learning, and concept drift detection, to improve the prediction performance.
    - The approach is applied to a real-world dataset of electricity price prediction, which contains hourly prices of electricity in the Australian market.
    - The approach outperforms existing methods in terms of accuracy, stability, and adaptability, and can handle high-dimensional and noisy data streams.
  - **An update on global mining land use**
    - This case study presents a global dataset of mining land use, which covers both active and inactive mining sites across different commodities and countries.
    - The dataset is derived from satellite imagery and other sources, and provides spatial and temporal information on the extent and impact of mining activities.
    - The dataset can be used for various purposes, such as environmental assessment, policy analysis, and resource management.
