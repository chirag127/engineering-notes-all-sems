### Mining frequent itemsets

- Frequent itemsets are sets of items that appear together frequently in a dataset, such as a transactional database or a relational database.
- Mining frequent itemsets is a technique used to discover patterns and relationships among items in a dataset, such as association rules, correlations, or sequential patterns.
- Mining frequent itemsets can help to understand the behavior and preferences of customers, users, or entities in various domains, such as retail, e-commerce, social media, or bioinformatics.
- Mining frequent itemsets can also help to improve the performance and efficiency of other data mining tasks, such as classification, clustering, or recommendation systems.
- Mining frequent itemsets involves two main steps: finding all the frequent itemsets in a dataset, and deriving interesting rules or patterns from them.
- A frequent itemset is defined by a minimum support threshold, which is the minimum number or percentage of transactions or records that contain the itemset in the dataset.
- A rule or pattern derived from a frequent itemset is defined by a minimum confidence threshold, which is the minimum ratio of transactions or records that contain both the antecedent and the consequent of the rule or pattern in the dataset.
- There are various algorithms for mining frequent itemsets, such as Apriori, Eclat, or FP-growth. These algorithms differ in their data structures, search strategies, and pruning techniques, but they all follow the same general principle: any subset of a frequent itemset must also be frequent, and any superset of an infrequent itemset must also be infrequent. This principle is also known as the Apriori property or the anti-monotonicity property.
- Apriori is the most commonly used algorithm for mining frequent itemsets. It works by generating candidate itemsets of increasing size and scanning the dataset to count their support. It prunes the candidates that do not meet the minimum support threshold and repeats the process until no more candidates can be generated.
- Eclat is a variation of Apriori that uses a vertical data format, where each item is associated with a set of transaction identifiers (TIDs) that contain the item. It works by intersecting the TID sets of items to find their common transactions and counting their support. It prunes the items that do not meet the minimum support threshold and repeats the process until no more items can be intersected.
- FP-growth is an alternative algorithm for mining frequent itemsets that uses a compact data structure called a frequent pattern tree (FP-tree). It works by compressing the dataset into an FP-tree that preserves the itemset information and the support information. It then mines the FP-tree by recursively extracting conditional FP-trees and finding their frequent itemsets. It does not generate any candidate itemsets and does not scan the dataset more than twice.
