### Linear Systems Analysis & Nonlinear Dynamics

- Linear systems are systems whose behavior can be described by linear equations, which means that the change in one variable is proportional to the change in another variable.
- Nonlinear systems are systems whose behavior cannot be described by linear equations, which means that the change in one variable is not proportional to the change in another variable, or that there are interactions between variables that produce nonlinear effects.
- Linear systems can be solved exactly, while most nonlinear systems can only be solved approximately or numerically.
- Linear systems have properties such as superposition, homogeneity, and stability, while nonlinear systems may exhibit complex phenomena such as chaos, bifurcations, and limit cycles.
- Linear systems analysis involves finding the solutions, eigenvalues, and eigenvectors of the system's matrix, which can be used to determine the system's behavior, such as oscillations, damping, and resonance.
- Nonlinear systems analysis involves finding the equilibrium points, fixed points, or attractors of the system, and studying their stability, sensitivity, and bifurcations using methods such as linearization, phase portraits, Lyapunov functions, and Poincaré maps.
