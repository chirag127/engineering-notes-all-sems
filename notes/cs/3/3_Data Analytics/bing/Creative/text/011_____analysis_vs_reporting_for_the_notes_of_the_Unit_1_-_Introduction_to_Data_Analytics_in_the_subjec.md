### Analysis vs Reporting

- Reporting is the process of presenting data in a structured and summarized way, such as tables, charts, or dashboards.
- Analysis is the process of exploring, interpreting, and explaining data, such as finding patterns, trends, outliers, or insights.
- Reporting answers the question of what happened, such as how many sales were made, what was the revenue, or what was the customer satisfaction score.
- Analysis answers the question of why it happened, such as what factors influenced the sales, what was the impact of the revenue, or what caused the customer satisfaction score to change.
- Reporting is usually based on predefined metrics and dimensions, such as sales by region, revenue by product, or customer satisfaction by segment.
- Analysis is usually based on ad hoc queries and hypotheses, such as what is the correlation between sales and revenue, what is the effect of a marketing campaign on customer satisfaction, or what are the best practices for improving product quality.
- Reporting is usually done periodically, such as daily, weekly, or monthly, to monitor the performance and status of the data.
- Analysis is usually done on demand, such as when there is a problem, a decision, or an opportunity, to understand the root cause and the potential actions.
- Reporting is usually consumed by a wide audience, such as managers, stakeholders, or customers, to inform them about the data.
- Analysis is usually consumed by a specific audience, such as analysts, experts, or decision makers, to advise them about the data.
