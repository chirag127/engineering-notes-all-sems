### Bayesian modeling

Bayesian modeling is a statistical model where you use probability to represent all uncertainty within the model, both the uncertainty regarding the output but also the uncertainty regarding the input (aka parameters) to the model.

Some key points about Bayesian modeling are:

- Bayesian modeling is based on Bayes' theorem, which describes how to update the probability of a hypothesis or an event based on new data and prior beliefs.
- Bayesian modeling can be applied to any type of data, such as categorical, continuous, discrete, or mixed.
- Bayesian modeling can handle complex and hierarchical structures, such as nested or grouped data, multilevel models, or latent variables.
- Bayesian modeling can incorporate prior knowledge or information from other sources, such as expert opinions, previous studies, or domain knowledge.
- Bayesian modeling can provide a quantitative assessment of the uncertainty and variability of the estimates, such as credible intervals, posterior distributions, or predictive distributions.
- Bayesian modeling can be used for decision-making and policy evaluation, by comparing the expected outcomes and risks of different actions or interventions.
- Bayesian modeling can be implemented using various computational methods, such as Markov chain Monte Carlo (MCMC), variational inference, or approximate Bayesian computation (ABC).
