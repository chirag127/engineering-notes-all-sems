### Descriptive statistics
- Descriptive statistics are numerical or graphical summaries of data that help us understand the main features and patterns of the data.
- Descriptive statistics can be divided into two types: measures of central tendency and measures of variability.
- Measures of central tendency describe the typical or average value of a data set, such as the mean, median, and mode.
- Measures of variability describe how spread out or dispersed the data values are, such as the range, standard deviation, and variance.
- Descriptive statistics can also include measures of shape, such as skewness and kurtosis, which describe how symmetric or asymmetric the data distribution is, and measures of association, such as correlation and covariance, which describe how two or more variables are related to each other.
- Descriptive statistics can be computed for different types of data, such as categorical, ordinal, interval, or ratio data, depending on the level of measurement and the appropriate statistical methods.
- Descriptive statistics can be presented in various ways, such as tables, charts, graphs, or diagrams, to help visualize the data and communicate the results effectively.
- Descriptive statistics are useful for exploring and summarizing data, but they do not allow us to make inferences or draw conclusions about the population from which the data are sampled. For that, we need inferential statistics, which use probability theory and hypothesis testing to make generalizations and predictions based on the data.
