### Real Time Sentiment Analysis

- Real time sentiment analysis is a machine learning technique that automatically recognizes and extracts the sentiment in a text whenever it occurs .
- Sentiment is the subjective opinion or attitude expressed by a person towards a topic, such as a brand, product, service, event, or person.
- Sentiment analysis can be used to analyze live social and online feeds, such as comments, posts, reviews, tweets, chats, etc., and derive insights from the text for various applications, such as customer service, marketing, reputation management, product development, etc .
- The process of sentiment analysis follows these four steps :
  - Breaking down the text into components: sentences, phrases, tokens, and parts of speech.
  - Identifying each phrase and component, such as subject, object, modifier, polarity, intensity, etc.
  - Assigning a sentiment score to each phrase with plus or minus points, based on predefined rules or trained models.
  - Combining scores for a final sentiment analysis, which can be binary (positive or negative), ternary (positive, negative, or neutral), or multiclass (happy, sad, angry, etc.).
- Real time sentiment analysis requires the following three criteria:
  - Speed: the ability to process large volumes of data in a short time and provide immediate feedback.
  - Accuracy: the ability to correctly identify and classify the sentiment in a text, taking into account the context, tone, sarcasm, irony, etc.
  - Scalability: the ability to handle different types of data sources, formats, languages, domains, and topics.
- Real time sentiment analysis can be performed using different approaches, such as rule-based, lexicon-based, or machine learning-based.
  - Rule-based: this approach uses predefined rules and patterns to identify and extract the sentiment in a text, such as keywords, phrases, emoticons, punctuation, etc. For example, the word "love" or the symbol "😊" can indicate a positive sentiment, while the word "hate" or the symbol "😡" can indicate a negative sentiment. This approach is simple and fast, but it can be limited by the complexity and variability of natural language, and it requires manual updates and maintenance.
  - Lexicon-based: this approach uses a dictionary or a corpus of words and phrases that have been annotated with sentiment scores or polarities, such as SentiWordNet, VADER, or LIWC. For example, the word "amazing" can have a positive score of +3, while the word "terrible" can have a negative score of -3. This approach is more flexible and comprehensive than the rule-based approach, but it can be affected by the subjectivity and ambiguity of words and phrases, and it may not capture the context and domain-specific nuances of the text.
  - Machine learning-based: this approach uses algorithms and models that learn from labeled or unlabeled data to identify and extract the sentiment in a text, such as Naive Bayes, Support Vector Machines, or Neural Networks. For example, a model can be trained on a large dataset of movie reviews that have been labeled as positive or negative, and then use the learned features and weights to predict the sentiment of a new review. This approach is more powerful and adaptable than the previous approaches, but it requires a lot of data and computational resources, and it may not be interpretable or explainable.
