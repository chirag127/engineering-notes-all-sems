# Visualization before analysis

- Visualization is the process of creating and displaying graphical representations of data or information.
- Visualization can help to explore, understand, communicate, and present data or information in a meaningful and effective way.
- Visualization can also support the analysis of data or information by revealing patterns, trends, outliers, relationships, distributions, and other insights that may not be obvious or easily detected by numerical or textual methods.
- Visualization before analysis refers to the practice of creating and examining visualizations of data or information before applying any formal or rigorous analytical techniques or methods.
- Visualization before analysis can have several benefits, such as:

  - It can help to gain an overview and a general understanding of the data or information, such as its structure, size, scope, quality, and variability.
  - It can help to identify and formulate relevant and meaningful questions, hypotheses, or problems to be addressed by the analysis.
  - It can help to select and apply appropriate and suitable analytical techniques or methods that match the data or information and the questions, hypotheses, or problems of interest.
  - It can help to verify and validate the results and outcomes of the analysis by comparing and contrasting them with the visualizations.
  - It can help to communicate and present the results and outcomes of the analysis to different audiences and stakeholders by using visualizations that are clear, concise, and compelling.

- Visualization before analysis can be done using various tools, techniques, and methods, such as:

  - Charts, graphs, plots, maps, diagrams, and other common types of visualizations that can show different aspects and dimensions of the data or information, such as quantities, categories, locations, time, and relationships.
  - Dashboards, reports, and interactive visualizations that can provide an overview and a summary of the data or information, as well as allow users to explore and manipulate the data or information by filtering, sorting, zooming, highlighting, and other actions.
  - Exploratory data analysis (EDA) and descriptive statistics that can provide basic and preliminary information and insights about the data or information, such as measures of central tendency, dispersion, shape, and correlation.
  - Data cleaning, preprocessing, and transformation that can improve the quality and usability of the data or information by removing errors, outliers, missing values, duplicates, and other anomalies, as well as by standardizing, normalizing, aggregating, and other operations.
  - Data reduction, sampling, and aggregation that can reduce the size and complexity of the data or information by selecting a subset or a summary of the data or information that is representative and relevant for the analysis.
  - Data integration, fusion, and enrichment that can enhance the value and utility of the data or information by combining, merging, or linking data or information from different sources, formats, or domains, as well as by adding additional or complementary data or information.
