# Discovery

Discovery is the first phase of the data analytics lifecycle, which defines the roadmap of how data is generated, collected, processed, used, and analyzed to achieve business goals. It offers a systematic way to manage data for converting it into information that can be used to fulfill organizational and project goals.

Some of the key activities in the discovery phase are:

- Understanding the business issue: This involves identifying the problem or opportunity that the data analytics project aims to address, defining the objectives and scope of the project, and establishing the success criteria and metrics.
- Understanding the data set: This involves collecting and exploring the available data sources, assessing the quality and relevance of the data, and identifying any data gaps or limitations.
- Preparing the data: This involves cleaning, transforming, integrating, and formatting the data to make it ready for analysis.
- Exploratory analysis: This involves applying descriptive and inferential statistics, data visualization, and hypothesis testing to gain insights from the data, identify patterns and trends, and discover any anomalies or outliers.
- Validation: This involves verifying the accuracy and reliability of the data and the analysis, as well as ensuring that the results align with the business objectives and expectations.

The discovery phase is crucial for setting the foundation for the rest of the data analytics lifecycle, as it helps to define the problem, understand the data, and generate initial insights that can guide the subsequent phases of modeling, deployment, and monitoring.
