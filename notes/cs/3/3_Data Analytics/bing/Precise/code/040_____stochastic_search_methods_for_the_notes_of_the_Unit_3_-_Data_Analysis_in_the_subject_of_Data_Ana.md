### Stochastic Search Methods

Stochastic search methods are a class of optimization algorithms that use randomness to find the global optimum of a problem. These methods are particularly useful when the search space is large, complex, and poorly understood. Some common stochastic search methods include:

1. **Simulated Annealing**: This method is inspired by the annealing process in metallurgy, where a material is heated and then slowly cooled to reduce defects and improve its structure. In simulated annealing, the algorithm starts with a high "temperature" and explores the search space randomly. As the temperature decreases, the algorithm becomes more selective in its exploration, eventually converging to the global optimum.

2. **Genetic Algorithms**: These algorithms are inspired by the process of natural selection and evolution. A population of candidate solutions is evolved over time through the application of genetic operators such as selection, crossover, and mutation. The fittest individuals in the population are more likely to survive and reproduce, leading to the emergence of better solutions over time.

3. **Particle Swarm Optimization**: This method is inspired by the social behavior of bird flocks and fish schools. A population of particles moves through the search space, influenced by their own best position and the best position of their neighbors. Over time, the swarm converges towards the global optimum.

These are just a few examples of stochastic search methods. There are many other methods in this class, each with its own strengths and weaknesses. In general, stochastic search methods are useful for solving complex optimization problems where traditional methods may struggle to find the global optimum.
