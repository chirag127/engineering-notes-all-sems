### Analysis vs Reporting

- **Reporting** is the process of organizing data into informational summaries in order to monitor how different areas of a business are performing.

- **Analysis** is the process of exploring data and reports in order to extract meaningful insights, which can be used to better understand and improve business performance.

- Reporting is focused on providing data in a structured manner, while analysis is focused on understanding the data and drawing conclusions from it.

- Reporting is often done on a regular basis, such as daily, weekly, or monthly, while analysis is done as needed to answer specific questions or solve problems.

- Reporting is often done using pre-defined templates and formats, while analysis may involve more ad-hoc exploration of data.

- Reporting is often done by operational staff, while analysis is often done by more specialized staff such as data analysts or business analysts.

- Reporting is often focused on what has happened, while analysis is focused on understanding why it happened and what can be done to improve performance.

- Reporting is often focused on providing information to others, while analysis is often focused on providing insights to support decision-making.

- Reporting and analysis are complementary processes, with reporting providing the data and information needed for analysis, and analysis providing the insights and recommendations needed to improve performance.
