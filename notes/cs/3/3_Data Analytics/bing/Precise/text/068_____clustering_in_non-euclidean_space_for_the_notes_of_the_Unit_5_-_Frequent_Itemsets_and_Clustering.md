### Clustering in Non-Euclidean Space

Clustering is the process of grouping similar data points together based on their characteristics. In non-Euclidean space, the distance between two points is not calculated using the standard Euclidean distance formula. Instead, other distance measures such as the Manhattan distance or the Minkowski distance can be used.

1. Non-Euclidean space refers to any space that is not flat, where the standard Euclidean geometry does not apply.
2. Clustering in non-Euclidean space can be more challenging than in Euclidean space due to the different distance measures used.
3. Common clustering algorithms such as k-means and hierarchical clustering can be adapted for use in non-Euclidean space by using appropriate distance measures.
4. Clustering in non-Euclidean space can be useful in fields such as image analysis, where the data is not necessarily flat, and the standard Euclidean distance measure may not be appropriate.
