# Visualization before analysis

- Visualization is the process of creating and displaying graphical representations of data or information.
- Visualization can help to explore, understand, communicate, and present data or information in a meaningful and effective way.
- Visualization can also support the analysis of data or information by revealing patterns, trends, outliers, relationships, distributions, and other insights that may not be easily detected by numerical or textual methods.
- Visualization before analysis refers to the practice of creating and using visualizations as a preliminary step or a complementary tool for data or information analysis.
- Visualization before analysis can have several benefits, such as:

  - It can help to identify the characteristics, quality, and limitations of the data or information, such as missing values, outliers, errors, biases, or inconsistencies.
  - It can help to formulate, refine, or test hypotheses or research questions based on the data or information.
  - It can help to select, filter, transform, or aggregate the data or information according to the analysis objectives and needs.
  - It can help to choose, apply, or evaluate appropriate analytical methods or techniques for the data or information.
  - It can help to communicate, explain, or justify the analysis results or findings to different audiences or stakeholders.

- Visualization before analysis can also have some challenges, such as:

  - It can be time-consuming, resource-intensive, or technically demanding to create and use visualizations, especially for large, complex, or heterogeneous data or information.
  - It can be difficult, subjective, or misleading to interpret or infer meaning from visualizations, especially without proper context, background, or domain knowledge.
  - It can be influenced, biased, or manipulated by the choices, assumptions, or preferences of the visualization creators or users, such as the data or information sources, formats, variables, scales, colors, shapes, or layouts.

- Visualization before analysis requires careful consideration, planning, and evaluation of the following aspects:

  - The purpose, scope, and audience of the analysis and the visualization.
  - The data or information sources, formats, types, and structures.
  - The data or information quality, validity, reliability, and relevance.
  - The data or information preprocessing, cleaning, integration, and transformation.
  - The visualization methods, techniques, tools, and platforms.
  - The visualization design, aesthetics, and functionality.
  - The visualization interpretation, communication, and dissemination.
