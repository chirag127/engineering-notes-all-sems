# Learning and Generalisation

- Learning is the process of finding patterns or relationships in data that can be used to make predictions or decisions.
- Generalisation is the ability of a learning model to perform well on new or unseen data that was not used for training.
- Learning and generalisation are closely related, as the goal of learning is to find a model that can generalise well to new situations.
- There are different types of learning, such as supervised learning, unsupervised learning, and reinforcement learning, depending on the availability and nature of the data and the feedback.
- Supervised learning is when the data has labels or outcomes that the model tries to predict or classify, such as regression or classification problems.
- Unsupervised learning is when the data has no labels or outcomes, and the model tries to discover hidden structures or patterns in the data, such as clustering or dimensionality reduction problems.
- Reinforcement learning is when the data is generated by the interaction of an agent with an environment, and the model tries to learn a policy or a strategy that maximises a reward or a goal, such as control or optimisation problems.
- There are different types of generalisation, such as inductive generalisation, deductive generalisation, and abductive generalisation, depending on the logic and reasoning involved in the learning process.
- Inductive generalisation is when the model infers a general rule or a hypothesis from specific observations or examples, such as learning from examples or experience.
- Deductive generalisation is when the model applies a general rule or a hypothesis to specific observations or examples, such as reasoning from principles or theories.
- Abductive generalisation is when the model infers the most plausible or likely explanation for specific observations or examples, such as reasoning from evidence or data.
- There are different factors that affect the learning and generalisation performance of a model, such as the complexity of the model, the size and quality of the data, the noise and uncertainty in the data, the bias and variance of the model, and the regularisation and validation techniques used to prevent overfitting or underfitting.
