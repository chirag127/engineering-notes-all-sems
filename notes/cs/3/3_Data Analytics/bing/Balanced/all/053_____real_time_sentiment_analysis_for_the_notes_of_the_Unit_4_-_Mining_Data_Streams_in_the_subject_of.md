# Real Time Sentiment Analysis

- Real time sentiment analysis is a machine learning technique that automatically recognizes and extracts the sentiment in a text whenever it occurs .
- Sentiment is the subjective attitude or emotion expressed by a person or a group of people towards a topic, such as a brand, a product, a service, or an event.
- Sentiment analysis can be used to analyze live social and online feeds, such as comments, posts, reviews, tweets, etc., and derive insights from them  .
- Some applications of real time sentiment analysis are:
  - Customer feedback analysis: to understand how customers feel about a product or service, and identify areas of improvement or satisfaction.
  - Brand reputation monitoring: to track how the public perceives a brand, and detect any positive or negative trends or events that affect its image .
  - Market research and analysis: to gauge the sentiment of potential or existing customers towards a new product, feature, or campaign, and measure its impact and effectiveness .
  - Social media marketing: to optimize the content and tone of social media posts, and engage with the target audience in a more personalized and relevant way.
- The process of real time sentiment analysis follows these four steps:
  1. Breaking down the text into components: sentences, phrases, tokens, and parts of speech.
  2. Identifying each phrase and component.
  3. Assigning a sentiment score to each phrase with plus or minus points.
  4. Combining scores for a final sentiment analysis.
- There are different methods and models for performing real time sentiment analysis, such as rule-based, lexicon-based, or machine learning-based approaches.
- Some challenges and limitations of real time sentiment analysis are:
  - Handling noisy and unstructured data, such as spelling errors, slang, abbreviations, emojis, etc..
  - Detecting sarcasm, irony, humor, and other forms of figurative language that can alter the meaning and sentiment of a text.
  - Dealing with context-dependent and domain-specific sentiment, such as cultural differences, personal preferences, or technical terms.
  - Balancing speed and accuracy, as real time sentiment analysis requires fast and reliable results, but also complex and robust models.
