### Analysis vs Reporting

- Reporting and analysis are two different processes that involve data and are essential for data-driven decision making.
- Reporting is the process of organizing data into summaries, tables, charts, or dashboards to monitor how different areas of a business are performing. Reporting answers questions like "What happened?" or "How much?" or "How often?" 
- Analysis is the process of exploring data and reports to extract meaningful insights, which can be used to better understand and improve business performance. Analysis answers questions like "Why did it happen?" or "What will happen next?" or "What should we do?" 
- Some of the key differences between reporting and analysis are  :
  - Required skills: Data analysis requires more advanced skills than data reporting, such as knowledge of different analytical models, statistical techniques, and business problems. Data reporting requires skills in data organization, visualization, and communication.
  - Order of operations: Data reporting must happen before data analysis, as it provides the raw material for analysis. Data analysis can happen after data reporting, or in some cases, during data reporting, as it provides the context and recommendations for reporting.
  - Time frame: Data reporting is usually done on a regular or scheduled basis, such as daily, weekly, or monthly. Data analysis is usually done on an ad hoc or as-needed basis, such as when a problem arises or a new opportunity emerges.
  - Purpose: Data reporting is mainly used to inform and educate stakeholders about the current state of the business or a specific area. Data analysis is mainly used to influence and persuade stakeholders to take action or make changes based on the insights derived from the data.
  - Outcome: Data reporting produces information, which is a collection of facts or data points. Data analysis produces knowledge, which is a deeper understanding of the patterns, trends, or relationships in the data.
