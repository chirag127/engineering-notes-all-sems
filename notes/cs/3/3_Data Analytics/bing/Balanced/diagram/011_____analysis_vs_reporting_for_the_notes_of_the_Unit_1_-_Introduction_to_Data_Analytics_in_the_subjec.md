### Analysis vs Reporting

- Reporting is the process of presenting data in a structured and summarized way, such as tables, charts, or dashboards.
- Analysis is the process of exploring, interpreting, and explaining data, such as finding patterns, trends, outliers, or causal relationships.
- Reporting answers the question of **what** is happening with the data, while analysis answers the question of **why** it is happening and **what** can be done about it.
- Reporting is often based on predefined metrics and dimensions, such as sales, revenue, or customer segments, while analysis is more flexible and creative, using various techniques and tools, such as statistical methods, machine learning, or data visualization.
- Reporting is usually done periodically and regularly, such as daily, weekly, or monthly, while analysis is done on demand and as needed, such as when a problem arises or a decision needs to be made.
- Reporting is mainly for informing and monitoring, while analysis is mainly for discovering and recommending.
