### Visualization before analysis

- Visualization is the process of creating and displaying graphical representations of data or information.
- Visualization can help to explore, understand, communicate, and present data or information in a meaningful and effective way.
- Visualization can also support the analysis of data or information by revealing patterns, trends, outliers, relationships, distributions, and other insights that may not be easily detected by numerical or textual methods.
- Visualization before analysis is the practice of creating and examining visualizations of data or information before applying any analytical techniques or methods.
- Visualization before analysis can have several benefits, such as:

  - It can help to identify the characteristics, quality, and structure of the data or information, such as the type, range, scale, dimensionality, granularity, completeness, accuracy, and consistency of the data or information.
  - It can help to formulate, refine, or validate the analytical questions, hypotheses, or goals, such as the purpose, scope, context, and audience of the analysis.
  - It can help to select, design, or evaluate the appropriate analytical techniques or methods, such as the type, level, and complexity of the analysis, the assumptions, limitations, and validity of the techniques or methods, and the expected or desired outcomes or results of the analysis.
  - It can help to explore, compare, or contrast different analytical scenarios, perspectives, or alternatives, such as the impact, sensitivity, or uncertainty of the analysis, the trade-offs, risks, or opportunities of the analysis, and the implications, recommendations, or actions based on the analysis.

- Visualization before analysis can be done using various tools, techniques, or methods, such as:

  - Descriptive statistics, such as measures of central tendency, dispersion, or distribution, that summarize or describe the data or information using numerical values or indicators.
  - Exploratory data analysis (EDA), such as histograms, box plots, scatter plots, or correlation matrices, that visualize the data or information using simple or basic graphical elements or features, such as points, lines, bars, or boxes, to reveal the main characteristics or properties of the data or information.
  - Data mining, such as clustering, classification, or association rules, that apply computational or mathematical algorithms or models to the data or information to discover hidden or unknown patterns, groups, or relationships in the data or information.
  - Data visualization, such as charts, graphs, maps, or dashboards, that use advanced or interactive graphical elements or features, such as colors, shapes, sizes, icons, labels, or animations, to represent the data or information in a more expressive, engaging, or intuitive way.
