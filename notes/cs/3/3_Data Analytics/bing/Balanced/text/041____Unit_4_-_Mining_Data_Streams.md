## Unit 4 - Mining Data Streams

- Data streams are continuous, rapid, and potentially infinite sequences of data that can be read only once or a small number of times using limited computing and storage capabilities .
- Data streams are often generated by sensors, web logs, social media, financial transactions, and other real-time applications that require timely and accurate analysis .
- Data streams are time-sensitive, and each data element carries a timestamp that indicates when it was generated or observed.
- Data stream mining is the process of extracting knowledge structures from data streams, such as patterns, trends, outliers, clusters, and classifiers .
- Data stream mining poses several challenges, such as:
  - High volume and velocity of data that requires efficient and scalable algorithms .
  - Concept drift, which is the change in the underlying distribution of the data over time, that requires adaptive and robust algorithms .
  - Limited memory and processing resources that require online and incremental algorithms that can update the knowledge structures without accessing the entire data history .
  - Evolving data characteristics that require flexible and dynamic algorithms that can handle different types of data and tasks .
- Data stream mining techniques can be classified into two categories: supervised and unsupervised .
  - Supervised techniques use labeled data to learn a model that can predict the label of new data, such as classification and regression .
  - Unsupervised techniques do not use labeled data, but rather discover the intrinsic structure of the data, such as clustering and frequent pattern mining .
- Some examples of data stream mining algorithms are:
  - Classification: Hoeffding tree, VFDT, CVFDT, ADWIN, MOA .
  - Regression: FIMT-DD, AMRules, MOA .
  - Clustering: STREAM, CluStream, DenStream, D-Stream, MOA .
  - Frequent pattern mining: Lossy Counting, Sticky Sampling, Space Saving, FP-Stream, MOA .
- Some applications of data stream mining are:
  - Anomaly detection: identifying unusual or suspicious events or behaviors in data streams, such as network intrusion, fraud detection, and sensor fault detection .
  - Recommendation systems: providing personalized suggestions to users based on their preferences and behaviors in data streams, such as e-commerce, online advertising, and social media .
  - Sentiment analysis: extracting the opinions, emotions, and attitudes of users from text data streams, such as product reviews, tweets, and blogs .
  - Predictive maintenance: monitoring the performance and health of machines and systems from sensor data streams, and predicting failures and faults before they occur .
