### Libra

Libra is a blockchain-based digital currency developed by Facebook. It was first introduced in 2019 and is designed to be a stablecoin, meaning that its value is pegged to a basket of stable assets such as the US dollar, euro, and yen. Here are some key points to know about Libra:

- Libra is a permissioned blockchain, meaning that only authorized entities can participate in the network.
- The Libra Association, a consortium of companies including Facebook, oversees the development and management of the currency.
- Transactions on the Libra network are recorded on a distributed ledger, which is maintained by the nodes in the network.
- Libra is designed to be fast and scalable, with the goal of processing thousands of transactions per second.
- The currency is backed by a reserve of assets, including bank deposits and short-term government securities.
- Libra is expected to be used for online purchases and peer-to-peer transactions, as well as for remittances and other financial services.
- The development of Libra has faced regulatory scrutiny and criticism from some governments and financial institutions, who are concerned about issues such as money laundering, financial stability, and privacy.
- Facebook has stated that it plans to launch Libra in 2021, but the project has faced delays and uncertainty due to regulatory challenges and the departure of several key partners from the Libra Association.

Overall, Libra represents a significant development in the world of digital currencies, with the potential to transform online commerce and financial services. However, the project faces significant challenges and uncertainties as it seeks to navigate regulatory hurdles and build a robust and secure platform for its users.
