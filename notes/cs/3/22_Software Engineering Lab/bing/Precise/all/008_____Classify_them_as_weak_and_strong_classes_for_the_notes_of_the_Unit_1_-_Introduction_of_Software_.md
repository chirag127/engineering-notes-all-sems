### Classify them as weak and strong classes

In the context of object-oriented programming, classes can be classified as weak or strong based on their level of cohesion and coupling.

- **Strong classes** have high cohesion and low coupling. This means that the methods and data within the class are closely related and work together to achieve a single, well-defined purpose. The class is also loosely coupled with other classes, meaning that it has minimal dependencies on other classes and can be easily reused or modified without affecting other parts of the system.

- **Weak classes**, on the other hand, have low cohesion and high coupling. This means that the methods and data within the class are not closely related and may not work together to achieve a single, well-defined purpose. The class is also tightly coupled with other classes, meaning that it has many dependencies on other classes and changes to the class may have a significant impact on other parts of the system.

In general, strong classes are preferred in software design as they promote modularity, reusability, and maintainability. Weak classes, on the other hand, can make the system more difficult to understand, modify, and maintain.
