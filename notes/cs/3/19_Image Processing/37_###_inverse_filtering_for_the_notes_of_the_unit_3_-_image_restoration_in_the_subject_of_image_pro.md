### Inverse Filtering for the notes of the Unit 3 - IMAGE RESTORATION in the subject of Image Processing

Inverse filtering is a technique used in image processing to restore a degraded image by inverting the degradation process. The following are some of the key concepts related to inverse filtering:

1. Degradation Model: Inverse filtering is based on a mathematical model of the degradation process, which is used to describe the relationship between the original image and the degraded image. This model can be used to estimate the degradation function, which is the transfer function that describes the degradation process.

2. Inverse Filter: The inverse filter is a filter that is used to invert the degradation process by removing the effects of the degradation function. The inverse filter is obtained by taking the inverse of the degradation function in the frequency domain.

3. Image Restoration: Inverse filtering is used to restore a degraded image by applying the inverse filter to the degraded image in the frequency domain. The restored image is obtained by transforming the filtered image back into the spatial domain.

4. Limitations: Inverse filtering can be limited by the presence of noise and other degradation factors, such as blurring or aliasing. Inverse filtering can amplify the noise and other degradation factors, leading to a degraded restored image.

5. Regularization: Regularization is a technique used to overcome the limitations of inverse filtering by adding a constraint to the inverse filter to prevent the amplification of noise and other degradation factors. Regularization can be performed using techniques such as Tikhonov regularization, ridge regression, and more.

In this unit, you will learn about inverse filtering, including degradation model, inverse filter, image restoration, limitations, and regularization.

This unit will provide a foundation for understanding the principles and practices of inverse filtering, and for exploring the various concepts and techniques used in the field of image processing.
