### Relationships between pixels

- A pixel is the smallest unit of a digital image that can be displayed or manipulated.
- Pixels have coordinates that indicate their position in the image, such as (x,y) for a two-dimensional image.
- Pixels also have values that represent their intensity or color, such as gray levels or RGB components.
- Pixels can have different types of relationships with each other, such as adjacency, connectivity, distance, and similarity.
- Adjacency is the property of being next to each other in a certain way, such as horizontally, vertically, or diagonally.
- Connectivity is the property of being linked by a path of adjacent pixels that share a common property, such as value or region.
- Distance is the measure of how far apart two pixels are, such as Euclidean, city-block, or chessboard distance.
- Similarity is the measure of how alike two pixels are, such as having the same or similar values or belonging to the same or similar regions.

- These relationships are important for defining the boundaries of objects and regions in an image, and for performing various operations on the image, such as segmentation, filtering, enhancement, and analysis.
