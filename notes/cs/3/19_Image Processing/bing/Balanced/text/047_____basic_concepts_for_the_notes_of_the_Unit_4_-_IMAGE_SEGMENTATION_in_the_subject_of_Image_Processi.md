### Basic Concepts for the Notes of the Unit 4 - Image Segmentation in the Subject of Image Processing

- Image segmentation is the process of dividing an image into meaningful regions or segments based on some criteria, such as color, texture, intensity, shape, etc.  
- Image segmentation is useful for many applications, such as medical image analysis, autonomous driving, face recognition, video surveillance, and satellite image analysis.   
- Image segmentation can be performed at different levels of granularity, such as pixel-level, region-level, or object-level. Pixel-level segmentation assigns a label to every pixel in an image, such as foreground or background, or a specific object class or instance. Region-level segmentation groups pixels into larger regions based on some similarity or homogeneity measure. Object-level segmentation detects and locates individual objects within an image and draws a boundary around them.   
- Image segmentation can be categorized into two main types: supervised and unsupervised. Supervised segmentation uses a set of labeled images as training data to learn a model that can segment new images. Unsupervised segmentation does not use any prior information or labels, but relies on the intrinsic properties of the image data to find natural clusters or groups of pixels.  
- Image segmentation can be achieved by various techniques, such as thresholding, clustering, edge detection, region growing, region splitting and merging, watershed, graph-based methods, active contours, level sets, and deep learning. Each technique has its own advantages and disadvantages, and the choice of the best technique depends on the characteristics of the image and the application domain.
