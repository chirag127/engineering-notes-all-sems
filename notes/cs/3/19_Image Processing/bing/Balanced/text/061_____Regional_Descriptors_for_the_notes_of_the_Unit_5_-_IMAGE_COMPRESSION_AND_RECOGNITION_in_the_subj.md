### Regional Descriptors

- Regional descriptors are numerical or symbolic attributes that describe the properties of a segmented region in an image .
- Regional descriptors can be classified into two categories: external and internal .
- External descriptors are based on the shape characteristics of the region boundary, such as perimeter, compactness, orientation, eccentricity, etc  .
- Internal descriptors are based on the reflectivity properties of the region, such as color, texture, mean value, standard deviation, etc .
- Regional descriptors can be used for various purposes, such as object recognition, classification, matching, retrieval, etc .
- Some examples of regional descriptors are:

  - Area: the number of pixels that belong to the region .
  - Perimeter: the length of the region boundary .
  - Compactness: the ratio of the area to the perimeter squared, which measures how close the region is to a circle .
  - Mean value: the average intensity or color value of the region .
  - Standard deviation: the measure of the variation of the intensity or color values in the region.
  - Orientation: the angle of the major axis of the region with respect to a reference axis .
  - Eccentricity: the ratio of the minor axis to the major axis of the region, which measures how elongated the region is .
  - Moments: the weighted averages of the pixel coordinates or values in the region, which can be used to derive other shape descriptors .
