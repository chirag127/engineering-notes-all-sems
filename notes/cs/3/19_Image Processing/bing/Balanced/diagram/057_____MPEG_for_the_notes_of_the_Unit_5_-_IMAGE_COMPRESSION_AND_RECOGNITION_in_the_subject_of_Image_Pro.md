### MPEG

MPEG stands for Moving Picture Experts Group, which is a group of experts that develops standards for digital video and audio compression. MPEG standards aim to achieve high compression ratios by exploiting the redundancy and irrelevancy in the data. MPEG standards are widely used for various applications such as digital television, streaming media, video conferencing, and DVD.

Some of the main features of MPEG standards are:

- They use both lossy and lossless compression techniques to reduce the data size.
- They use a hybrid of spatial and temporal compression, which means they compress the data both within a frame and across frames.
- They use a technique called Discrete Cosine Transform (DCT) to transform the data into frequency domain, where most of the energy is concentrated in low frequencies.
- They use a technique called quantization to reduce the number of bits needed to represent the data, by discarding the less significant bits.
- They use a technique called entropy coding to further compress the data, by assigning shorter codes to more frequent symbols.
- They use a technique called motion estimation and compensation to exploit the similarity between successive frames, by encoding only the difference between them.

Some of the main MPEG standards and their applications are:

- MPEG-1: This is the first MPEG standard, developed in 1993. It is designed for digital storage media at up to 1.5 Mbps. It supports video resolution of up to 352x288 pixels and audio sampling rate of up to 48 kHz. It is mainly used for Video CD (VCD) and MP3 audio format.
- MPEG-2: This is the second MPEG standard, developed in 1995. It is designed for digital broadcasting and DVD. It supports video resolution of up to 1920x1080 pixels and audio sampling rate of up to 96 kHz. It is mainly used for Digital Video Broadcasting (DVB), High Definition Television (HDTV), and DVD-Video.
- MPEG-4: This is the third MPEG standard, developed in 1999. It is designed for multimedia applications and interactive services. It supports video resolution of up to 4096x2160 pixels and audio sampling rate of up to 192 kHz. It is mainly used for streaming media, video conferencing, and mobile devices. It also supports advanced features such as object-based coding, scalability, and error resilience.
- MPEG-7: This is the fourth MPEG standard, developed in 2001. It is designed for multimedia content description and retrieval. It defines a set of metadata and descriptors that can be used to describe the features, structure, and semantics of multimedia content. It is mainly used for multimedia databases, search engines, and recommender systems.
- MPEG-21: This is the fifth MPEG standard, developed in 2003. It is designed for multimedia framework and delivery. It defines a set of components and protocols that can be used to create, manage, and distribute multimedia content. It is mainly used for digital rights management, multimedia adaptation, and quality of service.
