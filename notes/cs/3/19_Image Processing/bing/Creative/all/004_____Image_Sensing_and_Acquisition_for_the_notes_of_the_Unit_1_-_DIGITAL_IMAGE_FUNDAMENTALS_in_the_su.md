# Image Sensing and Acquisition

- Image sensing and acquisition are used for processing the analog images of physical scenes or the interior structure of an object, and converting it into digital .
- Image sensing is a process to detect or sense the information that constitutes an image.
- Image acquisition includes processing, compression, and finally storing of image into digital form.
- The types of images in which we are interested are generated by the combination of an illumination source and the reflection or absorption of energy from that source by the elements of the scene being imaged.
- The image sensing and acquisition process can be divided into three main steps:
  - Image formation: The interaction of the illumination source and the scene elements produces an optical image that can be captured by a sensor.
  - Image sampling and digitization: The sensor converts the optical image into an electrical signal that can be sampled and quantized to obtain a digital image.
  - Image storage and display: The digital image can be stored in a memory device or displayed on a screen.

- Some of the factors that affect the image sensing and acquisition process are:
  - The type and quality of the illumination source
  - The characteristics and geometry of the scene elements
  - The properties and configuration of the sensor
  - The sampling and quantization methods and parameters
  - The image processing and compression techniques and algorithms
  - The image storage and display devices and formats

- Image sensing and acquisition are essential for many applications such as:
  - Medical imaging: To diagnose and treat diseases using modalities such as X-ray, MRI, ultrasound, etc.
  - Remote sensing: To monitor and analyze the earth and its resources using satellites, drones, etc.
  - Industrial inspection: To detect and measure defects, flaws, or anomalies in products or processes using cameras, scanners, etc.
  - Security and surveillance: To identify and track people, objects, or events using face recognition, biometrics, etc.
  - Entertainment and education: To create and enhance visual effects, animations, games, etc. using computer graphics, virtual reality, etc.
