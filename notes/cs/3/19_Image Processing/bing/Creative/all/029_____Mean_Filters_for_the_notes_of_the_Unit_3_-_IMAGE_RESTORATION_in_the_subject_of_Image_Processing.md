# Mean Filters for the notes of the Unit 3 - IMAGE RESTORATION in the subject of Image Processing

- Mean filters are a type of spatial filters that are used to smooth images by reducing the amount of intensity variation between neighboring pixels .
- Mean filters work by moving through the image pixel by pixel, replacing each value with the average value of neighboring pixels, including itself .
- Mean filters can be implemented using a kernel or a mask, which is a small matrix that defines the size and shape of the neighborhood to be averaged.
- Mean filters can be classified into different types, such as arithmetic mean, geometric mean, harmonic mean, and contraharmonic mean, depending on the way the average is calculated.
- Mean filters can effectively reduce noise in images, especially random or Gaussian noise, by smoothing out the fluctuations in pixel values .
- Mean filters can also blur the edges and details of the images, resulting in a loss of sharpness and contrast .
- Mean filters can be modified to preserve the edges and details of the images, such as by using a weighted average, a median filter, or a bilateral filter .
- Mean filters are one of the simplest and most intuitive methods of image restoration, but they may not be sufficient for more complex or non-linear noise models.
