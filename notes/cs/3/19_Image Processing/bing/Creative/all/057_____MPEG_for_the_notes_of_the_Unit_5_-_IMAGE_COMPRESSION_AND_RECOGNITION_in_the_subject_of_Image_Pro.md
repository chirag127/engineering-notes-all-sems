# MPEG

MPEG stands for Moving Picture Experts Group, which is a group of experts that develops standards for digital video and audio compression. MPEG standards aim to achieve high compression ratios by exploiting the redundancy and irrelevancy in the data  .

Some of the main features of MPEG standards are:

- They use both lossy and lossless compression techniques to reduce the size of the data .
- They store only the changes from one frame to another, instead of each entire frame, which reduces the temporal redundancy .
- They encode the video information using a technique called Discrete Cosine Transform (DCT), which transforms the spatial domain into the frequency domain and allows to discard the high-frequency components that are less perceptible to the human eye .
- They use a technique called chroma subsampling, which reduces the amount of color information and keeps the brightness information, which exploits the human visual system's sensitivity to luminance more than chrominance.
- They support different levels of quality and resolution, depending on the application and the available bandwidth.

Some of the primary early MPEG compression formats and related standards include:

- MPEG-1 (1993): Coding of moving pictures and associated audio for digital storage media at up to about 1.5 Mbit/s (ISO/IEC 11172). This initial version is known as a lossy file format and is the first MPEG compression standard for audio and video. It is widely used for Video CD and MP3 formats.
- MPEG-2 (1995): Generic coding of moving pictures and associated audio information (ISO/IEC 13818). This version is an extension of MPEG-1 that supports higher quality and resolution, interlaced video, and additional audio formats. It is widely used for Digital TV, DVD, and Blu-ray formats.
- MPEG-4 (1999): Coding of audio-visual objects (ISO/IEC 14496). This version is a further extension of MPEG-2 that supports object-based coding, scene description, and content-based interactivity. It also introduces new compression techniques such as motion estimation and compensation, and advanced audio coding. It is widely used for Internet streaming, mobile devices, and multimedia applications .
