### MPEG

MPEG stands for Moving Picture Experts Group, which is a group of experts that develops standards for digital video and audio compression. MPEG standards aim to achieve high compression ratios by exploiting the redundancy and irrelevancy in the data .

Some of the main features of MPEG standards are:

- They use both intra-frame and inter-frame compression techniques. Intra-frame compression reduces the redundancy within a single frame, while inter-frame compression reduces the redundancy between successive frames.
- They use a technique called Discrete Cosine Transform (DCT) to transform the spatial domain data into the frequency domain data, which allows for more efficient quantization and encoding .
- They use a technique called chroma subsampling to reduce the amount of color information, since the human eye is more sensitive to brightness than to color.
- They use a technique called motion estimation and compensation to predict the motion of the objects in the video and encode only the difference between the predicted and the actual frames.
- They use a technique called variable length coding (VLC) to assign shorter codes to more frequent symbols and longer codes to less frequent symbols, which reduces the average code length.

Some of the main MPEG standards and their applications are:

- MPEG-1 (1993): Coding of moving pictures and associated audio for digital storage media at up to about 1.5 Mbit/s. It is used for video CDs, MP3 audio, and low-quality streaming video.
- MPEG-2 (1995): Coding of moving pictures and associated audio for broadcast-quality television. It is used for digital TV, DVD, and high-quality streaming video.
- MPEG-4 (1998): Coding of multimedia content for interactive applications. It supports object-based coding, scalability, error resilience, and advanced audio coding. It is used for web video, mobile video, and digital rights management.
- MPEG-7 (2001): Description and search of multimedia content. It defines a set of metadata and descriptors for multimedia content, such as color, shape, motion, and genre.
- MPEG-21 (2003): Multimedia framework. It defines a set of standards and technologies for the creation, delivery, and consumption of multimedia content, such as digital item declaration, identification, adaptation, and rights expression.
