## Unit 5 - IMAGE COMPRESSION AND RECOGNITION

- Image compression is the process of reducing the size of an image file without significantly affecting its quality or appearance.
- Image recognition is the process of identifying and classifying objects, faces, scenes, or text in an image using machine learning algorithms.
- Image compression and recognition are important applications of computer vision and artificial intelligence that have many uses in various domains such as security, entertainment, education, and medicine.

### Image Compression

- Image compression can be classified into two types: lossless and lossy.
- Lossless compression preserves the exact information of the original image and allows perfect reconstruction of the image after decompression. Examples of lossless compression algorithms are Run-Length Encoding (RLE), Huffman Coding, and Lempel-Ziv-Welch (LZW).
- Lossy compression discards some information of the original image and introduces some distortion or noise in the image after decompression. However, lossy compression can achieve higher compression ratios than lossless compression. Examples of lossy compression algorithms are JPEG, MPEG, and Wavelet Transform.
- The quality of a compressed image can be measured by various metrics such as Peak Signal-to-Noise Ratio (PSNR), Structural Similarity Index (SSIM), and Mean Squared Error (MSE).

### Image Recognition

- Image recognition can be performed by various methods such as template matching, feature extraction, and deep learning.
- Template matching is a simple method that compares an input image with a set of predefined templates and finds the best match based on some similarity measure such as correlation or Euclidean distance.
- Feature extraction is a more advanced method that extracts some meaningful and invariant features from an input image such as edges, corners, keypoints, or descriptors and uses them to compare with a database of features from different classes of images. Examples of feature extraction algorithms are Scale-Invariant Feature Transform (SIFT), Speeded Up Robust Features (SURF), and Histogram of Oriented Gradients (HOG).
- Deep learning is a state-of-the-art method that uses artificial neural networks to learn complex and hierarchical features from large amounts of labeled or unlabeled images and uses them to perform various tasks such as classification, detection, segmentation, or generation. Examples of deep learning architectures are Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs), Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs), and Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs).
