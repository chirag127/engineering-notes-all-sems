### Image Sampling and Quantization

In the field of image processing, digital images are created through a process called image sampling and quantization. This process involves the conversion of continuous analog signals into digital signals, which can be processed by a computer.

Here are some key points to understand about image sampling and quantization:

- Image sampling is the process of converting a continuous analog signal into a digital signal by taking samples of the signal at regular intervals. In the case of images, this means dividing the image into a grid of pixels and measuring the signal at each pixel.
- The sampling rate, or number of samples taken per unit of time, determines the resolution of the resulting digital image. A higher sampling rate results in a higher resolution image with more detail.
- Quantization is the process of assigning numerical values to the sampled signal. In the case of digital images, this means assigning a numerical value, or intensity, to each pixel in the image.
- The number of possible intensity values depends on the number of bits used to represent each pixel. For example, an 8-bit image can have 256 possible intensity values, while a 16-bit image can have 65,536 possible intensity values.
- The combination of sampling and quantization allows for the creation of digital images that can be manipulated and processed using computer algorithms.

Understanding image sampling and quantization is essential for anyone working in the field of image processing. By mastering these concepts, you can create high-quality digital images and develop algorithms that can analyze and manipulate them in a variety of ways.
