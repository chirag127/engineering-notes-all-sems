### Notch Filters

Notch filters are frequency domain filters that are used to remove specific frequencies from an image. They are commonly used in image restoration applications. Below are some key points to understand about notch filters in the context of image processing:

- Notch filters are used to remove specific frequencies from an image.
- They are designed to remove narrow bands of frequencies, hence the name "notch" filters.
- Notch filters are commonly used in image restoration applications, where they can be used to remove specific artifacts or noise from an image.
- A notch filter can be designed by placing a notch (i.e., a zero) in the frequency response of a filter at the frequency to be removed.
- Notch filters can be implemented in the frequency domain using Fourier transforms, or in the spatial domain using convolution.
- In the frequency domain, a notch filter can be designed by modifying the frequency response of a low-pass, high-pass, or band-pass filter.
- The frequency response of a notch filter consists of a deep trough at the specific frequency to be removed, surrounded by peaks that allow other frequencies to pass through.
- Notch filters can be used in combination with other filters, such as high-pass or low-pass filters, to achieve a desired result.
- The effectiveness of a notch filter depends on the specific frequency to be removed and the surrounding frequencies.
- Notch filters are commonly used in applications such as medical imaging, where they can be used to remove artifacts caused by medical equipment or other sources of interference.
