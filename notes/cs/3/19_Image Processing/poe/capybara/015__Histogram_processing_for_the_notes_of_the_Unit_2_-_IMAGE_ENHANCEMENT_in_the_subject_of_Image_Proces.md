### Histogram Processing for the Notes of Unit 2 - Image Enhancement in the Subject of Image Processing

Histogram processing is a technique used to manipulate the brightness and contrast of an image. Here are some important points to understand about histogram processing in the context of image enhancement:

- A histogram is a graphical representation of the intensity values of an image. It shows how many pixels in the image have a particular intensity value.
- Histogram equalization is a technique used to improve the contrast of an image by redistributing the intensity values. It works by mapping the original intensity values to new ones such that the resulting histogram is as uniform as possible.
- Histogram stretching is another technique used to improve the contrast of an image. It works by stretching the intensity values of an image to cover the entire range of possible values.
- Adaptive histogram equalization is a technique used to enhance the contrast of an image in regions with varying illumination levels. It works by dividing the image into small regions and applying histogram equalization to each region separately.
- Contrast Limited Adaptive Histogram Equalization (CLAHE) is a modified version of adaptive histogram equalization that limits the amplification of noise in the image.
- Histogram matching is a technique used to adjust the intensity values of an image to match those of a reference image. It is useful in situations where the lighting conditions of two images differ significantly.

In conclusion, histogram processing is an important technique used for image enhancement. Understanding the different methods of histogram processing can help in improving the quality of images and making them more visually appealing.
