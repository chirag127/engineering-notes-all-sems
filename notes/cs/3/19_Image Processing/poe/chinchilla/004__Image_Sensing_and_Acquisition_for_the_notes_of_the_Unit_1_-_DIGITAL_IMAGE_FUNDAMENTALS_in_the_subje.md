### Image Sensing and Acquisition

Digital images are formed by capturing light energy and converting it into digital signals using sensors. This process is known as image sensing and acquisition. In this section, we will discuss the different aspects of image sensing and acquisition.

#### Image Sensing

Image sensing is the process of capturing light energy and converting it into an electrical signal. There are two types of sensors used for image sensing: charge-coupled devices (CCD) and complementary metal-oxide-semiconductor (CMOS) sensors.

- CCD Sensors: CCD sensors are used in digital cameras and are known for their high-quality images. They are expensive and require a lot of power to operate. CCD sensors use a series of capacitors to capture and store the light energy.
- CMOS Sensors: CMOS sensors are used in smartphones and other portable devices. They are cheaper and require less power to operate. CMOS sensors use photodiodes to capture and convert the light energy into an electrical signal.

#### Image Acquisition

Image acquisition is the process of capturing an image and converting it into a digital form. There are two types of image acquisition: single-shot and scanning.

- Single-shot Image Acquisition: In single-shot image acquisition, the entire image is captured at once. This method is commonly used in digital cameras and other devices that require high-quality images.
- Scanning Image Acquisition: In scanning image acquisition, the image is captured one line at a time. This method is commonly used in flatbed scanners and other devices that require high-resolution images.

#### Image Resolution

Image resolution refers to the amount of detail that can be captured in an image. It is measured in pixels per inch (ppi) or dots per inch (dpi). The higher the resolution, the more detail can be captured in an image.

#### Image Compression

Image compression is the process of reducing the file size of an image. There are two types of image compression: lossy and lossless.

- Lossy Compression: In lossy compression, some of the image data is lost in the compression process. This results in a smaller file size but also a lower quality image.
- Lossless Compression: In lossless compression, none of the image data is lost in the compression process. This results in a smaller file size without a loss in image quality.

#### Conclusion

Image sensing and acquisition are important aspects of digital image processing. Understanding these concepts is essential for capturing high-quality images and processing them for various applications.
