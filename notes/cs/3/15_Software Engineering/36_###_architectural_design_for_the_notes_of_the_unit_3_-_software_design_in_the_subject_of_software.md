### Architectural Design for the notes of the Unit 3 - Software Design in the subject of Software Engineering

Architectural Design is the process of defining the overall structure and organization of a software system. The following are some of the key concepts related to architectural design:

1. Abstraction: Abstraction is the process of defining the high-level structure of a software system, including the modules, components, and interfaces.

2. Modularity: Modularity is the process of breaking down a software system into smaller, independent components. This makes it easier to understand, develop, and maintain the software system.

3. Coupling and Cohesion: Coupling refers to the degree to which the components of a software system are dependent on each other, while cohesion refers to the degree to which the components of a software system work together to achieve a common goal.

4. Scalability: Scalability refers to the ability of a software system to handle increasing amounts of data and users without sacrificing performance.

5. Reusability: Reusability refers to the ability of a software system to be used in different contexts and for different purposes.

6. Maintainability: Maintainability refers to the ease with which a software system can be modified and updated over time.

In this unit, you will learn about architectural design and the key concepts related to it, including abstraction, modularity, coupling and cohesion, scalability, reusability, and maintainability.

This unit will provide a foundation for understanding the principles and practices of software design, and for exploring the various concepts and techniques used in the field of Software Engineering.
