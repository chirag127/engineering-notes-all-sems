### SEI-CMM Model

The Software Engineering Institute (SEI) Capability Maturity Model (CMM) is a model for assessing and improving software development processes. The model is based on the premise that the quality of a software system is largely determined by the quality of the process used to develop and maintain it.

The SEI-CMM model consists of five levels of maturity, each representing a different level of process capability:

1. **Level 1 - Initial:** At this level, the software development process is ad hoc and unstructured. There is no formal process in place, and success depends largely on the individual efforts of the development team.

2. **Level 2 - Repeatable:** At this level, basic project management processes are in place, and the organization has established a disciplined process for managing requirements, planning, and tracking software development.

3. **Level 3 - Defined:** At this level, the organization has a well-defined and documented standard software development process, which is followed consistently across the organization.

4. **Level 4 - Managed:** At this level, the organization uses quantitative techniques to measure and control the quality of its software development process.

5. **Level 5 - Optimizing:** At this level, the organization continuously improves its software development process, using quantitative feedback to identify and address areas for improvement.

The SEI-CMM model provides a framework for assessing the maturity of an organization's software development process and for identifying areas for improvement. By following the model, organizations can improve the quality of their software development process and, ultimately, the quality of the software systems they produce.
