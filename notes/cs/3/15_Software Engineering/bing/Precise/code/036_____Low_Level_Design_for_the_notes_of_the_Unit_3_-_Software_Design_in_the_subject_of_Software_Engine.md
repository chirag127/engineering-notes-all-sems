### Low Level Design for the notes of the Unit 3 - Software Design in the subject of Software Engineering

Low-level design (LLD) is a component-level design process that follows a step-by-step refinement process. This process can be viewed as an iterative process that starts with the high-level design (HLD) and ends with the detailed design of the system components.

1. Low-level design is concerned with the design of individual modules and their interfaces.
2. It involves the detailed design of data structures, algorithms, and interfaces between modules.
3. The goal of low-level design is to translate the high-level design into a detailed design that can be implemented by the development team.
4. Low-level design is also known as detailed design or module design.
5. It is important to ensure that the low-level design is consistent with the high-level design and that it meets the requirements of the system.
6. Low-level design is typically performed by the development team and is reviewed by the architecture team to ensure consistency with the high-level design.
7. The output of the low-level design process is a detailed design document that describes the design of each module and its interfaces.
