### Change Control Process

Change control is a formal process used to ensure that changes to a product or system are introduced in a controlled and coordinated manner. It reduces the possibility that unnecessary changes will be introduced to a system without forethought, introducing faults into the system or undoing changes made by other users of software.

The change control process is typically described in the following steps:

1. **Request for change:** A change request is initiated by the user or stakeholder, who identifies the need for a change and submits a request for change to the change control board.

2. **Impact analysis:** The change control board evaluates the change request and performs an impact analysis to determine the potential effects of the change on the system, including cost, schedule, and technical impacts.

3. **Approval or rejection:** Based on the impact analysis, the change control board decides whether to approve or reject the change request. If the change is approved, it is scheduled for implementation.

4. **Implementation:** The approved change is implemented, typically by the development team. The change is tested and verified to ensure that it meets the requirements and does not introduce new issues.

5. **Documentation:** The change is documented, including the reason for the change, the impact analysis, and the details of the implementation. This documentation is important for future reference and for tracking the history of changes to the system.

6. **Release:** The change is released to the users, typically as part of a new version of the software.

The change control process is an important part of software maintenance and project management, as it helps to ensure that changes are introduced in a controlled and coordinated manner, reducing the risk of introducing new issues or undoing previous work. It is a key aspect of managing the evolution of a software system over time.
