### Formal Technical Reviews (Peer Reviews)

Formal Technical Reviews, also known as Peer Reviews, are a structured process for evaluating software artifacts such as requirements, design, code, and test cases. These reviews are conducted by a team of peers to identify defects and improve the quality of the software.

Some key points to note about Formal Technical Reviews are:

1. They are a structured process with defined roles for participants, including a moderator, presenter, and reviewers.
2. The goal is to identify defects and improve the quality of the software.
3. They can be applied to various software artifacts, including requirements, design, code, and test cases.
4. They are conducted by a team of peers, who are knowledgeable about the software and its development process.
5. The review process includes preparation, review meeting, and follow-up activities.
6. The outcome of the review is documented, including any defects identified and actions to be taken to address them.

Formal Technical Reviews are an important part of the software development process, helping to improve the quality of the software and reduce the cost of fixing defects later in the development cycle. They are a valuable tool for software engineers and should be incorporated into the development process.
