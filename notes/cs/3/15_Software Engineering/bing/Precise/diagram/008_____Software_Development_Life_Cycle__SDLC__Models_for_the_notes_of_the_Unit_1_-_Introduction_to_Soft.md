### Software Development Life Cycle (SDLC) Models

The Software Development Life Cycle (SDLC) is a framework that defines the process used by organizations to build an application from its inception to its decommission. There are several SDLC models that can be used to guide the development process. Here are some of the most commonly used models:

1. **Waterfall Model**: This is a linear sequential approach where each phase must be completed before the next one can begin. The phases are Requirements, Design, Implementation, Verification, and Maintenance.

2. **V-Model**: This is an extension of the Waterfall Model where the testing phase is integrated into each phase of the development process. The phases are Requirements, System Design, Architecture Design, Module Design, Coding, Unit Testing, Integration Testing, System Testing, and Acceptance Testing.

3. **Iterative Model**: This model involves building the software incrementally, with each iteration adding more functionality until the final product is complete. The phases are Planning, Requirements, Analysis and Design, Implementation, Testing, and Evaluation.

4. **Spiral Model**: This model combines the iterative approach with the systematic, controlled aspects of the Waterfall Model. The phases are Planning, Risk Analysis, Engineering, Evaluation, and Customer Communication.

5. **Agile Model**: This model emphasizes flexibility and adaptability, with a focus on delivering working software quickly and responding to changing requirements. The phases are Planning, Requirements, Design, Build, Test, and Review.

Each of these models has its own strengths and weaknesses, and the choice of model will depend on the specific needs and constraints of the project. It is important to carefully evaluate the options and choose the model that is best suited to the project at hand.
