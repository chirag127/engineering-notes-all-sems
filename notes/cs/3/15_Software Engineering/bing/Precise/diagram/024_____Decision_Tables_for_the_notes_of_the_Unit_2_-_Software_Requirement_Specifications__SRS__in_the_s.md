### Decision Tables

Decision tables are a precise yet compact way to model complicated logic. Decision tables, like flowcharts and if-then-else and switch-case statements, associate conditions with actions to perform, but in many cases do so in a more elegant way.

Here are some key points to remember about decision tables:

- A decision table is a table of rows and columns, separated into four quadrants.
- The top-left quadrant lists the conditions to be tested.
- The top-right quadrant lists the actions to be taken.
- The bottom-left quadrant lists the possible combinations of conditions.
- The bottom-right quadrant lists the actions to be taken for each combination of conditions.
- Each column represents a rule.
- Each row in the bottom-right quadrant represents an action to be taken if the conditions in the corresponding row in the bottom-left quadrant are met.
- Decision tables can be used to model complex logic in a clear and concise way.
