### Waterfall Model

The Waterfall Model is a linear sequential approach to software development. It is often considered the classic approach to the systems development life cycle. The model is divided into phases, with the output of one phase becoming the input for the next phase. The phases of the Waterfall Model are:

1. **Requirements**: In this phase, the requirements for the system are gathered and documented.
2. **Design**: In this phase, the system and software design is prepared from the requirement specifications.
3. **Implementation**: In this phase, the system is developed in small programs called units, which are integrated in the next phase.
4. **Integration and Testing**: In this phase, all the units developed in the implementation phase are integrated into a system after testing of each unit.
5. **Deployment**: In this phase, the product is delivered or deployed to the customer after testing.
6. **Maintenance**: In this phase, the system is maintained by fixing any issues that arise.

The Waterfall Model is a simple and easy to understand approach to software development. However, it has its limitations, such as the difficulty of accommodating changes once the process is underway. It is best suited for projects with well-defined requirements and a clear understanding of the final product.
