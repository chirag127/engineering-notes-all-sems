### Formal Technical Reviews (Peer Reviews)

Formal Technical Reviews (FTRs), also known as Peer Reviews, are a structured and organized approach to reviewing software artifacts, such as requirements, design, code, and test cases. The main objectives of FTRs are to:

1. Improve the quality of software products by identifying and removing defects early in the development process.
2. Promote teamwork and collaboration among team members.
3. Facilitate knowledge sharing and transfer among team members.
4. Ensure compliance with standards, guidelines, and best practices.

FTRs involve a team of reviewers, including the author of the artifact being reviewed, who work together to identify defects and suggest improvements. The review process typically includes the following steps:

1. Planning: The review team is selected, and the scope, objectives, and schedule of the review are defined.
2. Preparation: The reviewers study the artifact being reviewed and prepare for the review meeting.
3. Review Meeting: The review team meets to discuss the artifact and identify defects and suggestions for improvement.
4. Rework: The author of the artifact addresses the defects and suggestions identified during the review meeting.
5. Follow-up: The review team verifies that the defects have been addressed and the suggestions have been implemented.

FTRs can be applied at various stages of the software development process, including requirements review, design review, code review, and test review. They can help improve the quality of software products, reduce development costs, and shorten development time by identifying and removing defects early in the development process. FTRs also promote teamwork, collaboration, and knowledge sharing among team members, and help ensure compliance with standards, guidelines, and best practices.
