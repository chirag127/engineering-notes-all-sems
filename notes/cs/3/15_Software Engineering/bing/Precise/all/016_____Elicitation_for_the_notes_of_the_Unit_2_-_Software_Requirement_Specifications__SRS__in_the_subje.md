# Elicitation

Elicitation is the process of gathering information from stakeholders, customers, and users to define and prioritize the requirements for a software system. It is a critical step in the development of a Software Requirements Specification (SRS) document. Here are some key points to consider when conducting elicitation for an SRS:

1. Identify stakeholders: It is important to identify all the stakeholders who will be impacted by the software system. This includes end-users, customers, managers, and developers.

2. Use multiple elicitation techniques: There are several techniques that can be used to gather information from stakeholders, including interviews, surveys, focus groups, and observation. Using multiple techniques can help to ensure that all relevant information is captured.

3. Prioritize requirements: Not all requirements are equally important. It is important to prioritize requirements based on their importance to the success of the software system.

4. Document requirements: All requirements should be documented in a clear and concise manner. This will help to ensure that all stakeholders have a common understanding of what the software system is intended to do.

5. Validate requirements: It is important to validate the requirements with stakeholders to ensure that they accurately reflect their needs and expectations.

Elicitation is a critical step in the development of a Software Requirements Specification (SRS) document. By following these key points, you can help to ensure that your SRS accurately reflects the needs and expectations of all stakeholders.
