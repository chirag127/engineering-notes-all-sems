### Formal Technical Reviews (Peer Reviews)

Formal Technical Reviews (FTRs), also known as Peer Reviews, are a structured and organized approach to reviewing software artifacts, such as requirements, design documents, code, and test cases. The goal of FTRs is to identify and correct defects early in the software development process, before they become more costly to fix.

Some key points to consider when conducting FTRs include:

1. **Preparation**: Before the review, the review leader should ensure that all participants have the necessary materials and are familiar with the objectives and scope of the review.

2. **Roles**: FTRs typically involve a review leader, who facilitates the review, and several reviewers, who provide feedback on the artifact being reviewed.

3. **Process**: During the review, the review leader guides the discussion, while the reviewers provide feedback and identify defects. The review leader should ensure that the discussion remains focused and productive.

4. **Documentation**: The results of the review, including any defects identified, should be documented and tracked to ensure that they are addressed.

5. **Follow-up**: After the review, the review leader should ensure that all action items are addressed and that any necessary changes are made to the artifact being reviewed.

FTRs can be an effective way to improve the quality of software artifacts and to identify and correct defects early in the development process. However, they require careful planning and execution to be effective.
