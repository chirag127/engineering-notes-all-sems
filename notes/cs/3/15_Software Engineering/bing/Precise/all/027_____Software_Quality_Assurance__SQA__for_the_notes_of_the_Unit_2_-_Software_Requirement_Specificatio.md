# Software Quality Assurance (SQA)

Software Quality Assurance (SQA) is a process that ensures that developed software meets and complies with defined or standardized quality specifications. SQA is an ongoing process within the software development life cycle (SDLC) that routinely checks the developed software to ensure it meets desired quality measures.

SQA is important in the context of Software Requirement Specifications (SRS) because it helps to ensure that the requirements specified in the SRS are met by the developed software. Some key points to consider when discussing SQA in relation to SRS include:

1. SQA helps to ensure that the software meets the requirements specified in the SRS by routinely checking the software against the requirements.
2. SQA can help to identify any discrepancies between the requirements specified in the SRS and the developed software, allowing for corrective action to be taken.
3. SQA can help to ensure that the software is developed in a manner that is consistent with the requirements specified in the SRS, reducing the likelihood of defects or issues arising.
4. SQA can help to ensure that the software is of a high quality, meeting the expectations of the end-users and stakeholders.

Overall, SQA plays an important role in ensuring that the software developed meets the requirements specified in the SRS and is of a high quality. By routinely checking the software against the requirements and taking corrective action when necessary, SQA can help to ensure that the software meets the needs of the end-users and stakeholders.
