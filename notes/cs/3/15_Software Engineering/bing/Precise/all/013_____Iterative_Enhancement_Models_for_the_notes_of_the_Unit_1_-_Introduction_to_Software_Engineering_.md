### Iterative Enhancement Models

Iterative Enhancement Models are a type of software development model that involves the development of software through repeated cycles of development and refinement. This approach is based on the idea that software development is an iterative process, where each iteration builds upon the previous one, adding new functionality and improving the quality of the software.

Some key points to note about Iterative Enhancement Models are:

1. The development process is divided into small, manageable iterations, each of which results in a working version of the software.
2. Each iteration involves the design, implementation, and testing of a specific set of features or functionality.
3. Feedback from users and stakeholders is incorporated into the development process, allowing for the refinement of the software based on real-world usage.
4. The iterative approach allows for the early detection and resolution of issues, reducing the risk of project failure.
5. The model is flexible and can accommodate changes in requirements or priorities during the development process.

Iterative Enhancement Models are commonly used in agile software development methodologies, such as Scrum and Extreme Programming (XP). These methodologies emphasize collaboration, flexibility, and rapid response to change, making them well-suited to the iterative approach.

Overall, Iterative Enhancement Models provide a structured and disciplined approach to software development, while also allowing for flexibility and adaptability to changing requirements and priorities. This makes them a popular choice for many software development projects.
