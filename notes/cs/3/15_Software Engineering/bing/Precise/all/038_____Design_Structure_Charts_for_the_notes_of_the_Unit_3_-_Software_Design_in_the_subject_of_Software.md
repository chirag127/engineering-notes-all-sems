# Design Structure Charts

Design Structure Charts (DSCs) are a graphical representation of the design of a software system. They are used to represent the hierarchical structure of the system and the relationships between its components. DSCs are commonly used in the design phase of software development to help visualize and communicate the design of the system.

Some key features of DSCs include:

- They show the hierarchical structure of the system, with the highest level representing the system as a whole and lower levels representing subsystems or modules.
- They show the relationships between components, including calls, data flows, and control flows.
- They can be used to represent both static and dynamic aspects of the system.
- They can be used to represent both the logical and physical structure of the system.

DSCs are a useful tool for software designers, as they provide a clear and concise way to represent the design of a system. They can help to identify potential problems and inconsistencies in the design, and can also be used to communicate the design to other stakeholders, such as developers and project managers.

In summary, Design Structure Charts are a valuable tool for software designers, providing a clear and concise way to represent and communicate the design of a software system. They can help to identify potential problems and inconsistencies in the design, and can also be used to communicate the design to other stakeholders.
