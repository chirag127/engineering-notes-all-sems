# SQA Plans for the notes of the Unit 2 - Software Requirement Specifications (SRS) in the subject of Software Engineering

- SQA stands for Software Quality Assurance.
- SQA is a process that ensures that developed software meets and complies with defined or standardized quality specifications.
- SQA is an ongoing process within the software development life cycle (SDLC) that routinely checks the developed software to ensure it meets desired quality measures.
- An SQA plan is a document that outlines the process and procedures that will be followed to ensure that the software being developed meets the required quality standards.
- The SQA plan is typically developed during the planning phase of the SDLC and is updated as the project progresses.
- The SQA plan should include the following elements:
  - Objectives: The goals and objectives of the SQA plan should be clearly defined.
  - Roles and Responsibilities: The roles and responsibilities of the SQA team members should be clearly defined.
  - Procedures: The procedures that will be followed to ensure that the software meets the required quality standards should be outlined.
  - Standards: The standards that the software must comply with should be identified.
  - Tools: The tools that will be used to carry out the SQA activities should be identified.
  - Schedule: The schedule for carrying out the SQA activities should be defined.
  - Reporting: The reporting procedures for communicating the results of the SQA activities should be defined.
- The SQA plan is an important document that helps to ensure that the software being developed meets the required quality standards. It is important to develop an SQA plan early in the software development process and to update it as the project progresses.
