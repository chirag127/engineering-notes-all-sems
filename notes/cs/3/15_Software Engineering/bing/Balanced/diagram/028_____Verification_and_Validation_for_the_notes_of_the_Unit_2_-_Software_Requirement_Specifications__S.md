### Verification and Validation

- Verification and validation are two important activities in software engineering that aim to ensure the quality and reliability of software products.
- Verification is the process of checking whether the software meets the specified requirements and conforms to the design and standards.
- Validation is the process of checking whether the software meets the user's needs and expectations and fulfills its intended purpose.
- Verification and validation can be applied at different stages of the software development life cycle, such as planning, analysis, design, implementation, testing, and maintenance.
- Verification and validation can be performed using various techniques, such as reviews, inspections, walkthroughs, audits, testing, simulation, prototyping, and formal methods.
- Verification and validation can be classified into four levels, depending on the degree of rigor and formality involved:
  - Level 0: Informal verification and validation, which relies on the experience and judgment of the developers and users, and does not follow any systematic or documented procedure.
  - Level 1: Technical verification and validation, which involves the use of technical methods and tools, such as testing, debugging, and code analysis, to detect and correct errors and defects in the software.
  - Level 2: Quality verification and validation, which focuses on the quality aspects of the software, such as functionality, performance, usability, reliability, security, and maintainability, and uses quality standards and metrics to evaluate and improve the software.
  - Level 3: Formal verification and validation, which employs rigorous mathematical and logical methods, such as formal specification, verification, and proof, to ensure the correctness and completeness of the software.
