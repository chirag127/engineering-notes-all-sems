### Iterative Enhancement Models

- Iterative enhancement models are a type of software development life cycle models that focus on an initial, basic implementation that gradually adds more complexity and a broader feature set until the final system is complete.
- Iterative enhancement models combine elements of the linear sequential model with the iterative philosophy of prototyping.
- Iterative enhancement models break down the software into several modules that are incrementally developed and delivered.
- Iterative enhancement models comprise three stages, namely, analysis of software system, classification of requested modifications, and implementation of requested modifications.
- In the analysis stage, the requirements are analyzed to begin the software maintenance process.
- In the classification stage, the requested modifications are categorized into four types: corrective, adaptive, perfective, and preventive.
- In the implementation stage, the modifications are implemented according to their priority and complexity.
- Iterative enhancement models have some advantages, such as:
  - They allow feedback from the users and customers at each iteration.
  - They reduce the risk of failure by delivering working software early and often.
  - They accommodate changing requirements and new features easily.
  - They improve the quality and reliability of the software by testing and debugging each module.
- Iterative enhancement models also have some disadvantages, such as:
  - They require more management and planning skills to coordinate the iterations.
  - They may lead to scope creep and cost overrun if the iterations are not well-defined and controlled.
  - They may result in inconsistent design and architecture if the modules are not integrated properly.
  - They may increase the maintenance cost and effort if the software is not well-documented and structured.
