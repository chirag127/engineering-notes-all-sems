## Unit 3 - Software Design

- Software design is the process of defining the structure, behavior, and interfaces of a software system based on the requirements and specifications.
- Software design involves applying various principles, methods, tools, and techniques to create a software solution that meets the needs and expectations of the stakeholders.
- Software design can be divided into two main phases: architectural design and detailed design.
- Architectural design is the process of defining the high-level structure and organization of a software system, such as the components, modules, interfaces, and communication patterns.
- Detailed design is the process of defining the low-level details and implementation of each component or module, such as the algorithms, data structures, classes, methods, and variables.
- Software design can be influenced by various factors, such as the software development process, the software quality attributes, the software design principles, and the software design patterns.
- Software development process is the sequence of activities and tasks that are performed to plan, analyze, design, implement, test, and deploy a software system.
- Software quality attributes are the non-functional characteristics of a software system that affect its usability, reliability, performance, security, maintainability, and portability.
- Software design principles are the general guidelines and best practices that help to create a good software design, such as cohesion, coupling, abstraction, encapsulation, modularity, hierarchy, and reuse.
- Software design patterns are the reusable solutions to common software design problems that occur in specific contexts, such as creational, structural, and behavioral patterns.
