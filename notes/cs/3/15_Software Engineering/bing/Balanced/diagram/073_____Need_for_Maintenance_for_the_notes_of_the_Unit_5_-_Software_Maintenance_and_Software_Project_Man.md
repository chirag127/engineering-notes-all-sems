### Need for Maintenance

- Software maintenance is the process of modifying a software system after it has been delivered to the customer.
- Software maintenance is necessary for various reasons, such as:
  - To correct errors or bugs that were not detected during testing or that emerged after deployment.
  - To improve the performance, reliability, security, or usability of the software system.
  - To adapt the software system to changing requirements, environments, platforms, or technologies.
  - To enhance the functionality or features of the software system to meet new or evolving needs of the customer or the market.
- Software maintenance can be classified into four types, according to the nature and purpose of the modification:
  - Corrective maintenance: To fix faults or defects in the software system.
  - Adaptive maintenance: To adjust the software system to changes in the external environment, such as hardware, operating system, or regulations.
  - Perfective maintenance: To improve the quality attributes of the software system, such as performance, usability, or maintainability.
  - Preventive maintenance: To prevent potential problems or reduce the complexity of the software system, such as refactoring, documentation, or reengineering.
- Software maintenance is an important and costly activity in the software development life cycle. It can account for up to 90% of the total cost of software ownership. Therefore, it is essential to plan, manage, and control the software maintenance process effectively and efficiently. Some of the challenges and best practices of software maintenance are:
  - Establishing a maintenance plan that defines the scope, objectives, activities, resources, and schedule of the maintenance process.
  - Establishing a maintenance team that has the appropriate skills, knowledge, and experience to perform the maintenance tasks.
  - Establishing a maintenance process that follows a systematic and disciplined approach, such as the IEEE Standard for Software Maintenance (IEEE 14764).
  - Establishing a maintenance repository that stores and manages the software artifacts, such as source code, documentation, test cases, and change requests, related to the maintenance process.
  - Establishing a maintenance measurement and evaluation system that monitors and assesses the quality, productivity, and effectiveness of the maintenance process and the software system.
