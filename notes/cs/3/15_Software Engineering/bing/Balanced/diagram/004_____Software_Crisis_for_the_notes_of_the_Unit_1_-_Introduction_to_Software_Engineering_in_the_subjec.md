### Software Crisis

- Software crisis is a term used to describe the difficulty of developing high-quality software systems that meet the user requirements, within the budget and time constraints.
- Software crisis was more prevalent in the early days of computing, when the hardware capabilities were rapidly increasing, but the software development methods were not keeping up with the complexity and scale of the problems.
- Some of the causes of software crisis are:
  - The cost of owning and maintaining software was as expensive as developing the software.
  - Projects were running over-time and over-budget, due to poor estimation and planning.
  - Software was very inefficient and unreliable, due to lack of testing and quality assurance.
  - Software often did not meet user requirements, due to poor communication and analysis.
  - Software was difficult to modify and reuse, due to lack of documentation and standards.
- Some of the examples of software crisis are:
  - The OS/360 operating system, which was a decade-long project from the 1960s that produced one of the most complex software systems at the time, but also suffered from delays, bugs, and compatibility issues.
  - The Therac-25 radiation therapy machine, which was a medical device from the 1980s that caused several deaths and injuries due to software errors and design flaws.
  - The Ariane 5 rocket, which was a space launch vehicle from the 1990s that exploded shortly after launch due to a software exception caused by a data conversion error.
- Software crisis led to the emergence of software engineering as a discipline, which aims to apply systematic, disciplined, and quantifiable approaches to software development, in order to improve the quality, productivity, and satisfaction of software stakeholders.
