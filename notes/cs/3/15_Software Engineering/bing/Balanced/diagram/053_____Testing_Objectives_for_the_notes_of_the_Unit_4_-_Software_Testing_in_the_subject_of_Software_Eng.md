### Testing Objectives

- The main objective of software testing is to ensure that the software meets the specified requirements and satisfies the intended purpose.
- Testing also aims to identify and correct any defects or errors in the software that may affect its functionality, performance, reliability, security, usability, or maintainability.
- Testing can be done at different levels of abstraction, such as unit testing, integration testing, system testing, and acceptance testing, depending on the scope and complexity of the software.
- Testing can also be done from different perspectives, such as functional testing, structural testing, regression testing, and non-functional testing, depending on the criteria and techniques used to evaluate the software.
- Testing can be performed in different phases of the software development life cycle, such as planning, design, implementation, verification, and maintenance, depending on the testing strategy and model adopted.
- Testing can be conducted by different stakeholders, such as developers, testers, customers, users, or third parties, depending on the testing roles and responsibilities assigned.
- Testing can be influenced by various factors, such as software characteristics, testing objectives, testing resources, testing environment, testing standards, testing tools, and testing risks, depending on the testing context and constraints.
