### Similarity and Differences from Conventional Engineering Processes for Software Engineering

- Conventional engineering processes are the methods and techniques used to design, build, and maintain physical systems, such as buildings, bridges, machines, etc.
- Software engineering processes are the methods and techniques used to design, build, and maintain software systems, such as applications, websites, databases, etc.
- Both conventional and software engineering processes share some similarities, such as:
  - Both are getting automated slowly, with the use of tools and frameworks that simplify and speed up the tasks.
  - Both require in-depth knowledge of their field, as well as creativity, problem-solving, and communication skills.
  - Both follow some common principles, such as modularity, abstraction, reusability, reliability, etc.
  - Both involve planning, analysis, design, implementation, testing, and maintenance phases.
- However, conventional and software engineering processes also have some differences, such as:
  - Conventional engineering processes deal with physical systems that obey the laws of nature, whereas software engineering processes deal with abstract systems that are limited only by the imagination.
  - Conventional engineering processes have more standardized and regulated practices, whereas software engineering processes have more diversity and flexibility.
  - Conventional engineering processes have more predictable and measurable outcomes, whereas software engineering processes have more uncertainty and complexity.
  - Conventional engineering processes have more physical constraints and costs, whereas software engineering processes have more logical and quality constraints and costs.
  - Conventional engineering processes have a more physically active role, whereas software engineering processes have a more typical office job.
  - Conventional engineering processes have higher government sector opportunity, whereas software engineering processes have more opportunities of foreign settlement.
