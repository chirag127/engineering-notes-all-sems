### Prototype Model

- A prototype model is a **first or preliminary version** of the final software that has **limited functionality**, **low reliability**, and **inefficient performance**.
- A prototype model is used when the **customers do not know the exact project requirements** beforehand or when the requirements are **complex or unclear**.
- A prototype model helps to **get feedback from the customers** and **refine the design** as per their needs and expectations.
- A prototype model can be **built quickly** and **tested early** in the software development life cycle (SDLC).
- A prototype model can be **thrown away** after getting the feedback or **evolved** into the final software.

#### Steps of Prototype Model

- The steps of prototype model are:
  - **Communication**: The developer and the customer **discuss** the project objectives, scope, and requirements.
  - **Quick design**: The developer **creates a rough design** of the software based on the customer's input.
  - **Modeling**: The developer **builds a prototype** of the software using the design and **demonstrates** it to the customer.
  - **Deployment delivery and feedback**: The customer **evaluates the prototype** and **provides feedback** to the developer.
  - **Revision and enhancement**: The developer **modifies the prototype** according to the feedback and **repeats** the process until the customer is satisfied.

#### Advantages of Prototype Model

- Some advantages of prototype model are:
  - It **reduces the risk** of developing a software that does not meet the customer's needs or expectations.
  - It **improves the quality** of the software by incorporating the customer's feedback and suggestions.
  - It **enhances the communication** and understanding between the developer and the customer.
  - It **saves time and cost** by detecting and correcting the errors and defects early in the SDLC.

#### Disadvantages of Prototype Model

- Some disadvantages of prototype model are:
  - It **requires more resources** and effort to build and maintain multiple prototypes.
  - It **may create confusion** or misunderstanding between the developer and the customer if the prototype is not well documented or explained.
  - It **may compromise the performance** or functionality of the final software if the prototype is not properly refined or evolved.
  - It **may lead to incomplete or inadequate testing** of the software if the prototype is not thoroughly verified or validated.
