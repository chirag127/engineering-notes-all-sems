### Design Strategies for the notes of the Unit 3 - Software Design in the subject of Software Engineering

- System design is a critical component of software engineering and involves making decisions about the architecture, components, modules, interfaces, and data for a software system.
- System design strategy refers to the approach that is taken to design a software system.
- There are several strategies that can be used to design software systems, including the following :
  - Top-Down Design: This strategy starts with a high-level view of the system and gradually breaks it down into smaller, more manageable components. The top-level component defines the overall functionality and behavior of the system, and the lower-level components implement the details and sub-functions. This strategy is useful for complex systems that require a clear and consistent structure, but it may also introduce some challenges, such as incomplete or inaccurate specifications, communication overhead, and difficulty in testing and integration.
  - Bottom-Up Design: This strategy starts with the low-level components and builds them up into higher-level components that form the system. The low-level components are designed and tested individually, and then combined and integrated to form the higher-level components. This strategy is useful for systems that are based on existing or reusable components, but it may also introduce some challenges, such as lack of coherence, redundancy, and dependency issues.
  - Structured Design: This strategy is based on the principle of modularity, which means dividing the system into independent and well-defined modules that communicate through well-defined interfaces. The modules are designed to have high cohesion (meaning they perform a single function or a related set of functions) and low coupling (meaning they have minimal interdependence and interaction with other modules). This strategy is useful for systems that require maintainability, reusability, and reliability, but it may also introduce some challenges, such as complexity, inefficiency, and rigidity.
  - Function-Oriented Design: This strategy is based on the principle of decomposition, which means breaking down the system into functions that perform specific tasks or operations. The functions are designed to have high abstraction (meaning they hide the details and complexity of the implementation) and low complexity (meaning they have a limited number of inputs, outputs, and steps). This strategy is useful for systems that require simplicity, clarity, and efficiency, but it may also introduce some challenges, such as lack of flexibility, scalability, and security.
  - Object-Oriented Design: This strategy is based on the principle of encapsulation, which means grouping the data and the functions that operate on the data into objects that have a unique identity and behavior. The objects are designed to have high abstraction (meaning they hide the details and complexity of the implementation) and high cohesion (meaning they perform a single function or a related set of functions). The objects communicate with each other through well-defined interfaces that define the services they provide and the messages they exchange. This strategy is useful for systems that require flexibility, scalability, and security, but it may also introduce some challenges, such as complexity, inefficiency, and inconsistency.
- In addition to the above strategies, there are also some software architecture design patterns that can be used to design software systems. Software architecture design patterns are reusable schemes that address common software design challenges and provide solutions that are proven to work in practice. Some examples of software architecture design patterns are:
  - Layered Pattern: This pattern organizes the system into layers that have different levels of abstraction and responsibility. Each layer provides services to the layer above it and uses services from the layer below it. The layers are loosely coupled and can be changed or replaced independently. This pattern is useful for systems that require modularity, portability, and reusability, but it may also introduce some challenges, such as performance overhead, complexity, and latency.
  - Client-Server Pattern: This pattern divides the system into two types of components: clients and servers. Clients request services from servers, and servers provide services to clients. The clients and servers are loosely coupled and can be distributed across different machines or networks. This pattern is useful for systems that require scalability, availability, and interoperability, but it may also introduce some challenges, such as security, reliability, and synchronization.
