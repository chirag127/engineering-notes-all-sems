### Entity Relationship Diagrams

- Entity Relationship Diagrams (ERDs) are a data modeling method used in software engineering to produce a conceptual data model of an information system.
- ERDs help to identify different system elements and their relationships with each other. They are often used as the basis for data flow diagrams or DFDs.
- ERDs consist of different symbols and connectors that visualize two important information: the major entities within the system scope, and the inter-relationships among these entities.
- An entity is a thing or object in the real world that is distinguishable from other objects. For example, a student, a course, a teacher, etc. Entities are represented by rectangles in ERDs.
- A relationship is an association among entities. For example, a student enrolls in a course, a teacher teaches a course, etc. Relationships are represented by diamonds in ERDs.
- An attribute is a property or characteristic of an entity or a relationship. For example, a student has a name, a course has a code, a teacher has a salary, etc. Attributes are represented by ovals in ERDs.
- A key is a special kind of attribute that uniquely identifies an entity or a relationship. For example, a student ID, a course code, a teacher ID, etc. Keys are underlined in ERDs.
- A cardinality is a constraint that specifies the number of instances of one entity that can or must be associated with each instance of another entity. For example, a student can enroll in many courses, a course must have one teacher, etc. Cardinalities are represented by numbers or symbols on the relationship lines in ERDs.
