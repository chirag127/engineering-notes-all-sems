# Evolutionary Development Models

Evolutionary development models are a type of software engineering models that aim to deliver the software system in increments over time, rather than in a single release. Evolutionary models are suitable for projects where the requirements are unclear, dynamic, or complex, and where the customer wants to use the core features of the system as soon as possible. Evolutionary models are also compatible with object-oriented software development, as the system can be easily divided into units based on objects.

There are two main types of evolutionary models: prototyping and spiral.

## Prototyping Model

The prototyping model is an evolutionary model that focuses on creating a prototype of the system, which is a simplified and incomplete version that demonstrates the main functionality and user interface of the system. The prototype is developed based on the initial requirements and feedback from the customer, and is refined and enhanced until it meets the customer's expectations and needs. The prototyping model has the following advantages and disadvantages:

- Advantages:
  - It reduces the risk of developing a system that does not meet the customer's needs or expectations.
  - It allows the customer to get a feel of the system and provide feedback early in the development process.
  - It helps to clarify and validate the requirements and design of the system.
  - It facilitates communication and collaboration between the developers and the customer.
- Disadvantages:
  - It may lead to unrealistic expectations from the customer, as the prototype may not reflect the quality, performance, or reliability of the final system.
  - It may increase the cost and time of development, as the prototype may need to be discarded or reworked to fit the final system.
  - It may introduce errors or inconsistencies in the system, as the prototype may not follow the standards or specifications of the final system.

## Spiral Model

The spiral model is an evolutionary model that combines the iterative and risk-driven approaches of software engineering. The spiral model consists of four phases: planning, risk analysis, engineering, and evaluation. The system is developed in cycles, where each cycle corresponds to a level of abstraction or refinement of the system. The spiral model has the following advantages and disadvantages:

- Advantages:
  - It allows the system to be developed incrementally and adaptively, based on the changing requirements and feedback from the customer.
  - It emphasizes the identification and mitigation of risks at each stage of development, which reduces the uncertainty and complexity of the project.
  - It provides a flexible and scalable framework for software engineering, as the number and size of cycles can be adjusted according to the project's needs and constraints.
- Disadvantages:
  - It requires a high level of expertise and experience from the developers, as the risk analysis and management can be challenging and subjective.
  - It may increase the cost and time of development, as the risk analysis and evaluation may require additional resources and documentation.
  - It may be difficult to estimate the budget and schedule of the project, as the scope and duration of each cycle may vary.
