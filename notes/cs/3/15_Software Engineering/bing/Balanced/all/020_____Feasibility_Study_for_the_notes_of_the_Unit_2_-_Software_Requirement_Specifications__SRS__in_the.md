# Feasibility Study for the notes of the Unit 2 - Software Requirement Specifications (SRS) in the subject of Software Engineering

- A feasibility study is an analysis of the viability of a project or a system before it is developed or implemented.
- It helps to identify the potential benefits, costs, risks, and constraints of the project or system, and to evaluate the alternatives and select the best option.
- A feasibility study is usually conducted in the early stages of the software development life cycle (SDLC), after the problem definition and before the requirement analysis.
- A feasibility study consists of four main types of feasibility: technical, economic, operational, and legal.
- Technical feasibility assesses the availability and suitability of the hardware, software, network, and other resources and technologies required for the project or system.
- Economic feasibility evaluates the cost-effectiveness and profitability of the project or system, by estimating the development, operation, and maintenance costs, and comparing them with the expected benefits and revenues.
- Operational feasibility examines the compatibility and acceptability of the project or system with the existing business processes, organizational culture, user needs, and expectations.
- Legal feasibility analyzes the compliance of the project or system with the applicable laws, regulations, standards, and ethical principles.
- A feasibility study report summarizes the findings and recommendations of the feasibility study, and provides the rationale and justification for the chosen option.
