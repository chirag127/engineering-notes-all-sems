# Software as an Evolutionary Entity

- Software as an evolutionary entity is the concept that software is not a static product, but a dynamic and adaptive system that evolves over time to meet changing requirements, environments, and technologies .
- Software evolution is the process of developing, maintaining, and updating software for various reasons, such as fixing bugs, adding features, improving performance, enhancing security, or complying with standards .
- Software evolution is important because it affects the quality, reliability, usability, and maintainability of software, as well as the cost and effort of software development and maintenance .
- Software evolution is influenced by many factors, such as user feedback, market demand, competitor actions, technological innovation, organizational changes, legal regulations, and social trends .
- Software evolution can be classified into different types, such as corrective, adaptive, perfective, and preventive evolution .
  - Corrective evolution is the process of fixing errors or defects in software that affect its functionality or performance .
  - Adaptive evolution is the process of modifying software to cope with changes in its environment, such as hardware, operating system, or platform .
  - Perfective evolution is the process of improving software to enhance its quality, functionality, or usability, such as adding new features, optimizing algorithms, or redesigning interfaces .
  - Preventive evolution is the process of modifying software to reduce the risk of future problems, such as refactoring code, improving documentation, or applying standards .
- Software evolution can be performed manually, semi-automatically, or automatically, depending on the level of human intervention and tool support involved in the process .
  - Manual evolution is the process of changing software by software engineers without any automated assistance .
  - Semi-automatic evolution is the process of changing software by software engineers with the help of automated tools, such as refactoring tools, code generators, or testing tools .
  - Automatic evolution is the process of changing software by automated tools without any human intervention, such as self-adaptive systems, evolutionary algorithms, or artificial intelligence .
- Software evolution can be modeled and measured using various approaches, such as software metrics, software process models, software evolution laws, and software evolution theories   .
  - Software metrics are quantitative indicators of software characteristics, such as size, complexity, quality, or productivity .
  - Software process models are conceptual frameworks that describe the activities, tasks, roles, and artifacts involved in software evolution, such as waterfall, spiral, agile, or DevOps .
  - Software evolution laws are empirical observations or generalizations that describe the patterns, trends, or behaviors of software evolution, such as Lehman's laws, Belady's law, or Moore's law  .
  - Software evolution theories are formal or informal explanations or hypotheses that account for the causes, mechanisms, or outcomes of software evolution, such as Darwinian evolution, punctuated equilibrium, or co-evolution  .
