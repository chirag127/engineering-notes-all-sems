# Elicitation for the notes of the Unit 2 - Software Requirement Specifications (SRS) in the subject of Software Engineering

- Elicitation is the process of gathering, understanding, and documenting the software requirements from various stakeholders, such as customers, users, domain experts, etc.
- Elicitation is also known as requirements engineering, requirements analysis, or requirements discovery.
- Elicitation is a crucial activity in software engineering, as it helps to define the scope, objectives, and constraints of the software project, and to avoid ambiguity, inconsistency, incompleteness, and errors in the requirements.
- Elicitation involves various techniques, such as interviews, questionnaires, surveys, observation, brainstorming, prototyping, scenarios, use cases, etc.
- Elicitation also requires effective communication, negotiation, and validation skills, as well as the use of appropriate tools and models to represent and document the requirements.
- Elicitation results in the creation of a Software Requirement Specification (SRS) document, which is a formal and detailed description of the software requirements, including the functional, non-functional, and quality attributes, as well as the assumptions, dependencies, and risks involved.
- SRS is the basis for the design, development, testing, and maintenance of the software system, and serves as a contract between the stakeholders and the developers.
- SRS should follow some standards and guidelines, such as IEEE 830, to ensure its quality, consistency, and completeness.
- SRS should also be reviewed, verified, and validated by the stakeholders and the developers, to ensure that it meets the needs and expectations of the customers and users, and that it is feasible, testable, and traceable.
