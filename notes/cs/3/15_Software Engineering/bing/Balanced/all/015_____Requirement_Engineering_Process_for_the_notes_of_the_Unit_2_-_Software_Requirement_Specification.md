# Requirement Engineering Process

The requirement engineering process is a critical step in the software development life cycle as it helps to ensure that the software system being developed meets the needs and expectations of stakeholders, and that it is developed on time, within budget, and to the required quality.

The requirement engineering process is an iterative process that involves several steps, including :

- **Requirements inception or elicitation** – Developers and stakeholders meet; the latter are inquired about the objectives, expectations, constraints, and risks of the software system. Various techniques can be used to elicit requirements, such as interviews, questionnaires, workshops, observation, prototyping, etc.
- **Requirements analysis and negotiation** – Requirements are identified (including new ones if the development is incremental), classified, prioritized, and validated. Conflicts, inconsistencies, ambiguities, and gaps are resolved through negotiation and trade-offs among stakeholders.
- **System modeling** – Some engineering fields (or specific projects) require the use of models to represent and communicate the requirements of the system. Models can be textual, graphical, mathematical, or a combination of these. Examples of models are use cases, data flow diagrams, entity-relationship diagrams, state machines, etc.
- **Requirements specification** – Requirements are documented in a formal or informal way, depending on the project context and standards. A common format for requirements specification is the Software Requirements Specification (SRS) document, which contains a complete and consistent description of the functional and non-functional requirements of the system.
- **Requirements validation** – Requirements are verified and validated to ensure that they are correct, complete, consistent, feasible, testable, and traceable. Various techniques can be used to validate requirements, such as reviews, inspections, walkthroughs, simulations, testing, etc.
- **Requirements management** – Requirements are managed throughout the project life cycle, as they may change due to evolving needs, new constraints, feedback, or errors. Requirements management involves planning, monitoring, controlling, and communicating the requirements and their changes. Requirements management also involves tracing the requirements to their sources, dependencies, and work items.

Some of the main characteristics of good requirements are:

- **Consistent** – No two requirements should conflict with each other.
- **Complete** – All the necessary information and details should be provided.
- **Achievable** – The requirements should be realistic and feasible within the project scope, budget, and schedule.
- **Unambiguous** – The requirements should be clear and precise, without leaving room for interpretation or misunderstanding.
- **Verifiable** – The requirements should be testable and measurable, using objective criteria and methods.
