# Iterative Enhancement Models

- The iterative enhancement model is a software development life cycle (SDLC) approach that combines elements of the linear sequential model (also known as the waterfall model) with the iterative philosophy of prototyping .
- In this model, the software is broken down into several modules that are incrementally developed and delivered.
- The development team starts with the core module of the system that provides the basic functionality and then adds more features and enhancements in each iteration .
- The iterative enhancement model has the following advantages  :
  - It allows early feedback from the users and stakeholders, which can improve the quality and usability of the software.
  - It reduces the risk of failure by delivering working software in each iteration and testing it before moving to the next one.
  - It accommodates changing requirements and new features more easily than the linear sequential model.
  - It increases the productivity and efficiency of the development team by focusing on the most important and critical features first.
- The iterative enhancement model has the following disadvantages  :
  - It requires more management and coordination among the developers, testers, and users, as each iteration involves planning, designing, coding, testing, and reviewing.
  - It may lead to scope creep and budget overrun if the iterations are not well-defined and controlled.
  - It may result in poor architecture and design if the initial core module is not well-designed and modularized.
  - It may introduce more errors and bugs as the software becomes more complex and interdependent with each iteration.
