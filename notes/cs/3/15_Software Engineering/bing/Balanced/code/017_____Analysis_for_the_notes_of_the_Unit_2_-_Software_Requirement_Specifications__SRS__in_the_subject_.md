### Analysis for the notes of Unit 2 - Software Requirement Specifications (SRS)

- Software Requirement Specifications (SRS) is a document that describes the requirements of a software system in a clear, precise, and consistent manner.
- The purpose of SRS is to communicate the expectations and needs of the stakeholders to the developers, testers, and managers of the software project.
- The benefits of SRS are:
  - It helps to avoid ambiguity and misunderstanding among the parties involved in the software development process.
  - It provides a basis for estimating the cost, time, and resources needed for the software development.
  - It facilitates the verification and validation of the software system by defining the criteria for testing and evaluation.
  - It serves as a reference for the maintenance and evolution of the software system by documenting the changes and updates.
- The characteristics of a good SRS are:
  - Correct: It should state the true requirements of the system and avoid errors and inconsistencies.
  - Complete: It should cover all the functional and non-functional requirements of the system and avoid missing or vague information.
  - Consistent: It should avoid contradictions and conflicts among the requirements and use a uniform terminology and notation.
  - Clear: It should use simple, precise, and unambiguous language and avoid jargon and technical details.
  - Verifiable: It should state the requirements in a measurable and testable way and avoid subjective or vague terms.
  - Modifiable: It should allow for easy and controlled changes and updates to the requirements and maintain a consistent structure and format.
  - Traceable: It should link the requirements to their sources and to their corresponding design and implementation elements and provide a rationale for each requirement.
- The process of developing SRS involves the following steps:
  - Elicitation: It is the process of gathering the requirements from the stakeholders, such as users, customers, domain experts, etc., by using various techniques, such as interviews, surveys, observation, brainstorming, etc.
  - Analysis: It is the process of analyzing the elicited requirements to identify the needs, goals, constraints, and assumptions of the system and to resolve any conflicts, ambiguities, or inconsistencies among the requirements.
  - Specification: It is the process of documenting the analyzed requirements in a formal and structured way, using a standard format and notation, such as IEEE 830, UML, etc.
  - Validation: It is the process of verifying and validating the specified requirements to ensure that they are correct, complete, consistent, clear, verifiable, modifiable, and traceable and that they meet the expectations and needs of the stakeholders.
  - Management: It is the process of managing the requirements throughout the software development life cycle by using various tools and techniques, such as configuration management, change control, traceability matrix, etc.
