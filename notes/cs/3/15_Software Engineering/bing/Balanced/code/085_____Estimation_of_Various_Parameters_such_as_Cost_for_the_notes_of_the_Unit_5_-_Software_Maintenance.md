### Estimation of Various Parameters such as Cost for the notes of the Unit 5 - Software Maintenance and Software Project Management in the subject of Software Engineering

Estimation is the process of predicting the amount of time, effort, and resources required to complete a software project. Estimation is essential for planning, budgeting, and controlling the software project. Estimation also helps to assess the feasibility, risk, and quality of the software project.

There are various parameters that need to be estimated for a software project, such as:

- Size: The amount of functionality or features that the software delivers to the users or customers. Size can be measured in terms of lines of code, function points, user stories, or other units.
- Effort: The amount of human work or labor that is required to develop, test, and maintain the software. Effort can be measured in terms of person-hours, person-days, person-months, or person-years.
- Cost: The amount of money that is required to fund the software project. Cost can be calculated by multiplying the effort by the average hourly or daily rate of the software developers and other staff involved in the project.
- Duration: The amount of time that is required to complete the software project from start to finish. Duration can be calculated by dividing the effort by the number of available resources or by using a schedule estimation technique such as critical path method or PERT.
- Quality: The degree to which the software meets the requirements, expectations, and standards of the users or customers. Quality can be measured in terms of defects, errors, failures, reliability, usability, maintainability, or other attributes.

There are various techniques that can be used for estimating the parameters of a software project, such as:

- Expert judgment: This technique relies on the experience and expertise of one or more experts in the relevant field to provide an estimate based on their intuition, knowledge, and judgment. This technique is often used when there is limited information available about the project.
- Delphi technique: This technique involves a group of experts who provide their estimates independently and anonymously. The estimates are then aggregated and refined through a series of rounds until a consensus is reached. This technique is useful for reducing bias and uncertainty in the estimates.
- Work breakdown structure (WBS): This technique involves breaking down the project into smaller and manageable components or tasks in a hierarchical structure. Each component or task is then estimated separately and the estimates are summed up to obtain the total estimate for the project. This technique is helpful for identifying the scope, dependencies, and risks of the project.
- Three-point estimation: This technique involves providing three estimates for each component or task: optimistic, most likely, and pessimistic. The optimistic estimate is the best-case scenario, the most likely estimate is the most realistic scenario, and the pessimistic estimate is the worst-case scenario. The three estimates are then combined using a weighted average formula to obtain a single estimate that accounts for the uncertainty and variability in the project.
- Functional point method: This technique involves measuring the size of the software based on the number and complexity of the functional requirements or features that the software provides. The functional requirements or features are classified into five types: inputs, outputs, inquiries, logical internal files, and external interface files. Each type is assigned a weight based on its complexity and the total number of function points is calculated by multiplying the number of each type by its weight. The function points are then converted into effort, cost, and duration using productivity factors or historical data.
- Story point method: This technique involves measuring the size of the software based on the number and complexity of the user stories that describe the functionality or features that the software delivers to the users or customers. The user stories are assigned a relative value or points based on their complexity, amount of work, and risk or uncertainty. The total number of story points is calculated by adding up the points of all the user stories. The story points are then converted into effort, cost, and duration using velocity or historical data.
- Parametric estimation: This technique involves using mathematical models or formulas that relate the parameters of the software project to one or more independent variables or factors. The independent variables or factors are derived from historical data, industry standards, or expert opinions. The models or formulas are then applied to the current project to obtain the estimates for the parameters. This technique is useful for estimating large and complex projects with a high degree of accuracy.
