### Iterative Enhancement Models

- The iterative enhancement model is a software development life cycle (SDLC) approach that combines elements of the linear sequential model with the iterative philosophy of prototyping.
- In this model, the software is broken down into several modules which are incrementally developed and delivered .
- The development team develops the core module of the system first, which provides the basic functionality and minimal user interface.
- The core module is then tested and reviewed by the customer, who provides feedback and suggestions for improvement.
- Based on the feedback, the development team identifies the next module to be added to the system, which provides some additional functionality and enhanced user interface.
- The new module is then integrated with the existing modules, and the whole system is tested and reviewed again by the customer.
- This process is repeated until all the modules are developed and integrated, and the final system is delivered to the customer.
- The iterative enhancement model has the following advantages :
  - It allows early detection and correction of errors and defects.
  - It provides early feedback from the customer and improves customer satisfaction.
  - It reduces the risk of failure and increases the reliability of the system.
  - It accommodates changing requirements and evolving needs of the customer.
  - It facilitates reuse of existing modules and components.
- The iterative enhancement model has the following disadvantages :
  - It requires more management and coordination efforts to handle multiple iterations and modules.
  - It may lead to scope creep and budget overrun if the iterations are not well-defined and controlled.
  - It may result in poor system architecture and design if the modules are not integrated properly.
  - It may increase the complexity and maintenance cost of the system.
