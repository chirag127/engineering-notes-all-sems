### Similarity and Differences from Conventional Engineering Processes for Software Engineering

Conventional engineering processes are the methods and practices used to design, build, and maintain physical systems or products, such as bridges, buildings, machines, or vehicles. Software engineering processes are the methods and practices used to design, build, and maintain software systems or products, such as applications, websites, or databases.

Some of the similarities and differences between conventional engineering processes and software engineering processes are:

- Similarities:
  - Both are getting automated slowly. Both require in-depth knowledge of their field.
  - Both involve planning, designing, testing, and maintaining the systems or products.
  - Both follow some standards and principles to ensure the quality and reliability of the systems or products.
  - Both need to consider the requirements, constraints, and expectations of the customers and stakeholders.

- Differences:
  - Conventional engineering processes deal with physical and tangible systems or products, while software engineering processes deal with logical and intangible systems or products .
  - Conventional engineering processes have more established and mature methods and practices, while software engineering processes are more dynamic and evolving due to the rapid changes in technology and user needs .
  - Conventional engineering processes have more physical and environmental limitations and risks, while software engineering processes have more logical and conceptual limitations and risks .
  - Conventional engineering processes have higher government sector opportunity, while software engineering processes have more opportunities of foreign settlement. Conventional engineers have a more physically active role, while software engineers have a typical office job.
