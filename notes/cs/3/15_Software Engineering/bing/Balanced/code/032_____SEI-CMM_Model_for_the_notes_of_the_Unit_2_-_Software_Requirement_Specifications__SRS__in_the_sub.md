### SEI-CMM Model

- SEI stands for Software Engineering Institute, which is a research and development center at Carnegie Mellon University.
- CMM stands for Capability Maturity Model, which is a framework for assessing and improving the software development process of an organization.
- The CMM was developed by the SEI in 1987 based on a study of data collected from organizations that contracted with the U.S. Department of Defense   .
- The CMM defines five levels of organizational maturity, from level 1 (initial) to level 5 (optimizing), based on the degree of formality and optimization of the software processes  .
- The CMM also identifies key process areas (KPAs) for each maturity level, which are the areas that an organization should focus on to improve its software process capability  .
- The CMM helps organizations to:
  - Establish a common vision and understanding of software quality and process improvement among stakeholders .
  - Assess the current state of their software process and identify the gaps and weaknesses  .
  - Define the goals and priorities for software process improvement and plan the actions to achieve them  .
  - Implement the planned actions and monitor the results and feedback  .
  - Continuously learn and improve from the experience and best practices  .
- The CMM is not a software process model, but a framework that can be used with any software process model, such as waterfall, agile, or spiral .
- The CMM is also not a prescriptive or rigid standard, but a flexible and adaptable guide that can be tailored to the specific needs and context of each organization .
