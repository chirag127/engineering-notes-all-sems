### Schedule/Duration

- Schedule is the allocation of time and resources to a project or a task. Duration is the actual time taken to complete a project or a task.
- Schedule and duration are important aspects of software maintenance and software project management, as they affect the quality, cost, and customer satisfaction of software products and services.
- Schedule and duration can be estimated, planned, monitored, and controlled using various techniques and tools, such as:
  - Work breakdown structure (WBS): A hierarchical decomposition of the project scope into manageable tasks and subtasks, with assigned responsibilities, dependencies, and deliverables.
  - Gantt chart: A graphical representation of the project schedule, showing the start and end dates, durations, and dependencies of each task and subtask.
  - Critical path method (CPM): A technique to identify the longest sequence of dependent tasks that determines the minimum possible duration of the project.
  - Program evaluation and review technique (PERT): A technique to estimate the duration of each task and subtask, based on optimistic, pessimistic, and most likely scenarios, and to calculate the expected duration and variance of the project.
  - Earned value management (EVM): A technique to measure the progress and performance of the project, based on the comparison of the planned and actual schedule, cost, and scope.
- Schedule and duration can be affected by various factors, such as:
  - Requirements changes: Changes in the scope, features, or specifications of the software product or service, due to customer feedback, market demand, or technical issues.
  - Resource availability: Availability of the human, material, financial, and technical resources needed to execute the project or the task.
  - Risk management: Identification, analysis, mitigation, and monitoring of the potential threats and opportunities that may impact the project or the task.
  - Quality assurance: Verification and validation of the software product or service, to ensure that it meets the quality standards and customer expectations.
  - Communication and coordination: Exchange of information and collaboration among the project stakeholders, such as the project manager, the project team, the customer, and the suppliers.
