### Change Control Process

- Change control is a systematic approach to managing all changes made to a product or system.
- The purpose is to ensure that no unnecessary changes are made, that all changes are documented, that services are not disrupted and that resources are used efficiently.
- Change control is an important part of software maintenance and software project management.
- The change control process consists of the following steps:

  1. **Identify** the need for a change and its impact on the product or system.
  2. **Analyze** the feasibility, cost, benefits and risks of the proposed change.
  3. **Request** the approval of the change from the stakeholders or authorities.
  4. **Plan** the implementation of the change, including the schedule, resources and responsibilities.
  5. **Execute** the change according to the plan and monitor the progress and quality.
  6. **Review** the results of the change and verify that the objectives are met and the requirements are satisfied.
  7. **Close** the change and document the lessons learned and best practices.

- The change control process can be supported by various tools and techniques, such as:

  - **Change request form**: A document that captures the details of the proposed change, such as the description, justification, priority, impact, cost, benefits and risks.
  - **Change log**: A record of all the changes made to a product or system, including the date, author, status, reason and outcome of each change.
  - **Change control board**: A group of people who are responsible for reviewing and approving the change requests and overseeing the change control process.
  - **Change management software**: A software application that facilitates the tracking, management and communication of the change requests and the change control process.
