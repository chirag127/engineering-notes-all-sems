### Evolutionary Development Models

Evolutionary development models are models of software engineering that aim to deliver software systems incrementally over time, rather than in a single release. Evolutionary models are suitable for situations where the requirements are unclear, changing, or complex, and where the customer wants to use the core features of the system as soon as possible. Evolutionary models are also compatible with object-oriented software development, as the system can be partitioned into units based on objects.

There are two main types of evolutionary development models: prototyping and spiral.

#### Prototyping Model

The prototyping model is a model of software engineering that involves creating a simplified version of the system, called a prototype, to elicit feedback from the customer and refine the requirements. The prototype is not intended to be a complete or final product, but rather a demonstration of the main functionality and user interface of the system. The prototype can be either throwaway or evolutionary.

- Throwaway prototyping: The prototype is discarded after the feedback is obtained, and a new system is developed from scratch based on the refined requirements. This approach is useful for exploring different design alternatives and validating the feasibility of the system.
- Evolutionary prototyping: The prototype is gradually improved and enhanced based on the feedback, until it becomes the final product. This approach is useful for developing systems that are expected to evolve over time and adapt to changing needs.

#### Spiral Model

The spiral model is a model of software engineering that combines the iterative and incremental nature of evolutionary models with the risk analysis and evaluation of the waterfall model. The spiral model consists of four phases that are repeated for each iteration of the system: planning, risk analysis, engineering, and evaluation.

- Planning: The objectives, alternatives, and constraints of the system are defined and the resources and schedule are estimated.
- Risk analysis: The potential risks and uncertainties of the system are identified and assessed, and mitigation strategies are devised.
- Engineering: The system is designed, implemented, tested, and integrated based on the chosen alternative and the risk mitigation plan.
- Evaluation: The system is reviewed and evaluated by the customer and other stakeholders, and the feedback is used to plan the next iteration.

The spiral model is suitable for large, complex, and high-risk systems that require careful analysis and evaluation at each stage of development. The spiral model also allows for flexibility and adaptability to changing requirements and customer needs.
