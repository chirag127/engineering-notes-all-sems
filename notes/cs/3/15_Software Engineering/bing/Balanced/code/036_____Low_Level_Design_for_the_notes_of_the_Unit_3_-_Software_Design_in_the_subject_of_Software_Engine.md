### Low Level Design for the notes of the Unit 3 - Software Design in the subject of Software Engineering

Low level design (LLD) is a component-level design process that follows a step-by-step refinement process. It involves specifying the individual modules, data structures, algorithms, interfaces, and inputs/outputs of a system. It describes every module in detail by incorporating the logic behind every component in the system. It provides a micro-level design that can be implemented by the developers.

Some of the steps involved in low level design are:

- Applying object-oriented principles to the user requirements and identifying the classes, objects, attributes, methods, and relationships among them.
- Analyzing the real-world problems and breaking them down into smaller and manageable subproblems.
- Applying design patterns to solve common design problems and improve the quality, reusability, and maintainability of the code.
- Choosing the appropriate data structures and algorithms to optimize the performance, memory, and scalability of the system.
- Defining the interfaces and protocols for communication and interaction among the modules and components.
- Documenting the low level design using diagrams, such as class diagrams, sequence diagrams, state diagrams, etc.

Some of the benefits of low level design are:

- It helps to reduce the complexity and ambiguity of the system design.
- It facilitates the communication and collaboration among the developers, testers, and other stakeholders.
- It enables the verification and validation of the system functionality and quality.
- It supports the modularity, reusability, and maintainability of the code.
- It aids in the debugging and testing of the system.
