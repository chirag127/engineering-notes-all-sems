### Feasibility Study for the notes of the Unit 2 - Software Requirement Specifications (SRS) in the subject of Software Engineering

- A feasibility study is an analysis of the viability of a project or system before it is implemented.
- It helps to identify the potential benefits, costs, risks, and constraints of the project or system.
- It also helps to determine the feasibility of the project or system in terms of technical, operational, economic, social, legal, and environmental aspects.
- A feasibility study is usually conducted in the early stages of the software development life cycle (SDLC), after the problem definition and before the requirement analysis.
- A feasibility study can be classified into four types: technical feasibility, operational feasibility, economic feasibility, and social feasibility.

#### Technical feasibility
- Technical feasibility assesses the availability and suitability of the hardware, software, network, and other resources required for the project or system.
- It also evaluates the technical skills and expertise of the development team and the end-users.
- Technical feasibility helps to answer questions such as:
  - What are the technical requirements and specifications of the project or system?
  - What are the existing and emerging technologies that can be used for the project or system?
  - What are the technical challenges and risks involved in the project or system?
  - How can the technical quality and performance of the project or system be ensured and measured?

#### Operational feasibility
- Operational feasibility assesses the compatibility and integration of the project or system with the existing business processes, procedures, and policies of the organization.
- It also evaluates the usability and acceptability of the project or system by the end-users and other stakeholders.
- Operational feasibility helps to answer questions such as:
  - How will the project or system fit into the current and future operational environment of the organization?
  - How will the project or system affect the workflow, productivity, and efficiency of the organization?
  - How will the project or system meet the functional and non-functional requirements of the end-users and other stakeholders?
  - How will the project or system be maintained, supported, and updated?

#### Economic feasibility
- Economic feasibility assesses the costs and benefits of the project or system over its life cycle.
- It also evaluates the return on investment (ROI), payback period, net present value (NPV), and break-even point of the project or system.
- Economic feasibility helps to answer questions such as:
  - What are the initial and recurring costs of the project or system, such as development, implementation, operation, and maintenance costs?
  - What are the tangible and intangible benefits of the project or system, such as revenue, profit, customer satisfaction, and competitive advantage?
  - How will the project or system affect the financial performance and position of the organization?
  - How will the project or system be funded and budgeted?

#### Social feasibility
- Social feasibility assesses the impact of the project or system on the society, culture, ethics, and values of the end-users and other stakeholders.
- It also evaluates the legal, regulatory, and ethical compliance of the project or system.
- Social feasibility helps to answer questions such as:
  - How will the project or system affect the social welfare, equity, and justice of the end-users and other stakeholders?
  - How will the project or system comply with the relevant laws, regulations, standards, and codes of conduct?
  - How will the project or system address the ethical, moral, and social issues and dilemmas that may arise from the project or system?
