### SQA Plans for the notes of the Unit 2 - Software Requirement Specifications (SRS) in the subject of Software Engineering

- A software quality assurance (SQA) plan is a document that describes the standards, processes, procedures, and tools that will be used to ensure the quality of a software product or service.
- A SQA plan is usually prepared by the software quality assurance team or the software quality manager in collaboration with the project manager, the software development team, and the customer or end-user.
- A SQA plan should cover the following aspects of software quality assurance:

  - The scope and objectives of the SQA activities and the roles and responsibilities of the SQA team members.
  - The quality standards and criteria that will be applied to the software product or service, such as functional requirements, non-functional requirements, design specifications, coding standards, testing methods, etc.
  - The quality metrics and indicators that will be used to measure and monitor the quality of the software product or service, such as defect density, defect severity, customer satisfaction, etc.
  - The quality audits and reviews that will be conducted to verify and validate the compliance of the software product or service with the quality standards and criteria, such as peer reviews, inspections, walkthroughs, etc.
  - The quality tools and techniques that will be used to support the SQA activities, such as software configuration management, software testing, software documentation, etc.
  - The quality risks and issues that may affect the quality of the software product or service, and the mitigation and contingency plans to address them.
  - The quality improvement and corrective actions that will be taken to prevent or resolve the quality problems and defects, and the feedback and learning mechanisms to incorporate the lessons learned into the SQA process.

- A SQA plan should be aligned with the software development life cycle (SDLC) model and the project management plan, and should be updated and revised as the software project progresses and changes.
- A SQA plan should be communicated and agreed upon by all the stakeholders involved in the software project, and should be reviewed and approved by the software quality assurance authority or the senior management.
