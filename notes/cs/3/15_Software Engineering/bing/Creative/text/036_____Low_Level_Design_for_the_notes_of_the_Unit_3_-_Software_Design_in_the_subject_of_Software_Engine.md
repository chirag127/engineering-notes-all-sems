### Low Level Design for the notes of the Unit 3 - Software Design in the subject of Software Engineering

- Low-level design (LLD) is a component-level design process that follows a step-by-step refinement process.
- LLD involves specifying the individual modules, data structures, algorithms, interfaces, and inputs/outputs of a software system or component.
- LLD describes every module in detail by incorporating the logic behind every component in the system. It delves deep into every specification of every system, providing a micro-level design.
- LLD is based on the high-level design (HLD) that defines the overall architecture and functionality of the system.
- LLD is usually done by the developers who are responsible for implementing the system or component.
- LLD can be done using different approaches, such as object-oriented principles, design patterns, and UML diagrams.
- LLD helps to ensure the quality, reliability, maintainability, and performance of the software system or component.
- LLD also helps to facilitate the testing, debugging, and integration of the software system or component.
