### Analysis for the notes of Unit 2 - Software Requirement Specifications (SRS) in the subject of Software Engineering

- Software Requirement Specifications (SRS) is a document that describes the requirements of a software system, such as its functionality, quality, constraints, and assumptions.
- The purpose of SRS is to provide a clear and complete description of the software system to the stakeholders, such as the customers, developers, testers, and managers.
- The benefits of SRS are:
  - It helps to avoid ambiguity and misunderstanding among the stakeholders.
  - It helps to reduce the risk of errors, defects, and changes in the software development process.
  - It helps to facilitate the verification, validation, and testing of the software system.
  - It helps to provide a basis for estimating the cost, time, and resources of the software project.
- The characteristics of a good SRS are:
  - Correct: It should be consistent with the actual needs and expectations of the stakeholders.
  - Complete: It should cover all the requirements of the software system, without leaving any gaps or uncertainties.
  - Consistent: It should not have any contradictions or conflicts among the requirements or with other documents.
  - Clear: It should be easy to understand and unambiguous for the intended audience.
  - Verifiable: It should be possible to check whether the software system meets the requirements or not.
  - Modifiable: It should be easy to update and maintain as the requirements change over time.
  - Traceable: It should be possible to track the origin and rationale of each requirement and its relationship with other requirements and documents.
- The process of developing SRS involves the following steps:
  - Elicitation: It is the process of gathering the requirements from the stakeholders, such as by interviews, surveys, observation, brainstorming, etc.
  - Analysis: It is the process of analyzing the requirements to identify the scope, feasibility, risks, and priorities of the software system.
  - Specification: It is the process of documenting the requirements in a formal and structured way, such as by using natural language, diagrams, models, etc.
  - Validation: It is the process of checking the quality and accuracy of the SRS, such as by reviews, inspections, walkthroughs, etc.
  - Management: It is the process of controlling and tracking the changes and evolution of the SRS, such as by using version control, configuration management, change management, etc.
