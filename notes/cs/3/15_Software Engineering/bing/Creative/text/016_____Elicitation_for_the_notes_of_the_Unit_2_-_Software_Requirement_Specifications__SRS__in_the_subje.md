### Elicitation for the notes of the Unit 2 - Software Requirement Specifications (SRS) in the subject of Software Engineering

- Elicitation is the process of gathering, analyzing, and validating the needs and requirements of the stakeholders for a software system.
- Elicitation involves various activities, such as identifying the stakeholders, defining the scope and objectives, collecting and documenting the requirements, verifying and validating the requirements, and managing changes and conflicts.
- Elicitation techniques are the methods and tools used to elicit the requirements from the stakeholders. Some of the common elicitation techniques are:
  - Interviews: A direct and interactive way of asking questions and obtaining answers from the stakeholders.
  - Questionnaires: A structured and standardized way of collecting information from a large number of stakeholders.
  - Observation: A passive and non-intrusive way of understanding the current situation and problems of the stakeholders by watching them perform their tasks.
  - Workshops: A collaborative and participatory way of involving multiple stakeholders in brainstorming, prioritizing, and resolving the requirements.
  - Prototyping: A dynamic and iterative way of creating and demonstrating a mock-up or a model of the system to elicit feedback and refine the requirements.
  - Scenarios: A descriptive and narrative way of illustrating the use cases and user stories of the system to elicit the functional and non-functional requirements.
  - Document analysis: A systematic and analytical way of reviewing and extracting the requirements from existing documents, such as contracts, standards, regulations, manuals, etc.
- Elicitation challenges are the difficulties and risks that may arise during the elicitation process and affect the quality and completeness of the requirements. Some of the common elicitation challenges are:
  - Stakeholder availability and commitment: The stakeholders may not have enough time, interest, or authority to participate in the elicitation process or provide accurate and consistent information.
  - Stakeholder communication and collaboration: The stakeholders may have different backgrounds, perspectives, expectations, and preferences that may cause misunderstandings, conflicts, or disagreements among them or with the analysts.
  - Stakeholder knowledge and experience: The stakeholders may not have sufficient knowledge or experience about the domain, the problem, or the solution, or they may have tacit or implicit knowledge that is difficult to articulate or capture.
  - Requirement volatility and complexity: The requirements may change frequently or unpredictably due to changing business needs, market conditions, user feedback, or technical constraints, or they may be complex, ambiguous, or incomplete.
