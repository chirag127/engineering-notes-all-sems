### Elicitation
- Elicitation is the process of gathering, understanding, and documenting the needs and expectations of the stakeholders for a software system.
- Elicitation is also known as requirements engineering, requirements analysis, or requirements discovery.
- Elicitation is a crucial activity in software engineering, as it helps to define the scope, quality, and feasibility of the software project.
- Elicitation involves various techniques, such as interviews, questionnaires, workshops, observation, prototyping, brainstorming, etc.
- Elicitation aims to identify the functional, non-functional, and domain requirements of the software system, as well as the constraints, assumptions, and risks associated with the project.
- Elicitation also involves validating, prioritizing, and managing the requirements throughout the software development life cycle.

Some of the main challenges and issues in elicitation are:

- Incomplete or ambiguous requirements
- Conflicting or inconsistent requirements
- Volatile or changing requirements
- Stakeholder involvement and communication
- Cultural and social factors
- Technical and domain knowledge
- Scope creep and gold plating

Some of the best practices and guidelines for elicitation are:

- Define the elicitation goals and scope clearly and explicitly
- Identify and involve the relevant stakeholders and users
- Use multiple and complementary elicitation techniques
- Document and organize the elicitation results
- Validate and verify the elicited requirements
- Manage and trace the requirements changes
- Communicate and collaborate with the stakeholders and users
