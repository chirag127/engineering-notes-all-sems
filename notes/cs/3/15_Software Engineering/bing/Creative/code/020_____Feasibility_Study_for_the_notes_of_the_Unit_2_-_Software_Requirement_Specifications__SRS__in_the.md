### Feasibility Study

- A feasibility study is an analysis of the viability of a project before it is undertaken.
- It evaluates the technical, economic, social, legal, and operational aspects of the project and determines whether it is worth pursuing or not.
- A feasibility study helps to identify the risks, benefits, costs, and resources required for the project and provides a basis for decision making.
- A feasibility study is usually conducted in the early stages of the software development life cycle (SDLC) and is part of the software requirement analysis process.
- A feasibility study consists of the following steps:

  - Define the problem or opportunity: This step involves identifying the need or gap that the project aims to address and defining the objectives and scope of the project.
  - Conduct a preliminary analysis: This step involves gathering relevant information and data about the problem or opportunity, the existing system, the users, the stakeholders, and the environment.
  - Identify alternative solutions: This step involves generating and evaluating different possible solutions to the problem or opportunity, considering the technical, economic, social, legal, and operational factors.
  - Select the best solution: This step involves choosing the most feasible and desirable solution based on the criteria and constraints of the project.
  - Prepare a feasibility report: This step involves documenting the findings and recommendations of the feasibility study in a clear and concise manner.

- A feasibility study can be classified into four types based on the criteria used to evaluate the project:

  - Technical feasibility: This type of feasibility study assesses whether the project is technically possible and feasible with the available technology, skills, and resources.
  - Economic feasibility: This type of feasibility study assesses whether the project is economically viable and feasible with the expected costs, benefits, and returns.
  - Social feasibility: This type of feasibility study assesses whether the project is socially acceptable and feasible with the ethical, cultural, and environmental implications.
  - Operational feasibility: This type of feasibility study assesses whether the project is operationally feasible and compatible with the existing system, processes, and procedures.

- A feasibility study is an important tool for software requirement analysis as it helps to:

  - Define the problem or opportunity clearly and precisely
  - Identify and prioritize the user and stakeholder needs and expectations
  - Establish the feasibility and desirability of the project
  - Reduce the uncertainty and risk of the project
  - Provide a basis for planning and estimating the project
  - Communicate and justify the project to the management and the stakeholders
