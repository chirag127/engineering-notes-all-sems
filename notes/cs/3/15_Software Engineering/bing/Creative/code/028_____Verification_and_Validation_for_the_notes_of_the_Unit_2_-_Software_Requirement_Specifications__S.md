# Verification and Validation

- Verification and validation (V&V) are the processes of checking that a software system meets specifications and requirements and that it fulfills its intended purpose    .
- Verification is the process of determining if the software is designed and developed as per the specified requirements . It answers the question: "Are we building the product right?".
- Validation is the process of checking if the software (end product) has met the client’s true needs and expectations . It answers the question: "Are we building the right product?".
- V&V may also be referred to as software quality control  .
- V&V are essential for ensuring the quality, reliability, and usability of software systems    .
- V&V can be performed at different stages of the software development life cycle, such as planning, analysis, design, implementation, testing, and maintenance    .
- V&V can involve various techniques, such as reviews, inspections, walkthroughs, audits, testing, analysis, simulation, and prototyping    .
- V&V can be applied to different types of software artifacts, such as requirements, design, code, documentation, and user interface    .
- V&V can be performed by different roles, such as developers, testers, analysts, customers, users, and independent evaluators    .
- V&V can be classified into static and dynamic methods    .
  - Static methods are those that do not require the execution of the software, such as reviews, inspections, and analysis    .
  - Dynamic methods are those that require the execution of the software, such as testing, simulation, and prototyping    .
- V&V can also be classified into formal and informal methods    .
  - Formal methods are those that are based on rigorous mathematical models and proofs, such as formal specification, verification, and validation    .
  - Informal methods are those that are based on intuition, experience, and heuristics, such as informal reviews, testing, and user feedback    .
