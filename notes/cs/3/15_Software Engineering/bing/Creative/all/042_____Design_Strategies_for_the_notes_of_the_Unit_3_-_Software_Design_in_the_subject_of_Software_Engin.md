# Design Strategies for the notes of the Unit 3 - Software Design in the subject of Software Engineering

- Design strategies are the approaches that are taken to design a software system that meets the requirements and specifications .
- Design strategies involve making decisions about the architecture, components, modules, interfaces, and data for a software system.
- There are three major system design strategies: structured design, function-oriented design, and object-oriented design.
  - Structured design is a design strategy that focuses on the decomposition of a system into modules that have well-defined inputs and outputs.
  - Function-oriented design is a design strategy that focuses on the identification and grouping of functions that are performed by the system.
  - Object-oriented design is a design strategy that focuses on the identification and grouping of objects that have data and behavior.
- There are two common ways of applying design strategies: top-down design and bottom-up design .
  - Top-down design is a design strategy that starts with a high-level view of the system and gradually breaks it down into smaller, more manageable components.
  - Bottom-up design is a design strategy that starts with the low-level components and gradually integrates them into higher-level components.
- Design strategies can also be influenced by the software architecture patterns that are used to describe the structure and behavior of a software system.
  - Software architecture patterns are reusable schemes that address common software design challenges and goals.
  - Some examples of software architecture patterns are: layered pattern, client-server pattern, microservices pattern, event-driven pattern, etc.
