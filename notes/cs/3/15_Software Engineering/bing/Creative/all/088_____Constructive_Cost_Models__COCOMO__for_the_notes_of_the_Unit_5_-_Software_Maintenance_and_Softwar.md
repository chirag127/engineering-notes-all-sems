# Constructive Cost Models (COCOMO)

- COCOMO stands for **COnstructive COst MOdel**.
- It is a **procedural software cost estimation model** developed by **Barry W. Boehm**.
- It is based on the **number of lines of code (LOC)** for software product development .
- It is used to **predict the effort, cost, time, and quality** of software projects .
- It uses a **regression formula** with **model parameters** derived from **historical project data** .
- There are three types of COCOMO models: **Basic, Intermediate, and Detailed** .
- The Basic COCOMO model uses a **single formula** to estimate the **effort** and **duration** of a project based on the **project size** and a **mode** (Organic, Semi-Detached, or Embedded) .
- The Intermediate COCOMO model uses the **same formula** as the Basic model, but **adjusts** the effort and duration estimates based on **15 cost drivers** that affect the project complexity .
- The Detailed COCOMO model uses the **same formula** and **cost drivers** as the Intermediate model, but **divides** the project into **different phases** and **modules** and estimates the effort and duration for each of them separately .
- The COCOMO model has been **revised** and **updated** since its first publication in 1981. The latest version is **COCOMO II**, which was released in 2000 .
- COCOMO II has **four submodels**: **Application Composition, Early Design, Post-Architecture, and Reuse**.
- COCOMO II also uses **source lines of code (SLOC)** as the primary input, but allows for **different types** of SLOC (such as logical, physical, or non-commented) and **different languages**.
- COCOMO II also uses **17 cost drivers** and **five scale factors** to adjust the effort and duration estimates based on the project characteristics.
- COCOMO II also provides **ranges** of estimates (such as most likely, optimistic, or pessimistic) and **confidence levels** for the estimates.
