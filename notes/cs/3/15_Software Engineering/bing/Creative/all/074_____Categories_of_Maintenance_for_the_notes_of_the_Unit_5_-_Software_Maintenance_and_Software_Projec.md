# Categories of Maintenance

Software maintenance is the process of modifying and updating a software system after it has been delivered to the client. Software maintenance is necessary to ensure that the software continues to meet the changing requirements and expectations of the users and the environment. Software maintenance can be classified into four categories, namely, corrective, adaptive, perfective, and preventive. Each category has a different purpose, scope, and cost.

## Corrective Maintenance

Corrective maintenance is the process of fixing errors or defects that are discovered in the software system during its operation. These errors can be caused by design flaws, coding mistakes, hardware failures, or user input. Corrective maintenance aims to restore the software system to its intended functionality and performance. Corrective maintenance is usually reactive, meaning that it is performed after the errors have been reported by the users or detected by the system. Corrective maintenance is the most common and expensive type of maintenance, accounting for about 20% of the total maintenance effort.

## Adaptive Maintenance

Adaptive maintenance is the process of modifying the software system to cope with changes in the external environment, such as new hardware, operating systems, platforms, standards, or regulations. Adaptive maintenance aims to keep the software system compatible and interoperable with the evolving technology and business needs. Adaptive maintenance is usually proactive, meaning that it is performed before the changes in the environment affect the software system. Adaptive maintenance is the second most common and expensive type of maintenance, accounting for about 25% of the total maintenance effort.

## Perfective Maintenance

Perfective maintenance is the process of enhancing the software system to improve its functionality, performance, usability, reliability, or quality. Perfective maintenance aims to meet the changing or increasing expectations and requirements of the users and the stakeholders. Perfective maintenance is usually optional, meaning that it is performed based on the feedback, suggestions, or requests from the users or the market. Perfective maintenance is the third most common and expensive type of maintenance, accounting for about 50% of the total maintenance effort.

## Preventive Maintenance

Preventive maintenance is the process of modifying the software system to prevent or reduce the occurrence of future errors or defects. Preventive maintenance aims to improve the maintainability, readability, modularity, and documentation of the software system. Preventive maintenance is usually planned, meaning that it is performed based on the analysis, prediction, or estimation of the potential problems or risks in the software system. Preventive maintenance is the least common and expensive type of maintenance, accounting for about 5% of the total maintenance effort.
