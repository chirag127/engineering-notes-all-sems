# Similarity and Differences from Conventional Engineering Processes for Software Engineering

## Similarities:

- Both are getting automated slowly.
- Both require in-depth knowledge of their field.
- Both involve thinking, designing, and building tasks.
- Both follow some principles and standards to ensure quality and reliability.
- Both need to consider the requirements, constraints, and trade-offs of the problem domain.
- Both use models, tools, and methods to support the development process.
- Both require testing, verification, and validation of the products.
- Both need to maintain and update the products to meet the changing needs of the customers.

## Differences:

- Software engineering is only related to programming, whereas conventional engineering covers a broader range of disciplines such as civil, mechanical, electrical, etc.
- Software engineering products are intangible and abstract, whereas conventional engineering products are tangible and concrete.
- Software engineering products are more complex and difficult to measure, whereas conventional engineering products are simpler and easier to measure.
- Software engineering products are more prone to errors, defects, and failures, whereas conventional engineering products are more robust and reliable.
- Software engineering products have shorter life cycles and faster obsolescence, whereas conventional engineering products have longer life cycles and slower obsolescence.
- Software engineering products are more flexible and adaptable, whereas conventional engineering products are more rigid and fixed.
- Software engineering products are more dependent on human factors such as skills, creativity, and communication, whereas conventional engineering products are more dependent on physical factors such as materials, forces, and laws.
- Software engineering products have more opportunities of foreign settlement, whereas conventional engineering products have more opportunities of government sector.
