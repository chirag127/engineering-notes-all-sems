# Feasibility Study

- A feasibility study is an analysis of the viability of a project before it is undertaken.
- It evaluates the technical, economic, social, legal, and operational aspects of the project and determines whether it is worth pursuing or not.
- A feasibility study helps to identify the risks, benefits, costs, and resources required for the project and provides a basis for decision making.
- A feasibility study is usually conducted in the early stages of the software development life cycle (SDLC) and is part of the software requirement analysis process.
- A feasibility study can be classified into four types: technical feasibility, economic feasibility, operational feasibility, and social feasibility.

## Technical Feasibility

- Technical feasibility assesses whether the proposed system can be developed and implemented using the available technology, hardware, software, and human resources.
- Technical feasibility considers the following factors:
  - The current state of the technology and its future trends
  - The compatibility and interoperability of the system with the existing systems and platforms
  - The availability and reliability of the hardware and software components and tools
  - The skills and expertise of the development team and the end-users
  - The technical risks and challenges involved in the development and maintenance of the system
- Technical feasibility can be measured using criteria such as performance, scalability, security, usability, maintainability, and portability of the system.

## Economic Feasibility

- Economic feasibility assesses whether the proposed system is financially viable and beneficial for the organization or the client.
- Economic feasibility considers the following factors:
  - The estimated cost of the development, implementation, operation, and maintenance of the system
  - The expected benefits and returns from the system in terms of revenue, productivity, efficiency, quality, and customer satisfaction
  - The payback period and the return on investment (ROI) of the system
  - The opportunity cost and the alternative solutions for the problem
  - The economic risks and uncertainties involved in the project
- Economic feasibility can be measured using techniques such as cost-benefit analysis, cost-effectiveness analysis, net present value, internal rate of return, and break-even analysis.

## Operational Feasibility

- Operational feasibility assesses whether the proposed system can be integrated and used effectively in the existing operational environment and organizational culture.
- Operational feasibility considers the following factors:
  - The alignment of the system with the organizational goals, objectives, policies, and procedures
  - The compatibility and acceptance of the system by the end-users and the stakeholders
  - The impact of the system on the existing workflows, processes, and functions
  - The availability and adequacy of the training and support for the system
  - The operational risks and issues involved in the deployment and use of the system
- Operational feasibility can be measured using criteria such as functionality, reliability, availability, flexibility, and adaptability of the system.

## Social Feasibility

- Social feasibility assesses whether the proposed system is ethically, legally, and socially acceptable and responsible for the society and the environment.
- Social feasibility considers the following factors:
  - The compliance of the system with the relevant laws, regulations, standards, and codes of conduct
  - The ethical implications and consequences of the system for the individuals, groups, and communities involved or affected by the system
  - The social benefits and costs of the system in terms of social welfare, equity, justice, and human rights
  - The environmental impact and sustainability of the system in terms of resource consumption, waste generation, pollution, and carbon footprint
  - The social risks and challenges involved in the development and implementation of the system
- Social feasibility can be measured using criteria such as legality, morality, accountability, transparency, and social responsibility of the system.
