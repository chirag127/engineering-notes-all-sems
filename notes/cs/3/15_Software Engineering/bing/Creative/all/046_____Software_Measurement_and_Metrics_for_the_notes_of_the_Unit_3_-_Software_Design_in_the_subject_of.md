# Software Measurement and Metrics

- Software measurement and metrics are the means of assessing the quality, cost, and productivity of software development and maintenance activities.
- Software measurement is the process of collecting, analyzing, and reporting data related to software products or processes.
- Software metrics are the quantitative indicators of software attributes, such as size, complexity, reliability, maintainability, etc.
- Software measurement and metrics can help software engineers and managers to:
  - Plan and estimate software projects
  - Monitor and control software processes
  - Evaluate and improve software products
  - Benchmark and compare software practices
- Software measurement and metrics can be classified into three types:
  - Product metrics: These metrics measure the characteristics of software products, such as lines of code, function points, cyclomatic complexity, defect density, etc.
  - Process metrics: These metrics measure the effectiveness and efficiency of software processes, such as development time, effort, productivity, quality, etc.
  - Project metrics: These metrics measure the performance and progress of software projects, such as schedule, budget, scope, risk, etc.
- Software measurement and metrics should follow some principles, such as:
  - Relevance: The metrics should be relevant to the goals and objectives of the software project or organization.
  - Validity: The metrics should be valid and reliable, meaning that they measure what they are intended to measure and produce consistent results.
  - Simplicity: The metrics should be simple and easy to understand, collect, and analyze.
  - Cost-effectiveness: The metrics should be worth the cost and effort of measurement and analysis.
  - Actionability: The metrics should provide useful and actionable information for decision making and improvement.
