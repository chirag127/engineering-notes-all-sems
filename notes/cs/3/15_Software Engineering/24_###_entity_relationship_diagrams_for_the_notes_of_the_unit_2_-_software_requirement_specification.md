### Entity Relationship Diagrams for the notes of the Unit 2 - Software Requirement Specifications (SRS) in the subject of Software Engineering

An Entity Relationship Diagram (ERD) is a graphical representation of entities and their relationships to each other, used in software engineering to represent the structure of data in a database. The following are some of the key concepts related to ERDs:

1. Entities: An entity is an object or concept in the real world that can be represented in a database. Examples of entities include customers, products, and orders.

2. Attributes: Attributes are characteristics of entities that describe the data stored in the database. Examples of attributes include the name of a customer, the price of a product, and the date of an order.

3. Relationships: Relationships describe the connections between entities in the database. Examples of relationships include a customer placing an order, a product being part of an order, and a customer having multiple orders.

4. Cardinality: Cardinality refers to the number of instances of one entity that can be related to instances of another entity. Examples of cardinality include one-to-one, one-to-many, and many-to-many relationships.

5. Primary and Foreign Keys: Primary keys are unique identifiers for entities in the database, and foreign keys are used to link entities in relationships.

6. Normalization: Normalization is the process of organizing the data in a database to minimize redundancy and improve data integrity. ERDs are used to design and implement normalized databases.

In this unit, you will learn about ERDs and how they are used in software engineering. You will also learn about the key concepts related to ERDs, including entities, attributes, relationships, cardinality, primary and foreign keys, and normalization.

This unit will provide a foundation for understanding the principles and practices of database design, and for exploring the various concepts and techniques used in the field of Software Engineering.
