### Coupling and Cohesion Measures for the notes of the Unit 3 - Software Design in the subject of Software Engineering

Coupling and Cohesion are two important concepts in software design that are covered in Unit 3 - Software Design in the subject of Software Engineering.

Coupling refers to the degree to which one module or component of a software system depends on another. High coupling means that one component is tightly connected to another, while low coupling means that the components are loosely connected. High coupling can make a system more difficult to maintain and modify, while low coupling can make a system more flexible and easier to maintain.

Cohesion refers to the degree to which the elements within a single module or component work together to achieve a single, well-defined purpose. High cohesion means that the elements within a component work together to achieve a single purpose, while low cohesion means that the elements within a component are loosely related and do not work together to achieve a single purpose. High cohesion can make a system easier to understand and maintain, while low cohesion can make a system more difficult to understand and maintain.

In the context of Unit 3 - Software Design in the subject of Software Engineering, students will learn about coupling and cohesion and how they impact the design of a software system. This may include working with examples of coupling and cohesion in software design, analyzing the benefits and limitations of different coupling and cohesion measures, and learning how to apply coupling and cohesion measures to real-world software development projects.

In summary, in Unit 3 - Software Design in the subject of Software Engineering, students will learn about Coupling and Cohesion, two important concepts in software design. They will learn about the impact of coupling and cohesion on the design of a software system, and will learn how to apply coupling and cohesion measures to real-world software development projects.
