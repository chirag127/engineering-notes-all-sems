### Iterative Enhancement Models for the notes of the Unit 1 - Introduction to Software Engineering in the subject of Software Engineering.

1. Waterfall Model:
- Requirements are gathered before design
- Each phase must be complete before next phase begins
- Little flexibility and testing is done in the end
- Not suitable for projects with volatile requirements

2. Incremental Model:
- Requirements are broken into increments
- Each increment is like a mini waterfall model
- Increments are released sequentially
- Error handling is better and high risk components are developed first
- More flexible than waterfall model

3. Spiral Model:
- Software is developed in iterative phases called spirals
- Each spiral involves planning, risk analysis, engineering and evaluation
- Model promotes reuse of prototypes and incremental releases
- Risks are addressed early and continuous quality checks are done
- Most suited for large, expensive and complicated software projects

4. Agile Methods:
- Broken into sprints of 2 weeks to 1 month
- Requirements are gathered at the beginning of each sprint
- Emphasizes on code adaptability to changing requirements
- Close customer involvement and evaluates the end product early
- Examples: Scrum, Extreme Programming, Lean Software Development, etc.
- Best suited for projects with volatile or ambiguous requirements
