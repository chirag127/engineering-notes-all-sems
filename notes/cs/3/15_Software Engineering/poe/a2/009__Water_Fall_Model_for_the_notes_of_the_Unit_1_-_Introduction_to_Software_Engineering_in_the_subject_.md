### Waterfall Model

- The waterfall model is a sequential design process, often used in software development processes, in which progress is seen as flowing steadily downwards (like a waterfall) through the phases of initiation, analysis, design, construction, testing, production/implementation, and maintenance.
- The waterfall model provides a structured approach to software development that begins at the system level and progresses through analysis, design, coding, testing, and support.
- The waterfall model insists on proceeding methodically from one phase to the next in a sequential manner. No phase can begin until the previous phase is complete.
- The waterfall model is a popular version of the systems development life cycle model for software engineering.
- The major drawback of the waterfall model is that it does not allow for much reflection or revision. Once an early stage is completed, it is difficult to revisit it.
- The waterfall model is best suited for projects where the requirements are very well understood and fixed, or for large projects where thorough planning and risk mitigation are important. However, for projects where requirements are at risk of changing, an iterative approach may be more suitable.
