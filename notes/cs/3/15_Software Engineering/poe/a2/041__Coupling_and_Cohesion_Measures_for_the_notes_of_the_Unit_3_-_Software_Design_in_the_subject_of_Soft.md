### Coupling and Cohesion Measures for the notes of the Unit 3 - Software Design in the subject of Software Engineering

- Coupling refers to the degree of interdependence between modules or components. Lower coupling is desired as it reduces interdependence and increases modularity. Some measures of coupling are:
- Fan-in: Number of modules that send data to a module. Higher fan-in implies higher coupling.
- Fan-out: Number of modules that receive data from a module. Higher fan-out implies higher coupling.
- Data coupling: When modules share data. Tightly coupled.
- Stamp coupling: When modules share a global data area and reference each other's state information. Tightly coupled.
- Control coupling: When one module controls the logic of another module. Tightly coupled.

- Cohesion refers to the degree to which the elements inside a module belong together. Higher cohesion is desired as it increases the module's functionality and understandability. Some measures of cohesion are:
- Communicational cohesion: When modules share an output or input. Loosely coupled.
- Sequential cohesion: When modules follow a sequence. Moderately coupled.
- Procedural cohesion: When modules are bound by a procedure. Higher cohesion.
- Logical cohesion: When modules are bound by a common theme/logic. Higher cohesion.
- Functional cohesion: When modules collectively realize one function. Highest cohesion.
