### Similarity and Differences from Conventional Engineering Processes

- Software engineering borrows many concepts and practices from conventional engineering disciplines. Some of the key similarities are:
    - Requirements analysis: Understanding the requirements and constraints.
    - Design: Coming up with a design that meets the requirements.
    - Testing: Verifying and validating the software/system.
    - Quality control: Following processes and standards to ensure high quality.
- However, there are also some key differences from conventional engineering:
    - Software is intangible: Software cannot be physically sensed or touched like other engineering artifacts. This makes it harder to understand, specify, and verify.
    - Ease of change: It is relatively easy and inexpensive to change software. This can be an advantage but also introduces challenges in managing stability and consistency.
    - Inherent complexity: Software systems are often complex with emergent behaviors that are hard to fully analyze and understand. This makes it difficult to completely eliminate defects.
    - Tight coupling: Software components are often highly interconnected, so a change in one component can affect others in unpredictable ways. This makes software hard to modularize and reuse.
- To address these differences, software engineering has developed or adapted various processes, techniques, and tools to enable the engineering of high-quality software solutions. The key goal is to manage the complexity and risks inherent in software projects.
