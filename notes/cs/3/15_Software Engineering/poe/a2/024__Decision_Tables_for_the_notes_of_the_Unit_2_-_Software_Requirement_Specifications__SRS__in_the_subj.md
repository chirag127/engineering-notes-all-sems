### Decision Tables for Software Requirement Specifications (SRS)

1. Decision tables are a useful technique for specifying complex logical conditions and their outcomes.
2. They are organized into columns and rows. The first column lists conditions or logical inputs. Subsequent columns list possible outcomes or actions.
3. Each row specifies a separate set of conditions and corresponding outcomes.
4. Decision tables are commonly used to specify the action or output of a system based on combinations of inputs or conditions.
5. They are easy to read and understand and can be directly translated into logic as conditional expressions or control flows in the implementation.
6. Example: A decision table to specify the fare for a passenger based on age and travel class.

    Condition         Economy        Business
    Age <= 12        10% discount   15% discount
    Age >= 60        5% discount    10% discount
    Otherwise        Full fare      Double fare

7. Guidelines for creating good decision tables:
    - Use clear, concise wording for conditions and actions.
    - List conditions in order of priority (most important first).
    - Include explicit outcomes or actions for all possible combinations of conditions.
    - Keep tables simple and avoid combining multiple conditions or actions in one cell.
    - Provide headers to indicate the purpose of each column.
    - Include units or parameters when appropriate.
