
### Evolutionary Development Models

1. Evolutionary development models are software development models that involve the continuous evolution of a software product over time. 
2. This model is based on the idea that it is easier to evolve a software product over time than to start from scratch.
3. This approach involves the incremental development of a software product in small, iterative steps.
4. The aim of evolutionary development models is to produce a software product that meets user needs while minimizing cost and time.
5. This model is often used when the requirements of a software product are not known in advance, or when the requirements are rapidly changing.
6. This model is also used when the development team needs to quickly respond to changes in the market or customer needs.
7. The evolutionary development model is an iterative process that involves the following steps:
    - Analysis: Identifying the requirements of the software product and the user needs.
    - Design: Designing the architecture and components of the software product.
    - Implementation: Coding and testing the software product.
    - Deployment: Deploying the software product to its intended users.
    - Maintenance: Maintaining the software product over time.
8. Advantages of the evolutionary development model include:
    - Faster time to market: The iterative nature of the model allows for faster development and deployment of the software product.
    - Cost savings: The incremental development approach allows for cost savings as only the necessary components of the software product are developed.
    - Flexibility: The iterative nature of the model allows for changes in requirements to be easily incorporated.
    - Quality: The incremental development approach allows for the quality of the software product to be monitored and improved over time.
9. Disadvantages of the evolutionary development model include:
    - Complexity: The iterative nature of the model can lead to a complex codebase.
    - Risk: The incremental development approach can lead to increased risk as changes are made to the software product over time.
    - Cost: The iterative nature of the model can lead to increased costs as more resources are required for the development and maintenance of the software product.
