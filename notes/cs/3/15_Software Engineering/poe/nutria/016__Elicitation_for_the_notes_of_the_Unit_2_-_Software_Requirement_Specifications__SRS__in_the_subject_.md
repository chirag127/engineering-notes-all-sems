
### Elicitation for the notes of the Unit 2 - Software Requirement Specifications (SRS) in the subject of Software Engineering

1. The process of elicitation is the gathering of requirements from stakeholders and users for the purpose of creating a software system.
2. It involves gathering information about the system, its users, and their needs.
3. It is important to have a clear understanding of the problem and the solution before beginning the elicitation process.
4. The goal of elicitation is to identify the stakeholders and users, their goals and objectives, and the requirements for the system.
5. Elicitation techniques include interviews, surveys, focus groups, workshops, and observation.
6. The elicitation process should include a review of existing documentation, such as requirements documents, user manuals, and design documents.
7. It is important to identify the stakeholders and users, their goals and objectives, and the requirements for the system.
8. The requirements should be documented in a Software Requirements Specification (SRS).
9. The SRS should include the functional and non-functional requirements of the system, as well as any constraints that must be met.
10. The SRS should be reviewed by the stakeholders and users to ensure accuracy and completeness.
