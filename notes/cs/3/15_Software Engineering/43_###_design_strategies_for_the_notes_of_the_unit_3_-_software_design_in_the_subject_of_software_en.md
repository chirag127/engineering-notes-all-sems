### Design Strategies for the notes of the Unit 3 - Software Design in the subject of Software Engineering

Design Strategies are approaches used to develop the design of a software system. In the Unit 3 - Software Design in the subject of Software Engineering, students will learn about different design strategies and how they can be applied to software development projects.

Some common design strategies include:

1. Top-Down Design: A design strategy that starts with a high-level view of the software system and breaks it down into smaller, more manageable components.

2. Bottom-Up Design: A design strategy that starts with the smallest components of the software system and builds them up into a complete system.

3. Iterative Design: A design strategy that involves multiple cycles of design and refinement, with each cycle building on the results of the previous cycle.

4. Model-Driven Design: A design strategy that involves creating models of the software system, including data models, process models, and state models, and using these models to guide the design of the software system.

In the Unit 3 - Software Design in the subject of Software Engineering, students will learn about these and other design strategies, and will learn how to choose the most appropriate design strategy for a given software development project. They will also learn about the benefits and limitations of different design strategies, and will learn how to use design strategies to develop effective software designs.

They will learn about common design strategies, including Top-Down Design, Bottom-Up Design, Iterative Design, and Model-Driven Design, and will learn how to choose the most appropriate design strategy for a given software development project. They will also learn about the benefits and limitations of different design strategies, and will learn how to use design strategies to develop effective software designs.
