### Backward Chaining

Backward chaining is a method of reasoning used in artificial intelligence and is commonly used in expert systems. It is a form of inferencing that starts with the goal or conclusion and works backward to determine what facts must be true in order to reach that goal.

Here are some key points to remember about backward chaining:

1. Backward chaining is used in rule-based systems to determine what facts must be true in order to reach a specific goal or conclusion.
2. It starts with the goal and works backward, using the rules of the system to determine what facts must be true in order to reach that goal.
3. Backward chaining is commonly used in expert systems, where the system has a large knowledge base of rules and facts.
4. It is a form of inferencing, which is the process of drawing conclusions from available information.
5. Backward chaining can be contrasted with forward chaining, which starts with the known facts and works forward to determine what conclusions can be reached.
