### Backtracking Search

Backtracking search is a depth-first search algorithm that is used to solve problems with constraints. It is a form of recursive problem-solving that incrementally builds candidates to the solutions and abandons a candidate ("backtracks") as soon as it determines that the candidate cannot possibly be completed to a valid solution.

Some key points to remember about backtracking search are:

1. Backtracking search is an algorithm that is used to solve problems with constraints.
2. It is a form of depth-first search.
3. It incrementally builds candidates to the solutions.
4. It abandons a candidate as soon as it determines that the candidate cannot possibly be completed to a valid solution.
5. Backtracking search is a form of recursive problem-solving.

Backtracking search is commonly used in problems such as the N-Queens problem, Sudoku, and the Knight's Tour problem. It is an effective way to solve problems with a large search space, as it can quickly eliminate large portions of the search space that do not contain valid solutions. However, it can also be inefficient in some cases, as it may need to explore a large number of candidates before finding a valid solution. It is important to carefully design the backtracking search algorithm to ensure that it is as efficient as possible.
