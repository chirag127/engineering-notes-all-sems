### Backward Chaining

Backward chaining is a method of reasoning used in artificial intelligence and is often used in expert systems. It is a technique used in knowledge representation, which is a topic in Unit 3 of the subject of Artificial Intelligence KCS.

Here are some key points about backward chaining:

1. Backward chaining starts with the goal or conclusion and works backward to find the evidence or facts that support it.
2. It is a form of deductive reasoning, where the conclusion is known, and the reasoning process is used to find the premises that support it.
3. Backward chaining is often used in rule-based systems, where the rules are represented as if-then statements.
4. The system starts with the goal and tries to find a rule that has the goal as its conclusion. If such a rule is found, the system then tries to find evidence to support the premises of the rule.
5. This process continues until either the evidence is found to support the goal, or no more rules can be applied, and the goal is deemed unachievable.
6. Backward chaining is useful in situations where there are many possible solutions, and the goal is to find the most likely one.
