## Unit 2 - PROBLEM SOLVING METHODS

- Problem solving is the process of finding a solution to a given situation or task that meets some criteria or constraints.
- Problem solving methods are systematic approaches or strategies that can be applied to different types of problems, such as mathematical, logical, scientific, engineering, or everyday problems.
- Some common problem solving methods are:

  - Trial and error: Trying different possible solutions until finding one that works or satisfies the criteria. This method is simple but can be inefficient or impractical for complex or large problems.
  - Algorithm: A step-by-step procedure or set of rules that guarantees a correct or optimal solution to a problem. This method is precise and reliable but can be difficult to design or implement for some problems.
  - Heuristic: A rule of thumb or a shortcut that guides the search for a solution or reduces the problem space. This method is fast and flexible but can be inaccurate or incomplete for some problems.
  - Divide and conquer: Breaking down a problem into smaller and simpler subproblems that can be solved separately and then combined to form the solution for the original problem. This method is effective and scalable but can be complex or recursive for some problems.
  - Backtracking: Exploring different branches of a solution space and discarding or revisiting the ones that do not lead to a satisfactory solution. This method is exhaustive and thorough but can be time-consuming or memory-intensive for some problems.
  - Simulation: Creating a model or a representation of a problem and experimenting with different scenarios or parameters to observe the outcomes or effects. This method is realistic and informative but can be costly or inaccurate for some problems.
