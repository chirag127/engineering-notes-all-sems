### Search Strategies

- Search strategies are methods of finding solutions to problems by exploring the search space systematically or heuristically.
- Search space is the set of all possible states and actions that can be reached from the initial state by applying the problem operators.
- Systematic search strategies explore the search space in a predefined order, such as breadth-first, depth-first, or uniform-cost search.
- Heuristic search strategies use a function that estimates the cost or quality of a state or an action, such as best-first, hill-climbing, or A* search.
- Some search strategies are complete, meaning they are guaranteed to find a solution if one exists, while others are incomplete, meaning they may fail to find a solution even if one exists.
- Some search strategies are optimal, meaning they are guaranteed to find the best solution according to some criterion, while others are suboptimal, meaning they may find a solution that is not the best.
- Some search strategies are informed, meaning they use some knowledge about the problem domain to guide the search, while others are uninformed, meaning they use no such knowledge.
- Some search strategies are deterministic, meaning they make the same decision in the same situation, while others are nondeterministic, meaning they make a random or probabilistic decision in the same situation.
- Some search strategies are single-agent, meaning they assume that the problem solver is the only agent in the environment, while others are multi-agent, meaning they take into account the actions and goals of other agents in the environment.
