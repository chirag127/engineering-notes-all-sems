### Knowledge Representation

- Knowledge representation is a field of artificial intelligence that focuses on designing computer representations that capture information about the world that can be used for solving complex problems.
- Knowledge representation and reasoning (KRR) is the field of artificial intelligence dedicated to representing information about the world in a form that a computer system can use to solve complex tasks such as diagnosing a medical condition or having a dialog in a natural language.
- Knowledge representation is the study of how the beliefs, intentions, and value judgments of an intelligent agent can be expressed in a transparent, symbolic notation suitable for automated reasoning.
- Knowledge representation is essential for building intelligent systems that can perform tasks that require human-like intelligence and understanding.

- Requirements for knowledge representation system:
  - Representational Accuracy: KR system should have the ability to represent all kind of required knowledge.
  - Inferential Adequacy: KR system should have ability to manipulate the representational structures to produce new knowledge corresponding to the existing structure.
  - Inferential Efficiency: KR system should be able to derive the required knowledge in a reasonable time.
  - Acquisitional Efficiency: KR system should allow the acquisition of new knowledge easily.

- Some of the common methods of knowledge representation are:
  - Logic: Logic is a formal system of rules and symbols for reasoning and deriving conclusions from premises. Logic can be used to represent facts, rules, and goals in a precise and unambiguous way. Logic can also support various types of inference such as deduction, induction, and abduction.
  - Semantic Networks: Semantic networks are graphical representations of concepts and their relationships. Semantic networks can be used to represent hierarchical, associative, and causal knowledge. Semantic networks can also support various types of inference such as inheritance, similarity, and analogy.
  - Frames: Frames are data structures that represent a stereotypical situation or object. Frames can be used to represent complex and structured knowledge that involves attributes, values, and subframes. Frames can also support various types of inference such as default, exception, and slot-filling.
  - Production Rules: Production rules are conditional statements that specify what action to take when a certain condition is met. Production rules can be used to represent procedural and declarative knowledge that involves actions, conditions, and consequences. Production rules can also support various types of inference such as forward chaining, backward chaining, and conflict resolution.
  - Ontologies: Ontologies are formal specifications of the concepts, terms, and relationships that exist in a domain of interest. Ontologies can be used to represent shared and common knowledge that can be reused and communicated across different applications and agents. Ontologies can also support various types of inference such as classification, subsumption, and consistency checking.
