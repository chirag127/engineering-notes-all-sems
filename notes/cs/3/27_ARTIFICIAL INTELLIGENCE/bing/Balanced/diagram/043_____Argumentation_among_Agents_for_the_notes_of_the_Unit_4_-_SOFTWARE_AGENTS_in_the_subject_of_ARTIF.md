### Argumentation among Agents

- Argumentation is a process of reasoning and communication that involves the exchange and evaluation of arguments, which are claims supported by reasons and evidence.
- Argumentation can be used to model the internal reasoning of an agent, as well as the interaction among agents who have different beliefs, goals, preferences, or values.
- Argumentation can help agents to resolve conflicts, persuade others, negotiate agreements, justify actions, explain opinions, or learn from each other.
- Argumentation can be formalized using various logical frameworks, such as abstract argumentation, structured argumentation, or probabilistic argumentation, that define the structure, semantics, and dynamics of arguments and their relations.
- Argumentation can be implemented using various computational techniques, such as argumentation-based dialogues, argumentation schemes, argument mining, or argument visualization, that enable the generation, representation, analysis, and presentation of arguments and their outcomes.
- Argumentation can be applied to various domains and tasks, such as decision support, natural language processing, legal reasoning, multi-agent systems, or social media analysis, that require the use of rational and social reasoning.
