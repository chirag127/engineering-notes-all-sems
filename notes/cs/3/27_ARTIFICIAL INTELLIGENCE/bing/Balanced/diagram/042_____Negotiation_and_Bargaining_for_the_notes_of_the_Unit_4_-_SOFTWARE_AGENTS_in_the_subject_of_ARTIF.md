### Negotiation and Bargaining

- Negotiation is a process of reaching an agreement between two or more parties that have conflicting interests or preferences .
- Bargaining is a subset of negotiation that involves making offers and counteroffers until a deal is reached or rejected.
- Negotiation and bargaining can be applied to various domains, such as business, politics, law, diplomacy, and personal relationships .
- Negotiation and bargaining can be performed by humans, software agents, or a combination of both  .
- Software agents are autonomous programs that can act on behalf of humans or other agents in a negotiation or bargaining scenario  .
- Software agents can have different roles, such as negotiator, mediator, advisor, or observer  .
- Software agents can have different capabilities, such as communication, reasoning, learning, planning, and emotion  .
- Software agents can have different strategies, such as cooperative, competitive, or mixed-motive  .
- Software agents can have different objectives, such as maximizing their own utility, satisfying their preferences, or reaching a fair or efficient outcome  .
- Software agents can face different challenges, such as incomplete or uncertain information, multiple issues or criteria, time or resource constraints, or ethical or social norms  .
- Software agents can offer different benefits, such as reducing human effort, bias, or error, increasing human performance, satisfaction, or trust, or enabling new forms of negotiation or bargaining  .
- Software agents can also pose different risks, such as violating human privacy, autonomy, or dignity, manipulating human behavior, emotions, or decisions, or creating conflicts or disputes with other agents or humans  .
- Software agents can be evaluated by different criteria, such as effectiveness, efficiency, robustness, adaptability, or transparency  .
- Software agents can be designed by different methods, such as rule-based, heuristic, game-theoretic, or machine learning  .
- Software agents can be tested by different techniques, such as simulation, experimentation, or user study  .
