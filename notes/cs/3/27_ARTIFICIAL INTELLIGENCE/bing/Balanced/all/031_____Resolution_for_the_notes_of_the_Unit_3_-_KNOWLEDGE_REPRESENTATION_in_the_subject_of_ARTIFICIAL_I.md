# Resolution for the notes of the Unit 3 - KNOWLEDGE REPRESENTATION in the subject of ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE KCS

- Knowledge representation (KR) is a field of artificial intelligence that focuses on designing computer representations that capture information about the world that can be used for solving complex problems.
- KR is also the study of how the beliefs, intentions, and value judgments of an intelligent agent can be expressed in a transparent, symbolic notation suitable for automated reasoning.
- KR is essential for building intelligent systems that can perform tasks such as diagnosing a medical condition, having a dialog in a natural language, planning a route, or playing a game.
- Some of the requirements for a KR system are:
  - Representational accuracy: the ability to represent all kinds of required knowledge.
  - Inferential adequacy: the ability to manipulate the representational structures to produce new knowledge corresponding to the logical consequences of the existing knowledge.
  - Inferential efficiency: the ability to direct the inferential process towards the most relevant and productive combinations of knowledge.
  - Acquisitional efficiency: the ability to acquire new knowledge easily from users or other sources.
- Some of the common methods of KR are:
  - Logic: the use of formal systems of symbols and rules to capture the meaning and validity of statements and arguments.
  - Semantic networks: the use of graphs to represent concepts and their relationships in a hierarchical or associative manner.
  - Frames: the use of structures to organize and describe a collection of attributes and values that characterize an entity or a situation.
  - Production rules: the use of conditional statements to represent knowledge as a set of actions to be performed when certain conditions are met.
  - Ontologies: the use of a shared and common vocabulary to define the concepts and relations in a domain of interest.
  - Conceptual graphs: the use of a graphical notation to represent propositions and their interconnections in a logic-based framework.
  - Description logics: the use of a subset of logic to represent and reason about the properties and classifications of concepts and individuals.
