# Knowledge Representation

Knowledge representation is a field of artificial intelligence that focuses on designing computer representations that capture information about the world that can be used for solving complex problems.

Some of the objectives of knowledge representation are:

- To represent the knowledge of an intelligent agent in a transparent, symbolic notation suitable for automated reasoning.
- To provide a common vocabulary and syntax for expressing different types of knowledge, such as facts, rules, preferences, constraints, etc.
- To facilitate the sharing and reuse of knowledge among different agents and systems.
- To enable the manipulation and inference of new knowledge from existing knowledge using logical and computational methods.

Some of the requirements for a knowledge representation system are:

- Representational accuracy: The system should have the ability to represent all kinds of required knowledge.
- Inferential adequacy: The system should have the ability to manipulate the representational structures to produce new knowledge corresponding to the implicit information in the existing knowledge.
- Inferential efficiency: The system should have the ability to perform the inference process in a reasonable amount of time and space.
- Acquisitional efficiency: The system should have the ability to acquire new knowledge from various sources, such as human experts, databases, sensors, etc.

Some of the common techniques for knowledge representation are:

- Propositional logic: A simple and expressive language that uses symbols and connectives to represent facts and rules in a declarative way.
- Predicate logic: An extension of propositional logic that allows the use of variables, quantifiers, and predicates to represent complex and relational knowledge.
- Semantic networks: A graphical representation that uses nodes and links to represent concepts and their relationships in a hierarchical and associative way.
- Frames: A representation that uses structures and slots to represent objects and their attributes and values in a class-based and inheritance-based way.
- Scripts: A representation that uses schemas and slots to represent events and their participants and roles in a sequence-based and causal way.
- Production rules: A representation that uses condition-action pairs to represent knowledge and behavior in an if-then-else way.
- Ontologies: A representation that uses terms and definitions to represent a shared and formal vocabulary for a domain of interest.
