# Architecture for Intelligent Agents

- An intelligent agent is a system that perceives its environment via sensors and acts rationally upon that environment with its effectors.
- The architecture of an agent describes its modules and capabilities, and how these operate together.
- There are different types of agent architectures, depending on the level of complexity, rationality, and interaction of the agent.
- Some of the common types of agent architectures are  :
  - Reactive agents: Reactive agents are the simplest type of AI agent. They act based on the current percept, without any memory or reasoning. They are fast and robust, but lack flexibility and adaptability. An example of a reactive agent is a thermostat that adjusts the temperature based on the current reading.
  - Model-based reflex agents: Model-based reflex agents are more sophisticated than reactive agents. They maintain an internal state of the environment, based on the percept history and a model of how the environment works. They use this state to choose the best action, based on a set of rules or condition-action pairs. An example of a model-based reflex agent is a chess-playing program that uses a board representation and a set of moves to decide the next move.
  - Goal-based agents: Goal-based agents are more flexible than model-based reflex agents. They have a set of goals or desired outcomes, and they choose the action that leads to the achievement of the goals, based on the current state and the model of the environment. They can adapt to changing situations and preferences, but they may require a lot of search and planning. An example of a goal-based agent is a taxi driver that chooses the route that minimizes the travel time and the cost, based on the traffic and the fare.
  - Utility-based agents: Utility-based agents are more rational than goal-based agents. They have a utility function that assigns a numeric value to each state of the environment, based on the agent's preferences. They choose the action that maximizes the expected utility, based on the current state, the model, and the probabilities of the outcomes. They can handle uncertainty and trade-offs, but they may require a lot of computation and knowledge. An example of a utility-based agent is a stock trader that chooses the portfolio that maximizes the expected return, based on the market and the risk.
  - Learning agents: Learning agents are the most advanced type of AI agent. They have the ability to learn from their experience and improve their performance over time. They consist of four components: a learning element, a performance element, a critic, and a problem generator. The learning element updates the agent's knowledge or parameters based on the feedback from the critic. The performance element selects the action based on the current knowledge or parameters. The critic evaluates the action based on the percept and the goals. The problem generator suggests new actions or situations that can lead to better learning. An example of a learning agent is a self-driving car that learns from its sensors and the driver's feedback, and improves its driving skills over time.
- Some of the applications of intelligent agents are:
  - Information retrieval: Intelligent agents can help users find relevant information from large and heterogeneous data sources, such as the web, databases, or social media. They can use natural language processing, web mining, or recommender systems to filter, rank, or personalize the information.
  - E-commerce: Intelligent agents can facilitate online transactions, such as buying, selling, or bidding, by acting as brokers, mediators, or negotiators. They can use game theory, auction theory, or multi-agent systems to optimize the outcomes for the parties involved.
  - Robotics: Intelligent agents can control physical devices, such as robots, drones, or vehicles, by sensing the environment and executing the actions. They can use computer vision, speech recognition, or motion planning to perform tasks, such as navigation, manipulation, or communication.
  - Gaming: Intelligent agents can create realistic and engaging virtual environments, such as video games, simulations, or virtual reality, by acting as characters, opponents, or companions. They can use artificial neural networks, reinforcement learning, or evolutionary algorithms to generate behaviors, such as movement, combat, or dialogue.
