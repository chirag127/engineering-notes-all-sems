# Mental Events and Mental Objects

- Mental events are occurrences or phenomena that happen in the mind, such as thoughts, feelings, decisions, dreams, and realizations.
- Mental objects are entities or concepts that exist in the mind, such as beliefs, goals, plans, intentions, and knowledge.
- In artificial intelligence, mental events and mental objects are important for representing the internal state and reasoning process of an intelligent agent.
- An agent can have knowledge about its own mental events and objects, as well as those of other agents. This is called **metaknowledge** or **metareasoning**.
- Metaknowledge and metareasoning can help an agent to control its inference, plan its actions, communicate with other agents, and learn from its experience.
- Some examples of metaknowledge and metareasoning are:
  - An agent can have beliefs about its own beliefs, such as "I believe that X is true" or "I am uncertain about Y".
  - An agent can have beliefs about the beliefs of other agents, such as "Alice believes that Z is false" or "Bob does not know that W is true".
  - An agent can have goals about its own goals, such as "I want to achieve G" or "I want to avoid H".
  - An agent can have goals about the goals of other agents, such as "I want Alice to achieve G" or "I want Bob to avoid H".
  - An agent can have plans about its own plans, such as "I plan to do A, then B, then C" or "I plan to revise my plan if D happens".
  - An agent can have plans about the plans of other agents, such as "I plan to cooperate with Alice on A, then B, then C" or "I plan to interfere with Bob's plan if D happens".
  - An agent can have intentions about its own intentions, such as "I intend to do A" or "I intend to not do B".
  - An agent can have intentions about the intentions of other agents, such as "I intend to help Alice do A" or "I intend to prevent Bob from doing B".
  - An agent can have knowledge about its own knowledge, such as "I know that X is true" or "I do not know that Y is true".
  - An agent can have knowledge about the knowledge of other agents, such as "I know that Alice knows that Z is true" or "I know that Bob does not know that W is true".
  - An agent can have knowledge about its own reasoning, such as "I deduced X from Y and Z" or "I inferred Y from X and W".
  - An agent can have knowledge about the reasoning of other agents, such as "I know that Alice deduced Z from X and Y" or "I know that Bob inferred W from X and Z".
