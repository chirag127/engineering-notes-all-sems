### Natural Language Processing
- Natural language processing (NLP) is the branch of artificial intelligence that deals with the interaction between computers and human languages, such as speech and text.
- Some of the tasks and applications of NLP are:
  - Speech recognition: converting spoken language into text or commands.
  - Natural language understanding: extracting meaning and information from natural language input.
  - Natural language generation: producing natural language output from data or logic.
  - Machine translation: translating text or speech from one language to another.
  - Text summarization: creating a concise and informative summary of a longer text.
  - Sentiment analysis: detecting and classifying the emotions and opinions expressed in natural language.
  - Question answering: finding and providing relevant answers to natural language questions.
  - Dialogue systems: engaging in natural language conversations with users, such as chatbots and virtual assistants.
- Some of the techniques and tools used in NLP are:
  - Tokenization: splitting natural language input into smaller units, such as words, phrases, or symbols.
  - Parsing: analyzing the syntactic structure and grammatical rules of natural language input.
  - Semantics: representing and reasoning about the meaning and logic of natural language input.
  - Lexicon: a collection of words and their properties, such as spelling, pronunciation, part of speech, and meaning.
  - Corpus: a large and structured set of natural language texts, often annotated with linguistic information.
  - Machine learning: applying statistical and computational methods to learn from data and improve performance.

### Computer Vision
- Computer vision is the branch of artificial intelligence that enables machines to perceive and understand visual information, such as images, videos, and scenes.
- Some of the tasks and applications of computer vision are:
  - Image processing: enhancing, transforming, and manipulating images, such as filtering, resizing, cropping, and stitching.
  - Feature extraction: detecting and describing distinctive and informative parts of images, such as edges, corners, blobs, and keypoints.
  - Object recognition: identifying and classifying objects in images, such as faces, cars, animals, and plants.
  - Face recognition: recognizing and verifying the identity of a person based on their face image.
  - Scene understanding: analyzing and interpreting the context and content of a scene, such as the location, layout, objects, and activities.
  - Optical character recognition: converting printed or handwritten text in images into digital text.
  - Image captioning: generating natural language descriptions of images, such as "A man is riding a bicycle on a sunny day".
  - Video analysis: processing and understanding temporal and spatial information in videos, such as motion, events, and actions.
  - Computer vision and augmented reality: enhancing the real world with virtual elements, such as graphics, sounds, and haptics.
- Some of the techniques and tools used in computer vision are:
  - Pixel: the smallest unit of an image, representing a color or intensity value.
  - Histogram: a graphical representation of the distribution of pixel values in an image, such as brightness, hue, or saturation.
  - Convolution: a mathematical operation that applies a filter or kernel to an image, resulting in a new image, such as blurring, sharpening, or edge detection.
  - Convolutional neural network: a type of deep learning model that consists of multiple layers of convolutions, nonlinear activations, and pooling, and can learn complex features and patterns from images.
  - Template matching: a technique that compares a template or pattern to an image, and finds the best match or similarity, such as finding a logo or a face in an image.
  - Feature matching: a technique that matches features or keypoints between two or more images, and finds the best alignment or correspondence, such as finding the same object or scene
