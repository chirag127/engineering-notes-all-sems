## Unit 2 - PROBLEM SOLVING METHODS

Problem solving is the process of finding solutions to difficult or complex issues. Problem solving methods are strategies or techniques that can be used to solve problems effectively and efficiently. Some of the common problem solving methods are:

- **Trial and error:** This method involves trying different solutions until one works. It is useful when there are a limited number of possible solutions or when the problem is simple and straightforward. However, it can be time-consuming, inefficient, and frustrating if there are many possible solutions or if the problem is complex and unclear.
- **Algorithm:** This method involves following a set of rules or steps that guarantee a solution. It is useful when the problem is well-defined and the solution is known to exist. However, it can be difficult to design, implement, and debug an algorithm, and it may not be the most optimal or efficient solution.
- **Heuristic:** This method involves using a rule of thumb or a shortcut that can help find a good solution quickly. It is useful when the problem is ill-defined, complex, or has no known algorithm. However, it can be risky, as it does not guarantee a solution or the best solution, and it may be based on assumptions, biases, or incomplete information.
- **Divide and conquer:** This method involves breaking down a large or complex problem into smaller and simpler subproblems that can be solved more easily. It is useful when the problem is too difficult or time-consuming to solve as a whole. However, it can be challenging to identify and separate the subproblems, and to combine the solutions of the subproblems into a solution for the original problem.
- **Backtracking:** This method involves trying a partial solution and then undoing it if it does not lead to a complete solution. It is useful when the problem involves finding a sequence of choices or actions that satisfy some constraints or criteria. However, it can be inefficient, as it may explore many dead ends or repeat the same work.
- **Search:** This method involves exploring a space of possible solutions and finding the one that meets the goal or objective. It is useful when the problem involves finding an optimal or near-optimal solution among many alternatives. However, it can be computationally expensive, as it may require a lot of memory and time to search the entire space or to evaluate the quality of each solution.
