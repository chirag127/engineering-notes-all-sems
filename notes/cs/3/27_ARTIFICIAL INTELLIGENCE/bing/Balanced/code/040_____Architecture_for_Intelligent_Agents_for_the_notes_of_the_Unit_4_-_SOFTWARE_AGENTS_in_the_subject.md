### Architecture for Intelligent Agents

- An **intelligent agent** is a system that perceives its environment via sensors and acts rationally upon that environment with its effectors.
- The **architecture** of an agent describes its modules and capabilities, and how these operate together.
- The architecture determines how the agent function, which maps percepts to actions, is implemented in the agent program, which runs on the agent hardware.
- There are different types of agent architectures, depending on the level of complexity, rationality, and interaction of the agent.
- Some of the common types of agent architectures are :
  - **Reactive agents**: Reactive agents are the simplest type of AI agent. They act based on the current percept, without any memory or reasoning. They are fast and robust, but limited in their capabilities. An example of a reactive agent is a thermostat that adjusts the temperature based on the current reading.
  - **Model-based reflex agents**: Model-based reflex agents are more sophisticated than reactive agents. They maintain an internal state that represents some aspects of the environment that are not directly observable. They use this state to choose the best action, based on a set of rules or condition-action pairs. An example of a model-based reflex agent is a vacuum cleaner that keeps track of the locations it has cleaned and avoids obstacles.
  - **Goal-based agents**: Goal-based agents are more flexible than model-based reflex agents. They have a set of goals that describe desirable situations or outcomes. They use a search or planning algorithm to find a sequence of actions that will achieve their goals, given their current state and percept. An example of a goal-based agent is a chess player that tries to checkmate the opponent.
  - **Utility-based agents**: Utility-based agents are more rational than goal-based agents. They have a utility function that assigns a numeric value to each state, reflecting the agent's preferences or happiness. They use a decision-theoretic approach to maximize their expected utility, taking into account the uncertainty and consequences of their actions. An example of a utility-based agent is a stock trader that tries to maximize its profit.
  - **Learning agents**: Learning agents are more adaptive than utility-based agents. They have a learning component that allows them to improve their performance over time, based on their experience and feedback. They can modify their agent function, their utility function, or their internal model of the environment. An example of a learning agent is a self-driving car that learns from its own driving and from human drivers.
- Some of the applications of intelligent agents are:
  - **Information retrieval and filtering**: Intelligent agents can help users find and access relevant information from large and dynamic sources, such as the web, databases, or social media. They can also filter out unwanted or redundant information, based on the user's preferences and needs. An example of an information retrieval and filtering agent is a web search engine that ranks and displays the most relevant web pages for a given query.
  - **E-commerce and negotiation**: Intelligent agents can help users buy and sell goods and services online, by automating the tasks of searching, comparing, negotiating, and contracting. They can also coordinate and cooperate with other agents, representing different parties or interests, to reach mutually beneficial agreements. An example of an e-commerce and negotiation agent is an online auction system that bids on behalf of the user, based on their budget and preferences.
  - **Entertainment and education**: Intelligent agents can provide users with engaging and personalized experiences in various domains, such as games, music, movies, or learning. They can act as opponents, teammates, tutors, or companions, depending on the user's goals and preferences. They can also adapt to the user's behavior, mood, and feedback, to enhance their enjoyment and satisfaction. An example of an entertainment and education agent is a virtual character that interacts with the user in a realistic and immersive way.
