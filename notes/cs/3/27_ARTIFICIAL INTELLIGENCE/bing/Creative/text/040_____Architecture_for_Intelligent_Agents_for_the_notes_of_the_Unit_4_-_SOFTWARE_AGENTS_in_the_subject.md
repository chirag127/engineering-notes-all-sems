### Architecture for Intelligent Agents

- An **intelligent agent** is a system that perceives its environment via sensors and acts rationally upon that environment with its effectors.
- The **architecture** of an agent describes its modules and capabilities, and how these operate together.
- The architecture of an agent determines how it processes the information from its sensors and how it selects the actions for its effectors.
- There are different types of architectures for intelligent agents, depending on their level of complexity, rationality, and interaction.
- Some of the common types of architectures are  :
  - **Reactive agents**: Reactive agents are the simplest type of AI agent. They do not have any internal state or memory. They act only based on the current percept, without considering the past or the future. They are fast and robust, but they cannot handle complex or dynamic environments. An example of a reactive agent is a thermostat that adjusts the temperature based on the current reading.
  - **Model-based reflex agents**: Model-based reflex agents are more sophisticated than reactive agents. They have an internal state that represents some aspects of the environment. They use this state to keep track of the changes in the environment and to infer the hidden aspects that are not directly observable. They also have a set of rules that map the state and the percept to the action. They can handle partially observable and dynamic environments, but they still do not have any goals or plans. An example of a model-based reflex agent is a taxi driver that navigates the traffic based on the current location, speed, and road conditions.
  - **Goal-based agents**: Goal-based agents are more advanced than model-based reflex agents. They have a set of goals that they want to achieve, and they use their state and percept to decide which action will bring them closer to their goals. They can handle complex and dynamic environments, but they still do not have any preferences or utilities. An example of a goal-based agent is a chess player that chooses the best move based on the current board state and the goal of winning the game.
  - **Utility-based agents**: Utility-based agents are more refined than goal-based agents. They have a utility function that assigns a numerical value to each state, representing how desirable or preferable that state is. They use their state and percept to calculate the expected utility of each action, and they choose the action that maximizes their expected utility. They can handle uncertain and complex environments, but they still do not have any learning or adaptation capabilities. An example of a utility-based agent is a stock trader that buys and sells stocks based on the current market state and the expected profit.
  - **Learning agents**: Learning agents are the most sophisticated type of AI agent. They have the ability to learn from their experience and improve their performance over time. They have four components: a learning element that updates the agent's knowledge or parameters based on the feedback, a performance element that selects the actions based on the current knowledge or parameters, a critic element that evaluates the actions and provides the feedback, and a problem generator element that suggests new actions or situations to explore. They can handle unknown and complex environments, and they can adapt to changing conditions. An example of a learning agent is a self-driving car that learns from its own driving and from other drivers.
