### Argumentation among Agents

- Argumentation is a process of reasoning and communication that involves the exchange and evaluation of arguments, which are claims supported by reasons and evidence.
- Argumentation can be used to model the internal reasoning of an agent, as well as the interaction among agents who have different beliefs, goals, preferences, or values.
- Argumentation can help agents to resolve conflicts, persuade others, justify their actions, explain their reasoning, and learn from each other.
- Argumentation can also provide a basis for designing and implementing multi-agent systems that can perform complex tasks such as negotiation, deliberation, planning, and decision making.
- Argumentation in artificial intelligence draws on various disciplines, such as philosophy, logic, rhetoric, linguistics, psychology, and law, and has applications in various domains, such as natural language processing, knowledge representation, logic programming, legal reasoning, and decision support.

Some key concepts and techniques in argumentation among agents are:

- Argument schemes: patterns of reasoning that can be used to construct and evaluate arguments, such as modus ponens, analogy, or authority.
- Argument graphs: graphical representations of arguments and their relations, such as attack, support, or defeat.
- Argumentation frameworks: formal models of argumentation that define the syntax and semantics of arguments and their relations, such as abstract argumentation, structured argumentation, or probabilistic argumentation.
- Argumentation protocols: rules and conventions that regulate the exchange of arguments among agents, such as dialogue games, speech acts, or commitment stores.
- Argumentation strategies: methods and heuristics that guide the selection and generation of arguments by agents, such as goal-oriented, audience-adapted, or cooperative strategies.
