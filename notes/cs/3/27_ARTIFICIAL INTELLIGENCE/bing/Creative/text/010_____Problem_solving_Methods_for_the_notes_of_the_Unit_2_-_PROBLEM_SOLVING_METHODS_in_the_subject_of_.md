### Problem solving methods

Problem solving methods are general strategies or techniques that can be applied to different types of problems in artificial intelligence. They are often based on some search or reasoning process that explores the space of possible solutions or actions. Some of the common problem solving methods are:

- **Search methods**: Search methods are based on systematically exploring the space of possible states or actions that can lead to a goal or a solution. Search methods can be classified into different types, such as uninformed search, informed search, local search, constraint satisfaction, etc. depending on the amount and use of information available, the structure of the search space, and the criteria for evaluating the solutions.
- **Logic methods**: Logic methods are based on using formal systems of symbols and rules to represent and manipulate knowledge and reasoning. Logic methods can be used to encode facts, rules, goals, and queries in a precise and unambiguous way, and to infer new information or conclusions from existing knowledge. Logic methods can be classified into different types, such as propositional logic, first-order logic, modal logic, fuzzy logic, etc. depending on the expressiveness and complexity of the language and the inference mechanism.
- **Planning methods**: Planning methods are based on generating and executing sequences of actions that can achieve a desired goal or outcome. Planning methods can be used to model complex and dynamic domains, where the effects and preconditions of actions, the goals and preferences of agents, and the uncertainty and incompleteness of information need to be taken into account. Planning methods can be classified into different types, such as classical planning, hierarchical planning, conditional planning, probabilistic planning, etc. depending on the assumptions and techniques used to generate and execute plans.
- **Game methods**: Game methods are based on analyzing and playing strategic interactions between two or more agents, where the outcome of each agent depends on the actions of the others. Game methods can be used to model competitive or cooperative scenarios, where the agents have different or conflicting objectives, preferences, and beliefs, and where the agents need to anticipate and react to the actions of the others. Game methods can be classified into different types, such as zero-sum games, non-zero-sum games, cooperative games, non-cooperative games, etc. depending on the properties and characteristics of the game.
