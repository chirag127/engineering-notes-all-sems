# Negotiation and Bargaining

- Negotiation is a process in which a joint decision is made by two or more parties who have conflicting interests or preferences.
- Bargaining is a discussion about what the other side wants, while negotiation is a discussion about why they want it.
- Negotiation and bargaining are important skills for software agents that interact with humans or other agents in various domains, such as e-commerce, online dispute resolution, social media, etc.
- Software agents can use different strategies and techniques to negotiate and bargain effectively, such as:
  - Artificial intelligence (AI) agents: computer programs that can negotiate and help resolve disputes, either by mediating between humans, representing a human negotiator, or negotiating with other AI agents.
  - Game theory: a mathematical framework that models rational decision-making in situations of strategic interdependence, where the outcome for each agent depends on the actions of others.
  - Heuristics: simple rules of thumb that can guide the agent's behavior in complex and uncertain situations, such as anchoring, aspiration levels, deadlines, etc.
  - Machine learning: a branch of AI that enables the agent to learn from data and experience, and adapt its behavior accordingly, such as reinforcement learning, neural networks, etc.
  - Natural language processing: a branch of AI that enables the agent to understand and generate natural language, such as text or speech, and use it for communication and persuasion.
  - Multi-agent systems: a field of AI that studies how multiple agents can coordinate and cooperate to achieve their goals, such as distributed problem-solving, coalition formation, voting, etc.
- Software agents can also face various challenges and limitations when negotiating and bargaining, such as:
  - Ethical issues: how to ensure that the agent's behavior is fair, transparent, accountable, and respectful of human values and norms.
  - Social issues: how to deal with human emotions, trust, rapport, and cultural differences that may affect the negotiation outcome.
  - Computational issues: how to cope with the complexity, uncertainty, and dynamics of the negotiation environment, and how to balance the trade-off between computation and communication.
  - Design issues: how to define the agent's preferences, objectives, and constraints, and how to evaluate its performance and outcomes.
