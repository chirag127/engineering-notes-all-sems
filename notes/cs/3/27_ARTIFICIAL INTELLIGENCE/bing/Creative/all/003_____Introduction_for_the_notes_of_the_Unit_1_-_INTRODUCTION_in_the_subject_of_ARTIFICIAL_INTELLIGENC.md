# Introduction

- Artificial intelligence (AI) is the branch of computer science that deals with creating machines or software that can perform tasks that normally require human intelligence, such as reasoning, learning, planning, decision making, natural language processing, computer vision, speech recognition, etc.
- AI can be classified into two main categories: weak AI and strong AI. Weak AI, also known as narrow AI, is the type of AI that is designed to perform a specific task or domain, such as playing chess, driving a car, or diagnosing diseases. Strong AI, also known as general AI or artificial general intelligence (AGI), is the type of AI that can perform any intellectual task that a human can do, such as understanding natural language, solving complex problems, or exhibiting creativity and emotions. Weak AI is currently achievable and widely used, while strong AI is still a hypothetical and elusive goal.
- AI can also be classified into four types based on the degree of human-like intelligence and rationality they exhibit: systems that think like humans, systems that act like humans, systems that think rationally, and systems that act rationally. These types of AI are not mutually exclusive and can overlap with each other. For example, a system that thinks like a human may also act like a human, or a system that acts rationally may also think rationally.
- AI can be applied to various domains and fields, such as education, healthcare, entertainment, business, security, agriculture, etc. Some of the benefits of AI include enhancing human capabilities, improving efficiency and productivity, reducing errors and risks, providing convenience and comfort, and creating new opportunities and innovations. Some of the challenges and risks of AI include ethical, social, legal, and moral issues, such as privacy, bias, accountability, transparency, safety, security, human dignity, and human values.
