# Trust and Reputation in Multi-agent Systems

- Trust and reputation are important concepts for effective interactions in multi-agent systems (MAS), especially in open and dynamic environments where agents may have conflicting goals, preferences, or behaviors .
- Trust is the subjective belief or expectation that an agent has about the actions, outcomes, or characteristics of another agent or a group of agents .
- Reputation is the collective opinion or evaluation that a group of agents has about the trustworthiness of another agent or a group of agents .
- Trust and reputation can be used to cope with uncertainty, risk, and opportunism in MAS, and to facilitate cooperation, coordination, and communication among agents .
- Trust and reputation can be modeled and computed using various approaches, such as logic-based, probabilistic, game-theoretic, social network-based, or fuzzy methods .
- Trust and reputation can be influenced by various factors, such as direct experience, indirect information, context, time, feedback, incentives, or deception .
- Trust and reputation can have different types or dimensions, such as competence, benevolence, honesty, reliability, or sociability .
- Trust and reputation can be measured and aggregated using different metrics, such as those proposed by Marsh, Sen et al., SPORAS, eBay, or certified reputation .
- Trust and reputation can be applied to various domains and scenarios, such as e-commerce, peer-to-peer networks, social networks, recommender systems, or security .
