# Definition for the notes of the Unit 1 - INTRODUCTION in the subject of ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE KCS

- Artificial intelligence (AI) is the field of computer science that studies and develops machines and software that can perform tasks that normally require human intelligence, such as learning, reasoning, and perception .
- AI can be classified into two main categories: weak AI and strong AI. Weak AI, also known as narrow AI, is the type of AI that can only perform specific tasks or functions, such as playing chess, recognizing faces, or translating languages. Strong AI, also known as general AI or artificial general intelligence (AGI), is the type of AI that can perform any task that a human can do, such as understanding natural language, solving problems, or creating art .
- AI can also be classified based on the degree of human intervention or guidance required for its operation. Supervised learning is the type of AI that learns from labeled data, such as images with captions or texts with categories. Unsupervised learning is the type of AI that learns from unlabeled data, such as images without captions or texts without categories. Reinforcement learning is the type of AI that learns from its own actions and feedback, such as a robot that learns to navigate a maze or a game agent that learns to play a video game .
- AI has many applications and benefits across different industries and domains, such as finance, healthcare, education, entertainment, and security. AI can help automate tasks, optimize processes, enhance decision making, improve customer service, and generate new insights and innovations  .
- AI also poses some challenges and risks, such as ethical, social, legal, and technical issues. AI can raise questions about the impact of automation on human jobs, the accountability and transparency of AI systems, the privacy and security of data, the bias and fairness of algorithms, and the potential misuse or abuse of AI for malicious purposes  .
