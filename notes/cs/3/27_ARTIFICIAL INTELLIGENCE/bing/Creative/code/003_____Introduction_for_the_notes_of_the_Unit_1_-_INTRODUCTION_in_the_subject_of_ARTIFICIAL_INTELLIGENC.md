### Introduction for the notes of the Unit 1 - INTRODUCTION in the subject of ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE KCS

- Artificial intelligence (AI) is the branch of computer science that deals with creating machines or software that can perform tasks that normally require human intelligence, such as reasoning, learning, planning, decision making, natural language processing, computer vision, speech recognition, etc.
- AI can be classified into different types based on the degree of human-like intelligence and the scope of the problem domain. Some of the common types are:
  - Weak AI or Narrow AI: This type of AI can only perform a specific task or a narrow range of tasks within a predefined domain. It does not have general intelligence or the ability to understand and adapt to new situations. Examples of weak AI are chatbots, search engines, facial recognition systems, etc.
  - Strong AI or General AI: This type of AI can perform any intellectual task that a human can do. It has general intelligence or the ability to understand and learn from any domain. It can also reason, plan, and solve problems across different domains. Examples of strong AI are hypothetical or fictional, such as HAL 9000 from 2001: A Space Odyssey, Skynet from The Terminator, etc.
  - Super AI: This type of AI can surpass human intelligence and capabilities in every aspect. It can create and improve itself without human intervention. It can also have goals and values that may or may not align with human interests. Examples of super AI are also hypothetical or fictional, such as The Matrix, The Singularity, etc.
- AI can also be classified into different types based on the approach or the technique used to achieve the desired goal. Some of the common types are:
  - Symbolic AI or Classical AI: This type of AI uses symbols and rules to represent and manipulate knowledge. It relies on logic, search, and inference to solve problems. It is also known as rule-based AI or knowledge-based AI. Examples of symbolic AI are expert systems, theorem provers, etc.
  - Sub-symbolic AI or Connectionist AI: This type of AI uses numerical values and networks to represent and process knowledge. It relies on learning, adaptation, and emergence to solve problems. It is also known as neural network AI or machine learning AI. Examples of sub-symbolic AI are artificial neural networks, deep learning, reinforcement learning, etc.
  - Hybrid AI: This type of AI combines both symbolic and sub-symbolic AI to leverage the strengths and overcome the limitations of both approaches. It uses both rules and learning to represent and manipulate knowledge. It is also known as integrated AI or neuro-symbolic AI. Examples of hybrid AI are cognitive architectures, neural-symbolic systems, etc.
- AI has many applications and benefits in various fields and domains, such as education, health care, entertainment, business, security, etc. Some of the examples are:
  - Education: AI can enhance the quality and accessibility of education by providing personalized learning, adaptive assessment, intelligent tutoring, etc.
  - Health care: AI can improve the diagnosis and treatment of diseases, assist in surgery, monitor patients, discover new drugs, etc.
  - Entertainment: AI can create and enhance various forms of entertainment, such as games, movies, music, art, etc.
  - Business: AI can optimize the operations and performance of businesses, such as marketing, finance, customer service, etc.
  - Security: AI can protect and defend against various threats and risks, such as cyberattacks, fraud, terrorism, etc.
