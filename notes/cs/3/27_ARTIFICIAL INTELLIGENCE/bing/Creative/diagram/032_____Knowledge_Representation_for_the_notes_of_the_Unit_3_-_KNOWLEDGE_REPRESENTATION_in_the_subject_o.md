# Knowledge Representation

Knowledge representation is a field of artificial intelligence that focuses on designing computer representations that capture information about the world that can be used for solving complex problems. Knowledge representation and reasoning (KRR) is the subfield of AI that studies how to represent information in a form that a computer system can use to perform tasks such as diagnosing a medical condition or having a dialog in a natural language.

Some of the requirements for a knowledge representation system are:

- Representational accuracy: The system should have the ability to represent all kinds of required knowledge.
- Inferential adequacy: The system should have the ability to manipulate the representational structures to produce new knowledge corresponding to the existing knowledge.
- Inferential efficiency: The system should be able to derive the required knowledge in a reasonable amount of time and space.
- Acquisitional efficiency: The system should be able to acquire new knowledge from various sources and integrate it with the existing knowledge.

Some of the common methods of knowledge representation are:

- Logic: Logic is a formal system of symbols and rules that can be used to express statements and arguments. Logic can be used to represent facts, rules, and constraints, and to perform deductive reasoning and inference. Logic can be divided into different types, such as propositional logic, predicate logic, modal logic, and fuzzy logic.
- Semantic networks: Semantic networks are graphical representations of concepts and their relationships. Semantic networks can be used to represent hierarchical, associative, and causal knowledge, and to perform inheritance and spreading activation. Semantic networks can be divided into different types, such as frames, scripts, and conceptual graphs.
- Production rules: Production rules are representations of conditional actions that can be applied to a given situation. Production rules can be used to represent procedural and declarative knowledge, and to perform forward and backward chaining. Production rules can be divided into different types, such as if-then rules, decision trees, and decision tables.
- Ontologies: Ontologies are representations of the concepts and categories in a domain and their properties and relations. Ontologies can be used to represent taxonomic, partonomic, and functional knowledge, and to perform classification and reasoning. Ontologies can be divided into different types, such as formal ontologies, domain ontologies, and application ontologies.
