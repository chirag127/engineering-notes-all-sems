## Unit 2 - PROBLEM SOLVING METHODS

- Problem solving is the process of finding solutions to complex or difficult issues by breaking them down into simpler or more manageable parts.
- Problem solving methods are strategies or techniques that can be used to solve problems effectively and efficiently.
- Some common problem solving methods are:

  - Trial and error: Trying different solutions until finding one that works or eliminates the problem.
  - Algorithm: Following a step-by-step procedure that guarantees a correct solution or outcome.
  - Heuristic: Using a rule of thumb or a general guideline that can help narrow down the possible solutions or speed up the search process.
  - Divide and conquer: Breaking down a large or complex problem into smaller or simpler subproblems that can be solved separately and then combined to form the final solution.
  - Backtracking: Retracing the steps or decisions made in a problem solving process and revising them if they lead to a dead end or an incorrect solution.
  - Analogy: Using a similar or familiar problem or situation that has a known solution or outcome and applying it to the current problem or situation.
  - Means-ends analysis: Comparing the current state of the problem with the desired state or goal and identifying the actions or steps that can reduce the difference or gap between them.
  - Brainstorming: Generating as many ideas or solutions as possible without judging or evaluating them and then selecting the best or most feasible ones.
  - Lateral thinking: Thinking outside the box or using unconventional or creative approaches to solve problems or generate solutions.
