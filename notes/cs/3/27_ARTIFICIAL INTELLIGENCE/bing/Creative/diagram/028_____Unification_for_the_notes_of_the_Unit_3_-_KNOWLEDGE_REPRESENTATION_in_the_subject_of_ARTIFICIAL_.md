### Unification

- Unification is the process of combining multiple representations of information into a single, more comprehensive representation.
- Unification is done in order to reduce the complexity of the overall representation, and to make it easier to manipulate and reason about the information.
- Unification is an inherent part of algorithms used in artificial intelligence, natural language programming, pattern detection, and algorithms for automating other tasks.
- Unification is a key component of all first-order inference algorithms.
- Unification can be performed using the UNIFY algorithm, which takes two atomic sentences and returns a unifier for those sentences.
- A unifier is a substitution that makes two sentences identical when applied to both of them.
- Unification returns fail if the expressions do not match with each other.
- Unification can be seen as a way of finding the most general common structure between two expressions.
- Unification can also be seen as a way of finding the most specific common generalization between two expressions.
- Unification can be used to implement a grand unified theory of AI, which unites two prevailing but often opposed strains in the history of artificial-intelligence research: symbolic AI and connectionist AI.
