## Unit 4 - SOFTWARE AGENTS

Software agents are software programs that act as autonomous entities to perform tasks on behalf of a user or another program. These agents can interact with other agents, users or even physical systems to achieve their objectives. 

Here are some important points to understand about software agents:

- Software agents are designed to operate autonomously, meaning they can make decisions and take actions without constant human intervention. 
- Agents may be reactive, meaning they respond to changes in their environment or pro-active, where they take actions based on their own goals and objectives.
- Agents can communicate with other agents or systems, allowing them to collaborate and exchange information to achieve their goals.
- Agents can be classified based on their level of intelligence, ranging from simple rule-based systems to advanced machine learning models.
- There are several types of software agents, including mobile agents, web agents, intelligent agents, and autonomous agents.
- Mobile agents are agents that can migrate from one machine to another, while web agents are agents that operate within a web environment.
- Intelligent agents are agents that use machine learning and artificial intelligence techniques to make decisions and take actions, while autonomous agents are agents that can operate independently of human control.
- Software agents have several advantages, such as their ability to operate continuously, their capacity to perform complex tasks, and their potential to reduce human intervention in certain processes.
- However, software agents also present some challenges, such as their potential to cause harm or make errors without human oversight, and the ethical concerns surrounding their use. 
- Overall, software agents are a promising area of research and development, with the potential to revolutionize many industries and improve the efficiency of many processes.
