
### Uninformed Problem Solving Methods

* Uninformed problem solving methods are algorithms used to solve a problem without any prior knowledge about the problem. 
* These methods rely on trial and error and search through the possible solutions until the goal is reached. 
* Examples of uninformed problem solving methods include breadth-first search, depth-first search, and iterative deepening search. 
* Breadth-first search is a technique that starts at the root node and explores all of the neighboring nodes at the same level before moving on to the next level. 
* Depth-first search is a technique that starts at the root node and explores as far as possible along each branch before backtracking. 
* Iterative deepening search is a technique that combines the advantages of breadth-first search and depth-first search. It starts with a shallow search and gradually increases the depth of the search until the goal is found or all possible solutions have been explored. 
* Uninformed problem solving methods are useful when the search space is small and the goal is known, but they are not suitable for large or complex search spaces.
