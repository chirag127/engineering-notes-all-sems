### Trust and Reputation in Multi-agent systems

- In multi-agent systems, agents interact with other agents and cannot have full knowledge about the behavior and intentions of other agents. This introduces the need for trust and reputation mechanisms to evaluate other agents.
- Trust refers to the subjective assessment of the reliability and integrity of an agent. It involves risk and uncertainty about the actions of other agents.
- Reputation refers to the collectively held view of an agent's trustworthiness based on its past actions. It is a socially constructed assessment of trustworthiness.
- Some of the key requirements of trust and reputation systems are:
    - Accuracy: The systems should provide an accurate assessment of trustworthiness.
    - Robustness: The systems should be robust to various attacks like bad mouthing, ballot stuffing, whitewashing, etc.
    - Scalability: The systems should be scalable to large multi-agent systems.
    - Low-cost: The overhead of trust and reputation management should be low.
- Examples of trust and reputation systems:
    - Beta reputation system: Agents maintain a beta probability distribution over the reputations of other agents based on their interactions.
    - EigenTrust algorithm: It computes the reputation of agents based on the concept of eigenvectors and eigenvalues.
    - PowerTrust: It incorporates the power of the agents providing feedback to compute reputation scores. This makes the system more robust to attacks.
