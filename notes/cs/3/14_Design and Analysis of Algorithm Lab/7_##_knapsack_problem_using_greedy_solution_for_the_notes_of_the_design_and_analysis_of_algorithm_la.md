## Knapsack Problem using Greedy Solution for the notes of the Design and Analysis of Algorithm Lab in the subject of Real Time System

The Knapsack Problem is a classic optimization problem in computer science, and it involves finding the most valuable items to include in a limited-capacity knapsack. The Greedy Solution is a simple and efficient algorithm for solving the Knapsack Problem. The following are some of the key concepts related to the Knapsack Problem using the Greedy Solution:

1. Problem Statement: The Knapsack Problem involves finding the most valuable items to include in a limited-capacity knapsack, subject to the constraint that the total weight of the items must not exceed the knapsack's capacity.

2. Greedy Algorithm: The Greedy Solution is a simple and efficient algorithm for solving the Knapsack Problem, where the algorithm selects the most valuable item at each step until the knapsack is full or there are no more items to consider.

3. Value-to-Weight Ratio: The value-to-weight ratio is a key metric used by the Greedy Solution, where the algorithm selects the items with the highest value-to-weight ratio at each step.

4. Time Complexity: The time complexity of the Greedy Solution is O(n log n), where n is the number of items to consider, and it is efficient for small to medium-sized problems.

5. Limitations: The Greedy Solution may not always find the optimal solution, and it may be necessary to use other algorithms, such as dynamic programming or branch and bound, for larger or more complex problems.
