### Datagram

- A datagram is a self-contained, independent entity of data carrying sufficient information to be routed from the source to the destination computer without reliance on earlier exchanges between this source and destination computer and the transporting network.
- It is used in connectionless networking, where each packet is treated as an independent unit of data.
- The term datagram is used in the context of the User Datagram Protocol (UDP) of the Internet Protocol Suite.
- In contrast to the Transmission Control Protocol (TCP), UDP is a connectionless protocol that does not guarantee the delivery of datagrams to the destination.
- This means that datagrams may arrive out of order, be duplicated, or be lost without notice.
- However, the advantage of using datagrams is that they are lightweight and fast, making them suitable for applications such as online gaming, voice over IP, and video conferencing.
- In summary, a datagram is a self-contained, independent unit of data used in connectionless networking, providing fast and lightweight data transmission, but without the reliability of connection-oriented protocols such as TCP.
