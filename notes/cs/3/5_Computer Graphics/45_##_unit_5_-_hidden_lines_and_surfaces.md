## Unit 5 - Hidden Lines and Surfaces

Unit 5 of the Computer Graphics course focuses on Hidden Lines and Surfaces, which are aspects of computer graphics that involve determining what parts of a 3D scene are visible and what parts are hidden from view.

Hidden Lines and Surfaces are important in computer graphics because they determine what parts of a 3D scene are visible in the final image. When a 3D scene is viewed from a particular perspective, some parts of the scene may be hidden from view by other parts of the scene. To determine what parts of the scene are hidden, computer graphics algorithms are used to calculate the visibility of each part of the scene.

There are several algorithms that can be used to determine the visibility of lines and surfaces in a 3D scene, including the Z-Buffer Algorithm and the Painters Algorithm. These algorithms work by calculating the depth of each part of the scene, and then determining which parts are visible based on their depth relative to other parts of the scene.

In the context of the Computer Graphics course, students in Unit 5 will learn about Hidden Lines and Surfaces as part of the discussion on computer graphics. This may include working with examples of Hidden Lines and Surfaces, analyzing the benefits and limitations of different algorithms for determining visibility, and learning how to implement these algorithms in a computer graphics program.
