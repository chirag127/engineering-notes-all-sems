### Intensity Attenuation for the notes of the Unit 5 - Hidden Lines and Surfaces in the subject of Computer Graphics

Intensity Attenuation is a method used in computer graphics to simulate the way light decreases in intensity as it travels through space. Intensity Attenuation is used to create more realistic lighting effects in 3D scenes, and to make objects in a 3D scene appear to be lit by distant light sources.

The following are some of the key concepts related to Intensity Attenuation:

1. Inverse Square Law: The Inverse Square Law is a mathematical formula that describes the way light decreases in intensity as it travels through space.

2. Attenuation Factors: Attenuation Factors are coefficients used to control the amount of intensity attenuation in a 3D scene.

3. Light Sources: Light Sources are objects in a 3D scene that emit light, and that are used to create realistic lighting effects.

4. Shadows: Shadows are areas in a 3D scene that are not lit by light sources, and that are used to create more realistic lighting effects.

In this unit, you will learn about the Inverse Square Law, the Attenuation Factors, and the different types of light sources used in Intensity Attenuation. You will also learn how to implement Intensity Attenuation algorithms in a program, and you will learn about the advantages and disadvantages of different algorithms.

This unit will provide a foundation for understanding the principles and practices of Intensity Attenuation in computer graphics, and for exploring the various concepts and techniques used in the field of computer graphics.
