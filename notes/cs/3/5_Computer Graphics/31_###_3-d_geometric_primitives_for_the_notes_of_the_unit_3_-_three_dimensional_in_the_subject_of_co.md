### 3-D Geometric Primitives for the notes of the Unit 3 - Three Dimensional in the subject of Computer Graphics

Unit 3 of the Computer Graphics course focuses on Three Dimensional graphics, including the aspect of 3-D Geometric Primitives.

3-D Geometric Primitives are basic shapes that are used to create 3-D models in computer graphics. These shapes include points, lines, polyglines, polyggon meshes, and curved surfaces.

Some common 3-D Geometric Primitives include:
- Points, which are single points in 3-D space.
- Lines, which are straight lines connecting two points in 3-D space.
- Polylines, which are sequences of connected lines in 3-D space.
- Polygon meshes, which are collections of interconnected polyggonal faces in 3-D space.
- Curved surfaces, which are smooth surfaces that can be represented by a mathematical equation.

In the context of the Computer Graphics course, students in Unit 3 will learn about 3-D Geometric Primitives as part of the discussion on Three Dimensional graphics. This may include working with examples of each type of 3-D Geometric Primitive, analyzing the benefits and limitations of each type of primitive, and learning how to use 3-D Geometric Primitives to create 3-D models in computer graphics.
