### Windowing and Clipping for the notes of the Unit 2 - Transformations in the subject of Computer Graphics

The Windowing and Clipping section of the Unit 2 - Transformations in the subject of Computer Graphics should cover the following topics:

1. Definition of Windowing (the process of mapping a world coordinate system to a device coordinate system)
2. Overview of Windowing (what it is, when it is used, advantages and disadvantages)
3. Definition of Clipping (the process of removing parts of an object that are outside of a specified region)
4. Overview of Clipping (what it is, when it is used, advantages and disadvantages)
5. Clipping algorithms (Cohen-Sutherland, Cyrus-Beck, etc.)
6. Overview of Cohen-Sutherland algorithm (a simple line clipping algorithm)
7. Overview of Cyrus-Beck algorithm (a more advanced line clipping algorithm)
8. Polygon clipping (clipping polyggonal objects)
9. Overview of Sutherland-Hodgman algorithm (a polygon clipping algorithm)
10. Viewport transformation (mapping a world coordinate system to a device coordinate system)
11. Clipping and computer graphics (how clipping is used in computer graphics to improve performance and reduce visual artifacts)
12. Limitations of clipping (computational complexity, loss of detail, etc.)
13. Techniques for overcoming limitations (choosing appropriate algorithms, using hardware acceleration, etc.)
14. Further reading and resources (books, articles, online resources)

Windowing and Clipping is an important component of the subject of Computer Graphics. Understanding the principles and techniques of Windowing and Clipping helps students to develop the skills required to map and clip objects in a computer graphics system. The hands-on practice and further reading resources will help students to develop their skills and deepen their understanding of the material.
