## Unit 3 - Three Dimensional

Three-dimensional geometry is the study of shapes and objects in three-dimensional space. This includes the study of points, lines, planes, and other geometric figures in three dimensions.

Some key concepts in three-dimensional geometry include:

1. Coordinate systems: In three-dimensional space, a point can be represented using a coordinate system, such as the Cartesian coordinate system. This system uses three coordinates (x, y, z) to specify the location of a point in space.

2. Distance and angles: The distance between two points in three-dimensional space can be calculated using the distance formula. Angles between lines and planes can also be calculated using various formulas.

3. Planes and lines: A plane in three-dimensional space can be defined by a point and a normal vector, or by three non-collinear points. A line can be defined by two points or by a point and a direction vector.

4. Polyhedra: A polyhedron is a three-dimensional shape with flat faces and straight edges. Examples of polyhedra include cubes, pyramids, and prisms.

5. Surface area and volume: The surface area and volume of three-dimensional shapes can be calculated using various formulas. For example, the surface area of a cube is 6s^2, where s is the length of a side, and the volume is s^3.

These are just a few of the many concepts and topics covered in three-dimensional geometry. This subject is important for understanding the world around us and has many practical applications in fields such as engineering, architecture, and physics.
