### Ellipsoid

An ellipsoid is a quadric surface that is a three-dimensional analogue of an ellipse. It is defined as the set of points such that the sum of the distances from two fixed points (the foci) is constant. In standard position, the equation of an ellipsoid centered at the origin with semi-axes of lengths a, b, and c aligned with the x, y, and z axes, respectively, is given by:

x^2/a^2 + y^2/b^2 + z^2/c^2 = 1

- An ellipsoid has three axes of symmetry, which intersect at the center of the ellipsoid.
- The lengths of the semi-axes are the principal radii of the ellipsoid.
- If two of the semi-axes are equal in length, the ellipsoid is an ellipsoid of revolution, also known as a spheroid.
- If all three semi-axes are equal in length, the ellipsoid is a sphere.
- The volume of an ellipsoid is given by the formula V = (4/3)πabc, where a, b, and c are the lengths of the semi-axes.
- The surface area of an ellipsoid can be approximated by the formula S ≈ 4π[(a^p * b^p + a^p * c^p + b^p * c^p)/3]^(1/p), where p is a constant typically chosen to be between 1.6 and 1.7.
