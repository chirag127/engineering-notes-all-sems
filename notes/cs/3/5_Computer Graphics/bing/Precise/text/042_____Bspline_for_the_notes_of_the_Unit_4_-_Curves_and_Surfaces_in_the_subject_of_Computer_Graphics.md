### Bspline for the notes of the Unit 4 - Curves and Surfaces in the subject of Computer Graphics

- B-spline, or basis spline, is a piecewise-defined polynomial curve that is represented in terms of a set of control points and a set of basis functions.
- B-splines are commonly used in computer graphics, computer-aided design, and numerical analysis.
- The term B-spline was introduced by Isaac Jacob Schoenberg and is short for basis spline.
- B-splines can be used to approximate other curves and can be used to represent curves of any degree.
- B-splines have several desirable properties, including local control, smoothness, and the ability to represent a wide range of shapes.
- B-spline curves are defined by a set of control points and a knot vector.
- The control points determine the shape of the curve, while the knot vector determines the parametrization of the curve.
- B-spline curves can be evaluated using the Cox-de Boor recursion formula.
- B-spline curves can be subdivided, which means that a B-spline curve can be split into two B-spline curves that join smoothly at the subdivision point.
- B-spline surfaces are an extension of B-spline curves and are defined by a set of control points arranged in a grid and two knot vectors.
- B-spline surfaces can be used to represent complex shapes and have many of the same desirable properties as B-spline curves.
