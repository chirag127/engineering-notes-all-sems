### Windowing and Clipping

Windowing and clipping are two important concepts in computer graphics. They are used to control the visibility of objects on the screen.

1. **Windowing** refers to the process of selecting a rectangular region of the screen, called the window, to display the graphics. The window defines the area of the screen where the graphics will be displayed. The rest of the screen is left blank.

2. **Clipping** refers to the process of removing parts of the graphics that are outside the window. This is done to ensure that only the graphics that are inside the window are displayed on the screen.

These two concepts are used together to control the visibility of objects on the screen. The window defines the area of the screen where the graphics will be displayed, and clipping removes any parts of the graphics that are outside the window.

In summary, windowing and clipping are used to control the visibility of objects on the screen. Windowing selects a rectangular region of the screen to display the graphics, and clipping removes any parts of the graphics that are outside the window. These concepts are important in computer graphics and are used to create more complex graphics and animations.
