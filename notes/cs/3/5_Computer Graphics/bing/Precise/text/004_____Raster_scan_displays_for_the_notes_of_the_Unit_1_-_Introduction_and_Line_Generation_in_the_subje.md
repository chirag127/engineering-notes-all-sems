### Raster scan displays for the notes of the Unit 1 - Introduction and Line Generation in the subject of Computer Graphics

- Raster scan displays, also known as bitmap displays, are a type of display technology used in computer graphics.
- In a raster scan display, the image is composed of a grid of pixels, with each pixel representing a single point of color or light.
- The image is generated by scanning an electron beam across the screen, with the beam turning on and off to create the individual pixels.
- The electron beam moves in a pattern known as a raster scan, moving from left to right across each row of pixels, then moving down to the next row and starting again from the left.
- Raster scan displays are commonly used in computer monitors, televisions, and other display devices.
- One advantage of raster scan displays is their ability to display a wide range of colors and shades, allowing for detailed and realistic images.
- However, raster scan displays can suffer from a number of limitations, including jagged edges on diagonal lines, and difficulty displaying very fine details.
- To address these limitations, a number of techniques have been developed, including anti-aliasing and sub-pixel rendering.
- In the context of line generation in computer graphics, rasterization algorithms are used to convert vector graphics, such as lines and curves, into the pixel-based representation used by raster scan displays.
