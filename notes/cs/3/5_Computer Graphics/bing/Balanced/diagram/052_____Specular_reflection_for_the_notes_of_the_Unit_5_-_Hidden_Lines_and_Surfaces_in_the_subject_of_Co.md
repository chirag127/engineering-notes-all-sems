### Specular reflection

- Specular reflection is the phenomenon of light reflecting off a smooth surface in a mirror-like manner, creating a bright spot or highlight on the surface.
- Specular reflection depends on the angle of incidence of the light ray, the angle of reflection of the light ray, and the viewing angle of the observer.
- The angle of incidence is equal to the angle of reflection, and both are measured with respect to the normal vector of the surface.
- The viewing angle is the angle between the reflected ray and the line of sight of the observer.
- The intensity of the specular reflection is higher when the viewing angle is smaller, meaning the observer is closer to the direction of the reflected ray.
- Specular reflection is influenced by the material properties of the surface, such as its roughness, shininess, and reflectivity .
- Rough surfaces tend to scatter light in different directions, reducing the intensity of the specular reflection.
- Shininess is a measure of how concentrated the specular reflection is around the direction of the perfect reflection.
- Reflectivity is a measure of how much light is reflected by the surface, as opposed to being absorbed or transmitted.
- Specular reflection is often modeled using empirical formulas, such as the Phong model, which was proposed by Bui-Tuong Phong in 1975 .
- The Phong model calculates the specular reflection as a function of the angle between the reflected ray and the viewing direction, raised to a power that represents the shininess of the surface.
- The Phong model also takes into account the color and intensity of the light source, and the color and reflectivity of the surface.
- Specular reflection is important in 3D computer graphics, as it provides a strong visual cue for the shape of an object and its location with respect to light sources in the scene.
- Specular reflection can be rendered using different techniques, such as ray tracing, rasterization, or shading.
- Ray tracing simulates the path of light rays from the light source to the surface, and then to the observer, accounting for reflection, refraction, and shadowing.
- Rasterization converts the 3D geometry of the scene into a 2D image, and applies the specular reflection formula to each pixel based on its normal vector and viewing direction.
- Shading is a technique that approximates the specular reflection using precomputed values stored in a texture map, such as an environment map or a normal map.
- Specular reflection can be combined with other lighting effects, such as ambient, diffuse, and emissive, to create realistic and appealing images.
