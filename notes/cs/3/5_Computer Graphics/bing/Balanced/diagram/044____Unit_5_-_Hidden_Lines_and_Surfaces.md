## Unit 5 - Hidden Lines and Surfaces

- Hidden lines and surfaces are used to represent the parts of an object that are not visible from a given viewpoint.
- Hidden lines are usually drawn as dashed or dotted lines on a 2D drawing or a 3D model.
- Hidden surfaces are usually shaded or colored differently from the visible surfaces on a 3D model or a rendering.
- The purpose of hidden lines and surfaces is to show the shape and structure of the object more clearly and completely, and to avoid ambiguity or confusion.
- Hidden lines and surfaces are also used to indicate the internal features of the object, such as holes, slots, ribs, etc.
- There are different methods and conventions for drawing hidden lines and surfaces, depending on the type of object, the projection system, the industry standards, and the personal preference of the drafter or designer.
- Some common methods and conventions are:
  - In orthographic projection, hidden lines are usually drawn as dashed lines with equal dashes and spaces, and are thinner than the visible lines.
  - In isometric projection, hidden lines are usually drawn as dashed lines with short dashes and long spaces, and are thinner than the visible lines.
  - In perspective projection, hidden lines are usually omitted or drawn as dotted lines with small dots and large spaces, and are thinner than the visible lines.
  - In 3D modeling, hidden surfaces are usually shaded or colored differently from the visible surfaces, such as gray, transparent, or wireframe.
  - In rendering, hidden surfaces are usually removed or hidden by the visible surfaces, using algorithms such as z-buffering, ray tracing, or occlusion culling.
  - In some cases, hidden lines and surfaces may be omitted or simplified to reduce the complexity or clutter of the drawing or model, especially if they are not essential or relevant for the purpose of the representation.
