### 3-D Geometric Primitives

- 3-D geometric primitives are basic geometric forms that can be used to model more complex 3-D shapes and objects.
- They are the building blocks of 3-D modeling and design, and can be modified with transforms and Booleans operations.
- Some of the most common 3-D geometric primitives are:
  - Cubes: A six-faced polyhedron with all faces being squares.
  - Pyramids: A polyhedron with a polygonal base and triangular faces that meet at a common vertex.
  - Cones: A surface that is generated by a line that passes through a fixed point (the vertex) and a curve (the base).
  - Spheres: A surface that is equidistant from a fixed point (the center).
  - Tori: A surface that is generated by revolving a circle around an axis that does not intersect the circle.
- These primitives can have a resolution level assigned to them, which determines the number of sides and steps used to define them. A higher resolution level makes the primitives look smoother, but also increases the computational complexity.
- Some 3-D modeling software packages provide other primitives, such as cylinders, prisms, platonic solids, etc.
- In some cases, curves (such as Bézier curves, circles, etc.) may be considered primitives, while in other cases, they are complex forms created from many straight, primitive shapes.
