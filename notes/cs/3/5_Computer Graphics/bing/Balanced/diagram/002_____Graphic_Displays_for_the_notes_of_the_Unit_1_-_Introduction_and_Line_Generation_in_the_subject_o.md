### Graphic Displays

- A graphic display is a device that can show images generated by a computer or other electronic system.
- There are different types of graphic displays, depending on the technology used to create and display the images.
- Some common types of graphic displays are:

  - **Raster display**: A raster display uses a grid of tiny dots called pixels to form images. Each pixel can have a different color and brightness. Raster displays are also called bitmap displays or pixel-based displays. Examples of raster displays are LCD, LED, OLED, and plasma monitors.
  - **Vector display**: A vector display uses a beam of electrons to draw lines on a phosphor-coated screen. The lines can have different colors and intensities. Vector displays are also called stroke displays or calligraphic displays. Examples of vector displays are oscilloscopes and some arcade games.
  - **Random scan display**: A random scan display is a type of vector display that draws only the lines that are needed to form the image, rather than the entire screen. This reduces the flickering and increases the resolution of the image. Random scan displays are also called direct view storage tubes (DVST) or refresh storage tubes (RST).
  - **3D display**: A 3D display is a device that can create the illusion of depth in an image, making it appear as if it has three dimensions. There are different methods to achieve this effect, such as stereoscopy, holography, volumetric display, and light field display.

- Some factors to consider when choosing a graphic display for computer graphics are:

  - **Resolution**: The resolution of a display is the number of pixels or lines that it can show on the screen. Higher resolution means more detail and clarity in the image, but also more memory and processing power required to generate it.
  - **Color depth**: The color depth of a display is the number of bits used to represent the color of each pixel or line. Higher color depth means more shades and hues of colors available, but also more memory and bandwidth required to transmit it.
  - **Refresh rate**: The refresh rate of a display is the number of times per second that it updates the image on the screen. Higher refresh rate means smoother motion and less flickering in the image, but also more power consumption and heat generation by the display.
  - **Contrast ratio**: The contrast ratio of a display is the ratio of the brightness of the brightest white to the darkest black that it can show. Higher contrast ratio means more vivid and realistic colors, but also more difficulty in adjusting the brightness and viewing angle of the display.
  - **Size and aspect ratio**: The size and aspect ratio of a display are the physical dimensions and the ratio of the width to the height of the screen. Larger and wider displays can show more information and content, but also take more space and cost more. The aspect ratio should match the intended format and orientation of the image.
