### Bspline for the notes of the Unit 4 - Curves and Surfaces in the subject of Computer Graphics

Unit 4 of the Computer Graphics course focuses on Curves and Surfaces, including the aspect of B-splines.

B-splines are a type of curve that are widely used in computer graphics to represent smooth curves and surfaces. B-splines are defined as a set of control points and a set of basis functions, and are used to interpolate or approximate a curve that passes through the control points.

B-splines have several advantages over other types of curves, including:
- Smoothness, as B-splines are defined as a set of control points and basis functions, they can be used to represent smooth curves and surfaces.
- Flexibility, B-splines can be easily modified by adjusting the control points, making them ideal for use in computer graphics applications.
- Ease of use, B-splines can be easily implemented in computer graphics programs using existing libraries and algorithms.

In the context of the Computer Graphics course, students in Unit 4 will learn about B-splines as part of the discussion on Curves and Surfaces. This may include working with examples of B-splines, analyzing the benefits and limitations of B-splines, and learning how to use B-splines to represent curves and surfaces in computer graphics.
