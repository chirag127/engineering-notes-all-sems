### Specular reflection for the notes of the Unit 5 - Hidden Lines and Surfaces in the subject of Computer Graphics

Specular reflection is a type of reflection that occurs when light is reflected from a surface in a single, concentrated direction. In computer graphics, specular reflection is used to create a shiny or metallic appearance on an object. The intensity and direction of the specular reflection is determined by the angle between the surface normal and the direction of the incoming light.

The following are some of the key concepts related to specular reflection in computer graphics:

1. Surface Normal: The surface normal is a vector that is perpendicular to a surface, and it is used to determine the direction of the specular reflection.

2. Light Direction: The direction of the incoming light is used to determine the direction of the specular reflection.

3. Specular Reflection Equation: The specular reflection equation is used to calculate the intensity and direction of the specular reflection.

4. Shininess: Shininess is a parameter that determines the size and intensity of the specular reflection.
