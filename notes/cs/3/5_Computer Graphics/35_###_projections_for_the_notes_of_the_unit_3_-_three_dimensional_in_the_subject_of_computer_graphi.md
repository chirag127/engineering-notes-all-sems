### projections for the notes of the Unit 3 - Three Dimensional in the subject of Computer Graphics

Unit 3 of the Computer Graphics course focuses on Three Dimensional graphics and includes the aspect of projections.

Projections in computer graphics are mathematical operations used to convert 3D objects into 2D images that can be displayed on a screen. There are several types of projections, including:

- Orthographic projection, which is a type of parallel projection that creates an image of a 3D object without any perspective distortion. Orthographic projections are commonly used in technical drawings and architectural plans.
- Perspective projection, which is a type of convergent projection that creates an image of a 3D object with perspective distortion, making objects that are farther away appear smaller. Perspective projections are commonly used in video games, films, and other forms of entertainment.
- Cabinet projection, which is a type of perspective projection that is commonly used in architectural drawings and interior design. Cabinet projections are similar to perspective projections, but with the camera positioned at a lower height, to create a more dramatic perspective.
- Cavalier projection, which is a type of perspective projection that is commonly used in architectural drawings and interior design. Cavalier projections are similar to cabinet projections, but with the camera positioned at a higher height, to create a more dramatic perspective.

In the context of the Computer Graphics course, students in Unit 3 will learn about projections as part of the discussion on Three Dimensional graphics. This may include working with examples of each type of projection, analyzing the benefits and limitations of each projection, and learning how to implement projections in a computer graphics program.
