### basic illumination models for the notes of the Unit 5 - Hidden Lines and Surfaces in the subject of Computer Graphics

Unit 5 of the Computer Graphics course focuses on Hidden Lines and Surfaces, including the aspect of basic illumination models.

Basic illumination models are mathematical models used to simulate the way light interacts with objects in a 3D scene. These models are used to determine the color and brightness of an object in a scene, based on the light sources and the properties of the object.

There are several basic illumination models, including:

- Ambient lighting, which is a type of lighting that provides a constant level of illumination to all objects in a scene, regardless of their position or orientation.
- Diffuse lighting, which is a type of lighting that simulates the way light scatters evenly in all directions after striking a rough surface.
- Specular lighting, which is a type of lighting that simulates the way light reflects off a smooth surface, creating highlights and reflections.
- Phong shading, which is a type of shading that combines ambient, diffuse, and specular lighting to create a more realistic representation of an object's appearance.

In the context of the Computer Graphics course, students in Unit 5 will learn about basic illumination models as part of the discussion on Hidden Lines and Surfaces. This may include working with examples of each type of basic illumination model, analyzing the benefits and limitations of each model, and learning how to implement basic illumination models in a computer graphics program.
