### 3-D viewing for the notes of the Unit 3 - Three Dimensional in the subject of Computer Graphics

3-D viewing is a fundamental aspect of computer graphics, and it involves the process of generating a 2-D representation of a 3-D scene on a computer screen. In 3-D viewing, a 3-D scene is transformed into a 2-D image by projecting the 3-D objects onto a 2-D plane.

The following are some of the key concepts related to 3-D viewing:

1. 3-D Transformations: The process of transforming 3-D objects into a 2-D representation involves a series of 3-D transformations, including translation, rotation, and scaling.

2. Viewing Coordinate System: A viewing coordinate system is used to define the position and orientation of the observer in the 3-D scene.

3. Projection: Projection is the process of projecting 3-D objects onto a 2-D plane. There are several types of projections, including parallel projection and perspective projection.

4. Clipping: Clipping is the process of removing parts of a 3-D object that are outside of the viewing volume.

In this unit, you will learn about the different types of 3-D transformations, the different types of projections, and the different types of clipping algorithms used in 3-D viewing. You will also learn how to implement 3-D viewing algorithms in a program, and you will learn about the advantages and disadvantages of different algorithms.

This unit will provide a foundation for understanding the principles and practices of 3-D viewing in computer graphics, and for exploring the various concepts and techniques used in the field of computer graphics.
