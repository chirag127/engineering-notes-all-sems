### Working with Lists and Tables

- Lists and tables are common elements in web pages that can be styled with CSS.
- Lists are used to display a series of items in a vertical or horizontal order. There are three types of lists in HTML: ordered lists, unordered lists, and definition lists.
- Ordered lists (`<ol>`) use numbers, letters, or other symbols to indicate the order of the items. Unordered lists (`<ul>`) use bullets or other markers to show the items. Definition lists (`<dl>`) consist of pairs of terms (`<dt>`) and definitions (`<dd>`).
- To style lists with CSS, you can use properties such as `list-style-type`, `list-style-image`, `list-style-position`, and `list-style` to customize the appearance of the list markers and their position relative to the list items.
- You can also use properties such as `padding`, `margin`, `border`, and `background` to style the list items and the list container.
- Tables are used to display data in rows and columns. Tables are composed of table elements such as `<table>`, `<tr>`, `<td>`, `<th>`, `<thead>`, `<tbody>`, `<tfoot>`, `<col>`, and `<colgroup>`.
- To style tables with CSS, you can use properties such as `border`, `border-collapse`, `border-spacing`, `empty-cells`, `caption-side`, and `table-layout` to control the appearance and layout of the table and its cells.
- You can also use properties such as `padding`, `margin`, `background`, `text-align`, `vertical-align`, and `width` to style the table cells and their content.
- You can use selectors such as `:nth-child()`, `:first-child`, `:last-child`, `:nth-of-type()`, `:first-of-type`, `:last-of-type`, and `:empty` to target specific table elements based on their position or content.
- You can use pseudo-elements such as `::before` and `::after` to insert content before or after table elements.
