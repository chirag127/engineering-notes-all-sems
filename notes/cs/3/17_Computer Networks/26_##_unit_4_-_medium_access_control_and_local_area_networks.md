## Unit 4 - Medium Access Control and Local Area Networks

Medium Access Control (MAC) and Local Area Networks (LANs) are two important concepts in computer networking. The following are some of the key concepts related to MAC and LANs:

1. Medium Access Control: Medium Access Control (MAC) is a sublayer of the data link layer that is responsible for controlling access to a shared communication medium, such as a cable or wireless channel.

2. Local Area Networks: Local Area Networks (LANs) are computer networks that are used to connect devices within a small geographic area, such as a building or campus.

3. Ethernet: Ethernet is a popular LAN technology that uses a combination of copper cables and optical fiber to transmit data.

4. MAC Addresses: MAC addresses are unique identifiers assigned to devices in a network, and are used by the MAC sublayer to control access to the shared communication medium.

5. Carrier Sense Multiple Access (CSMA): Carrier Sense Multiple Access (CSMA) is a MAC protocol that uses carrier sensing to avoid collisions on the shared communication medium.

6. Token Passing: Token passing is a MAC protocol that uses tokens to control access to the shared communication medium.

In this unit, you will learn about Medium Access Control (MAC) and Local Area Networks (LANs), including MAC addresses, Carrier Sense Multiple Access (CSMA), and Token Passing.

This unit will provide a foundation for understanding the principles and practices of MAC and LANs and for exploring the various concepts and techniques used in the field of computer networking.
