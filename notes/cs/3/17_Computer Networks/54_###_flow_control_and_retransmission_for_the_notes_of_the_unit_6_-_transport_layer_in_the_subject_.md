### Flow control and retransmission for the notes of the Unit 6 - Transport Layer in the subject of Computer Networks

Flow control and retransmission are two important concepts in the transport layer of computer networks. The following are some of the key concepts related to flow control and retransmission:

1. Flow Control: Flow control is the process of regulating the flow of data between devices, to prevent overloading of the network.

2. Retransmission: Retransmission is the process of retransmitting data that was lost or corrupted during transmission.

3. TCP (Transmission Control Protocol): TCP is a transport layer protocol that provides reliable, ordered, and error-checked delivery of data, and uses flow control and retransmission to ensure the delivery of data.

4. UDP (User Datagram Protocol): UDP is a transport layer protocol that provides fast, unreliable delivery of data, and does not use flow control or retransmission.

5. Window Size: The window size is the amount of data that can be transmitted without receiving an acknowledgment, and is used in flow control.

6. Timeout: A timeout is the amount of time that a device waits for an acknowledgment before retransmitting data, and is used in retransmission.

In this unit, you will learn about flow control and retransmission in computer networking, including TCP, UDP, window size, and timeout.

This unit will provide a foundation for understanding the principles and practices of the transport layer in computer networking and for exploring the various concepts and techniques used in the field of computer networks.
