### Categories of networks for the notes of the Unit 1 - Introductory Concepts in the subject of Computer Networks

Unit 1 - Introductory Concepts in the subject of Computer Networks will cover the basics of computer networks, including the categories of networks.

Computer networks are collections of interconnected devices that allow communication and resource sharing between devices. The categories of computer networks are based on the size, scope, and purpose of the network.

The main categories of computer networks are:
1. Local Area Networks (LANs) - small networks that connect devices within a limited geographic area, such as a home, office, or building.
2. Metropolitan Area Networks (MANs) - networks that connect devices within a metropolitan area, such as a city.
3. Wide Area Networks (WANs) - networks that connect devices over large geographic areas, such as countries or continents.
4. Storage Area Networks (SANs) - networks that connect storage devices, such as disk arrays, to servers.
5. Personal Area Networks (PANs) - networks that connect devices in close proximity to a person, such as a smartphone and a laptop.

In the context of Unit 1 - Introductory Concepts in the subject of Computer Networks, students will learn about the categories of computer networks, including the characteristics and uses of each type of network. This may include working with examples of networks, analyzing the differences between the categories of networks, and learning how to choose the appropriate type of network for a given situation.

In summary, in Unit 1 - Introductory Concepts in the subject of Computer Networks, students will learn about the categories of computer networks, including Local Area Networks (LANs), Metropolitan Area Networks (MANs), Wide Area Networks (WANs), Storage Area Networks (SANs), and Personal Area Networks (PANs). They will learn about the characteristics and uses of each type of network, and how to choose the appropriate type of network for a given situation.
