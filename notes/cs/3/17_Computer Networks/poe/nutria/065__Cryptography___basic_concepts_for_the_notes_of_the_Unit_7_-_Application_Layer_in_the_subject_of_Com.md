
### Cryptography – basic concepts for the Unit 7 - Application Layer in the subject of Computer Networks

1. Cryptography is the practice and study of techniques for secure communication in the presence of third parties. It is a type of security measure designed to protect data from unauthorized access or manipulation. 
2. Cryptography is used to protect sensitive information in a variety of applications, such as financial transactions, medical records, and communications between parties. 
3. Cryptography is based on the use of algorithms to encrypt and decrypt data. These algorithms use mathematical functions to transform plain text into an unreadable form, known as ciphertext. 
4. Cryptography also involves the use of digital signatures to authenticate the identity of the sender and recipient. 
5. Cryptography is used to ensure the confidentiality, integrity, and authenticity of data. It is also used to protect data from unauthorized access or manipulation. 
6. Cryptography is used in a variety of applications, such as banking, military communications, and secure communication systems. 
7. Cryptographic algorithms are divided into two categories: symmetric and asymmetric. Symmetric algorithms use a single key to both encrypt and decrypt data, while asymmetric algorithms use two keys, one for encryption and one for decryption. 
8. Cryptographic algorithms are also divided into two categories: stream ciphers and block ciphers. Stream ciphers encrypt data one bit or byte at a time, while block ciphers encrypt data in blocks of fixed length. 
9. Cryptography is also used to protect data from being modified or deleted. This is known as data integrity. 
10. Cryptography is used to protect data from being accessed by unauthorized parties. This is known as data confidentiality. 
11. Cryptography is also used to authenticate the identity of the sender and recipient of data. This is known as data authentication.
