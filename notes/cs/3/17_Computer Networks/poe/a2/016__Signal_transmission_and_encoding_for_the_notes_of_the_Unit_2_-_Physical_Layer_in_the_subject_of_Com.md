### Signal transmission and encoding for the notes of the Unit 2 - Physical Layer in the subject of Computer Networks:

1. Signal transmission: The physical layer is responsible for transmitting raw bits over a physical data link connecting two devices. The bits are converted into electromagnetic signals that are sent from the transmitting device and received by the receiving device. Various signal encoding techniques are used to represent the 1s and 0s of the digital data as an analog waveform suitable for transmission.
2. Line coding: The raw bits are encoded into signals that can be transmitted over the physical medium. Common line coding techniques are:
- Unipolar: Only one voltage level is used to represent 1s and 0s like in NRZ.
- Polar: Two voltage levels are used to represent 1s and 0s like in AMI and Pseudo-ternary.
- Multilevel: More than two voltage levels are used like in PAM, CAP and MLPPP.
3. Baseband transmission: The transmission bandwidth is equal to the bandwidth of the data signal. The encoded signal is transmitted directly over the transmission medium. Baseband transmission is simple but the high-frequency signal cannot travel long distances.
4. Broadband transmission: The transmission bandwidth is greater than the bandwidth of the data signal. The encoded signal is modulated to a high-frequency carrier signal which is then transmitted. Broadband transmission can cover longer distances but the modulation/demodulation process is complex.
