### LAN Standards for the Notes of Unit 4 - Medium Access Control and Local Area Networks in the Subject of Computer Networks

When it comes to Local Area Networks (LANs), there are several standards that have been developed over the years to ensure that devices from different manufacturers can communicate with each other. In this unit, we will discuss some of the most commonly used LAN standards. 

Here are some important points to keep in mind:

- **Ethernet:** Ethernet is one of the oldest and most widely used LAN standards. It was first developed in the 1970s by Xerox, and has since become the standard for wired LANs. Ethernet uses a carrier sense multiple access with collision detection (CSMA/CD) protocol to avoid data collisions. 

- **Wi-Fi:** Wi-Fi is a wireless LAN standard that was first introduced in the late 1990s. It is based on the IEEE 802.11 standard and is widely used for wireless networking. Wi-Fi uses radio waves to transmit data between devices, and is commonly used in homes, offices, and public spaces.

- **Token Ring:** Token Ring is an older LAN standard that was developed by IBM in the 1980s. It uses a token passing protocol to ensure that only one device can transmit data at a time. Token Ring networks are less common today as Ethernet has become the dominant wired LAN standard.

- **Fiber Distributed Data Interface (FDDI):** FDDI is a LAN standard that uses fiber optic cables to transmit data. It was developed in the 1980s as a high-speed alternative to Ethernet. FDDI uses a token passing protocol similar to Token Ring, but is faster and more reliable due to its use of fiber optic cables.

- **Powerline Communication (PLC):** PLC is a relatively new LAN standard that uses existing electrical wiring to transmit data between devices. It is commonly used in homes and small offices where running Ethernet cables may be difficult or impractical.

- **Bluetooth:** Bluetooth is a wireless LAN standard that is commonly used for short-range communication between devices. It is often used in smartphones, tablets, and other mobile devices to connect to wireless headphones, speakers, and other accessories.

By understanding these LAN standards, you will be better equipped to design and implement LANs that meet the needs of your organization or clients.
