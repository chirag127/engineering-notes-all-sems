### Switching techniques and multiplexing for the notes of the Unit 2 - Physical Layer in the subject of Computer Networks

Switching techniques and multiplexing are important concepts in the physical layer of computer networks. Here are some key points to remember:

1. **Switching techniques** refer to the methods used to transfer data between different devices in a network. There are three main types of switching techniques: circuit switching, packet switching, and message switching.

2. **Circuit switching** involves establishing a dedicated communication path between two devices for the duration of their conversation. This path is reserved exclusively for the use of these two devices until the conversation is finished.

3. **Packet switching** involves breaking data into small packets and sending them individually over the network. Each packet is routed independently, and may take a different path to reach its destination. This allows for more efficient use of network resources.

4. **Message switching** is similar to packet switching, but instead of breaking data into packets, the entire message is sent as a single unit. This technique is less commonly used than the other two.

5. **Multiplexing** refers to the process of combining multiple signals into a single signal for transmission over a shared medium. This allows multiple devices to share the same communication channel, increasing the efficiency of the network.

6. There are several types of multiplexing, including frequency division multiplexing (FDM), time division multiplexing (TDM), and code division multiple access (CDMA).

7. **Frequency division multiplexing (FDM)** involves dividing the available bandwidth into multiple frequency bands, with each band assigned to a different signal.

8. **Time division multiplexing (TDM)** involves dividing time into slots, with each slot assigned to a different signal. Each signal is transmitted in its assigned time slot, allowing multiple signals to be transmitted over the same channel.

9. **Code division multiple access (CDMA)** involves assigning a unique code to each signal, allowing multiple signals to be transmitted simultaneously over the same channel.

These are some of the key points to remember when studying switching techniques and multiplexing in the context of the physical layer of computer networks. It is important to understand these concepts in order to have a solid foundation in the subject of computer networks.
