### Cryptography – basic concepts for the notes of the Unit 7 - Application Layer in the subject of Computer Networks

Cryptography is the practice of securing communication in the presence of third parties. It involves techniques for secure communication, data storage, and data transmission. Here are some basic concepts in cryptography:

1. **Encryption**: The process of converting plaintext (readable data) into ciphertext (encoded data) using an encryption algorithm and a key.
2. **Decryption**: The reverse process of encryption, where ciphertext is converted back into plaintext using a decryption algorithm and a key.
3. **Key**: A piece of information used in encryption and decryption algorithms to control the transformation of plaintext into ciphertext and vice versa.
4. **Symmetric-key cryptography**: A type of cryptography where the same key is used for both encryption and decryption.
5. **Asymmetric-key cryptography**: A type of cryptography where different keys are used for encryption and decryption. It involves the use of a public key and a private key.
6. **Hash function**: A mathematical function that takes an input and produces a fixed-size output, commonly used for verifying the integrity of data.
7. **Digital signature**: A cryptographic technique used to verify the authenticity and integrity of a message or document.
8. **Certificate authority (CA)**: An entity that issues digital certificates, which are used to verify the identity of the owner of a public key.

These are some of the basic concepts in cryptography that are important to understand in the context of the application layer in computer networks. Cryptography plays a crucial role in ensuring the security and privacy of data transmitted over a network.
