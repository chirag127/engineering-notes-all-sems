## Unit 2 - Physical Layer

The physical layer is the first layer of the OSI model. It is responsible for the transmission and reception of raw data between a device and a physical transmission medium. The physical layer is concerned with the following aspects of data transmission:

1. Physical characteristics of interfaces and media: This includes the mechanical, electrical, and functional specifications of the interfaces and transmission media.

2. Representation of bits: The physical layer defines how bits are encoded and decoded into electrical, optical, or radio signals.

3. Data rate: The physical layer determines the transmission rate of data, measured in bits per second.

4. Synchronization of bits: The physical layer provides synchronization of the bit stream by providing a clock signal to the sender and receiver.

5. Line configuration: The physical layer defines the way in which devices are connected to the transmission medium, such as point-to-point or multipoint configurations.

6. Physical topology: The physical layer defines the physical layout of the network, such as bus, star, ring, or mesh topologies.

7. Transmission mode: The physical layer determines the direction of data transmission, such as simplex, half-duplex, or full-duplex modes.

The physical layer is responsible for the actual transmission of data over the physical medium. It converts the digital data into a form that can be transmitted over the physical medium, such as electrical signals, light pulses, or radio waves. The physical layer also provides error detection and correction mechanisms to ensure the reliable transmission of data.
