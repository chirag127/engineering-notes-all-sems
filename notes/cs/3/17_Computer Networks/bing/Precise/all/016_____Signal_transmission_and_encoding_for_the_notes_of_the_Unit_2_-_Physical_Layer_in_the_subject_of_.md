# Signal Transmission and Encoding

Signal transmission and encoding are important concepts in the physical layer of computer networks. Here are some key points to remember:

1. **Signal transmission** refers to the process of transmitting data from one point to another in the form of electromagnetic or optical signals.
2. **Encoding** is the process of converting data into a format that can be transmitted over a communication channel.
3. There are several types of encoding techniques, including **analog encoding** and **digital encoding**.
4. Analog encoding involves the use of continuous signals to represent data, while digital encoding uses discrete signals.
5. Some common analog encoding techniques include **amplitude modulation (AM)**, **frequency modulation (FM)**, and **phase modulation (PM)**.
6. Some common digital encoding techniques include **non-return-to-zero (NRZ)**, **return-to-zero (RZ)**, and **Manchester encoding**.
7. The choice of encoding technique depends on factors such as the type of communication channel, the data rate, and the level of noise and interference.
8. The physical layer is responsible for converting the digital data from the upper layers into signals that can be transmitted over the communication channel.
