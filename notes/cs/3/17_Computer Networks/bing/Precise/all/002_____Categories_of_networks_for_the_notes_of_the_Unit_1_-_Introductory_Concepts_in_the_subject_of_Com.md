# Categories of Networks

In the subject of Computer Networks, Unit 1 - Introductory Concepts, one of the important topics is the categories of networks. Networks can be categorized based on their size, geographical area, and the purpose they serve. Here are the main categories of networks:

1. **Local Area Network (LAN):** A LAN is a network that connects computers and devices within a limited geographical area, such as a home, school, or office building. LANs are typically used for sharing resources, such as printers and files, among the connected devices.

2. **Wide Area Network (WAN):** A WAN is a network that connects computers and devices over a large geographical area, such as between cities or even countries. WANs are typically used by organizations to connect their various branches and offices.

3. **Metropolitan Area Network (MAN):** A MAN is a network that connects computers and devices within a metropolitan area, such as a city or a large campus. MANs are typically used by organizations to connect their various branches within a city.

4. **Personal Area Network (PAN):** A PAN is a network that connects devices within the personal space of an individual, such as a person's smartphone, laptop, and smartwatch. PANs are typically used for personal communication and data transfer.

5. **Storage Area Network (SAN):** A SAN is a network that connects storage devices, such as hard drives and tape libraries, to servers. SANs are typically used by organizations to provide fast and reliable access to large amounts of data storage.

6. **Virtual Private Network (VPN):** A VPN is a network that uses the public Internet to connect remote sites or users together, but with the added security of encryption and authentication. VPNs are typically used by organizations to provide secure remote access to their network for employees working from home or traveling.

These are the main categories of networks that are covered in Unit 1 - Introductory Concepts of the subject of Computer Networks. Each category serves a specific purpose and has its own characteristics and features.
