## Unit 4 - Medium Access Control and Local Area Networks

1. **Medium Access Control (MAC)** is a sublayer of the Data Link Layer in the OSI model. It is responsible for controlling how devices in a network gain access to a shared medium and transmit data.
2. MAC protocols are used to coordinate the access of multiple devices to a shared communication medium, such as a cable or wireless channel.
3. Some common MAC protocols include Carrier Sense Multiple Access with Collision Detection (CSMA/CD), Carrier Sense Multiple Access with Collision Avoidance (CSMA/CA), and Time Division Multiple Access (TDMA).
4. **Local Area Networks (LANs)** are computer networks that span a relatively small area, such as a single building or group of buildings.
5. LANs are typically used to connect computers and other devices within a limited area, allowing them to share resources such as printers, files, and internet access.
6. Ethernet is a widely used LAN technology that uses MAC protocols such as CSMA/CD to coordinate access to the shared medium.
7. Wireless LANs (WLANs) use radio waves to transmit data between devices, and typically use MAC protocols such as CSMA/CA to coordinate access to the shared medium.
8. WLANs are commonly used in homes, offices, and public places to provide wireless internet access.
