### Quality of Service (QoS) - Transport Layer in Computer Networks

Quality of Service (QoS) refers to the ability of a network to provide improved service to certain network traffic. This is achieved by providing dedicated bandwidth, controlled jitter and latency, and improved loss characteristics.

1. **Bandwidth:** QoS mechanisms can allocate a minimum amount of bandwidth to certain traffic, ensuring that it always has enough resources to function properly.
2. **Jitter:** Jitter refers to the variation in delay of packets. QoS mechanisms can control jitter by buffering packets and releasing them at a constant rate.
3. **Latency:** Latency refers to the time it takes for a packet to travel from the sender to the receiver. QoS mechanisms can reduce latency by prioritizing certain traffic and routing it through faster paths.
4. **Loss:** QoS mechanisms can improve loss characteristics by reserving resources for certain traffic and using error correction techniques.

QoS is important for applications that require a certain level of performance, such as voice and video streaming, online gaming, and real-time data transmission. QoS mechanisms can be implemented at different layers of the OSI model, including the transport layer. At the transport layer, QoS can be achieved through the use of protocols such as the Resource Reservation Protocol (RSVP) and the Real-time Transport Protocol (RTP).
