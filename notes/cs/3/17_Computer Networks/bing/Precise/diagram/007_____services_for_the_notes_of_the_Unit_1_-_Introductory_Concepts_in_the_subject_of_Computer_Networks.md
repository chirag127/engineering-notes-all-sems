### Services
- In the context of computer networks, a service refers to a set of functions or capabilities provided by one system or application to another over a network.
- Services can be categorized into two types: connection-oriented and connectionless.
- Connection-oriented services establish a dedicated connection between the communicating devices before any data is transmitted. This type of service is reliable and ensures that data is delivered in the correct order.
- Connectionless services, on the other hand, do not establish a dedicated connection before transmitting data. Instead, data is sent in individual packets, each with its own destination address. This type of service is faster but less reliable than connection-oriented services.
- Some common examples of network services include file transfer, email, remote login, and web browsing.
- Network services are typically provided by servers, which are specialized computers or programs that listen for and respond to requests from other devices on the network.
- The quality of a network service is often measured by its availability, reliability, and performance.
- Protocols, such as the Transmission Control Protocol (TCP) and the User Datagram Protocol (UDP), are used to implement network services and ensure that data is transmitted correctly between devices.
- Network services are an essential component of modern computer networks and enable the sharing of resources and information between devices.
