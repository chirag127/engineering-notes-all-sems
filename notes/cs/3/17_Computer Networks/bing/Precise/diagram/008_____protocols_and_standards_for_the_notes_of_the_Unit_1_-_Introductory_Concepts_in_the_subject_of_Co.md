### Unit 1 - Introductory Concepts: Protocols and Standards

- **Protocols** are a set of rules and procedures that govern the communication between devices on a network. They define how data is transmitted, formatted, and processed.

- **Standards** are agreed-upon guidelines that ensure interoperability between different devices and systems. They are developed by organizations such as the International Organization for Standardization (ISO) and the Institute of Electrical and Electronics Engineers (IEEE).

- Some common protocols used in computer networks include:
  - **Transmission Control Protocol (TCP)**: a reliable, connection-oriented protocol used for transmitting data over the internet.
  - **User Datagram Protocol (UDP)**: a connectionless protocol used for transmitting data over the internet, often used for real-time applications such as online gaming or video streaming.
  - **Internet Protocol (IP)**: a protocol used for routing data across the internet.
  - **Hypertext Transfer Protocol (HTTP)**: a protocol used for transmitting data over the World Wide Web.

- Standards play a crucial role in ensuring that devices from different manufacturers can communicate with each other. Some common standards used in computer networks include:
  - **Ethernet**: a standard for transmitting data over a local area network (LAN).
  - **Wi-Fi**: a standard for transmitting data over a wireless local area network (WLAN).
  - **Bluetooth**: a standard for transmitting data over short distances between devices.

- Protocols and standards are essential for ensuring the smooth operation of computer networks. They provide a common language for devices to communicate with each other and ensure that data is transmitted reliably and efficiently.
