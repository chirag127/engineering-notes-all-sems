### Network Structure and Architecture

#### Unit 1 - Introductory Concepts

1. **Network structure** refers to the layout or arrangement of the components of a computer network. These components include nodes, links, and other devices that facilitate communication and data transfer between devices.

2. **Network architecture** refers to the design principles, protocols, and standards that govern the operation of a computer network. It defines how data is transmitted, routed, and processed within the network.

3. Common network structures include **bus**, **ring**, **star**, **mesh**, and **tree** topologies. Each topology has its own advantages and disadvantages in terms of scalability, reliability, and performance.

4. Network architecture can be categorized into two main types: **peer-to-peer** and **client-server**. In a peer-to-peer architecture, all devices on the network have equal status and can communicate directly with each other. In a client-server architecture, devices are divided into clients and servers, with servers providing services to clients.

5. Network architecture also includes the **protocol stack**, which is a hierarchical set of protocols that govern communication between devices on the network. Common protocol stacks include the **TCP/IP** and **OSI** models.

6. The design of a network's structure and architecture is crucial to its performance, reliability, and security. Careful planning and consideration of the network's requirements and goals are necessary to ensure its success.
