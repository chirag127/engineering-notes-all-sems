### Cryptography – basic concepts

Cryptography is the study and practice of techniques for secure communication in the presence of third parties called adversaries. It deals with developing and analyzing protocols which prevent malicious third parties from retrieving information being shared between two entities, thereby following the various aspects of information security.

Some of the basic concepts of cryptography are:

- **Encryption**: Encryption is the process of transforming plaintext (readable data) into ciphertext (unreadable data) using a secret key and an algorithm . Encryption ensures confidentiality and integrity of the data by making it unreadable to unauthorized parties.
- **Decryption**: Decryption is the reverse process of encryption, where ciphertext is converted back to plaintext using the same or a different secret key and algorithm . Decryption enables the authorized parties to recover the original data from the ciphertext.
- **Key**: A key is a piece of information that is used to encrypt or decrypt data. The key determines the output of the encryption or decryption algorithm . The key should be kept secret from the adversaries, as it is the only way to access the encrypted or decrypted data.
- **Algorithm**: An algorithm is a set of rules or steps that are followed to perform encryption or decryption. The algorithm takes the plaintext or ciphertext and the key as inputs and produces the ciphertext or plaintext as outputs . The algorithm should be secure and efficient, meaning that it should be hard to break and fast to execute.
- **Symmetric cryptography**: Symmetric cryptography is a type of cryptography where the same key is used for both encryption and decryption. The key is shared between the sender and the receiver of the data, and they must keep it secret from the adversaries . Symmetric cryptography is fast and simple, but it requires a secure way to distribute the key.
- **Asymmetric cryptography**: Asymmetric cryptography, also known as public-key cryptography, is a type of cryptography where different keys are used for encryption and decryption. The keys are called public key and private key, and they are mathematically related to each other . The public key can be freely distributed to anyone, while the private key is kept secret by the owner . When a message is encrypted with the public key, only the owner of the corresponding private key can decrypt it, and vice versa . Asymmetric cryptography is more secure and flexible than symmetric cryptography, but it is also slower and more complex.
- **Hashing**: Hashing is the process of generating a fixed-length string, called a hash or a digest, from any input data, using a one-way function . Hashing ensures integrity and authenticity of the data, by verifying that the data has not been modified or tampered with. Hashing is not encryption, as it cannot be reversed to recover the original data.
