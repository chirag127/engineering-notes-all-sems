## Unit 1 - Introductory Concepts

- This unit covers some basic concepts and definitions that are essential for the study of economics.
- Economics is the social science that studies how people make choices under conditions of scarcity and uncertainty.
- Scarcity means that human wants are unlimited, but the resources available to satisfy them are limited.
- Resources are the inputs used to produce goods and services. They can be classified into four categories: land, labor, capital, and entrepreneurship.
- A good is anything that satisfies human wants, and a service is an intangible activity that satisfies human wants.
- Goods and services can be further classified into private and public goods, and into final and intermediate goods.
- Private goods are those that are both rival and excludable, meaning that their consumption by one person reduces the amount available for others, and that people can be prevented from consuming them if they do not pay.
- Public goods are those that are both non-rival and non-excludable, meaning that their consumption by one person does not reduce the amount available for others, and that people cannot be prevented from consuming them even if they do not pay.
- Final goods are those that are consumed by the end users, and intermediate goods are those that are used as inputs to produce other goods.
- Opportunity cost is the value of the next best alternative that is forgone as a result of making a choice. It measures the trade-off between two options.
- A production possibilities frontier (PPF) is a graph that shows the maximum combinations of two goods that can be produced with a given amount of resources and technology, assuming full and efficient use of resources.
- The PPF illustrates the concepts of scarcity, choice, opportunity cost, efficiency, and economic growth.
- The slope of the PPF represents the marginal opportunity cost of producing one good in terms of the other good.
- The PPF can shift outward or inward due to changes in the quantity or quality of resources, or due to changes in technology.
- The PPF can also be used to show the concept of comparative advantage, which is the ability to produce a good at a lower opportunity cost than another producer.
- Comparative advantage is the basis for trade and specialization, which can increase the total output and welfare of all trading parties.
