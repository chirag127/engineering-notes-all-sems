### Types of connections for the notes of the Unit 2 - Physical Layer in the subject of Computer Networks

- The physical layer is the lowest layer in the OSI model that deals with the physical and electrical aspects of data transmission.
- The physical layer provides an interface to the transmission medium, such as cables, wires, radio waves, etc.
- The physical layer defines the characteristics of the transmission medium, such as voltage levels, bit rates, modulation schemes, etc.
- The physical layer also defines the physical network topology, which is the arrangement of devices and connections in a network.
- There are two main types of physical network topologies: bus and star.
  - Bus topology: In this topology, all devices are connected to a single cable, called the bus. The bus acts as a shared communication medium for the devices. The advantage of this topology is that it is simple and cheap to implement. The disadvantage is that if the bus breaks, the entire network fails. Also, the performance of the network degrades as the number of devices increases.
  - Star topology: In this topology, each device has a dedicated connection to a central device, called the hub or the switch. The hub or the switch acts as a relay for the data transmission between the devices. The advantage of this topology is that it is easy to add or remove devices, and the network performance is not affected by the number of devices. The disadvantage is that it requires more cables and devices, and if the hub or the switch fails, the entire network fails.
- There are other types of physical network topologies, such as ring, mesh, tree, etc., but they are less common or derived from the bus or star topologies.
- The physical layer also defines the physical network components, such as connectors, plugs, receivers, transmitters, etc.
- The physical layer also defines the physical network standards, such as Ethernet, Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, etc., that specify the protocols and specifications for data transmission over different media.
