### Transmission media for the notes of the Unit 2 - Physical Layer in the subject of Computer Networks

- Transmission media in computer networks is a communication channel between sender and receiver and it is used to transfer data from one network to another  .
- Transmission media can be classified into two types: guided media and unguided media.
- Guided media, also known as wired or bounded media, use physical links to direct and confine the signals being transmitted. Examples of guided media are twisted pair cables, coaxial cables, and optical fibers .
- Unguided media, also known as wireless or unbounded media, use electromagnetic waves to carry the signals through the air or space. Examples of unguided media are radio waves, microwaves, infrared waves, and visible light .
- The choice of transmission media depends on several factors, such as the distance, speed, security, cost, and reliability of the communication .
- The physical layer of the computer network is responsible for encoding, transmitting, and receiving the data bits over the transmission media. It also defines the network architecture components, such as hardware, software, network topology, and communication protocols .
