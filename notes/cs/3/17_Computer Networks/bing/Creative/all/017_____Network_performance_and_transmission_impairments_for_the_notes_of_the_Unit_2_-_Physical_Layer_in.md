# Network performance and transmission impairments

- Network performance is the measure of how well a network can deliver data and services to its users.
- Network performance can be affected by various factors, such as bandwidth, latency, jitter, packet loss, throughput, availability, reliability, and security.
- Transmission impairments are the damage or harm caused to the signal during the signal transmission. They may also be called noise or interference.
- Transmission impairments can degrade the quality and integrity of the signal, resulting in errors, distortion, or loss of data.
- There are various types of transmission impairments, such as:

  - Attenuation: The gradual loss of signal strength as it travels through the medium. Attenuation depends on the distance, frequency, and characteristics of the medium. It can be compensated by using amplifiers or repeaters.
  - Delay distortion: The variation in signal propagation time due to the different frequencies or modes of the signal. Delay distortion can cause the signal to spread or smear, resulting in overlapping or misalignment of bits. It can be reduced by using equalizers or filters.
  - Noise: The unwanted or random signals that interfere with the original signal. Noise can be classified into four types:

    - Thermal noise: The random fluctuations of electric current due to the thermal agitation of electrons in the medium. Thermal noise depends on the temperature and resistance of the medium. It can be minimized by using low-resistance and low-temperature materials.
    - Induced noise: The noise that is induced by external sources, such as motors, appliances, power lines, or other signals. Induced noise can be minimized by using shielding, twisting, or differential signaling techniques.
    - Crosstalk: The noise that is caused by the coupling of signals from one wire or channel to another. Crosstalk can be minimized by using proper spacing, insulation, or filtering techniques.
    - Impulse noise: The noise that is caused by sudden and irregular changes in the signal, such as spikes, surges, or glitches. Impulse noise can be minimized by using error detection and correction techniques.
