## Unit 2 - Physical Layer

The physical layer is the lowest layer of the OSI model. It is responsible for transmitting and receiving raw bits over a physical medium. The physical layer covers the following topics:

- Data encoding: The process of converting digital data into electrical, optical, or radio signals that can be transmitted over a physical medium. The physical layer uses different encoding schemes, such as NRZ, RZ, Manchester, 4B/5B, etc., to represent bits with different voltage levels, pulse widths, or frequencies.
- Transmission modes: The way of transferring data between two devices. The physical layer supports three transmission modes: simplex, half-duplex, and full-duplex. In simplex mode, data can only flow in one direction, such as from a transmitter to a receiver. In half-duplex mode, data can flow in both directions, but not at the same time, such as in a walkie-talkie. In full-duplex mode, data can flow in both directions simultaneously, such as in a telephone.
- Multiplexing: The technique of combining multiple signals into one signal that can be transmitted over a single medium. The physical layer uses different multiplexing methods, such as frequency division multiplexing (FDM), time division multiplexing (TDM), wavelength division multiplexing (WDM), etc., to increase the efficiency and capacity of the transmission medium.
- Switching: The process of connecting multiple devices in a network and transferring data from one device to another. The physical layer uses different switching techniques, such as circuit switching, packet switching, message switching, etc., to establish, maintain, and terminate connections between devices.
- Transmission media: The physical medium that carries the data signals from one device to another. The physical layer supports different types of transmission media, such as twisted pair, coaxial cable, fiber optic cable, radio waves, microwaves, infrared, etc., each with different characteristics, such as bandwidth, attenuation, noise, interference, etc.
- Error detection and correction: The process of detecting and correcting errors that may occur during data transmission. The physical layer uses different error detection and correction methods, such as parity check, checksum, cyclic redundancy check (CRC), hamming code, etc., to ensure the reliability and accuracy of data transmission.
