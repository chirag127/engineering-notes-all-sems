## Unit 1 - Introductory Concepts

- This unit covers some basic concepts and terms that are essential for understanding the field of artificial intelligence (AI).
- AI is the study of how to create machines or software that can perform tasks that normally require human intelligence, such as reasoning, learning, planning, decision making, perception, natural language processing, etc.
- AI can be classified into different types based on the goals, methods, and applications of the systems. Some common types are:
  - Weak AI or narrow AI: systems that are designed to perform a specific task or domain, such as face recognition, chess playing, spam filtering, etc. They do not have general intelligence or understanding of other domains.
  - Strong AI or general AI: systems that can perform any intellectual task that a human can, such as common sense reasoning, creativity, general knowledge, etc. They have human-like or superhuman intelligence and can adapt to new situations and domains.
  - Artificial neural networks (ANNs): systems that are inspired by the structure and function of biological neurons and can learn from data and experience. They are composed of layers of interconnected nodes that process information and adjust their weights based on feedback. They can be used for various tasks such as classification, regression, clustering, etc.
  - Expert systems: systems that use a knowledge base and a set of rules to provide solutions or advice for specific problems or domains, such as medical diagnosis, legal reasoning, etc. They use inference mechanisms to apply the rules to the facts and derive conclusions or recommendations.
  - Natural language processing (NLP): systems that can understand, generate, and manipulate natural language, such as speech, text, etc. They can be used for various tasks such as translation, summarization, sentiment analysis, question answering, etc.
  - Computer vision: systems that can process and interpret visual information, such as images, videos, etc. They can be used for various tasks such as face detection, object recognition, scene understanding, etc.
  - Robotics: systems that can sense and interact with the physical world through mechanical devices, such as robots, drones, etc. They can be used for various tasks such as navigation, manipulation, exploration, etc.
  - Machine learning (ML): systems that can learn from data and experience without being explicitly programmed. They can be used for various tasks such as prediction, classification, clustering, etc. They use various algorithms and techniques such as supervised learning, unsupervised learning, reinforcement learning, etc.
- AI systems can also be evaluated based on different criteria and measures, such as:
  - Accuracy: the degree to which the system produces correct or expected results or outputs.
  - Efficiency: the amount of time, space, or resources that the system consumes or requires to perform a task or function.
  - Robustness: the ability of the system to handle errors, uncertainties, or changes in the input, environment, or conditions.
  - Explainability: the degree to which the system can provide reasons or justifications for its results or outputs.
  - Ethicality: the degree to which the system respects and follows the moral values and principles of humans and society.
