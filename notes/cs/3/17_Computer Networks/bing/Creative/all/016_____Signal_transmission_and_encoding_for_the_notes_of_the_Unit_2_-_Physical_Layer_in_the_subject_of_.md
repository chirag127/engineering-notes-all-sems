# Signal transmission and encoding

- Signal transmission is the process of sending digital or analog data over a physical medium, such as a wire, a cable, or a wireless channel.
- Encoding is the process of converting data into a format that can be transmitted and recognized by the sender and the receiver.
- Encoding can be classified into two types: digital-to-digital encoding and analog-to-digital encoding.
- Digital-to-digital encoding is the representation of digital information by a digital signal, such as a series of voltage pulses or light flashes.
- Analog-to-digital encoding is the representation of analog information by a digital signal, such as a sequence of bits or symbols.
- Encoding techniques can be categorized into four groups: unipolar, polar, bipolar, and Manchester.
- Unipolar encoding uses only one voltage level to represent a bit. For example, 0 volts for 0 and 5 volts for 1.
- Polar encoding uses two voltage levels to represent a bit. For example, -5 volts for 0 and 5 volts for 1.
- Bipolar encoding uses three voltage levels to represent a bit. For example, -5 volts for 0, 0 volts for no change, and 5 volts for 1.
- Manchester encoding uses a transition in the middle of each bit period to represent a bit. For example, a low-to-high transition for 0 and a high-to-low transition for 1.
- Encoding techniques have different advantages and disadvantages in terms of bandwidth, power, noise, and synchronization.
