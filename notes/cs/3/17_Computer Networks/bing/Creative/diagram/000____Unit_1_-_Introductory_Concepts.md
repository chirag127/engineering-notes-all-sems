## Unit 1 - Introductory Concepts

This unit covers some basic concepts that are essential for understanding the rest of the course. These include:

- What is artificial intelligence (AI) and how it differs from other types of computing.
- What are the main goals and challenges of AI research and applications.
- What are some of the common methods and techniques used in AI, such as search, logic, knowledge representation, machine learning, natural language processing, computer vision, robotics, etc.
- What are some of the ethical, social, and legal implications of AI, such as bias, fairness, privacy, accountability, etc.

The following are some of the key points to remember from this unit:

- AI is the study of how to create machines or systems that can perform tasks that normally require human intelligence, such as reasoning, learning, planning, decision making, etc.
- AI is a broad and interdisciplinary field that draws from various disciplines, such as computer science, mathematics, logic, psychology, linguistics, philosophy, etc.
- AI can be classified into different types, such as weak or strong, narrow or general, symbolic or sub-symbolic, etc., depending on the level of intelligence, scope of application, and representation of knowledge.
- AI can be applied to various domains and problems, such as games, education, health, business, security, etc., to enhance human capabilities, automate tasks, or provide new services or insights.
- AI can also pose various risks and challenges, such as ethical dilemmas, social impacts, legal issues, technical limitations, etc., that need to be addressed and regulated by the AI community and society at large.
