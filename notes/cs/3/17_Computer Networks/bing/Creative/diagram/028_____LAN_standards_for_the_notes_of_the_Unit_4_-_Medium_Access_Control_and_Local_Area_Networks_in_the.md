### LAN standards

- A local area network (LAN) is a data communication network connecting various terminals or computers within a building or limited geographical area.
- The connection among the devices could be wired or wireless.
- The Institute of Electrical and Electronics Engineers (IEEE) has defined a series of standards for LAN technologies under the 802 family.
- Some of the best-known IEEE standards for LANs are as follows:
  - IEEE 802.1: LAN/MAN internetworking standards, such as bridging and VLAN.
  - IEEE 802.3: Ethernet, the most widely used LAN technology, using twisted-pair or coaxial cable.
  - IEEE 802.5: Token Ring, a LAN technology using a token-passing mechanism on a ring topology.
  - IEEE 802.11: Wireless LAN (WLAN), a LAN technology using radio waves or infrared signals.
- Each IEEE standard specifies the physical layer and the media access control (MAC) sublayer of the LAN.
- The physical layer defines the characteristics of the transmission medium, such as voltage levels, bit rates, connectors, etc.
- The MAC sublayer defines the rules for accessing the shared medium, such as collision detection, collision avoidance, polling, etc.
- The IEEE standards are compatible with the higher layers of the OSI model, such as the network layer and the transport layer.
- The IEEE standards enable interoperability and compatibility among different LAN technologies and devices.
