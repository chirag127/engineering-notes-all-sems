### Link layer switches & bridges

- Switches and bridges are devices that operate at the data link layer (layer 2) of the OSI model and connect multiple LANs together to form a larger LAN  .
- Switches and bridges use MAC addresses to forward data frames according to switching requirements .
- Switches and bridges can filter, forward, or flood frames based on the destination MAC address and a MAC address table that maps MAC addresses to ports.
- Switches and bridges can learn MAC addresses by observing the source MAC address of incoming frames and updating the MAC address table accordingly.
- Switches and bridges can improve network performance by reducing collision domains and increasing bandwidth .
- Switches and bridges can also provide some security features by isolating traffic and preventing unauthorized access.
- Switches and bridges can be classified into different types based on their functionality and features, such as:
  - Transparent or source routing bridges: Transparent bridges use the spanning tree protocol to avoid loops and select the best path for forwarding frames, while source routing bridges use the source routing information in the frame header to determine the path.
  - Local or remote bridges: Local bridges connect LANs in the same geographical area, while remote bridges connect LANs over a wide area network (WAN) using a point-to-point link.
  - Simple or multiport bridges: Simple bridges have only two ports, while multiport bridges have more than two ports and can connect more than two LANs.
  - Layer 3 or multilayer switches: Layer 3 switches can also forward data at the network layer (layer 3) by incorporating routing functionality, while multilayer switches can forward data at multiple layers of the OSI model .
  - Wireless access points: Wireless access points are specialized bridges that create a link between a wired and a wireless LAN.
