## Unit 6 - Transport Layer

The transport layer is the fourth layer of the OSI model. It is responsible for providing end-to-end communication services between applications running on different hosts. The transport layer performs the following functions:

- **Segmentation and reassembly**: The transport layer divides the data received from the application layer into smaller units called segments, and adds a header to each segment. The header contains information such as source and destination port numbers, sequence and acknowledgment numbers, and checksum. The transport layer at the destination reassembles the segments into the original data.
- **Connection-oriented and connectionless communication**: The transport layer can provide either connection-oriented or connectionless communication services, depending on the protocol used. Connection-oriented communication involves establishing, maintaining, and terminating a logical connection between the source and destination hosts. Connectionless communication does not require a connection setup or teardown, and sends data as independent datagrams.
- **Reliable and unreliable delivery**: The transport layer can provide either reliable or unreliable delivery of data, depending on the protocol used. Reliable delivery ensures that the data is delivered without errors, duplication, or loss, and in the correct order. Unreliable delivery does not guarantee any of these properties, and may result in data corruption, loss, or reordering.
- **Flow control**: The transport layer can regulate the rate of data transmission between the source and destination hosts, to prevent the sender from overwhelming the receiver. Flow control can be implemented using mechanisms such as sliding window, stop-and-wait, or credit-based schemes.
- **Congestion control**: The transport layer can detect and avoid network congestion, which occurs when the demand for network resources exceeds the available capacity. Congestion control can be implemented using mechanisms such as additive increase multiplicative decrease (AIMD), slow start, congestion avoidance, and fast recovery.
- **Multiplexing and demultiplexing**: The transport layer can enable multiple applications to share the same network connection, by using port numbers to identify the source and destination applications. Multiplexing is the process of combining data from different applications into a single stream of segments. Demultiplexing is the process of separating data from different applications based on the port numbers in the segment headers.
- **Error detection and correction**: The transport layer can detect and correct errors in the data transmission, by using checksums or other error detection codes in the segment headers. The transport layer can also use retransmission, acknowledgment, and timeout mechanisms to ensure that the data is delivered correctly.

Some of the common protocols used in the transport layer are:

- **Transmission Control Protocol (TCP)**: TCP is a connection-oriented, reliable, and full-duplex protocol that provides byte-stream service. TCP uses a three-way handshake to establish a connection, and a four-way handshake to terminate a connection. TCP uses sequence and acknowledgment numbers to ensure reliable and in-order delivery of data. TCP also uses sliding window, slow start, congestion avoidance, and fast recovery algorithms to provide flow and congestion control.
- **User Datagram Protocol (UDP)**: UDP is a connectionless, unreliable, and best-effort protocol that provides datagram service. UDP does not perform any connection setup or teardown, and does not guarantee reliable or in-order delivery of data. UDP also does not provide any flow or congestion control. UDP is suitable for applications that require low latency, high throughput, or multicast/broadcast communication.
- **Stream Control Transmission Protocol (SCTP)**: SCTP is a connection-oriented, reliable, and full-duplex protocol that provides message-oriented service. SCTP supports multiple streams of data within a single connection, and allows for partial reliability and unordered delivery of data. SCTP also provides features such as multihoming, path selection, and congestion control. SCTP is designed for applications that require high availability, mobility, or security.
