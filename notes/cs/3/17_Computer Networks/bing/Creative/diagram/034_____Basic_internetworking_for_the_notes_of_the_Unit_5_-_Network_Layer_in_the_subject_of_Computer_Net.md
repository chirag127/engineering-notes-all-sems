### Basic internetworking for the notes of the Unit 5 - Network Layer in the subject of Computer Networks

- The network layer is the part of the Internet communications process where packets of data are sent back and forth between different networks.
- The network layer is also called the Internet layer in the TCP/IP model, which is a simpler and more widely used model than the OSI model.
- The network layer is responsible for routing, addressing, fragmentation, and reassembly of packets.
- Internetworking is the process of connecting different physical networks into a logical network using routers and other devices.
- Internetworking allows data to be transmitted across heterogeneous networks that may have different architectures, protocols, and topologies.
- The most notable example of internetworking is the Internet, which is a global network of networks.
- Internetworking requires three types of addresses: data-link layer addresses, network-layer addresses, and media access control (MAC) addresses.
- Data-link layer addresses are used to identify each physical network connection of a device, such as an Ethernet card or a Wi-Fi adapter.
- Network-layer addresses are used to identify each logical network connection of a device, such as an IP address or a domain name.
- MAC addresses are used to identify each device on a local area network (LAN), such as a switch or a hub.
- Internetworking devices, such as routers, use network-layer addresses to forward packets to the appropriate destination network.
- Internetworking devices, such as switches, use MAC addresses to forward packets to the appropriate destination device on a LAN.
- Internetworking devices, such as bridges, use data-link layer addresses to forward packets between different types of physical networks, such as Ethernet and Wi-Fi.
- Internetworking devices, such as gateways, use application-layer protocols to translate data between different types of logical networks, such as email and web.
- Internetworking devices, such as firewalls, use network-layer and transport-layer protocols to filter and control the traffic between different networks.
