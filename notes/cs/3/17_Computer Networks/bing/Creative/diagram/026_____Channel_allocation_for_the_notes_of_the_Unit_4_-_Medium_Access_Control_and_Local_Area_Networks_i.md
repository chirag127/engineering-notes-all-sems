### Channel allocation for the notes of the Unit 4 - Medium Access Control and Local Area Networks in the subject of Computer Networks

- Channel allocation is a process in which a single channel is divided and allotted to multiple users in order to carry user specific tasks.
- Channel allocation techniques are used to manage the access of multiple users to a shared network and to avoid collisions or interference.
- There are three main types of channel allocation techniques: static, dynamic and hybrid.

#### Static Channel Allocation
- In static channel allocation, a fixed portion of the frequency channel is allotted to each user or station, regardless of the traffic or demand .
- Static channel allocation is suitable for LANs and MANs, where the number of users and the traffic pattern are relatively stable.
- Static channel allocation can be implemented using frequency division multiplexing (FDM) or time division multiplexing (TDM).
- The advantages of static channel allocation are simplicity, low overhead and guaranteed performance.
- The disadvantages of static channel allocation are inefficient utilization of channel resources, waste of bandwidth and lack of flexibility.

#### Dynamic Channel Allocation
- In dynamic channel allocation, the frequency channel is not permanently assigned to any user or station, but is allocated on demand according to the traffic or request .
- Dynamic channel allocation is suitable for wireless networks, where the number of users and the traffic pattern are highly variable.
- Dynamic channel allocation can be implemented using various algorithms or protocols, such as ALOHA, CSMA, CSMA/CD, CSMA/CA, polling, token passing, reservation, etc .
- The advantages of dynamic channel allocation are efficient utilization of channel resources, adaptive to traffic variations and flexible to accommodate new users.
- The disadvantages of dynamic channel allocation are complexity, high overhead and unpredictable performance.

#### Hybrid Channel Allocation
- In hybrid channel allocation, a combination of static and dynamic channel allocation techniques is used to achieve a balance between efficiency and performance.
- Hybrid channel allocation is suitable for cellular networks, where the frequency channel is divided into smaller cells, each with a fixed number of channels, and the channels are dynamically allocated within each cell.
- Hybrid channel allocation can be implemented using various schemes, such as fixed channel allocation (FCA), dynamic channel allocation (DCA), borrowing channel allocation (BCA), etc.
- The advantages of hybrid channel allocation are improved utilization of channel resources, reduced interference and enhanced quality of service.
- The disadvantages of hybrid channel allocation are increased complexity, higher overhead and coordination challenges.
