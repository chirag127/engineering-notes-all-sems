### Switching techniques and multiplexing

Switching techniques are methods of connecting multiple devices in a network and transferring data between them. There are three main types of switching techniques: circuit switching, message switching, and packet switching.

- Circuit switching: In circuit switching, two nodes communicate with each other over a dedicated communication path. The path is established before the data transmission and remains active until the communication is over. The advantage of circuit switching is that it provides a guaranteed and continuous connection between the nodes. The disadvantage is that it wastes bandwidth if the connection is idle or the data rate is low.
- Message switching: In message switching, the whole message is treated as a data unit. The message is stored and forwarded by intermediate nodes until it reaches the destination. The advantage of message switching is that it does not require a dedicated path and can use any available link. The disadvantage is that it introduces delay and overhead due to the storage and forwarding process.
- Packet switching: The packet switching technique is derived from message switching where the message is broken down into smaller chunks called packets. The packets are transmitted independently and may follow different routes to reach the destination. The advantage of packet switching is that it can utilize the network resources more efficiently and handle variable data rates. The disadvantage is that it may cause packet loss, duplication, or reordering due to network congestion or errors.

Multiplexing is a technique of combining multiple signals into one signal over a shared medium. Multiplexing allows the simultaneous transmission of multiple signals across a single data link. There are different types of multiplexing techniques, such as frequency division multiplexing (FDM), time division multiplexing (TDM), wavelength division multiplexing (WDM), and code division multiplexing (CDM).

- Frequency division multiplexing: In FDM, the frequency spectrum of the channel is divided into several non-overlapping frequency bands. Each signal is modulated by a different carrier frequency and transmitted over the channel. The signals are separated at the receiver by using filters or demodulators.
- Time division multiplexing: In TDM, the time axis of the channel is divided into several time slots. Each signal is assigned a different time slot and transmitted over the channel. The signals are separated at the receiver by using synchronizers or demultiplexers.
- Wavelength division multiplexing: In WDM, the wavelength spectrum of the channel is divided into several non-overlapping wavelength bands. Each signal is modulated by a different wavelength of light and transmitted over the channel. The signals are separated at the receiver by using prisms or filters.
- Code division multiplexing: In CDM, each signal is assigned a different code sequence and transmitted over the channel. The signals are separated at the receiver by using correlators or decoders.

Statistical multiplexing is a communication link sharing technique, which is used in packet switching. The shared linking is variable in statistical multiplexing, whereas it is fixed in TDM or FDM. This is a strategic application for maximizing the utilization of bandwidth. This can increase the efficiency of network, as well.
