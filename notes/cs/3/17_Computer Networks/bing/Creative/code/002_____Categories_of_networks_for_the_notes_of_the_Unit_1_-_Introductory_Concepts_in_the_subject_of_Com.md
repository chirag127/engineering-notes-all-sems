### Categories of networks for the notes of the Unit 1 - Introductory Concepts in the subject of Computer Networks

A computer network is a system of interconnected devices that can communicate and share data, resources, and applications. Computer networks can be classified based on several criteria, such as the transmission medium, the network size, the topology, and organizational intent. Based on a geographical scale, the different types of networks are:

- **Personal Area Network (PAN)**: A PAN is the smallest and simplest type of network. PANs connect devices within the range of an individual and are no larger than about 10 meters. Examples of PANs are wireless headphones, smartwatches, and Bluetooth speakers.
- **Local Area Network (LAN)**: A LAN is a network that connects devices within a small area, such as a building, a home, or an office. LANs are usually owned and managed by a single entity, such as a company or a school. LANs can use wired or wireless technologies, such as Ethernet, Wi-Fi, or fiber optics .
- **Wireless Local Area Network (WLAN)**: A WLAN is a type of LAN that uses wireless technologies, such as Wi-Fi, to connect devices. WLANs are more flexible and convenient than wired LANs, but they may have lower security and reliability. WLANs are commonly used in public places, such as airports, cafes, and hotels.
- **Metropolitan Area Network (MAN)**: A MAN is a network that connects devices within a large area, such as a city or a region. MANs are usually owned and operated by a public or private organization, such as a government or a telecom company. MANs can use various technologies, such as cable, DSL, or fiber optics .
- **Wide Area Network (WAN)**: A WAN is a network that connects devices across a large geographical area, such as a country or a continent. WANs are typically composed of multiple LANs or MANs that are interconnected by routers and switches. WANs can use various technologies, such as satellite, microwave, or cellular .
- **Campus Area Network (CAN)**: A CAN is a network that connects devices within a limited geographical area, such as a university campus or a military base. CANs are similar to LANs, but they are larger and more complex. CANs can use various technologies, such as Ethernet, Wi-Fi, or fiber optics.
- **Virtual Private Network (VPN)**: A VPN is a network that creates a secure and encrypted connection between devices over a public network, such as the internet. VPNs are used to protect the privacy and security of the data and the users. VPNs can use various protocols, such as IPsec, SSL, or PPTP .
