## Unit 4 - Medium Access Control and Local Area Networks

- Medium access control (MAC) is the sublayer of the data link layer that controls the hardware responsible for interaction with the transmission medium.
- MAC protocols enforce a methodology to allow multiple devices access to a shared media network.
- MAC protocols can be classified into two categories: contention-based and contention-free.
- Contention-based protocols allow devices to compete for the channel access, such as ALOHA, CSMA, CSMA/CD, and CSMA/CA.
- Contention-free protocols allocate the channel access to devices in a deterministic or centralized manner, such as TDMA, FDMA, CDMA, and polling.
- Local area networks (LANs) are networks that connect devices within a limited geographical area, such as a building or a campus.
- LANs can use wired, optical, or wireless transmission media, depending on the physical layer (PHY) specifications.
- Wireless LANs (WLANs) are LANs that use radio frequency (RF) or infrared (IR) signals to transmit data between devices.
- WLANs are specified by the IEEE 802.11 standard, which defines one MAC and several PHY specifications for wireless connectivity   .
- WLANs can support various applications with quality of service (QoS) requirements, such as voice, audio, and video streaming .
- WLANs can operate in two modes: infrastructure mode and ad hoc mode.
- Infrastructure mode involves a central device called an access point (AP) that coordinates the communication between the wireless devices (stations or STAs) and the wired network.
- Ad hoc mode involves direct communication between STAs without an AP, forming a self-organized network called an independent basic service set (IBSS).
