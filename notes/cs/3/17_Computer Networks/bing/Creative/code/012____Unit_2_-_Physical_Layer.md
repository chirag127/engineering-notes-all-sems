## Unit 2 - Physical Layer

The physical layer is the lowest layer of the OSI model. It is responsible for transmitting and receiving raw bits over a physical medium. The physical layer covers the following topics:

- Data encoding: The process of converting bits into signals that can be transmitted over the physical medium. There are different types of data encoding, such as NRZ, RZ, Manchester, 4B/5B, etc.
- Transmission modes: The ways of transmitting data over the physical medium. There are three main transmission modes: simplex, half-duplex, and full-duplex.
- Multiplexing: The technique of combining multiple signals into one signal that can be transmitted over a shared medium. There are different types of multiplexing, such as frequency division multiplexing (FDM), time division multiplexing (TDM), wavelength division multiplexing (WDM), etc.
- Transmission media: The physical medium that carries the signals from one device to another. There are two types of transmission media: guided and unguided. Guided media are those that provide a physical path for the signals, such as twisted pair, coaxial cable, and optical fiber. Unguided media are those that do not provide a physical path for the signals, such as radio waves, microwaves, and infrared waves.
- Error detection and correction: The methods of detecting and correcting errors that may occur during the transmission of data over the physical medium. There are different types of error detection and correction techniques, such as parity check, checksum, cyclic redundancy check (CRC), hamming code, etc.
