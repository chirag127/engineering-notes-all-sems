### Network structure and architecture

- Network architecture refers to a network’s structural and logical layout  .
- It describes how the network devices are connected and the rules that govern data transfer between them .
- Network devices typically include switches, routers, hubs, bridges, gateways, firewalls, etc.
- Network services include functions that support network communication, such as DHCP, DNS, FTP, HTTP, SMTP, etc.
- Network architecture components include hardware, software, transmission media (wired or wireless), network topology, and communications protocols.
- Network topology is the physical or logical arrangement of network nodes and links.
- Communications protocols are the rules and standards that define how data is formatted, transmitted, and received over a network.
- There are many ways to approach network architecture design, which depend on the purpose and size of the network.
- The main types of network architecture are peer-to-peer (P2P) and client/server.
- In a P2P network, each node can act as both a client and a server, and there is no central authority or server.
- In a client/server network, one or more servers provide services to clients, and there is a clear distinction between the roles of each node.
- P2P networks are usually simpler, cheaper, and more decentralized, but they may have issues with security, scalability, and performance.
- Client/server networks are usually more complex, expensive, and centralized, but they may have advantages in security, scalability, and performance.
- Some examples of P2P networks are BitTorrent, Skype, and Bitcoin.
- Some examples of client/server networks are web servers, email servers, and database servers.
- Network architecture can also be classified based on the scope or scale of the network, such as LAN, WAN, MAN, PAN, etc.
- LAN stands for local area network, which is a network that connects devices within a small geographic area, such as a home, office, or school.
- WAN stands for wide area network, which is a network that connects devices across a large geographic area, such as a city, country, or the world.
- MAN stands for metropolitan area network, which is a network that connects devices within a metropolitan area, such as a city or a campus.
- PAN stands for personal area network, which is a network that connects devices within a personal range, such as a smartphone, a laptop, and a wearable device.
- Some examples of LANs are Ethernet, Wi-Fi, and Bluetooth.
- Some examples of WANs are the Internet, cellular networks, and satellite networks.
- Some examples of MANs are cable TV networks, fiber-optic networks, and WiMAX networks.
- Some examples of PANs are NFC, RFID, and ZigBee networks.
