### Transmission media for the notes of the Unit 2 - Physical Layer in the subject of Computer Networks

- Transmission media in computer networks is a communication channel that is used to carry the data in the form of bits from the sender to the receiver using LAN .
- Transmission media can be classified into two types: guided media and unguided media.
- Guided media, also known as wired or bounded media, are physical links that direct and confine the signals being transmitted. Examples of guided media are twisted pair, coaxial cable, and optical fiber .
- Unguided media, also known as wireless or unbounded media, are electromagnetic waves that propagate through the air or space without any physical medium. Examples of unguided media are radio waves, microwaves, infrared waves, and visible light .
- The characteristics of transmission media include bandwidth, attenuation, noise, interference, and security.
- Bandwidth is the measure of the data rate that can be transmitted over a given medium. It is expressed in bits per second (bps) or Hertz (Hz).
- Attenuation is the loss of signal strength as it travels over a medium. It is measured in decibels (dB) and depends on the distance, frequency, and medium properties.
- Noise is the unwanted signal that interferes with the original signal and causes distortion or errors. It can be classified into thermal noise, crosstalk, impulse noise, and intermodulation noise.
- Interference is the overlapping of signals from different sources that affect the quality and performance of the transmission. It can be classified into electromagnetic interference (EMI), radio frequency interference (RFI), and electromagnetic compatibility (EMC).
- Security is the protection of the transmitted data from unauthorized access, modification, or destruction. It can be achieved by using encryption, authentication, and access control techniques.
