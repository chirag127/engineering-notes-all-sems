### Signal transmission and encoding

- Signal transmission is the process of sending and receiving data over a physical medium, such as a wire, a cable, or a wireless channel.
- Encoding is the process of converting data into a format that can be transmitted and recognized by the sender and the receiver.
- Encoding can be classified into two types: digital-to-digital encoding and analog-to-digital encoding.
- Digital-to-digital encoding is the conversion of binary data (0s and 1s) into a series of voltage pulses or electromagnetic waves that can be transmitted over a digital medium.
- Analog-to-digital encoding is the conversion of analog data (such as sound or video) into a digital format that can be transmitted over a digital medium.
- Encoding techniques can be categorized based on the polarity, level, and transition of the signals. Some common encoding techniques are:
  - Unipolar encoding: uses only one voltage level to represent 0s and 1s. For example, 0V for 0 and +5V for 1.
  - Polar encoding: uses two voltage levels (positive and negative) to represent 0s and 1s. For example, +5V for 0 and -5V for 1.
  - Bipolar encoding: uses three voltage levels (positive, negative, and zero) to represent 0s and 1s. For example, +5V for 0, -5V for 1, and 0V for no data.
  - Manchester encoding: uses a transition from high to low or low to high in the middle of each bit period to represent 0s and 1s. For example, high to low for 0 and low to high for 1.
- Encoding techniques have different advantages and disadvantages in terms of bandwidth, power, synchronization, error detection, and interference. The choice of encoding technique depends on the characteristics and requirements of the transmission medium and the application.
