## Unit 4 - Medium Access Control and Local Area Networks

- Medium access control (MAC) is the sublayer of the data link layer that controls the hardware responsible for interaction with the transmission medium.
- MAC protocols enforce a methodology to allow multiple devices access to a shared media network.
- MAC protocols can be classified into two categories: contention-based and contention-free.
- Contention-based protocols allow devices to compete for the channel access, such as CSMA/CD, CSMA/CA, and ALOHA.
- Contention-free protocols allocate the channel access to devices in a deterministic or random manner, such as TDMA, FDMA, CDMA, and polling.
- Local area networks (LANs) are networks that connect devices within a limited geographic area, such as a building or a campus.
- LANs can use wired, optical, or wireless transmission media, depending on the physical layer specifications.
- Wireless LANs (WLANs) are LANs that use radio waves as the transmission medium.
- WLANs are governed by the IEEE 802.11 standard, which defines the MAC and physical layer specifications for wireless connectivity  .
- The IEEE 802.11 standard specifies several physical layer technologies, such as OFDM, DSSS, FHSS, and IR, and operates in different frequency bands, such as 2.4 GHz, 5 GHz, and 60 GHz .
- The IEEE 802.11 standard also specifies the MAC procedures to support quality of service (QoS) requirements for applications that transport voice, audio, and video over WLANs .
