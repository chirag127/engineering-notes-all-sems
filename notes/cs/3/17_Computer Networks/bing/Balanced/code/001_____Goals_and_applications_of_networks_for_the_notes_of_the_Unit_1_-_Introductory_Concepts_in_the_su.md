### Goals and applications of networks

A network is a system of two or more devices (e.g. computers, printers, scanners, etc.) that are connected by some communication medium (e.g. cables, wireless, etc.) in order to share resources, exchange data, or enable electronic communication  .

Some of the main goals of networks are:

- **Resource sharing**: This means to make all programs, data, and equipment available to anyone on the network regardless of the physical location of the resource and the user. For example, a group of office workers can share a common printer, fax, modem, scanner, etc. Resource sharing also enables cost reduction, as fewer devices need to be purchased and maintained.
- **High reliability**: This means to provide alternative sources of supply or backup in case of failure or damage of any component of the network. For example, if one server goes down, another server can take over its tasks. High reliability also improves performance, as the workload can be distributed among multiple devices.
- **Scalability**: This means to allow the network to grow or shrink according to the changing needs and demands of the users and applications. For example, a network can add or remove devices, increase or decrease bandwidth, or change the network topology without affecting the functionality of the network.
- **Security**: This means to protect the network and its resources from unauthorized access, modification, or destruction. For example, a network can use encryption, authentication, firewalls, or antivirus software to prevent cyberattacks or data breaches.

Some of the main applications of networks are :

- **The internet**: This is the global network of networks that connects billions of devices and enables various online services and activities, such as web browsing, email, online search, social media, online shopping, online gaming, etc.
- **Online search**: This is the process of finding information or resources on the internet using keywords or queries. Online search engines, such as Google or Bing, use algorithms and databases to provide relevant and accurate results to the users.
- **Email**: This is the electronic transmission of messages and attachments between users over the internet. Email protocols, such as SMTP, POP3, or IMAP, define the rules and formats for sending and receiving emails.
- **Audio and video sharing**: This is the online distribution and consumption of multimedia content, such as music, podcasts, movies, TV shows, etc. Audio and video sharing platforms, such as YouTube or Spotify, allow users to upload, stream, download, or share their content with others.
- **Online commerce**: This is the buying and selling of goods and services over the internet. Online commerce websites, such as Amazon or eBay, allow users to browse, compare, order, pay, or review products or services online.
- **Live-streaming**: This is the real-time transmission and reception of audio or video content over the internet. Live-streaming applications, such as Zoom or Twitch, allow users to broadcast or watch live events, such as webinars, concerts, games, etc.
- **Social networks**: This is the online creation and maintenance of personal or professional relationships and communities. Social network platforms, such as Facebook or LinkedIn, allow users to create profiles, post updates, send messages, join groups, or follow others.
