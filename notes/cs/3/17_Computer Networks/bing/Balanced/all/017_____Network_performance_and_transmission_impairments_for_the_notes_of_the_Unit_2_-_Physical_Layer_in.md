# Network performance and transmission impairments

- Network performance is the measure of how well a network can deliver data and services to its users. It can be evaluated by various metrics, such as throughput, delay, jitter, packet loss, availability, reliability, etc.
- Transmission impairments are the factors that degrade the quality of a signal during its transmission over a medium. They can cause errors, distortions, or interruptions in the data communication. Some common types of transmission impairments are:
  - Attenuation: The loss of signal strength as it travels over a distance. It can be caused by the resistance of the medium, the absorption of the signal by the medium, or the leakage of the signal from the medium. Attenuation can be measured in decibels (dB) and can be compensated by using amplifiers or repeaters.
  - Distortion: The alteration of the shape or form of the signal due to the non-uniform characteristics of the medium or the interference of other signals. It can result in the loss of information or the misinterpretation of the data. Distortion can be classified into two types:
    - Linear distortion: The distortion that affects the amplitude and phase of the signal, but not the frequency. It can be caused by the frequency-dependent attenuation or delay of the medium. Linear distortion can be reduced by using equalizers or filters.
    - Non-linear distortion: The distortion that affects the frequency and harmonics of the signal, as well as the amplitude and phase. It can be caused by the saturation, clipping, or modulation of the signal by the medium or other signals. Non-linear distortion can be minimized by using limiters or compressors.
  - Noise: The unwanted or random signals that are added to the original signal during the transmission. Noise can reduce the signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) and increase the bit error rate (BER) of the communication. There are several types of noise, such as:
    - Thermal noise: The noise that is generated by the random motion of electrons in the medium or the devices. It is proportional to the temperature and the bandwidth of the signal. Thermal noise can be modeled by a Gaussian distribution and can be reduced by using cooling techniques or shielding.
    - Induced noise: The noise that is induced by external sources, such as motors, appliances, power lines, etc. These sources act as sending antennas and the transmission medium acts as receiving antennas. Induced noise can be minimized by using twisted-pair cables, coaxial cables, or optical fibers.
    - Crosstalk: The noise that is caused by the coupling of signals from adjacent wires or channels. It can result in the interference or the leakage of the signals. Crosstalk can be avoided by using proper spacing, shielding, or multiplexing techniques.
    - Impulse noise: The noise that is caused by sudden and transient changes in the voltage or current of the signal. It can be due to the switching, sparking, or lightning of the devices or the medium. Impulse noise can be very disruptive and can be reduced by using error detection and correction methods.
