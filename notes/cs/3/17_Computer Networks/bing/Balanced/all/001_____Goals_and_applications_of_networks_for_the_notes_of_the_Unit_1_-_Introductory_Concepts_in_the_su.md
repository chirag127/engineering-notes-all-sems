# Goals and applications of networks

A network is a system of two or more devices (such as computers, printers, scanners, etc.) that are connected by some communication medium (such as cables, wireless, satellites, etc.) to share resources, exchange information, or enable communication. 

Some of the main goals and applications of networks are:

- **Resource sharing**: This is the ability to make all the resources (such as hardware, software, data, etc.) available to any device on the network, regardless of the physical location of the resource and the device. This can reduce the cost and complexity of managing and maintaining the resources, and improve the efficiency and productivity of the users. For example, a group of office workers can share a common printer, fax, modem, scanner, etc.     

- **High reliability**: This is the ability to provide alternative sources of supply or service in case of failure or unavailability of some resources or devices. This can increase the availability and performance of the network, and reduce the risk of data loss or interruption. For example, a network can have multiple servers, routers, links, etc. that can take over the load or route the traffic in case of a failure.  

- **Communication**: This is the ability to enable the exchange of information (such as messages, files, audio, video, etc.) among the devices or users on the network. This can facilitate the collaboration and coordination of work, and enhance the social and professional interactions. For example, a network can support email, chat, video conferencing, online gaming, social media, etc.   

- **Scalability**: This is the ability to accommodate the growth or change of the network size, structure, or traffic without affecting the quality or functionality of the network. This can allow the network to adapt to the changing needs and demands of the users or the environment. For example, a network can use modular components, hierarchical design, or dynamic routing protocols to scale up or down as needed.  

- **Security**: This is the ability to protect the network and its resources from unauthorized access, modification, or damage. This can ensure the confidentiality, integrity, and availability of the network and its data, and prevent the loss of privacy or trust. For example, a network can use encryption, authentication, firewall, antivirus, etc. to secure the network.
