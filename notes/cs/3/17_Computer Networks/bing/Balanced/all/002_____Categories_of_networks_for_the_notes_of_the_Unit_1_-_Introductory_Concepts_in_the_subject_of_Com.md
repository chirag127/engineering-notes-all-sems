# Categories of networks

A computer network is a system of interconnected devices that can communicate and share data, resources, and applications. Computer networks can be classified based on several criteria, such as the transmission medium, the network size, the topology, and organizational intent. Based on a geographical scale, the different types of networks are:

- **Personal Area Network (PAN)**: A PAN is the smallest and simplest type of network. PANs connect devices within the range of an individual and are no larger than about 10 meters. Examples of PANs are wireless headphones, smartwatches, and Bluetooth keyboards.
- **Local Area Network (LAN)**: A LAN is a network that connects devices within a small area, such as a home, an office, or a building. LANs are usually wired with Ethernet cables or wireless with Wi-Fi. LANs allow users to share files, printers, scanners, and internet access.
- **Wireless Local Area Network (WLAN)**: A WLAN is a type of LAN that uses wireless technology, such as Wi-Fi or Bluetooth, to connect devices. WLANs are more flexible and convenient than wired LANs, but they may have lower security and reliability. WLANs are commonly used in public places, such as airports, cafes, and hotels.
- **Campus Area Network (CAN)**: A CAN is a network that connects devices within a campus, such as a university, a school, or a business park. A CAN is larger than a LAN but smaller than a MAN. A CAN may consist of several LANs connected by switches or routers.
- **Metropolitan Area Network (MAN)**: A MAN is a network that connects devices within a city or a metropolitan area. A MAN is larger than a LAN but smaller than a WAN. A MAN may use different technologies, such as fiber-optic cables, microwave links, or satellite transmissions. Examples of MANs are cable TV networks, city-wide Wi-Fi networks, and cellular networks .
- **Wide Area Network (WAN)**: A WAN is a network that connects devices across a large geographical area, such as a country, a continent, or the world. A WAN may use different technologies, such as telephone lines, optical fibers, or satellite links. The internet is the largest and most well-known example of a WAN .
- **Virtual Private Network (VPN)**: A VPN is a type of network that uses encryption and tunneling to create a secure and private connection between devices over a public network, such as the internet. A VPN can protect the data and identity of the users from hackers, spies, or censorship. A VPN can also allow users to access geo-restricted or blocked content, such as streaming services, websites, or online games.
