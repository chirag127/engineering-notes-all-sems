# Network devices and components

Network devices and components are the hardware and software elements that enable communication and data exchange between different devices on a network. They can be classified into different types based on their functions, locations, and roles in the network. Some of the common network devices and components are:

- **Sender and receiver**: These are the end points of a network communication, such as computers, printers, scanners, etc. They use network interface cards (NICs) to connect to the network media and send or receive data packets.
- **Network media**: These are the physical or wireless channels that carry the data signals between the sender and receiver. Examples of network media are cables, such as coaxial, twisted pair, or fiber optic, and wireless technologies, such as radio waves, infrared, or microwave.
- **Connectors**: These are the devices that attach the network media to the NICs or other network devices. Examples of connectors are RJ45, BNC, or ST.
- **Switches**: These are the devices that forward data packets based on the MAC address of the destination device. They create separate collision domains and reduce network congestion. They can also segment the network into different logical groups, called VLANs, for security and efficiency purposes.
- **Routers**: These are the devices that forward data packets based on the IP address of the destination network. They create separate broadcast domains and connect different networks, such as LANs, WANs, or the Internet. They can also perform routing protocols, such as RIP, OSPF, or BGP, to find the best path for the data packets.
- **Hubs**: These are the devices that broadcast data packets to all the connected devices on the network. They create a single collision domain and a single broadcast domain. They are considered obsolete and replaced by switches in modern networks.
- **Repeaters**: These are the devices that amplify and regenerate the data signals on the network media. They extend the range of the network and overcome signal attenuation. They do not filter or forward data packets and operate at the physical layer of the OSI model.
- **Gateways**: These are the devices that convert data formats and protocols between different networks. They enable communication between incompatible networks, such as a LAN and a mainframe, or a LAN and a telephone network. They can operate at different layers of the OSI model depending on the type of conversion they perform.
- **Bridges**: These are the devices that filter and forward data packets based on the MAC address of the destination device. They create separate collision domains and connect different segments of the same network. They are considered obsolete and replaced by switches in modern networks.
- **Servers**: These are the devices that provide network services and resources to the client devices, such as file sharing, printing, web hosting, email, etc. They can run different operating systems and applications depending on the type of service they offer.
