### Network structure and architecture

- Network structure and architecture refer to the way network devices and services are structured to serve the connectivity needs of client devices.
- Network devices typically include switches, routers, hubs, bridges, gateways, firewalls, etc.
- Network services typically include DHCP, DNS, FTP, HTTP, SMTP, etc.
- Network structure and architecture also describe how the network devices are connected and the rules that govern data transfer between them .
- The medium of data transmission, such as wired or wireless, also constitutes the network structure and architecture .
- There are many ways to approach network architecture design, which depend on the purpose and size of the network.
- The main types of network architecture are peer-to-peer (P2P) and client/server.
  - In a P2P network, each node can act as both a client and a server, and there is no central authority or server. P2P networks are suitable for small networks with limited resources and security requirements.
  - In a client/server network, there is a central server that provides services to the clients, and the clients request services from the server. Client/server networks are suitable for large networks with high performance and security requirements.
- Network structure and architecture can also be classified based on the network topology, which is the physical or logical arrangement of the network devices. The common network topologies are bus, star, ring, mesh, tree, and hybrid.
  - In a bus topology, all the devices are connected to a single cable, called the bus. The bus acts as the backbone of the network and carries the data signals. Bus networks are easy to install and expand, but they have low bandwidth and are prone to collisions and faults.
  - In a star topology, all the devices are connected to a central device, called the hub or switch. The hub or switch acts as the central point of the network and forwards the data signals to the appropriate device. Star networks are easy to manage and troubleshoot, but they have high cost and depend on the central device.
  - In a ring topology, all the devices are connected in a circular fashion, forming a ring. The data signals travel in one direction around the ring, from one device to another. Ring networks are simple and reliable, but they have low scalability and are affected by a single device failure.
  - In a mesh topology, each device is connected to every other device, forming a mesh. The data signals can take multiple paths to reach the destination, depending on the availability and congestion of the links. Mesh networks are robust and fault-tolerant, but they have high complexity and cost.
  - In a tree topology, the devices are arranged in a hierarchical structure, forming a tree. The root node is the top-level device, and the other nodes are the lower-level devices. The data signals travel from the root node to the leaf nodes, or vice versa. Tree networks are scalable and organized, but they have high latency and depend on the root node.
  - In a hybrid topology, two or more different topologies are combined to form a hybrid network. The hybrid network inherits the advantages and disadvantages of the constituent topologies. Hybrid networks are flexible and adaptable, but they have high complexity and design challenges.
