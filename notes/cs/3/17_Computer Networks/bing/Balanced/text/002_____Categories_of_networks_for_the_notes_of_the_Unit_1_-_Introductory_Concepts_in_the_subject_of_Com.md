### Categories of networks for the notes of the Unit 1 - Introductory Concepts in the subject of Computer Networks

- A computer network is a system of interconnected devices that can communicate and share data, resources, and applications.
- Computer networks can be classified based on several criteria, such as the transmission medium, the network size, the topology, and organizational intent.
- Based on the network size and geographical scale, the most common types of computer networks are:

  - **Personal Area Network (PAN)**: The smallest and simplest type of network that connects devices within the range of an individual, such as a smartphone, a laptop, a printer, and a smartwatch. PANs are usually wireless and use technologies like Bluetooth, infrared, or Wi-Fi Direct. PANs are suitable for personal use and convenience.
  - **Local Area Network (LAN)**: A network that connects devices within a small area, such as a home, an office, a building, or a campus. LANs are usually wired and use technologies like Ethernet, fiber optics, or power-line communication. LANs are suitable for high-speed data transfer and resource sharing among a limited number of users.
  - **Wireless Local Area Network (WLAN)**: A type of LAN that uses wireless technologies like Wi-Fi, radio waves, or microwaves to connect devices without cables. WLANs are suitable for mobility and flexibility within a local area.
  - **Metropolitan Area Network (MAN)**: A network that connects devices within a large area, such as a city, a town, or a region. MANs are usually a combination of wired and wireless technologies and use technologies like cable TV, DSL, or cellular networks. MANs are suitable for providing public services and access to the internet for a large number of users.
  - **Wide Area Network (WAN)**: A network that connects devices across a large geographical area, such as a country, a continent, or the world. WANs are usually a collection of LANs and MANs and use technologies like satellite, fiber optics, or telephone lines. WANs are suitable for long-distance communication and data exchange among a large number of users.
  - **Virtual Private Network (VPN)**: A type of WAN that uses encryption and tunneling techniques to create a secure and private connection over a public network, such as the internet. VPNs are suitable for protecting the confidentiality and integrity of data and accessing remote resources securely.
  - **Campus Area Network (CAN)**: A type of LAN that connects devices within a specific area, such as a university, a school, or a military base. CANs are usually a combination of wired and wireless technologies and use technologies like Ethernet, Wi-Fi, or fiber optics. CANs are suitable for providing academic and administrative services and access to the internet for a specific community of users.
