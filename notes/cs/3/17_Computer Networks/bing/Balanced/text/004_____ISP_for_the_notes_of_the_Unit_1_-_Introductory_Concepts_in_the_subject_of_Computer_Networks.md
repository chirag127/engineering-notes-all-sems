### ISP

- ISP stands for Internet Service Provider. It is an organization that provides access to the Internet and other related services, such as email, web hosting, and domain name registration.
- ISPs can be classified into different types based on the level of service they offer, the network infrastructure they use, and the customers they serve. Some common types of ISPs are:
  - Tier 1 ISPs: These are the largest and most powerful ISPs that have access to the entire Internet backbone. They do not pay any fees to other ISPs for exchanging traffic. They usually have global or continental coverage and serve other ISPs as well as large customers. Examples of Tier 1 ISPs are AT&T, Verizon, and Level 3.
  - Tier 2 ISPs: These are smaller ISPs that have access to some parts of the Internet backbone, but not all. They usually pay fees to Tier 1 ISPs or other Tier 2 ISPs for exchanging traffic. They may have regional or national coverage and serve other ISPs as well as medium-sized customers. Examples of Tier 2 ISPs are Comcast, Cox, and Sprint.
  - Tier 3 ISPs: These are the smallest ISPs that have access to the local network infrastructure, but not the Internet backbone. They usually pay fees to Tier 2 ISPs or other Tier 3 ISPs for exchanging traffic. They may have local or metropolitan coverage and serve residential or small business customers. Examples of Tier 3 ISPs are DSL, cable, and wireless providers.
- ISPs can also be classified into different types based on the technology they use to deliver Internet access to their customers. Some common types of technologies are:
  - DSL: This stands for Digital Subscriber Line. It is a technology that uses existing telephone lines to provide high-speed Internet access. DSL can be further divided into different types, such as ADSL, SDSL, and VDSL, based on the speed and symmetry of the data transmission.
  - Cable: This is a technology that uses existing coaxial cable lines to provide high-speed Internet access. Cable can also provide other services, such as television and voice, over the same line.
  - Fiber: This is a technology that uses optical fiber cables to provide very high-speed Internet access. Fiber can also provide other services, such as television and voice, over the same line. Fiber can be further divided into different types, such as FTTH, FTTN, and FTTC, based on the location of the fiber termination point.
  - Wireless: This is a technology that uses radio waves to provide wireless Internet access. Wireless can be further divided into different types, such as Wi-Fi, WiMAX, and cellular, based on the frequency, range, and standard of the wireless transmission.
  - Satellite: This is a technology that uses satellites to provide Internet access to remote or rural areas. Satellite can also provide other services, such as television and voice, over the same link. Satellite can be further divided into different types, such as GEO, MEO, and LEO, based on the orbit and altitude of the satellite.
