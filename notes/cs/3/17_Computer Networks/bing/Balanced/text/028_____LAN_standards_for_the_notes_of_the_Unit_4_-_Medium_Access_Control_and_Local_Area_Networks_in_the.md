### LAN standards

- A local area network (LAN) is a data communication network connecting various terminals or computers within a building or limited geographical area.
- The connection among the devices could be wired or wireless.
- The IEEE 802 family of specifications defines the standards for LAN technologies.
- Some of the best-known IEEE 802 standards are as follows:
  - IEEE 802.1: LAN/MAN internetworking standards
  - IEEE 802.3: Ethernet
  - IEEE 802.5: Token Ring
  - IEEE 802.11: Wireless LAN (Wi-Fi)
  - IEEE 802.15: Wireless Personal Area Network (WPAN)
  - IEEE 802.16: Wireless Metropolitan Area Network (WMAN or WiMAX)
- Each IEEE 802 standard has different sub-standards or modes that specify the physical layer and the medium access control (MAC) layer protocols for different transmission speeds, frequencies, modulations, and topologies.
- For example, IEEE 802.11 has the following modes:
  - IEEE 802.11a: 54 Mbps, 5 GHz, OFDM
  - IEEE 802.11b: 11 Mbps, 2.4 GHz, DSSS
  - IEEE 802.11g: 54 Mbps, 2.4 GHz, OFDM
  - IEEE 802.11n: 600 Mbps, 2.4/5 GHz, MIMO-OFDM
  - IEEE 802.11ac: 1.3 Gbps, 5 GHz, MIMO-OFDM
- The LAN standards enable interoperability, compatibility, and efficiency among different devices and networks.
