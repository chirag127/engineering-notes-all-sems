### LAN standards

A local area network (LAN) is a data communication network connecting various terminals or computers within a building or limited geographical area. The connection among the devices could be wired or wireless. LAN standards are the specifications that define the physical and logical characteristics of LANs, such as the transmission medium, the data rate, the access method, the topology, the addressing scheme, the error control, and the security. Some of the most common LAN standards are:

- **Ethernet**: It is the most widely used LAN technology that uses a bus or star topology and supports data rates of 10 Mbps, 100 Mbps, 1 Gbps, and 10 Gbps. Ethernet uses the carrier sense multiple access with collision detection (CSMA/CD) protocol to access the shared medium. Ethernet is defined by the IEEE 802.3 standard  .
- **Token Ring**: It is a LAN technology that uses a ring topology and supports data rates of 4 Mbps and 16 Mbps. Token Ring uses a token-passing protocol to access the shared medium. Token Ring is defined by the IEEE 802.5 standard  .
- **Wireless LAN**: It is a LAN technology that uses radio waves or infrared signals to communicate among devices without cables. Wireless LAN supports data rates of up to 54 Mbps and uses the carrier sense multiple access with collision avoidance (CSMA/CA) protocol to access the shared medium. Wireless LAN is defined by the IEEE 802.11 standard and has several variants, such as 802.11a, 802.11b, 802.11g, 802.11n, and 802.11ac  .
- **Fiber Distributed Data Interface (FDDI)**: It is a LAN technology that uses optical fibers to transmit data at 100 Mbps. FDDI uses a dual ring topology and a token-passing protocol to access the shared medium. FDDI is defined by the ANSI X3T9.5 standard .
- **Integrated Services LAN (ISLAN)**: It is a LAN technology that integrates voice, video, and data on a single network. ISLAN uses a star topology and supports data rates of 16 Mbps and 64 Mbps. ISLAN uses the demand priority protocol to access the shared medium. ISLAN is defined by the IEEE 802.9 standard .
