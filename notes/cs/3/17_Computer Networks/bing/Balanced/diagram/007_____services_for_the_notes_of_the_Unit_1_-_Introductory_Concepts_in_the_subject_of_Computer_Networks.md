### Services in Computer Networks

- Services are applications that run at the network application layer and provide various capabilities to users and devices connected to a network.
- Services can be classified into two types: connection-oriented and connectionless.
  - Connection-oriented services establish a logical connection between the sender and the receiver before exchanging data. They guarantee reliable and in-order delivery of data, but they consume more resources and introduce more delay. An example of a connection-oriented service is TCP (Transmission Control Protocol).
  - Connectionless services do not require a logical connection between the sender and the receiver. They send data as independent packets without any guarantee of reliability or order. They consume less resources and introduce less delay, but they may lose or reorder data. An example of a connectionless service is UDP (User Datagram Protocol).
- Some examples of services in computer networks are :
  - Internet and cloud connectivity: These services enable users and devices to access the internet and cloud-based applications and data. They use protocols such as HTTP (Hypertext Transfer Protocol), HTTPS (Hypertext Transfer Protocol Secure), FTP (File Transfer Protocol), and SMTP (Simple Mail Transfer Protocol).
  - Branch office and campus connectivity: These services enable users and devices in different locations to communicate and collaborate with each other. They use protocols such as VPN (Virtual Private Network), MPLS (Multiprotocol Label Switching), and SD-WAN (Software-Defined Wide Area Network).
  - Private data center services: These services enable users and devices to access and manage data and applications stored in private servers. They use protocols such as NFS (Network File System), SMB (Server Message Block), and iSCSI (Internet Small Computer System Interface).
  - Secure cloud-connectivity services: These services enable users and devices to access and manage data and applications stored in public or hybrid cloud servers. They use protocols such as SSL (Secure Sockets Layer), TLS (Transport Layer Security), and IPSec (Internet Protocol Security).
  - Virtual network services: These services enable users and devices to create and manage virtual networks that are independent of the physical network infrastructure. They use protocols such as VLAN (Virtual Local Area Network), VxLAN (Virtual Extensible Local Area Network), and NFV (Network Function Virtualization).
