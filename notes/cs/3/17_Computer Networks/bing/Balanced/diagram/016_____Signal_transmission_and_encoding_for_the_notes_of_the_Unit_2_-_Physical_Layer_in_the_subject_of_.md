### Signal transmission and encoding

- Signal transmission is the process of sending data from one node to another over a communication channel.
- Encoding is the process of converting data into signals that can be transmitted and understood by the receiver.
- Data can be either analog or digital, and signals can be either analog or digital as well.
- Analog data is continuous and varies in amplitude and frequency, such as sound or video.
- Digital data is discrete and consists of binary bits, such as text or numbers.
- Analog signals are continuous and vary in amplitude and frequency, such as sine waves or radio waves.
- Digital signals are discrete and consist of voltage pulses or electromagnetic impulses, such as on-off signals or light pulses.

- There are four types of encoding techniques for data transmission:

  - Analog-to-analog encoding: This is used to transmit analog data over an analog channel, such as modulating a carrier wave with voice or video signals. There are three methods of analog-to-analog encoding: amplitude modulation (AM), frequency modulation (FM), and phase modulation (PM).
  - Analog-to-digital encoding: This is used to transmit analog data over a digital channel, such as converting voice or video signals into binary bits. There are two methods of analog-to-digital encoding: pulse code modulation (PCM) and delta modulation (DM).
  - Digital-to-analog encoding: This is used to transmit digital data over an analog channel, such as modulating a carrier wave with binary bits. There are four methods of digital-to-analog encoding: amplitude shift keying (ASK), frequency shift keying (FSK), phase shift keying (PSK), and quadrature amplitude modulation (QAM).
  - Digital-to-digital encoding: This is used to transmit digital data over a digital channel, such as converting binary bits into voltage pulses or electromagnetic impulses. There are many methods of digital-to-digital encoding, such as unipolar, polar, bipolar, Manchester, differential Manchester, and 4B/5B encoding.

- The choice of encoding technique depends on factors such as the type of data, the type of channel, the bandwidth, the noise, the error rate, the cost, and the complexity of the encoding and decoding process.
