### Categories of networks

A computer network is a system of interconnected devices that can communicate and share data, resources, and applications. Computer networks can be classified based on several criteria, such as the transmission medium, the network size, the topology, and organizational intent. Based on a geographical scale, the different types of networks are:

- **Personal area network (PAN)**: A PAN is the smallest and simplest type of network. PANs connect devices within the range of an individual and are no larger than about 10 meters (m). Examples of PANs are wireless headphones, Bluetooth speakers, smartwatches, and smartphones.
- **Local area network (LAN)**: A LAN is a network that connects devices within a limited area, such as a home, an office, or a building. LANs are typically owned and managed by a single organization or individual. LANs can use wired or wireless technologies, such as Ethernet, Wi-Fi, or Bluetooth. LANs enable fast and secure data transfer, resource sharing, and communication among the connected devices .
- **Wireless local area network (WLAN)**: A WLAN is a type of LAN that uses wireless technologies, such as Wi-Fi, to connect devices. WLANs provide mobility, flexibility, and scalability to the network users. WLANs can be standalone or connected to a wired LAN. WLANs are commonly used in public places, such as airports, hotels, cafes, and libraries, to provide internet access to the visitors.
- **Campus area network (CAN)**: A CAN is a network that connects devices within a campus, such as a university, a school, or a corporate complex. A CAN is larger than a LAN but smaller than a metropolitan area network (MAN). A CAN can use wired or wireless technologies, such as fiber optic cables, Ethernet, or Wi-Fi. A CAN can provide high-speed data transfer, resource sharing, and communication among the campus buildings and departments.
- **Metropolitan area network (MAN)**: A MAN is a network that connects devices within a city or a metropolitan area. A MAN is larger than a LAN or a CAN but smaller than a wide area network (WAN). A MAN can use wired or wireless technologies, such as coaxial cables, fiber optic cables, microwave links, or WiMAX. A MAN can provide internet access, cable TV, telephone, and other services to the residents and businesses in the area .
- **Wide area network (WAN)**: A WAN is a network that connects devices across a large geographical area, such as a country, a continent, or the world. A WAN can span multiple LANs, CANs, or MANs. A WAN can use wired or wireless technologies, such as copper wires, fiber optic cables, satellite links, or cellular networks. A WAN can provide long-distance data transfer, resource sharing, and communication among the connected devices. The internet is the largest and most well-known example of a WAN .
- **Virtual private network (VPN)**: A VPN is a network that creates a secure and encrypted connection between a device and a remote network over a public network, such as the internet. A VPN can use various protocols, such as IPsec, SSL, or OpenVPN, to establish the connection. A VPN can provide privacy, security, and anonymity to the network users. A VPN can also enable access to restricted or censored content, such as geo-blocked websites or streaming services .
