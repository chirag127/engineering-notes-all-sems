### Multiple access protocols for the notes of the Unit 4 - Medium Access Control and Local Area Networks in the subject of Computer Networks

Multiple access protocols are used in computer networks to control access to a shared communication medium, such as a cable or wireless channel. The following are some of the key concepts related to multiple access protocols:

1. Carrier Sense Multiple Access (CSMA): CSMA is a protocol that listens for a carrier signal before transmitting data, to avoid collisions with other devices.

2. Carrier Sense Multiple Access with Collision Detection (CSMA/CD): CSMA/CD is a protocol that listens for a carrier signal and detects collisions, and retransmits data if a collision is detected.

3. Token Passing: Token passing is a protocol that passes a token between devices to grant access to the communication medium.

4. Demand Priority: Demand priority is a protocol that assigns priority levels to devices, and grants access to the communication medium based on priority.

5. Wireless Multiple Access Protocols: Wireless multiple access protocols are used in wireless networks to control access to the wireless channel, such as Wi-Fi and cellular networks.

6. Channelization: Channelization is the process of dividing a communication medium into multiple channels, to increase the capacity of the medium.

In this unit, you will learn about multiple access protocols, including Carrier Sense Multiple Access (CSMA), Carrier Sense Multiple Access with Collision Detection (CSMA/CD), Token Passing, Demand Priority, Wireless Multiple Access Protocols, and Channelization.

This unit will provide a foundation for understanding the principles and practices of medium access control and local area networks, and for exploring the various concepts and techniques used in the field of computer networking.
