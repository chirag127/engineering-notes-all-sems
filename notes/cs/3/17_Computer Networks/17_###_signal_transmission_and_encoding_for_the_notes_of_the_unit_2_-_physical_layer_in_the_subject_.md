### Signal transmission and encoding for the notes of the Unit 2 - Physical Layer in the subject of Computer Networks

Signal transmission and encoding are two important concepts in the physical layer of computer networks. The following are some of the key concepts related to signal transmission and encoding:

1. Signal Transmission: Signal transmission refers to the process of transmitting data over a physical medium, such as a cable or wireless connection.

2. Encoding: Encoding is the process of converting data into a form that can be transmitted over a physical medium, such as binary or analog signals.

3. Analog Signals: Analog signals are continuous signals that can take on any value within a specified range, and are used in analog transmission systems.

4. Digital Signals: Digital signals are discrete signals that can take on only a limited number of values, and are used in digital transmission systems.

5. Modulation: Modulation is the process of encoding data into a signal for transmission, such as amplitude modulation (AM) or frequency modulation (FM).

6. Multiplexing: Multiplexing is the process of combining multiple signals into a single signal for transmission, such as time-division multiplexing (TDM) or frequency-division multiplexing (FDM).

In this unit, you will learn about signal transmission and encoding, including analog signals, digital signals, modulation, and multiplexing.

This unit will provide a foundation for understanding the principles and practices of the physical layer of computer networks and for exploring the various concepts and techniques used in the field of computer networking.
