The process of shift-reduce parsing involves two basic operations: shift and reduce. The shift operation is used to move the next input token onto the stack, and the reduce operation is used to apply a grammar rule to the top of the stack, replacing the right-hand side of the rule with the non-terminal symbol on the left-hand side of the rule.

Shift-reduce parsing is a powerful parsing technique that can handle a wide range of grammars, including context-free grammars, LR(k) grammars, and LALR(k) grammars. It is also relatively efficient, and it is widely used in compiler design.
