### semantic errors for the notes of the Unit 4 - Symbol Tables in the subject of Compiler Design

The Semantic Errors section of the Unit 4 - Symbol Tables in the subject of Compiler Design should cover the following topics:

1. Definition of semantic errors (errors in a program that occur due to incorrect meaning)
2. Overview of semantic errors (how they occur, when they are detected, common causes)
3. Characteristics of semantic errors (abstraction, generality, complexity)
4. Components of semantic errors (type mismatches, undeclared variables, incorrect use of operators)
5. Techniques for detecting semantic errors (type checking, symbol tables, error reporting)
6. Overview of type checking (verifying that variables and expressions have the correct type)
7. Overview of symbol tables (data structures used to store information about symbols in a program)
8. Overview of error reporting (providing information about errors in a program)
9. The role of semantic errors in compiler design (ensuring that programs have correct meaning)
10. Applications of semantic errors in different fields (programming languages, text processing, network security)
11. Limitations of semantic errors (complexity of systems, performance overhead)
12. Techniques for overcoming limitations (optimization, awareness, education)
13. Further reading and resources (books, journals, online resources)

Semantic errors are an important component of symbol tables in compiler design. Understanding the principles and techniques of semantic errors helps students to develop the skills required to ensure that programs have correct meaning. The hands-on practice and further reading resources will help students to develop their skills and deepen their understanding of the material.
