### ambiguity for the notes of the Unit 1 - Introduction to Compiler in the subject of Compiler Design

Unit 1 of the Compiler Design course focuses on the Introduction to Compilers, including the aspect of Ambiguity.

Ambiguity refers to the situation when a programming language construct has more than one possible interpretation. This can occur in a variety of contexts, such as in grammar rules, in syntax, or in semantic analysis. Ambiguity can cause problems in a compiler, as it can lead to unpredictable results or errors in the compiled code.

To avoid ambiguity, compilers use a variety of techniques to resolve the ambiguity, such as using context-free grammars, precedence rules, and disambiguation rules. These techniques help to ensure that the compiler can accurately interpret the source code and generate correct compiled code.

In the context of the Compiler Design course, students in Unit 1 will learn about ambiguity and its impact on compilers. They will learn about the various techniques used to resolve ambiguity, and will have hands-on experiences working with these techniques to resolve ambiguity in a programming language.
