Recoverability:
- In a database management system, recoverability refers to the ability to restore the consistency of a database after a failure.
- Failures can be caused by hardware malfunctions, software bugs, power outages, or other events that disrupt normal operation.
- Recoverability is achieved through the use of transaction logs, backup and restore operations, and other techniques.
- The goal of recoverability is to ensure that the database can be restored to a consistent state after a failure, without losing any data or introducing corruption.
- There are several levels of recoverability, including full recoverability, partial recoverability, and no recoverability.
- Full recoverability requires that the database be restored to its exact state before the failure, while partial recoverability may only restore a portion of the data.
- No recoverability means that the database cannot be restored to a consistent state after a failure, and all data is lost.
- The level of recoverability required depends on the criticality of the data stored in the database and the consequences of data loss.
