
### Relationships of Higher Degree

* A relationship of higher degree is a relationship between entities that is not of the first degree, i.e. it is not a one-to-one or one-to-many relationship. 
* A higher degree relationship is typically a many-to-many relationship. 
* An example of a higher degree relationship is a student-course relationship, where one student can take multiple courses and one course can be taken by multiple students. 
* Higher degree relationships are often implemented using a junction table or a bridge table, which is a table that stores the relationship between two entities. 
* The junction table stores the foreign keys of the two entities in the relationship, and is used to link the two entities together. 
* Higher degree relationships can also be implemented using an associative entity, which is an entity that is used to represent the relationship between two entities. 
* The associative entity stores the primary keys of the two entities in the relationship, and is used to link the two entities together.
