## Unit 1 - Introduction of Real Time System

- A real time system is a system that must respond to events or inputs within a specified time limit, otherwise it may fail to meet its objectives or cause undesirable consequences.
- Real time systems are often used in applications that require high reliability, safety, or performance, such as industrial control, avionics, robotics, medical devices, etc.
- Real time systems can be classified into two types: hard real time systems and soft real time systems.
- Hard real time systems are systems that must meet their deadlines without any exception, otherwise they may cause catastrophic failures or loss of life. For example, a pacemaker, an airbag controller, or a missile guidance system are hard real time systems.
- Soft real time systems are systems that can tolerate some degree of deadline misses, but the quality of service or user satisfaction may degrade. For example, a video streaming, a voice recognition, or a online gaming system are soft real time systems.
- Real time systems have some common characteristics, such as concurrency, predictability, responsiveness, and dependability.
- Concurrency means that a real time system can handle multiple events or tasks simultaneously, and coordinate their execution and communication.
- Predictability means that a real time system can guarantee the timing behavior of its tasks and events, and avoid any unexpected delays or interferences.
- Responsiveness means that a real time system can react to events or inputs quickly and efficiently, and meet the specified deadlines.
- Dependability means that a real time system can provide correct and consistent results, and handle any faults or errors gracefully.
- Real time systems face some common challenges, such as resource constraints, complexity, uncertainty, and verification.
- Resource constraints mean that a real time system has limited hardware and software resources, such as CPU, memory, disk, network, etc., and must optimize their utilization and allocation.
- Complexity means that a real time system has many components and interactions, and must manage their coordination and synchronization.
- Uncertainty means that a real time system has to deal with dynamic and unpredictable environments, and must adapt to changes and disturbances.
- Verification means that a real time system has to ensure its correctness and safety, and must test and validate its functionality and performance.
