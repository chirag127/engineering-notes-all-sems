### Dynamic Versus Static Systems

- A **static system** is one where the tasks and their attributes (such as arrival time, execution time, deadline, priority, etc.) are known in advance and do not change during the system execution. A static system can be scheduled offline, meaning that the task allocation and ordering can be determined before the system runs. A static system can be validated for its correctness and timeliness, meaning that it can be proven that the system will meet all the task deadlines and functional requirements. Static systems are suitable for hard real-time systems, where missing a deadline can have catastrophic consequences. Static systems provide predictable and deterministic behavior, but they are not flexible or adaptable to changes in the workload or the environment. Static systems may also have poor performance in terms of resource utilization and response time, as they may have to reserve resources for the worst-case scenarios. Static systems are often implemented using cyclic executives, which are loops of fixed-length frames that execute a set of tasks in a predefined order.   

- A **dynamic system** is one where the tasks and their attributes can vary during the system execution. A dynamic system cannot be scheduled offline, meaning that the task allocation and ordering have to be determined online, as the system runs. A dynamic system cannot be validated for its correctness and timeliness, meaning that it cannot be guaranteed that the system will meet all the task deadlines and functional requirements. Dynamic systems are suitable for soft real-time systems, where missing a deadline can have degraded but acceptable consequences. Dynamic systems provide flexible and adaptable behavior, as they can respond to changes in the workload or the environment. Dynamic systems may also have better performance in terms of resource utilization and response time, as they can optimize the use of resources based on the current situation. Dynamic systems are often implemented using priority-based preemptive schedulers, which assign priorities to tasks based on their attributes and allow higher-priority tasks to interrupt lower-priority tasks.   

- The choice between static and dynamic systems depends on the characteristics and requirements of the real-time system. Static systems are preferred for hard real-time systems that have a fixed and predictable workload and need high reliability and safety. Dynamic systems are preferred for soft real-time systems that have a variable and unpredictable workload and need high adaptability and efficiency.
