### Processors and Resources

- Processors and resources are two major types of system components that are involved in the execution of real-time tasks.
- Processors are also known as servers or active resources. They are essential for the execution of a job. A job must have one or more processors in order to execute and proceed towards completion. Example: computer, transmission link, disk, database server.
- Resources are also known as passive resources. A job may or may not require a resource during its execution. A resource is a system component that can be shared by multiple jobs, but only one job can access it at a time. A resource can be either preemptable or non-preemptable. A preemptable resource can be released by a job before its completion, while a non-preemptable resource must be held by a job until its completion. Example: printer, semaphore, file.
- Processors and resources can affect the timing behavior and performance of real-time tasks. For example, a processor can have different scheduling policies, such as first-come first-served (FCFS), priority-based, or deadline-based, that determine the order of execution of tasks. A resource can have different access protocols, such as priority inheritance, priority ceiling, or stack resource policy, that prevent or resolve deadlocks and priority inversions among tasks.
- Processors and resources can also have different characteristics, such as speed, capacity, availability, reliability, and power consumption, that influence the quality of service (QoS) and energy efficiency of real-time systems.
- Processors and resources can be distributed across different nodes or locations in a real-time system, such as edge devices, gateways, or cloud servers. This introduces challenges such as communication delays, network congestion, data consistency, and security. To address these challenges, real-time systems can use techniques such as time synchronization, time-sensitive networking (TSN), and real-time configuration and optimization .
