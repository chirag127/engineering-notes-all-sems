### Precedence Constraints and Data Dependency

- Precedence constraints and data dependency are two types of constraints that may affect the execution order of jobs in real time systems.
- Precedence constraints are imposed by the logical or temporal dependencies among jobs, such as control flow or synchronization. For example, a job J1 may need to finish before another job J2 can start, or a job J3 may need to wait for a signal from another job J4. Precedence constraints can be represented by a directed graph called a precedence graph, where vertices are jobs and edges are precedence relations  .
- Data dependency is imposed by the communication or sharing of data among jobs, such as message passing or shared memory. For example, a job J5 may need to read some data produced by another job J6, or a job J7 may need to write some data to a shared buffer accessed by another job J8. Data dependency cannot be captured by a precedence graph, but it may affect the schedulability and correctness of the system .
- Precedence constraints and data dependency are important factors to consider when designing and analyzing real time systems, as they may introduce additional constraints or conflicts on the available resources, such as processors, memory, or communication channels. Therefore, they may affect the feasibility, optimality, and performance of the scheduling algorithms and protocols used in real time systems .
