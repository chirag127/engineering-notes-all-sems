# Scheduling Aperiodic and Sporadic Jobs in Priority Driven and Clock Driven Systems

## Aperiodic and Sporadic Jobs

- Aperiodic jobs are jobs that have random release times and no deadlines. They are usually generated by external events, such as user inputs, interrupts, or network packets. Aperiodic jobs are typically soft real-time jobs, meaning that they do not have strict timing constraints, but their performance degrades as their response time increases.
- Sporadic jobs are jobs that have random release times and hard deadlines. They are also generated by external events, but they have a minimum inter-arrival time between consecutive jobs. Sporadic jobs are typically hard real-time jobs, meaning that they must meet their deadlines to avoid system failure or unacceptable consequences.
- Aperiodic and sporadic jobs are different from periodic jobs, which have fixed release times and deadlines, and are usually generated by internal events, such as timers, sensors, or controllers. Periodic jobs are also hard real-time jobs, and they form the basis of most real-time systems.

## Priority Driven and Clock Driven Systems

- A priority driven system is a system that assigns a fixed priority to each job, and schedules the jobs according to their priorities. The highest priority job is always executed first, and lower priority jobs are preempted by higher priority jobs. A priority driven system can be either preemptive or non-preemptive, depending on whether a job can be interrupted by a higher priority job or not. A priority driven system is also called a fixed priority system, because the priorities of the jobs do not change during the execution.
- A clock driven system is a system that assigns a fixed schedule to each job, and executes the jobs according to their schedules. The schedule is determined offline, based on the worst-case execution times and deadlines of the jobs. A clock driven system is also called a static or cyclic system, because the schedule is repeated periodically. A clock driven system is always preemptive, meaning that a job can be interrupted by another job according to the schedule.

If the aperiodic jobs are executed too late, they may have long response times, reducing their quality of service.
