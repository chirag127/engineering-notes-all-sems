
## Unit 2 - Real Time Scheduling

* Real-time scheduling is a process of scheduling tasks to be completed within a certain period of time. 
* It is used in a variety of applications, from embedded systems to operating systems. 
* It is important to ensure that tasks are completed within the allotted time period, as failure to do so can result in disastrous consequences.
* Real-time scheduling algorithms are designed to ensure that tasks are completed within the allotted time frame, while also taking into account other factors such as resource availability, priority, and deadlines.
* Common real-time scheduling algorithms include Earliest Deadline First (EDF), Rate Monotonic (RM), and Least Laxity First (LLF).
* EDF assigns tasks to the earliest deadline, while RM assigns tasks to the highest priority. LLF assigns tasks to the least laxity, which is the difference between the current time and the deadline. 
* Real-time scheduling algorithms are used to assign tasks to processors, to ensure that tasks are completed in a timely manner. 
* Real-time scheduling algorithms are also used to optimize system performance, by ensuring that tasks are completed within the allotted time frame.
