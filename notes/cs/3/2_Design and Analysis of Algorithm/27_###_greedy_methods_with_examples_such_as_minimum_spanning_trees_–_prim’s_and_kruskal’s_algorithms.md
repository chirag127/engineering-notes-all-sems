Greedy methods are a class of algorithms that are designed to solve optimization problems by making a series of locally optimal choices. These algorithms work by selecting the optimal choice at each step, and they are often used to solve problems in the areas of combinatorial optimization, graph theory, and computer science.

An MST is a tree that connects all the vertices in a graph with the minimum total weight, where the weight of an edge is the cost of connecting two vertices. There are two well-known algorithms for solving the MST problem: Prim's algorithm and Kruskal's algorithm.

2. Kruskal's Algorithm: This algorithm sorts the edges in ascending order and adds an edge to the tree if it does not create a cycle. The process is repeated until all vertices are in the tree.

Both Prim's and Kruskal's algorithms are efficient and widely used for solving the MST problem. However, Prim's algorithm is generally considered to be faster for dense graphs, while Kruskal's algorithm is generally considered to be faster for sparse graphs.
