## Unit 3 - Divide and Conquer with Examples Such as Sorting, Matrix Multiplication, Convex Hull and Searching. Greedy Methods with Examples Such as Optimal Reliability Allocation, Knapsack, Minimum Spanning Trees – Prim’s and Kruskal’s Algorithms, Single Source Shortest Paths - Dijkstra’s and Bellman Ford Algorithms.

Unit 3 of the Design and Analysis of Algorithms course focuses on Divide and Conquer and Greedy Methods.

Divide and Conquer is a technique for solving problems by breaking down the problem into smaller sub-problems, solving each sub-problem, and combining the solutions to the sub-problems to form the solution to the original problem. Examples of problems solved using Divide and Conquer include Sorting, Matrix Multiplication, and Convex Hull.

Greedy Methods are algorithms that make the locally optimal choice at each step, with the hope of finding a global optimum. Examples of problems solved using Greedy Methods include Optimal Reliability Allocation, Knapsack, Minimum Spanning Trees, and Single Source Shortest Paths.

In the context of the Design and Analysis of Algorithms course, students in Unit 3 will learn about Divide and Conquer and Greedy Methods, including working with examples of each method, analyzing the benefits and limitations of each method, and learning how to implement each method to solve specific problems.
