
### Binomial Heaps

Binomial heaps are a type of data structure used in the design and analysis of algorithms. They are a collection of binomial trees, which are trees with at most two children. The trees are arranged in a heap-like structure, so that the root of each tree is greater than the roots of its children.

Binomial heaps are used for several operations, including finding the minimum element, merging two heaps, and deleting the minimum element. The running time of these operations is logarithmic in the number of elements in the heap.

Binomial heaps are related to Fibonacci heaps, which are a more complex data structure with better running times for certain operations. Binomial heaps are simpler and easier to implement than Fibonacci heaps, but have worse running times for some operations.
