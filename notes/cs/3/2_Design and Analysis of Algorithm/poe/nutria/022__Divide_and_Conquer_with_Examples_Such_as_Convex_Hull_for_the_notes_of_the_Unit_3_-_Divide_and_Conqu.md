
### Divide and Conquer with Examples Such as Convex Hull

* Divide and Conquer is an algorithmic technique used to solve problems by breaking them down into smaller subproblems. 
* Examples of problems that can be solved using Divide and Conquer include sorting, matrix multiplication, convex hull and searching. 
* Sorting algorithms such as Quick Sort, Merge Sort, and Heap Sort use the Divide and Conquer technique to divide the problem into smaller subproblems and then combine the solutions to the subproblems to obtain the overall solution.
* Matrix multiplication can be done using the Divide and Conquer technique by dividing the two matrices into four submatrices and then combining the solutions of the submatrices to obtain the overall solution.
* Convex hull is a problem that can be solved using the Divide and Conquer technique by dividing the set of points into two subproblems and then combining the solutions of the two subproblems to obtain the overall solution.
* Searching is a problem that can be solved using the Divide and Conquer technique by dividing the problem into two subproblems and then combining the solutions of the two subproblems to obtain the overall solution.

### Greedy Methods with Examples Such as Optimal Reliability Allocation, Knapsack, Minimum Spanning Trees – Prim’s and Kruskal’s Algorithms, Single Source Shortest Paths - Dijkstra’s and Bellman Ford Algorithms

* Greedy methods are algorithms that make decisions based on the best immediate outcome, without considering the long-term consequences. 
* Examples of problems that can be solved using Greedy methods include optimal reliability allocation, knapsack, minimum spanning trees – Prim’s and Kruskal’s algorithms, and single source shortest paths – Dijkstra’s and Bellman Ford algorithms. 
* Optimal reliability allocation is a problem that can be solved using a Greedy method by selecting the components with the highest reliability until the desired reliability is achieved.
* Knapsack is a problem that can be solved using a Greedy method by selecting the items with the highest value until the desired weight is achieved.
* Minimum spanning trees – Prim’s and Kruskal’s algorithms are problems that can be solved using Greedy methods by selecting the edges with the lowest weights until a minimum spanning tree is formed.
* Single source shortest paths – Dijkstra’s and Bellman Ford algorithms are problems that can be solved using Greedy methods by selecting the shortest paths from the source to all other vertices.
