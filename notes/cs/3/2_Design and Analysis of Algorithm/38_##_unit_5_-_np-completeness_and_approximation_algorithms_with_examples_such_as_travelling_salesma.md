NP-Completeness and Approximation Algorithms with Examples Such as Travelling Salesman Problem, Graph Coloring, n-Queen Problem, Hamiltonian Cycles and Sum of Subsets.

NP-completeness is a concept in computer science that refers to problems that are believed to be computationally difficult to solve. These problems are called NP-complete problems, and they are considered to be the most difficult problems in computer science.

NP-complete problems are characterized by the fact that they are difficult to solve, but they are relatively easy to verify. This means that it is relatively easy to check whether a solution to an NP-complete problem is correct, but it is difficult to find a solution in the first place.

One example of an NP-complete problem is the Travelling Salesman Problem. In this problem, a salesman must visit a set of cities and return to the starting city, visiting each city exactly once and minimizing the total distance travelled.

Another example of an NP-complete problem is the Graph Coloring problem. In this problem, the goal is to color a graph with a minimum number of colors, such that no two adjacent vertices have the same color.
