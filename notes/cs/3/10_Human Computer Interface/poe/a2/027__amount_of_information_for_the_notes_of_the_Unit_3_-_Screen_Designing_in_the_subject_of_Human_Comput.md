 Here are the notes for Unit 3 - Screen Designing in the subject of Human Computer Interface:

### Amount of Information
- Determine the right amount of information to display on the screen. Too much information can overwhelm and confuse the user. Too little information can frustrate the user.
- Group related information together and separate unrelated information. Use headings and formatting to distinct different sections of information.
- Remove unnecessary words and be concise. Only display information that is relevant and useful for the user.
- Ensure the information displayed is easy to read and comprehend. Use appropriate font sizes, colors, and spacing.
- Allow users to access additional details or related information through expandable sections, popups, or hyperlinks. But do not clutter the screen with too much information upfront.
- Consider how users will process and use the information. What information do they need to complete their tasks? What is most important for them to know? Organize and prioritize the information accordingly.

### Screen Layout and Design
- Use a simple and consistent layout with clear groupings of related elements. Align objects and utilize whitespace to make the layout easy to understand.
- Place the most important information or actions in the most visible and accessible areas of the screen (top left). Items at the bottom or right of the screen may be missed by users.
- Use minimal distractions and design the screen to draw attention to the key content or functions. Avoid busy backgrounds, gaudy colors, and excessive graphics or images which can be distracting.
- Ensure the screen layout accommodates different screen sizes and devices. A responsive and flexible design is necessary to support varying viewport dimensions.
- Include navigation options to allow users to easily move between pieces of information or screens. Clear labels, icons, and pathways improve usability.
