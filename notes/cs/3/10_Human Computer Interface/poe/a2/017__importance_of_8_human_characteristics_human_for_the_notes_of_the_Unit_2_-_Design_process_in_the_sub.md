### Importance of 8 human characteristics for the notes of the Unit 2 - Design process in the subject of Human Computer Interface

1. Physiology: The design of the interface must be suitable for human physiology. For example, the size of buttons and text must be suitable for human fingers and eyes respectively. The placement of elements must be suitable for human reach and visibility.

2. Perception: The design must be perceptually simple and intuitive. It should match user expectations of conventions, affordances, etc. For example, a button must look like a button. Use of colors, shapes, etc must be as per conventions.

3. Physical capabilities: The demands placed on the human, in terms of dexterity and physical abilities to interact with the system, must be within the physical capabilities of the user population. For example, if the target users may have motor difficulties, the interface must not require high dexterity.

4. Cognitive capabilities: The design must be within the cognitive capabilities of the user population. The complexity, language, memory load, etc must be suitable for the intended users. For example, complex or technical interfaces may not suit children or lay users.

5. Emotional/motivational: The design should motivate the users and have a positive emotional appeal. For example, pleasant aesthetics, feedback, rewards, etc can motivate and please the users. Bad designs or errors can frustrate the users.

6. Cultural: The design must suit the cultural context of the users. For example, colors or images have cultural interpretations. Also, conventions and meanings differ across cultures. The interface must account for these cultural aspects.

7. Social: The interface may have a social use or social implications. For example, social media interfaces. The design must suit collaboration, sharing, etc. It must account for privacy, social validation, etc.

8. Task: The interface must enable the users to complete necessary tasks effectively, efficiently and satisfactorily. It must have relevant functions/information/controls to enable task completion. The mapping between user actions and system responses must enable learning and task completion.
