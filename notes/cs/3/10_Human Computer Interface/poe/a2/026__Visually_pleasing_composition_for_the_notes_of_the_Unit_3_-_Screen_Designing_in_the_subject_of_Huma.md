### Visually pleasing composition for the notes of the Unit 3 - Screen Designing in the subject of Human Computer Interface

1. Ensure ample white space: Having adequate white space makes the content easy to read and comprehend. Crowding the screen with excessive information makes it cluttered and confusing for the user.

2. Use consistent and minimal formatting: Choose a simple and minimal theme for the formatting. This includes using consistent header sizes, font types and sizes, bullet points or numbered lists, etc. Too many formats and styles make the screen look jarring and haphazard.

3. Highlight important elements: Use formatting techniques like bold, italics, highlighting or captions to draw attention to the most important pieces of information. This makes it easy for the user to skim and locate key points quickly.

4. Use images and graphics appropriately: Only include images and graphics that add value to the content. They should not be used to merely decorate the page or screen. All images must have descriptive alt text for accessibility and be relevant to the topic.

5. Align and organize: Arrange all elements in an orderly fashion. Headings and subheadings should follow a logical flow. All content should be aligned properly without crooked or uneven placement. An organized layout makes the information easy to follow and understand.

6. Maintain balance: The use of white space, formatting, images and written content should be balanced well on the screen. No one element should dominate while others are lacking in presence. A balanced composition is more appealing and useful for the user.

7. Limit clutter: Keep the screen free of clutter like advertisements, promotions or unnecessary widgets. Only include content that is relevant to the user's goals. Too much clutter is distracting, annoying and hampers usability.

8. Use contrast effectively: Employ contrasting colors and fonts to highlight important elements and make the content easy to read. However, excessive use of bright or dark contrasts can make the screen stressful or inaccessible for some users. Contrast should be used judiciously for best results.
