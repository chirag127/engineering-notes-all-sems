### Characteristics for the notes of the Unit 1 - Introduction in the subject of Human Computer Interface

1. Human-Computer Interaction (HCI) is the study of how people interact with computers and to what extent computers are or are not developed for successful interaction with human beings.
2. A significant number of major corporations and academic institutions now study HCI.
3. HCI is a broad field which overlaps with areas such as user-centered design, user interface design, ergonomics, human factors engineering, cognitive psychology, and computer science.
4. The goals of HCI are to produce usable and safe systems, as well as functional systems.
5. Usability includes user satisfaction, efficiency, and effectiveness.
6. Safety includes protecting the user from dangerous conditions and undesirable situations.
7. Functional systems refer to the extent to which a system is able to perform the tasks for which it was designed.
8. HCI is an iterative process, where design is based on user feedback and then tested with users to ensure that it meets their needs and expectations.
9. HCI practitioners use a variety of methods to gather feedback from users, including surveys, interviews, and observations.
10. HCI design principles include consistency, visibility, feedback, flexibility, and error prevention and recovery.
