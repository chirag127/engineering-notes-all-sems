### Pointing Devices

Pointing devices are input devices that allow a user to interact with a computer by moving a cursor on the screen. They are used to select, move, and manipulate objects on the screen. Some common pointing devices include:

1. **Mouse**: A mouse is a small handheld device that is moved across a flat surface to control the movement of the cursor on the screen. It typically has one or more buttons that can be used to select and manipulate objects on the screen.

2. **Trackball**: A trackball is similar to a mouse, but instead of moving the entire device, the user moves a ball that is mounted on the top of the device. The movement of the ball is translated into movement of the cursor on the screen.

3. **Touchpad**: A touchpad is a flat, touch-sensitive surface that is typically found on laptop computers. The user moves their finger across the surface to control the movement of the cursor on the screen.

4. **Stylus**: A stylus is a small pen-like device that is used to interact with touchscreens. The user can use the stylus to tap, swipe, and draw on the screen.

5. **Joystick**: A joystick is a handheld device that consists of a stick that pivots on a base. The user moves the stick to control the movement of the cursor on the screen.

These are some of the common pointing devices used in Human-Computer Interaction. Each device has its own advantages and disadvantages, and the choice of device may depend on the specific task or application.
