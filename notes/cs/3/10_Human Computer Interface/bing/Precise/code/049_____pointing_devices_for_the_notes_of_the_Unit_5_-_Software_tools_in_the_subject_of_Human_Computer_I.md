### Pointing Devices

Pointing devices are input devices that allow a user to interact with a computer by moving a cursor or pointer on the screen. These devices are commonly used for selecting, dragging, and clicking on objects on the screen. Some common pointing devices include:

1. **Mouse**: A mouse is a handheld device that is moved across a flat surface to control the movement of the cursor on the screen. It typically has one or more buttons for clicking and a scroll wheel for scrolling.

2. **Touchpad**: A touchpad is a flat, touch-sensitive surface that is used to control the movement of the cursor on the screen. It is commonly found on laptops and is operated by moving a finger across the surface.

3. **Trackball**: A trackball is similar to a mouse, but instead of moving the entire device, the user moves a ball that is mounted on the top of the device to control the movement of the cursor on the screen.

4. **Stylus**: A stylus is a pen-like device that is used to interact with touchscreens. It allows for more precise input than using a finger.

5. **Joystick**: A joystick is a handheld device that is used to control the movement of an object on the screen. It typically has a stick that can be moved in different directions and one or more buttons for clicking.

6. **Graphics tablet**: A graphics tablet is a flat surface that is used to create digital drawings and illustrations. It is operated by using a stylus to draw on the surface, and the movements of the stylus are translated into digital strokes on the screen.

These are some of the common pointing devices used in Human-Computer Interaction. Each device has its own advantages and disadvantages, and the choice of device may depend on the specific task or application.
