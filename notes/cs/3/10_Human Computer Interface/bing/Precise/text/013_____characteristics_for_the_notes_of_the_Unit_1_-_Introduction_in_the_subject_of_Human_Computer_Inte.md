### Characteristics for the notes of the Unit 1 - Introduction in the subject of Human Computer Interface

1. Human-Computer Interaction (HCI) is the study of how people interact with computers and to what extent computers are or are not developed for successful interaction with human beings.
2. A significant number of major corporations and academic institutions now study HCI.
3. HCI is a broad field which overlaps with areas such as user-centered design, user interface design, ergonomics, human factors engineering, cognitive psychology, and computer science.
4. HCI is concerned with the design, evaluation, and implementation of interactive computing systems for human use and with the study of major phenomena surrounding them.
5. HCI practitioners aim to create user interfaces that are easy to learn and use, efficient, and enjoyable.
6. HCI practitioners also aim to create interfaces that are accessible to people with disabilities and that can be used in a variety of contexts and environments.
7. HCI practitioners use a variety of methods to gather data on user needs and preferences, including interviews, surveys, and observations.
8. HCI practitioners also use a variety of methods to evaluate the usability of interfaces, including usability testing, heuristic evaluation, and cognitive walkthroughs.
9. HCI practitioners work in a variety of settings, including software development companies, research labs, and universities.
10. HCI is an interdisciplinary field, and practitioners often have backgrounds in computer science, psychology, design, or other related fields.
