### Graphical System for the Notes of the Unit 1 - Introduction in the Subject of Human Computer Interface

- Human-computer interaction (HCI) is a design field that focuses on the interfaces between people and computers.
- HCI incorporates multiple disciplines, such as computer science, psychology, human factors, and ergonomics, into one field.
- HCI aims to create intuitive and usable interfaces that meet the needs and preferences of the users.
- The graphical user interface (GUI) is a form of user interface that allows users to interact with electronic devices through graphical icons and audio indicators, instead of text-based UIs, typed command labels or text navigation.
- The GUI was developed at the Xerox Palo Alto Research Center (PARC) in the late 1970s, but many argue that its concept was theorized long before.
- The GUI lets users communicate with the computer with the help of the mouse, which can manipulate the graphical icons on the screen.
- The GUI is based on the WIMP (windows, icons, menus, and pointers) paradigm, which is comprised of a graphic and text interface navigated by a mouse and keyboard.
- The GUI enables users to perform tasks such as opening, closing, resizing, and moving windows, selecting and activating icons, accessing menus, and using pointers.
- The GUI also supports direct manipulation, which is a principle of HCI that states that users should be able to directly manipulate the objects of interest on the screen, rather than using abstract commands or menus.
- The GUI has advantages such as being easy to learn, intuitive, consistent, and efficient.
- The GUI also has challenges such as being complex, requiring more resources, and needing careful design and evaluation.
- The GUI is one of the main topics of HCI, as it involves graphics, visualization, and interaction design.
- Graphics, visualization, and interaction design are the science, engineering, and design of graphical, visual, informational, and interactive representations for use by humans.
- Graphics, visualization, and interaction design can be used for various purposes, such as data analysis, communication, education, entertainment, and collaboration.
- Graphics, visualization, and interaction design require knowledge and skills in areas such as computer graphics, visual perception, user interface design, user experience design, usability engineering, and user-centered design.
- Graphics, visualization, and interaction design also involve methods and tools such as sketching, prototyping, testing, evaluation, and implementation.
- Graphics, visualization, and interaction design are essential for creating effective and engaging GUIs that can enhance the human-computer interaction.
