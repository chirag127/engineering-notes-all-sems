### Understanding business junctions for the notes of the Unit 2 - Design process in the subject of Human Computer Interface

- Business junctions are the points where different business functions or processes interact with each other or with external entities, such as customers, suppliers, or regulators.
- Business junctions can be used to identify the requirements and constraints of a human-computer interface (HCI) design, as they represent the critical tasks and goals of the users and stakeholders.
- The design process of HCI involves the following steps :
  - **Problem definition**: Understand the problem domain, the user needs, the business goals, and the context of use.
  - **User research**: Conduct user research methods, such as interviews, surveys, observations, or personas, to gather data about the users, their tasks, their preferences, and their pain points.
  - **Ideation**: Generate and evaluate possible design solutions, such as sketches, wireframes, prototypes, or scenarios, using techniques such as brainstorming, affinity diagrams, or storyboarding.
  - **Implementation**: Implement the design solution using appropriate tools and technologies, such as coding, testing, or debugging.
  - **Evaluation**: Evaluate the design solution using methods such as usability testing, heuristic evaluation, or user feedback, to measure its effectiveness, efficiency, and satisfaction.
- The design principles of HCI are the guidelines or best practices that help to create user-friendly and usable interfaces. Some of the common design principles are :
  - **Visibility**: Make the interface elements visible and easy to find, such as buttons, menus, or icons.
  - **Feedback**: Provide feedback to the user about the status and outcome of their actions, such as sounds, messages, or animations.
  - **Consistency**: Maintain consistency across the interface elements, such as colors, fonts, or layouts, to reduce confusion and increase familiarity.
  - **Affordance**: Make the interface elements suggest their functionality and usage, such as shapes, sizes, or labels.
  - **Simplicity**: Reduce the complexity and cognitive load of the interface, such as minimizing the number of steps, options, or inputs.
  - **Flexibility**: Allow the user to customize and adapt the interface to their needs and preferences, such as changing the settings, preferences, or modes.
  - **Error prevention and recovery**: Prevent or minimize the occurrence of errors and provide ways to recover from them, such as validation, confirmation, or undo.
