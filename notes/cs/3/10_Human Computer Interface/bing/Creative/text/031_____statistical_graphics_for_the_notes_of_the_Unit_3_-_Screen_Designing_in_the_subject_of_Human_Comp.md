### Statistical Graphics

- Statistical graphics are graphics used in the field of statistics for data visualization.
- They can be used to present data and the results of statistical analysis, assist in the analysis of data, and occasionally facilitate statistical computation.
- Some common types of statistical graphics are:
  - Bar graph: A graph that uses rectangular bars of different heights or lengths to compare the values of a categorical variable or the frequencies of a discrete variable.
  - Pie chart: A circular chart that uses sectors of different angles or areas to show the proportions of a whole.
  - Line graph: A graph that uses points connected by lines to show the changes of a continuous variable over time or another variable.
  - Scatterplot: A graph that uses dots to show the relationship between two continuous variables.
  - Statistical map: A map that uses colors, symbols, or patterns to show the spatial distribution of a variable or a phenomenon.
- Statistical graphics can be designed to be effective, efficient, and engaging by following some general principles, such as:
  - Choosing an appropriate type of graph for the data and the message.
  - Using scales, labels, legends, and titles to provide clear and accurate information.
  - Avoiding unnecessary clutter, distortion, or deception that may confuse or mislead the audience.
  - Using colors, shapes, sizes, and textures to enhance the visual appeal and the communication of the data.
  - Comparing and contrasting different groups, categories, or variables to highlight the similarities and differences.
