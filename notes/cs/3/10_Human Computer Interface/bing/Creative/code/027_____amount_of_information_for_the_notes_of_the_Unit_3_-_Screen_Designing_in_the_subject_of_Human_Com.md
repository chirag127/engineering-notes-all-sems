# Unit 3 - Screen Designing

## Introduction
Screen designing is the process of creating the visual layout and appearance of the user interface for an application or a website. Screen designing involves the following steps:

- Identifying the user needs and goals
- Defining the content and functionality of the screen
- Organizing the content and functionality into logical groups and hierarchies
- Choosing the appropriate screen elements and controls to present the content and functionality
- Applying the principles of visual design, such as color, typography, alignment, balance, contrast, etc.
- Evaluating and testing the screen design with the users and stakeholders

## Screen Elements and Controls
Screen elements and controls are the components that make up the user interface of a screen. They can be classified into four categories:

- **Input controls**: These are the components that allow the user to enter or manipulate data, such as text fields, buttons, checkboxes, radio buttons, sliders, etc.
- **Output controls**: These are the components that display the data or feedback to the user, such as labels, icons, images, graphs, charts, etc.
- **Navigation controls**: These are the components that enable the user to move between different screens or sections of the screen, such as menus, tabs, breadcrumbs, links, etc.
- **Interaction controls**: These are the components that facilitate the user to perform specific actions or tasks, such as dialogs, pop-ups, tooltips, notifications, etc.

## Principles of Visual Design
Visual design is the aspect of screen designing that focuses on the aesthetics and appeal of the user interface. Visual design can influence the user's perception, emotion, and behavior. Some of the principles of visual design are:

- **Color**: Color can be used to create contrast, hierarchy, harmony, and mood. Color can also convey meaning and symbolism, such as red for danger, green for success, etc. Color should be chosen carefully to suit the context and purpose of the screen, and to ensure readability and accessibility.
- **Typography**: Typography is the art and technique of arranging text on the screen. Typography can affect the legibility, readability, and comprehension of the text. Typography can also create hierarchy, emphasis, and style. Typography should be chosen based on the type of text, the target audience, and the tone of voice. Typography should also follow the principles of alignment, spacing, and consistency.
- **Alignment**: Alignment is the arrangement of screen elements and controls along a common edge or axis. Alignment can create order, structure, and balance on the screen. Alignment can also improve the readability and usability of the screen. Alignment should be consistent and logical, and follow the natural reading direction of the user.
- **Balance**: Balance is the distribution of visual weight and attention on the screen. Balance can create stability, harmony, and symmetry on the screen. Balance can also affect the mood and emotion of the user. Balance can be achieved by using equal or proportional sizes, shapes, colors, and positions of screen elements and controls.
- **Contrast**: Contrast is the difference between two or more screen elements and controls in terms of color, size, shape, texture, etc. Contrast can create focus, hierarchy, and interest on the screen. Contrast can also enhance the visibility and accessibility of the screen. Contrast should be used to highlight the most important or relevant information or actions on the screen, and to separate or group related or unrelated elements and controls.
