### Interaction Devices for the notes of the Unit 5 - Software tools in the subject of Human Computer Interface

Interaction devices are the hardware and software components that enable the communication and exchange of information between humans and computers. Interaction devices can be classified into three categories: input devices, output devices, and combined devices.

- Input devices are the ones that allow humans to provide data, commands, or feedback to the computer. Examples of input devices are keyboards, mice, touchscreens, microphones, cameras, scanners, etc.
- Output devices are the ones that allow computers to present data, information, or feedback to the humans. Examples of output devices are monitors, speakers, printers, projectors, haptic devices, etc.
- Combined devices are the ones that allow both input and output functions in a single device. Examples of combined devices are smartphones, tablets, smartwatches, virtual reality headsets, etc.

The design and evaluation of interaction devices are important aspects of human-computer interaction (HCI), as they affect the usability, accessibility, and user experience of the system. Some of the criteria that can be used to assess the quality of interaction devices are:

- Affordance: the extent to which the device suggests how it can be used by its appearance, shape, or feedback.
- Mapping: the correspondence between the actions performed on the device and the effects on the system.
- Consistency: the degree to which the device behaves in a predictable and coherent manner across different contexts and tasks.
- Feedback: the information provided by the device or the system to indicate the status, results, or errors of the interaction.
- Constraints: the limitations or restrictions imposed by the device or the system to prevent errors or guide the user.
- Compatibility: the alignment between the device and the user's expectations, preferences, skills, and abilities.

Some of the new trends and challenges in interaction devices are:

- Natural user interfaces (NUI): the use of natural modalities, such as gestures, speech, or eye movements, to interact with the system without the need for physical devices.
- Multimodal interfaces: the use of multiple input and output modalities, such as audio, visual, or tactile, to enhance the richness and flexibility of the interaction.
- Tangible user interfaces (TUI): the use of physical objects, such as toys, cards, or tokens, to manipulate digital information in a tangible and intuitive way.
- Wearable interfaces: the use of devices that are worn on the body, such as glasses, watches, or clothing, to provide ubiquitous and context-aware interaction.
- Brain-computer interfaces (BCI): the use of devices that measure and interpret brain signals, such as electroencephalography (EEG), to enable direct communication between the brain and the computer.
