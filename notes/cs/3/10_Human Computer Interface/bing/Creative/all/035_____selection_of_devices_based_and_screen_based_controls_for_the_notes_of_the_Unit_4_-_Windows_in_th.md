# Selection of Device-Based and Screen-Based Controls for Windows

Device-based controls, often called input devices, are the mechanisms through which people communicate their desires to the system . Screen-based controls, often called widgets, are the graphical components that display information or allow interaction on the screen.

## Device-Based Controls

Some of the characteristics and capabilities of device-based controls are:

- To point at an object on the screen.
- To select the object or identify it as the focus of attention.
- To manipulate the object by moving, resizing, rotating, etc.
- To enter text, numbers, symbols, or commands.
- To provide feedback to the user, such as sound, vibration, or force.

Some of the common device-based controls are:

- Mouse: A pointing device that can move a cursor on the screen and has one or more buttons for clicking, dragging, or scrolling.
- Keyboard: A text input device that has a set of keys for typing letters, numbers, symbols, or commands.
- Touchscreen: A display device that can detect the location and pressure of a finger or a stylus on its surface and can act as both a pointing and a text input device.
- Trackball: A pointing device that has a rotating ball that can be moved by the user's thumb or fingers and has one or more buttons for clicking, dragging, or scrolling.
- Joystick: A pointing device that has a stick that can be tilted in different directions and has one or more buttons for clicking, dragging, or scrolling.
- Gamepad: A gaming device that has a set of buttons, triggers, and analog sticks for controlling the actions of a character or a vehicle in a game.
- Microphone: A sound input device that can capture the user's voice or other sounds and can be used for speech recognition, voice commands, or audio communication.
- Webcam: A video input device that can capture the user's image or other visual scenes and can be used for face recognition, gesture recognition, or video communication.

## Screen-Based Controls

Some of the characteristics and capabilities of screen-based controls are:

- To display information, such as text, images, icons, or graphics.
- To organize information, such as menus, toolbars, tabs, or panels.
- To navigate information, such as buttons, links, sliders, or scrollbars.
- To edit information, such as text fields, checkboxes, radio buttons, or drop-down lists.
- To provide feedback to the user, such as labels, tooltips, messages, or progress bars.

Some of the common screen-based controls are:

- Window: A rectangular area on the screen that can display information or allow interaction with a specific application or document.
- Menu: A list of commands or options that can be selected by the user.
- Toolbar: A row or column of buttons or icons that can provide quick access to frequently used commands or functions.
- Tab: A graphical element that can divide a window into multiple sections and can be used to switch between different views or modes of information or interaction.
- Panel: A sub-window that can display additional information or options related to the main window or the current task.
- Button: A graphical element that can initiate an action or a command when clicked by the user.
- Link: A graphical element that can connect to another location or resource when clicked by the user.
- Slider: A graphical element that can adjust a numerical value or a range of values by dragging a handle along a track.
- Scrollbar: A graphical element that can move the visible portion of a window or a document by dragging a thumb along a track or by clicking the arrows at the ends of the track.
- Text field: A graphical element that can accept text input from the user.
- Checkbox: A graphical element that can indicate a binary choice or a multiple selection by showing a check mark or a cross mark when clicked by the user.
- Radio button: A graphical element that can indicate a single choice or a multiple selection by showing a dot or a circle when clicked by the user.
- Drop-down list: A graphical element that can display a list of options that can be selected by the user by clicking the arrow at the end of the element.
- Label: A graphical element that can display a short text or an icon that can describe or identify another graphical element or a piece of information.
- Tooltip: A graphical element that can display a brief text or an icon that can provide additional information or guidance about another graphical element or a piece of information when the user hovers the cursor over it.
