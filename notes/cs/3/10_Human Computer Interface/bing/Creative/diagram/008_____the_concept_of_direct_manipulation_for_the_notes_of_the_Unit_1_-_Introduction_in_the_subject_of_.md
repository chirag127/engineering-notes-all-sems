### The concept of direct manipulation

- Direct manipulation is an approach to human-computer interaction (HCI) that involves continuous representation of objects of interest and rapid, reversible, and incremental actions and feedback.
- Direct manipulation aims to make the user feel like they are performing the task themselves, not through an intermediary like a computer.
- Direct manipulation is sometimes equated with "what you see is what you get" (WYSIWYG), which means that the interface shows the user the exact result of their actions.
- Some examples of direct manipulation interfaces are:
  - Graphical user interfaces (GUIs) that allow the user to manipulate icons, windows, menus, and widgets with a mouse or a touch screen.
  - Virtual reality (VR) and augmented reality (AR) systems that allow the user to interact with virtual or enhanced objects and environments with gestures, voice, or controllers.
  - Drawing and painting applications that allow the user to create and modify images with a stylus or a finger.
  - Spreadsheet applications that allow the user to enter and manipulate data with formulas and functions.
- Some advantages of direct manipulation interfaces are:
  - They are easy to learn and use, as they rely on natural and intuitive actions and feedback.
  - They are engaging and satisfying, as they give the user a sense of control and accomplishment.
  - They are efficient and productive, as they reduce errors and allow the user to perform complex tasks with fewer steps.
- Some disadvantages of direct manipulation interfaces are:
  - They may require more screen space and computational resources than other interfaces, as they need to display rich and dynamic graphics and animations.
  - They may not support all types of tasks and users, as they may be limited by the physical constraints and capabilities of the input and output devices.
  - They may not provide enough guidance and feedback for novice or infrequent users, as they may rely on implicit cues and conventions that are not always obvious or consistent.
