## Unit 1 - Introduction

- This unit introduces the basic concepts and principles of artificial intelligence (AI), which is the study of how to create machines and software that can perform tasks that require human intelligence.
- AI is a broad and interdisciplinary field that draws from computer science, mathematics, logic, psychology, philosophy, linguistics, neuroscience, and other disciplines.
- AI can be divided into two main branches: **narrow AI** and **general AI**.
  - Narrow AI refers to systems that can perform specific tasks that require intelligence, such as playing chess, recognizing faces, or translating languages. These systems are usually designed for a particular domain or application, and they may not be able to handle situations that are outside their scope or context.
  - General AI refers to systems that can exhibit human-like intelligence across a wide range of domains and tasks, such as reasoning, learning, planning, creativity, and common sense. These systems are not yet fully realized, and they are the ultimate goal of AI research.
- AI can also be classified into different types based on the level of human involvement or intervention: **weak AI**, **strong AI**, and **super AI**.
  - Weak AI refers to systems that can simulate or mimic human intelligence, but they do not actually understand or possess the underlying meaning or intention of their actions or outputs. These systems are usually based on rules, algorithms, or data that are provided by humans, and they may not be able to explain or justify their decisions or behaviors.
  - Strong AI refers to systems that can actually understand and possess human intelligence, and they can act autonomously and independently of human guidance or control. These systems are usually based on self-learning, self-improvement, or self-awareness, and they may be able to generate their own goals, values, or emotions.
  - Super AI refers to systems that can surpass human intelligence in all aspects, and they can perform tasks that are beyond the capabilities or comprehension of humans. These systems are usually based on exponential growth, recursive self-improvement, or artificial evolution, and they may pose ethical, social, or existential risks or challenges for humanity.
- AI can also be categorized into different areas based on the methods or techniques that are used to achieve intelligence: **symbolic AI**, **sub-symbolic AI**, and **hybrid AI**.
  - Symbolic AI refers to systems that use symbols, rules, logic, or knowledge representation to model and manipulate abstract concepts and reasoning. These systems are usually based on deductive, inductive, or abductive inference, and they can perform tasks such as problem-solving, planning, or natural language processing.
  - Sub-symbolic AI refers to systems that use numerical, statistical, or probabilistic methods to model and manipulate low-level data and patterns. These systems are usually based on learning, adaptation, or optimization, and they can perform tasks such as perception, classification, or neural networks.
  - Hybrid AI refers to systems that combine both symbolic and sub-symbolic methods to achieve a balance between high-level abstraction and low-level data processing. These systems are usually based on integration, coordination, or collaboration, and they can perform tasks such as knowledge discovery, decision making, or cognitive architectures.
