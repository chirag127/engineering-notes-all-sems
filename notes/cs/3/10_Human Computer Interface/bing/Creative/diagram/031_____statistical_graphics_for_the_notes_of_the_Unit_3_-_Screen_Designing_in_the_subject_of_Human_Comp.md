### Statistical Graphics

- Statistical graphics are graphics used in the field of statistics for data visualization.
- They allow the results of statistical analysis to be displayed in some sort of pictorial form, such as graphs, charts, maps, etc.
- They can be used for different purposes, such as:
  - Presentation: to communicate data and findings to others in a clear and concise way.
  - Exploration: to discover patterns, trends, outliers, and relationships in data.
  - Analysis: to support or refute hypotheses, test assumptions, and perform calculations.
- Some examples of common statistical graphics are:
  - Bar graph: a graph that uses bars of different heights or lengths to compare quantities or frequencies of different categories.
  - Pie chart: a circular chart that uses sectors of different angles or areas to show the proportions of a whole.
  - Line graph: a graph that uses lines to connect points that represent values of a variable over time or another continuous dimension.
  - Scatterplot: a graph that uses dots to show the relationship between two variables, usually on a Cartesian plane.
  - Statistical map: a map that uses colors, symbols, or other visual elements to show the distribution or variation of a variable across a geographic area.
- Some principles of good statistical graphics are:
  - Simplicity: to avoid unnecessary clutter and complexity that may distract or confuse the viewer.
  - Clarity: to use appropriate labels, legends, scales, and units that make the data and message easy to understand.
  - Accuracy: to ensure that the data and graphics are consistent, reliable, and free of errors or distortions.
  - Relevance: to choose the type and format of graphics that best suit the data and the purpose of the analysis.
  - Aesthetics: to use colors, shapes, and fonts that are pleasing and appropriate for the context and audience.
