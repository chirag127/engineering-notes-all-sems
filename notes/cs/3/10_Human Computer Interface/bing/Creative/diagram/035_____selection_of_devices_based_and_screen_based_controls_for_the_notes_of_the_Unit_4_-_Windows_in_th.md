### Selection of Device-Based and Screen-Based Controls for Windows

Device-based controls, often called input devices, are the mechanisms through which people communicate their desires to the system . Screen-based controls, often called widgets, are the graphical components that appear on the screen and allow users to interact with the system.

Some of the factors that influence the selection of device-based and screen-based controls for windows are:

- The characteristics and capabilities of device-based controls, such as pointing, selecting, entering, and manipulating .
- The characteristics and capabilities of screen-based controls, such as appearance, behavior, feedback, and compatibility.
- The nature and purpose of the window, such as primary, secondary, dialog, or pop-up.
- The type and amount of information to be displayed and entered in the window, such as text, graphics, numbers, or commands.
- The user's preferences, expectations, and skills, such as familiarity, comfort, and efficiency.
- The context and environment of use, such as multiple monitors, touchscreens, or ambient light  .

Some of the guidelines for selecting device-based and screen-based controls for windows are:

- Use device-based controls that are appropriate for the task and the user, such as mouse, keyboard, touchpad, stylus, or voice .
- Use screen-based controls that are consistent, clear, concise, and responsive, such as buttons, menus, icons, sliders, or checkboxes.
- Use screen-based controls that are compatible with the device-based controls, such as providing keyboard shortcuts, mouse-over effects, or touch gestures.
- Use screen-based controls that are suitable for the window type, such as providing navigation, confirmation, or error messages.
- Use screen-based controls that are optimal for the information type, such as using text fields, radio buttons, or drop-down lists.
- Use screen-based controls that are adaptable to the user's preferences, such as allowing customization, resizing, or repositioning.
- Use screen-based controls that are adaptable to the context and environment of use, such as adjusting brightness, contrast, or font size  .
