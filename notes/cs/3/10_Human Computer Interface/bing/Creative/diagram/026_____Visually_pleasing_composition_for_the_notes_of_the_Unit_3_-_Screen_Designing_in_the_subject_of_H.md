# Unit 3 - Screen Designing

## Introduction

Screen designing is the process of creating the layout, appearance, and functionality of the user interface (UI) of a software application or a website. Screen designing involves the following steps:

- Identifying the user needs and goals
- Defining the content and functionality of the screen
- Organizing the content and functionality into logical groups and hierarchies
- Choosing the appropriate visual elements and styles to present the content and functionality
- Applying the principles and guidelines of screen design to ensure usability, accessibility, and aesthetics

## Principles and Guidelines of Screen Design

Screen design is guided by some general principles and specific guidelines that help to create effective and user-friendly UIs. Some of the common principles and guidelines are:

- **Consistency**: The screen design should be consistent across the application or the website, as well as with the platform and the domain conventions. Consistency helps to reduce the user's cognitive load, increase the user's confidence, and enhance the user's satisfaction.
- **Clarity**: The screen design should be clear and easy to understand, without any ambiguity or confusion. Clarity helps to convey the purpose and meaning of the screen, as well as the actions and feedback of the user. Clarity can be achieved by using simple and familiar words, icons, and symbols, avoiding unnecessary elements, and providing adequate contrast and spacing.
- **Alignment**: The screen design should be aligned along a grid or a baseline, to create a sense of order and harmony. Alignment helps to improve the readability and scannability of the screen, as well as the visual balance and stability. Alignment can be achieved by using margins, guides, and rulers, and by following the common patterns of left-to-right and top-to-bottom.
- **Proximity**: The screen design should group related elements together, and separate unrelated elements apart, to create a sense of connection and distinction. Proximity helps to show the relationship and hierarchy of the elements, as well as the structure and organization of the screen. Proximity can be achieved by using whitespace, borders, backgrounds, and colors.
- **Contrast**: The screen design should use different colors, shapes, sizes, and fonts, to create a sense of difference and emphasis. Contrast helps to highlight the important and relevant elements, as well as the variety and diversity of the screen. Contrast can be achieved by using complementary or analogous colors, bold or italic fonts, large or small icons, and solid or dashed lines.
- **Repetition**: The screen design should repeat some elements, such as colors, shapes, sizes, and fonts, to create a sense of unity and coherence. Repetition helps to reinforce the identity and style of the screen, as well as the familiarity and recognition of the elements. Repetition can be achieved by using a consistent color scheme, a common shape language, a uniform size scale, and a standard font family.
- **Balance**: The screen design should distribute the elements evenly, to create a sense of equilibrium and symmetry. Balance helps to avoid the feeling of heaviness or lightness, as well as the impression of instability or chaos. Balance can be achieved by using a central or an off-center axis, a symmetrical or an asymmetrical layout, and a positive or a negative space.
- **Hierarchy**: The screen design should arrange the elements according to their importance and relevance, to create a sense of order and priority. Hierarchy helps to guide the user's attention and focus, as well as the user's navigation and interaction. Hierarchy can be achieved by using different levels of headings, subheadings, and body text, different sizes and weights of fonts, different positions and orientations of elements, and different degrees of brightness and saturation of colors.
