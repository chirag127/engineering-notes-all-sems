### A brief history of screen design

- Screen design is the process of creating visual interfaces for digital devices, such as computers, smartphones, and tablets.
- Screen design has evolved over time, influenced by technological innovations, user needs, and aesthetic trends.
- Some of the major milestones in the history of screen design are:

  - The first screen displays were attached to computers in the 1950s, using cathode ray tubes (CRTs) that emitted beams of electrons onto phosphor-coated screens. These screens could only display monochrome text and simple graphics, and had low resolution and refresh rates.
  - In the 1970s, graphical user interfaces (GUIs) were introduced, allowing users to interact with computers using icons, menus, windows, and pointers, instead of typing commands. The first GUI was developed by Xerox PARC for the Alto computer, and later popularized by Apple Macintosh and Microsoft Windows. GUIs made computers more user-friendly and accessible, and enabled more complex and diverse screen designs.
  - In the 1980s and 1990s, color screens became more common, as well as higher resolution and faster refresh rates. Screen design also became more influenced by graphic design principles, such as typography, layout, color theory, and visual hierarchy. Screen designers experimented with different styles, such as skeuomorphism, minimalism, and flat design, to create more engaging and intuitive interfaces.
  - In the 2000s and 2010s, screen design was transformed by the emergence of mobile devices, such as smartphones and tablets, that had touch-sensitive screens and sensors. Screen design had to adapt to different screen sizes, orientations, and contexts, and to support gestures, animations, and transitions. Screen design also became more responsive, adaptive, and interactive, using data, feedback, and personalization to create more relevant and immersive experiences.
  - In the 2020s and beyond, screen design is expected to face new challenges and opportunities, such as incorporating voice, gesture, and facial recognition, as well as augmented and virtual reality, into the screen design process. Screen design will also have to consider ethical, social, and environmental issues, such as accessibility, inclusivity, privacy, and sustainability, and to balance human and artificial intelligence in the screen design process.
