### Selection of Device-Based and Screen-Based Controls

Device-based controls, often called input devices, are the mechanisms through which people communicate their desires to the system. Screen-based controls, often called widgets, are the components that appear on the graphical user interface and allow users to interact with the system. The selection of the proper device-based and screen-based controls depends on several factors, such as:

- The characteristics and capabilities of the device-based control, such as accuracy, speed, feedback, and ease of use.
- The characteristics and capabilities of the screen-based control, such as appearance, functionality, and usability.
- The nature and context of the task, such as the frequency, complexity, and importance of the interaction.
- The characteristics and preferences of the user, such as the level of expertise, the cognitive style, and the physical abilities.

Some general guidelines for selecting device-based and screen-based controls are:

- Choose the device-based control that matches the natural movement and input mode of the user, such as a mouse for pointing and clicking, a keyboard for typing, or a voice for speaking.
- Choose the screen-based control that matches the type and range of data or options that the user needs to enter or select, such as a text field for free-form text, a radio button for a binary choice, or a slider for a continuous value.
- Choose the device-based and screen-based control combination that minimizes the physical and mental effort of the user, such as avoiding unnecessary switches between devices or controls, providing clear and consistent feedback, and reducing errors and ambiguity.
- Choose the device-based and screen-based control combination that maximizes the user satisfaction and performance, such as providing aesthetic and engaging design, supporting user control and flexibility, and enhancing user learning and retention.

Some examples of device-based and screen-based controls are:

- Trackball: A device-based control that consists of a ball that can be rotated in any direction to move the cursor on the screen. It is similar to a mouse, but does not require a flat surface to operate. It can be used for tasks that require precise and fast cursor movement, such as drawing or gaming.
- Joystick: A device-based control that consists of a stick that can be tilted in different directions to control the movement of an object on the screen. It is often used for tasks that involve navigation or simulation, such as flying or driving.
- Graphic tablet: A device-based control that consists of a flat surface that can detect the position and pressure of a stylus or a finger. It can be used for tasks that require natural and expressive input, such as sketching or handwriting.
- Light pen: A device-based control that consists of a pen-shaped device that can emit or detect light. It can be used for tasks that require direct and accurate pointing or selection on the screen, such as editing or annotating.
- Touch screen: A device-based control that consists of a screen that can detect the touch of a finger or a stylus. It can be used for tasks that require intuitive and convenient input, such as tapping or swiping.
- Voice: A device-based control that consists of a microphone that can capture the speech of the user. It can be used for tasks that require hands-free or eyes-free input, such as dictating or commanding.

- Button: A screen-based control that consists of a square or rectangular-shaped component with a label inside that indicates the action to be accomplished. It can be used for tasks that require simple and discrete input, such as confirming or canceling.
- Text field: A screen-based control that consists of a rectangular-shaped component that allows the user to enter free-form text. It can be used for tasks that require textual input, such as searching or naming.
- Radio button: A screen-based control that consists of a circular-shaped component that allows the user to select one option from a predefined set of mutually exclusive options. It can be used for tasks that require binary or categorical input, such as choosing or filtering.
- Checkbox: A screen-based control that consists of a square-shaped component that allows the user to select one or more options from a predefined set of independent options. It can be used for tasks that require multiple or optional input, such as agreeing or selecting.
- Slider: A screen-based control that consists of a horizontal or vertical component that allows the user to adjust a continuous value within a predefined range. It can be used for tasks that require quantitative or relative input, such as setting or comparing.
- Spinner: A screen-based control that consists of a rectangular-shaped component that allows the user to increment or decrement a discrete value within a predefined range. It can be used for tasks that require numerical or ordinal input
