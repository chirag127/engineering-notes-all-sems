### Human interaction speeds

Human interaction speeds are the rates at which people can perform various tasks using different communication methods. They are important to consider in the design process of human-computer interfaces, as they affect the usability, efficiency and satisfaction of the users.

Some of the common human interaction speeds are:

- Reading: The average adult reading speed for English prose text in the United States is around 250 to 300 words per minute. This speed can be increased by using rapid serial visual presentation (RSVP), which displays words one at a time in the same location on the screen.
- Listening: People can comfortably hear words that are spoken at from 150 to 160 words per minute. However, the comprehension and retention of the information may vary depending on the content, context and speaker's accent.
- Speaking: People can speak at an average rate of 125 words per minute, with a range of 100 to 200 words per minute depending on the language, topic and emotion. Speaking is faster than typing or writing, but it may be less accurate and more prone to errors.
- Keying: People can type on a keyboard at an average speed of 40 words per minute, with a range of 10 to 120 words per minute depending on the skill, experience and device. Keying is slower than speaking, but it may be more precise and easier to edit.
- Handwriting: People can write by hand at an average speed of 31 words per minute, with a range of 10 to 50 words per minute depending on the style, legibility and paper. Handwriting is slower than keying, but it may be more personal and expressive.
- Reacting: People can react to a stimulus, such as a visual or auditory cue, in an average time of 200 to 250 milliseconds, with a range of 150 to 300 milliseconds depending on the complexity, intensity and expectation of the stimulus. Reaction time is influenced by factors such as age, fatigue, attention and distraction.
