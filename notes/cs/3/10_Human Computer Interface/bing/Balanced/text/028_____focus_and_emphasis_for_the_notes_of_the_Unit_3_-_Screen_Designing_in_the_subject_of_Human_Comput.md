### Focus and Emphasis for the Notes of the Unit 3 - Screen Designing in the Subject of Human Computer Interface

- Focus and emphasis are important principles of screen design that help to guide the user's attention and convey the intended message.
- Focus refers to the ability to draw the user's eye to a specific element or area of the screen, while emphasis refers to the degree of importance or prominence given to that element or area.
- Focus and emphasis can be achieved by using various techniques, such as contrast, alignment, proximity, repetition, hierarchy, color, size, shape, texture, animation, etc.
- Contrast is the difference between two or more elements on the screen, such as light and dark, warm and cool, large and small, etc. Contrast can create interest, variety, and clarity on the screen.
- Alignment is the arrangement of elements on the screen according to a common edge, axis, or center. Alignment can create order, harmony, and balance on the screen.
- Proximity is the closeness or distance between elements on the screen. Proximity can create grouping, separation, and association on the screen.
- Repetition is the use of the same or similar elements on the screen, such as color, shape, font, etc. Repetition can create consistency, unity, and recognition on the screen.
- Hierarchy is the arrangement of elements on the screen according to their relative importance or significance. Hierarchy can create structure, organization, and emphasis on the screen.
- Color is the hue, saturation, and brightness of an element on the screen. Color can create mood, emotion, and meaning on the screen.
- Size is the dimension or scale of an element on the screen. Size can create contrast, proportion, and emphasis on the screen.
- Shape is the form or outline of an element on the screen. Shape can create contrast, variety, and symbolism on the screen.
- Texture is the surface quality or appearance of an element on the screen. Texture can create contrast, depth, and realism on the screen.
- Animation is the movement or change of an element on the screen. Animation can create attention, interest, and interactivity on the screen.

- To create effective focus and emphasis on the screen, the designer should consider the following guidelines:
  - Use contrast to highlight the most important or relevant elements on the screen, and avoid using too many contrasting elements that may distract or confuse the user.
  - Use alignment to create a clear and logical layout on the screen, and avoid using misaligned or random elements that may disrupt or clutter the screen.
  - Use proximity to group related elements on the screen, and avoid using too much or too little space between elements that may affect the readability or usability of the screen.
  - Use repetition to create a consistent and coherent look and feel on the screen, and avoid using too many or too few repeated elements that may make the screen boring or chaotic.
  - Use hierarchy to organize the elements on the screen according to their importance or function, and avoid using too many or too few levels of hierarchy that may make the screen unclear or overwhelming.
  - Use color to create a suitable mood and message on the screen, and avoid using too many or too few colors that may make the screen dull or garish.
  - Use size to create contrast and proportion on the screen, and avoid using too large or too small elements that may make the screen unbalanced or illegible.
  - Use shape to create variety and symbolism on the screen, and avoid using too many or too few shapes that may make the screen monotonous or ambiguous.
  - Use texture to create depth and realism on the screen, and avoid using too much or too little texture that may make the screen flat or busy.
  - Use animation to create attention and interactivity on the screen, and avoid using too much or too little animation that may make the screen annoying or boring.
