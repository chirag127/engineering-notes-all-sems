### Presentation information simply and meaningfully

- The goal of screen design is to present information in a way that is easy to understand, attractive, and efficient for the user.
- Some principles of screen design are:
  - **Consistency**: Use the same fonts, colors, icons, layouts, and terminology throughout the interface to reduce confusion and cognitive load.
  - **Alignment**: Align elements on the screen to create a sense of order, balance, and harmony. Use grids, guides, and margins to help with alignment.
  - **Contrast**: Use contrast to highlight important elements, create visual hierarchy, and draw attention. Contrast can be achieved by using different sizes, shapes, colors, or fonts.
  - **Repetition**: Repeat elements or patterns to create unity, coherence, and familiarity. Repetition can also help with navigation and recognition.
  - **Proximity**: Group related elements together to show their relationship and meaning. Use white space to separate unrelated elements and create clarity.
  - **Simplicity**: Avoid unnecessary or redundant elements that may distract or confuse the user. Use clear and concise language, icons, and graphics to convey the message.
  - **Feedback**: Provide feedback to the user to indicate the status, progress, or outcome of their actions. Feedback can be visual, auditory, or tactile.
  - **Affordance**: Design elements that suggest their functionality and how they can be interacted with. For example, buttons should look clickable, sliders should look draggable, and links should look clickable.
  - **Accessibility**: Design for users with different abilities, preferences, and devices. Use appropriate colors, fonts, sizes, and contrasts to ensure readability and visibility. Provide alternative text, captions, or audio descriptions for images, videos, or sounds. Use keyboard shortcuts, touch gestures, or voice commands to support different input methods.
