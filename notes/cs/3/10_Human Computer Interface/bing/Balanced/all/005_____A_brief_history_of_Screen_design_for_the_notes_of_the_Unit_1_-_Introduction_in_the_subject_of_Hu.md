# A brief history of screen design

- Screen design is the process of creating visual interfaces for digital devices, such as computers, smartphones, and tablets.
- Screen design has evolved over time, influenced by technological developments, user needs, and aesthetic trends.
- Some of the major milestones in the history of screen design are:

  - The first screens were cathode ray tube (CRT) displays, which were attached to computers in the 1950s and 1960s. They could only display monochrome text and simple graphics, and had low resolution and contrast.
  - In the 1970s, screens became more interactive, allowing users to enter commands and data using keyboards and light pens. However, the screens were still cluttered with many fields, menus, and buttons, and lacked visual hierarchy and consistency.
  - In the 1980s, graphical user interfaces (GUIs) emerged, which used icons, windows, menus, and pointers to make screens more intuitive and user-friendly. The first GUI was developed by Xerox PARC, and later popularized by Apple Macintosh and Microsoft Windows.
  - In the 1990s, screens became more colorful, multimedia-rich, and dynamic, thanks to the advancement of display technologies, such as liquid crystal display (LCD), and the emergence of the World Wide Web, which enabled the creation and sharing of web pages.
  - In the 2000s, screens became more diverse, as different types of devices, such as mobile phones, PDAs, and game consoles, required different screen sizes, resolutions, and orientations. Screen design also became more responsive, adaptive, and personalized, to cater to the varying needs and preferences of users.
  - In the 2010s, screens became more immersive, interactive, and intelligent, as new technologies, such as touchscreens, gesture recognition, voice control, augmented reality, and artificial intelligence, enabled new ways of interacting with screens and the digital world.
  - In the 2020s, screens are expected to become more ubiquitous, seamless, and ambient, as they integrate with the physical environment and the human body, and provide context-aware and anticipatory services and experiences.
