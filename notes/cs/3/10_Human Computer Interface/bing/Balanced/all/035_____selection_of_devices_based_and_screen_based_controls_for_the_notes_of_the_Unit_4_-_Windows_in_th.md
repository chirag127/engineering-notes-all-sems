# Selection of Device-Based and Screen-Based Controls for Windows

Device-based controls, often called input devices, are the mechanisms through which people communicate their desires to the system. Screen-based controls, often called widgets, are the graphical elements that display information or allow interaction on the screen. Both types of controls are important for designing effective windows and user interfaces.

## Device-Based Controls

- Device-based controls can be classified into three categories: pointing devices, selection devices, and entry devices.
- Pointing devices are used to point at an object on the screen, such as a mouse, a trackball, a touchpad, a stylus, or a finger on a touchscreen.
- Selection devices are used to select the object or identify it as the focus of attention, such as a mouse button, a keyboard key, a voice command, or a gesture.
- Entry devices are used to enter data or commands into the system, such as a keyboard, a microphone, a scanner, a barcode reader, or a biometric device.
- The characteristics and capabilities of device-based controls depend on several factors, such as accuracy, speed, feedback, compatibility, consistency, and learnability.
- The selection of device-based controls should be based on the following criteria:
  - The nature of the task and the user's goals
  - The characteristics of the user, such as age, skill, preference, and physical ability
  - The characteristics of the environment, such as noise, lighting, space, and safety
  - The characteristics of the system, such as hardware, software, and network
  - The cost and availability of the device

## Screen-Based Controls

- Screen-based controls can be classified into four categories: text boxes, selection controls, command buttons, and composite controls.
- Text boxes are used to display or enter text, such as labels, captions, messages, or data fields.
- Selection controls are used to select one or more options from a set of choices, such as check boxes, radio buttons, list boxes, drop-down lists, or sliders.
- Command buttons are used to initiate an action or a command, such as OK, Cancel, Save, or Print.
- Composite controls are used to combine two or more controls into a single unit, such as a spin box, a date picker, a color picker, or a file chooser.
- The characteristics and capabilities of screen-based controls depend on several factors, such as visibility, clarity, alignment, grouping, spacing, and color.
- The selection of screen-based controls should be based on the following criteria:
  - The nature of the data and the user's input
  - The frequency and importance of the user's interaction
  - The consistency and compatibility with the system and the user's expectations
  - The efficiency and ease of use for the user
  - The aesthetics and appeal of the interface

## Windows

- Windows are the primary containers for displaying and organizing screen-based controls on the screen.
- Windows can be classified into three types: primary windows, secondary windows, and dialog boxes.
- Primary windows are the main windows that display the core functionality and content of the system, such as a word processor, a web browser, or a game.
- Secondary windows are the windows that support the primary windows, such as a toolbar, a menu, a status bar, or a palette.
- Dialog boxes are the windows that request or provide information or confirmation from or to the user, such as a message box, an alert box, a prompt box, or a property sheet.
- The characteristics and capabilities of windows depend on several factors, such as size, shape, position, title, border, scroll bar, and menu.
- The selection and design of windows should be based on the following criteria:
  - The nature and purpose of the window and its content
  - The relationship and hierarchy among the windows
  - The consistency and compatibility with the system and the user's expectations
  - The usability and accessibility for the user
  - The adaptability and responsiveness of the window to different devices and screen sizes
