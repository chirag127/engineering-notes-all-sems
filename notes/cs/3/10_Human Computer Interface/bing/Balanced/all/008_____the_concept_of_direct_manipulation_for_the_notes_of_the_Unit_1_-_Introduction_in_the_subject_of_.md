# The Concept of Direct Manipulation

- Direct manipulation is an approach to interfaces which involves continuous representation of objects of interest together with rapid, reversible, and incremental actions and feedback.
- Direct manipulation is a style of human-computer interaction (HCI) design which features a natural representation of task objects and actions promoting the notion of people performing a task themselves (directly) not through an intermediary like a computer.
- Direct manipulation is one of the central concepts of graphical user interfaces (GUIs) and is sometimes equated with "what you see is what you get" (WYSIWYG).
- Some examples of direct manipulation interfaces are:
  - Dragging and dropping files or icons on a desktop
  - Resizing windows or images by dragging their corners or edges
  - Rotating or zooming a map or a photo by pinching or spreading fingers on a touchscreen
  - Drawing or writing on a canvas or a document with a stylus or a finger
- Some benefits of direct manipulation interfaces are:
  - They reduce the cognitive load and the need for memorization by providing visual cues and feedback
  - They increase the sense of control and engagement by allowing users to directly manipulate the objects of interest
  - They enhance the learnability and usability by providing intuitive and consistent interactions
  - They support exploratory and creative tasks by allowing users to experiment and undo their actions
- Some challenges of direct manipulation interfaces are:
  - They may require more screen space and graphical resources than other interfaces
  - They may not support complex or abstract tasks that require more than simple actions or feedback
  - They may not be accessible or suitable for users with different abilities or preferences
  - They may not be compatible or consistent with other interfaces or platforms
