# Statistical Graphics

- Statistical graphics are graphics used in the field of statistics for data visualization.
- They allow the display of numerical or tabular data in some sort of pictorial form, such as bar graphs, pie charts, line graphs, scatterplots, and maps .
- They can be used for different purposes, such as:
  - Presenting data and the results of statistical analysis in a clear and concise way.
  - Assisting in the analysis of data by revealing patterns, trends, outliers, and relationships .
  - Facilitating statistical computation by providing visual aids or shortcuts.
- They can be classified into different types, such as:
  - Univariate graphs: show the distribution of a single variable, such as histograms, boxplots, and stem-and-leaf plots.
  - Bivariate graphs: show the relationship between two variables, such as scatterplots, line graphs, and bar graphs.
  - Multivariate graphs: show the relationship among three or more variables, such as contour plots, surface plots, and parallel coordinates plots.
  - Specialized graphs: show specific types of data or analysis, such as time series plots, frequency polygons, pie charts, and maps.
- They can be designed and evaluated according to some principles, such as:
  - Clarity: the graph should convey the intended message without ambiguity or confusion.
  - Simplicity: the graph should avoid unnecessary complexity or clutter.
  - Accuracy: the graph should represent the data faithfully and avoid distortion or bias.
  - Relevance: the graph should suit the purpose and context of the data and the analysis.
  - Aesthetics: the graph should appeal to the eye and the mind of the viewer.
