## Identifying the Requirements from Problem Statements

1. Analyze the problem statement thoroughly to understand the actual problem and identify the key points.
2. Identify the actors or stakeholders involved in the problem. Determine their roles and responsibilities.
3. Identify the inputs and outputs of the system. Also identify any constraints and assumptions.
4. Identify the key requirements and group them into functional and non-functional requirements.
5. Functional requirements specify the functions or capabilities the system must have, e.g. calculate total price, generate report, etc.
6. Non-functional requirements specify how the system must behave, e.g. performance, security, compatibility, reliability, etc.
7. Analyze the requirements to check for completeness, clarity, consistency, feasibility, and verifiability.
8. Get customer feedback or verification on the requirements to ensure they accurately capture the needs.
9. Review and update requirements periodically as the project progresses.

The above points cover the key steps to identify requirements from a problem statement for building a software system or product. Following a systematic approach to analyzing the problem and eliciting accurate requirements leads to the development of a useful and usable system.
