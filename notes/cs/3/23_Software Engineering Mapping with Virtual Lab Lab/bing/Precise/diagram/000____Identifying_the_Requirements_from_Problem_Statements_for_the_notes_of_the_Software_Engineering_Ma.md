## Identifying the Requirements from Problem Statements

In the context of software engineering, identifying the requirements from problem statements is a crucial step in the development process. This involves analyzing the problem statement to determine the needs and expectations of the stakeholders, and translating these into specific, measurable, and testable requirements for the software system.

Here are some steps that can be followed to identify the requirements from problem statements:

1. **Understand the problem statement**: Read the problem statement carefully and make sure you understand the context, goals, and constraints of the project.

2. **Identify the stakeholders**: Determine who will be affected by the software system and what their needs and expectations are.

3. **Extract the requirements**: Identify the functional and non-functional requirements from the problem statement. Functional requirements describe what the system should do, while non-functional requirements describe how the system should behave.

4. **Organize the requirements**: Group related requirements together and organize them in a logical manner.

5. **Validate the requirements**: Ensure that the requirements are complete, consistent, and testable. This can be done by reviewing the requirements with the stakeholders and getting their feedback.

By following these steps, you can effectively identify the requirements from problem statements and ensure that the software system meets the needs and expectations of the stakeholders. This is an important step in the software engineering process and can help ensure the success of the project.
