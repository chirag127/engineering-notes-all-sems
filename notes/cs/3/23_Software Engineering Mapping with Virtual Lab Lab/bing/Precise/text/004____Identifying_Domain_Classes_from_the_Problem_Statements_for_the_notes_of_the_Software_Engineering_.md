## Identifying Domain Classes from the Problem Statements for the notes of the Software Engineering Mapping with Virtual Lab Lab in the subject of Software Engineering

1. Domain classes are the fundamental building blocks of object-oriented software systems.
2. They represent the real-world entities or concepts that are relevant to the problem domain.
3. To identify domain classes from a problem statement, one must analyze the problem statement and extract the nouns and noun phrases that represent real-world entities or concepts.
4. These nouns and noun phrases can then be used as a starting point for identifying potential domain classes.
5. It is important to note that not all nouns and noun phrases in a problem statement will necessarily represent domain classes.
6. Some may represent attributes or relationships between domain classes, while others may not be relevant to the problem domain at all.
7. Once potential domain classes have been identified, they should be evaluated to determine if they are indeed relevant to the problem domain and if they represent distinct concepts.
8. This process may involve refining or combining potential domain classes, or even discarding some that are not relevant.
9. The resulting set of domain classes will form the foundation of the object-oriented software system being developed.
