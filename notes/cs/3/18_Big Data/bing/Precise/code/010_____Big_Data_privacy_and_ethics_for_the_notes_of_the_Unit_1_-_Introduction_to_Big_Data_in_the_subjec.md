### Unit 1 - Introduction to Big Data: Big Data Privacy and Ethics

1. **Big Data Ethics**: Big Data is producing increased institutional awareness and power that requires the development of Big Data ethics to protect individual rights.
2. **Potential for Abuses**: There is potential for abuses of informational reuse and unauthorized data that could result in privacy problems.
3. **Privacy**: The ethics of privacy involve many different concepts such as liberty, autonomy, security, and in a more modern sense, data protection and data exposure.
4. **Three Categories of Big Data Privacy**: The condition of privacy can be understood by breaking it down into three categories.
5. **Five Main Areas of Concern**: There are five main areas of concern in big data ethics that outline the potential for immoral use of data: Informed Consent.
6. **Informed Consent**: To consent means that you give uncoerced permission for something to happen to you.
7. **Data Ethics**: Data ethics requires leaders to reckon with the ongoing rise in global inequality and the increasing concentration of wealth and value both in geographical tech hubs and among AI-enabled organizations.
8. **Embedding Data Principles**: Data principles should be embedded in operations.
9. **Big Data Privacy and Ethical Challenges**: Big data is a complex phenomenon of technical advances in storage capacity, computational speed, and the low cost of data acquisition.
10. **Privacy and Big Data Management**: The advancement of technology that led to the possibility of big data occurred over a short period of time, leading to challenges in privacy and big data management.
11. **Ethical Challenges**: There are several ethical challenges associated with big data privacy.
