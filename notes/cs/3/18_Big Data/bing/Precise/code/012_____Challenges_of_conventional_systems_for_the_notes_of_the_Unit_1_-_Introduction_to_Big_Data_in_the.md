### Challenges of Conventional Systems

1. **Scalability**: Conventional systems are not designed to handle the large volumes of data that are generated by modern applications. As the volume of data grows, the performance of these systems degrades, making it difficult to process and analyze the data in a timely manner.

2. **Data Variety**: Conventional systems are designed to handle structured data, which is organized in a predefined schema. However, modern applications generate a wide variety of data, including unstructured and semi-structured data, which do not fit into a predefined schema. This makes it difficult to store, process, and analyze the data using conventional systems.

3. **Data Velocity**: Conventional systems are not designed to handle the high velocity at which data is generated by modern applications. This makes it difficult to process and analyze the data in real-time, which is often necessary for applications such as fraud detection and real-time analytics.

4. **Data Complexity**: Conventional systems are not designed to handle the complex relationships that exist between data elements in modern applications. This makes it difficult to perform complex analyses and derive insights from the data.

5. **Cost**: Conventional systems can be expensive to scale and maintain, particularly as the volume of data grows. This can make it difficult for organizations to justify the cost of storing and processing large volumes of data.

6. **Security**: Conventional systems may not provide the necessary security measures to protect sensitive data. This can make it difficult for organizations to comply with data protection regulations and to protect their data from unauthorized access.

7. **Data Integration**: Conventional systems may not provide the necessary tools and capabilities to integrate data from multiple sources. This can make it difficult for organizations to derive insights from their data and to make data-driven decisions.

These are some of the challenges that conventional systems face when dealing with big data. In order to overcome these challenges, new approaches and technologies are needed, which are specifically designed to handle the volume, variety, velocity, complexity, and cost of big data.
