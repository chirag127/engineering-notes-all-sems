### Job Scheduling in MapReduce

- Job scheduling is the process of assigning tasks to workers in a distributed system, such as Hadoop, to process large-scale data in parallel.
- Job scheduling aims to optimize the performance, efficiency, and resource utilization of the system, by considering factors such as data locality, task dependencies, load balancing, and fault tolerance.
- MapReduce is a programming model and framework for processing large-scale data using a cluster of machines. It consists of two types of tasks: map tasks and reduce tasks.
- Map tasks read and process input data splits, and produce intermediate key-value pairs. Reduce tasks aggregate and summarize the intermediate key-value pairs by the same key, and produce the final output.
- A MapReduce job is a unit of work that consists of a set of map tasks and a set of reduce tasks. A job can be submitted by a user or an application to a job queue, where it waits to be scheduled by the master node.
- The master node is responsible for distributing and monitoring the tasks to the worker nodes, and collecting the results. The worker nodes are responsible for executing the tasks and reporting the progress and status to the master node.
- There are different types of job schedulers in MapReduce, such as FIFO, Fair, Capacity, and Delay. Each scheduler has its own advantages and disadvantages, and can be configured and customized according to the needs and preferences of the users and applications.
- FIFO scheduler is the simplest and default scheduler in MapReduce. It schedules the jobs in the order of their submission to the queue, and assigns tasks to the workers on a first-come, first-served basis. It is easy to implement and understand, but it can cause starvation and poor resource utilization for long-running or high-priority jobs.
- Fair scheduler is a scheduler that aims to provide fair and equal share of resources to the jobs in the queue, regardless of their submission order or size. It divides the available slots among the jobs, and assigns tasks to the workers based on their data locality and availability. It can also support multiple queues, priorities, and preemption for different users and applications. It is more flexible and efficient than FIFO scheduler, but it can be more complex and difficult to configure and tune.
- Capacity scheduler is a scheduler that aims to maximize the throughput and utilization of the cluster, by allocating resources to the jobs based on their capacity requirements and guarantees. It supports multiple queues, each with its own capacity, limit, and priority. It assigns tasks to the workers based on their data locality and availability, and can also support preemption and elasticity for different queues. It is suitable for large and heterogeneous clusters, but it can be more complex and difficult to configure and tune.
- Delay scheduler is a scheduler that aims to improve the data locality of the tasks, by delaying the assignment of tasks to the workers until a local slot is available. It can reduce the network traffic and improve the performance of the jobs, but it can also increase the waiting time and latency of the tasks. It can be used as a complement to other schedulers, such as FIFO or Fair.
