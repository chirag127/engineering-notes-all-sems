### Challenges of conventional systems for big data

Big data is the term used to describe large and complex data sets that are difficult to process and analyze using traditional methods and tools. Big data can be structured, unstructured, or semi-structured, and can come from various sources and formats, such as social media, sensors, web logs, images, videos, etc. Big data has the potential to provide valuable insights and opportunities for businesses, governments, and society, but it also poses many challenges and issues for conventional systems.

Some of the challenges of conventional systems for big data are:

- **Scalability**: Conventional systems are not designed to handle the massive volume and variety of big data, which can exceed the storage and processing capacity of traditional databases and servers. Conventional systems may require costly hardware upgrades, such as massively parallel processing (MPP) systems, to scale up to the big data demands, but this may not be feasible or efficient for all scenarios.
- **Performance**: Conventional systems are not optimized to process and analyze big data at high velocity and veracity, which means they may not be able to deliver timely and accurate results. Conventional systems are often batch-oriented, which means they need to wait for the data to be collected, extracted, transformed, and loaded (ETL) before performing any analysis. This can cause delays and inefficiencies in obtaining the required insights and actions from big data.
- **Flexibility**: Conventional systems are not adaptable to the dynamic and diverse nature of big data, which can change rapidly and unpredictably. Conventional systems are often based on the relational data model, which requires a predefined schema and structure for the data. This can limit the ability of conventional systems to work with unstructured or semi-structured data, such as text, images, videos, etc., which may contain valuable information that is not captured by the relational model.
- **Integration**: Conventional systems are not capable of integrating and harmonizing big data from different sources and formats, which can result in data silos and inconsistencies. Conventional systems may lack the standards and protocols to communicate and exchange data across different platforms and systems, which can hinder the interoperability and compatibility of big data. Conventional systems may also face challenges in ensuring the quality, security, and privacy of big data, which can affect the reliability and trustworthiness of the data and the analysis.
