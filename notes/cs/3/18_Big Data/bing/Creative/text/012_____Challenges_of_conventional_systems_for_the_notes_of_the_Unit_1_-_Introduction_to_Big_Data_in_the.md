### Challenges of conventional systems

Conventional systems are the traditional data processing and analysis systems that are based on the relational data model and use structured query language (SQL) for data manipulation. They are designed to handle structured data that can fit into predefined tables and schemas. However, they face several challenges when dealing with big data, which is characterized by high volume, variety, velocity, and veracity. Some of the challenges are:

- **Scalability**: Conventional systems are not scalable enough to handle the massive amount of data generated by various sources, such as social media, sensors, web logs, etc. They require expensive hardware upgrades and complex data partitioning techniques to increase their storage and processing capacity. Big data, on the other hand, requires horizontal scalability, which means adding more nodes or servers to the system without affecting its performance or functionality.
- **Flexibility**: Conventional systems are not flexible enough to handle the diverse and dynamic nature of big data, which can be structured, semi-structured, or unstructured. They impose rigid schemas and data types on the data, which can limit the scope and quality of data analysis. Big data, on the other hand, requires schema-on-read, which means applying the appropriate schema and data type at the time of data analysis, rather than at the time of data ingestion.
- **Latency**: Conventional systems are not fast enough to handle the high velocity and real-time nature of big data, which can be generated and consumed at a rapid rate. They are batch-oriented and rely on periodic data loading and processing, which can introduce delays and inefficiencies in data analysis. Big data, on the other hand, requires stream processing, which means processing the data as it arrives, without storing it in intermediate stages.
- **Quality**: Conventional systems are not robust enough to handle the low quality and uncertainty of big data, which can be noisy, incomplete, inconsistent, or inaccurate. They assume that the data is clean and reliable, and do not have effective mechanisms to deal with data errors and anomalies. Big data, on the other hand, requires data cleansing, integration, and validation, which means applying various techniques to improve the quality and consistency of the data before or during data analysis.
