### History of Big Data Innovation

- Big Data is a term that refers to a large set of data that is almost impossible to manage and process using traditional business intelligence tools.
- The term Big Data was coined by Roger Mougalas from OReilly Media in 2005, only a year after they created the term Web 2.0.
- However, the concept of Big Data has a long history that dates back to the early days of computing and information science.
- Some of the milestones in the history of Big Data are:

  - In 1880, the US Census Bureau faced a problem of handling and processing the data collected during the 1880 census. They estimated it would take eight years to complete the task, and predicted the data from the 1890 census would take more than 10 years to process.
  - In 1890, Herman Hollerith invented the tabulating machine, which used punched cards to store and process data. This machine reduced the time required to process the 1890 census data from 10 years to three months.
  - In 1944, John von Neumann proposed the concept of a stored-program computer, which could store both data and instructions in its memory. This paved the way for the development of modern computers and programming languages.
  - In 1965, the US Government planned the world’s first data center to store 742 million tax returns and 175 million sets of fingerprints on magnetic tape.
  - In 1970, Edgar F Codd presented his framework for a “relational database”, which allowed data to be organized in tables and accessed using a query language. This became the standard model for data management and analysis for decades.
  - In 1976, Bill Inmon defined the concept of a data warehouse, which is a centralized repository of data from multiple sources that supports decision making and business intelligence.
  - In 1989, Tim Berners-Lee invented the World Wide Web, which enabled the sharing and linking of information across the internet. This led to an exponential growth of data available online, as well as new ways of accessing and analyzing it.
  - In 1995, John Mashey, a computer scientist at Silicon Graphics, popularized the phrase “Big Data” in his presentation “Big Data and the Next Wave of InfraStress”.
  - In 1997, Michael Cox and David Ellsworth coined the term “data-intensive computing” to describe the challenges and opportunities of analyzing large and complex data sets using high-performance computing.
  - In 2001, Doug Laney, an analyst at Gartner, defined the three V’s of Big Data: volume, velocity, and variety. He argued that these characteristics pose new challenges and opportunities for data management and analysis.
  - In 2003, Google published a paper on its distributed file system, which enabled the storage and processing of massive amounts of data across thousands of servers.
  - In 2004, Google published another paper on its MapReduce framework, which simplified the parallel processing of large data sets using a functional programming model.
  - In 2006, Amazon launched its Elastic Compute Cloud (EC2) and Simple Storage Service (S3), which provided on-demand computing and storage resources over the internet. This enabled the emergence of cloud computing and big data as a service.
  - In 2008, Yahoo released Hadoop, an open-source implementation of Google’s distributed file system and MapReduce framework. Hadoop became the de facto standard for big data processing and analysis in the industry and academia.
  - In 2010, IBM created Watson, a cognitive computing system that can understand natural language and answer complex questions. Watson demonstrated its capabilities by winning the Jeopardy! game show against human champions.
  - In 2012, the Obama administration announced the Big Data Research and Development Initiative, which allocated $200 million to support big data projects in various domains, such as health, energy, education, and defense.
  - In 2014, Facebook revealed that it stores and processes over 500 terabytes of data every day, including 2.5 billion pieces of content, 2.7 billion likes, and 300 million photos.
  - In 2015, the European Union launched the Horizon 2020 program, which allocated €2.5 billion to fund big data
