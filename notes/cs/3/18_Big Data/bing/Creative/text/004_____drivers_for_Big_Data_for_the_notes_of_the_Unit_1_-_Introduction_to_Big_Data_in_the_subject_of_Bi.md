### Drivers for Big Data

Big data is the term used to describe the large and complex datasets that are generated from various sources and require advanced techniques and technologies to store, process, and analyze. Big data has become a valuable asset for many organizations and industries that want to gain insights, improve decision making, and create new products and services. However, big data also poses many challenges and opportunities that require understanding the drivers and motivations behind its adoption and use.

Some of the main drivers for big data are:

- **The digitization of society**: As more and more aspects of human life and activity are captured and recorded by digital devices and platforms, the amount and variety of data that is generated increases exponentially. Examples of such data sources include online transactions, social media posts, web browsing, mobile applications, sensors, cameras, GPS, etc. This data can provide rich and detailed information about human behavior, preferences, needs, and interactions, as well as the state and dynamics of the physical and natural world.
- **The drop in technology costs**: The advances in hardware and software technologies have made it possible and affordable to store, process, and analyze big data. The cost of data storage has decreased significantly over the years, making it feasible to store large volumes of data in different formats and locations. The cost of data processing has also decreased due to the development of parallel and distributed computing systems, such as cloud computing, that can handle big data workloads efficiently and scalably. The cost of data analysis has also decreased due to the availability of open source tools and frameworks, such as Hadoop, Spark, R, Python, etc., that can perform various data mining, machine learning, and visualization tasks on big data.
- **Connectivity through cloud computing**: Cloud computing is a model of delivering computing resources and services over the internet, on demand, and as a service. Cloud computing enables users to access and use big data without having to invest in and maintain their own infrastructure and software. Cloud computing also provides scalability, elasticity, and reliability for big data applications, as well as security and privacy features. Cloud computing also facilitates the integration and sharing of big data across different sources and platforms, as well as the collaboration and communication among different users and stakeholders.
- **Increased knowledge about data science**: Data science is the interdisciplinary field that combines mathematics, statistics, computer science, and domain knowledge to extract meaningful insights from big data. Data science has emerged as a key skill and profession in the era of big data, as more and more organizations and industries require data scientists to analyze and interpret their big data and provide actionable recommendations and solutions. Data science also involves the development and application of new methods and techniques for big data, such as artificial intelligence, machine learning, deep learning, natural language processing, computer vision, etc.
- **Social media applications**: Social media is the collective term for the online platforms and applications that enable users to create, share, and consume content and information, as well as to interact and communicate with each other. Social media is one of the main sources and consumers of big data, as it generates and collects massive amounts of data from millions of users every day. Examples of such data include text, images, videos, audio, likes, comments, ratings, reviews, etc. Social media data can provide valuable insights into the opinions, sentiments, emotions, trends, and patterns of users and communities, as well as their influence and impact on various topics and domains.
- **The rise of Internet-of-Things (IoT)**: Internet-of-Things (IoT) is the network of physical objects and devices that are embedded with sensors, actuators, and communication capabilities that enable them to collect, exchange, and act on data. IoT is one of the main drivers and enablers of big data, as it generates and consumes huge amounts of data from various sources and domains, such as smart homes, smart cities, smart health, smart agriculture, smart transportation, smart energy, etc. IoT data can provide real-time and contextual information about the status and performance of the physical and natural world, as well as the opportunities and challenges for improving efficiency, quality, and sustainability.
