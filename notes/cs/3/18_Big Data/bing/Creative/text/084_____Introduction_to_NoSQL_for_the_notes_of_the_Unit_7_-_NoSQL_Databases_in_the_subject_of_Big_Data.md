### Introduction to NoSQL

- NoSQL stands for **Not only SQL** or **Non-relational** .
- NoSQL is a type of **database management system (DBMS)** that is designed to handle and store large volumes of **unstructured and semi-structured data** .
- NoSQL databases use flexible data models that can adapt to changes in data structures and are capable of performing **lightning fast queries** .
- NoSQL databases have their roots in the **open source community** and have become more popular due to the demands of **Big Data**.
- NoSQL databases come in a variety of types including **document databases, key-values databases, wide-column stores, and graph databases**.
- NoSQL databases provide a variety of benefits including:
  - **Flexible data models**: NoSQL databases do not require a pre-defined schema to store data, unlike relational databases. This allows for more flexibility and agility in handling complex and dynamic data.
  - **Horizontal scaling**: NoSQL databases can scale out across multiple servers or nodes, rather than scaling up by adding more resources to a single server. This enables NoSQL databases to handle large volumes of data and high throughput with lower costs and higher availability.
  - **Lightning fast queries**: NoSQL databases can perform queries on data without the need for complex joins or transactions, as they store data in a way that is optimized for the query patterns. This results in faster and more efficient queries, especially for real-time applications.
  - **Ease of use for developers**: NoSQL databases provide simple and intuitive APIs and tools for developers to interact with data, without the need for complex SQL queries or ORM layers. NoSQL databases also support various programming languages and frameworks, making them more accessible and convenient for developers.
