# Analysis vs Reporting

- Analysis and reporting are two different processes that involve working with data, but they have different purposes and outcomes.
- Analysis involves interpreting data to answer why something is happening, what are the causes and effects, and what are the possible solutions or recommendations.
- Reporting involves presenting data in an organized and summarized way to show what is happening, what are the trends and patterns, and what are the facts and figures.
- Analysis requires more skills and knowledge than reporting, such as statistical techniques, analytical models, and business understanding.
- Reporting requires more tools and methods than analysis, such as data visualization, dashboards, and reports.
- Analysis is more subjective and exploratory than reporting, as it involves drawing inferences and conclusions from data, and testing hypotheses and assumptions.
- Reporting is more objective and descriptive than analysis, as it involves displaying data in a clear and accurate manner, and providing context and explanations.
- Analysis is more time-consuming and complex than reporting, as it involves cleaning, transforming, and modeling data, and finding insights and recommendations.
- Reporting is more time-efficient and simple than reporting, as it involves organizing, summarizing, and visualizing data, and communicating results and findings.
