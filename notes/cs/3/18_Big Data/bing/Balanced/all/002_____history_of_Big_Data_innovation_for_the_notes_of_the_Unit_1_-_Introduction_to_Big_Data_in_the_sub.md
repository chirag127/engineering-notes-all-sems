# History of Big Data Innovation

- Big Data is a term that refers to the collection, storage, analysis, and use of large and complex datasets that exceed the capabilities of traditional data processing systems.
- The history of Big Data can be traced back to the origins of data processing and analysis, as well as the development of technologies that enabled the creation and management of massive amounts of information.
- Some of the key milestones in the history of Big Data are:

  - **Before 1980**: Companies used mainframe computers to store and analyse data. Two key technologies were critical to the first formations of data centers as we think of them today: the relational database model, proposed by IBM mathematician Edgar F. Codd in 1970, and the Structured Query Language (SQL), developed in the 1970s by IBM researchers.
  - **1980s-1990s**: The emergence of the Internet and the World Wide Web increased the volume and variety of data available to businesses and individuals. Data mining, data warehousing, and online analytical processing (OLAP) became popular techniques for extracting insights from large datasets. The term "Big Data" was first used in a 1997 paper by NASA researchers Michael Cox and David Ellsworth, who described the challenges of processing large amounts of data for scientific visualization.
  - **2000s**: The rise of social media, e-commerce, cloud computing, and mobile devices generated an explosion of data from different sources and formats, such as text, images, videos, audio, geolocation, and sensor data. The traditional data processing systems could not handle the volume, velocity, and variety of Big Data, leading to the development of new technologies and frameworks, such as Hadoop, MapReduce, NoSQL, and Spark, that enabled distributed, parallel, and scalable data processing and analysis.
  - **2010s-present**: The advancement of artificial intelligence, machine learning, and deep learning enabled new ways of extracting value from Big Data, such as natural language processing, computer vision, speech recognition, sentiment analysis, recommendation systems, and anomaly detection. Big Data also became a key driver of innovation and transformation in various domains and industries, such as healthcare, education, finance, manufacturing, retail, and government. The challenges and opportunities of Big Data continue to evolve, as new sources and types of data emerge, such as the Internet of Things (IoT), blockchain, and quantum computing.
