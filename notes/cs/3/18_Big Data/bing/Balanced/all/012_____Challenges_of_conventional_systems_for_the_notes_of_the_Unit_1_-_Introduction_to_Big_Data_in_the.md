# Challenges of Conventional Systems

- Conventional systems are the traditional data processing and analytics systems that are based on the relational data model and use structured query language (SQL) for data manipulation.
- Conventional systems face several challenges when dealing with big data, which is the storage and analysis of large and complex data sets that can be structured, unstructured, or semi-structured.
- Some of the challenges of conventional systems for big data are:

  - **Scalability**: Conventional systems are not designed to handle the volume, variety, and velocity of big data. They rely on expensive and limited hardware resources, such as massively parallel processing (MPP) systems, to achieve parallelism and performance. However, these systems are not scalable enough to meet the growing demands of big data applications. Big data requires distributed and elastic systems that can scale up and down as needed, using commodity hardware and cloud computing.
  - **Flexibility**: Conventional systems are built on the assumption that data is well-defined, consistent, and static. They use predefined schemas and data models to enforce data quality and integrity. However, big data is often dynamic, heterogeneous, and noisy. It can change rapidly and frequently, and it can come from various sources and formats. Big data requires flexible and adaptive systems that can handle schema-less and semi-structured data, and that can evolve with the data characteristics and requirements.
  - **Timeliness**: Conventional systems are batch-oriented and rely on extract, transform, and load (ETL) processes to move and process data from different sources. These processes can take hours or days to complete, depending on the size and complexity of the data. However, big data is often real-time or near-real-time, and it requires fast and continuous processing and analysis. Big data requires stream-oriented and in-memory systems that can ingest and process data as it arrives, and that can provide timely and actionable insights.
  - **Complexity**: Conventional systems are based on simple and linear data processing and analytics techniques, such as aggregation, filtering, and sorting. They use SQL as the main query language, which is expressive and standardized, but also limited and rigid. However, big data is often complex and nonlinear, and it requires advanced and diverse data processing and analytics techniques, such as machine learning, natural language processing, and graph analysis. Big data requires complex and heterogeneous systems that can support multiple query languages and paradigms, and that can integrate and leverage various tools and frameworks.
