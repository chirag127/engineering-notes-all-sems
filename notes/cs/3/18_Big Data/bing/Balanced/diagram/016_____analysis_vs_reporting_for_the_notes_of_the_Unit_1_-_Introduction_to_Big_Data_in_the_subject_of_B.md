### Analysis vs Reporting

- Reporting is the process of presenting data in a structured and summarized way, usually in the form of tables, charts, or dashboards.
- Analysis is the process of exploring, interpreting, and deriving insights from data, usually with the help of statistical or analytical tools.
- Reporting and analysis are both important aspects of big data, but they have different purposes and outcomes.
- Reporting helps to monitor the performance and status of a business or a process, by providing factual and timely information.
- Analysis helps to understand the causes and effects of a business or a process, by providing deeper and actionable insights.
- Reporting answers questions such as what, when, where, and how much.
- Analysis answers questions such as why, how, and what if.
- Reporting is more descriptive and retrospective, while analysis is more diagnostic and predictive.
- Reporting is more standardized and automated, while analysis is more customized and interactive.
- Reporting is more suitable for operational and tactical decisions, while analysis is more suitable for strategic and innovative decisions.
