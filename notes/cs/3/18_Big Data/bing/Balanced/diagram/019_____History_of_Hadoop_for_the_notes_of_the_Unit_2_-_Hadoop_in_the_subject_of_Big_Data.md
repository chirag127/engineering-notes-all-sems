### History of Hadoop

- Hadoop is an open-source software framework for storing and processing big data in a distributed manner on large clusters of commodity hardware.
- Hadoop was started by Doug Cutting and Mike Cafarella in 2002 when they began working on the Apache Nutch project, which aimed to build a search engine system that could index 1 billion pages.
- The inspiration for Hadoop came from two papers published by Google in 2003 and 2004, describing the Google File System (GFS) and the MapReduce programming model, respectively.
- Cutting, who was working at Yahoo at the time, realized that the existing distributed computing solutions were inadequate for the scale and complexity of web data, and decided to implement the GFS and MapReduce concepts in Java as part of the Nutch project.
- In 2006, Cutting joined Yahoo and moved the Nutch project to the Apache Software Foundation, where it was renamed Hadoop, after his son's toy elephant .
- In 2008, Hadoop broke the world record for sorting 1 terabyte of data in 209 seconds, beating the previous record held by a supercomputer.
- Since then, Hadoop has evolved into a large and diverse ecosystem of subprojects and tools, such as Hadoop YARN, Hadoop MapReduce, Hadoop Ozone, Hadoop HDFS, Hadoop Hive, Hadoop Pig, Hadoop Spark, and many more .
- Hadoop has become the de facto standard for big data processing, and is used by many prominent companies and organizations, such as Facebook, Twitter, Netflix, Amazon, IBM, NASA, and the US government .
