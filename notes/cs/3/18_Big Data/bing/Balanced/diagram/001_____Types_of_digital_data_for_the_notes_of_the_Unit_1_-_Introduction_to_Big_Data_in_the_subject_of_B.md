### Types of digital data

Digital data is any information that can be stored, processed, or transmitted in a binary form, which consists of sequences of bits (0 or 1). Digital data can be contrasted with analog data, which is represented by a value from a continuous range of real numbers.

There are three main types of digital data, based on the level of structure and organization they have:

- **Unstructured data** is data that has no predefined format or schema, and is usually in the form of text, images, audio, video, or other multimedia. Unstructured data is difficult to analyze and extract information from, as it requires natural language processing, computer vision, or other techniques to understand its meaning and context. Examples of unstructured data include emails, social media posts, web pages, blogs, news articles, etc. According to Merrill Lynch, 80–90% of business data is either unstructured or semi-structured.
- **Semi-structured data** is data that has some level of organization and structure, but not in a fixed or rigid format. Semi-structured data often contains tags, labels, or metadata that describe the content or attributes of the data, but not in a consistent or standardized way. Examples of semi-structured data include XML, JSON, HTML, CSV, and other file formats that can be parsed and processed by software, but may vary in their syntax and semantics.
- **Structured data** is data that has a well-defined format and schema, and is usually stored in a relational database or a spreadsheet. Structured data is easy to query, analyze, and manipulate, as it follows a set of rules and conventions that are understood by software and humans. Examples of structured data include tables, records, fields, columns, rows, etc.
