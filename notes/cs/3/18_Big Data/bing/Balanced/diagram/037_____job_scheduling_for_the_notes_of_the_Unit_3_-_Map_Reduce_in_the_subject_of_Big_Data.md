### Job Scheduling for Map Reduce

- Job scheduling is the process of assigning and executing tasks in a distributed system to process large-scale data efficiently and effectively.
- Map Reduce is a programming model and framework for processing and analyzing big data in parallel using multiple nodes (machines) in a cluster.
- A Map Reduce job consists of two phases: map and reduce. The map phase applies a user-defined function to each input split (a chunk of the input data) and produces intermediate key-value pairs. The reduce phase aggregates the intermediate values associated with the same key and produces the final output.
- Job scheduling for Map Reduce involves two main challenges: resource allocation and task assignment. Resource allocation is the process of deciding how many nodes and how much CPU, memory, disk, and network resources to allocate for each job. Task assignment is the process of deciding which node will execute which map or reduce task.
- Different job scheduling algorithms have different objectives and trade-offs, such as fairness, throughput, response time, resource utilization, data locality, load balancing, fault tolerance, etc. Some of the common types of job schedulers for Map Reduce are:

  - FIFO: First-In-First-Out scheduler assigns jobs in the order of their arrival and gives each job equal priority and resources. It is simple and easy to implement, but it may cause starvation and poor performance for large or complex jobs.
  - Fair: Fair scheduler assigns jobs based on their resource demands and tries to ensure that each job gets a fair share of the cluster resources over time. It is more flexible and adaptive than FIFO, but it may incur high overhead and complexity for managing and adjusting the resource shares.
  - Capacity: Capacity scheduler assigns jobs based on their resource requirements and tries to maximize the cluster utilization and throughput. It divides the cluster into multiple queues with different capacities and priorities, and allocates resources to each queue according to its demand and availability. It is more scalable and efficient than Fair, but it may cause resource fragmentation and waste.
  - Deadline: Deadline scheduler assigns jobs based on their deadlines and tries to minimize the number of missed deadlines and the total tardiness. It estimates the execution time of each job and schedules the jobs in the order of their urgency and importance. It is more responsive and reliable than Capacity, but it may require accurate and timely information about the job characteristics and the cluster status.
