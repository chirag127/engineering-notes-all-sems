### History of Big Data Innovation

- Big Data is a term that refers to the collection, processing, and analysis of large and complex datasets that exceed the capabilities of traditional data management systems.
- The history of Big Data can be traced back to the origins of data storage and analysis, as well as the development of new technologies and applications that enabled the creation and use of Big Data.
- Some of the key milestones in the history of Big Data are:

  - **Before 1980**: Companies used mainframe computers to store and analyse data. Two key technologies were critical to the first formations of data centers as we think of them today: relational databases and magnetic tapes. Relational databases, proposed by IBM mathematician Edgar F Codd in 1970, allowed data to be organized in tables and accessed by queries. Magnetic tapes, used by the US Government to store tax returns and fingerprints since 1965, provided a cheap and durable medium for data storage.  
  - **1980s-1990s**: The emergence of the Internet and the World Wide Web increased the volume and variety of data available online. The first web server was created by Tim Berners-Lee in 1990, and the first web browser, Mosaic, was launched in 1993. The web enabled the sharing and dissemination of information across the globe, as well as the generation of new types of data, such as web logs, clickstreams, and social media posts. The first search engines, such as AltaVista and Google, were developed to help users find and access relevant information on the web. The first data warehouses, such as Teradata, were built to store and analyse large amounts of structured and semi-structured data from multiple sources. 
  - **2000s-2010s**: The explosion of data from various sources, such as mobile devices, sensors, social media, e-commerce, and cloud computing, created new challenges and opportunities for data management and analysis. The term Big Data was popularized by industry analysts and researchers to describe the phenomenon of data that is too big, too fast, or too diverse for conventional tools and techniques. The first frameworks and platforms for Big Data processing, such as Hadoop, MapReduce, and Spark, were developed to enable distributed, parallel, and scalable computation on large clusters of commodity hardware. The first applications and services that leveraged Big Data, such as recommendation systems, sentiment analysis, fraud detection, and machine learning, were deployed by companies such as Amazon, Netflix, Facebook, and Google.  
  - **2010s-present**: The advancement of data science and artificial intelligence has enabled new ways of extracting value and insights from Big Data. The emergence of new data sources, such as the Internet of Things, biometrics, and genomics, has increased the complexity and diversity of data. The development of new technologies and techniques, such as cloud computing, edge computing, stream processing, deep learning, and natural language processing, has improved the efficiency and effectiveness of data processing and analysis. The adoption of Big Data across various domains and industries, such as healthcare, education, finance, and government, has created new opportunities and challenges for data-driven decision making and innovation.
