### Nature of Data

- Data is any information that can be stored, processed, or communicated by humans or machines.
- Data can be classified into different types based on various criteria, such as:
  - Source: where the data comes from, such as sensors, web, social media, etc.
  - Format: how the data is represented, such as structured, unstructured, semi-structured, etc.
  - Quality: how accurate, complete, consistent, and timely the data is, such as valid, invalid, missing, noisy, etc.
  - Value: how useful, relevant, and actionable the data is, such as high-value, low-value, etc.
- Data can also be characterized by different properties, such as:
  - Volume: how much data is generated, stored, or processed, such as terabytes, petabytes, exabytes, etc.
  - Velocity: how fast data is generated, collected, or analyzed, such as real-time, near-real-time, batch, etc.
  - Variety: how diverse data is in terms of sources, formats, types, and semantics, such as text, image, video, audio, etc.
  - Veracity: how trustworthy data is in terms of quality, reliability, and credibility, such as true, false, uncertain, etc.
  - Value: how beneficial data is for decision making, problem solving, or innovation, such as insights, patterns, trends, etc.
- Data can also be analyzed by different methods, such as:
  - Descriptive: what happened in the past or what is happening in the present, such as statistics, reports, dashboards, etc.
  - Diagnostic: why something happened or why something is happening, such as root cause analysis, correlation analysis, etc.
  - Predictive: what will happen in the future or what is likely to happen, such as forecasting, modeling, simulation, etc.
  - Prescriptive: what should be done or what is the best course of action, such as optimization, recommendation, etc.
