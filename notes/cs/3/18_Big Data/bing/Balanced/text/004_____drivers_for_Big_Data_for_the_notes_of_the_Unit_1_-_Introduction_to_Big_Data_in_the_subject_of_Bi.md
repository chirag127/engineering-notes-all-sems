### Drivers for Big Data

Big data is the term used to describe the large and complex datasets that are generated from various sources and require advanced techniques and technologies to store, process, and analyze. Big data has become a key asset for many organizations and industries that want to gain insights, improve decision making, and create value from their data. 

There are several factors that have contributed to the emergence and growth of big data in the last decade. These factors are known as drivers for big data, and they include:

- **The digitization of society**: As more and more aspects of human life and activity are captured and recorded by digital devices and platforms, the amount of data that is generated and collected increases exponentially. Examples of such data sources include online transactions, social media posts, web browsing, mobile applications, sensors, cameras, GPS, etc. According to some estimates, the global data volume is expected to reach 175 zettabytes by 2025, up from 33 zettabytes in 2018  .
- **The drop in technology costs**: The advances in hardware and software technologies have made it possible and affordable to store, process, and analyze big data. The cost of data storage has decreased significantly over the years, from $437,000 per gigabyte in 1980 to $0.02 per gigabyte in 2020 . The development of distributed computing frameworks, such as Hadoop and Spark, has enabled parallel processing of big data across multiple nodes, reducing the time and resources required. The emergence of cloud computing has also provided scalable and flexible solutions for big data storage and analytics, without the need for large upfront investments in infrastructure  .
- **Connectivity through cloud computing**: Cloud computing is the delivery of computing services, such as servers, storage, databases, networking, software, analytics, etc., over the internet. Cloud computing offers several benefits for big data, such as scalability, elasticity, availability, reliability, security, and cost-effectiveness. Cloud computing allows users to access and analyze big data from anywhere and anytime, without worrying about the maintenance and management of the underlying infrastructure. Cloud computing also enables the integration and interoperability of different data sources and platforms, facilitating data sharing and collaboration  .
- **Increased knowledge about data science**: Data science is the interdisciplinary field that combines mathematics, statistics, computer science, and domain knowledge to extract meaningful insights from data. Data science has become a popular and in-demand skill in the era of big data, as more and more organizations and industries seek to leverage data for competitive advantage. Data science involves various methods and techniques, such as data mining, machine learning, artificial intelligence, natural language processing, visualization, etc., to analyze and interpret big data and generate actionable outcomes  .
- **Social media applications**: Social media is the collective term for the online platforms and applications that enable users to create and share content, such as text, images, videos, audio, etc., and interact with other users. Social media has become a ubiquitous and influential phenomenon in the modern society, affecting various aspects of human behavior, communication, culture, politics, etc. Social media also generates a massive amount of data, both structured and unstructured, that can be used for various purposes, such as sentiment analysis, trend detection, recommendation systems, targeted advertising, etc. According to some statistics, there are about 4.2 billion active social media users in the world, generating about 500 million tweets, 350 million photos, and 100 hours of video per day  .
- **The rise of Internet-of-Things (IoT)**: Internet-of-Things (IoT) is the network of physical objects, such as devices, vehicles, appliances, etc., that are embedded with sensors, software, and connectivity, enabling them to collect and exchange data with other objects and systems. IoT has many applications and benefits, such as smart homes, smart cities, smart health, smart agriculture, smart transportation, etc., that can improve the efficiency, convenience, and quality of life. IoT also generates a huge amount of data, both structured and unstructured, that can be used for various purposes, such as monitoring, control, optimization, prediction, etc. According to some estimates, there will be about 75 billion IoT devices connected to the internet by 2025, generating about 79.4 zettabytes
