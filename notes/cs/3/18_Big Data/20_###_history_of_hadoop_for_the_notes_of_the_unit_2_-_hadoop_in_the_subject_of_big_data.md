### History of Hadoop for the notes of the Unit 2 - Hadoop in the subject of Big Data

Unit 2 - Hadoop in the subject of Big Data will cover the history of Hadoop, an open-source software framework for storing and processing large amounts of data.

Hadoop was created by Doug Cutting and Mike Cafarella in 2005 and was inspired by Google's MapReduce and Google File System (GFS) papers. The initial version of Hadoop was released in 2006 and quickly gained popularity as an open-source solution for storing and processing large amounts of data.

Over the years, Hadoop has evolved and expanded to include a number of related projects, such as the Hadoop Distributed File System (HDFS), a scalable and reliable data storage system, and the Hadoop MapReduce framework, a powerful tool for processing large amounts of data in parallel.

In the context of Unit 2 - Hadoop in the subject of Big Data, students will learn about the history of Hadoop, including its origins, evolution, and current state. This may include working with examples of Hadoop, analyzing the syntax and structure of Hadoop, and learning how to use Hadoop to store and process large amounts of data.

In Unit 2 - Hadoop in the subject of Big Data, students will also learn about the benefits and limitations of using Hadoop, and will learn about alternative approaches to storing and processing large amounts of data, such as using data warehousing and business intelligence tools.

In summary, in Unit 2 - Hadoop in the subject of Big Data, students will learn about the history of Hadoop, an open-source software framework for storing and processing large amounts of data. They will learn about the origins, evolution, and current state of Hadoop, and will learn about the benefits and limitations of using Hadoop and alternative approaches to storing and processing large amounts of data.
