### HiveQL for the notes of the Unit 11 - Hadoop Eco System Frameworks in the subject of Big Data

The HiveQL section of the Unit 11 - Hadoop Eco System Frameworks in the subject of Big Data should cover the following topics:

1. Definition of HiveQL (a SQL-like language used for querying data stored in Apache Hive)
2. Overview of HiveQL (what it is, when it is used, advantages and disadvantages)
3. Hive Architecture (e.g. how Hive works and its components)
4. Overview of Hive Architecture (what it is, when it is used, advantages and disadvantages)
5. Hive Data Types (e.g. the different data types supported by Hive)
6. Overview of Hive Data Types (what they are, when they are used, advantages and disadvantages)
7. Hive Operators (e.g. the operations used in HiveQL for processing data)
8. Overview of Hive Operators (what they are, when they are used, advantages and disadvantages)
9. Hive UDFs (User-Defined Functions) (e.g. functions written in a programming language such as Java and used in Hive)
10. Overview of Hive UDFs (what they are, when they are used, advantages and disadvantages)
11. Hive Partitions (e.g. how data is organized into partitions in Hive)
12. Overview of Hive Partitions (what they are, when they are used, advantages and disadvantages)
13. Hive Buckets (e.g. how data is organized into buckets in Hive)
14. Overview of Hive Buckets (what they are, when they are used, advantages and disadvantages)
15. HiveQL in industry applications (how HiveQL is used in various industries)
16. Further reading and resources (books, articles, online resources)

HiveQL is an important component of the subject of Big Data, especially in the context of Hadoop Eco System Frameworks. Understanding the principles and techniques of HiveQL helps students to develop the skills required to design and implement effective big data processing solutions using Hive. The hands-on practice and further reading resources will help students to develop their skills and deepen their understanding of the material.
