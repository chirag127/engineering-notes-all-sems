### Analysis vs Reporting

1. Analysis: It is the process of inspecting, cleaning, transforming and modeling data with the goal of discovering useful information, suggesting conclusions and supporting decision making. It focuses on understanding the data and deriving insights from it.

2. Reporting: It is the process of organizing and summarizing data and analyses into a clear and coherent document. It focuses on effective communication of insights and conclusions to stakeholders.

3. Key Differences:

- Analysis is about exploration while reporting is about explanation.
- Analysis uses data to find insights while reporting uses insights to tell a story.
- Analysis is iterative and open-ended while reporting has a defined beginning and end.
- Analysis requires statistics and data modeling skills while reporting requires communication skills.

4. Both analysis and reporting are equally important in the data lifecycle. Analysis is useless unless insights are reported and reporting has no purpose without analysis. They are complementary processes and effective data analysis and reporting requires skills in both.
