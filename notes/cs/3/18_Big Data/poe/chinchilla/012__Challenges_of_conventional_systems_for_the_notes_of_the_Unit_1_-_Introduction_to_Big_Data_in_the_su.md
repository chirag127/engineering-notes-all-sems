### Challenges of Conventional Systems for the Notes of the Unit 1 - Introduction to Big Data in the Subject of Big Data

Big Data is a rapidly growing field that deals with the collection, storage, analysis, and interpretation of large and complex data sets. In this unit, we will be discussing the challenges of conventional systems for handling Big Data. Here are some of the key challenges:

- **Data Volume:** Conventional systems are not designed to handle massive amounts of data that are available today. With the rise of the Internet of Things (IoT) and other digital technologies, we are generating more data than ever before. Conventional systems are not equipped to store and process these massive amounts of data.

- **Data Variety:** Another challenge of conventional systems is their inability to handle different types of data. Traditional systems are designed to handle structured data, which is organized in a predefined format. However, Big Data includes unstructured or semi-structured data that comes in various formats, such as text, audio, video, and images.

- **Data Velocity:** Conventional systems are also not capable of processing data in real-time. They are designed to handle batch processing, which involves storing data and processing it in batches. However, Big Data requires real-time processing, where data is analyzed and processed as it is generated.

- **Data Veracity:** Veracity refers to the accuracy and reliability of data. With conventional systems, it is difficult to ensure the accuracy and reliability of data. Big Data includes data from various sources, and it can be challenging to verify the accuracy and reliability of the data.

- **Infrastructure:** Conventional systems require a specific hardware infrastructure to store and process data. However, Big Data requires a more powerful and flexible infrastructure that can handle massive amounts of data and process it in real-time. This infrastructure includes high-performance computing systems, distributed file systems, and cloud computing.

- **Cost:** Building and maintaining a Big Data infrastructure can be expensive. Conventional systems require a specific infrastructure, which can be costly to build and maintain. Big Data requires a more powerful and flexible infrastructure, which can be even more expensive.

In conclusion, conventional systems face various challenges when it comes to handling Big Data. These challenges include data volume, variety, velocity, veracity, infrastructure, and cost. It is essential to understand these challenges to develop effective Big Data solutions.
