### Challenges of conventional systems for the notes of the Unit 1 - Introduction to Big Data in the subject of Big Data

Conventional systems refer to traditional data processing systems that were designed to handle small to medium-sized data sets. The following are some of the key challenges faced by conventional systems when dealing with big data:

1. Scalability: Conventional systems are not designed to scale to handle large and complex data sets, leading to performance bottlenecks and increased latency.

2. Data Volume: Conventional systems struggle to handle the volume of big data, leading to data loss, data duplication, and data corruption.

3. Data Variety: Conventional systems are not designed to handle the variety of data types and formats found in big data, leading to data integration and data quality issues.

4. Data Velocity: Conventional systems are not designed to handle the high velocity of big data, leading to real-time data processing and analysis challenges.

5. Data Veracity: Conventional systems are not designed to handle the uncertainty and complexity of big data, leading to data quality and data accuracy issues.

6. Cost: Conventional systems are often more expensive to maintain and operate than big data systems, due to the need for more hardware, software, and personnel.

In this unit, you will learn about the challenges of conventional systems when dealing with big data, including scalability, data volume, data variety, data velocity, data veracity, and cost.

This unit will provide a foundation for understanding the limitations of conventional systems and the need for big data technology, and for exploring the various concepts and techniques used in the field of Big Data.
