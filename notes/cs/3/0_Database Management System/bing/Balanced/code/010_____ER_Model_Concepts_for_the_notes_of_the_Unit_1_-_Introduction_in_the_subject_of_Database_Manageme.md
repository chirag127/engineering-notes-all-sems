### ER Model Concepts

- The ER model is a conceptual data model that describes the entities, attributes, and relationships in a database.
- An entity is a real-world object or concept that can be identified by a unique key and has some properties. For example, a student, a course, or a department are entities.
- An attribute is a property or characteristic of an entity that can have a value. For example, name, age, or grade are attributes of a student entity.
- A relationship is an association or connection between two or more entities that has some meaning or purpose. For example, a student enrolls in a course, or a department offers a course are relationships.
- A relationship can have a degree, which is the number of entities involved in the relationship. For example, a binary relationship has a degree of two, a ternary relationship has a degree of three, and so on.
- A relationship can also have a cardinality, which is the number of occurrences of one entity that can be associated with each occurrence of another entity in the relationship. For example, a one-to-one relationship means that each entity in the relationship can be associated with at most one entity of the other type. A one-to-many relationship means that one entity can be associated with many entities of the other type, but not vice versa. A many-to-many relationship means that both entities can be associated with many entities of the other type.
- An ER diagram is a graphical representation of the ER model using symbols and notation. An entity is represented by a rectangle, an attribute is represented by an oval, and a relationship is represented by a diamond. A line connects an entity to its attributes and to the relationships it participates in. A key attribute is underlined, and a derived attribute is shown with a dashed line. The cardinality of a relationship is shown with symbols or numbers on the line connecting the entities. For example, a crow's foot symbol indicates a many cardinality, and a 1 or N indicates a one or many cardinality.
