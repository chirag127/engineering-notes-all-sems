### Notation for ER Diagram for the notes of the Unit 1 - Introduction in the subject of Database Management System

ER (Entity-Relationship) diagram is a graphical representation of entities and their relationships to each other, used in database design. In the context of Unit 1 - Introduction in the subject of Database Management System, the following notations are commonly used in ER diagrams:

1. Entity: Represented as a rectangle, an entity is a real-world object or concept that can be uniquely identified.

2. Attribute: Represented as an oval, an attribute is a characteristic or property of an entity.

3. Relationship: Represented as a diamond, a relationship is a connection between two or more entities.

4. Cardinality: Represented as a line connecting entities, cardinality indicates the number of instances of one entity that can be associated with a single instance of another entity. For example, one-to-one, one-to-many, or many-to-many relationships.

5. Key: Represented as an underline, a key is an attribute or combination of attributes that uniquely identifies an instance of an entity.

6. Weak Entity: Represented as a rectangle with double borders, a weak entity is an entity that depends on another entity for its existence.

7. Inheritance: Represented as an is-a relationship, inheritance is a relationship between a parent entity and a child entity that inherits the attributes and relationships of the parent entity.

By using these notations, ER diagrams help to visualize the structure of a database and the relationships between entities, making it easier to understand and design a database.
