### Activity Diagrams

Activity diagrams are graphical representations of workflows of stepwise activities and actions with support for choice, iteration, and concurrency. They are used to model the dynamic aspects of a system. In the context of Object Oriented System Design, activity diagrams can be used to describe the business and operational step-by-step workflows of components in a system.

Some key points to remember about activity diagrams are:

1. Activity diagrams are used to model the behavior of a system, and show the flow of control from activity to activity.
2. They are similar to flowcharts, but can also include elements such as swimlanes, which represent different actors or objects within the system.
3. Activity diagrams can be used to model both sequential and concurrent activities.
4. They can also be used to model decisions, where the flow of control is determined by the outcome of a condition or a set of conditions.
5. Activity diagrams are useful for modeling complex, real-world processes that involve multiple actors and objects, and that may have multiple possible outcomes.

In summary, activity diagrams are a powerful tool for modeling the dynamic behavior of a system, and can help to provide a clear and concise visual representation of complex workflows and processes. They are an important part of the basic structural modeling unit in Object Oriented System Design.
