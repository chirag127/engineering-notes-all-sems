### Activity Diagrams for the notes of the Unit 2 - Basic Structural Modeling in the subject of Object Oriented System Design

Activity diagrams are a type of UML diagram used to model the flow of activities in a software system. They are used to describe the processes and workflows in a system, and to represent the flow of control in a system.

The following are some of the key concepts related to activity diagrams:

1. Activities: Activities are the steps in a process or workflow, and they represent the actions that are performed in a system.

2. Transitions: Transitions are the connections between activities, and they represent the flow of control in a system.

3. States: States are the conditions or situations in which an activity can occur, and they represent the different stages in a process or workflow.

4. Swimlanes: Swimlanes are the horizontal or vertical partitions in an activity diagram, and they are used to organize activities into functional or organizational units.

In this unit, you will learn about the basic concepts of activity diagrams, and you will learn how to use activity diagrams to model the flow of activities in a software system. You will also learn about the different types of transitions that can be used in activity diagrams, and you will learn how to choose the appropriate type of transition for a specific task.

This unit will provide a foundation for understanding the principles and practices of activity diagrams in Object Oriented System Design, and for exploring the various concepts and techniques used in the field of software engineering.
