### principles of modelling for the notes of the Unit 1 - Introduction: The meaning of Object Orientation in the subject of Object Oriented System Design

The principles of modelling in Object Oriented System Design refer to the guidelines and best practices for creating models of software systems. These principles help to ensure that the models are accurate, complete, and easy to understand. The following are some of the key principles of modelling in Object Oriented System Design:

1. Abstraction: Abstraction is the process of identifying and representing the essential features of a system, while ignoring the less important details.

2. Encapsulation: Encapsulation is the process of hiding the implementation details of a system, and exposing only the necessary information to the outside world.

3. Inheritance: Inheritance is the mechanism by which a new class can be derived from an existing class, inheriting its attributes and behaviour.

4. Polymorphism: Polymorphism is the ability of a single method or operator to operate on objects of different types, or to have different meanings in different contexts.

5. Composition: Composition is the process of combining objects to form more complex objects, and to reuse existing functionality.

In this unit, you will learn about the principles of modelling in Object Oriented System Design, and you will learn how to apply these principles to create accurate and effective models of software systems. You will also learn about the importance of modelling in the software development process, and you will learn how to choose the appropriate modelling techniques for a specific task.

This unit will provide a foundation for understanding the principles and practices of Object Oriented System Design, and for exploring the various concepts and techniques used in the field of software engineering.
