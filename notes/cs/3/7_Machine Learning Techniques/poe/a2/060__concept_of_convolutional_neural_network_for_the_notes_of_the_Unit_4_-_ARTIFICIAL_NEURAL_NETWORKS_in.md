### Concept of Convolutional Neural Network

- Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) are a type of feed-forward artificial neural network that has proven very effective for image recognition and classification.
- CNNs use a variation of multilayer perceptrons designed to require minimal preprocessing. They are also known as shift invariant or space invariant artificial neural networks (SIANNs)
- CNNs are composed of alternating convolutional and pooling layers stacked on top of each other. The layers are fit progressively to learn about various features of the input data.
- The convolutional layers apply a convolution operation to the input passing the result to the next layer. The pooling layers then downsample the input reducing its dimensionality and allowing for some invariance to translation.
- The convolutional layers learn filters that activate in response to various features in the input, roughly corresponding to edges or shapes at various angles and positions in the visual field. The pooling layers integrate information spatially leading to invariance to small translations of the input.
- The key advantage of CNNs for image classification is their ability to automatically learn hierarchical feature representations directly from the raw input data. This avoids the need for manual feature extraction and selection that would traditionally be required for machine learning on images.
