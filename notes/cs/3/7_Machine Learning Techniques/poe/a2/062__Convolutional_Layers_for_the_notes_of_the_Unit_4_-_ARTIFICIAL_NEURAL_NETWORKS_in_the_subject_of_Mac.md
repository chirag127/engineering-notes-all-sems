### Convolutional Layers

1. Convolutional layers are a type of layers used in CNNs that apply a convolution operation to the input passing through them.
2. They detect spatial patterns in the data and are inspired by biological processes in the visual cortex.
3. A convolutional layer consists of a set of filters that are convolved with the input volume to produce a filtered output.
4. Each filter is small spatially (e.g. 3x3 or 5x5) but extends through the full depth of the input volume.
5. As the filters slide over the volume, they produce a 2D activation map that indicates the responses of the filter at every spatial position.
6. Multiple filters are used in parallel, each producing a separate 2D activation map. The collection of these activation maps forms the output volume of the convolutional layer.
7. The values in the filters are the trainable parameters of a convolutional layer which are adjusted during training to produce desirable activations.
8. Pooling layers are often used after convolutional layers to downsample the data and control overfitting.
9. Convolutional layers are particularly useful in computer vision applications as they can detect spatial features in images such as edges and patterns.
