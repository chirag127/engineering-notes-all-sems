### Decision surface for the notes of the Unit 2 - REGRESSION in the subject of Machine Learning Techniques.

1. A decision surface is a curve or surface that separates regions of different classification. For a binary classification problem, the decision surface is a curve that separates the two classes. For a regression problem with one input variable, the decision surface is simply a line.

2. To classify a new data point, we check which side of the decision surface it lies on, and assign it to the corresponding class. The goal of supervised learning is to find the decision surface that best separates the classes or fits the target values.

3. For linear regression, the decision surface is a hyperplane. The goal is to find the hyperplane that minimizes the regression error. The residual sum of squares is used as the loss function for linear regression that is to be minimized. The resulting hyperplane is the linear regression model that predicts the target variable.

4. For non-linear regression, the decision surface is more complex and corresponds to a non-linear function. Non-linear regression is required when the relationship between the input and target variables is non-linear. More complex machine learning models such as neural networks are needed to determine the non-linear decision surface.

5. The characteristics and accuracy of the decision surface depends on the machine learning model and hyperparameters used. More flexible models can capture complex non-linear relationships but may risk overfitting the training data. Finding the right balance through model selection and hyperparameter tuning is crucial to building an effective machine learning model.
