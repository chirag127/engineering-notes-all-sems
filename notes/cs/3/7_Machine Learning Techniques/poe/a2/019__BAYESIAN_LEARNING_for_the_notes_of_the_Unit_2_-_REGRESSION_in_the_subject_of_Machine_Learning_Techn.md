### BAYESIAN LEARNING for the notes of the Unit 2 - REGRESSION in the subject of Machine Learning Techniques

1. Bayesian learning is a method of statistical inference in which Bayes' theorem is used to update the probability estimate for a hypothesis as more evidence or information becomes available.
2. In Bayesian learning, we start with an initial probability distribution over the hypotheses and then update this distribution based on the data. The strength of Bayesian learning is that it allows you to incorporate your prior beliefs and update them in the light of observed data.
3. The steps involved in Bayesian learning are:

- Specify a prior distribution over the hypotheses (represent initial beliefs)
- Obtain data (evidence)
- Calculate the likelihood of the data under each hypothesis
- Apply Bayes' theorem to obtain the posterior distribution over hypotheses
- Make predictions or decisions based on the posterior distribution

4. Some advantages of Bayesian learning are:

- It allows you to incorporate prior knowledge into the model.
- It gives you a natural way to handle both parametric and structural uncertainty.
- It provides a principled way to incorporate new data and update beliefs.
- It allows you to compare models and weigh evidence.

5. Some disadvantages of Bayesian learning are:

- It can be computationally intensive.
- The choice of prior distribution can influence results.
- It is not always straightforward to specify priors.

6. Examples of Bayesian learning applications include spam filtering, speech recognition, computer vision, expert systems, etc.
