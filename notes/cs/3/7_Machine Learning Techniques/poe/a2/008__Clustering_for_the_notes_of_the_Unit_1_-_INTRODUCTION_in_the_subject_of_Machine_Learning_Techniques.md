### Clustering for the notes of the Unit 1 - INTRODUCTION in the subject of Machine Learning Techniques

1. Clustering is the task of grouping a set of objects in a way that objects in the same group (called a cluster) are more similar (in some sense) to each other than to those in other groups (clusters).

2. It is a main task of exploratory data mining, and a common technique for statistical data analysis, used in many fields, including machine learning, pattern recognition, image analysis, information retrieval, and bioinformatics.

3. Clustering is a method of unsupervised learning, and a common technique for statistical data analysis used to find hidden patterns or grouping in data.

4. The clustering problem has been addressed in many algorithms and by researchers in many fields, including statistics, pattern recognition, machine learning, and computer science.

5. The core idea is that the objects are grouped based on their similarity. But there are many notions of similarity, and different algorithms may use different measures or combine multiple measures. The choice of the right notion of similarity and cluster algorithm depends on the problem context and application.

6. The results of cluster analysis can be used to gain insight into the distribution of data to identify atypical observations, discover hidden patterns, and make data easy to browse.

7. The goal of clustering is to determine the intrinsic grouping in a set of unlabeled data. But the objective functions and constraints vary depending on the problem and different clustering algorithms make different assumptions on the structure of data.
