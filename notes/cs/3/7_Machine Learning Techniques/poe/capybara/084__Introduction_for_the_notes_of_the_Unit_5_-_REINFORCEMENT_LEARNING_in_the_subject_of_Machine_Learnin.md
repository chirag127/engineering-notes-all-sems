### Introduction

In this unit, we will be exploring reinforcement learning, which is a subfield of machine learning that focuses on training agents to make decisions based on rewards and punishments. Here are some key points to keep in mind as we delve deeper into this topic:

- Reinforcement learning is used in scenarios where an agent needs to make a sequence of decisions in an environment to achieve a specific goal.
- The agent learns from the rewards (or punishments) it receives as a consequence of its actions in the environment.
- The environment is modeled as a Markov decision process (MDP), which consists of states, actions, rewards, and a transition function that describes the probability of moving from one state to another.
- The agent's goal is to learn a policy, which is a mapping from states to actions that maximizes the expected cumulative reward over time.
- There are two main approaches to reinforcement learning: model-based and model-free. In the former, the agent learns the transition function of the environment, while in the latter, the agent directly learns the optimal policy.
- Reinforcement learning has applications in a wide range of fields, including robotics, finance, gaming, and more.

As we delve deeper into this topic, we will explore various algorithms and techniques used in reinforcement learning, such as Q-learning, policy gradients, and more. By the end of this unit, you should have a solid understanding of the fundamentals of reinforcement learning and be able to apply them to real-world scenarios.
