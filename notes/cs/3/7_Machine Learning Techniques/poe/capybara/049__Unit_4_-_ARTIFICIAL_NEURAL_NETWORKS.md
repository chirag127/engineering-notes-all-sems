## Unit 4 - ARTIFICIAL NEURAL NETWORKS

Artificial Neural Networks (ANN) are a computational model that simulates the functioning of the human brain. They are used in various fields, including image and speech recognition, natural language processing, and robotics. 

Here are some key points to understand about Artificial Neural Networks:

- ANNs are composed of interconnected nodes, called neurons, which transmit and process information. The neurons are organized in layers, with input and output layers, and one or more hidden layers.

- The neurons apply mathematical functions to the input signals they receive, and the output of each neuron is transmitted to the next layer until the final output is produced.

- The ANN learns from experience by adjusting the weights and biases of the neurons based on the error between the predicted output and the actual output. This process is called training, and it is usually done with a large dataset.

- There are various types of ANNs, including feedforward, recurrent, and convolutional. Feedforward ANNs are the most common type and are used for tasks such as classification, regression, and pattern recognition. Recurrent ANNs are used for tasks that involve sequential data, such as language modeling and speech recognition. Convolutional ANNs are used for image and video analysis.

- ANNs have several advantages over traditional algorithms, including their ability to handle large and complex datasets, their ability to learn from experience, and their ability to generalize to new data.

- ANNs have several limitations as well, including their need for large amounts of data, their vulnerability to overfitting, and their lack of transparency, which makes it difficult to understand how they arrive at their predictions.

Overall, Artificial Neural Networks are an exciting field that has the potential to revolutionize many industries. By understanding their working principles and limitations, we can make informed decisions about when and how to use them.
