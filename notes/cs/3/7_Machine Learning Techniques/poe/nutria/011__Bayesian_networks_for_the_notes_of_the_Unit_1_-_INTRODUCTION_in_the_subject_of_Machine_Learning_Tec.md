
### Bayesian Networks

Bayesian networks are graphical models used to represent probabilistic relationships between variables. They are used in many applications such as medical diagnosis, speech recognition, and natural language processing.

- Bayesian networks are directed acyclic graphs (DAGs) where the nodes represent random variables and the edges represent the conditional dependencies between them. 
- Each node has a set of states and the probability of each state is determined by the parents of the node. 
- The probability of each state is determined using Bayes’ theorem, which states that the probability of an event is determined by the prior probability of the event, the likelihood of the event given the evidence, and the prior probability of the evidence. 
- Bayesian networks are used to represent uncertain knowledge and are a powerful tool for reasoning under uncertainty. 
- They can be used to make predictions, determine the most likely causes of an event, and detect patterns in data. 
- They can also be used for decision making, where the optimal decision is determined by the network's structure and parameters.
