### DEEP LEARNING for the notes of the Unit 4 - ARTIFICIAL NEURAL NETWORKS in the subject of Machine Learning Techniques

Deep Learning (DL) is a subfield of Artificial Neural Networks (ANNs) that uses multi-layered artificial neural networks to model complex patterns in data. It is based on the idea that a machine can learn to perform tasks without being explicitly programmed. DL models are trained using large amounts of data and algorithms that iteratively adjust the model weights to minimize the error between the predicted output and the actual output.

DL has revolutionized computer vision, speech recognition, and natural language processing tasks. It is used in various industries such as finance, healthcare, and transportation.

DL models are trained using supervised, unsupervised, or reinforcement learning. In supervised learning, the model is trained on labeled data and makes predictions based on the patterns learned from the data. In unsupervised learning, the model is trained on unlabeled data and must find patterns in the data on its own. In reinforcement learning, the model learns from trial and error and is rewarded for making correct decisions.

DL models can be implemented using various algorithms such as Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs), Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs), and Autoencoders. CNNs are used for image classification and object detection tasks. RNNs are used for sequence data such as speech and text. Autoencoders are used for dimensionality reduction and anomaly detection.

DL requires a significant amount of computational resources and can be time-consuming to train. It is important to carefully select the architecture and hyperparameters of the model to avoid overfitting and underfitting. Overfitting occurs when the model is too complex and memorizes the training data, leading to poor generalization to new data. Underfitting occurs when the model is too simple and cannot capture the underlying patterns in the data.
