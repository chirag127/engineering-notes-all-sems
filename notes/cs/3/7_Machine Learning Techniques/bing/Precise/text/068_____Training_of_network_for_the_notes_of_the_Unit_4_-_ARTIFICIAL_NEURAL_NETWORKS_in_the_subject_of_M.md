### Training of network for the notes of the Unit 4 - ARTIFICIAL NEURAL NETWORKS in the subject of Machine Learning Techniques

1. Training a neural network involves adjusting the weights and biases of the network to improve its performance on a given task.
2. The training process typically involves presenting the network with a set of input-output pairs and adjusting the weights and biases to minimize the error between the network's output and the desired output.
3. One common approach to training neural networks is backpropagation, which involves computing the gradient of the error with respect to the weights and biases and using this gradient to update the weights and biases.
4. Another approach is to use evolutionary algorithms, which involve generating a population of networks and selecting the best performing networks to produce offspring for the next generation.
5. The choice of training algorithm can depend on factors such as the size and complexity of the network, the amount of training data available, and the desired performance of the network.
6. It is important to monitor the training process to avoid overfitting, where the network becomes too specialized to the training data and performs poorly on new data.
7. Techniques such as early stopping, where training is stopped when the performance on a validation set stops improving, can help prevent overfitting.
8. Regularization techniques, such as L1 and L2 regularization, can also be used to prevent overfitting by adding a penalty term to the error function that encourages the network to have small weights.
9. Once the network has been trained, it can be used to make predictions on new data by feeding the data through the network and computing the output.
