### Fully Connected

A fully connected layer in an artificial neural network is a layer in which all the neurons are connected to all the neurons in the previous layer. This type of layer is also known as a dense layer.

- In a fully connected layer, each neuron receives input from all the neurons in the previous layer, and its output is sent to all the neurons in the next layer.
- The weights of the connections between the neurons determine the strength of the connections.
- The weights are adjusted during the training process to minimize the error between the predicted output and the actual output.
- Fully connected layers are commonly used in feedforward neural networks, where the information flows in one direction from the input layer to the output layer.
- They can also be used in other types of neural networks, such as recurrent neural networks, where the information can flow in cycles.
- Fully connected layers can be computationally expensive, especially when the number of neurons in the layers is large.
- There are other types of layers, such as convolutional layers and pooling layers, that can be used to reduce the computational cost and improve the performance of the neural network.
