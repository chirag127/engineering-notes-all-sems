## Unit 2 - Wireless Networking, Wireless LAN Overview: MAC issues, IEEE 802.11, Blue Tooth, Wireless

Wireless networking has become an integral part of our lives. It has revolutionized the way we communicate, work and entertain. In this unit, we will explore the basics of wireless networking, including its architecture, protocols, and standards. We will also discuss the most commonly used wireless technologies, such as IEEE 802.11 and Bluetooth.

### MAC Issues in Wireless LAN

A Media Access Control (MAC) address is a unique identifier assigned to a network interface controller (NIC) for use as a network address in communications within a network segment. In wireless LANs, MAC addresses play a critical role in ensuring that data packets are delivered to the intended recipient.

However, wireless LANs face several MAC issues, such as:

- Hidden node problem: occurs when two nodes are out of range of each other, but within range of a third node. This can cause packet collisions and interference.
- Exposed node problem: occurs when a node hears another node transmitting, and mistakenly assumes that it cannot transmit. This can result in decreased network efficiency.
- MAC spoofing: occurs when an attacker impersonates a legitimate device by changing its MAC address. This can lead to unauthorized access and data breaches.

### IEEE 802.11

IEEE 802.11 is a set of standards for wireless local area network (WLAN) communication. It is commonly known as Wi-Fi. The IEEE 802.11 standard has several versions, each with different data transfer rates, frequency bands, and modulation schemes.

Some key features of IEEE 802.11 are:

- Multiple access points can be used to provide coverage over a large area.
- It supports several security protocols, such as WPA2 and AES.
- It has a range of up to 100 meters indoors and up to 300 meters outdoors.
- It operates at different frequencies, including 2.4 GHz and 5 GHz.

### Bluetooth

Bluetooth is a wireless technology that allows devices to communicate with each other over short distances. It is commonly used for connecting devices such as smartphones, headphones, and speakers.

Some key features of Bluetooth are:

- It operates at a frequency of 2.4 GHz.
- It has a range of up to 10 meters.
- It supports several profiles, such as A2DP for audio streaming and HFP for hands-free calling.
- It has low power consumption, making it ideal for use in portable devices.

- To remember the different versions of IEEE 802.11, use the mnemonic "Please Stop Taking Lousy Tests" where P stands for 802.11a, S stands for 802.11b, T stands for 802.11g, L stands for 802.11n, and T stands for 802.11ac.
- To remember the frequency of Bluetooth, use the mnemonic "Two point four, you can go far. Ten meters is all you need to hear."
