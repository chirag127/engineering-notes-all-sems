### WAP: Architecture for the notes of the Unit 2 - Wireless Networking, Wireless LAN Overview: MAC issues, IEEE 802.11, Blue Tooth, Wireless in the subject of Mobile Computing

Wireless Application Protocol (WAP) is a protocol that provides a standard way to access information and services on the Internet from mobile devices such as smartphones, tablets, and laptops. In this unit, we will discuss the architecture of WAP and its various components. We will also cover wireless LAN overview, MAC issues, IEEE 802.11, Bluetooth, and wireless in the subject of Mobile Computing. Let's dive into the details!

#### WAP Architecture

1. **WAP Gateway:** The WAP gateway acts as a mediator between the mobile device and the Internet. It is responsible for translating the WAP protocol into HTTP or other Internet protocols that can be understood by web servers. The gateway also compresses the data to reduce the amount of data transmitted over the wireless network.

2. **WAP Proxy:** The WAP proxy is responsible for caching frequently accessed web pages and data, which reduces the amount of network traffic and improves the performance of the system. The proxy also performs content adaptation, which modifies the content of a web page to make it more suitable for small screens.

3. **WAP Browser:** The WAP browser is a web browser designed for mobile devices. It supports the WAP protocol and can display web pages that are designed for small screens. The browser also supports WML (Wireless Markup Language), which is a markup language used for creating web pages for mobile devices.

#### Wireless LAN Overview

1. **MAC Issues:** One of the major issues in wireless LAN is the Medium Access Control (MAC) layer. The MAC layer is responsible for controlling access to the wireless medium. In wireless LAN, multiple devices share the same wireless medium, which can lead to collisions and interference. To avoid collisions, the MAC layer uses various techniques such as Carrier Sense Multiple Access with Collision Avoidance (CSMA/CA).

2. **IEEE 802.11:** IEEE 802.11 is a set of standards for wireless LANs. It defines the physical layer and MAC layer specifications for wireless LANs. The most commonly used standards are 802.11a, 802.11b, 802.11g, and 802.11n.

3. **Bluetooth:** Bluetooth is a wireless technology used for short-range communication between devices. It uses radio waves to transmit data over short distances. Bluetooth is commonly used for connecting mobile devices, such as smartphones and headphones, to other devices, such as laptops and speakers.

#### Wireless in the subject of Mobile Computing

1. **Advantages of Wireless:** Wireless communication has several advantages over wired communication. It allows users to access information and services from anywhere, at any time. It also eliminates the need for physical connections, which makes it more convenient and flexible.

2. **Disadvantages of Wireless:** Wireless communication also has some disadvantages. It is more prone to interference and security issues than wired communication. It also has limited bandwidth and range, which can affect the performance of the system.

3. **Examples of Wireless Applications:** Wireless communication is used in various applications, such as mobile computing, IoT, and smart homes. In mobile computing, wireless communication allows users to access the Internet and other services from their mobile devices. In IoT, wireless communication is used for connecting sensors and devices to the Internet. In smart homes, wireless communication is used for controlling various devices, such as lights and thermostats, from a central hub.

In conclusion, WAP architecture, wireless LAN overview, MAC issues, IEEE 802.11, Bluetooth, and wireless in the subject of Mobile Computing are important concepts in mobile computing. Understanding these concepts can help us design and develop better mobile applications and services.
