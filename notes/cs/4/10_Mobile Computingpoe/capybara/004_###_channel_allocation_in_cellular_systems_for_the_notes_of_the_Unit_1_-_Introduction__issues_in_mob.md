### Channel Allocation in Cellular Systems

In cellular systems, the available frequency spectrum is divided into channels that are allocated to individual cells. Channel allocation is a critical aspect of cellular network design as it determines the capacity and quality of service that can be delivered to users. The allocation of channels to cells is typically done through a process known as frequency reuse, which involves reusing the available frequency spectrum across multiple cells in a controlled manner.

#### Frequency Reuse

Frequency reuse is a technique used in cellular systems to maximize the use of available frequency spectrum while minimizing interference between cells. The process involves dividing the available frequency spectrum into a set of frequency channels and then allocating these channels to cells in a way that minimizes interference.

In cellular systems, the available frequency spectrum is divided into three main frequency bands: the cellular band, the PCS band, and the AWS band. Each of these bands is further divided into a set of frequency channels, which are typically 30 kHz wide.

Frequency reuse is achieved by dividing the cells in a cellular network into a set of clusters. Each cluster is assigned a set of frequency channels that are used exclusively by the cells within the cluster. Adjacent clusters are assigned different sets of frequency channels to avoid interference between cells.

#### Channel Allocation Strategies

There are several channel allocation strategies that can be used in cellular systems. These include fixed channel allocation, dynamic channel allocation, and hybrid channel allocation.

##### Fixed Channel Allocation

Fixed channel allocation involves assigning a fixed set of frequency channels to each cell in a cellular network. This strategy is simple but inefficient as it does not take into account the varying traffic demands of individual cells. As a result, some cells may have more channels than they need, while others may not have enough.

##### Dynamic Channel Allocation

Dynamic channel allocation involves dynamically allocating channels to cells based on their traffic demands. This strategy is more efficient than fixed channel allocation as it allows channels to be allocated where they are needed most. However, it requires more complex network management and can result in increased call setup times.

##### Hybrid Channel Allocation

Hybrid channel allocation combines the benefits of fixed and dynamic channel allocation. In this strategy, a fixed set of channels is allocated to each cell as a baseline, with additional channels allocated dynamically as needed to meet traffic demands.

#### Advantages and Disadvantages of Channel Allocation Strategies

Each channel allocation strategy has its own advantages and disadvantages. Fixed channel allocation is simple and easy to manage, but it is inefficient and can result in unused channels. Dynamic channel allocation is more efficient, but it requires more complex network management and can result in longer call setup times. Hybrid channel allocation combines the benefits of both strategies but requires more complex network management than fixed channel allocation.

#### GSM Channel Allocation

In GSM, the available frequency spectrum is divided into 124 carrier frequency channels, each of which is 200 kHz wide. These channels are further divided into eight time slots, each of which is 577 microseconds long. The combination of a carrier frequency channel and a time slot is known as a physical channel.

GSM uses a combination of fixed and dynamic channel allocation. Each cell is allocated a fixed set of physical channels, with additional channels allocated dynamically as needed to meet traffic demands. The allocation of channels to cells is managed by the Base Station Controller (BSC), which is responsible for assigning channels to cells based on their traffic demands.
