
### Location Management: HLR-VLR, Hierarchical, Handoffs

This section covers the basics of location management in mobile computing, including an overview of the hierarchical location register (HLR) and visitor location register (VLR), as well as handoff procedures.

#### Overview of HLR and VLR

The hierarchical location register (HLR) is a network element in a mobile network that stores information about the location of a mobile subscriber. It is used to route calls to the correct location. The HLR also stores additional information about the subscriber, such as the services they are subscribed to and their current status.

The visitor location register (VLR) is a network element in a mobile network that stores information about the location of a mobile subscriber when they are in a different network than their home network. It is used to route calls to the correct location, and also stores additional information about the subscriber, such as the services they are subscribed to and their current status.

#### Handoffs

A handoff is the process of transferring a mobile subscriber from one base station to another. This is necessary when the mobile subscriber moves from one cell to another. During the handoff process, the subscriber's location is updated in the HLR and VLR so that calls can be routed correctly.

The handoff process involves the following steps:

1. The mobile subscriber moves from one cell to another.
2. The new cell detects the mobile subscriber.
3. The new cell sends a request to the HLR and VLR to update the subscriber's location.
4. The HLR and VLR update the subscriber's location.
5. The new cell sends a message to the old cell to inform it that the subscriber has been handed off.
6. The old cell sends a message to the new cell to confirm the handoff.
7. The new cell sends a message to the HLR and VLR to confirm the handoff.
8. The HLR and VLR update the subscriber's location.

#### Advantages and Disadvantages of Handoffs

Handoffs are an essential part of a mobile network, as they allow mobile subscribers to move around without interruption. However, there are some disadvantages to handoffs, such as increased latency and dropped calls.

#### Examples of Handoffs

Handoffs are used in many different types of networks, including cellular networks, Wi-Fi networks, and satellite networks. In cellular networks, handoffs are used when a mobile subscriber moves from one cell to another. In Wi-Fi networks, handoffs are used when a device moves from one access point to another. In satellite networks, handoffs are used when a device moves from one satellite to another.
