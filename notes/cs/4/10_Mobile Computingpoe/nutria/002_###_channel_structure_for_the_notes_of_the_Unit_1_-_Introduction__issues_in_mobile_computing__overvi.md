
### Channel Structure for the Notes of the Unit 1 - Introduction, Issues in Mobile Computing, Overview of Wireless Telephony: Cellular Concept, GSM

* Cellular concept: Cellular concept is a method of dividing a geographical area into multiple smaller sections or cells. Each cell is served by a base station which transmits and receives signals from the user. The cellular concept is used to provide wireless communication services such as voice and data services.

* GSM: GSM stands for Global System for Mobile Communications and is a digital mobile communication system used by mobile phones and other wireless devices. GSM uses a combination of time division multiple access (TDMA) and frequency division multiple access (FDMA) to provide communication services. GSM networks are divided into multiple channels and each channel is divided into multiple timeslots. 

* Channel Structure: The channel structure of GSM networks consists of two types of channels: traffic channels and control channels. Traffic channels are used to carry data and voice traffic while control channels are used to control the network. The control channels are divided into several types such as broadcast control channels (BCCH), common control channels (CCCH), dedicated control channels (DCCH) and access control channels (ACCH). 

* Mnemonics and Learning Tricks: To remember the different types of control channels in GSM networks, it is helpful to use the mnemonic “BCDAC” which stands for Broadcast Control Channel (BCCH), Common Control Channel (CCCH), Dedicated Control Channel (DCCH), and Access Control Channel (ACCH). 

* Advantages: The main advantage of GSM networks is that they provide high-speed data and voice services with low power consumption. GSM networks also provide coverage over large geographical areas and are capable of providing services such as SMS, call forwarding, call waiting, call conferencing and data services.

* Disadvantages: One of the main disadvantages of GSM networks is that they are not suitable for providing high-speed data services due to the limited bandwidth available. GSM networks also consume more power than other mobile communication systems, which can lead to increased power consumption and higher costs. 

* Examples: GSM networks are used by many mobile phone operators around the world, including AT&T, T-Mobile, Verizon, Sprint, and Vodafone. 

* Applications: GSM networks are used to provide wireless communication services such as voice and data services. GSM networks are also used to provide other services such as SMS, call forwarding, call waiting, call conferencing, and data services.
