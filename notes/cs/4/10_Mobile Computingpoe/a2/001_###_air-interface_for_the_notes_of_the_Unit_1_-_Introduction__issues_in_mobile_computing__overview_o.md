### Air-interface

The air-interface refers to the radio frequency spectrum used by the mobile network to communicate with mobile devices. The key aspects of the air-interface are:

- Frequency band - The allocation of a specific radio frequency spectrum band to the mobile network by the regulatory authority of the country. Popular frequency bands for mobile networks are 700 MHz, 800 MHz, 900 MHz, 1800 MHz, 2100 MHz and 2600 MHz. Higher frequency bands allow for faster data speeds but have a shorter range.
- Modulation scheme - The modulation scheme refers to the way the data is encoded onto the radio frequency carrier wave. Popular modulation schemes for mobile networks are GMSK used in GSM, QPSK and QAM used in UMTS and LTE. Higher order modulation schemes allow for faster data speeds but are more susceptible to interference.
- Duplexing method - The duplexing method refers to the approach used to enable simultaneous uplink and downlink communication between the network and the mobile device. The two options are frequency division duplex (FDD) where the uplink and downlink channels are on different frequencies, and time division duplex (TDD) where the uplink and downlink channels share the same frequency but are separated in the time domain.
- Multiple access scheme - The multiple access scheme refers to the approach used to enable multiple mobile devices to share the same radio frequency spectrum. Common multiple access schemes are FDMA used in GSM, CDMA used in cdmaOne and UMTS, and OFDMA and SC-FDMA used in LTE. The multiple access scheme controls how the radio frequency spectrum is divided up between different mobile devices.

Some mnemonics for remembering the key aspects of the air-interface:

- Frequency band - Higher frequency, higher speed; shorter range
- Modulation scheme - Higher order, higher speed; more interference
- Duplexing method - FDD: different frequencies; TDD: different times
- Multiple access scheme - FDMA: spectrum division; CDMA: code division; OFDMA: orthogonal frequency division
