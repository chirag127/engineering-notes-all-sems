### Wireless applications for the notes of the Unit 2 - Wireless Networking, Wireless LAN Overview: MAC issues, IEEE 802.11, Blue Tooth, Wireless in the subject of Mobile Computing

- Wireless LANs (WLANs) provide wireless connectivity over a limited range using radio waves instead of cables. They are easy to set up and configure and allow devices to be connected to the network from almost anywhere within the coverage area.
- The most popular WLAN technologies are those defined by the IEEE 802.11 standards (often referred to as Wi-Fi). Wi-Fi uses the 2.4 GHz and 5 GHz bands and can provide bandwidth of up to 54 Mbps (802.11a/b/g) or 600 Mbps (802.11n). Wi-Fi is suitable for applications like web browsing, email, and streaming music.
- Bluetooth is a short-range wireless technology primarily designed for connecting nearby devices and streaming audio between devices like headphones and mobile phones. It operates in the 2.4 GHz band and has a maximum bandwidth of 720 Kbps.
- Issues with wireless LANs include interference, security vulnerabilities, and limited range. Protocols and standards define ways to mitigate interference and several security features are built into the 802.11 and Bluetooth standards. Range can be extended using wireless repeaters or high-gain antennas.
- Wireless networks are used in homes, offices, and public hotspots to provide convenient network access for laptops, tablets, and other devices without the need for cables. They enable roaming network access and are ideal for portable devices. The relatively low bandwidth can be a limitation for some applications though.
