# Channel Allocation in Cellular Systems

- Cellular systems divide the available spectrum into channels.
- Channels are allocated to cells to avoid interference and enable frequency reuse.
- Fixed channel allocation (FCA) - Each cell is allocated a fixed set of channels. Does not adapt to traffic variations leading to spectrum wastage.
- Dynamic channel allocation (DCA) - Channels are allocated dynamically based on traffic. Better spectrum utilization but requires complex algorithms and coordination between cells.
- Hybrid schemes also exist that use a mix of fixed and dynamic allocation.
- Frequency reuse involves allocating same set of frequencies to cells that are sufficiently separated to avoid co-channel interference. Cluster size decides how often frequencies can be reused.
- Co-channel interference limits frequency reuse and capacity of cellular systems. Interference can be mitigated using directional antennas, cell sectoring, power control, etc.

Mnemonics:
Forget Copies Always (For FCA)
Dynamic Choice Adjusts (For DCA)

Advantages:
- DCA leads to higher capacity and spectrum efficiency.
- Frequency reuse increases spectral efficiency.

Disadvantages:
- DCA requires complex algorithms and coordination.
- Interference limits frequency reuse and system capacity.

Applications:
- All cellular systems use some form of channel allocation and frequency reuse.
- LTE uses OFDMA for downlink channel allocation and SC-FDMA for uplink.
