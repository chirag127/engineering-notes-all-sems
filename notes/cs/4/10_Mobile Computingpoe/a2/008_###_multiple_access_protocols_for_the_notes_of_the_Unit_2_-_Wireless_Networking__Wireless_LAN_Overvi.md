### Multiple Access Protocols

Some key multiple access protocols used in wireless networks are:

- CSMA/CA: Carrier Sense Multiple Access with Collision Avoidance used in 802.11 wireless LANs. Devices sense the medium for activity and wait for a random backoff time before transmission to avoid collisions.
- TDMA: Time Division Multiple Access divides the time into slots and allocates a slot to each device. Used in 2G digital cellular systems like GSM.
- FDMA: Frequency Division Multiple Access allocates a different frequency channel to each device. Used in 2G analog cellular systems.
- CDMA: Code Division Multiple Access uses spread spectrum technology to allocate codes to devices. Used in 3G and 4G cellular networks.

**Mnemonics:**

- CSMA/CA: *C*arrier *S*ense *M*ultiple *A*ccess with *C*ollision *A*voidance
- TDMA: *T*ime *D*ivision *M*ultiple *A*ccess
- FDMA: *F*requency *D*ivision *M*ultiple *A*ccess
- CDMA: *C*ode *D*ivision *M*ultiple *A*ccess

**Learning Tricks:**

- Think of CSMA/CA as devices being polite by sensing the medium and waiting to avoid collisions
- Think of time slots in TDMA as dividing time into pieces for devices
- Think of frequency channels in FDMA as dividing the frequency spectrum into pieces for devices
- Think of spread spectrum codes in CDMA as special codes allocated to devices to separate them

**Advantages:**

- CSMA/CA: Simple, no fixed slots/frequencies needed
- TDMA: Efficient use of limited bandwidth
- FDMA: Interference avoidance by using different frequencies
- CDMA: Can accommodate many devices simultaneously in a small bandwidth

**Disadvantages:**

- CSMA/CA: Possible collisions can lead to retransmissions and inefficiency
- TDMA: Synchronization required between devices and base station
- FDMA: Limited number of frequency channels can be allocated
- CDMA: Complex technology and power control is critical

**Applications:**

- CSMA/CA: 802.11 wireless LANs
- TDMA: 2G digital cellular (GSM)
- FDMA: 2G analog cellular
- CDMA: 3G and 4G cellular (CDMA2000, UMTS, LTE)
