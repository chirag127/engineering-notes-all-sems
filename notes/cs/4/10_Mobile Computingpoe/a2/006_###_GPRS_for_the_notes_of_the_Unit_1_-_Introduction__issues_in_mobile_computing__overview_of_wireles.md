### GPRS for the notes of the Unit 1 - Introduction, issues in mobile computing, overview of wireless telephony: cellular concept, GSM: in the subject of Mobile Computing

GPRS stands for General Packet Radio Service. It is a packet oriented mobile data service on the 2G and 3G cellular communication system's global system for mobile communications (GSM). GPRS provides data rates from 56 kbit/s up to 114 kbit/s with continuous connection to the internet for mobile devices.

Advantages:

- Always on internet connectivity for mobile devices. Data is sent in packets so no circuit switching is required as in SMS or voice calls.
- Higher data rates than SMS and CSD.
- Billed based on amount of data transferred instead of time.
- Uses existing GSM infrastructure. So easier to implement and deploy.

Disadvantages:

- Slower than 3G and 4G networks.
- Shares bandwidth with GSM voice calls. So data speed decreases when more voice calls are active in a cell.
- Limited coverage in rural areas.

Applications:

- Wireless email and messaging.
- Internet browsing.
- Multimedia messaging service (MMS).

Learning tricks:

- Think G as in General, P as in Packet, R as in Radio, S as in Service to remember GPRS.
- Higher data rates than 2G SMS and CSD but slower than 3G and 4G. So place GPRS between 2G and 3G in terms of speed in the mind to remember its data rates.
