### QoS in Ad Hoc Networks

Quality of Service (QoS) in Ad Hoc Networks refers to the ability of the network to provide certain levels of service to different types of traffic. It is crucial for Ad Hoc Networks to ensure that different types of traffic, such as voice, video, and data, are delivered with appropriate levels of reliability, delay, and throughput.

Here are some important points to understand QoS in Ad Hoc Networks:

1. QoS Parameters: The main parameters that define QoS in Ad Hoc Networks are delay, jitter, packet loss, and throughput. These parameters are critical for ensuring that different types of traffic get the necessary levels of service.

2. QoS Techniques: Different techniques are used in Ad Hoc Networks to ensure QoS. These include scheduling algorithms, admission control, packet prioritization, and flow control.

3. Scheduling Algorithms: Scheduling algorithms are used to manage the transmission of packets in Ad Hoc Networks. The most common scheduling algorithms include Round Robin, Weighted Round Robin, Priority-Based, and Fair Queuing.

4. Admission Control: Admission control is used to ensure that the network can handle the traffic it receives. It involves checking whether the network has enough resources to support the new traffic before allowing it onto the network.

5. Packet Prioritization: Packet prioritization is used to ensure that high-priority traffic, such as voice and video, is given priority over lower-priority traffic, such as data. This helps to ensure that the high-priority traffic can be delivered with minimal delay and jitter.

6. Flow Control: Flow control is used to manage the flow of packets in Ad Hoc Networks. It involves controlling the rate at which packets are transmitted to ensure that the network is not overloaded.

Mnemonics and learning tricks:

- "DJP-T" can be used as a mnemonic to remember the QoS parameters: Delay, Jitter, Packet loss, and Throughput.
- "SPAF" can be used as a mnemonic to remember the QoS techniques: Scheduling algorithms, Packet prioritization, Admission control, and Flow control.
