## Unit 2 - Wireless Networking, Wireless LAN Overview: MAC issues, IEEE 802.11, Blue Tooth, Wireless

Wireless networking has become an essential part of our daily lives. From smartphones to laptops, almost every device we use today has the ability to connect wirelessly to the internet. This unit covers the basics of wireless networking, including MAC issues, IEEE 802.11, Bluetooth, and wireless LAN overview.

### MAC Issues

MAC (Media Access Control) is a protocol used to manage access to a shared network medium. Some of the common MAC issues in wireless networking include:

- Collision: When two or more devices try to send data at the same time, a collision can occur, which leads to data loss and network congestion.
- Hidden Node Problem: When two devices are too far from each other, they may not be able to detect each other's signals. This can cause data collision and loss.
- Exposed Node Problem: When a device detects the signal of another device that is transmitting data to a third device, it may refrain from sending data, even though it could do so without causing a collision.

### IEEE 802.11

IEEE 802.11 is a set of standards for wireless LANs (Local Area Networks) that cover wireless transmission protocols, security, and service sets. Some of the key features of IEEE 802.11 include:

- Multiple Access Points: IEEE 802.11 allows multiple access points to be connected to the same network, which increases the coverage area and reduces signal interference.
- Frequency Band: IEEE 802.11 operates on two frequency bands: 2.4 GHz and 5 GHz. The 2.4 GHz band has a longer range but is more susceptible to interference, while the 5 GHz band has a shorter range but is less susceptible to interference.
- Security: IEEE 802.11 provides several security protocols, including WEP, WPA, and WPA2, to protect wireless networks from unauthorized access.

### Bluetooth

Bluetooth is a wireless technology that allows devices to communicate with each other over short distances. Some of the key features of Bluetooth include:

- Low Power Consumption: Bluetooth uses low power consumption, which makes it ideal for devices like smartphones and wearables.
- Pairing: To establish a connection, Bluetooth devices need to be paired. Once paired, they can communicate with each other without the need for a password or PIN.
- Range: Bluetooth has a range of up to 10 meters, making it ideal for use in small spaces.

### Wireless LAN Overview

Wireless LANs are networks that use wireless signals to connect devices to the internet. Some of the advantages of wireless LANs include:

- Mobility: Wireless LANs allow users to connect to the internet without being tethered to a physical connection.
- Cost-Effective: Wireless LANs are often more cost-effective than wired networks, as they require less hardware and installation costs.
- Easy to Set Up: Wireless LANs can be set up quickly and easily, without the need for extensive cabling and wiring.

However, wireless LANs also have some disadvantages, including:

- Interference: Wireless LANs are susceptible to interference from other wireless signals, such as Bluetooth and Wi-Fi.
- Security: Wireless LANs are more vulnerable to security threats, such as hacking and unauthorized access, than wired networks.
- Range: The range of wireless LANs is limited, which can be a problem in larger spaces.
