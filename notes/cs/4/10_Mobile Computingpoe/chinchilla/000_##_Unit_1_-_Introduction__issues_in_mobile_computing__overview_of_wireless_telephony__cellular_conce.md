## Unit 1 - Introduction, issues in mobile computing, overview of wireless telephony: cellular concept, GSM:

Mobile computing has become an essential part of our daily lives, and wireless telephony plays a crucial role in it. In this unit, we will discuss the introduction of mobile computing, issues related to it, and the cellular concept of wireless telephony, with a focus on the Global System for Mobile Communications (GSM) standard.

### Introduction to Mobile Computing:

Mobile computing refers to the use of portable computing devices, such as smartphones, tablets, and laptops, to access and process information while on the move. It allows users to stay connected to the internet, access data, and perform various tasks from anywhere, at any time.

### Issues in Mobile Computing:

Mobile computing also poses several challenges and issues. Some of them are:

- Security concerns: Mobile devices are vulnerable to hacking, malware, and other security threats.
- Limited battery life: Mobile devices have limited battery life, which affects their usability and performance.
- Network connectivity: Mobile devices rely on wireless networks, which may have limited coverage or connectivity issues.
- Compatibility: Mobile devices may have compatibility issues with different operating systems, applications, and hardware.

### Overview of Wireless Telephony: 

Wireless telephony is a technology that allows voice and data communication over wireless networks. The cellular concept of wireless telephony divides a geographic area into cells, each served by a base station that communicates with mobile devices within its range.

### GSM (Global System for Mobile Communications):

GSM is a digital wireless communication standard that is widely used in mobile networks worldwide. It provides several advantages over earlier analog cellular systems, such as better call quality, more efficient use of bandwidth, and enhanced security.

Some of the key features of GSM are:

- Digital transmission: GSM uses digital transmission instead of analog, which improves call quality and reduces interference.
- SIM cards: GSM uses SIM (Subscriber Identity Module) cards to store user data and authenticate users on the network.
- Roaming: GSM allows users to use their phones in different countries and on different networks without changing their phone numbers.
- Encryption: GSM uses encryption techniques to secure voice and data communication and protect user privacy.

Mnemonics and Learning Tricks:

- To remember the issues in mobile computing, you can use the acronym "SLNC" for Security, Limited battery life, Network connectivity, and Compatibility.
- To remember the key features of GSM, you can use the acronym "DRRE" for Digital transmission, SIM cards, Roaming, and Encryption.
