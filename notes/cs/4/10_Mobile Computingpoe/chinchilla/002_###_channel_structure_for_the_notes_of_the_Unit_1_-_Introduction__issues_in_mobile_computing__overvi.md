### Channel Structure for the Notes of the Unit 1 - Introduction, Issues in Mobile Computing, Overview of Wireless Telephony: Cellular Concept, GSM

In mobile communication systems, a channel refers to a specific frequency band that is used for transmitting data between the mobile devices and the base station. The channel structure in mobile communication systems is designed to optimize the use of available spectrum while ensuring efficient and reliable communication.

The channel structure for the notes of the Unit 1 - Introduction, Issues in Mobile Computing, Overview of Wireless Telephony: Cellular Concept, GSM is based on the Global System for Mobile Communications (GSM) standard, which is widely used in Europe and other parts of the world. The GSM standard defines various types of channels that are used for different purposes in mobile communication systems.

The following are the different types of channels used in the GSM standard:

1. Traffic Channels (TCH): These channels are used for carrying voice or user data between the mobile device and the base station. The TCH channels are further divided into Full Rate (TCH/F) and Half Rate (TCH/H) channels, depending on the bandwidth required for the communication.

2. Control Channels: These channels are used for transmitting control information between the mobile device and the base station. The control channels are further divided into three types:

   a. Broadcast Control Channel (BCCH): This channel is used for broadcasting control information to all mobile devices in the cell, such as cell identity, frequency, and synchronization parameters.

   b. Common Control Channel (CCCH): This channel is used for transmitting control information between the mobile device and the base station, such as call setup and release, paging, and authentication.

   c. Dedicated Control Channel (DCCH): This channel is used for transmitting control information between the mobile device and the base station, such as handover, power control, and ciphering.

3. Synchronization Channels: These channels are used for synchronizing the mobile device with the base station. The synchronization channels are further divided into two types:

   a. Frequency Correction Channel (FCCH): This channel is used for synchronizing the frequency of the mobile device with the base station.

   b. Synchronization Channel (SCH): This channel is used for synchronizing the timing of the mobile device with the base station.

4. Logical Channels: These channels are used for carrying signaling information between the mobile device and the base station. The logical channels are further divided into two types:

   a. Control Logical Channel (CCH): This channel is used for carrying control signaling information between the mobile device and the base station.

   b. Traffic Logical Channel (TCH): This channel is used for carrying user data between the mobile device and the base station.
