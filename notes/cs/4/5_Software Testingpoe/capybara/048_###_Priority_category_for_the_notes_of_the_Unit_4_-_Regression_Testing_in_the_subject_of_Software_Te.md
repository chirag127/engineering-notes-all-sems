### Priority category for the notes of the Unit 4 - Regression Testing in the subject of Software Testing

In software testing, regression testing is an essential process that ensures that changes made to the software do not introduce new bugs or break existing functionality. It is a critical step in the software development life cycle and requires careful planning and execution. In this regard, prioritizing regression testing is crucial to ensure that the most critical areas of the software are tested first.

The priority category for the notes of the Unit 4 - Regression Testing can be divided into three categories - High, Medium, and Low. These categories are based on the importance of the functionality being tested and the impact of any defects found.

Here are the details of each of the priority categories:

#### High Priority

This category includes the most critical functionalities of the software that must be tested thoroughly to ensure that they are working as expected. These functionalities may include:

- Core functionalities of the software that directly impact the user experience.
- Critical business processes that involve financial transactions or customer data.
- Changes made to the software that have a significant impact on existing functionality.

Mnemonic: "H for High Priority - Hit the core functionalities first."

#### Medium Priority

This category includes functionalities that are important but not critical to the overall functioning of the software. These functionalities may include:

- Features that are frequently used by the end-users.
- Non-critical business processes.
- Changes made to the software that have a moderate impact on existing functionality.

Mnemonic: "M for Medium Priority - Manage the important features."

#### Low Priority

This category includes functionalities that have a low impact on the software's overall functioning. These functionalities may include:

- Features that are rarely used by the end-users.
- Non-critical functionalities that do not impact the business processes.
- Changes made to the software that have a minimal impact on existing functionality.

Mnemonic: "L for Low Priority - Leave the least important features to test later."

By prioritizing regression testing based on the above categories, testers can focus their efforts on the most critical areas of the software and ensure that they are thoroughly tested. This approach can help save time and resources while ensuring the software's quality and stability.
