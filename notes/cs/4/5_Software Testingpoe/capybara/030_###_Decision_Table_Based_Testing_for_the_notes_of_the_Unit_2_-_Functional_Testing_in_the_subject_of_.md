### Decision Table Based Testing for the notes of the Unit 2 - Functional Testing in the subject of Software Testing

Decision Table Based Testing is a black box testing technique that is used to test the correctness of a system or software application. In this technique, test cases are designed in the form of a decision table, which is a tabular representation of a set of rules and conditions that govern the behavior of the system. The decision table consists of three major components: conditions, actions, and rules.

#### Mnemonics and Learning Tricks

Unfortunately, there are no easy-to-remember mnemonics or learning tricks for Decision Table Based Testing. However, it is important to understand the concept thoroughly and practice designing decision tables in order to become proficient in this technique.

#### How to Design a Decision Table?

The following are the steps to design a decision table:

1. Identify the conditions: The first step in designing a decision table is to identify the conditions that affect the behavior of the system. These conditions can be either true or false and are represented in the table as columns.

2. Identify the actions: The next step is to identify the actions that the system can take based on the conditions. These actions are represented in the table as rows.

3. Define the rules: The rules define the relationship between the conditions and the actions. Each rule is represented in the table as a cell and specifies the action that the system should take when the conditions are met.

4. Test the decision table: Once the decision table is designed, it is tested by running test cases that cover all possible combinations of conditions and actions.

#### Advantages of Decision Table Based Testing

The following are the advantages of Decision Table Based Testing:

1. Provides a systematic approach to test case design.
2. Helps in identifying all possible combinations of conditions and actions.
3. Reduces the number of test cases required to achieve complete coverage.
4. Enhances the effectiveness and efficiency of testing.

#### Disadvantages of Decision Table Based Testing

The following are the disadvantages of Decision Table Based Testing:

1. Requires a thorough understanding of the system and its behavior.
2. Can be time-consuming and complex to design and maintain.
3. May not be suitable for complex systems with numerous conditions and actions.

#### Example of Decision Table Based Testing

Consider a system that calculates the bonus of employees based on their performance rating and the number of years of service. The decision table for this system would look like:

| Performance Rating | Years of Service | Bonus |
|---|---|---|
| High | >5 | 10% of salary |
| High | <=5 | 5% of salary |
| Medium | >5 | 5% of salary |
| Medium | <=5 | 2.5% of salary |
| Low | >5 | 2.5% of salary |
| Low | <=5 | No bonus |

#### Applications of Decision Table Based Testing

The following are the applications of Decision Table Based Testing:

1. Used in software testing to identify all possible outcomes of a system or application.
2. Can be used in decision-making processes to evaluate different options and outcomes.
3. Used in business analysis to identify the relationships between different factors and their impact on business decisions.

In conclusion, Decision Table Based Testing is a powerful and effective testing technique that helps in identifying all possible combinations of conditions and actions in a system or application. By following the steps outlined above and practicing the design of decision tables, you can become proficient in this technique and enhance the effectiveness and efficiency of your testing process.
