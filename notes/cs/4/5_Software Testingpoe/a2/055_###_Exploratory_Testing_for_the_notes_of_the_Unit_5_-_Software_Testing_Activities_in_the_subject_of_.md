### Exploratory Testing

- Exploratory Testing is an unscripted testing technique where testers dynamically design and execute tests.
- Testers explore the software to discover and learn about its behavior and potential issues.
- Key characteristics:
    - Unscripted - no predefined test cases or test scripts are used. Testers dynamically design and execute tests.
    - Mission-driven - testers are focused on exploring areas of the software and finding issues, not just executing tests.
    - Learning-oriented - the goal is to learn about the software and discover potential issues, not just verify requirements.
    - Discovery of issues is prioritized over test coverage.
- Advantages:
    - Effective at finding issues fast, especially usability issues.
    - Adjusts to changes easily since tests are not scripted.
    - Works well when documentation is lacking since testers explore and learn about the software.
- Disadvantages:
    - Difficult to ensure consistent test coverage over multiple exploratory testing sessions. Some areas may be missed.
    - Results and coverage may vary between testers since testing is unscripted.
    - Challenging to report and track issues found since tests are unstructured. Testers need to be diligent about note-taking.
- Applications:
    - Works well for usability testing and testing early prototypes.
    - Useful for testing complex software where exhaustive testing is not feasible and issues are difficult to anticipate.
    - Powerful technique to complement scripted testing, not replace it. Both techniques have pros and cons.
