### SDLC

Software Development Life Cycle (SDLC) is a process used by the software industry to design, develop and test high quality softwares. The SDLC aims to produce a high-quality software that meets or exceeds customer expectations, reaches completion within times and cost estimates.

The SDLC includes the following stages:

1. Requirements Analysis: The first phase where the project objectives and requirements are gathered from the customer. This is done through meetings, questionnaires, etc. The requirements are then analyzed for their validity and the possibility of incorporating them into the software.

2. Design: The software design is prepared based on the requirements gathered. The design phase aims to define the software architecture that meets the input requirements. The system design is broken down into modules that can be coded separately.

3. Implementation or Coding: The design is converted into code through programming using a suitable programming language. The programming is done module-wise and testing is performed for individual modules as well as the overall system during and after coding.

4. Testing: The software is tested thoroughly to identify any bugs or errors. It is tested against the requirements and the issues found are reported and fixed. The testing process continues until the software meets the quality standards.

5. Deployment: Once the software is tested and ready, it is deployed to the customer for use. The deployment process includes installation, configuration, and user training. Maintenance services are also provided to solve any issues faced by the users.

6. Maintenance: The software is continuously monitored and improved to fix any issues and to incorporate new features or updates required by the customer. The maintenance process continues throughout the life of the software.

Some advantages of using SDLC are:

- It leads to the development of high-quality software that meets customer needs.
- It reduces the risk of project failures and budget overruns through proper planning and reviews.
- It enables estimation of time and budget required for the project through careful planning.
- It leads to clear documentation of the software development process.

Some disadvantages are:

- It can be time-consuming for large projects due to excessive planning and documentation overhead.
- The requirements can change during the process leading to redesign and rework.
- The end product can be different from customer expectations if there are issues in the requirements gathering phase.
