
### Cloud Computing Architecture

1. Cloud computing is a type of computing that relies on sharing computing resources rather than having local servers or personal devices to handle applications. 
2. It is a model for enabling ubiquitous, convenient, on-demand network access to a shared pool of configurable computing resources (e.g., networks, servers, storage, applications, and services) that can be rapidly provisioned and released with minimal management effort or service provider interaction. 
3. Cloud computing architectures are designed to provide users with access to computing resources on demand, with the ability to scale up or down as needed. 
4. The most common cloud computing architectures are public clouds, private clouds, hybrid clouds, and community clouds. 
5. Public clouds are owned and operated by a third-party cloud provider, who manages the underlying infrastructure and provides services to multiple customers. 
6. Private clouds are owned and operated by an organization for their own use, and can be hosted on-premises or off-premises. 
7. Hybrid clouds are a combination of public and private clouds, and can be used to extend an organization’s existing infrastructure. 
8. Community clouds are shared by multiple organizations and provide a secure environment for sharing resources. 
9. Cloud computing architectures provide organizations with the ability to rapidly provision, configure, and deploy applications and services. 
10. They also provide improved resource utilization and cost savings, as well as increased flexibility and scalability.
