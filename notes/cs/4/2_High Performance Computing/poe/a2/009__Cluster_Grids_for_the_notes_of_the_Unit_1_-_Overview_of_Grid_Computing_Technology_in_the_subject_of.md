### Cluster Grids

* A cluster is a group of computers that work together closely so that in many respects they can be viewed as a single system.
* Cluster computing is a type of computing in which a group of computers are connected together to provide increased computational power and redundancy.
* The components of a cluster are commonly, but not always, connected to each other through fast local area networks.
* Clusters are usually set up to provide a higher level of performance by splitting up and distributing pieces of a program among multiple computers.
* One of the major motivations for adopting cluster architectures is cost-effectiveness. Compared to specially designed, high-end supercomputers, cluster systems are generally cheaper to build and maintain.
* A computational grid is a heterogeneous network which consists of a collection of different resources. These resources can be supercomputers, clusters, desktop machines, data storage devices or sensors etc.
* The resources in a grid are typically geographically distributed and owned by different organizations with varying security domains and policies.
* The main feature of grid systems is that they provide unified access to vast computational resources, while taking into account security aspects and policies of participating organizations.
* The goal of grid computing is to enable the sharing, selection, and aggregation of geographically distributed resources depending on their availability, capability, performance, cost, and users' quality-of-service requirements.
