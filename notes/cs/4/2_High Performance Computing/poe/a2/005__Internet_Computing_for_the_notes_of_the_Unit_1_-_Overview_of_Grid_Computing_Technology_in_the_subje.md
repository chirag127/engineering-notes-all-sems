### Internet Computing for the notes of the Unit 1 - Overview of Grid Computing Technology in the subject of High Performance Computing

1. Internet Computing refers to the use of internet and web technologies to perform computing. It utilizes the internet for communication, computation and data access.
2. The key features of Internet Computing are:
    - Heterogeneity: The resources connected over the internet are diverse and heterogeneous in nature including computers, databases, sensors, etc.
    - Scalability: The scale of internet computing is huge with billions of resources and users connected. The systems must be able to scale to large sizes.
    - Openness: The internet is open and the resources are contributed voluntarily. The systems must be able to handle unreliable resources and openness.
    - Ubiquity: The internet and the resources are ubiquitous making the computing infrastructure pervasive.
3. The key applications of internet computing include:
    - Grid computing: Pooling resources from multiple organizations to solve large-scale problems.
    - Peer-to-peer computing: Sharing resources and services between peers.
    - Cloud computing: Providing on-demand access to shared computing resources.
    - Distributed sensing and monitoring: Using sensors and resources across the internet.
    - Social networks: Building applications enabling people to connect and collaborate.
4. The key research challenges in internet computing include:
    - Heterogeneity and interoperability: Using diverse, heterogeneous resources in a seamless manner.
    - Reliability and fault tolerance: Handling unreliable and varying resources and services.
    - Scalability: Scaling systems to handle billions of resources and users.
    - Performance: Optimizing performance with complex software stacks and unpredictable networks.
    - Security and privacy: Ensuring security and privacy in open, distributed systems.
    - Data management: Managing data in distributed and high-volume data systems.
    - Programming models: Providing programming models to simplify development of internet-scale applications.
