### Services for the notes of the Unit 5 - Overview of Cloud Computing in the subject of High Performance Computing

- Cloud computing is the delivery of different services through the Internet, such as data storage, servers, databases, networking, software, analytics, and intelligence.
- Cloud computing services offer faster innovation, flexible resources, and economies of scale, as well as reduced costs, increased efficiency, and improved security.
- There are different types of cloud computing services, depending on the level of abstraction and the degree of control the user has over the resources. These are:
  - Infrastructure as a Service (IaaS): The user can access and manage the basic computing resources, such as servers, storage, network, and operating systems, but the cloud provider is responsible for maintaining and updating them. Examples of IaaS providers are Amazon Web Services (AWS), Microsoft Azure, and Google Cloud Platform (GCP).
  - Platform as a Service (PaaS): The user can deploy and run their own applications and software on the cloud platform, without worrying about the underlying infrastructure or middleware. The cloud provider offers a range of tools and services to support the development, testing, and deployment of applications. Examples of PaaS providers are Heroku, Google App Engine, and Azure App Service.
  - Software as a Service (SaaS): The user can access and use software applications that are hosted and managed by the cloud provider, without installing or maintaining them on their own devices. The cloud provider handles the security, performance, and availability of the applications. Examples of SaaS providers are Gmail, Salesforce, and Microsoft Office 365.
  - Function as a Service (FaaS): The user can execute small pieces of code or functions on the cloud platform, without provisioning or managing any servers or infrastructure. The cloud provider automatically scales and runs the functions in response to events or triggers. Examples of FaaS providers are AWS Lambda, Azure Functions, and Google Cloud Functions.
- There are also different models of cloud computing, depending on the location and ownership of the cloud resources. These are:
  - Public cloud: The cloud resources are owned and operated by a third-party cloud provider, and are available to the general public or multiple customers over the Internet. Examples of public cloud providers are AWS, Azure, and GCP.
  - Private cloud: The cloud resources are owned and operated by a single organization, and are used exclusively by its members or customers. The private cloud can be located on the organization's premises or hosted by a third-party cloud provider. Examples of private cloud providers are VMware, IBM Cloud, and Oracle Cloud.
  - Hybrid cloud: The cloud resources are a combination of public and private clouds, and are connected by a secure network. The hybrid cloud allows the organization to leverage the benefits of both models, such as scalability, cost-efficiency, security, and flexibility. Examples of hybrid cloud providers are AWS Outposts, Azure Arc, and Google Anthos.
