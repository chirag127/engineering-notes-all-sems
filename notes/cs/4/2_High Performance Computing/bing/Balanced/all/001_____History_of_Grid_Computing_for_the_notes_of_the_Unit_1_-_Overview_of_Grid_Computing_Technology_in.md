# History of Grid Computing

- Grid computing is a form of distributed computing that allows multiple computers to share resources and work together on a common task.
- The term grid computing originated in the early 1990s as a metaphor for making computer power as easy to access as an electric power grid.
- The concept of grid computing was developed by Steve Tuecke, Ian Foster, and Carl Kesselman, who created the Globus Toolkit, a standard for building and managing grids.
- The Globus Toolkit provided tools for data transfer, resource allocation, security, monitoring, and execution management on grids.
- The first grid projects were mainly focused on scientific applications, such as high-energy physics, astronomy, and bioinformatics.
- Some examples of early grid projects are the European DataGrid, the NASA Information Power Grid, the TeraGrid, and the Earth System Grid.
- Grid computing also enabled the emergence of volunteer computing, where users donate their idle computing resources to support scientific or humanitarian causes.
- Some examples of volunteer computing projects are SETI@home, Folding@home, and BOINC.
- Grid computing evolved over time to address the challenges and opportunities of new technologies, such as cloud computing, big data, and the Internet of Things.
- Grid computing also expanded to cover different domains and applications, such as business, education, health, and social sciences.
- Some examples of current grid projects are the Open Science Grid, the European Grid Infrastructure, the Worldwide LHC Computing Grid, and the e-Science Central.
