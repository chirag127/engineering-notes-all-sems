# Types of Grids

Grid computing is a distributed computing paradigm that involves a network of computers that share resources and work together to solve a common problem. Grid computing can be classified into different types based on the nature of the problem, the data, and the collaboration involved. Here are some of the common types of grids:

- **Computational grid**: This is a type of grid that acts as a mediator of many computers in a given network to solve one single problem at a time. The problem is divided into smaller subtasks that are distributed among the available computers, which perform the computation and return the results to the mediator. Computational grids are useful for solving large-scale, CPU-intensive problems that require high performance and parallel processing. Examples of computational grids are SETI@home, Folding@home, and LHC@home .

- **Data grid**: The grid that deals with the sharing and managing the distributed data in a controlled manner is termed as a data grid. Data grids provide access to large volumes of data that are stored across multiple locations, and enable data replication, caching, migration, and synchronization. Data grids are useful for applications that require data-intensive processing and analysis, such as scientific experiments, data mining, and business intelligence. Examples of data grids are Earth System Grid, Globus Data Grid, and Hazelcast In-Memory Data Grid .

- **Collaborative grid**: Such types of grids help in solving collective problems that involve multiple users or organizations that share resources, data, and expertise. Collaborative grids enable communication, coordination, and collaboration among the participants, and support applications such as video conferencing, online gaming, and e-learning. Examples of collaborative grids are Access Grid, Collaboratory, and Gridbus Broker .

- **Service grid**: The grid that provides access to various services that are distributed across the network is called a service grid. Service grids use web services and service-oriented architecture (SOA) to expose and invoke the functionalities of the grid resources. Service grids are useful for applications that require dynamic and flexible service composition and orchestration, such as e-commerce, e-government, and e-health. Examples of service grids are Globus Toolkit, OGSA, and WSRF .
