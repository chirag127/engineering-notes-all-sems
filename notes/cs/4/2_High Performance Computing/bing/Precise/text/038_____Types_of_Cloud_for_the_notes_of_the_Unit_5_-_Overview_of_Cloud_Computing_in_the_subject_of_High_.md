### Types of Cloud

Clouds are classified into types based on their shape, altitude, and thickness. The classification of clouds into types was first proposed by Luke Howard in 1802 and we largely use the same system today. This splits clouds into three main types - stratus, cumulus, and cirrus, plus a fourth special type, nimbus .

1. **Stratus clouds** are low-level clouds that are usually uniform and flat, producing a gray layer of cloud cover which may be precipitation-free or may cause periods of light precipitation or drizzle.

2. **Cumulus clouds** are detached, generally dense clouds and have sharp outlines. They are usually white, and their tops are dome-shaped with protuberances while their bases are horizontal.

3. **Cirrus clouds** are high clouds that are thin, wispy, and composed of ice crystals. They are often white and predict fair weather.

4. **Nimbus clouds** are clouds that produce precipitation. They are often dark and featureless, and can be seen in various forms, including as a featureless layer or as individual, towering clouds.

Clouds can also be grouped by the altitude level or levels in the troposphere at which they are normally found. The list of cloud types groups all cloud genera as high (cirro-, cirrus), middle (alto-), multi-level (nimbo-, cumulo-, cumulus), and low (strato-, stratus).
