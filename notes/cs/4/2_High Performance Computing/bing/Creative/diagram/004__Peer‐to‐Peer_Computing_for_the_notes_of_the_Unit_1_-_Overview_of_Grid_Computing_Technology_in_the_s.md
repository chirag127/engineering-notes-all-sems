Peer-to-peer computing is a distributed application architecture that partitions tasks or workloads between peers, which are equally privileged, equipotent participants in the network. Peers can share resources, such as processing power, disk storage, or network bandwidth, directly with other peers, without the need for a central server or a stable host. Peer-to-peer computing can be used for various purposes, such as file sharing, communication, collaboration, or distributed computing.

Each peer can act as both a client and a server, requesting and providing services or resources to other peers. The peers are connected by a network, such as the Internet, and can communicate using a common protocol, such as Gnutella or BitTorrent. The peers can join or leave the network at any time, and the network can dynamically adjust to the changes. The peers can also form groups or clusters based on their interests, preferences, or capabilities, and cooperate with each other to achieve a common goal.
