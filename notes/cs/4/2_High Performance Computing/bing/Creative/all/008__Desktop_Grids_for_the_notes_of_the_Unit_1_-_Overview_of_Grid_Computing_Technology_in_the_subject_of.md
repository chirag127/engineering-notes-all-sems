### Desktop Grids for the notes of the Unit 1 - Overview of Grid Computing Technology in the subject of High Performance Computing

- Desktop Grids are a type of Grid Computing that use the idle CPU time of geographically distributed personal computers (PCs) connected over general-purpose networks (such as the Internet) to perform computationally intensive tasks.
- Desktop Grids are also known as Volunteer Computing, Peer-to-Peer Computing, or Cycle Stealing .
- Desktop Grids are different from other types of Grid Computing such as Cluster Computing or Cloud Computing in terms of the following aspects :
  - **Heterogeneity**: Desktop Grids are composed of diverse and dynamic sets of PCs with varying hardware, software, and network characteristics.
  - **Reliability**: Desktop Grids are prone to failures and interruptions due to the voluntary and non-dedicated nature of the PCs. PCs may join or leave the Desktop Grid at any time, or may not return the results of the tasks assigned to them.
  - **Availability**: Desktop Grids have unpredictable and fluctuating availability of resources, depending on the usage patterns and preferences of the PC owners. PCs may be idle for long periods of time, or may be busy with other applications.
  - **Security**: Desktop Grids have lower security and privacy guarantees than other types of Grid Computing, as the PCs are not under the control of a single organization or authority. PCs may be malicious or compromised, and may try to tamper with the tasks or the results.
  - **Communication**: Desktop Grids have higher communication latency and lower bandwidth than other types of Grid Computing, as the PCs are connected over general-purpose networks that may be congested or unreliable. PCs may also have firewalls or NATs that limit the communication capabilities.
- Desktop Grids are suitable for solving **Bag-of-Tasks** problems, which are composed of a large number of independent and identical tasks that can be executed in parallel without any communication or synchronization .
- Desktop Grids are not suitable for solving problems that require frequent or intensive communication, synchronization, or coordination among the tasks, such as parallel algorithms or distributed databases.
- Desktop Grids are based on a **server-client** architecture, where a central server generates and distributes the tasks to the client PCs, and collects and validates the results .
- Desktop Grids use a software platform or middleware to manage and coordinate the tasks and the resources, such as BOINC (Berkeley Open Infrastructure for Desktop Computing), which is the most popular and widely used platform for Desktop Grids .
- Desktop Grids have many advantages, such as :
  - **Cost-effectiveness**: Desktop Grids can provide a large amount of computing power at a low cost, by utilizing the existing and underutilized resources of PCs.
  - **Scalability**: Desktop Grids can scale up to millions of PCs, and can handle very large and complex problems that would otherwise be infeasible or impractical to solve on other types of Grid Computing.
  - **Flexibility**: Desktop Grids can adapt to the dynamic and heterogeneous nature of the resources, and can support a variety of applications and domains.
  - **Social impact**: Desktop Grids can enable the participation and contribution of the general public to scientific and humanitarian projects, and can foster the awareness and education of the society on these topics.
- Desktop Grids have many challenges, such as :
  - **Performance**: Desktop Grids have to deal with the variability and unpredictability of the resources, and have to optimize the task scheduling and load balancing strategies to achieve high performance and efficiency.
  - **Quality**: Desktop Grids have to ensure the correctness and validity of the results, and have to cope with the possibility of errors, failures, or malicious behavior of the resources.
  - **Security**: Desktop Grids have to protect the confidentiality and integrity of the tasks and the results, and have to prevent or mitigate the attacks or threats from the resources or the network.
  - **Usability**: Desktop Grids have to provide user-friendly and intuitive interfaces and tools for the users, both the project owners and the resource providers, and have to support the interoperability and compatibility of the software and the hardware.
