### Types of Grids for the notes of the Unit 1 - Overview of Grid Computing Technology in the subject of High Performance Computing

Grid computing is a distributed architecture that uses a group of computers to combine resources and work together to accomplish a joint task. Grid computing can be seen as a special type of parallel computing that relies on complete computers connected to a network by a conventional network interface. Grid computing can be used for computationally intensive scientific, mathematical, and academic problems as well as for commercial applications.  

Grids can be classified into four categories depending on their usage and usefulness. They are: 

- **Computational Grids**: These grids provide access to a large amount of processing power by harnessing the idle cycles of multiple computers. Computational grids are useful for applications that require a lot of CPU time, such as simulations, modeling, and data analysis. Computational grids can be further divided into two types: 
  - **CPU Scavenging Grids**: These grids use the spare cycles of desktop computers or workstations that are otherwise idle or underutilized. CPU scavenging grids are also known as desktop grids or volunteer computing. Examples of CPU scavenging grids are SETI@home, Folding@home, and BOINC. 
  - **High-Throughput Grids**: These grids use dedicated clusters of servers or supercomputers to provide high-performance computing for large-scale applications. High-throughput grids are also known as cluster grids or production grids. Examples of high-throughput grids are TeraGrid, EGEE, and Open Science Grid. 
- **Data Grids**: These grids provide access to a large amount of distributed data by managing the storage, replication, transfer, and analysis of data. Data grids are useful for applications that require a lot of data, such as scientific experiments, digital libraries, and e-commerce. Data grids can be further divided into two types: 
  - **Data Access Grids**: These grids provide efficient and secure access to distributed data sources, such as databases, file systems, and web services. Data access grids are also known as data federation grids or data integration grids. Examples of data access grids are OGSA-DAI, Globus Data Grid, and SRB. 
  - **Data Management Grids**: These grids provide reliable and scalable storage and replication of distributed data, such as files, documents, and images. Data management grids are also known as data storage grids or data replication grids. Examples of data management grids are iRODS, GridFTP, and BitTorrent. 
- **On-Demand Grids**: These grids provide access to a large amount of dynamic and heterogeneous resources by provisioning and configuring them on demand. On-demand grids are useful for applications that require a lot of flexibility, scalability, and adaptability, such as web hosting, cloud computing, and service-oriented computing. On-demand grids can be further divided into two types: 
  - **Resource Grids**: These grids provide access to various types of resources, such as hardware, software, network, and storage, by allocating and releasing them on demand. Resource grids are also known as utility grids or service grids. Examples of resource grids are Amazon EC2, Google Cloud Platform, and Microsoft Azure. 
  - **Application Grids**: These grids provide access to various types of applications, such as web applications, scientific applications, and business applications, by deploying and executing them on demand. Application grids are also known as application service grids or application hosting grids. Examples of application grids are Salesforce.com, Google App Engine, and IBM WebSphere. 
- **Management Grids**: These grids provide access to a large amount of management and coordination capabilities by automating and optimizing the operation of grids. Management grids are useful for applications that require a lot of administration, monitoring, and control, such as grid security, grid scheduling, and grid accounting. Management grids can be further divided into two types: 
  - **Security Grids**: These grids provide secure and trustworthy access to grid resources and data by enforcing policies and mechanisms for authentication, authorization, encryption, and auditing. Security grids are also known as security service grids or security infrastructure grids. Examples of security grids are GSI, PKI, and Kerberos.
