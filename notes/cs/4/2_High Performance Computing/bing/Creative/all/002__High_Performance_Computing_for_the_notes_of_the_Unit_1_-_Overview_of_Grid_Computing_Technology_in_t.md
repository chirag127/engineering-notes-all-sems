### High Performance Computing for the notes of the Unit 1 - Overview of Grid Computing Technology in the subject of High Performance Computing

- High Performance Computing (HPC) is the use of large numbers of CPU or GPU-based computers to solve complex mathematical tasks in real time or near-real time .
- HPC systems can perform at speeds more than one million times faster than the fastest commodity desktop, laptop or server systems.
- HPC applications can scale to thousands of compute cores, extend on-premises clusters, or run as a 100% cloud-native solution.
- HPC workloads uncover important new insights that advance human knowledge and create significant competitive advantage in various domains such as genomics, oil and gas, finance, engineering, weather modeling, etc .
- HPC leverages massively parallel computing, which runs multiple tasks simultaneously on multiple computer servers or processors.
- HPC clusters consist of multiple high-speed computer servers networked together, with a centralized scheduler that manages the parallel computing workload.
- HPC clusters use either high-performance multi-core CPUs or GPUs, which are well suited for rigorous mathematical calculations, machine learning models and graphics-intensive tasks.
- HPC clusters also use high-speed, high-throughput and low-latency components for networking, memory, storage and file systems to optimize the computing power and performance of the cluster.
- HPC in the cloud offers a faster, more scalable and more affordable way for organizations to take advantage of HPC.
- Grid computing is a type of computing where it constitutes a network of computers that work together to perform tasks that may be difficult for a single machine to handle .
- Grid computing is different from HPC in that the resources in grid are owned by different organizations which have their own policies, computation capability, framework, and cost and access model.
- Grid computing is also different from HPC in that the tasks in grid are usually loosely coupled and independent, while the tasks in HPC are tightly coupled and interdependent.
- Grid computing can be seen as a form of distributed computing, where the resources are geographically dispersed and heterogeneous.
- Grid computing can provide high performance computing in several scientific environments, such as aircrafts and weapons design, by utilizing the idle resources of the grid.
- Grid computing can also provide high availability, load balancing, fault tolerance, and scalability to the applications running on the grid.
