### Peer‐to‐Peer Computing for the notes of the Unit 1 - Overview of Grid Computing Technology in the subject of High Performance Computing

- Peer-to-peer (P2P) computing is a decentralized communications model in which each party has the same capabilities and either party can initiate a communication session  .
- Unlike the client-server model, in which the client makes a service request and the server fulfills the request, the P2P network model enables each node to function as both a client and server  .
- P2P systems can provide anonymized routing of network traffic, massive parallel computing environments, distributed storage and other functions .
- Most P2P programs focus on media sharing, so P2P mostly associates with software piracy and copyright violation.
- P2P applications typically enable users to control many parameters of operation, such as how many member connections to seek or allow at one time, which systems to connect or avoid, which services to offer, and how many system resources to devote to the network.
- Some P2P applications simply connect to some subset of active nodes in the network with little user control.
- P2P networking topologies have existed since the days of Advanced Research Projects Agency Network, but the advantages of the P2P communications model didn't become obvious to the general public until the late 1990s when music-sharing P2P applications, like Napster, appeared .
- Napster and its successors, such as Gnutella and BitTorrent, cut into music and movie industry profits and changed how people thought about acquiring and consuming media .
- System administrators tend to discourage the use of P2P applications for a few reasons. For one, P2P applications can tie up bandwidth. P2P applications can also bypass firewalls and distribute malware, which can possibly expose an administrator's organization legally .
- Network administrators often set up networks to prevent PCs from engaging in P2P side talk.
- When a user downloads a file through a P2P network, the file downloads in bits that come from various computers in the network that already have the file. Simultaneously, data also travels from the user's computer to other computers in the network that ask for it .
- This situation is similar to a two-way street -- the file is like numerous small bits of data that come to the user's computer but also leave when requested .

#### Advantages of P2P computing

- P2P computing can reduce the cost and complexity of maintaining a centralized server and network infrastructure .
- P2P computing can increase the scalability, reliability, and performance of the network by distributing the workload and data among multiple nodes .
- P2P computing can enhance the privacy and security of the users by encrypting the data and anonymizing the traffic .
- P2P computing can enable the sharing of diverse and dynamic resources, such as CPU cycles, disk space, bandwidth, and content, among different users and applications .
- P2P computing can foster the creation of new and innovative services, such as file sharing, distributed computing, collaborative work, and social networking .

#### Disadvantages of P2P computing

- P2P computing can pose legal and ethical issues, such as copyright infringement, piracy, and censorship, due to the lack of centralized control and regulation .
- P2P computing can expose the users and the network to various security risks, such as malware, viruses, worms, spyware, and denial-of-service attacks, due to the openness and anonymity of the system .
- P2P computing can degrade the quality of service and user experience, due to the variability and unpredictability of the network conditions, such as latency, bandwidth, availability, and reliability .
- P2P computing can increase the overhead and complexity of the system, due to the need for additional protocols, algorithms, and mechanisms to coordinate, discover, and manage the resources and the nodes .
