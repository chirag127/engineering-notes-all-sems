## Unit 3 - Overview of Cluster Computing

- Cluster computing is a form of parallel computing that involves connecting multiple computers, called nodes, to work together as a single system.
- Cluster computing can improve the performance, availability, scalability, and reliability of applications that require high computational power or large data processing.
- Cluster computing can be classified into different types based on the architecture, functionality, and application domain of the nodes, such as:
  - High-performance computing (HPC) clusters: These clusters are designed to run computationally intensive tasks, such as scientific simulations, machine learning, and data analysis. HPC clusters typically use fast interconnects, such as InfiniBand or Ethernet, to achieve low latency and high bandwidth communication among the nodes. HPC clusters may also use specialized hardware, such as GPUs or FPGAs, to accelerate specific operations.
  - High-availability (HA) clusters: These clusters are designed to provide continuous service and fault tolerance for critical applications, such as web servers, databases, and file systems. HA clusters use redundant hardware and software components, such as power supplies, disks, network interfaces, and heartbeat mechanisms, to detect and recover from failures. HA clusters may also use load balancing and failover techniques to distribute the workload and ensure availability.
  - Load-balancing clusters: These clusters are designed to balance the incoming requests or traffic among multiple nodes, such as web servers, application servers, or proxy servers. Load-balancing clusters can improve the responsiveness, scalability, and reliability of the applications by distributing the load and avoiding overloading or underutilizing the nodes. Load-balancing clusters may use different algorithms, such as round-robin, least-connection, or weighted, to assign the requests to the nodes based on various criteria, such as availability, capacity, or priority.
  - Grid computing clusters: These clusters are designed to share and utilize the idle resources of geographically distributed and heterogeneous nodes, such as desktops, laptops, or servers. Grid computing clusters can enable the execution of large-scale and complex tasks, such as scientific experiments, collaborative projects, or distributed applications, by harnessing the collective power of the nodes. Grid computing clusters may use middleware, such as Globus Toolkit or Condor, to manage the resource discovery, allocation, scheduling, and communication among the nodes.
  - Cloud computing clusters: These clusters are designed to provide on-demand and scalable access to the computing resources, such as servers, storage, networks, or software, over the internet. Cloud computing clusters can offer various benefits, such as cost-effectiveness, elasticity, reliability, and security, to the users who can rent the resources as per their needs and pay only for what they use. Cloud computing clusters may use different service models, such as Infrastructure as a Service (IaaS), Platform as a Service (PaaS), or Software as a Service (SaaS), to deliver the resources to the users.
