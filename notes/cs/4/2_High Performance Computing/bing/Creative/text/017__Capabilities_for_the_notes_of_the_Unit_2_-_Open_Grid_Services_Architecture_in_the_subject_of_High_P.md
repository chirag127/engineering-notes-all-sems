### Capabilities

The Open Grid Services Architecture (OGSA) defines a set of core capabilities and behaviors that address key concerns in grid systems. OGSA uses most of Web service technologies, notably WSDL and SOAP, but it aims to be largely agnostic in relation to the transport-level handling of data upon the grid. 

According to the OGSA document, Version 1.5, the following capabilities are described: 

- **Infrastructure services**: These are the basic services that provide the foundation for the grid system, such as naming, messaging, notification, logging, and fault handling.
- **Execution Management services**: These are the services that manage the execution of user tasks on the grid, such as job submission, scheduling, monitoring, and accounting.
- **Data services**: These are the services that manage the access, transfer, replication, and integration of data on the grid, such as data sources, catalogs, and brokers.
- **Resource Management services**: These are the services that manage the allocation and sharing of resources on the grid, such as processors, storage, networks, and software licenses.
- **Security services**: These are the services that provide the security mechanisms for the grid, such as authentication, authorization, encryption, and auditing.
- **Self-management services**: These are the services that enable the grid to adapt to changing conditions and requirements, such as configuration, deployment, discovery, and optimization.
- **Information services**: These are the services that provide the information about the grid components, such as their capabilities, status, performance, and availability.

These capabilities are not mutually exclusive, and some services may provide more than one capability. For example, a data service may also provide resource management and self-management capabilities.
