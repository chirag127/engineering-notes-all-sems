### Applications and Architectures of High Performance Grids

- High performance grids are distributed systems that enable the sharing and coordination of large-scale computational resources across different organizations and domains.
- High performance grids can support various applications that require high throughput, high availability, high scalability, and high reliability, such as scientific simulations, data analysis, e-science, e-business, and e-government.
- High performance grids can also enable the integration of heterogeneous and legacy systems, the collaboration of geographically dispersed teams, and the access to remote data and services.
- High performance grids typically consist of the following layers :
  - The **fabric layer** provides the basic hardware and software resources, such as processors, memory, storage, networks, operating systems, middleware, and libraries.
  - The **connectivity layer** provides the communication and security protocols and services, such as TCP/IP, SSL, SSH, and MPI.
  - The **resource layer** provides the mechanisms for discovering, monitoring, allocating, and managing the resources, such as directories, brokers, schedulers, and agents.
  - The **collective layer** provides the services and tools for coordinating and optimizing the use of resources, such as load balancing, fault tolerance, data replication, and workflow management.
  - The **application layer** provides the grid applications and development toolkits for supporting the applications. Grid users interface with this layer and also provide general management functions and auditing functions.
- High performance grids can have different architectures depending on the scope, scale, and purpose of the grid. Some common types of grid architectures are :
  - **Cluster grids** are composed of clusters of tightly coupled computers that are connected by high-speed networks. Cluster grids are suitable for parallel and distributed applications that require low latency and high bandwidth communication.
  - **Campus grids** are composed of clusters and individual computers that are connected by local area networks within a campus or an organization. Campus grids are suitable for applications that require moderate communication and can tolerate some heterogeneity and dynamism of the resources.
  - **Enterprise grids** are composed of clusters and individual computers that are connected by wide area networks across different campuses or organizations within a single enterprise. Enterprise grids are suitable for applications that require high availability and reliability and can leverage the existing IT infrastructure and policies of the enterprise.
  - **Global grids** are composed of clusters and individual computers that are connected by the Internet across different enterprises and domains. Global grids are suitable for applications that require high scalability and diversity and can benefit from the collaboration and sharing of resources among different communities.
