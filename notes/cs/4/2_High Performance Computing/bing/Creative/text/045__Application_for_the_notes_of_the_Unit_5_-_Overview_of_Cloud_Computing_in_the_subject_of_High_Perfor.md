### Application for the notes of the Unit 5 - Overview of Cloud Computing in the subject of High Performance Computing

- Cloud computing is the delivery of computing services over the internet, such as servers, storage, databases, networking, software, analytics, and intelligence.
- Cloud computing offers various benefits, such as scalability, elasticity, cost-efficiency, reliability, security, and innovation.
- Cloud computing can be classified into three service models: Infrastructure as a Service (IaaS), Platform as a Service (PaaS), and Software as a Service (SaaS).
  - IaaS provides the basic computing resources, such as virtual machines, storage, networks, and operating systems, that can be rented and managed by the user.
  - PaaS provides the platform for developing, testing, deploying, and managing applications, without the need to manage the underlying infrastructure.
  - SaaS provides the software applications that are hosted and managed by the cloud provider, and can be accessed by the user through a web browser or a mobile app.
- Cloud computing can also be classified into four deployment models: public cloud, private cloud, hybrid cloud, and community cloud.
  - Public cloud is the cloud service that is available to the general public over the internet, and is owned and operated by a cloud provider.
  - Private cloud is the cloud service that is exclusively used by a single organization, and can be hosted on-premises or off-premises by a cloud provider or a third party.
  - Hybrid cloud is the combination of public cloud and private cloud, that allows the data and applications to move between them, depending on the needs and policies of the organization.
  - Community cloud is the cloud service that is shared by a group of organizations that have common goals or interests, and can be hosted on-premises or off-premises by a cloud provider or a third party.
- Cloud computing faces various challenges, such as data privacy, security, compliance, interoperability, portability, performance, availability, and vendor lock-in.
  - Data privacy refers to the protection of the personal and sensitive data of the users and the organizations from unauthorized access, use, or disclosure by the cloud provider or other parties.
  - Security refers to the protection of the data and the applications from malicious attacks, such as denial-of-service, malware, phishing, or data breaches, by implementing various measures, such as encryption, authentication, authorization, firewalls, and intrusion detection systems.
  - Compliance refers to the adherence of the cloud provider and the user to the laws, regulations, standards, and policies that govern the data and the applications in the cloud, such as GDPR, HIPAA, PCI DSS, or ISO 27001.
  - Interoperability refers to the ability of the data and the applications to communicate and exchange information with other systems or platforms in the cloud or outside the cloud, using common protocols, formats, and APIs.
  - Portability refers to the ability of the data and the applications to be moved or transferred from one cloud provider or platform to another, without significant changes or loss of functionality.
  - Performance refers to the quality of the service and the user experience in the cloud, such as the speed, latency, throughput, availability, and reliability of the data and the applications.
  - Availability refers to the degree to which the cloud service is accessible and operational at any given time, and is measured by metrics such as uptime, downtime, or SLA (service level agreement).
  - Vendor lock-in refers to the difficulty or cost of switching from one cloud provider or platform to another, due to the dependence on the proprietary features, standards, or APIs of the cloud provider.
