## Unit 3 - Service-Oriented Applications

- Service-Oriented Applications are software applications that are built as a combination of loosely coupled services. These services can be reusable software components that are made accessible over a network (typically, the Internet) to provide business functionality.
- Some key characteristics of Service-Oriented Applications are:
- They are built using services that are loosely coupled, i.e. the services have little to no dependencies on each other.
- The services can be discovered via directories and registries, and can be used/reused multiple times.
- Services use open standards (like HTTP, XML, SOAP, WSDL) to enable communication and interoperability.
- Services can be combined into so-called composite applications or business processes to automate tasks.

Mnemonics:
- Think of services as LEGO blocks that can be plugged together in different ways to build applications.
- SOA is about LOC - Loose Coupling and Open standards.

Advantages:
- Increased reusability of services and reduced duplication.
- Interoperability between systems and platforms.
- Flexibility and agility to change. Individual services can evolve independently.
- Potential for cost reduction and increased ROI.

Disadvantages:
- Can be complex to design, develop and deploy.
- Challenges with service discovery, composition, and versioning.
- Potential performance overhead due to network latency.
- Security considerations since services are accessed over a network.
