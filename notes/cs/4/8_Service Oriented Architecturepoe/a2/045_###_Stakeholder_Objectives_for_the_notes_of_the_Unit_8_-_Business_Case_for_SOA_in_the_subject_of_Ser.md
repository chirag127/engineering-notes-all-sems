### Stakeholder Objectives for the notes of the Unit 8 - Business Case for SOA in the subject of Service Oriented Architecture

- **Reduce Cost**: SOA reduces cost through reuse of services and increased flexibility. Shared services can be reused by multiple applications, reducing redundant development effort and costs.
- **Increase Agility**: SOA enables increased business agility through flexible, adaptable systems and processes. Services can be reconfigured and recomposed to support changing business needs.
- **Simplify Integration**: SOA enables integration of systems and applications through the use of standards-based, reusable services. This simplifies integration compared to traditional point-to-point integration approaches.
- **Enable Business Alignment**: An SOA can be designed to directly support business processes and goals. This alignment of IT and business provides business value and can improve business-IT collaboration.
- **Leverage Existing Assets**: Services can be constructed from existing applications, databases, and other assets, allowing those assets to be leveraged in new ways. This can provide a fast, low-risk path to SOA adoption.

Some advantages of SOA:
- Loose coupling between services
- Increased interoperability
- Increased reusability
- Platform and language independence
- Flexibility to meet changing business needs

Some disadvantages of SOA:
- Can be complex to design
- Challenging to implement governance and management
- Requires skills in new technologies and architectures
- Standards are still evolving

SOA finds applications in various domains like healthcare, banking, telecommunications, etc. It is a popular choice for enterprise application integration and development.
