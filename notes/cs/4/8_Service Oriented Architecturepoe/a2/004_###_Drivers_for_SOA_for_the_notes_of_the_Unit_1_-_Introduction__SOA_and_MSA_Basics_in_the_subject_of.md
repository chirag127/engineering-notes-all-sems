### Drivers for SOA

The following are the key drivers for adopting Service Oriented Architecture:

1. Reuse of Services: SOA enables reuse of services across multiple applications. The services can be reused as is or can be composed in different ways to build new applications. This reduces redundancy and increases efficiency.
2. vendor neutral: SOA is based on standards and hence is vendor neutral. The services can be developed using different technologies and platforms and can still interact with each other using standard protocols. This provides flexibility and avoids vendor lock-in.
3. Business and IT alignment: SOA models the business processes as services which are aligned with the business functions. This increases the business and IT alignment and enables business processes to be automated easily through services.
4. Cost efficiency: By reusing services and increasing reuse, SOA leads to cost savings. The services need to be developed only once but can be used many times. This reduces the duplicate effort required and in turn reduces the costs.
5. Flexibility: The SOA services are loosely coupled and can be composed or recomposed in different ways to build flexible applications that can adapt to changing business needs easily. The services can be reused in different compositions to create new applications to meet changing requirements. This flexibility and reusability of SOA enables businesses to become more agile.

Some key points to remember:

- SOA enables reuse of services leading to reduced redundancy and increased efficiency.
- SOA is vendor neutral as it is based on standards. Services can be developed in different technologies but can interoperate.
- SOA aligns IT and business as it models business processes as services.
- SOA leads to cost savings through reuse of services and reduced duplicate effort.
- SOA provides flexibility through loosely coupled services that can be composed and recomposed in different ways.
