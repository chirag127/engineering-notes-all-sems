### Standards and Guidelines for SOA

- Loose Coupling: Services should be loosely coupled so that they can be used independently and interchangeably. This makes the SOA system flexible and resilient to changes.
- Service Contract: All services should have a defined service contract which specifies the interface and functional details of the service. This contract should be evolving and versioned properly.
- Service Discoverability: Services should be easily discoverable by searching based on functional capabilities. Service registry and repository can be used for service discovery.
- Service Autonomy: Services should be self-contained and autonomous so that they can be managed, upgraded, and scaled independently. They should not have dependencies on the internal details of other services.
- Service Reusability: Services should be designed to be reusable across multiple applications and business processes. This increases the ROI of service development.
- Service Governance: There should be a proper governance process in place to manage the service portfolio, address changes, monitor SLAs, ensure security, etc. This is needed for an enterprise-wide adoption of SOA.
- Service Versioning: Services will evolve over time so they should be properly versioned to handle backward compatibility and avoid breaking existing service consumers.
- Service Interoperability: Services should be interoperable so that they can interact with each other and be composed easily into end-to-end business processes and applications. Industry standards can be leveraged to enable interoperability.
- Service Location Transparency: The physical locations of services should be transparent to service consumers. The interaction should be based on the service contract without worrying about the service endpoints.

The key standards and guidelines enable loose coupling, reusability, autonomy, discoverability, and governance which are the core characteristics of a service-oriented architecture. Following these guidelines leads to a flexible, adaptable, and scalable SOA system.
