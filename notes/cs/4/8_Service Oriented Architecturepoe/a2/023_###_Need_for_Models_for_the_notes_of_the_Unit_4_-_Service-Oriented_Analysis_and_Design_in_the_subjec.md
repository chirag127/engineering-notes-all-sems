### Need for Models for the notes of the Unit 4 - Service-Oriented Analysis and Design in the subject of Service Oriented Architecture

- Models are essential to analyze and design complex software systems. They help in abstraction and simplification of the system by ignoring unnecessary details and focusing on the key aspects.
- Some of the key models used in Service Oriented Analysis and Design are:
- Use Case Model: It represents the functionality of the system from an end user's perspective. It captures the business requirements and use cases of the system.
- Domain Model: It represents the key concepts and relationships in the problem domain. It is useful in modeling the business logic and data of the system.
- Interaction Model: It represents the interactions between the components and services of the system. It is useful in modeling the runtime behavior and interactions in a service oriented system.
- Technology Model: It represents the technological infrastructure and middleware on which the service oriented system will be built. It defines the hardware, network and software technologies to be used.

Advantages of using models:
- They simplify complex problems and help in understanding the key aspects of the system.
- They facilitate communication between stakeholders by providing a common terminology and reference.
- They are useful in detecting errors, inconsistencies and omissions early in the development process.
- They support automated analysis and simulation to evaluate non-functional requirements.

Disadvantages of using models:
- Modeling is time consuming and requires skilled resources.
- It is difficult to choose the right level of abstraction and select appropriate models.
- The models may become complex and difficult to understand as the system grows.
- The models have to be continuously evolved and maintained as the system requirements change.

Thus, models play a key role in Service Oriented Analysis and Design by facilitating understanding, communication and analysis of service oriented systems in a structured manner. However, they need to be used judiciously by choosing the right levels of abstractions and granularity based on the system and stakeholders.
