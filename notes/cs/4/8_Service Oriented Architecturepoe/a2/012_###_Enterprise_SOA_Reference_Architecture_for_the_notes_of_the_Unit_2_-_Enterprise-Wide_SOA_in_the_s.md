### Enterprise SOA Reference Architecture

The main components of an Enterprise SOA Reference Architecture are:

1. Service Registry: Stores the metadata about all the services in the SOA. It acts as a service repository and enables service discovery.
2. Service Bus: Routes messages between services. It handles message transformation, protocol conversion, etc.
3. Service Composition: Orchestrates various services to automate a business process.
4. Gateway: Acts as an entry point to the SOA. It handles security, throttling, caching, etc.
5. Monitoring: Monitors the performance of services and the entire SOA. It logs errors, tracks SLAs, etc.

Some key principles of an Enterprise SOA Reference Architecture:

- Loosely coupled services: Services are independent and interact through loose coupling.
- Service abstraction: Services hide the underlying complexity and provide a simple interface.
- Service reusability: Services are reusable across multiple applications and business processes.
- Service composability: Services can be composed to automate complex business processes.
- Service autonomy: Services have control over their data and logic.
- Service statelessness: Services minimize retaining user state to enable scalability.
- Service discoverability: Services can be discovered and bound dynamically.

Advantages of an Enterprise SOA:

- Increased agility: SOA enables accelerated change and innovation.
- Cost reduction: Reuse of services reduces development and maintenance costs.
- Business and IT alignment: SOA models business processes as linked services which improves alignment.
- Flexibility: SOA can work with existing systems and supports multiple platforms and languages.

Disadvantages of an Enterprise SOA:

- Complexity: SOA adds an additional layer of complexity which can impact performance and scalability.
- Governance: Effective governance is required to manage the SOA and services.
- Security: Security must be handled carefully due to exposure of services over a network.
- Versioning: Versioning of services and handling backward compatibility can be challenging.
