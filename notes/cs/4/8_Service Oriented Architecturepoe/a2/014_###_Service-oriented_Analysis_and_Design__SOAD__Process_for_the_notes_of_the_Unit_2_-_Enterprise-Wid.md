### Service-oriented Analysis and Design (SOAD) Process for the notes of the Unit 2 - Enterprise-Wide SOA in the subject of Service Oriented Architecture

The Service-oriented Analysis and Design (SOAD) process involves the following steps:

1. Identify business processes and services:
- Identify the key business processes in the enterprise.
- Decompose the business processes into services that can be automated.
- Name the services from a business perspective.

Mnemonic: Identify processes, decompose into services.

2. Specify service contracts:
- Define the service contract for each service in terms of its input, output, preconditions, and postconditions.
- The service contract acts as an agreement between the service provider and consumer.

3. Design services:
- For each service, determine a suitable design in terms of procedures, components, and data.
- The design should fulfill the specifications in the service contract.

4. Define service interfaces:
- Specify the technical interfaces to access each service.
- The interfaces are defined using a service-oriented architecture approach such as web services.

Mnemonic: Contract, Design, Interface

Advantages:
- Aligns IT with business goals.
- Increased agility through loosely coupled services.
- Increased reuse via shared services.

Disadvantages:
- Can be complex to analyze businesses and decompose into services.
- Governance is required to manage shared services.
- Performance issues can arise due to network delays.

Examples and applications:
- E-commerce websites decomposed into services such as product catalog, shopping cart, payment processing, etc.
- Travel booking systems decomposed into flight booking, hotel booking, car rental, etc. services.
