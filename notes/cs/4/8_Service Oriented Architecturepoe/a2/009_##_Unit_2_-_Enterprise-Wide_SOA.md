## Unit 2 - Enterprise-Wide SOA

### Introduction
- Enterprise-wide SOA refers to the implementation of Service-Oriented Architecture (SOA) across an entire enterprise/ organization.
- It enables the discovery, composition, and integration of services across multiple departments/ domains within an enterprise.
- The key goals of enterprise-wide SOA are:
- Increased business agility through flexibility and reuse
- Improved IT resource utilization through consolidation and sharing
- Reduced integration costs through a common set of services

### Key Characteristics
- Loose coupling: Services are loosely coupled from each other, enabling them to be independently deployed, discovered, and composed.
- Service reuse: Services are designed to be reused across multiple applications and processes.
- Service abstraction: Services abstract the underlying logic and data to provide a simple interface.
- Service composability: Services can be composed/ orchestrated into new applications and business processes.
- Service autonomy: Services have control over their own processes and data.
- Service discoverability: Services can be dynamically discovered and bound.

### Key Benefits
- Increased flexibility and agility: An enterprise can quickly adapt to changing business requirements by reusing and recomposing existing services.
- Reduced duplication: The use of shared services reduces duplication of effort across systems and departments.
- Improved interoperability: Services use open standards and common protocols to enable interoperability across technologies and domains.
- Economies of scale: The costs associated with service development and maintenance can be leveraged across multiple applications and domains.
