### Emergence of MSA

- Microservice Architecture (MSA) emerged as an evolution of Service Oriented Architecture (SOA) to address the following pain points of SOA:
    - Large monolithic applications leading to inflexibility and scalability issues.
    - Tight coupling between services leading to domino effect of changes and complex deployments.
- The key characteristics of MSA are:
    - Small, focused services: Each service is designed to address a specific task/function.
    - Loosely coupled: Services are loosely coupled and communicate over well-defined interfaces/APIs. A change in one service does not affect other services.
    - Independently deployable: Services can be deployed, upgraded, scaled, and managed independently.
    - Built for business capabilities: Services are aligned with business capabilities and can be developed and evolved independently by autonomous teams.
    - Resilient: Failure of a service should not affect the functioning of the overall system. Other services should continue to function even if a service is down.
- The advantages of MSA are:
    - Increased agility and flexibility: Small services can be developed and deployed rapidly.
    - Scalability: Services can be scaled independently based on demand.
    - Fault tolerance: Failure of one service does not bring down the entire system. Other services continue to function.
    - Technology heterogeneity: Services can be developed using different technologies based on requirements.
- Examples of companies using MSA: Netflix, Amazon, Uber, etc.
