### Considerations for Service-oriented Applications

The following are some key considerations for service-oriented applications:

1. **Loose coupling**: Services should be loosely coupled so that they can be independently developed, deployed, and scaled. They should have minimal dependencies on other services.
2. **Service granularity**: Services should be chunked into components of appropriate granularity. Neither too fine-grained nor too coarse-grained. Fine-grained services may result in excessive inter-service communication overhead while coarse-grained services may be overly complex and difficult to maintain.
3. **Service interface**: Services should have simple, logical, and consistent interfaces that are technology-agnostic. The interfaces should be robust and fault-tolerant.
4. **Service discovery**: There should be a service registry or discovery mechanism to enable services to locate and bind to other services dynamically at runtime.
5. **Service contract and versioning**: Services should have explicit contracts that are rigorously defined and versioned. Backward compatibility needs to be handled carefully when evolving service contracts and interfaces.
6. **Service autonomy**: Services should be autonomous and independently deployable. They should not have excessive dependencies on the availability of other services.
7. **Service governance**: There should be effective service governance to oversee the service portfolio, minimize duplication, deal with changes, ensure quality, and more.
8. **Security**: Appropriate security mechanisms are needed to ensure authentication, authorization, confidentiality, integrity, and other security aspects for services and service-oriented applications.
9. **Performance optimization**: The performance of service-oriented applications can be optimized via techniques like caching, parallel execution, resource pooling, and more.
10. **Reliability**: Reliability of services and overall service-oriented applications should be maximized through fault tolerance, failover, recovery, and other reliability mechanisms.
