### Evolution of SOA and MSA for the notes of the Unit 1 - Introduction: SOA and MSA Basics in the subject of Service Oriented Architecture

1. Service Oriented Architecture (SOA)
- Evolved in the 1990s to enable application integration through interoperable services.
- Focused on exposing business processes as services that can be discovered and composed.
- Services communicate via protocols like SOAP over HTTP.
- Examples: SOAP, WSDL, UDDI.

Advantages:
- Increased reuse of services.
- Platform and language independent.
- Loose coupling between services.

Disadvantages:
- Complex architecture.
- Performance overhead due to XML processing and SOAP.
- Vendor lock-in due to usage of proprietary standards.

2. Microservices Architecture (MSA)
- Evolved from SOA in the 2010s to build modular, distributed systems.
- Focuses on developing single-purpose, independently deployable small services.
- Services communicate over lightweight mechanisms like HTTP APIs/REST.
- Examples: REST, JSON, Docker.

Advantages:
- Increased scalability and resilience.
- Improved modularity.
- Technology heterogeneity.
- Suitability for cloud-native development.

Disadvantages:
- Complex operational management.
- Difficult debugging and monitoring.
- Eventual consistency challenges.
- Ideal for greenfield projects, not brownfield.
