### Principles of Service Design

The key principles of service design are:

1. Standardized service contract - The interface of a service should be standardized through a service contract which defines the operations, parameters, return types, error handling, etc. This allows services to be reused and composed easily.
2. Loose coupling - Services should be loosely coupled such that they have minimal dependencies on each other. This makes services flexible, robust and reusable.
3. Service abstraction - A service should abstract the underlying logic/implementation details and expose a simple interface to the user. This increases the reusability of services.
4. Service autonomy - Services should be autonomous and independent such that they can be developed, deployed and scaled independently. This makes the system more robust, flexible and manageable.
5. Service reuse - Services should be designed such that they can be reused by multiple service consumers. This reduces redundancy and increases ROI on service development.
6. Service composability - Services should be designed to be composed with other services to provide increased functionality to service consumers. This enables new solutions to be created easily by combining existing services.
7. Statelessness - Services should be stateless such that each request can be processed independently without relying on previous requests. This makes services scalable and robust. State can be maintained externally using databases or caches if required.
8. Discoverability - Services and their contracts should be discoverable by potential service consumers. This enables services to be found and reused easily. Service registries and repositories can be used for service discovery.

Some mnemonics and learning tricks for remembering the principles:

- SCARF stands for Standardized Contract, Autonomy, Reusability, Flexibility
- Loose Coupling -minimize dependencies, increases robustness
- Keep it Simple, Stupid (KISS) - use service abstraction
- Compose services to get more functionality (like Legos)
- Stateless services scale better, externalize state if needed
- Make services discoverable to increase reuse
