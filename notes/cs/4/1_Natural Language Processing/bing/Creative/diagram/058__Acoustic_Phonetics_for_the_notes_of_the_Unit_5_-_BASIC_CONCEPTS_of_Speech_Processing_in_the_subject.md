Acoustic phonetics is the study of the acoustic characteristics of speech, such as frequency, intensity, and duration, and how they relate to other aspects of phonetics and linguistics . A diagram for acoustic phonetics can be drawn as follows:

The diagram shows the three main components of acoustic phonetics: the speech source, the vocal tract, and the speech signal. The speech source is the production of sound by the vocal folds or other mechanisms, such as clicks or ejectives. The vocal tract is the modification of the sound by the articulators, such as the tongue, lips, and teeth. The speech signal is the acoustic waveform that results from the speech source and the vocal tract. The diagram also shows the three main methods of acoustic phonetics: the source filter model, the transfer function, and the acoustic analysis. The source filter model is a mathematical representation of how the speech source and the vocal tract interact to produce the speech signal. The transfer function is a function that describes the frequency response of the vocal tract, i.e., how it amplifies or attenuates different frequencies of the speech source. The acoustic analysis is the measurement and description of the speech signal in terms of its physical properties, such as amplitude, frequency, and spectrum. The acoustic analysis can be done using various tools, such as the sound spectrograph, the oscilloscope, or the computer. The acoustic analysis can reveal information about the speech source, the vocal tract, and the linguistic content of the speech signal.
