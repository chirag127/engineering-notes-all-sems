Dependency grammar is a class of modern grammatical theories that are all based on the dependency relation (as opposed to the constituency relation of phrase structure) and that can be traced back primarily to the work of Lucien Tesnière. Dependency is the notion that linguistic units, e.g. words, are connected to each other by directed links. The (finite) verb is taken to be the structural center of clause structure. All other syntactic units (words) are either directly or indirectly connected to the verb in terms of the directed links, which are called dependencies.

A dependency tree is a graphical representation of the dependency structure of a sentence, where nodes represent words and labeled arcs represent different types of dependency relations. For example, the sentence "She gave him a book" can be represented by the following dependency tree:

```
    gave
   / |  \
She  |  book
     |
    him
```

The labels on the arcs indicate the grammatical function of the dependent word, such as subject (nsubj), direct object (obj), or indirect object (iobj). Different dependency frameworks may use different sets of labels and criteria for identifying dependencies.

### Dependency Grammar for the notes of the Unit 3 - SYNTACTIC ANALYSIS in the subject of Natural Language Processing

Dependency grammar can be used to analyze the syntactic structure of natural language sentences and to capture the semantic relations between words. Dependency grammar is especially suitable for languages with free word order, such as Czech or Warlpiri, because it does not rely on the linear order of words to determine their syntactic roles.

Dependency grammar can also be used as a basis for natural language processing tasks, such as parsing, generation, and machine translation. Dependency parsing is the task of automatically producing a dependency tree for a given sentence, using either rule-based or statistical methods. Dependency generation is the task of producing a natural language sentence from a given dependency tree, using either template-based or statistical methods. Dependency-based machine translation is the task of translating a sentence from one language to another by mapping the dependency structures of the source and target languages, using either rule-based or statistical methods.

Dependency grammar is a descriptive and theoretical tradition in linguistics that has a long history and a wide range of applications. It is also a perspective that can be used to compare and contrast different languages and grammatical frameworks.
