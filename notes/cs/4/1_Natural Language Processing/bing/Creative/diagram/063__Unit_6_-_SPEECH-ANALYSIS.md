Speech analysis is the process of studying the acoustic and linguistic features of human speech. Speech analysis can be used for various purposes, such as speech recognition, speech synthesis, speech enhancement, speech coding, speech segmentation, speech emotion recognition, speech pathology, speech forensics, and speech education.

One way to represent the stages of speech analysis is by using a speech chain diagram. A speech chain diagram shows how a message moves from the mind of the speaker to the mind of the listener through different processes and codes. The speech chain diagram illustrates the basic architecture of a speech analysis system. Each stage involves different types of information and transformations. For example, the grammatical code converts the speaker's intention into a sequence of words and phrases that follow the rules of the language. The phonological code converts the words and phrases into a sequence of sounds and intonation patterns that convey the meaning and emotion of the message. The articulatory process converts the sounds and intonation patterns into movements of the vocal organs, such as the lungs, larynx, tongue, and lips. The acoustic signal is the result of the air pressure variations produced by the vocal organs. The acoustic signal travels through the air and reaches the listener's ear, where it is converted into electrical impulses by the auditory system. The perceptual process converts the electrical impulses into a representation of the sounds and intonation patterns that the listener hears. The phonological code converts the sounds and intonation patterns into words and phrases that the listener recognizes. The grammatical code converts the words and phrases into a representation of the meaning and emotion of the message. The listener's understanding is the result of the interpretation of the message by the listener's cognitive and social context.
