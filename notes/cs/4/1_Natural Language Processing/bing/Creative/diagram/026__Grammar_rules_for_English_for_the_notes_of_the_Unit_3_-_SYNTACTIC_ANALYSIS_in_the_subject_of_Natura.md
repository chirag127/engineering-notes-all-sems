Grammar rules for English are the system of language rules that allows you to combine individual words to make complex meanings. There are many grammar rules in English, but some of the basic ones are:

- A complete sentence must include a subject and a verb. The subject is the person, place, thing or idea that performs the action or is described by the verb. The verb is the action word or the state of being word. For example, "The bird flew." The subject is "the bird" and the verb is "flew".
- The first word in a sentence must be capitalized. For example, "She likes ice cream." The first word "She" is capitalized.
- A sentence must end with a punctuation mark, such as a period, a question mark, or an exclamation point. For example, "Do you like ice cream?" The sentence ends with a question mark.
- A singular subject must have a singular verb, and a plural subject must have a plural verb. This is called subject-verb agreement. For example, "He runs fast." The subject "he" is singular and the verb "runs" is singular. "They run fast." The subject "they" is plural and the verb "run" is plural.
- A noun can be modified by an adjective, which describes the noun. For example, "a red apple." The adjective "red" modifies the noun "apple".
- A verb can be modified by an adverb, which describes the verb. For example, "She runs quickly." The adverb "quickly" modifies the verb "runs".
- A noun can be replaced by a pronoun, which refers to the noun. For example, "She likes ice cream. She eats it every day." The pronoun "she" replaces the noun "she" and the pronoun "it" replaces the noun "ice cream".
- A preposition shows the relationship between a noun or a pronoun and another word in the sentence. For example, "She is in the park." The preposition "in" shows the relationship between the pronoun "she" and the noun "park".
- A conjunction connects two words, phrases, or clauses in a sentence. For example, "She likes ice cream and cake." The conjunction "and" connects the two phrases "she likes ice cream" and "she likes cake".
- An interjection expresses a strong emotion or a sudden feeling. For example, "Wow! That was amazing." The interjection "wow" expresses surprise or admiration.
