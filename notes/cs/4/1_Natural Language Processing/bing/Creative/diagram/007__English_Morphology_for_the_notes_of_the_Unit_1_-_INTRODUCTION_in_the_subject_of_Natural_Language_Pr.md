Morphology is the study of the internal structure of words and the rules by which words are formed. English morphology can be divided into two main types: derivational and inflectional. Derivational morphology creates new words from existing ones, such as adding prefixes or suffixes to change the meaning or category of a word. For example, the word unkind is derived from the word kind by adding the prefix un-. Inflectional morphology modifies existing words to indicate grammatical information, such as number, tense, person, or case. For example, the word books is inflected from the word book by adding the suffix -s to indicate plural number.
