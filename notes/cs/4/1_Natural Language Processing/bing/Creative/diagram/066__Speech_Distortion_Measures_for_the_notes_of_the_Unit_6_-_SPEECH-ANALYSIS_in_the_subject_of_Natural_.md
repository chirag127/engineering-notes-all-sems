Speech distortion measures are objective methods to quantify the degradation of speech quality due to various factors, such as noise, coding, transmission, or articulation disorders. Speech distortion measures can be classified into two categories: waveform-based and spectral-based. Waveform-based measures compare the original and distorted speech signals in the time domain, while spectral-based measures compare them in the frequency domain. Some examples of waveform-based measures are mean squared error (MSE), signal-to-noise ratio (SNR), and segmental SNR. Some examples of spectral-based measures are log spectral distance (LSD), Itakura-Saito (IS) distance, and cepstral distance.
