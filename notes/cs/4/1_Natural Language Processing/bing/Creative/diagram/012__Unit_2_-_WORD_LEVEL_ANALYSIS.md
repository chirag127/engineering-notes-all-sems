## Unit 2 - WORD LEVEL ANALYSIS

Word level analysis is the process of examining the structure, meaning, and usage of words in a text. Word level analysis can help to improve the readability, vocabulary, and accuracy of a text. Word level analysis can also help to identify the tone, mood, and sentiment of a text.

There are different tools and methods for word level analysis, such as:

- Statistics and readability: These are numerical measures that indicate how easy or difficult a text is to read and understand. Some common statistics and readability tests are:

  - Flesch Reading Ease: This test rates the text on a 100-point scale, where higher scores indicate easier readability. The test considers the average number of words per sentence and the average number of syllables per word. A score of 60 to 70 is considered standard for most texts.
  - Flesch-Kincaid Grade Level: This test rates the text on a U.S. school grade level, where higher grades indicate more difficult readability. The test considers the same factors as the Flesch Reading Ease test, but uses a different formula. A score of 7 to 8 is considered standard for most texts.
  - Other statistics and readability tests include the Gunning Fog Index, the SMOG Index, the Automated Readability Index, and the Coleman-Liau Index.

- Lexical diversity: This is a measure of how varied the vocabulary of a text is. A text with high lexical diversity uses a wide range of words and avoids repetition. A text with low lexical diversity uses a narrow range of words and repeats them frequently. Lexical diversity can be calculated by dividing the number of different words in a text by the total number of words in the text. A lexical diversity score of 0.5 or higher is considered good for most texts.

- Corpus analysis: This is the method of comparing the words and phrases of a text to a large collection of texts, called a corpus, that represent a certain language, genre, or domain. Corpus analysis can help to identify the frequency, collocation, and register of the words and phrases in a text. Some common corpora are:

  - British National Corpus (BNC): This is a corpus of 100 million words of written and spoken British English from a variety of sources and genres. The BNC can help to analyze the general usage and distribution of words and phrases in British English.
  - Corpus of Contemporary American English (COCA): This is a corpus of 560 million words of written and spoken American English from a variety of sources and genres. The COCA can help to analyze the general usage and distribution of words and phrases in American English.
  - Academic Word List (AWL) and Phrases: This is a list of 570 words and 118 phrases that are commonly used in academic texts across different disciplines. The AWL and phrases can help to analyze the academic vocabulary and style of a text.
  - English Vocabulary Profile (EVP): This is a database of over 20,000 words and phrases that are classified according to the Common European Framework of Reference (CEFR) levels, from A1 to C2. The CEFR levels indicate the proficiency and complexity of the language learners and users. The EVP can help to analyze the level and suitability of the vocabulary of a text for different learners and users.

- Scorecard: This is a tool that summarizes the results of the word level analysis and provides feedback and suggestions for improving the text. A scorecard can include the following information:

  - Statistics and readability scores: These are the numerical measures that indicate how easy or difficult the text is to read and understand, such as the Flesch Reading Ease and Flesch-Kincaid Grade Level scores.
  - Lexical diversity score: This is the measure of how varied the vocabulary of the text is, such as the lexical diversity score.
  - Word level feedback: This is the feedback that identifies the strengths and weaknesses of the text at the word level, such as the use of common/rare words, difficult/easy words, academic/non-academic words, and positive/negative words.
  - Word level suggestions: These are the suggestions that provide guidance and examples for improving the text at the word level, such as replacing words, adding words, deleting words, and changing words.
