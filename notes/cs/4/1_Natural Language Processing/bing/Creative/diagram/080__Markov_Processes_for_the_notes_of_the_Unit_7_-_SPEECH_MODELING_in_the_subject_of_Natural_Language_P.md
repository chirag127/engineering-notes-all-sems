A Markov process is a stochastic model that describes the evolution of a system with random variables that change over time. A Markov process assumes that the future state of the system depends only on the current state, not on the past history. This is known as the Markov property. 

A Markov process can be used for speech modeling, which is the task of representing the statistical characteristics of speech signals and the linguistic units that compose them. Speech modeling is essential for speech recognition, synthesis, and analysis. 

One of the most common Markov processes for speech modeling is the hidden Markov model (HMM). A hidden Markov model is a Markov process where the states of the system are not directly observable, but only through some noisy or incomplete observations. For example, the states of a speech signal can be the phonemes or words that are spoken, and the observations can be the acoustic features or the speech waveform. A hidden Markov model can be used to infer the most likely sequence of states given a sequence of observations, or to estimate the probabilities of the states and the observations from a training data set.
