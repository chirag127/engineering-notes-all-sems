The acoustics of speech production is the study of how speech sounds are generated by the human vocal tract and how they are perceived by the human auditory system. The basic architecture of the vocal tract consists of a sound source, usually the vibrating vocal folds, and a filter, which is the shape and configuration of the oral and nasal cavities. The sound source produces a complex waveform that contains a range of frequencies, and the filter modifies this waveform by enhancing or attenuating certain frequencies, creating peaks and valleys in the spectrum. The resulting speech sound is a combination of the source and the filter characteristics.
