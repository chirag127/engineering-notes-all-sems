## Unit 5 - BASIC CONCEPTS of Speech Processing

Speech processing is the study of how humans produce, perceive, and understand speech sounds. It involves various disciplines such as linguistics, acoustics, psychology, and computer science. Speech processing has many applications, such as speech recognition, speech synthesis, speech enhancement, speech coding, and speech analysis.

The basic architecture of a speech processing system can be divided into three main components: speech production, speech transmission, and speech perception. The following diagram illustrates the basic architecture of a speech processing system:

```
|    preparation   |     |    signal        |     |    processing    |
|  - Formulation   |     |  - Channel       |     |  - Parsing       |
|  - Articulation  |     |    distortion    |     |  - Interpretation|
|                  |     |                  |     |                  |
+------------------+     +------------------+     +------------------+
```

Speech production is the process of generating speech sounds from a desired concept or message. It involves three major levels of processing: conceptualization, formulation, and articulation. Conceptualization is the process of linking a desired concept to the particular spoken words to be expressed. Formulation is the process of constructing a linguistic structure that conveys the intended meaning. Articulation is the process of producing the speech sounds using the vocal organs.

Speech transmission is the process of conveying the speech signal from the speaker to the listener. It involves the acoustic properties of the speech signal and the channel through which it travels. The acoustic signal is the physical representation of the speech sounds as variations in air pressure. The channel is the medium that carries the speech signal, such as air, wire, or radio. The channel may introduce noise or distortion to the speech signal, which may affect its quality and intelligibility.

Speech perception is the process of receiving, analyzing, and understanding the speech signal by the listener. It involves three major levels of processing: auditory processing, parsing, and interpretation. Auditory processing is the process of converting the acoustic signal into neural impulses that can be processed by the brain. Parsing is the process of segmenting the speech signal into meaningful units, such as words and phrases. Interpretation is the process of assigning meaning to the parsed units, based on the context and the knowledge of the listener.
