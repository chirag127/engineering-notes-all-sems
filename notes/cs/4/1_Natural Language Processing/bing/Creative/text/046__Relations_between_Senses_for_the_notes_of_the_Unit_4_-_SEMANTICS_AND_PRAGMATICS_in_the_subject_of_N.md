### Relations between Senses

- Relations between senses are the ways in which the meanings of words or expressions are related to each other in a language.
- There are two main types of relations between senses: paradigmatic and syntagmatic.
- Paradigmatic relations hold between words or expressions that can be substituted for each other in the same syntactic contexts, without changing the meaning of the sentence. For example, synonyms, antonyms, hyponyms, and meronyms are paradigmatic relations.
- Syntagmatic relations hold between words or expressions that can occur together in a sentence, and affect each other's meaning or interpretation. For example, selectional restrictions, collocations, and idioms are syntagmatic relations.
- In this section, we will focus on some of the most common paradigmatic relations: synonymy, antonymy, hyponymy, and meronymy.
