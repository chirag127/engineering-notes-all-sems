### Production And Classification Of Speech Sounds

- Speech sounds are the result of the modification of an air stream that originates in the lungs and passes through the oral and nasal cavities.
- The production of a speech sound involves four interrelated processes: initiation, phonation, oro-nasal process, and articulation .
- Initiation is the generation of the air stream by the lungs and the respiratory system.
- Phonation is the vibration of the vocal cords in the larynx, which produces voiced or voiceless sounds depending on whether the vocal cords are close together or apart.
- Oro-nasal process is the direction of the air stream into either the oral cavity or the nasal cavity by the velum, which is a muscular flap at the back of the mouth.
- Articulation is the shaping of the air stream by the tongue, lips, teeth, and other organs in the oral cavity, which creates different speech sounds.
- Speech sounds are classified into two broad phonetic categories: vowels and consonants.
- Vowels are speech sounds that are produced without any obstruction or narrowing of the air stream in the oral cavity. They are characterized by the height, backness, and roundness of the tongue, and the tenseness or laxness of the vocal cords.
- Consonants are speech sounds that are produced with some degree of obstruction or narrowing of the air stream in the oral cavity. They are characterized by the place, manner, and voicing of the articulation.
- Place of articulation refers to the location of the obstruction or narrowing in the oral cavity, such as bilabial (between the lips), dental (between the tongue and the teeth), alveolar (at the alveolar ridge), etc.
- Manner of articulation refers to the type of obstruction or narrowing in the oral cavity, such as plosive (complete closure and release), fricative (narrowing that causes friction), affricate (combination of plosive and fricative), etc.
- Voicing of articulation refers to the presence or absence of vocal cord vibration during the articulation, such as voiced (vocal cords vibrate) or voiceless (vocal cords do not vibrate).
- Some speech sounds are also classified as glides, liquids, or nasals, depending on their specific features of articulation.
- Glides are speech sounds that involve a smooth transition from one vowel to another, such as /w/ and /j/.
- Liquids are speech sounds that involve a partial closure of the air stream by the tongue, but allow some air to escape, such as /l/ and /r/.
- Nasals are speech sounds that involve a complete closure of the oral cavity, but allow the air to escape through the nose, such as /m/, /n/, and /ŋ/.
