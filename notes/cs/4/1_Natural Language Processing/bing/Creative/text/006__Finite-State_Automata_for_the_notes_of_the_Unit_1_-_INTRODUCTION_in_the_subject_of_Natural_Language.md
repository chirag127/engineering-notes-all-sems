### Finite-State Automata for the notes of the Unit 1 - INTRODUCTION in the subject of Natural Language Processing

- Finite-state automata (FSA) are abstract machines that can process natural language by following a predetermined sequence of operations based on a finite number of states .
- FSA can be used to model various natural language processing (NLP) tasks, such as speech recognition, spelling correction, information retrieval, etc .
- FSA can be classified into two types: deterministic finite automata (DFA) and non-deterministic finite automata (NDFA) .
- DFA are FSA that have a unique transition for each input symbol and state, whereas NDFA are FSA that can have multiple transitions for the same input symbol and state .
- DFA and NDFA are equivalent in terms of expressive power, meaning that they can recognize the same class of languages, which are called regular languages .
- FSA can also be extended to produce some output for a given input, which are called finite-state transducers (FST) .
- FST can be used to model various natural language processing tasks, such as morphological analysis, phonological rules, text rewriting, etc  .
- FST can also be classified into two types: deterministic finite-state transducers (DFST) and non-deterministic finite-state transducers (NFST) .
- DFST are FST that have a unique output for each input, whereas NFST are FST that can have multiple outputs for the same input .
- DFST and NFST are equivalent in terms of expressive power, meaning that they can perform the same class of transformations, which are called rational relations .
- FSA and FST have many advantages for natural language processing, such as being mathematically well-defined, compact, efficient, and easy to compile  .
