### English Morphology

- Morphology is the study of the internal structure and formation of words.
- Words are the smallest units of meaning that can stand alone in a language.
- Words can be composed of smaller units called morphemes, which are the minimal units of meaning or grammatical function in a language.
- Morphemes can be classified into two types: roots and affixes.
- Roots are the core of the word, which carry the main meaning and cannot be further divided into smaller units. For example, in the word "unhappy", the root is "happy".
- Affixes are the additional units that can be attached to the root to modify its meaning or grammatical function. For example, in the word "unhappy", the affix is "un-", which changes the meaning of the root to its opposite.
- Affixes can be further classified into two types: prefixes and suffixes.
- Prefixes are the affixes that are attached to the beginning of the root. For example, in the word "unhappy", the prefix is "un-".
- Suffixes are the affixes that are attached to the end of the root. For example, in the word "happiness", the suffix is "-ness".
- Some words can have more than one affix, such as "unhappiness", which has both a prefix and a suffix.
- Some words can have more than one root, such as "blackboard", which is a compound word formed by combining two roots: "black" and "board".
- Some words can have neither roots nor affixes, such as "the", which is a function word that does not carry any meaning by itself, but serves a grammatical role in a sentence.
- Some words can have the same form but different meanings, such as "bat", which can be a noun meaning a flying mammal or a verb meaning to hit with a stick. These words are called homonyms.
- Some words can have different forms but the same meaning, such as "dog" and "dogs", which are singular and plural forms of the same noun. These words are called inflectional variants.
- Some words can have different forms and different meanings, such as "dog" and "dogged", which are a noun and an adjective derived from the same root. These words are called derivational variants.
