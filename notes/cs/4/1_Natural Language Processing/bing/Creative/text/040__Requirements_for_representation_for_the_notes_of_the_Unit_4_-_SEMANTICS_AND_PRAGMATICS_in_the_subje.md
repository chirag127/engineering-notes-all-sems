### Requirements for representation

- In natural language processing, semantics and pragmatics are two aspects of meaning that need to be represented and processed by computational models.
- Semantics is the study of the literal meaning of words and sentences, while pragmatics is the study of how context and situation affect meaning.
- To represent and process semantics and pragmatics, natural language processing systems need to have some requirements for representation, such as:
  - Expressiveness: the ability to capture the various aspects of meaning, such as truth conditions, entailment, presupposition, implicature, reference, etc.
  - Formality: the ability to define the representation and the operations on it in a precise and unambiguous way, using mathematical or logical tools.
  - Computability: the ability to implement the representation and the operations on it in an efficient and effective way, using algorithms and data structures.
  - Generality: the ability to apply the representation and the operations on it to a wide range of natural language phenomena, domains, and tasks.
  - Compatibility: the ability to integrate the representation and the operations on it with other components of natural language processing systems, such as syntax, morphology, phonology, etc.
  - Learnability: the ability to acquire the representation and the operations on it from natural language data, using machine learning techniques.
- Different types of representations have different strengths and weaknesses with respect to these requirements, and there is no single best representation for all purposes.
- Some examples of representations for semantics and pragmatics are:
  - Logic-based representations: use formal logic to capture the truth conditions and logical relations of natural language expressions, such as first-order logic, modal logic, description logic, etc.
  - Frame-based representations: use structured schemas to capture the conceptual and contextual knowledge of natural language expressions, such as frames, scripts, scenarios, etc.
  - Network-based representations: use graphs or networks to capture the semantic and pragmatic relations of natural language expressions, such as semantic networks, conceptual graphs, wordnets, etc.
  - Vector-based representations: use numerical vectors or matrices to capture the distributional and contextual properties of natural language expressions, such as word embeddings, sentence embeddings, topic models, etc.
