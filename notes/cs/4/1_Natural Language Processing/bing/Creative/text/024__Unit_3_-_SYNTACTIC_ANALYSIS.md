## Unit 3 - SYNTACTIC ANALYSIS

- Syntactic analysis is the process of analyzing the structure and meaning of natural language sentences or phrases.
- Syntactic analysis involves identifying the syntactic categories and functions of the words and phrases in a sentence, and how they are related to each other.
- Syntactic analysis can be performed at different levels of granularity, such as morphological, lexical, phrasal, and sentential.
- Syntactic analysis can be based on different formalisms or models, such as constituency, dependency, or phrase structure grammars.
- Syntactic analysis can be used for various applications, such as parsing, translation, summarization, question answering, and natural language generation.

Some of the topics covered in this unit are:

- Syntactic categories and functions: how to classify words and phrases into categories such as nouns, verbs, adjectives, adverbs, prepositions, etc., and how to assign functions such as subject, object, modifier, complement, etc., to them.
- Constituency and dependency: how to represent the hierarchical and linear relationships between words and phrases in a sentence, using tree diagrams or graphs.
- Phrase structure rules and grammars: how to define and apply rules that generate well-formed phrases and sentences in a language, using symbols and variables.
- Parsing algorithms and techniques: how to use algorithms and techniques such as top-down, bottom-up, chart, or probabilistic parsing to analyze the syntactic structure of a sentence, given a grammar and a lexicon.
- Syntactic ambiguity and disambiguation: how to identify and resolve cases where a sentence can have more than one syntactic interpretation, using context, semantics, or probabilities.
