### Selectional Restrictions

- Selectional restrictions are constraints on the possible combinations of words in a phrase or sentence, based on their semantic properties or categories.
- Selectional restrictions are a type of semantic rule that can be used to filter out nonsensical or anomalous sentences, such as "The chair barked" or "The idea is green".
- Selectional restrictions can be expressed as features or predicates that specify the semantic class or role of a word or phrase, such as [+animate], [+human], [agent], [patient], etc.
- Selectional restrictions can be violated for various reasons, such as metaphor, irony, humor, poetic license, etc. In such cases, the context or pragmatics of the utterance can help to resolve the intended meaning or effect.
- Selectional restrictions can be formalized using various methods, such as lambda calculus, type theory, or feature structures. These methods can capture the semantic compositionality and compatibility of words and phrases in a logical or algebraic way.
