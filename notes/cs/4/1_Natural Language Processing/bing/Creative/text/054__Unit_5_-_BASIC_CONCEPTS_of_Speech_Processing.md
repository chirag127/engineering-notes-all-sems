## Unit 5 - BASIC CONCEPTS of Speech Processing

Speech processing is the study of how humans produce, perceive, and understand speech, as well as how machines can process, synthesize, and recognize speech signals.

Some of the basic concepts of speech processing are:

- **Speech signal**: A speech signal is a time-varying waveform that represents the acoustic pressure variations produced by the vocal tract during speech production. A speech signal can be analyzed in terms of its frequency, amplitude, and phase components, which reflect the characteristics of the sound source (e.g., pitch, loudness, timbre) and the sound filter (e.g., vocal tract shape, articulation, resonance).
- **Speech analysis**: Speech analysis is the process of extracting information from a speech signal, such as its spectral, temporal, or prosodic features, or its linguistic content. Speech analysis can be performed in different domains, such as time, frequency, or cepstrum, using different techniques, such as Fourier transform, linear prediction, or mel-frequency cepstral coefficients (MFCCs).
- **Speech synthesis**: Speech synthesis is the process of generating a speech signal from a given text or symbolic representation, such as phonetic, prosodic, or semantic information. Speech synthesis can be performed using different methods, such as concatenative, parametric, or neural network-based synthesis, which differ in the level of naturalness, flexibility, and intelligibility they can achieve.
- **Speech recognition**: Speech recognition is the process of identifying the words or phrases spoken by a speaker from a speech signal, using a predefined vocabulary and grammar, or a statistical language model. Speech recognition can be performed using different approaches, such as acoustic, phonetic, or semantic decoding, which differ in the level of accuracy, robustness, and efficiency they can achieve.
- **Speech enhancement**: Speech enhancement is the process of improving the quality or intelligibility of a speech signal, by reducing the effects of noise, reverberation, or distortion. Speech enhancement can be performed using different techniques, such as spectral subtraction, Wiener filtering, or beamforming, which differ in the level of performance, complexity, and computational cost they require.
- **Speech coding**: Speech coding is the process of compressing a speech signal, by exploiting its redundancy and irrelevancy, to reduce its bit rate or storage size, while preserving its quality or intelligibility. Speech coding can be performed using different methods, such as waveform, source, or hybrid coding, which differ in the trade-off between compression ratio, quality, and delay they offer.
