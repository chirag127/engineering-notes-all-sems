### Filter Bank and LPC Methods

Filter bank and LPC methods are two techniques for feature extraction in speech processing. Feature extraction is the process of converting the speech signal into a parametric representation that captures the relevant information for further analysis and processing. Feature extraction is important for speech recognition, speech synthesis, speech coding, and speech enhancement.

Filter bank methods are based on dividing the frequency spectrum of the speech signal into several sub-bands, and computing the energy or power of each sub-band. The sub-bands are usually chosen to match the human auditory system, such as the mel-scale or the bark-scale. The filter bank methods can capture the spectral envelope of the speech signal, which reflects the resonances of the vocal tract. The filter bank methods include:

- Mel-frequency cepstral coefficients (MFCC): MFCC are obtained by applying a discrete cosine transform (DCT) to the logarithm of the filter bank energies. MFCC are widely used in speech recognition and speech synthesis, as they are robust to noise and speaker variability.  
- Perceptual linear predictive coefficients (PLP): PLP are obtained by applying an inverse DCT to the filter bank energies, and then applying a linear predictive analysis to the resulting spectrum. PLP are designed to model the human auditory perception of speech, and incorporate several psychoacoustic effects, such as critical band masking and equal-loudness pre-emphasis. 
- Relative spectra filtering of log domain coefficients (RASTA): RASTA are obtained by applying a band-pass filter to the logarithm of the filter bank energies, and then applying a DCT. RASTA are designed to reduce the effects of channel noise and environmental noise on the speech signal, and to enhance the temporal dynamics of the speech features. 

Linear predictive coding (LPC) methods are based on modeling the speech signal as a linear combination of past samples, and estimating the coefficients of the linear predictor. The LPC methods can capture the spectral envelope of the speech signal, as well as the excitation source of the speech production. The LPC methods include:

- Linear predictive coefficients (LPC): LPC are obtained by minimizing the mean squared error between the actual speech signal and the predicted speech signal, using methods such as autocorrelation, covariance, or Burg's algorithm. LPC can be used for speech coding, speech synthesis, and speech enhancement.  
- Linear predictive cepstral coefficients (LPCC): LPCC are obtained by applying a DCT to the logarithm of the LPC coefficients. LPCC are widely used in speech recognition, as they are more robust to noise and speaker variability than LPC.  
- Line spectral frequencies (LSF): LSF are obtained by converting the LPC coefficients into a set of frequencies that represent the locations of the zeros of the LPC polynomial. LSF are useful for speech coding, as they have a lower bit rate and a better quantization performance than LPC.
