### Stochastic

- Stochastic means involving randomness or probability.
- Stochastic methods are often used in natural language processing (NLP) to deal with the ambiguity and variability of natural languages.
- Stochastic methods use statistical models to assign probabilities to linguistic units, such as words, phrases, sentences, or grammars.
- Stochastic methods can help to disambiguate, generate, or analyze natural language texts based on the frequency and distribution of linguistic units in a large corpus of data.
- Some examples of stochastic methods in NLP are:

  - Stochastic grammar: a grammar framework that assigns probabilities to grammar rules and defines the grammaticality of a sentence as a function of its probability .
  - Statistical parsing: a parsing method that uses stochastic grammars and probabilistic algorithms to find the most likely syntactic structure of a sentence .
  - Data-oriented parsing: a parsing method that uses a large corpus of parsed sentences as a source of grammar rules and probabilities.
  - Hidden Markov model: a probabilistic model that represents a sequence of observations as a sequence of hidden states, each of which has a probability distribution over the possible observations.
  - Estimation theory: a branch of statistics that deals with finding the best estimates of unknown parameters or variables based on observed data.
  - Language model: a probabilistic model that assigns probabilities to sequences of words or symbols, based on their occurrence and co-occurrence in a corpus .
  - Stochastic semantic analysis: an approach to semantic analysis that uses segments of words as basic semantic units and assigns probabilities to them based on their context and meaning.
