## Unit 7 - SPEECH MODELING

- Speech modeling is the process of creating mathematical representations of speech signals and speech units, such as words, syllables, phonemes, etc.
- Speech modeling is essential for speech recognition, speech synthesis, speech enhancement, speech coding, and speech analysis applications.
- Speech modeling can be divided into two main categories: parametric and non-parametric models.
- Parametric models assume that speech signals follow a certain probability distribution or a generative process, and use a set of parameters to describe the distribution or the process. Examples of parametric models are:
  - Hidden Markov Models (HMMs): HMMs are widely used for speech recognition and speech synthesis. HMMs model speech signals as a sequence of discrete states, each associated with a probability distribution over acoustic features. HMMs can capture the temporal dynamics and variability of speech signals.
  - Gaussian Mixture Models (GMMs): GMMs are often used for speaker recognition and speech enhancement. GMMs model speech signals as a weighted sum of Gaussian distributions, each representing a different acoustic class or cluster. GMMs can capture the statistical properties and diversity of speech signals.
  - Linear Prediction (LP) Models: LP models are commonly used for speech coding and speech synthesis. LP models assume that speech signals can be approximated by a linear combination of past samples, and use a set of coefficients to represent the linear filter. LP models can capture the spectral envelope and the pitch of speech signals.
- Non-parametric models do not make any assumptions about the underlying distribution or process of speech signals, and use data-driven methods to learn the model from the data. Examples of non-parametric models are:
  - Neural Network Models: Neural network models are increasingly popular for speech recognition, speech synthesis, speech enhancement, and speech analysis. Neural network models use a network of artificial neurons to learn complex nonlinear mappings between speech signals and desired outputs, such as words, phonemes, emotions, etc. Neural network models can learn from large amounts of data and generalize to unseen data.
  - Deep Generative Models: Deep generative models are a class of neural network models that can generate realistic speech signals from latent variables or inputs. Examples of deep generative models are:
    - Variational Autoencoders (VAEs): VAEs are a type of autoencoder that can learn a probabilistic latent representation of speech signals, and can generate new speech signals by sampling from the latent space. VAEs can model the uncertainty and diversity of speech signals.
    - Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs): GANs are a type of generative model that can learn to produce realistic speech signals by competing with a discriminator network that tries to distinguish between real and fake speech signals. GANs can model the high-dimensional and complex structure of speech signals.
    - WaveNet: WaveNet is a type of convolutional neural network that can generate high-quality speech signals by modeling the conditional probability of each sample given the previous samples and other inputs, such as text or speaker identity. WaveNet can model the fine-grained details and naturalness of speech signals.
