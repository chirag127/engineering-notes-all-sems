### Treebanks

- A treebank is a parsed text corpus that annotates syntactic or semantic sentence structure. 
- Treebanks are often created on top of a corpus that has already been annotated with part-of-speech tags. 
- Treebanks can be created completely manually, where linguists annotate each sentence with syntactic structure, or semi-automatically, where a parser assigns some syntactic structure which linguists then check and, if necessary, correct. 
- Treebanks can be divided into two main groups: treebanks that annotate phrase structure (for example the Penn Treebank or ICE-GB) and those that annotate dependency structure (for example the Prague Dependency Treebank or the Quranic Arabic Dependency Treebank ). 
- Treebanks are useful for natural language processing systems such as part-of-speech taggers, parsers, semantic analyzers and machine translation systems.  
- Treebanks are also useful for linguistic research and theory, as they provide empirical data and evidence for syntactic and semantic analysis. 
- Treebanks are expensive and time-consuming to create, as they require expert linguists, trained annotators, annotation tools, and quality management. 
- Treebanks are not perfect, as they may contain errors, inconsistencies, ambiguities, or biases.
