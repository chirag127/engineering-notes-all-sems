### Shallow parsing

- Shallow parsing, also known as chunking, is a process of extracting phrases from a sentence without analyzing its internal structure.
- Shallow parsing aims to identify the boundaries and labels of non-overlapping phrases, such as noun phrases, verb phrases, prepositional phrases, etc.
- Shallow parsing is useful for applications that do not require deep syntactic analysis, such as information extraction, text summarization, sentiment analysis, etc.
- Shallow parsing can be performed using rule-based or machine learning methods, or a combination of both.
- Rule-based methods use hand-crafted grammars or patterns to identify and label phrases based on part-of-speech tags, word classes, or lexical features.
- Machine learning methods use supervised or unsupervised algorithms to learn phrase structures from annotated or unannotated data, such as decision trees, hidden Markov models, conditional random fields, neural networks, etc.
- Shallow parsing can be evaluated using metrics such as precision, recall, and F-measure, which compare the predicted phrases with the gold standard phrases in a test set.
