### Time Alignment And Normalization for the notes of the Unit 6 - SPEECH-ANALYSIS in the subject of Natural Language Processing

- Time alignment is the process of finding the correspondence between the frames of two speech signals, such as the source and the target speakers in voice conversion, or the reference and the test speakers in speaker recognition.
- Time alignment is important for many applications of speech processing, such as speech recognition, text to speech conversion, voice conversion, speech synthesis, and speaker verification.
- Time alignment can be done by various methods, such as dynamic time warping (DTW), hidden Markov model (HMM), or speech recognition tools.
- Dynamic time warping (DTW) is a method that uses a distance measure, such as Euclidean distance or cosine distance, to compare the frames of two speech signals and find the optimal alignment path that minimizes the total distance.
- Hidden Markov model (HMM) is a method that uses a probabilistic model of speech, such as Gaussian mixture model (GMM) or deep neural network (DNN), to estimate the likelihood of each frame of one speech signal given the other speech signal and find the optimal alignment path that maximizes the total likelihood.
- Speech recognition tools are methods that use a linguistic model of speech, such as a phonetic dictionary or a language model, to segment and label the speech signals and find the alignment based on the word or phoneme boundaries.
- Normalization is the process of adjusting the acoustic features of speech signals, such as pitch, energy, duration, or formant frequencies, to reduce the variability due to speaker differences or environmental factors.
- Normalization is important for improving the performance of speech processing applications, such as enhancing the quality of voice conversion, increasing the accuracy of speaker recognition, or facilitating the comparison of speech signals.
- Normalization can be done by various methods, such as mean and variance normalization, z-score normalization, cepstral mean and variance normalization (CMVN), or vocal tract length normalization (VTLN).
- Mean and variance normalization is a method that subtracts the mean and divides by the standard deviation of each feature dimension of the speech signals to make them have zero mean and unit variance.
- Z-score normalization is a method that subtracts the mean and divides by the standard deviation of each feature dimension of the speech signals to make them have zero mean and unit variance, and then scales them by a factor to match the range of the original features.
- Cepstral mean and variance normalization (CMVN) is a method that subtracts the mean and divides by the standard deviation of each cepstral coefficient of the speech signals to make them have zero mean and unit variance, and then adds back the mean of the target speaker to match the target speaker's characteristics.
- Vocal tract length normalization (VTLN) is a method that applies a frequency warping function to the speech signals to make them have similar vocal tract lengths, and then adjusts the formant frequencies to match the target speaker's formants.
