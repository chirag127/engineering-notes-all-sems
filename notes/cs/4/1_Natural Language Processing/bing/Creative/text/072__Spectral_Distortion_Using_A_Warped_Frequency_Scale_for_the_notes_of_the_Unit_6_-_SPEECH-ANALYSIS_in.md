### Spectral Distortion Using A Warped Frequency Scale

- Spectral distortion is the difference between the original and the reconstructed spectrum of a signal, such as speech.
- A warped frequency scale is a nonlinear transformation of the frequency axis that changes the relative spacing of the frequency components of a signal.
- Warping the frequency scale can be used to improve the perceptual accuracy of spectral modeling, especially for low bit rate speech compression and speaker recognition applications.
- The idea behind warping the frequency scale is to emphasize the spectral regions that are more important for human perception, such as the formants, and de-emphasize the regions that are less important or more corrupted by noise, such as the high-frequency harmonics.
- A common way to warp the frequency scale is to use a perceptually motivated scale, such as the Bark scale or the Mel scale, which are based on the characteristics of the human auditory system, such as the critical bandwidths or the just noticeable differences in frequency.
- Another way to warp the frequency scale is to use a parameterized function, such as the bilinear transform or the all-pass transform, which can be adjusted to fit the desired warping characteristics.
- Warping the frequency scale can be applied to different spectral modeling techniques, such as linear predictive coding (LPC), discrete cosine transform (DCT), or sinusoidal coding.
- Warping the frequency scale can affect the perceived quality of the reconstructed spectrum, depending on the underlying signal spectrum or vowel quality. For example, warping the frequency scale can improve the quality of high-pitched vowels, but degrade the quality of low-pitched vowels.
- Warping the frequency scale can also affect the performance of speaker recognition systems, especially in noisy conditions. For example, warping the frequency scale can reduce the spectral distortion caused by additive noise, and enhance the speaker-specific features, such as the formant frequencies and bandwidths.
- Warping the frequency scale can be measured by different objective distortion measures, such as the mean squared error (MSE), the log spectral distortion (LSD), or the auditory distance measure (ADM). The ADM is based on the partial loudness of the spectral components, and takes into account the frequency masking effects of the human auditory system. The ADM has been shown to have a high correlation with the subjective test scores of the perceived quality of warped spectra.
