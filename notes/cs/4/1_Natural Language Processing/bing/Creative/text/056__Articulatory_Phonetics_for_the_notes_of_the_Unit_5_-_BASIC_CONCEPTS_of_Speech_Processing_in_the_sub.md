### Articulatory Phonetics

- Articulatory phonetics is a branch of phonetics that studies how humans produce speech sounds by using the movements and positions of the vocal organs (articulators)  .
- Articulatory phonetics is concerned with the transformation of aerodynamic energy (airflow through the vocal tract) into acoustic energy (sound waves) .
- Articulatory phonetics is usually treated within a broader context of the full speech chain, which also includes speech aerodynamics, speech acoustics, and speech perception .
- Articulatory phonetics is important for understanding the nature and diversity of speech sounds and sound patterns across languages, as well as the factors that influence speech production and change over time .
- Articulatory phonetics uses various terms and categories to describe the speech sounds and their articulations, such as place of articulation, manner of articulation, voicing, airstream mechanism, etc. .
- Articulatory phonetics also uses various experimental techniques and instruments to measure and analyze the speech movements and sounds, such as palatography, electromyography, ultrasound, etc.  .
