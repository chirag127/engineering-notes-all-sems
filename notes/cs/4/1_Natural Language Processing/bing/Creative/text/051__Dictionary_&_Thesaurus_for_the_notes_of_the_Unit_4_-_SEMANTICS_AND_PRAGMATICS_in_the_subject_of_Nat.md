### Dictionary & Thesaurus for the notes of the Unit 4 - SEMANTICS AND PRAGMATICS in the subject of Natural Language Processing

- A dictionary is a collection of words and their meanings, often with additional information such as pronunciation, usage, synonyms, antonyms, etymology, etc.
- A thesaurus is a collection of words and their synonyms, often with additional information such as usage, related terms, categories, etc.
- Both dictionary and thesaurus are useful resources for natural language processing, as they can help with tasks such as:
  - Word sense disambiguation: determining the intended meaning of a word in a given context, based on its definition and usage examples in a dictionary.
  - Lexical substitution: replacing a word with another word that has a similar or identical meaning, based on its synonyms in a thesaurus.
  - Lexical expansion: generating more words that are related to a given word, based on its synonyms, antonyms, hypernyms, hyponyms, meronyms, holonyms, etc. in a thesaurus.
  - Lexical simplification: replacing a word with another word that has a simpler or more common meaning, based on its definition and usage examples in a dictionary.
  - Lexical analysis: identifying the part of speech, morphology, and semantic features of a word, based on its entry in a dictionary.
- However, dictionary and thesaurus also have some limitations for natural language processing, such as:
  - Incompleteness: not all words and meanings are covered in a dictionary or a thesaurus, especially for new, rare, or domain-specific terms.
  - Ambiguity: some words and meanings are ambiguous or vague in a dictionary or a thesaurus, and may require human interpretation or additional context to resolve.
  - Inconsistency: different dictionaries and thesauri may have different definitions, synonyms, or usage examples for the same word or meaning, and may not agree on the semantic relations among words.
  - Subjectivity: some words and meanings are subjective or evaluative in a dictionary or a thesaurus, and may reflect the opinions or preferences of the authors or users, rather than objective facts.
