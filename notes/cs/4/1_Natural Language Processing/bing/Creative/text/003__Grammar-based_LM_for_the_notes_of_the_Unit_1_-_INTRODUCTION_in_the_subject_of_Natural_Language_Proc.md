### Grammar-based LM

- A grammar-based language model (LM) is a type of probabilistic model that assigns probabilities to sentences or sequences of words based on a set of rules or constraints that define the structure and syntax of a language.
- A grammar-based LM can be seen as a generative model that produces sentences by applying rules from a grammar, such as a context-free grammar (CFG) or a tree-adjoining grammar (TAG).
- A grammar-based LM can also be seen as a discriminative model that evaluates the likelihood of a sentence by parsing it into a syntactic tree and computing the probability of the tree given the grammar and the words.
- A grammar-based LM can capture long-range dependencies and complex syntactic phenomena that are difficult to model with n-gram LMs, which only consider local word contexts.
- A grammar-based LM can also incorporate semantic and pragmatic information into the grammar rules, such as word sense disambiguation, anaphora resolution, or discourse coherence.
- A grammar-based LM can be trained on a corpus of sentences annotated with syntactic trees, or on a corpus of plain text using unsupervised or semi-supervised methods, such as the inside-outside algorithm, expectation-maximization, or variational inference.
- A grammar-based LM can be used for various natural language processing tasks, such as speech recognition, machine translation, text summarization, natural language generation, or natural language understanding.
