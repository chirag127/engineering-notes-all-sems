### Bootstrapping methods for the notes of the Unit 4 - SEMANTICS AND PRAGMATICS in the subject of Natural Language Processing

- Bootstrapping methods are a type of semi-supervised learning techniques that use a small set of labeled examples (seeds) and a large set of unlabeled examples to learn a mapping from input to output.
- Bootstrapping methods aim to enlarge the labeled set by iteratively selecting the most appropriate unlabeled examples and assigning them labels based on some criteria.
- Bootstrapping methods can be applied to various natural language processing tasks, such as relation extraction, semantic categorization, named entity recognition, information extraction, and subjectivity detection.
- Bootstrapping methods typically follow a general framework that consists of the following steps:
  - Start with an empty list of things (e.g., words, phrases, patterns, etc.).
  - Initialize the list with carefully chosen seeds (e.g., seed words, seed patterns, etc.).
  - Repeat until a stopping criterion is met:
    - Use the current list to find new candidates from the unlabeled set (e.g., by matching patterns, by querying classifiers, by applying heuristics, etc.).
    - Rank the candidates by some measure of confidence or relevance (e.g., by uncertainty, by committee, by entropy, by frequency, by similarity, etc.).
    - Add the top-ranked candidates to the list and assign them labels.
- Bootstrapping methods have some advantages and disadvantages compared to other semi-supervised learning techniques:
  - Advantages:
    - They can leverage large amounts of unlabeled data without requiring human annotation.
    - They can discover new knowledge from the data that is not present in the seeds.
    - They can adapt to different domains and tasks by changing the seeds and the criteria.
  - Disadvantages:
    - They are sensitive to the quality and diversity of the seeds, which may affect the performance and generalization of the learned model.
    - They are prone to semantic drift, which is the gradual loss of the original meaning of the seeds due to the inclusion of noisy or irrelevant candidates.
    - They may suffer from data sparsity, which is the lack of sufficient examples for some classes or categories in the unlabeled set.
