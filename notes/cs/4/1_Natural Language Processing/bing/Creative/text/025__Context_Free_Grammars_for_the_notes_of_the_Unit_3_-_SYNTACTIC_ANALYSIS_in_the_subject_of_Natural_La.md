### Context Free Grammars

- A context free grammar (CFG) is a formal system for describing the syntax of a natural language.
- A CFG consists of four components: a set of terminal symbols, a set of non-terminal symbols, a start symbol, and a set of production rules.
- Terminal symbols are the basic units of a language, such as words or punctuation marks. Non-terminal symbols are placeholders for sequences of terminal symbols, such as phrases or clauses.
- The start symbol is a special non-terminal symbol that represents the whole sentence or utterance.
- Production rules are the instructions for how to rewrite non-terminal symbols into sequences of terminal or non-terminal symbols, following the syntax of the language.
- A CFG can generate a set of sentences that belong to the language by starting from the start symbol and applying production rules until only terminal symbols are left.
- A CFG can also parse a given sentence by reversing the generation process and finding a sequence of production rules that can derive the sentence from the start symbol.
- A CFG is said to be context free because the production rules only depend on the non-terminal symbol being rewritten, and not on the surrounding symbols or the position in the sentence.
- A CFG can capture the hierarchical structure of natural language sentences, such as the distinction between noun phrases and verb phrases, or the nesting of clauses within clauses.
- A CFG can also account for some of the ambiguity and variability of natural language, such as the multiple ways to parse a sentence with coordination or prepositional phrases, or the different word orders that are possible in some languages.
