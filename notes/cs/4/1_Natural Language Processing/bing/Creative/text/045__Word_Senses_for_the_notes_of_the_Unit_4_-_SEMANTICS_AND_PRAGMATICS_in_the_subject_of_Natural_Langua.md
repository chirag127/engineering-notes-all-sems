### Word Senses

- A word sense is a specific meaning or usage of a word in a language.
- A word can have multiple senses depending on the context, such as "bank" meaning a financial institution or the edge of a river.
- Word senses are often represented by a sense identifier, such as a number or a label, that distinguishes them from other senses of the same word.
- Word senses can be organized into a hierarchy or a network, where more general senses are linked to more specific ones, such as "animal" being a hypernym of "dog" and "dog" being a hyponym of "animal".
- Word senses can also be related by other semantic relations, such as synonymy, antonymy, meronymy, holonymy, etc.
- For example, "bright" and "brilliant" are synonyms, "hot" and "cold" are antonyms, "finger" and "hand" are meronyms, and "car" and "wheel" are holonyms.
- Word sense disambiguation (WSD) is the task of identifying the correct sense of a word in a given context, such as a sentence or a document.
- WSD is important for natural language processing applications, such as machine translation, information retrieval, text summarization, etc.
- WSD can be performed using various methods, such as rule-based, knowledge-based, supervised, unsupervised, or semi-supervised approaches.
- Rule-based methods use manually crafted rules or heuristics to assign senses based on linguistic cues, such as part-of-speech tags, collocations, etc.
- Knowledge-based methods use external resources, such as dictionaries, thesauri, ontologies, etc., to find semantic similarities or associations between words and senses.
- Supervised methods use annotated corpora, where each word is labeled with its correct sense, to train machine learning models, such as decision trees, neural networks, etc., to predict senses for new words.
- Unsupervised methods use unannotated corpora, where the senses are not given, to cluster words based on their co-occurrence patterns, distributional features, etc., and then assign senses to the clusters.
- Semi-supervised methods use a combination of annotated and unannotated corpora, where the annotated data is used to bootstrap the learning process and the unannotated data is used to refine the sense inventory or the model parameters.
