### Rule-based

- Rule-based methods are one of the approaches to perform word level analysis in natural language processing (NLP).
- Word level analysis involves identifying and categorizing the units of meaning in a given text, such as words, morphemes, or tokens.
- Rule-based methods rely on predefined rules and patterns to perform word level analysis, such as regular expressions, finite state automata, or context-free grammars.
- Rule-based methods have some advantages and disadvantages compared to other methods, such as statistical or neural methods.
- Some of the advantages are:
  - They are transparent and interpretable, as the rules and patterns are explicitly defined and can be inspected or modified.
  - They are robust and consistent, as they do not depend on external data or resources that may be noisy or incomplete.
  - They are efficient and scalable, as they can process large amounts of text quickly and with low computational cost.
- Some of the disadvantages are:
  - They are brittle and rigid, as they may fail to handle exceptions, variations, or ambiguities that are not covered by the rules or patterns.
  - They are labor-intensive and domain-specific, as they require manual creation and maintenance of the rules or patterns, which may not generalize well to different domains or languages.
  - They are limited and simplistic, as they may not capture the complexity and richness of natural language, such as semantics, pragmatics, or discourse.
