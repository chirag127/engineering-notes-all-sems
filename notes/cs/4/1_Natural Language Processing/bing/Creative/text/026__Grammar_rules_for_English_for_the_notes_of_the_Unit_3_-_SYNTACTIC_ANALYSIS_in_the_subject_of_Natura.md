### Grammar rules for English for the notes of the Unit 3 - SYNTACTIC ANALYSIS in the subject of Natural Language Processing

- Grammar rules are the conventions that govern how words and phrases are arranged to form sentences in a language.
- Grammar rules can be divided into two categories: **prescriptive** and **descriptive**.
  - Prescriptive rules are the norms and standards that prescribe how a language should be used, such as avoiding split infinitives or ending sentences with prepositions.
  - Descriptive rules are the observations and generalizations that describe how a language is actually used, such as the fact that some speakers use double negatives or drop the subject in informal speech.
- Grammar rules can also be classified according to the level of linguistic analysis they belong to: **phonological**, **morphological**, **syntactic**, **semantic**, or **pragmatic**.
  - Phonological rules are the rules that govern the sound system of a language, such as the patterns of stress, intonation, and pronunciation.
  - Morphological rules are the rules that govern the structure and formation of words, such as the rules of inflection, derivation, and compounding.
  - Syntactic rules are the rules that govern the structure and formation of phrases and sentences, such as the rules of word order, agreement, and subordination.
  - Semantic rules are the rules that govern the meaning and interpretation of words, phrases, and sentences, such as the rules of reference, entailment, and ambiguity.
  - Pragmatic rules are the rules that govern the use and function of language in context, such as the rules of politeness, relevance, and implicature.
- Grammar rules can be represented using different formalisms and notations, such as **phrase structure rules**, **dependency rules**, **transformational rules**, or **feature structures**.
  - Phrase structure rules are the rules that specify how words can be combined into phrases and phrases into sentences, using symbols and brackets to indicate the hierarchical structure and category of each constituent.
  - Dependency rules are the rules that specify how words can be linked to each other by syntactic relations, such as subject, object, modifier, or complement, using arrows and labels to indicate the direction and type of each dependency.
  - Transformational rules are the rules that specify how sentences can be derived from or transformed into other sentences, using symbols and operators to indicate the movement, deletion, or insertion of constituents.
  - Feature structures are the rules that specify how words and phrases can be characterized by sets of features and values, such as number, gender, case, or tense, using brackets and colons to indicate the feature-value pairs of each constituent.
