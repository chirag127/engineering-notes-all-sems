### Probabilistic CFG

- Probabilistic Context Free Grammar (PCFG) is an extension of Context Free Grammar (CFG) with a probability for each production rule .
- The probability of a production rule indicates how likely it is to be used in a derivation of a sentence from the grammar.
- The sum of the probabilities of all the production rules with the same left-hand side must be equal to one.
- PCFGs are useful for dealing with ambiguity, which is the situation where a sentence can have more than one possible parse tree or meaning.
- PCFGs can also provide a probabilistic language model, which assigns a probability to any string of words based on how well it conforms to the grammar.
- PCFGs can be learned from a corpus of annotated sentences, such as the Penn Treebank, using the maximum likelihood estimation method.
- PCFGs can be used for parsing, which is the task of finding the most likely parse tree for a given sentence and grammar.
- PCFGs can be converted to Chomsky Normal Form (CNF), which is a simplified form where each production rule has either two non-terminals or one terminal on the right-hand side.
- PCFGs are related to Hidden Markov Models (HMMs), which are probabilistic models for regular grammars.
- PCFGs have applications in natural language processing, such as syntactic analysis, machine translation, speech recognition, and information extraction.
- PCFGs have also been applied in other domains, such as modeling the structure of RNA molecules and designing programming languages.
