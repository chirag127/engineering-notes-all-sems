### Evaluating N-grams for the notes of the Unit 2 - WORD LEVEL ANALYSIS in the subject of Natural Language Processing

- N-grams are sequences of N words from a given text or speech. For example, "natural language processing" is a 3-gram or trigram. N-grams are used to model the probability of a word given its previous words in a sentence.
- N-gram models are based on the Markov assumption, which states that the probability of a word depends only on a fixed number of previous words, not on the entire history. For example, a bigram model assumes that P(wi|w1,...,wi-1) ≈ P(wi|wi-1), meaning that the probability of a word only depends on the previous word.
- N-gram models can be estimated by counting the frequency of N-grams in a large corpus of text and applying smoothing techniques to deal with unseen or rare N-grams. For example, the maximum likelihood estimate of a bigram probability is P(wi|wi-1) = C(wi-1,wi)/C(wi-1), where C is the count function.
- N-gram models can be evaluated by using intrinsic or extrinsic methods. Intrinsic methods measure how well the model fits the data, usually by using metrics such as perplexity or log-likelihood. Perplexity is the inverse of the average probability assigned by the model to each word in a test set. A lower perplexity means a better fit. Log-likelihood is the sum of the logarithms of the probabilities assigned by the model to each N-gram in a test set. A higher log-likelihood means a better fit.
- Extrinsic methods measure how well the model performs on a specific task, such as machine translation or text summarization. Extrinsic methods usually compare the output of the model with a reference or a human judgment, using metrics such as BLEU or ROUGE. BLEU is a metric that compares the N-grams in the model output with the N-grams in the reference, and computes a score based on the precision and brevity of the output. ROUGE is a metric that compares the N-grams in the model output with the N-grams in the reference, and computes a score based on the recall and length of the output.
- N-gram models have some advantages and disadvantages. Some advantages are that they are simple, fast, and easy to implement and train. They can capture some local syntactic and semantic information, such as word collocations and idioms. They can also be combined with other models, such as neural networks or hidden Markov models, to improve their performance. Some disadvantages are that they suffer from data sparsity, meaning that they cannot assign probabilities to unseen or rare N-grams. They also have a limited context, meaning that they cannot capture long-range dependencies or global information. They can also produce unnatural or ungrammatical sentences, especially when using higher-order N-grams.
