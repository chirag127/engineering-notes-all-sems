### Relations between Senses for the notes of the Unit 4 - SEMANTICS AND PRAGMATICS in the subject of Natural Language Processing

- Natural language processing (NLP) is the subfield of computer science concerned with using computational techniques to learn, understand, and produce human language content.
- One of the challenges of NLP is to deal with the ambiguity and variability of natural language, especially when words can have multiple meanings or senses depending on the context.
- Word sense disambiguation (WSD) is the task of identifying the correct sense of a word in a given sentence or text .
- For example, the word "bank" can have different senses, such as a financial institution, a river shore, or a verb meaning to tilt or turn. WSD aims to determine which sense is intended by the speaker or writer in a specific situation.
- WSD is important for NLP applications such as machine translation, information retrieval, text summarization, question answering, and sentiment analysis, as it can improve the accuracy and relevance of the output.
- There are different types of relations between senses that can help in WSD, such as synonymy, antonymy, hyponymy, hypernymy, meronymy, and holonymy.
- Synonymy is the relation between words that have the same or similar meaning, such as "big" and "large". Synonyms can be used to expand or refine the search space for a given word sense.
- Antonymy is the relation between words that have opposite or contrasting meanings, such as "hot" and "cold". Antonyms can be used to eliminate or reduce the search space for a given word sense.
- Hyponymy is the relation between words that denote a specific instance or subclass of a more general class, such as "dog" and "animal". Hyponyms can be used to specify or narrow down the search space for a given word sense.
- Hypernymy is the relation between words that denote a more general or superordinate class of a specific instance or subclass, such as "animal" and "dog". Hypernyms can be used to generalize or broaden the search space for a given word sense.
- Meronymy is the relation between words that denote a part of a whole, such as "finger" and "hand". Meronyms can be used to identify or locate the search space for a given word sense.
- Holonymy is the relation between words that denote a whole of which a part is denoted by another word, such as "hand" and "finger". Holonyms can be used to identify or locate the search space for a given word sense.
- These relations between senses can be represented in a hierarchical or network structure, such as WordNet, which is a large lexical database of English words and their semantic relations.
- WordNet can be used as a knowledge source for WSD, as it provides a set of possible senses for each word, along with their definitions, examples, and relations to other words.
- WSD methods can be classified into two main categories: knowledge-based and supervised.
- Knowledge-based methods rely on external sources of information, such as dictionaries, thesauri, ontologies, or corpora, to infer the word sense from the context. They do not require annotated data, but they may suffer from low coverage or accuracy.
- Supervised methods rely on machine learning techniques, such as decision trees, neural networks, or support vector machines, to learn the word sense from annotated data. They require a large amount of labeled data, but they may achieve higher accuracy and robustness.
