### Ambiguity for the notes of the Unit 3 - SYNTACTIC ANALYSIS in the subject of Natural Language Processing

- Ambiguity is the property of having more than one meaning or being understood in more than one way .
- Ambiguity is a challenging task in natural language understanding (NLU) and natural language generation (NLG) because it can cause confusion, misunderstanding, or incorrect interpretation .
- Ambiguity can occur at various levels of natural language processing (NLP), such as lexical, syntactic, semantic, and pragmatic .
- Lexical ambiguity occurs when a word or phrase has more than one possible meaning, such as "bank" or "play" .
- Syntactic ambiguity occurs when a sentence or phrase has more than one possible structure or interpretation, such as "I saw the man with the telescope" or "They are flying planes" .
- Semantic ambiguity occurs when a word, phrase, or sentence has more than one possible meaning or implication, such as "He is mad" or "She is looking for a match" .
- Pragmatic ambiguity occurs when a word, phrase, or sentence has more than one possible meaning or implication depending on the context, such as "Can you pass the salt?" or "Do you have the time?" .
- The process of resolving or eliminating ambiguity is called disambiguation .
- Disambiguation can be done by using various techniques, such as word sense disambiguation, part-of-speech tagging, parsing, named entity recognition, chunking, anaphora resolution, etc  .
- Disambiguation can also be done by using external knowledge sources, such as ontologies, dictionaries, thesauri, corpora, etc .
- Disambiguation is important for improving the accuracy and efficiency of NLP applications, such as question answering, information extraction, machine translation, text summarization, sentiment analysis, etc .

Some mnemonics and learning tricks for ambiguity are:

- Lexical ambiguity can be remembered as "one word, many meanings" .
- Syntactic ambiguity can be remembered as "one sentence, many structures" .
- Semantic ambiguity can be remembered as "one meaning, many implications" .
- Pragmatic ambiguity can be remembered as "one implication, many contexts" .
- Disambiguation can be remembered as "many to one" mapping .
