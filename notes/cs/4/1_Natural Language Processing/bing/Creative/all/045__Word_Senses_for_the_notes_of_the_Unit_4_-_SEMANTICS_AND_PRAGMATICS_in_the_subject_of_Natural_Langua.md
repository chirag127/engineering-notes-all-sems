### Word Senses for the notes of the Unit 4 - SEMANTICS AND PRAGMATICS in the subject of Natural Language Processing

- A word sense is a representation of one aspect of a word's meaning.
- A word can have multiple senses, depending on the context in which it is used.
- For example, the word "bank" can have different senses, such as "financial institution", "sloping mound", "biological repository", or "building where a bank does its business".
- Word senses can be related to each other in various ways, such as synonymy, antonymy, hypernymy, metonymy, or meronymy.
- Synonymy: Two senses of different words are synonyms of each other if their meaning is nearly identical. For example, "couch" and "sofa" are synonyms.
- Antonymy: Two senses of different words are antonyms of each other if their meaning is nearly opposite. For example, "hot" and "cold" are antonyms.
- Hypernymy: A sense of a word is a hypernym of another sense of a word if it is more general or abstract. For example, "animal" is a hypernym of "dog".
- Metonymy: A sense of a word is a metonym of another sense of a word if it is used to refer to something that is associated with it. For example, "the White House" is a metonym of "the U.S. government".
- Meronymy: A sense of a word is a meronym of another sense of a word if it is a part or a member of it. For example, "finger" is a meronym of "hand".
- Word sense disambiguation (WSD) is a subfield of natural language processing (NLP) that deals with determining the intended meaning of a word in a given context .
- WSD is a challenging task because natural language is ambiguous and many words can be interpreted in multiple ways depending on the context.
- WSD can be useful for various NLP applications, such as machine translation, information retrieval, text summarization, sentiment analysis, etc .
- WSD can be performed using different methods, such as rule-based, knowledge-based, supervised, unsupervised, or semi-supervised .
- Rule-based methods use manually crafted rules or heuristics to disambiguate words based on linguistic features or clues.
- Knowledge-based methods use external sources of information, such as dictionaries, thesauri, ontologies, or corpora, to find semantic relations or similarities between words.
- Supervised methods use annotated data to train machine learning models, such as decision trees, naive Bayes, support vector machines, or neural networks, to classify words into their senses .
- Unsupervised methods use unannotated data to cluster words into their senses based on their co-occurrence patterns or distributional features .
- Semi-supervised methods use a combination of annotated and unannotated data to leverage both the advantages of supervised and unsupervised methods .

Some possible mnemonics and learning tricks for word senses are:

- To remember the types of word sense relations, think of SAM HM: Synonymy, Antonymy, Metonymy, Hypernymy, Meronymy.
- To remember the types of WSD methods, think of RUK SS: Rule-based, Unsupervised, Knowledge-based, Supervised, Semi-supervised.
- To remember the difference between hypernymy and meronymy, think of the phrase "a hypernym is higher, a meronym is a member".
- To remember the difference between metonymy and synonymy, think of the phrase "a metonym is a mate, a synonym is the same".
