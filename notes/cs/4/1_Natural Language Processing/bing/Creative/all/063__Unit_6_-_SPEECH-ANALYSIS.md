## Unit 6 - SPEECH-ANALYSIS

Speech analysis is the process of evaluating a speech for its purpose, audience, and rhetorical choices. Speech analysis can help improve the communication skills of both the speaker and the listener. Speech analysis can also be used to critique a speech for its effectiveness and persuasiveness.

### Elements of a Speech

A speech typically consists of three elements: the introduction, the body, and the conclusion.

- The introduction is the opening of the speech. It usually contains a hook, which is an attention-getter that draws the audience into the speech. The hook can be a joke, a fact, a story, or a question. The introduction also provides a preview of the main idea and the purpose of the speech.

- The body is the longest section of the speech. It contains the main ideas and the supporting details of the speech. The body can also include arguments and counterarguments, which are the speaker's side and the anticipated opposition of a topic. The body is where the speaker makes various rhetorical choices, such as the use of facts, statistics, anecdotes, examples, metaphors, similes, analogies, or repetition.

- The conclusion is the closing of the speech. It usually summarizes the main points and the main idea of the speech. The conclusion can also include a call to action, which is a final motivation for the audience to agree with the speaker or to do something.

### Steps of Speech Analysis

Speech analysis can be simplified into three basic steps: analyzing the purpose, identifying the audience, and assessing the effectiveness of the rhetorical choices.

- Analyzing the purpose is the first step in speech analysis. The purpose can be to inform, to persuade, or to entertain the audience. An informative speech is meant to share information, a persuasive speech is meant to argue a point, and an entertaining speech is meant to amuse or delight the audience.

- Identifying the audience is the second step in speech analysis. The audience is the group of people who listen to the speech. The audience can have different characteristics, such as age, gender, education, culture, or interests. The speaker should tailor the speech to suit the audience's needs, expectations, and preferences.

- Assessing the effectiveness of the rhetorical choices is the third step in speech analysis. The rhetorical choices are the techniques that the speaker uses to achieve the purpose and to connect with the audience. The rhetorical choices can include the tone, the style, the language, the structure, the delivery, and the visual aids of the speech. The effectiveness of the rhetorical choices can be measured by how well they support the main idea, how well they appeal to the audience's emotions, logic, or values, and how well they create a lasting impression on the audience.
