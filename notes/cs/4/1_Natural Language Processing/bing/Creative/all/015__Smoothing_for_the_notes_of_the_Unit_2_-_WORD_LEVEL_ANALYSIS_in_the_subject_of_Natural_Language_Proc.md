### Smoothing for the notes of the Unit 2 - WORD LEVEL ANALYSIS in the subject of Natural Language Processing

- Smoothing is the process of flattening a probability distribution implied by a language model so that all reasonable word sequences can occur with some probability.
- Smoothing is needed to address the data sparsity problem, which occurs when some words or n-grams have zero or very low frequency in the training data, but may appear in the test data .
- Smoothing techniques usually involve redistributing some probability mass from the observed events to the unseen events, based on some assumptions or heuristics.
- Some common smoothing techniques are:

  - **Laplace smoothing** (also known as **add-one smoothing**): This technique adds one to the count of every word or n-gram, regardless of whether it is observed or not. This avoids zero probabilities, but also overestimates the probability of unseen events and underestimates the probability of frequent events .
  - **Additive smoothing** (also known as **add-k smoothing**): This technique is a generalization of Laplace smoothing, where a constant k (usually less than one) is added to the count of every word or n-gram. This allows more flexibility in adjusting the amount of smoothing, but also requires tuning the value of k .
  - **Good-Turing smoothing**: This technique estimates the probability of unseen events based on the frequency of events that occurred only once (called singletons) in the training data. It also adjusts the probability of other events based on their frequency. This technique is more sophisticated than additive smoothing, but also more complex and computationally expensive .
  - **Kneser-Ney smoothing**: This technique is a refinement of Good-Turing smoothing, which takes into account the context of the n-grams. It uses the concept of absolute discounting, which subtracts a constant d from the count of every n-gram, and then allocates the discounted mass to the lower-order n-grams based on their continuation probability (the probability of an n-gram appearing as a suffix of another n-gram). This technique is considered one of the best smoothing methods for n-gram models .
  - **Katz smoothing**: This technique is another refinement of Good-Turing smoothing, which uses a back-off model to estimate the probability of unseen n-grams. A back-off model is a hierarchical model that falls back to a lower-order n-gram model when the higher-order n-gram model is unreliable or undefined. Katz smoothing assigns a discounted probability to the observed n-grams based on their frequency, and a back-off probability to the unseen n-grams based on the lower-order n-grams .
  - **Church and Gale smoothing**: This technique is similar to Katz smoothing, but uses a different way of computing the back-off probability. It uses a deleted interpolation method, which combines the probabilities of different n-gram models with some weights that are estimated from a held-out data set (a separate data set that is not used for training or testing). This technique is more data-driven than Katz smoothing, but also requires more data and computation.

- A mnemonic to remember the names of these smoothing techniques is **LAGKKC** (pronounced as "lag kick"), which stands for **L**aplace, **A**dditive, **G**ood-Turing, **K**neser-Ney, **K**atz, and **C**hurch and Gale.
