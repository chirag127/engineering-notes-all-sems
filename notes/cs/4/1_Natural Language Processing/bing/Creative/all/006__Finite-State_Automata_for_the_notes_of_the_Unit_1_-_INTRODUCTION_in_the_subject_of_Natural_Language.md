### Finite-State Automata for the notes of the Unit 1 - INTRODUCTION in the subject of Natural Language Processing

- Finite-state automata (FSA) are abstract machines that can process natural language by following a predetermined sequence of operations and transitions between a finite number of states .
- FSA can be used to model various natural language processing tasks, such as speech recognition, spelling correction, information retrieval, morphological analysis, etc    .
- FSA can be classified into two types: deterministic finite automata (DFA) and non-deterministic finite automata (NDFA)  .
- DFA are FSA that have a unique transition for each input symbol and state, while NDFA are FSA that can have multiple transitions for the same input symbol and state  .
- DFA and NDFA can be represented by a 5-tuple (Q, Σ, δ, q0, F), where Q is a finite set of states, Σ is a finite set of symbols (the alphabet), δ is the transition function, q0 is the initial state, and F is the set of final states  .
- DFA and NDFA can also be represented by state diagrams, where the states are represented by vertices, the transitions are shown by labeled arcs, the initial state is represented by an empty incoming arc, and the final state is represented by a double circle  .
- DFA and NDFA are equivalent in terms of expressive power, meaning that for every NDFA there exists an equivalent DFA that accepts the same language, and vice versa  .
- However, DFA are more efficient in terms of time and space complexity, as they can process any input in linear time and constant space, while NDFA may require exponential time and space in the worst case  .
- A special type of FSA that can produce an output for a given input is called a finite-state transducer (FST)  .
- FST can be used to model natural language transformations, such as phonological rules, morphological analysis, text rewriting, etc  .
- FST can be classified into two types: sequential FST and subsequential FST  .
- Sequential FST are FST that have a unique output for each input, while subsequential FST are FST that can have multiple outputs for the same input, but only one output for each input prefix  .
- Sequential FST and subsequential FST can be represented by a 6-tuple (Q, Σ, Γ, δ, q0, F), where Q, Σ, δ, q0, and F are the same as in FSA, and Γ is the output alphabet  .
- Sequential FST and subsequential FST can also be represented by state diagrams, where the transitions are shown by labeled arcs with the input symbol and the output symbol separated by a slash  .
- Sequential FST and subsequential FST are equivalent in terms of expressive power, meaning that for every subsequential FST there exists an equivalent sequential FST that produces the same output, and vice versa  .
- However, sequential FST are more efficient in terms of time and space complexity, as they can process any input in linear time and constant space, while subsequential FST may require exponential time and space in the worst case  .
