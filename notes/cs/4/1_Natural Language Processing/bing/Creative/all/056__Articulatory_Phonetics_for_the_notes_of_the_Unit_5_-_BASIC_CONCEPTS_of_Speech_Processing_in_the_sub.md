### Articulatory Phonetics for the notes of the Unit 5 - BASIC CONCEPTS of Speech Processing in the subject of Natural Language Processing

- Articulatory phonetics is the branch of phonetics that studies how humans produce speech sounds by using the movements and positions of the vocal organs (articulators) .
- Articulatory phonetics is concerned with the transformation of aerodynamic energy (airflow through the vocal tract) into acoustic energy (sound waves) .
- Articulatory phonetics is usually treated within a broader context of the full speech chain, which also includes speech aerodynamics, speech acoustics, and speech perception .
- Articulatory phonetics can be divided into two main components: initiation and articulation .
- Initiation is the process of generating airflow from the lungs or other sources to create sound .
- Articulation is the process of modifying the airflow by using various articulators to produce different speech sounds .
- The main articulators are the glottis, the pharynx, the velum, the tongue, and the lips .
- Articulatory phonetics can be used to describe and classify speech sounds in terms of their place of articulation, manner of articulation, and voicing .
- Place of articulation refers to the location of the main constriction or contact between the articulators .
- Manner of articulation refers to the degree and type of constriction or contact between the articulators .
- Voicing refers to the presence or absence of vocal fold vibration during the production of a speech sound .
- Articulatory phonetics can also be used to analyze the prosodic features of speech, such as stress, intonation, and tone, which are related to the variations in pitch, loudness, and duration of speech sounds .
- Articulatory phonetics can help us understand the linguistic diversity and theoretical implications of speech production, such as the possible and impossible speech sounds and sound patterns, the sources of variation and change in pronunciation, and the influence of perception and communication on articulation .
- Articulatory phonetics can be studied experimentally by using various techniques and instruments, such as palatography, electropalatography, ultrasound, X-ray, MRI, EMG, and EPG, to measure and visualize the movements and positions of the articulators and the airflow during speech production  .
