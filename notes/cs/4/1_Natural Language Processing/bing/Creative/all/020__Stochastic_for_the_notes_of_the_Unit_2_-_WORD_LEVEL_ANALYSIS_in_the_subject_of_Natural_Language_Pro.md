### Stochastic for the notes of the Unit 2 - WORD LEVEL ANALYSIS in the subject of Natural Language Processing

- Stochastic means involving randomness or probability. Stochastic methods in natural language processing (NLP) use statistical, probabilistic and data-driven techniques to deal with the ambiguity and complexity of natural languages.  
- Stochastic methods can be applied to various levels of linguistic analysis, such as phonology, morphology, syntax, semantics and pragmatics. For example, stochastic methods can be used to model the probability of a word given its context, the probability of a syntactic structure given a sentence, or the probability of a semantic interpretation given a discourse.  
- Stochastic methods can also be used for various NLP tasks, such as speech recognition, machine translation, information extraction, text summarization, sentiment analysis, question answering and dialogue systems. For example, stochastic methods can be used to find the most likely transcription of a speech signal, the most likely translation of a source text, the most likely entities and relations in a text, the most likely summary of a document, the most likely sentiment of a text, the most likely answer to a question, or the most likely response to a user input.  
- Stochastic methods rely on large corpora of natural language data, which are used to estimate the parameters of probabilistic models, such as n-gram models, hidden Markov models, probabilistic context-free grammars, probabilistic semantic models, or neural network models. These models can capture various aspects of natural language, such as word frequencies, collocations, word classes, syntactic categories, syntactic rules, semantic concepts, semantic relations, or pragmatic intentions.  
- Stochastic methods have some advantages and disadvantages compared to rule-based or knowledge-based methods in NLP. Some advantages are that stochastic methods can handle noisy, incomplete or inconsistent data, can adapt to new domains or languages, can learn from data without requiring extensive human annotation or intervention, and can provide confidence scores or multiple hypotheses for the output. Some disadvantages are that stochastic methods require large amounts of data, can be computationally expensive, can be difficult to interpret or explain, and can be prone to errors or biases due to data sparsity, data quality, or model assumptions.  

Some mnemonics and learning tricks for stochastic methods in NLP are:

- Remember that stochastic methods are based on statistics, so they use numbers to represent natural language phenomena. A simple way to remember this is: stochastic = statistics = numbers.
- Remember that stochastic methods use probability to model uncertainty and variability in natural language. A simple way to remember this is: stochastic = probability = chance.
- Remember that stochastic methods use data to estimate the parameters of probabilistic models. A simple way to remember this is: stochastic = data = evidence.
