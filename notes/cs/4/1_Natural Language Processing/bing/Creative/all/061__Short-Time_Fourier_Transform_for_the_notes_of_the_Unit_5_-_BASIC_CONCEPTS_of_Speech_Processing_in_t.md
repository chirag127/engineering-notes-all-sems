### Short-Time Fourier Transform for the notes of the Unit 5 - BASIC CONCEPTS of Speech Processing in the subject of Natural Language Processing

- The Short-Time Fourier Transform (STFT) is a powerful general-purpose tool for audio signal processing that defines a class of time-frequency distributions that specify complex amplitude versus time and frequency for any signal.
- The STFT is useful for analyzing speech signals, which are non-stationary, meaning that their spectral content changes over time. For example, the formants of speech change as a function of the spoken phonemes.
- The STFT is obtained by applying the Discrete Fourier Transform (DFT) to short segments of the speech signal that are windowed by a windowing function, such as a Hann or Hamming window. The windowing function is generally tapered at its ends to avoid unnatural discontinuities in the speech segment.
- The windowing function also affects the spectral estimate computed on the windowed segment. The window is selected to trade off the width of its main lobe and attenuation of its side lobes. A narrow window gives better time resolution but worse frequency resolution, and vice versa.
- The STFT can be viewed as a 2D plot of the relative energy content in frequency at different time locations, also known as a spectrogram. A spectrogram can be displayed in linear or logarithmic scale, and can be narrowband or wideband depending on the window length.
- The STFT can be used for various speech processing tasks, such as filtering, enhancement, modification, recognition, and synthesis. The STFT can also be inverted to reconstruct the speech signal from its time-frequency representation.
- A common algorithm for reconstructing the speech signal from the STFT is the overlap-add method, where the input signal is windowed in overlapping segments, such that when the overlapping parts are added together, we can perfectly reconstruct the original signal. It is like a cross-fade between subsequent windows.
- A mnemonic to remember the steps of the STFT is: **WATFIR**. This stands for **W**indowing, **A**nalysis, **T**ransform, **F**iltering, **I**nverse transform, **R**econstruction.
- A learning trick to understand the STFT is to use a graphical tool that allows you to interactively change the parameters of the STFT, such as the window length, the window type, the overlap, and the scale. You can observe how these parameters affect the time-frequency representation and the reconstructed signal. One such tool is available at https://academo.org/demos/spectrum-analyzer/.
