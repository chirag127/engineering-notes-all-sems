## Unit 2 - WORD LEVEL ANALYSIS

Word level analysis is the process of analyzing natural language at the level of individual words or tokens. It involves identifying the basic units of meaning and structure in a text, such as morphemes, words, parts of speech, and regular expressions. Word level analysis is an important step in natural language processing, as it helps to prepare the text for further processing, such as syntactic and semantic analysis.

Some of the topics covered in word level analysis are:

- **Morphological analysis**: This is the process of identifying the morphemes, the smallest units of meaning, in a word. For example, the word "unhappy" consists of two morphemes: the prefix "un-" and the root "happy". Morphological analysis can help to determine the meaning, spelling, and pronunciation of a word, as well as its grammatical category and inflectional forms.
- **Lexical analysis**: This is the process of identifying the words or tokens in a text, and assigning them to their corresponding lexical categories or parts of speech, such as nouns, verbs, adjectives, etc. For example, the sentence "She likes apples" consists of three words or tokens: "She", "likes", and "apples". Lexical analysis can help to determine the syntactic role and function of each word in a sentence, as well as its semantic properties and relations.
- **Regular expressions**: These are a language for specifying text search patterns, using a specialized syntax. Regular expressions can help to match or find other strings or sets of strings in a text, based on certain criteria, such as characters, positions, repetitions, etc. For example, the regular expression "[a-z]+" can match any string of lowercase letters, such as "apple", "banana", or "cat". Regular expressions are useful for text processing tasks, such as extracting, replacing, or validating information from a text.

Some of the applications of word level analysis are:

- **Information retrieval**: This is the process of finding relevant information from a large collection of documents, such as web pages, books, articles, etc. Word level analysis can help to index and query the documents based on the words or tokens they contain, as well as their lexical categories and morphological features. For example, a search engine can use word level analysis to find documents that contain the word "apple" as a noun, and not as a verb or an adjective.
- **Text summarization**: This is the process of creating a concise and informative summary of a longer text, such as a news article, a report, or a book. Word level analysis can help to identify the key words or tokens in a text, as well as their frequency, importance, and relevance. For example, a text summarizer can use word level analysis to extract the main topics and keywords from a text, and use them to generate a summary.
- **Text generation**: This is the process of creating natural language text from some input, such as data, keywords, images, etc. Word level analysis can help to select and generate the appropriate words or tokens for the output text, based on their lexical categories, morphological features, and semantic properties. For example, a text generator can use word level analysis to create a natural language description of an image, based on the objects, colors, and actions detected in the image.
