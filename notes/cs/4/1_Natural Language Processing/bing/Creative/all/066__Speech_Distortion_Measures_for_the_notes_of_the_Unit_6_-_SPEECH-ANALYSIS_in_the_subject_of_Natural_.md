### Speech Distortion Measures for the notes of the Unit 6 - SPEECH-ANALYSIS in the subject of Natural Language Processing

- Speech distortion measures are quantitative methods to evaluate the quality of speech signals after processing, such as compression, enhancement, synthesis, or recognition.
- Speech distortion measures can be classified into two categories: subjective and objective.
- Subjective measures are based on human perception and evaluation of speech quality, such as mean opinion score (MOS), which is the average rating given by a group of listeners on a scale from 1 (bad) to 5 (excellent).
- Objective measures are based on mathematical or statistical formulas that compare the original and processed speech signals, such as signal-to-noise ratio (SNR), which is the ratio of the power of the signal to the power of the noise.
- Objective measures can be further divided into two types: waveform-based and perceptual-based.
- Waveform-based measures compare the waveform or spectral features of the original and processed speech signals, such as root mean square error (RMSE), which is the square root of the average of the squared differences between the original and processed samples.
- Perceptual-based measures compare the perceptual or psychoacoustic features of the original and processed speech signals, such as perceptual evaluation of speech quality (PESQ), which is a standardized method that simulates the human auditory system and computes a score from -0.5 (poor) to 4.5 (excellent).
- Speech distortion measures have different advantages and disadvantages, depending on the application and the type of speech processing involved.
- Waveform-based measures are easy to compute and interpret, but they may not reflect the perceptual quality of speech, especially when the speech signals are nonlinearly distorted or masked by noise.
- Perceptual-based measures are more accurate and consistent with human perception, but they are more complex and computationally expensive, and they may not be applicable to all types of speech processing or distortion.
- Some examples of applications of speech distortion measures are:
  - Speech compression: to evaluate the trade-off between bit rate and speech quality.
  - Speech enhancement: to evaluate the effectiveness of noise reduction or speech intelligibility improvement.
  - Speech synthesis: to evaluate the naturalness and intelligibility of synthetic speech.
  - Speech recognition: to evaluate the impact of speech distortion on recognition accuracy or word error rate.
