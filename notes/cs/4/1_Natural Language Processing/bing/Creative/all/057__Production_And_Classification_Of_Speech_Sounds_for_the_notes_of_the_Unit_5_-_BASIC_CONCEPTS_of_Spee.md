### Production And Classification Of Speech Sounds for the notes of the Unit 5 - BASIC CONCEPTS of Speech Processing in the subject of Natural Language Processing

- Speech sounds are the acoustic signals that humans produce and perceive to communicate with each other.
- Speech sounds are produced by the coordinated action of various organs of speech, such as the lungs, the larynx, the velum, the tongue, the lips, etc.
- Speech sounds are classified into two main categories: vowels and consonants.
- Vowels are speech sounds that are produced with a relatively open vocal tract, allowing the air to flow freely without any significant obstruction or friction.
- Consonants are speech sounds that are produced with a relatively closed vocal tract, creating some degree of obstruction or friction that modifies the airflow.
- Vowels and consonants can be further classified according to various features, such as the following:
  - The state of the vocal cords: voiced or voiceless. Voiced sounds are produced with the vocal cords vibrating, creating a periodic sound wave. Voiceless sounds are produced with the vocal cords apart, creating a noise-like sound wave.
  - The place of articulation: the location of the main constriction or closure in the vocal tract. Some common places of articulation are bilabial (both lips), labiodental (lower lip and upper teeth), dental (tongue and teeth), alveolar (tongue and alveolar ridge), palatal (tongue and hard palate), velar (tongue and soft palate), and glottal (vocal cords).
  - The manner of articulation: the type and degree of constriction or closure in the vocal tract. Some common manners of articulation are plosive (complete closure and release), fricative (narrow constriction and turbulent airflow), affricate (plosive followed by fricative), nasal (closure in the oral cavity and open velum), lateral (closure in the center and open sides of the tongue), approximant (slight constriction and smooth airflow), and trill (rapid vibration of an articulator).
- Speech sounds can be represented by symbols that indicate their phonetic characteristics. One widely used system of symbols is the International Phonetic Alphabet (IPA), which assigns a unique symbol to each speech sound.
- Speech sounds can also be analyzed in terms of their acoustic properties, such as frequency, amplitude, and spectrum. Frequency is the number of cycles per second of a sound wave, measured in Hertz (Hz). Amplitude is the magnitude or intensity of a sound wave, measured in decibels (dB). Spectrum is the distribution of energy across different frequency bands of a sound wave, which can be visualized by a spectrogram.
- Speech sounds can be grouped into larger units, such as syllables, words, and sentences, based on their phonological patterns and rules. Phonology is the study of the sound system of a language, which determines how speech sounds can be combined and contrasted to convey meaning.
