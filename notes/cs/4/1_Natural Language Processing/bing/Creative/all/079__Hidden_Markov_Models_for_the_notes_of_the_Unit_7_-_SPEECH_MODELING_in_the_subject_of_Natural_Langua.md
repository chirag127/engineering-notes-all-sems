### Hidden Markov Models for the notes of the Unit 7 - SPEECH MODELING in the subject of Natural Language Processing

- Hidden Markov Models (HMMs) are a statistical framework for modeling sequential data, such as speech signals.
- HMMs assume that the observed data are generated by a stochastic process that has some hidden states.
- The hidden states are not directly observable, but they have some probabilistic relationship with the observable data.
- HMMs can capture the temporal dependencies and variations in the data, and allow for efficient inference and learning algorithms .
- HMMs are widely used for speech recognition and modeling, as they can represent the acoustic and linguistic features of speech signals  .
- HMMs can also be used for other natural language processing tasks, such as part-of-speech tagging, named entity recognition, and text generation.

#### Basic components of HMMs

- An HMM consists of the following components :
  - A set of hidden states, denoted by Q = {q1, q2, ..., qN}, where N is the number of states.
  - A set of observable symbols, denoted by V = {v1, v2, ..., vM}, where M is the size of the vocabulary.
  - A transition matrix, denoted by A = [aij], where aij is the probability of transitioning from state qi to state qj, i.e., aij = P(qt+1 = qj | qt = qi).
  - An emission matrix, denoted by B = [bj(k)], where bj(k) is the probability of emitting symbol vk from state qj, i.e., bj(k) = P(vt = vk | qt = qj).
  - An initial state distribution, denoted by π = [πi], where πi is the probability of starting from state qi, i.e., πi = P(q1 = qi).

- An HMM can be represented by a graph, where the nodes are the hidden states and the edges are the transitions, labeled by the transition probabilities . The emission probabilities are usually shown in a table or a matrix .

- An example of an HMM with three states and four symbols is shown below:

```
  q1 ----> q2 ----> q3
  |  ^     |  ^     |  ^
  |  |     |  |     |  |
  v  |     v  |     v  |
  0.2      0.4      0.1
  |        |        |
  v        v        v
  q1       q2       q3

  Emission matrix B:

       v1   v2   v3   v4
  q1  0.2  0.4  0.1  0.3
  q2  0.3  0.1  0.3  0.3
  q3  0.4  0.2  0.1  0.3

  Initial state distribution π:

  q1  q2  q3
  0.5 0.2 0.3
```

#### Basic problems of HMMs

- There are three basic problems that HMMs can solve :
  - Evaluation: Given an HMM λ = (A, B, π) and an observation sequence O = (o1, o2, ..., oT), what is the probability of O given λ, i.e., P(O | λ)?
