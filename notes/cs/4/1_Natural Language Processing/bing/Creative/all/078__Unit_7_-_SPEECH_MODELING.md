## Unit 7 - SPEECH MODELING

- Speech modeling is the process of representing speech signals in a mathematical or statistical way, such as using vectors, matrices, probability distributions, etc.
- Speech modeling is useful for various speech processing tasks, such as speech recognition, speech synthesis, speech enhancement, speech coding, etc.
- Speech modeling can be divided into two main categories: parametric and non-parametric models.
- Parametric models assume that speech signals follow a certain structure or pattern, and use a finite number of parameters to describe them. Examples of parametric models are linear prediction, hidden Markov models, Gaussian mixture models, etc.
- Non-parametric models do not assume any specific structure or pattern for speech signals, and use data-driven methods to learn the features or characteristics of speech signals. Examples of non-parametric models are neural networks, deep learning, etc.
- Parametric models have the advantages of being simple, compact, and interpretable, but they may not capture the complexity and variability of speech signals well.
- Non-parametric models have the advantages of being flexible, powerful, and adaptable, but they may require a lot of data, computation, and tuning, and they may not be easily explainable or generalizable.
- A common approach to speech modeling is to use a combination of parametric and non-parametric models, such as using linear prediction coefficients as input features for neural networks, or using hidden Markov models as acoustic models and deep neural networks as language models for speech recognition.
