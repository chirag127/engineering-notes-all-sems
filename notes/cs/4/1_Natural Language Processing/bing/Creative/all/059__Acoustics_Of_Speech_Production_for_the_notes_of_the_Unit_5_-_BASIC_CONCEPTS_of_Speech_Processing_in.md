### Acoustics Of Speech Production for the notes of the Unit 5 - BASIC CONCEPTS of Speech Processing in the subject of Natural Language Processing

- Acoustics of speech production is the study of the physical properties of speech sounds, such as frequency, intensity, and duration, and how they are generated by the human vocal tract.
- The human vocal tract consists of a source of sound energy (usually the larynx) and a filter (the supralaryngeal vocal tract) that shapes the sound spectrum by creating resonances and anti-resonances.
- The source-filter model of speech production is a common theoretical framework that assumes that the source and the filter are independent of each other and can be analyzed separately.
- The source of sound energy can be either periodic (as in voiced sounds) or aperiodic (as in voiceless sounds). The periodic source produces a complex wave with a fundamental frequency (F0) and a series of harmonics (integer multiples of F0). The aperiodic source produces a noise-like wave with a flat spectrum.
- The filter function of the vocal tract depends on the shape and size of the cavities (oral, nasal, pharyngeal) and the articulators (tongue, lips, jaw, velum, etc.) that modify the airflow. The filter function can be represented by an area function, which specifies the cross-sectional area of the vocal tract at different points along its length.
- The filter function can also be modeled as a multi-tube system, where each tube corresponds to a segment of the vocal tract with a uniform cross-sectional area. The acoustic properties of each tube depend on its length, area, and the boundary conditions at its ends (open, closed, or connected to another tube).
- The filter function determines the resonant frequencies of the vocal tract, which are called formants. Formants are regions of high energy in the sound spectrum, where the sound wave is amplified by constructive interference. The formant frequencies depend on the length and shape of the vocal tract, and they vary with different speech sounds.
- The filter function also determines the anti-resonant frequencies of the vocal tract, which are called zeros. Zeros are regions of low energy in the sound spectrum, where the sound wave is attenuated by destructive interference. The zero frequencies depend on the presence and location of side branches in the vocal tract, such as the nasal cavity or the piriform sinus. Zeros are important for the production of nasal and lateral sounds.
- The acoustic theory of speech production can be used to analyze and synthesize speech sounds by measuring or estimating the source and filter parameters and applying the appropriate mathematical models and transformations. The acoustic theory can also be used to explain the perceptual features and categories of speech sounds, such as vowel quality, consonant manner and place, and suprasegmental aspects such as stress, tone, and intonation.
