### Cepstral Distances for the notes of the Unit 6 - SPEECH-ANALYSIS in the subject of Natural Language Processing

- Cepstral distance is a measure of similarity between two speech signals based on their cepstrum, which is the inverse Fourier transform of the log magnitude of the Fourier transform of the signal.
- Cepstral distance can be used for various applications in speech analysis, such as endpoint detection, source-filter separation, emotion recognition, and speaker recognition.
- Cepstral distance can be computed using different methods, such as Euclidean distance, cosine distance, or Mahalanobis distance, between the cepstral coefficients of two signals.
- Cepstral coefficients are often derived from the mel-frequency cepstral coefficients (MFCC), which are based on a filter bank algorithm that mimics the human auditory system.
- Cepstral distance can be combined with other features, such as speech energy, to improve the performance of speech analysis tasks.
- Cepstral distance can be influenced by various factors, such as noise, pitch, and vocal tract length, and may require normalization or compensation techniques to reduce the variability.

Some possible mnemonics and learning tricks for cepstral distance are:

- Cepstrum is spectrum with the letters swapped, and quefrency is frequency with the letters swapped.
- Cepstrum is the spectrum of the spectrum, and cepstral distance is the distance between the spectra of the spectra.
- Cepstral distance can be used to detect the end of speech, because the cepstrum of silence is different from the cepstrum of speech.
- Cepstral distance can be used to separate the source and the filter of speech, because the log spectrum of speech is the sum of the log spectra of the source and the filter.
- Cepstral distance can be used to recognize emotions and speakers, because the cepstrum of speech reflects the characteristics of the vocal tract and the vocal cords.
