### Word Sense Disambiguation for the notes of the Unit 4 - SEMANTICS AND PRAGMATICS in the subject of Natural Language Processing

- Word Sense Disambiguation (WSD) is the task of identifying the correct meaning or sense of a word in a given context, when the word has multiple possible meanings or senses.
- WSD is important for natural language processing applications such as machine translation, information retrieval, text summarization, question answering, etc. that require semantic understanding of the input text.
- WSD can be classified into two types: lexical and structural.
  - Lexical WSD is based on the individual words and their meanings, without considering the syntactic structure or the discourse context of the sentence. For example, the word "bank" can have different meanings depending on the domain or the topic of the text, such as financial, geographical, or biological.
  - Structural WSD is based on the syntactic structure and the discourse context of the sentence, as well as the individual words and their meanings. For example, the word "bank" can have different meanings depending on the part of speech, the grammatical role, the semantic role, or the co-references in the sentence, such as noun, verb, subject, object, agent, patient, or anaphora.
- WSD can be performed using different methods, such as knowledge-based, supervised, semi-supervised, unsupervised, or hybrid.
  - Knowledge-based methods use external sources of linguistic or domain knowledge, such as dictionaries, thesauri, ontologies, or corpora, to disambiguate the word senses. For example, Lesk's algorithm uses the definitions of the word senses from a dictionary and compares them with the words in the context to find the most overlapping sense.
  - Supervised methods use annotated data, such as sense-tagged corpora, to train machine learning models, such as decision trees, naive Bayes, support vector machines, or neural networks, to disambiguate the word senses. For example, WordNet is a large lexical database that provides sense tags for words and their synonyms, hypernyms, hyponyms, etc.
  - Semi-supervised methods use a combination of annotated and unannotated data, such as sense-tagged and raw corpora, to train machine learning models, such as bootstrapping, co-training, or self-training, to disambiguate the word senses. For example, Yarowsky's algorithm uses a small set of seed words with known senses and a large set of unlabeled words to iteratively learn the sense distribution and the contextual features of the words.
  - Unsupervised methods use unannotated data, such as raw corpora, to cluster the word senses based on their distributional or contextual similarity, without using any external knowledge or sense inventory. For example, Brown clustering is a hierarchical clustering algorithm that groups words based on their co-occurrence patterns in a large corpus.
  - Hybrid methods use a combination of different methods, such as knowledge-based and supervised, or supervised and unsupervised, to disambiguate the word senses. For example, Navigli and Velardi's algorithm uses WordNet and a sense-tagged corpus to train a supervised classifier and an unsupervised clustering algorithm to disambiguate the word senses.
- WSD can be evaluated using different metrics, such as accuracy, precision, recall, F1-score, or inter-annotator agreement.
  - Accuracy is the ratio of the number of correctly disambiguated word senses to the total number of word senses in the test set.
  - Precision is the ratio of the number of correctly disambiguated word senses to the total number of word senses predicted by the system.
  - Recall is the ratio of the number of correctly disambiguated word senses to the total number of word senses in the gold standard.
  - F1-score is the harmonic mean of precision and recall, which balances the trade-off between them.
  - Inter-annotator agreement is the measure of the consistency or reliability of the human annotators who provide the sense tags for the words in the corpus. It can be calculated using different methods, such as percentage agreement, Cohen's kappa, or Krippendorff's alpha.
- WSD can be improved by using different techniques, such as feature engineering, feature selection, ensemble learning, or domain adaptation.
  - Feature engineering is the process of creating or extracting relevant and informative features from the input data, such as lexical, syntactic, semantic, or pragmatic features, to represent the word senses and the context.
