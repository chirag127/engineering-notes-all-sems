## Unit 4 - SEMANTICS AND PRAGMATICS

Semantics and pragmatics are two branches of linguistics that deal with the meaning and use of language. Semantics studies the literal meaning of words, phrases, sentences, and texts, while pragmatics studies the meaning in context, taking into account the speaker's intention, the listener's inference, the common knowledge, and the situational factors.

Some of the main topics covered in this unit are:

- **Meaning and reference**: How words and expressions refer to entities in the world, and how they can be ambiguous, vague, or specific.
- **Sense and denotation**: How words and expressions have both a sense (the way they present their referents) and a denotation (the set of referents they pick out).
- **Truth conditions and entailment**: How sentences can be evaluated as true or false based on the facts of the world, and how some sentences logically follow from others.
- **Semantic relations**: How words and expressions are related to each other in terms of synonymy, antonymy, hyponymy, meronymy, etc.
- **Speech acts and illocutionary force**: How utterances can perform different kinds of actions, such as asserting, questioning, requesting, promising, etc., and how they can have different effects on the listener, such as informing, persuading, obliging, etc.
- **Implicature and presupposition**: How speakers can imply or presuppose more than what they literally say, and how listeners can infer or accommodate these additional meanings.
- **Politeness and relevance**: How speakers can follow or flout certain principles or maxims of communication, such as being cooperative, informative, truthful, relevant, clear, polite, etc., and how these affect the interpretation and evaluation of utterances.

Some of the mnemonics and learning tricks for this unit are:

- To remember the difference between semantics and pragmatics, think of the acronym SAP: Semantics is About meaning, Pragmatics is about Purpose.
- To remember the difference between sense and denotation, think of the acronym SAD: Sense is how we Analyze the meaning, Denotation is what we Determine from the meaning.
- To remember the difference between speech acts and illocutionary force, think of the acronym SIF: Speech acts are what we Say, Illocutionary force is what we want to achieve.
- To remember the difference between implicature and presupposition, think of the acronym IP: Implicature is what we Infer from the utterance, Presupposition is what we assume Prior to the utterance.
- To remember the four maxims of the cooperative principle, think of the acronym QRIC: Quantity (be as informative as required), Quality (be truthful), Relation (be relevant), Manner (be clear).
