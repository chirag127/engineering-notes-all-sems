### Feature Extraction and Pattern Comparison Techniques for the notes of the Unit 6 - SPEECH-ANALYSIS in the subject of Natural Language Processing

- Feature extraction is the process of transforming raw speech signals into a set of features that can be used for further analysis or modeling  .
- Feature extraction aims to reduce the dimensionality of the speech data, capture the relevant information, and remove the noise and variability  .
- Some common feature extraction techniques for speech analysis are:
  - Mel-frequency cepstral coefficients (MFCCs): These are the most widely used features for speech recognition and speaker identification  . MFCCs are based on the human perception of sound frequency and represent the spectral envelope of the speech signal  . MFCCs are computed by applying a series of steps, such as pre-emphasis, framing, windowing, discrete Fourier transform, mel-scale filter bank, logarithm, and discrete cosine transform  .
  - Linear predictive coding (LPC): This is another popular feature extraction technique for speech analysis, especially for speech synthesis and coding  . LPC models the speech signal as a linear combination of past samples and a prediction error  . LPC coefficients are obtained by minimizing the mean squared error between the actual and predicted samples  .
  - Perceptual linear prediction (PLP): This is a variant of LPC that incorporates the human auditory system characteristics, such as equal-loudness curves and critical-band resolution  . PLP coefficients are computed by applying a series of steps, such as pre-emphasis, framing, windowing, discrete Fourier transform, bark-scale filter bank, equal-loudness weighting, inverse Fourier transform, linear prediction, and cepstral transformation  .
  - Zero-crossing rate (ZCR): This is a simple feature extraction technique that measures the number of times the speech signal crosses the zero amplitude level in a given frame  . ZCR is useful for detecting the presence of speech, as well as distinguishing voiced and unvoiced segments  .
  - Energy: This is another simple feature extraction technique that measures the sum of squared amplitudes of the speech signal in a given frame  . Energy is useful for detecting the presence of speech, as well as distinguishing silence and non-silence segments  .
  - Pitch: This is a feature extraction technique that measures the fundamental frequency of the speech signal in a given frame  . Pitch is useful for identifying the speaker's gender, emotion, and intonation  . Pitch can be estimated by various methods, such as autocorrelation, cepstrum, harmonic product spectrum, and subharmonic summation  .
  - Formants: These are feature extraction techniques that measure the resonant frequencies of the vocal tract in a given frame  . Formants are useful for characterizing the vowel sounds and the speaker's identity  . Formants can be estimated by various methods, such as LPC, cepstrum, and linear prediction residual  .

- Pattern comparison aims to find the best match or similarity between the input features and the reference patterns or models, and to assign a label or score to the input speech signal  .
