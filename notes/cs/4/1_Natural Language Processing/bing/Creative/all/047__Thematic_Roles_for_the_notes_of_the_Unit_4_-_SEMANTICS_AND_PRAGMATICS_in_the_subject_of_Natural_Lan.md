### Thematic Roles for the notes of the Unit 4 - SEMANTICS AND PRAGMATICS in the subject of Natural Language Processing

- Thematic roles are the semantic roles that the arguments of a predicate (such as a verb, noun, or adjective) play in a sentence.
- Thematic roles capture the relationship between the predicate and its arguments, such as who did what to whom, when, where, why, and how.
- Thematic roles are also called theta roles, case roles, or semantic roles.
- Some common thematic roles are:

  - **Agent**: the initiator or doer of an action, usually animate and volitional.
    - Example: *John* broke the window. (*John* is the agent of the verb broke)
  - **Patient**: the entity that is affected by an action, usually inanimate and non-volitional.
    - Example: John broke *the window*. (*the window* is the patient of the verb broke)
  - **Experiencer**: the entity that perceives or feels a state or an event, usually animate and non-volitional.
    - Example: *Mary* likes chocolate. (*Mary* is the experiencer of the verb likes)
  - **Theme**: the entity that is moved or undergoes a change of state or location, usually inanimate and non-volitional.
    - Example: John gave *the book* to Mary. (*the book* is the theme of the verb gave)
  - **Instrument**: the entity that is used to perform an action, usually inanimate and non-volitional.
    - Example: John broke the window *with a hammer*. (*a hammer* is the instrument of the verb broke)
  - **Beneficiary**: the entity that benefits from or is adversely affected by an action, usually animate and non-volitional.
    - Example: John baked a cake *for Mary*. (*Mary* is the beneficiary of the verb baked)
  - **Source**: the entity from which a movement or a transfer originates, usually inanimate and non-volitional.
    - Example: John took the book *from the shelf*. (*the shelf* is the source of the verb took)
  - **Goal**: the entity to which a movement or a transfer is directed, usually inanimate and non-volitional.
    - Example: John put the book *on the table*. (*the table* is the goal of the verb put)
  - **Location**: the entity where an action, state, or event takes place, usually inanimate and non-volitional.
    - Example: John lives *in London*. (*London* is the location of the verb lives)
  - **Manner**: the entity that specifies how an action, state, or event is performed, usually inanimate and non-volitional.
    - Example: John ran *quickly*. (*quickly* is the manner of the verb ran)
  - **Cause**: the entity that causes or triggers an action, state, or event, usually inanimate and non-volitional.
    - Example: *The storm* caused the flood. (*The storm* is the cause of the verb caused)
  - **Purpose**: the entity that expresses the intention or reason for an action, state, or event, usually inanimate and non-volitional.
    - Example: John went to the store *to buy milk*. (*to buy milk* is the purpose of the verb went)

- Thematic roles are assigned by the predicate to its arguments based on their syntactic position and semantic features.
- Thematic roles help to determine the meaning and the grammaticality of a sentence.
- Thematic roles can vary across languages, depending on the syntactic and semantic properties of the predicates and the arguments.
- Thematic roles can be represented by using a notation called thematic grids, which show the predicate and its arguments along with their thematic roles.
- Example: The thematic grid for the verb give is:

  | Predicate | Argument 1 | Argument 2 | Argument 3 |
  | --------- | ---------- | ---------- | ---------- |
  | give      | Agent      | Theme      | Goal       |

- A mnemonic to remember some of the common thematic roles is:

  - **A PET LIGS**: Agent, Patient, Experiencer, Theme, Location, Instrument, Goal, Source
  - **A PET BIG CLAMS**: Agent, Patient, Experiencer, Theme, Beneficiary, Instrument, Goal, Cause, Location, Manner, Source
