### Evaluation for the notes of the Unit 7 - SPEECH MODELING in the subject of Natural Language Processing

- Speech modeling is the task of building computational models that can generate or recognize natural speech signals.
- Speech modeling can be divided into two subtasks: speech synthesis and speech recognition.
- Speech synthesis is the task of generating speech signals from text or other symbolic representations of speech content.
- Speech recognition is the task of converting speech signals into text or other symbolic representations of speech content.
- Speech modeling can be performed at different levels of abstraction, such as acoustic, phonetic, lexical, syntactic, semantic, and pragmatic.
- Speech modeling can also be performed using different approaches, such as statistical, rule-based, neural, or hybrid.
- Speech modeling can be evaluated using different metrics, depending on the subtask, the level of abstraction, and the approach.
- Some common metrics for speech modeling are:

  - Perplexity: a measure of how well a model predicts the next word or symbol in a sequence of speech or text. It is defined as the inverse of the average probability assigned by the model to each word or symbol in a test set. Lower perplexity means better prediction and lower uncertainty. Perplexity is commonly used to evaluate language models, which are models that assign probabilities to sequences of words or symbols. Perplexity can be computed using equation (1) :

    ```
    PP = 1 / (product of P(w_i | w_1, ..., w_i-1) ^ (1 / N))
    ```
    where PP is perplexity, P(w_i | w_1, ..., w_i-1) is the probability of word or symbol w_i given the previous words or symbols w_1, ..., w_i-1, and N is the number of words or symbols in the test set.

  - Word error rate (WER): a measure of how many errors a model makes when transcribing speech signals into text. It is defined as the ratio of the number of substitutions, deletions, and insertions of words or symbols to the number of words or symbols in the reference transcription. Lower WER means better transcription accuracy. WER is commonly used to evaluate speech recognition models, which are models that convert speech signals into text or other symbolic representations. WER can be computed using equation (2) :

    ```
    WER = (S + D + I) / N
    ```
    where WER is word error rate, S is the number of substitutions, D is the number of deletions, I is the number of insertions, and N is the number of words or symbols in the reference transcription.

  - SpeechLMScore: a measure of how well a model generates speech signals from text or other symbolic representations of speech content. It is defined as the average log-probability assigned by a speech language model to the speech signals generated by the model. Higher SpeechLMScore means better speech generation quality. SpeechLMScore is a novel metric proposed by  and  to evaluate speech synthesis models, which are models that generate speech signals from text or other symbolic representations. SpeechLMScore can be computed using equation (3) :

    ```
    SpeechLMScore = 1 / M * sum of log P(x_i | x_1, ..., x_i-1)
    ```
    where SpeechLMScore is the average log-probability, P(x_i | x_1, ..., x_i-1) is the probability of speech signal x_i given the previous speech signals x_1, ..., x_i-1, and M is the number of speech signals in the test set.

- Some mnemonics and learning tricks for speech modeling evaluation are:

  - Perplexity is inversely proportional to probability: the more probable a word or symbol is, the less perplexed the model is.
  - Word error rate is proportional to the number of errors: the more errors a model makes, the higher the word error rate is.
  - SpeechLMScore is proportional to log-probability: the more probable a speech signal is, the higher the SpeechLMScore is.
