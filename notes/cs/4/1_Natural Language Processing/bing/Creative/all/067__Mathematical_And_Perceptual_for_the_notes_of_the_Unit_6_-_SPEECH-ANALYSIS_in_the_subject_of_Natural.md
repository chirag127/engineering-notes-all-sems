### Mathematical And Perceptual Aspects of Speech Analysis

Speech analysis is the process of transforming a speech signal into a representation that can be used for further processing, such as synthesis, recognition, or compression. Speech analysis involves both mathematical and perceptual aspects, which are interrelated and complementary.

Mathematical aspects of speech analysis include:

- The use of mathematical models and methods to describe and manipulate the speech signal, such as Fourier analysis, linear prediction, wavelets, filter banks, etc.
- The use of mathematical concepts and tools to measure and quantify the properties and features of the speech signal, such as frequency, amplitude, phase, spectrum, pitch, formants, energy, entropy, etc.
- The use of mathematical techniques and algorithms to extract and classify the information and structure of the speech signal, such as segmentation, clustering, feature extraction, pattern recognition, etc.

Perceptual aspects of speech analysis include:

- The use of perceptual models and principles to account for and predict how humans perceive and process speech sounds, such as auditory scene analysis, phonetic features, categorical perception, etc.
- The use of perceptual criteria and metrics to evaluate and compare the quality and intelligibility of speech signals, such as mean opinion score, signal-to-noise ratio, speech transmission index, etc.
- The use of perceptual experiments and data to validate and refine the mathematical models and methods of speech analysis, such as psychoacoustics, speech perception, speech production, etc.

Some examples of the interplay between mathematical and perceptual aspects of speech analysis are:

- The use of linear prediction to model the vocal tract as a source-filter system, which is based on the perceptual observation that speech sounds can be decomposed into a glottal source and a vocal tract filter.
- The use of Fourier analysis to compute the spectrum of the speech signal, which is related to the perceptual notion of frequency and pitch.
- The use of wavelets to analyze the speech signal at different scales and resolutions, which is inspired by the perceptual organization of sounds into auditory objects and events.
- The use of feature extraction to represent the speech signal by a set of parameters that capture its salient characteristics, such as mel-frequency cepstral coefficients, which are based on the perceptual similarity of sounds with different spectral shapes but similar pitch.

Some mnemonics and learning tricks for speech analysis are:

- To remember the difference between Fourier analysis and linear prediction, think of the acronym FALP: Fourier Analysis is based on the frequency domain, Linear Prediction is based on the time domain.
- To remember the difference between pitch and formants, think of the analogy of a musical instrument: pitch is the fundamental frequency of the sound, formants are the resonant frequencies of the instrument.
- To remember the difference between segmentation and clustering, think of the analogy of a puzzle: segmentation is dividing the speech signal into pieces, clustering is grouping the pieces into categories.
