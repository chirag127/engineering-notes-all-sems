### Word Similarity using Thesaurus and Distributional methods

Word similarity is a measure of the degree to which two words are related in meaning. There are two main approaches to measuring word similarity: thesaurus-based methods and distributional methods.

#### Thesaurus-based methods

Thesaurus-based methods use a thesaurus, which is a dictionary of synonyms and antonyms, to determine the similarity between two words. The basic idea is that if two words are listed as synonyms in a thesaurus, they are considered to be similar in meaning. The degree of similarity can be determined by the distance between the two words in the thesaurus hierarchy.

#### Distributional methods

Distributional methods, on the other hand, use the distribution of words in large corpora of text to determine their similarity. The basic idea is that words that occur in similar contexts are likely to have similar meanings. The degree of similarity can be determined by comparing the distributional profiles of the two words, which are vectors representing the co-occurrence of the words with other words in the corpus.

Both thesaurus-based and distributional methods have their strengths and weaknesses. Thesaurus-based methods are limited by the coverage and accuracy of the thesaurus, while distributional methods require large amounts of text data and can be computationally expensive. A combination of the two approaches can often yield the best results.
