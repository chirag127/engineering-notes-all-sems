### Statistical LM for the notes of the Unit 1 - INTRODUCTION in the subject of Natural Language Processing

- Statistical Language Model (LM) is a probability distribution over sequences of words.
- It assigns a probability to a sequence of words, which can be used to predict the likelihood of a given sentence or document.
- Statistical LMs are widely used in natural language processing tasks such as speech recognition, machine translation, and text generation.
- There are several types of statistical LMs, including n-gram models, neural network-based models, and latent variable models.
- N-gram models are the simplest type of statistical LM, where the probability of a word is estimated based on the previous n-1 words.
- Neural network-based models, such as recurrent neural networks (RNNs) and transformers, can capture long-range dependencies and are widely used in state-of-the-art natural language processing systems.
- Latent variable models, such as topic models and hidden Markov models, introduce latent variables to capture the underlying structure of the data.
- Statistical LMs can be trained on large amounts of text data to learn the patterns and regularities of natural language.
- They can be used to generate coherent and fluent text, as well as to improve the performance of other natural language processing tasks.
