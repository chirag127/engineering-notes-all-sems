### Articulatory Phonetics for the notes of the Unit 5 - BASIC CONCEPTS of Speech Processing in the subject of Natural Language Processing

- Articulatory phonetics is the study of how speech sounds are produced by the movement of the articulators, which include the lips, tongue, and vocal cords.
- The production of speech sounds involves the movement of air from the lungs, through the vocal cords, and out of the mouth or nose.
- The vocal cords can be adjusted to produce different sounds by changing their tension and position.
- The position of the tongue and lips can also be adjusted to produce different sounds.
- Consonants are produced by constricting the airflow in some way, while vowels are produced by shaping the vocal tract to create a particular resonance.
- There are several different ways to classify speech sounds, including place of articulation, manner of articulation, and voicing.
- Place of articulation refers to where in the mouth or throat the sound is produced, while manner of articulation refers to how the airflow is constricted.
- Voicing refers to whether the vocal cords are vibrating during the production of the sound.
- Articulatory phonetics is an important part of the study of speech processing and natural language processing, as it provides a way to understand and analyze the physical production of speech sounds.
