### Probabilistic CFG

Probabilistic Context-Free Grammar (PCFG) is a type of Context-Free Grammar (CFG) that associates a probability with each production rule. The probability of a rule is the likelihood of its usage in generating a sentence in the language.

- A PCFG is defined as a tuple (N, Σ, R, S, P), where:
  - N is a finite set of non-terminal symbols.
  - Σ is a finite set of terminal symbols.
  - R is a finite set of production rules.
  - S is the start symbol.
  - P is the probability function that assigns a probability to each production rule.

- The probabilities of all production rules with the same left-hand side must sum to 1.

- The probability of a parse tree generated by a PCFG is the product of the probabilities of the production rules used to generate it.

- PCFGs can be used for syntactic analysis by finding the most probable parse tree for a given sentence.

- PCFGs can be learned from a treebank, which is a corpus of sentences annotated with their parse trees.

- The learning process involves estimating the probabilities of the production rules based on their frequency of occurrence in the treebank.

- PCFGs can be used for language generation by sampling production rules according to their probabilities.

- PCFGs can also be used for language modeling by computing the probability of a sentence as the sum of the probabilities of all its possible parse trees.

- PCFGs have been widely used in natural language processing tasks such as parsing, language generation, and language modeling. However, they have limitations in capturing long-distance dependencies and other complex syntactic phenomena. To address these limitations, more advanced grammar formalisms such as dependency grammar and combinatory categorial grammar have been proposed.
