### Word Similarity using Thesaurus and Distributional methods

Word similarity is a measure of the degree to which two words are related in meaning. There are two main approaches to measuring word similarity: thesaurus-based methods and distributional methods.

1. **Thesaurus-based methods** use a thesaurus, which is a dictionary of synonyms and antonyms, to determine the similarity between two words. If two words are synonyms, they are considered to be highly similar. If they are antonyms, they are considered to be dissimilar. Thesaurus-based methods can also use other types of relationships between words, such as hypernymy and hyponymy, to determine similarity.

2. **Distributional methods** are based on the idea that words that occur in similar contexts tend to have similar meanings. These methods use large corpora of text to determine the contexts in which words occur, and then use statistical techniques to measure the similarity between the contexts of different words. Distributional methods can be further divided into count-based methods, which count the co-occurrences of words, and prediction-based methods, which use machine learning techniques to predict the contexts of words.

Both thesaurus-based and distributional methods have their strengths and weaknesses. Thesaurus-based methods can provide precise and accurate measures of similarity for words that are included in the thesaurus, but they may not be able to handle words that are not included in the thesaurus or words that have multiple meanings. Distributional methods can handle a wider range of words and can capture more subtle relationships between words, but they may not be as precise as thesaurus-based methods for some types of relationships.

In practice, a combination of thesaurus-based and distributional methods is often used to measure word similarity. This allows the strengths of both methods to be leveraged, resulting in more accurate and comprehensive measures of similarity.
