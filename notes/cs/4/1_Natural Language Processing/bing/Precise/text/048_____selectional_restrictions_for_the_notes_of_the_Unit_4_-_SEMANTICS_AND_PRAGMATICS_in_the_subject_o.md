### Selectional Restrictions

Selectional restrictions are constraints on the arguments that a verb, noun, or adjective can take. These constraints are based on the semantic properties of the arguments and the meaning of the word itself. In the field of natural language processing, selectional restrictions are used to help disambiguate the meaning of sentences and improve the accuracy of language understanding systems.

Some examples of selectional restrictions include:
- Verbs of consumption, such as "eat" or "drink", typically require an argument that is edible or potable.
- Verbs of perception, such as "see" or "hear", typically require an argument that is visible or audible.
- Adjectives that describe physical properties, such as "tall" or "heavy", typically require an argument that has a physical form and can be measured in terms of height or weight.

Selectional restrictions can be violated for rhetorical or poetic effect, but in general, they help to ensure that sentences are semantically well-formed and can be understood by both humans and machines. In natural language processing, selectional restrictions can be used to improve the accuracy of tasks such as parsing, semantic role labeling, and machine translation.
