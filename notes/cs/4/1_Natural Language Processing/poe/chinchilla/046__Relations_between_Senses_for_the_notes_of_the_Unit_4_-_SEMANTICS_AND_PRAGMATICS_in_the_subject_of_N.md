### Relations between Senses

In natural language processing, understanding the relationships between different senses of words is crucial for tasks such as word sense disambiguation and semantic role labeling. Here are some relations between senses that are commonly studied:

1. Synonymy: This relation exists between senses that have the same or similar meanings. For example, "big" and "large" are synonyms.

2. Antonymy: This relation exists between senses that have opposite meanings. For example, "hot" and "cold" are antonyms.

3. Hyponymy: This relation exists between a more general sense (hypernym) and a more specific sense (hyponym). For example, "apple" is a hyponym of "fruit," which is its hypernym.

4. Meronymy: This relation exists between a whole and its parts. For example, "wheel" is a meronym of "car," which is its whole.

5. Homonymy: This relation exists between senses that have the same spelling or pronunciation but different meanings. For example, "bank" can refer to a financial institution or the side of a river.

6. Polysemy: This relation exists between senses that have multiple meanings, but those meanings are related in some way. For example, "bat" can refer to a flying mammal or a piece of sports equipment used for hitting a ball.

7. Metonymy: This relation exists between a word and something closely associated with it. For example, "the White House" can refer to the U.S. government or the president.

Understanding these relations between senses is essential for natural language processing tasks that involve semantic analysis. By identifying these relationships, we can disambiguate word senses more accurately and build better semantic representations of text.
