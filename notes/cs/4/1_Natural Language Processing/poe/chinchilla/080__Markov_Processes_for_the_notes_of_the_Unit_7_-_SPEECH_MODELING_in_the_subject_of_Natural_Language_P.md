### Markov Processes for the notes of the Unit 7 - SPEECH MODELING in the subject of Natural Language Processing

In the study of speech modeling, Markov processes are a crucial concept. Markov processes are models that assume that the future state of a system is dependent only on its current state.

Here are some key points to keep in mind when studying Markov processes for speech modeling:

- Markov processes are used to model a wide range of systems, including speech signals.
- Markov processes are based on the Markov property, which states that the probability of transitioning from one state to another depends only on the current state and not on any previous states.
- In speech modeling, Markov processes are used to model the probability distribution of speech signals over time.
- The simplest type of Markov process is the first-order Markov process, also known as a Markov chain. In a Markov chain, the probability of transitioning from one state to another is represented by a transition matrix.
- Hidden Markov models (HMMs) are a type of Markov process that are commonly used in speech modeling. HMMs are used to model the probability distribution of speech signals, as well as the probability of transitioning from one state to another.
- In an HMM, the states represent different phonemes or speech sounds, and the observation probabilities represent the probability of observing a particular speech signal given the current state.
- HMMs are used in a variety of speech processing applications, including speech recognition, speaker identification, and speech synthesis.

In summary, Markov processes are a fundamental concept in speech modeling, and understanding them is essential for anyone studying natural language processing. By using Markov processes, we can model the probability distribution of speech signals and make accurate predictions about future states of the system.
