### Speech Fundamentals

In this section, we will cover the fundamental concepts of speech processing. These concepts are essential for understanding the basics of speech processing in natural language processing.

1. Speech Signal
   - A speech signal is a time-varying waveform that represents the speech signal generated by the human vocal tract.
   - The speech signal consists of different frequency components that are the result of the vocal cord movements.
   - The speech signal is recorded using a microphone and is processed by a speech processing system.

2. Phonemes
   - Phonemes are the smallest units of sound in a language.
   - The human vocal tract can produce over 100 different phonemes, depending on the language.
   - The phonemes are represented by different symbols in the International Phonetic Alphabet (IPA).

3. Spectrogram
   - A spectrogram is a visual representation of the speech signal that shows the frequency content over time.
   - The spectrogram is obtained by applying a Fourier transform to the speech signal.
   - The spectrogram is used to analyze the frequency content of the speech signal and to identify different phonemes.

4. Mel-Frequency Cepstral Coefficients (MFCCs)
   - MFCCs are a set of features that are extracted from the speech signal.
   - MFCCs are used to represent the spectral envelope of the speech signal.
   - MFCCs are commonly used in speech recognition systems.

5. Speech Recognition
   - Speech recognition is the process of transcribing spoken words into written text.
   - Speech recognition systems use various techniques to recognize speech, such as hidden Markov models and neural networks.
   - Speech recognition systems are used in many applications, such as dictation software and voice assistants.

In conclusion, speech fundamentals are essential for understanding the basics of speech processing in natural language processing. This section covered the basic concepts of speech signals, phonemes, spectrograms, MFCCs, and speech recognition.
