### Hidden Markov Models for the notes of the Unit 7 - SPEECH MODELING in the subject of Natural Language Processing

In this unit, we will learn about Hidden Markov Models (HMMs) and how they are used in speech modeling. HMMs are a type of statistical model that is used to represent a sequence of observations or events. 

Here are some key points to keep in mind while studying HMMs for speech modeling:

- HMMs are used to model sequences of observations or events, where each observation is assumed to be generated by one of a finite number of underlying states.
- In speech modeling, the states represent different phonemes or sounds that make up the speech signal, and the observations are the acoustic features of the signal, such as the frequency or amplitude of the sound waves.
- The HMM model consists of a set of states, a transition matrix that specifies the probabilities of transitioning between states, and an emission matrix that specifies the probabilities of generating each observation from each state.
- The model parameters are typically learned from a set of training data using the Baum-Welch algorithm, which is a type of Expectation-Maximization algorithm.
- Once the model is trained, it can be used to recognize or classify speech signals by computing the likelihood of the observed acoustic features given the HMM model.
- HMMs can also be used for other tasks in speech processing, such as speech synthesis or speaker recognition.

In summary, HMMs are a powerful tool for speech modeling and have many applications in speech processing. Understanding the basic principles of HMMs and how they are used in speech modeling is essential for anyone working in the field of natural language processing.
