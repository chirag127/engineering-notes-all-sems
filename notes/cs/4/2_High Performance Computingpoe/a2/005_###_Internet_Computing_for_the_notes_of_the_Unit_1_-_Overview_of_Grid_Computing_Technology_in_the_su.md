### Internet Computing

- Internet Computing refers to using the internet for distributed computing. It utilizes the computational resources and data from multiple computers connected over the internet to solve a complex problem.
- The key components of Internet Computing are:
  - Client: The machine that initiates the computing task.
  - Broker: The server that accepts computing tasks from clients and distributes the tasks to participants.
  - Participants: The machines that donate their unused computational resources to complete the computing tasks distributed by the broker.
- The advantages of Internet Computing are:
  - Increased computational power: By harnessing the combined computational resources of multiple computers, more complex problems can be solved.
  - Low cost: As the computational resources are donated, the cost incurred is minimal.
  - Flexibility: The computational resources can be easily scaled by adding more participant machines.
- The disadvantages of Internet Computing are:
  - Security: There are security risks in sharing computing resources over the public internet.
  - Reliability: The unreliable and heterogeneous nature of the internet may affect the computing performance.
  - Software Compatibility: The software compatibility between the client, broker and participants may pose challenges.
- Examples of Internet Computing projects are SETI@home, Folding@Home, etc. They are used for applications like molecular modeling, weather forecasting, etc.

- Mnemonics: "IC contacts CLI, B delegates to P" - Internet Computing contacts Client, Broker delegates to Participants.
- Learning Trick: Visualize the flow of tasks and data between the client, broker and participants to understand the working of Internet Computing.
