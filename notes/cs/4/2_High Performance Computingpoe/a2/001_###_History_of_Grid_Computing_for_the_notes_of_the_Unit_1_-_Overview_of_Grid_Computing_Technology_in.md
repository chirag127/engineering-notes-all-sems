### History of Grid Computing

- The concept of Grid computing originated in the early 1990s as a metaphor for making computer power as easy to access as an electric power grid.
- The first use of the term "grid" in the context of distributed computing was by researchers at CERN in the mid 1990s to describe their vision of a distributed computing infrastructure for scientific computing.
- The DataGrid project (2001-2006) was instrumental in creating many core Grid technologies including Grid security, resource brokers, data management, and portals. The project developed many technologies still in use today including the Globus Toolkit.
- The European Union funded several large Grid research projects including DataGrid, EGEE, and EGI. These projects brought together many organizations and researchers to build Grid infrastructures and applications.
- In the United States, the National Science Foundation funded the TeraGrid project (2001-2011) to provide high-performance computing resources for scientific research. TeraGrid made large supercomputers available to researchers over high-speed networks, pioneering a service model for high-end computing.
- Commercial companies like Platform Computing started offering commercial workload management products for Grid computing in the early 2000s. The use of virtualization and cloud technologies led to the development of more flexible computing and cloud platforms that build on the experiences from Grid computing.
- Today, the lines between Grid computing, high-performance computing, and cloud computing are increasingly blurred. However, many of the core concepts and technologies originally developed for Grid computing remain in use.
