### High Performance Computing

- High Performance Computing (HPC) refers to the aggregation of computing power to solve complex computational problems that are beyond the capability of a single system.
- HPC systems are used to solve advanced scientific and engineering problems that require large amounts of computing power and data processing.
- The key characteristics of HPC systems are:
-- Massively parallel processing: Using thousands of processors to solve a problem simultaneously.
-- High speed interconnects: Used to connect the processors and memory with low latency and high bandwidth.
-- Tightly coupled systems: The processors work together closely and depend on each other to solve the problem.
-- Scalability: The systems can be scaled up by adding more processors to solve larger problems.

**Mnemonics:** HPC - Aggregation of computing power to solve complex problems beyond single system capability.

**Learning tricks:** Remember HPC using the key characteristics - Massively parallel processing, High speed interconnects, Tightly coupled systems, Scalability.

**Advantages:** Can solve complex problems fast. Used in advanced scientific research and engineering tasks like weather forecasting, molecular modeling, physical simulations, etc.
**Disadvantages:** Very expensive to build and maintain. Programming such systems is challenging and requires parallel programming expertise.

**Applications:** Scientific research, weather forecasting, physical simulations, quantum chemistry, aerospace engineering, etc.
