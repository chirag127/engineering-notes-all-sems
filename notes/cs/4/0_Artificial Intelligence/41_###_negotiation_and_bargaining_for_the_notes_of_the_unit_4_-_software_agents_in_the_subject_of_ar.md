### Negotiation and Bargaining for the notes of the Unit 4 - Software Agents in the subject of Artificial Intelligence
Negotiation and bargaining are processes in which two or more parties come together to reach an agreement on a specific issue or set of issues. In the context of software agents, negotiation and bargaining refer to the process of allowing agents to communicate and make decisions in order to maximize their individual objectives.

Negotiation can be viewed as a problem-solving process in which the agents work together to find a mutually acceptable solution. This process involves exchanging information, making offers and counter-offers, and reaching a final agreement.

Bargaining, on the other hand, is a decision-making process in which the agents compete to achieve their individual objectives. This process often involves making trade-offs and compromises in order to reach a final agreement.

In both negotiation and bargaining, the agents must be able to understand and interpret the information being exchanged, as well as the objectives of the other agents involved in the process. This requires the use of decision-making algorithms and models, such as game theory, decision theory, and utility theory.

It is important to note that negotiation and bargaining are not always straightforward processes, and may involve multiple rounds of communication and decision-making before a final agreement is reached. Additionally, the outcome of the negotiation or bargaining process may not always be optimal for all parties involved.
