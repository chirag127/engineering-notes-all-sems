### English Morphology for the notes of the Unit 1 - INTRODUCTION in the subject of Natural Language Processing

Morphology is the study of the structure of words and how they are formed. It is an important aspect of Natural Language Processing (NLP) as it helps in understanding the words and their meanings in a language. English Morphology is the study of the structure of words in the English language. In this unit, we will learn about the basic concepts of English Morphology.

1. Words and Morphemes
   - A word is the smallest unit of language that carries meaning.
   - A morpheme is the smallest unit of meaning in a word.
   - Words can be made up of one or more morphemes.

2. Types of Morphemes
   - Free Morphemes: These are the morphemes that can stand alone as words, such as 'cat', 'dog', 'happy', etc.
   - Bound Morphemes: These are the morphemes that cannot stand alone as words and need to be attached to free morphemes to create meaning. For example, the prefix 'un-' in 'unhappy' changes the meaning of the word 'happy'.

3. Affixes
   - Affixes are the bound morphemes that are attached to free morphemes to create new words.
   - There are two types of affixes:
     - Prefixes: These are the affixes that are attached to the beginning of a word, such as 'un-' in 'unhappy'.
     - Suffixes: These are the affixes that are attached to the end of a word, such as '-ness' in 'happiness'.

4. Inflectional and Derivational Morphemes
   - Inflectional Morphemes: These are the morphemes that are added to a word to change its grammatical function, such as plurals, tenses, and possessives. For example, the '-s' in 'cats' indicates plural.
   - Derivational Morphemes: These are the morphemes that are added to a word to create a new word with a different meaning. For example, the prefix 'dis-' in 'dislike' changes the meaning of the word 'like'.

5. Stem and Root Words
   - Stem Words: These are the words to which affixes are added to create new words. For example, in the word 'unhappy', 'happy' is the stem word.
   - Root Words: These are the basic words that cannot be further broken down into smaller parts. For example, in the word 'happiness', 'happy' is the root word.

Mnemonics and Learning Tricks:
- A helpful trick to remember the difference between inflectional and derivational morphemes is to think of inflectional morphemes as "grammar glue" that changes the form of the word, while derivational morphemes are "meaning makers" that change the meaning of the word.
- Another trick to remember the difference between prefix and suffix is to think of prefix as "pre-game" that comes before the main word, while suffix is "after-party" that comes after the main word.

English morphology is an essential part of natural language processing, and understanding its basic concepts is crucial for developing language models and processing natural language data.
