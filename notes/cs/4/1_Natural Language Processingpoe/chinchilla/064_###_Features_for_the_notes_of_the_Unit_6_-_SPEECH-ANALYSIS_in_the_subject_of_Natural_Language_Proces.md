### Features for the notes of the Unit 6 - SPEECH-ANALYSIS in the subject of Natural Language Processing

Speech analysis is the process of extracting meaningful information from speech signals. It involves the use of various techniques and tools to analyze different aspects of speech such as pitch, intensity, and duration. In this unit, we will learn about the different features used in speech analysis. 

Here are some of the important features that we will cover in this unit:

1. Pitch: Pitch is the perceived highness or lowness of a sound. In speech analysis, pitch is measured in Hertz (Hz) and is used to determine the fundamental frequency of a speech signal. The fundamental frequency is an important feature used to identify different phonemes and to distinguish between different speakers.

2. Intensity: Intensity is the perceived loudness of a sound. In speech analysis, intensity is measured in decibels (dB) and is used to determine the energy level of a speech signal. The energy level is an important feature used to identify different emotions and to distinguish between different speakers.

3. Duration: Duration is the length of time that a sound persists. In speech analysis, duration is used to determine the length of time that a speech sound or phoneme is produced. The duration of a speech sound is an important feature used to identify different phonemes and to distinguish between different speakers.

4. Formants: Formants are resonant frequencies that are present in the speech signal. In speech analysis, formants are used to identify different vowels and to distinguish between different speakers.

5. Mel-frequency cepstral coefficients (MFCCs): MFCCs are a type of feature used in speech analysis that are based on the human auditory system. They are used to represent the spectral envelope of a speech signal and are commonly used in speech recognition and speaker recognition systems.

6. Prosodic features: Prosodic features are features that relate to the rhythm, intonation, and stress patterns of speech. They are used to identify different emotions and to distinguish between different speakers.

Mnemonics and learning tricks:

- For remembering the different features of speech analysis, you can use the acronym "PID FM". 
- To remember the different aspects of prosodic features, you can use the acronym "RIS" which stands for rhythm, intonation, and stress. 

In conclusion, the features of speech analysis play a crucial role in various applications of natural language processing such as speech recognition, speaker recognition, and emotion recognition. Understanding these features is essential for building accurate and reliable speech analysis systems.
