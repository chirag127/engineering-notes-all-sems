### Articulatory Phonetics for the notes of the Unit 5 - BASIC CONCEPTS of Speech Processing in the subject of Natural Language Processing

Articulatory phonetics is the study of how speech sounds are produced by the human vocal tract. It is an important field of study in natural language processing as it helps in understanding the basic concepts of speech processing. Here are some key points to help you understand articulatory phonetics:

1. The human vocal tract is made up of various parts, including the lips, teeth, tongue, palate, and vocal cords. These parts work together to produce speech sounds.

2. There are two main types of speech sounds: vowels and consonants. Vowels are produced when air flows freely through the vocal tract, while consonants are produced when the flow of air is obstructed or constricted in some way.

3. Vowels are classified based on the position of the tongue and lips when they are produced. There are different types of vowels, including front vowels, back vowels, and rounded vowels.

4. Consonants are classified based on the manner and place of articulation. The manner of articulation refers to how the flow of air is obstructed or constricted, while the place of articulation refers to where the obstruction or constriction occurs in the vocal tract.

5. Mnemonics and learning tricks can be helpful in remembering the different types of vowels and consonants. For example, to remember the different types of front vowels (i, e, æ), you can use the mnemonic "I Eat Apples". To remember the different types of stops (p, b, t, d, k, g), you can use the phrase "Please Be Toadally Good Kids".

6. Advanced topics in articulatory phonetics include coarticulation, which refers to how speech sounds influence one another, and prosody, which refers to the rhythm, intonation, and stress of speech.

7. Articulatory phonetics has applications in fields such as speech therapy, language learning, and speech recognition technology.

In summary, articulatory phonetics is an important field of study in natural language processing. It helps in understanding how speech sounds are produced and classified, and can be useful in various applications related to speech processing.
