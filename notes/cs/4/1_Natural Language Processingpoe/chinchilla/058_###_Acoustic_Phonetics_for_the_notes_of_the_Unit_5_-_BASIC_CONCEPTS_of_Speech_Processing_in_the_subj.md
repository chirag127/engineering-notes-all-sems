### Acoustic Phonetics for the notes of the Unit 5 - BASIC CONCEPTS of Speech Processing in the subject of Natural Language Processing

Acoustic phonetics is the study of the physical properties of speech sounds, including their frequency, amplitude, and duration. It is an important subfield of phonetics and is used to analyze and describe the sounds of spoken language. In this unit, we will cover the basic concepts of acoustic phonetics.

#### Properties of Sound Waves

Sound waves are longitudinal waves that travel through a medium, such as air. Some of the important properties of sound waves are:

- Frequency: The number of cycles (or vibrations) per second is called frequency, and it is measured in hertz (Hz). The frequency of a sound wave determines its pitch, with higher frequencies corresponding to higher pitches.
- Amplitude: The magnitude of the pressure variation in the sound wave is called amplitude, and it is measured in decibels (dB). The amplitude of a sound wave determines its loudness, with higher amplitudes corresponding to louder sounds.
- Wavelength: The distance between two successive points in a sound wave that are in phase (i.e., have the same displacement and velocity) is called wavelength, and it is measured in meters (m). The wavelength of a sound wave is inversely proportional to its frequency.
- Phase: The position of a point in a sound wave relative to a reference point is called phase, and it is measured in degrees or radians.

#### Spectrogram

A spectrogram is a visual representation of the frequency content of a sound signal over time. It is a commonly used tool in acoustic phonetics for analyzing speech sounds. A spectrogram displays the frequency spectrum of a sound signal at each point in time, with time represented on the x-axis and frequency represented on the y-axis. The intensity of the frequency components is typically indicated by color or grayscale.

#### Formants

Formants are the resonant frequencies of the vocal tract during speech production. They are important acoustic cues for distinguishing between different vowel sounds. The first formant (F1) corresponds to the resonant frequency of the vocal tract when it is in its most open configuration, and it is inversely proportional to the height of the tongue. The second formant (F2) corresponds to the resonant frequency of the vocal tract when it is in its most constricted configuration, and it is proportional to the front-back position of the tongue.

#### Mnemonics and Learning Tricks

- To remember the properties of sound waves, use the mnemonic "FLAP": Frequency is Likelier to Affect Pitch, while Amplitude is Likelier to Affect Loudness, and Wavelength is Likelier to Affect Speed.
- To remember the relationship between formants and vowel sounds, use the mnemonic "F2 is like the vowel's shoe size": just as shoe size varies with the length and width of the foot, F2 varies with the front-back position of the tongue.

In conclusion, acoustic phonetics is a crucial area of study in phonetics and natural language processing. Understanding the physical properties of speech sounds, such as frequency, amplitude, and formants, is essential for analyzing and synthesizing speech signals. Spectrograms are a useful tool for visualizing the frequency content of speech sounds over time. Mnemonics and learning tricks can be helpful in remembering these concepts and relationships.
