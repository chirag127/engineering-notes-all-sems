### Mathematical And Perceptual for the notes of the Unit 6 - SPEECH-ANALYSIS in the subject of Natural Language Processing

Speech analysis is the study of the acoustic properties of speech. It involves both mathematical and perceptual aspects. The mathematical aspect deals with the quantification and measurement of various acoustic properties of speech while the perceptual aspect deals with how we perceive and interpret these properties.

In this unit, we will study the mathematical and perceptual aspects of speech analysis. Here are the key points to keep in mind:

#### Mathematical Aspect

1. Speech Signal - Speech signal is a time-varying signal that carries information about the speech sound. It is typically represented as a waveform, which is a plot of the amplitude of the signal over time.

2. Fourier Analysis - Fourier analysis is a mathematical technique used to decompose a complex signal into its constituent frequencies. It is used extensively in speech analysis to extract information about the spectral properties of speech.

3. Spectrogram - Spectrogram is a visual representation of the spectrum of a speech signal as it varies with time. It is a 2D plot where the x-axis represents time, the y-axis represents frequency, and the color represents the amplitude of the signal at that time and frequency.

4. Mel-Frequency Cepstral Coefficients (MFCCs) - MFCCs are a popular feature extraction technique used in speech analysis. They are based on the human auditory system's perception of sound and are used to represent the spectral envelope of a speech signal.

#### Perceptual Aspect

1. Phonemes - Phonemes are the smallest units of sound that can distinguish words in a language. They are perceived differently by different individuals, and their perception is influenced by factors such as context and speaker variability.

2. Coarticulation - Coarticulation is the phenomenon where the articulation of one sound influences the articulation of the surrounding sounds. It plays an important role in speech perception and can affect the acoustic properties of speech.

3. Speech Perception - Speech perception is the process by which we interpret the acoustic properties of speech to extract meaning. It is a complex process that involves both bottom-up (acoustic) and top-down (linguistic and contextual) processes.

4. Speech Recognition - Speech recognition is the process of converting spoken language into text or commands. It is an important application of speech analysis and involves both mathematical and perceptual aspects.

Mnemonics and Learning Tricks:

1. To remember the difference between the mathematical and perceptual aspects of speech analysis, think of "math" and "percept" as two different lenses through which we can view speech.

2. To remember the concept of coarticulation, think of it as "co-articulation," where the articulation of one sound is influenced by the articulation of another sound.

Overall, understanding the mathematical and perceptual aspects of speech analysis is crucial for natural language processing applications such as speech recognition and synthesis. By mastering the key concepts and techniques covered in this unit, you will be better equipped to analyze and interpret speech signals.
