### Speech Distortion Measures for the notes of the Unit 6 - SPEECH-ANALYSIS in the subject of Natural Language Processing

Speech distortion measures are important in speech analysis as they help to evaluate the quality of speech signals. These measures are used to assess the level of distortion in speech signals caused by noise, channel distortions, and other factors that affect the quality of speech signals. In this section, we will discuss some of the common speech distortion measures used in speech analysis.

#### Mean Opinion Score (MOS)

Mean Opinion Score (MOS) is a subjective measure used to evaluate the quality of speech signals. It is obtained by asking human listeners to rate the quality of speech signals on a scale of 1 to 5, where 1 represents poor quality and 5 represents excellent quality. MOS is widely used in speech analysis as it provides a direct measure of the quality of speech signals.

#### Signal-to-Noise Ratio (SNR)

Signal-to-Noise Ratio (SNR) is a measure used to evaluate the level of noise in speech signals. It is obtained by dividing the power of the speech signal by the power of the noise signal. SNR is expressed in decibels (dB) and a higher SNR value indicates a lower level of noise in the speech signal.

#### Perceptual Evaluation of Speech Quality (PESQ)

Perceptual Evaluation of Speech Quality (PESQ) is an objective measure used to evaluate the quality of speech signals. It is based on the human perception of speech quality and is widely used in speech analysis. PESQ measures the difference between the original speech signal and the degraded speech signal and provides a score between -0.5 and 4.5, where a higher score indicates better quality.

#### Itakura-Saito Distance (ISD)

Itakura-Saito Distance (ISD) is a measure used to evaluate the spectral distortion in speech signals. It measures the difference between the original speech signal and the degraded speech signal in the frequency domain. ISD is widely used in speech analysis as it provides a measure of the spectral distortion caused by channel distortions and other factors.

#### Mel-Frequency Cepstral Coefficients (MFCCs)

Mel-Frequency Cepstral Coefficients (MFCCs) are a feature extraction technique widely used in speech analysis. They are obtained by taking the Fourier transform of the speech signal and then taking the logarithm of the magnitude of the resulting spectrum. MFCCs are widely used in speech recognition as they provide a compact representation of the speech signal that is robust to noise and other distortions.

#### Learning Tricks and Mnemonics

Learning tricks and mnemonics can be helpful in remembering the different speech distortion measures. For example, to remember the Itakura-Saito Distance (ISD), one could use the mnemonic "ISD measures the spectral distortion in speech signals". Similarly, to remember the Mel-Frequency Cepstral Coefficients (MFCCs), one could use the mnemonic "MFCCs are obtained by taking the Fourier transform of the speech signal and then taking the logarithm of the magnitude of the resulting spectrum". However, it is important to note that these tricks and mnemonics should only be used if they are helpful and easy to remember.
