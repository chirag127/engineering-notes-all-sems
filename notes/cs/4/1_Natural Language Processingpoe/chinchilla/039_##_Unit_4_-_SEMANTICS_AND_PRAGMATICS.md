## Unit 4 - Semantics and Pragmatics

This unit focuses on two important aspects of language: semantics and pragmatics. Semantics deals with the meaning of words and sentences, while pragmatics deals with how people use language in different social contexts. Here are some key concepts and topics that you should know for this unit:

### Semantics

- Semantics is the study of meaning in language. It involves analyzing the meanings of words, phrases, and sentences, as well as the relationships between them.
- One important concept in semantics is word sense. Words can have multiple senses or meanings depending on the context in which they are used.
- Another important concept is reference. Reference is the relationship between a linguistic expression (e.g., a word or phrase) and the real-world objects or concepts that it refers to.
- Other topics in semantics include truth conditions, entailment, presupposition, and implicature.

### Pragmatics

- Pragmatics is the study of how people use language in social contexts. It involves analyzing the communicative functions of language, as well as the social and cultural factors that influence language use.
- One important concept in pragmatics is speech act theory. Speech acts are the actions that speakers perform when they use language (e.g., making a request or giving an apology).
- Another important concept is implicature. Implicatures are the inferences that listeners make based on the context and the speaker's intended meaning, even if that meaning is not explicitly stated.
- Other topics in pragmatics include deixis, presupposition, politeness, and discourse analysis.

### Mnemonics and Learning Tricks

- For word sense, try to associate each sense of a word with a different context or example. For example, the word "bank" can mean a financial institution or the side of a river, so you could remember the financial sense by associating it with money and the river sense by associating it with nature.
- For speech acts, try to remember the different types by associating them with common phrases or situations. For example, "Can you pass the salt?" is a request, while "I'm sorry" is an apology.
- For implicature, try to remember that it involves making inferences based on context. Think about how you would interpret a statement differently depending on the situation or the speaker's tone of voice.

Overall, understanding semantics and pragmatics is essential for understanding how language works in different contexts and for communicating effectively with others. By remembering key concepts and using mnemonic devices, you can better prepare for exams and improve your language skills.
