### Relations between Senses for the notes of the Unit 4 - SEMANTICS AND PRAGMATICS in the subject of Natural Language Processing

In Natural Language Processing (NLP), Semantics and Pragmatics are two important fields that deal with the meaning of language. In this unit, we will focus on the relations between senses, which is an important concept in Semantics.

#### What are Senses?

In Semantics, a sense is a way of representing the meaning of a word or phrase. Every word or phrase can have multiple senses, depending on the context in which it is used.

#### Types of Relations between Senses

There are various relations between senses, which are classified into the following types:

1. Synonymy: It is a relation between two words that have the same or similar meaning. For example, 'big' and 'large' are synonyms.

2. Antonymy: It is a relation between two words that have opposite meanings. For example, 'hot' and 'cold' are antonyms.

3. Hyponymy/Hypernymy: It is a hierarchical relation between two words, where one word (the hyponym) is a type of the other word (the hypernym). For example, 'apple' is a hyponym of 'fruit', and 'fruit' is a hypernym of 'apple'.

4. Meronymy/Holonymy: It is a part-whole relation between two words, where one word (the meronym) is a part of the other word (the holonym). For example, 'wheel' is a meronym of 'car', and 'car' is a holonym of 'wheel'.

5. Polysemy: It is a relation between multiple senses of a word, where the different senses are related to each other in some way. For example, 'bank' can refer to a financial institution, a river bank, or a slope.

#### Mnemonics and Learning Tricks

To remember the relations between senses, you can use the following mnemonics:

1. Synonymy: Think of 'syn' as meaning 'together', and 'onym' as meaning 'name'. So, synonymy is a relation between words that have the same name.

2. Antonymy: Think of 'anti' as meaning 'opposite'. So, antonymy is a relation between words that have opposite meanings.

3. Hyponymy/Hypernymy: Think of 'hypo' as meaning 'below', and 'hyper' as meaning 'above'. So, hyponymy is a relation between words that are below (types of) the hypernym, and hypernymy is a relation between words that are above (generalizations of) the hyponym.

4. Meronymy/Holonymy: Think of 'mero' as meaning 'part', and 'holo' as meaning 'whole'. So, meronymy is a relation between words that are parts of the holonym, and holonymy is a relation between words that are wholes that contain the meronym.

5. Polysemy: Think of 'poly' as meaning 'many', and 'semy' as meaning 'meaning'. So, polysemy is a relation between words that have many meanings.

#### Conclusion

Relations between senses are an important concept in Semantics, and understanding them is crucial for developing effective NLP applications. By using the above mnemonics and learning tricks, you can remember the different types of relations between senses and their meanings.
