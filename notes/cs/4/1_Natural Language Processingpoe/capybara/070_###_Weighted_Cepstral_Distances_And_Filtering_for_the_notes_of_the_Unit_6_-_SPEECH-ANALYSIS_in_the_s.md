### Weighted Cepstral Distances And Filtering for the notes of the Unit 6 - SPEECH-ANALYSIS in the subject of Natural Language Processing

Speech analysis is an important area of research in Natural Language Processing. Weighted cepstral distances and filtering are two techniques used for speech analysis. In this note, we will discuss these techniques in detail.

#### Weighted Cepstral Distances

Weighted cepstral distances are used to measure the similarity between two speech signals. The cepstral distance is a measure of the distance between the cepstral coefficients of two signals. The cepstral coefficients represent the spectral envelope of the speech signal.

The weighted cepstral distance is calculated by weighting the cepstral coefficients with a weight function. The weight function is designed to give more weight to the important regions of the speech spectrum. The weighted cepstral distance is more robust to noise and other distortions in the speech signal.

#### Filtering

Filtering is a technique used to remove unwanted noise and other distortions from the speech signal. There are two types of filtering techniques used in speech analysis:

1. Pre-emphasis filtering: This technique is used to boost the high-frequency components of the speech signal. The pre-emphasis filter is a high-pass filter that emphasizes the higher frequencies of the speech signal. This makes the speech signal more robust to noise and other distortions.

2. De-emphasis filtering: This technique is used to reduce the high-frequency components of the speech signal. The de-emphasis filter is a low-pass filter that reduces the higher frequencies of the speech signal. This makes the speech signal more natural and easier to understand.

#### Mnemonics and Learning Tricks

To remember the difference between pre-emphasis and de-emphasis filtering, you can use the mnemonic "PEE and DEE." PEE stands for pre-emphasis filtering, and DEE stands for de-emphasis filtering. Another trick is to remember that pre-emphasis filtering makes the speech signal sharper, while de-emphasis filtering makes the speech signal smoother.

In conclusion, weighted cepstral distances and filtering are important techniques used in speech analysis. They help to measure the similarity between two speech signals and remove unwanted noise and other distortions from the speech signal. Remembering the difference between pre-emphasis and de-emphasis filtering can be made easier by using mnemonics and learning tricks.
