### Articulatory Phonetics for the notes of the Unit 5 - BASIC CONCEPTS of Speech Processing in the subject of Natural Language Processing

Articulatory phonetics is a branch of phonetics that deals with the physical mechanisms involved in producing speech sounds. It is concerned with the movements of the speech organs and their effect on the sounds produced. In this section, we will discuss the basic concepts of articulatory phonetics.

#### Speech Organs

The speech organs are the parts of the body involved in producing speech sounds. These include:

- Lungs: They produce the air needed for speech.
- Trachea: It is the windpipe that carries air from the lungs to the larynx.
- Larynx: It contains the vocal cords that vibrate to produce speech sounds.
- Pharynx: It is the cavity at the back of the mouth that connects the nasal and oral cavities.
- Oral cavity: It includes the lips, teeth, tongue, and the roof and floor of the mouth.
- Nasal cavity: It is the cavity behind the nose that connects to the pharynx.

#### Consonants

Consonants are speech sounds that are produced by obstructing the airflow from the lungs in some way. They can be classified based on the place of articulation, manner of articulation, and voicing.

- Place of articulation: It refers to the point in the vocal tract where the obstruction occurs. Examples include bilabial, labiodental, dental, alveolar, palatal, and velar.
- Manner of articulation: It refers to the way in which the airflow is obstructed. Examples include stops, fricatives, affricates, nasals, and liquids.
- Voicing: It refers to whether the vocal cords are vibrating or not. Examples include voiced and voiceless consonants.

#### Vowels

Vowels are speech sounds that are produced without any obstruction to the airflow. They can be classified based on their tongue position and lip position.

- Tongue position: It refers to the position of the tongue in the oral cavity. Examples include front, central, and back.
- Lip position: It refers to the position of the lips. Examples include rounded and unrounded.

#### Mnemonic

A useful mnemonic for remembering the places of articulation is "Peter Parker Picks Peppers, Mary Marries More". Each word corresponds to a place of articulation in order from left to right: bilabial, labiodental, dental, alveolar, palatal, and velar.

Another helpful trick for remembering the difference between voiced and voiceless consonants is to place your hand on your throat while making the sounds. Voiced consonants will cause vibration in your throat, while voiceless consonants will not.

#### Conclusion

Articulatory phonetics is an important field for understanding how speech sounds are produced. By understanding the movements of the speech organs, we can better analyze and generate speech using natural language processing techniques.
