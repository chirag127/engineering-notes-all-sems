### Word Senses for the notes of the Unit 4 - SEMANTICS AND PRAGMATICS in the subject of Natural Language Processing

In natural language processing, understanding the meaning of words is crucial. However, words can have multiple senses depending on the context in which they are used. Therefore, it is important to identify and distinguish between the different word senses. Here are some important points to remember:

1. Word Sense Disambiguation (WSD): WSD is the process of identifying the correct sense of a word in a given context. There are two main approaches to WSD: supervised and unsupervised. Supervised approaches use labeled data to train classifiers, while unsupervised approaches rely on clustering and distributional similarity.

2. Types of Word Senses: Words can have two main types of senses - lexical sense and contextual sense. Lexical senses are the inherent meanings of words, while contextual senses depend on the context in which they are used.

3. Homonyms: Homonyms are words that have the same spelling but different meanings. For example, the word "bat" can refer to a flying mammal or a piece of sports equipment.

4. Polysemous Words: Polysemous words are words that have multiple related meanings. For example, the word "bank" can refer to a financial institution or the edge of a river.

5. Synonyms and Antonyms: Synonyms are words that have similar meanings, while antonyms are words that have opposite meanings. For example, "happy" and "joyful" are synonyms, while "happy" and "sad" are antonyms.

6. Mnemonics and Learning Tricks: One mnemonic for remembering different word senses is to use the acronym "HOPS" - Homonyms, Opposites, Polysemous, and Synonyms. Another trick is to create word associations or use visual aids to help remember different word senses.

In conclusion, understanding the different word senses is essential for natural language processing. Word Sense Disambiguation techniques can help identify the correct sense of a word in a given context, and being familiar with different types of word senses can aid in building better models for NLP.
