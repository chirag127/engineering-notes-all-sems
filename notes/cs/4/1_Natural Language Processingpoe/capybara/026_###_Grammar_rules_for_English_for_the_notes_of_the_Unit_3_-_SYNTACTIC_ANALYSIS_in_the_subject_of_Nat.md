### Grammar rules for English for the notes of the Unit 3 - SYNTACTIC ANALYSIS in the subject of Natural Language Processing

English language has a set of rules that govern the way words are combined to form sentences. These rules are known as grammar rules. Here are some of the important grammar rules for English that are necessary for syntactic analysis in natural language processing:

1. Parts of Speech: English language has eight parts of speech, namely, noun, pronoun, verb, adjective, adverb, preposition, conjunction, and interjection. Each part of speech has its own function in a sentence.

2. Subject-Verb Agreement: The subject of a sentence must agree with the verb in number (singular or plural). For instance, “He runs” is correct while “He run” is incorrect.

3. Tense: The tense of a verb indicates the time of an action. There are three main tenses in English: past, present, and future. Each tense has four forms: simple, progressive, perfect, and perfect progressive.

4. Articles: Articles are used to indicate whether a noun is specific or general. There are two articles in English: “a” and “an” (for singular nouns) and “the” (for both singular and plural nouns).

5. Syntax: Syntax refers to the arrangement of words in a sentence to convey meaning. In English, the usual word order is subject-verb-object (SVO), but other orders are also possible.

6. Modifiers: Modifiers are words that describe or qualify other words in a sentence. Adjectives modify nouns, while adverbs modify verbs, adjectives, or other adverbs.

7. Pronouns: Pronouns are used to replace nouns in a sentence. They include personal pronouns (I, you, he, she, it, we, they), possessive pronouns (mine, yours, his, hers, its, ours, theirs), and reflexive pronouns (myself, yourself, himself, herself, itself, ourselves, themselves).

Mnemonics and Learning Tricks:

1. To remember the eight parts of speech in English, you can use the mnemonic “FANBOYS” for conjunctions (for, and, nor, but, or, yet, so) and “VIP” for interjections (wow, oh, ouch).

2. To remember subject-verb agreement, you can use the trick of mentally inserting the word “and” between the subject and verb to check if they agree. For example, “The dog barks” becomes “The dog and barks”, which is incorrect.

3. To remember the four forms of each tense, you can use the acronym “SPPF” for simple, progressive, perfect, and perfect progressive.

In conclusion, understanding and applying these grammar rules is essential for syntactic analysis in natural language processing. By using mnemonics and learning tricks, you can make it easier to remember and apply these rules in your work.
