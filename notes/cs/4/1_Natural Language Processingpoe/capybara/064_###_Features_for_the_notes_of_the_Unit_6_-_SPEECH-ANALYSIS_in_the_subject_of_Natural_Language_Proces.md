### Features for the notes of the Unit 6 - SPEECH-ANALYSIS in the subject of Natural Language Processing

Speech analysis is the process of extracting information from the spoken language. It is a crucial part of natural language processing, as speech is one of the primary modes of communication. In this unit, we will learn about the various features used in speech analysis. Here are the key features that you should take note of:

1. Phonemes: Phonemes are the basic units of sound in a language. They are the smallest units of sound that can change the meaning of a word. For example, the words "cat" and "bat" differ by only one phoneme, the "c" and "b" sound respectively. In speech analysis, phonemes are used to identify and distinguish different sounds in the spoken language.

2. Spectrograms: Spectrograms are visual representations of sound waves. They are used to analyze the frequency and amplitude of different sounds in speech. Spectrograms are useful in identifying patterns and trends in speech, such as the rise and fall of pitch or the length of pauses between words.

3. Mel-Frequency Cepstral Coefficients (MFCCs): MFCCs are a set of features that are commonly used in speech analysis. They are derived from the spectrogram and are used to represent the frequency content of speech. MFCCs are useful in speech recognition tasks, as they capture important details about the speaker's voice, such as their accent and intonation.

4. Pitch: Pitch is the perceived highness or lowness of a sound. It is an important feature in speech analysis, as it can convey information about the speaker's emotions and intentions. Pitch is measured in Hertz (Hz) and is often used in speech recognition tasks to distinguish between different words and phrases.

5. Formants: Formants are the resonant frequencies of the vocal tract. They are used to identify different vowel sounds in speech. Formants are important in speech analysis, as they can be used to distinguish between different accents and dialects.

Mnemonics and learning tricks:

- To remember the difference between phonemes and spectrograms, you can think of phonemes as the building blocks of speech, while spectrograms are the visual representation of those blocks.
- To remember what MFCCs are, you can think of them as a way to capture the unique "fingerprint" of a person's voice.
- To remember the importance of pitch, you can think of it as the musicality of speech, conveying the emotion and tone of the speaker.
- To remember what formants are, you can think of them as the "shape" of the vowel sounds, distinguishing between different accents and dialects.
