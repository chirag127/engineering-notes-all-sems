## Unit 4 - SEMANTICS AND PRAGMATICS

In this unit, we will learn about semantics and pragmatics, which are two important branches of linguistics that deal with meaning in language. Here are some key points to keep in mind:

### Semantics
- Semantics is the study of meaning in language.
- It is concerned with how words and sentences convey meaning and how people interpret that meaning.
- The basic unit of meaning in language is the word.
- Words have denotation (literal meaning) and connotation (associative meaning).
- Words also have sense (dictionary definition) and reference (what the word refers to in the real world).
- Semantics also deals with how words combine to form sentences and how the meaning of a sentence is derived from the meanings of its component parts.
- There are several approaches to studying semantics, including truth-conditional semantics, cognitive semantics, and lexical semantics.

### Pragmatics
- Pragmatics is the study of how people use language in context.
- It is concerned with how speakers convey meaning beyond the literal meaning of words and sentences.
- Pragmatics deals with issues such as implicature (what is implied but not explicitly stated), presupposition (what is assumed to be true), and speech acts (what speakers accomplish by saying something).
- Pragmatics also involves the study of language use in social contexts, such as politeness, indirectness, and humor.
- There are several approaches to studying pragmatics, including speech act theory, relevance theory, and conversational implicature.

### Mnemonics and Learning Tricks
- To remember the difference between semantics and pragmatics, think of semantics as dealing with the literal meaning of language (what is said) and pragmatics as dealing with the implied meaning (what is meant).
- To remember the different types of meaning associated with words (denotation, connotation, sense, and reference), use the acronym DCSR (Denotation, Connotation, Sense, Reference).
- To remember some key concepts in pragmatics, use the acronym PIPS (Presupposition, Implicature, Politeness, Speech acts).
