### English Morphology for the notes of the Unit 1 - INTRODUCTION in the subject of Natural Language Processing

Morphology is the study of the structure of words and how they are formed. English morphology deals with the study of the structure and formation of English words. It is essential to understand English morphology to comprehend the language's meaning and identify the words' grammatical functions.

Here are some important points to remember about English morphology:

1. Morpheme:
   - A morpheme is the smallest unit of meaning in a word.
   - It cannot be divided into smaller meaningful units.
   - Example: "in-" and "credible" are two morphemes in the word "incredible." "In-" is a prefix that means "not," and "credible" means "believable."

2. Types of Morphemes:
   - Free morphemes: These morphemes can stand alone as words.
   - Bound morphemes: These morphemes cannot stand alone and need to be attached to a free morpheme to form a word.
   - Inflectional morphemes: These morphemes do not change the meaning of the word; they only indicate its grammatical function.
   - Derivational morphemes: These morphemes change the meaning of the word and create new words.
   
3. Affixes:
   - Affixes are bound morphemes that are added to the beginning or end of a free morpheme to create a new word.
   - Prefixes are added to the beginning of a word, while suffixes are added to the end.
   - Example: "un-" is a prefix that means "not," and "happy" is a free morpheme. When "un-" is added to "happy," we get "unhappy," which means "not happy."

4. Roots:
   - A root is a free morpheme that forms the basis of a word.
   - It carries the word's core meaning.
   - Example: "act" is the root in the words "actor," "action," and "activate."

5. Compound Words:
   - Compound words are formed by combining two or more words.
   - They can be written together, hyphenated, or separated by a space.
   - Example: "blackboard" is a compound word formed by combining "black" and "board."

6. Homophones:
   - Homophones are words that are pronounced the same but have different meanings and spellings.
   - Example: "meet" and "meat" are homophones.

7. Mnemonic:
   - A useful mnemonic to remember the difference between inflectional and derivational morphemes is to think of "in-" as meaning "inside" or "within." Inflectional morphemes are "inside" the word and do not change its meaning, while derivational morphemes are "outside" the word and change its meaning.

By understanding English morphology, one can identify the parts of a word and better comprehend its meaning and grammatical function.
