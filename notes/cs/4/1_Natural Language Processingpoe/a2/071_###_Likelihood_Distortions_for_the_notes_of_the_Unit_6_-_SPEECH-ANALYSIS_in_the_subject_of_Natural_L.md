### Likelihood Distortions for the notes of the Unit 6 - SPEECH-ANALYSIS in the subject of Natural Language Processing

- Likelihood distortions refer to the deviations of the acoustic signal from the actual spoken sounds. These are caused due to articulatory variations, coarticulation effects, and speaking rate variations.
- Some common likelihood distortions are:
-- Nasalization: When nasal sounds influence neighboring vowels, causing the vowels to be nasalized. Eg: "sun" sounding like "sun".
-- Fronting: When back vowels are pronounced more towards the front of the mouth. Eg: " caught" sounding like "cot".
-- Backing: When front vowels are pronounced more towards the back of the mouth. Eg: "bit" sounding like "bet".
-- Gliding: When a vowel changes quality during its articulation. Eg: "down" sounding like "daun" (with the "ow" gliding from back to front).
-- Flapping: When a 't' or 'd' sound is replaced by a flap in words like "water" or "father".
-- Elision: When a sound is dropped. Eg: "film" sounding like "fim".
-- Assimilation: When a sound takes on properties of a neighboring sound. Eg: "ten books" sounding like "tem books".

Some mnemonics and learning tricks for likelihood distortions:
- For nasalization: "Naughty nasal makes neighboring vowels nasal too"
- For fronting and backing: "Vowels head either front or back, when positions are swapped it's off-track!"
- For gliding, flapping, elision: No easy mnemonics, understand the concepts and practice identifying examples of these distortions.
- For assimilation: "One sound's trait, the neighbor does take"

Likelihood distortions are common in casual speech and make speech sound more native-like. However, they can interfere with automatic speech recognition systems and lead to recognition errors. Compensating for these distortions is an active area of research in speech analysis.
