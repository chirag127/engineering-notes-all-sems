### Log–Spectral Distance for the notes of the Unit 6 - SPEECH-ANALYSIS in the subject of Natural Language Processing

- Log–Spectral Distance is a distance metric used for measuring the similarity between two speech signals in the frequency domain.
- It calculates the distance between the log-spectral vectors of the two speech signals.
- The log-spectral vectors are obtained by taking the logarithm of the magnitude spectrum of the speech signals.
- The distance between the two log-spectral vectors is calculated as the Euclidean distance.
- A smaller Log–Spectral Distance indicates that the two speech signals are more similar while a larger distance indicates that they are more dissimilar.
- Advantages:
-- Insensitive to amplitude changes in the speech signal.
-- Does not require speech segmentation and can be applied to continuous speech.
-- Simple to compute as it just involves Euclidean distance calculation between log-spectral vectors.
- Disadvantages:
-- Does not consider the phase spectrum of the speech signals.
-- Vulnerable to distortions that affect the spectral envelope like filtering.
-- Mnemonics: Think Log–Spectrally, Measure Similarity Nearly ( relate log-spectral distance to measuring spectral similarity between speech signals)
- Applications: Speech recognition, Speaker recognition, Speech enhancement, etc.
