## Unit 2 - WORD LEVEL ANALYSIS

- **Lexical Analysis**: Breaking up input text into basic units called tokens. Tokens are individual instances of lexemes.
- **Lexeme**: The vocabulary item or set of characters in the text that a token represents. For example, the lexeme for the tokens "fish", "fished", and "fishes" is "fish".
- **Part of Speech Tagging (POS)**: Assigning grammatical categories to each token, such as noun, verb, adjective, adverb, preposition, etc. Helps identify the role of each word in a sentence. Useful for further NLP tasks.
- **Stop Words**: Common words like "the", "a", "is", "to", etc. that are filtered out before processing text further since they do not contribute much to the meaning.
- **Stemming**: Reducing related words to their word stem, root or base form. For example, stemming can reduce "fishing", "fished", and "fisher" to the base form "fish".
- **Lemmatization**: Similar to stemming but uses vocabulary and morphological analysis to map related words to their dictionary base forms. Usually produces better results than stemming.

**Mnemonics**:
- **"Lexicon" leads to "Lexeme"**: Remember that lexical analysis breaks input into lexemes (vocabulary items).
- **Part of Speech = Word's Job**: Part of speech tagging identifies the role of each word (noun, verb, etc.) in a sentence.
- **Stop filtering "common" workers**: Stop words are common words filtered out before processing text further.

**Advantages**: Word level analysis is fundamental to NLP and enables further processing and understanding of text. It identifies the building blocks of text and their roles.
**Disadvantages**: The rules for analysis may not always be precise. Ambiguities can arise, especially for POS tagging. Domain-specific vocabularies can also be problematic.
**Applications**: Word level analysis is applied in tasks like sentiment analysis, topic modeling, summarization, machine translation, etc. It is a crucial first step for most NLP applications.
