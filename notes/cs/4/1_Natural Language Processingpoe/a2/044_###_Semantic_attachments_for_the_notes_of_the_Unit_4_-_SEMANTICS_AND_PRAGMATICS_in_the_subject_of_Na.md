## Semantic attachments

- Semantic attachments refer to the meaning that words, phrases and sentences convey. They are the core of semantics.
- Some mnemonics and learning tricks for remembering semantic attachments:
    - Think of semantic attachments as the 'meaning magnets' that words and phrases attach to. The meaning of a word or phrase depends on what semantic attachment it has.
    - Associate colors or images with different semantic attachments like red for animacy, blue for agency, green for possession, etc. This can make them easier to remember.
- Types of semantic attachments:
    - Animacy: Alive (humans, animals) vs non-alive (objects, abstract concepts)
    - Agency: Capable of action (humans, animals) vs not capable of action (objects, natural forces)
    - Possession: Owning vs being owned
    - temporality: Past vs present vs future
    - Modality: Certain vs uncertain possibilities
    - Negation: Presence vs absence
- Examples of semantic attachments:
    - The boy (animacy: alive) chased the ball (animacy: non-alive)
    - The storm (agency: not capable) destroyed the house (agency: not capable)
    - Jack's (possession: owning) car (possession: being owned)
    - Yesterday (temporality: past), today (temporality: present), tomorrow (temporality: future)
    - It may (modality: uncertain possibility) rain (modality: uncertain possibility) today (temporality: present)
    - There are no (negation: absence) clouds (negation: absence) in the sky (negation: presence)
- Applications of understanding semantic attachments:
    - Machine translation: Translating based on semantic meanings rather than just word mappings
    - Question answering systems: Understanding the semantic roles of words and phrases in questions and passages to determine answers
    - Summarization: Identifying key semantic elements of a passage to create summaries
    - Other NLP tasks: Part-of-speech tagging, named entity recognition, sentiment analysis, etc. can utilize semantic attachments
