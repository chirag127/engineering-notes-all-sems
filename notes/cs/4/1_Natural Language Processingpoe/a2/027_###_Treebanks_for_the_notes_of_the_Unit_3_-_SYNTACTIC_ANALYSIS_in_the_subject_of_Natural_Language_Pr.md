### Treebanks for the notes of the Unit 3 - SYNTACTIC ANALYSIS in the subject of Natural Language Processing

A treebank is a parsed corpus where syntactic structures of sentences are annotated and stored. Treebanks are a crucial resource for training and evaluating syntactic parsers.

Some key points about treebanks:

- They contain syntactically parsed sentences where nodes are annotated with syntactic categories and syntactic relationships.
- They allow us to learn probabilistic models of syntax from data as they provide large amounts of parsed sentences.
- They are manually annotated which makes them highly accurate but also time-consuming and expensive to create.
- Popular treebanks include Penn Treebank (English), Negra (German), Tiger (Swedish), Oslo/Bergen (Norwegian), Prague Dependency Treebank (Czech), etc.

Advantages of using treebanks:

- They capture syntactic structures and dependencies in a hierarchical, easy to understand format (trees).
- They can be used to learn and evaluate syntactic parsers, especially data-driven probabilistic parsers.
- They provide insight into syntactic patterns and constructions of a language.

Disadvantages of using treebanks:

- They are expensive and time-consuming to create as they require manual annotation of syntactic structures.
- The annotation schemas and guidelines may be inconsistent or imperfect.
- The sentences in treebanks may not reflect the natural distribution of sentences. They tend to contain simpler sentences.

In conclusion, despite some disadvantages, treebanks are a crucial resource for training and evaluating syntactic parsers. They have enabled significant progress in parsing models and our understanding of syntax. With growing computing power and crowdsourcing techniques, creation of larger treebanks is becoming more feasible.
