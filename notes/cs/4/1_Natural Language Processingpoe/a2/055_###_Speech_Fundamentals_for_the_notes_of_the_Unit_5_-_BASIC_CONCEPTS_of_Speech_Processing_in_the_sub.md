### Speech Fundamentals for the notes of the Unit 5 - BASIC CONCEPTS of Speech Processing in the subject of Natural Language Processing

- Speech is produced by generating sound waves in the vocal tract. The vocal tract acts as an acoustic filter that shapes the sound wave into speech.
- The sound waves are generated by vibrating vocal folds in the larynx. The rate of vibration of vocal folds determines the pitch of the speech signal.
- The vocal tract can be modeled as a tube with varying cross-sectional area. The shape of the vocal tract is determined by the positions of lips, tongue, jaws, and other articulators.
- The shape of the vocal tract determines the acoustic filtering of the sound wave produced by the vocal folds. This results in certain frequencies being amplified more than others, which we perceive as speech sounds or phonemes.
- The flow of air through the vocal tract can be periodic or aperiodic. Periodic airflow results in voiced sounds like vowels and sonorants. Aperiodic airflow results in unvoiced consonant sounds.
- The positions of articulators change with time, resulting in dynamic filtering of the sound wave and producing the continuous speech signal. The rate at which articulator positions change determines the speech rate or speaking rate.

Some mnemonics for remembering the speech production process:

- Vocal folds vibrate, vocal tract filters: To produce speech, vocal folds first vibrate to generate sound wave which is then filtered by the vocal tract.
- Voiced sounds are periodic, unvoiced sounds are aperiodic: Voiced sounds like vowels have periodic airflow through the vocal tract, while unvoiced consonants have aperiodic airflow.

The speech production process can be visualized using diagrams showing the vocal tract and positions of articulators. Detailed mathematical models of speech production are also available using source-filter models.

Speech processing has various applications like automatic speech recognition, speech synthesis, speaker identification, speech coding, etc. However, it is a challenging task due to the complex, dynamic and highly variable nature of speech.
