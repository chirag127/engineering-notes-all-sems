## Production And Classification Of Speech Sounds

### Production of Speech Sounds

- Speech sounds are produced by the passage of air through the vocal tract. The vocal tract acts as an acoustic tube with variable shape and length.
- The movements of lips, tongue, jaw, and other articulators modify the vocal tract shape. This results in different speech sounds.
- The flow of air through the vocal tract produces sound waves that we perceive as speech.
- The parameters used to classify speech sounds are:
	- Voicing: Presence or absence of vocal cord vibration. Voiced and unvoiced sounds.
	- Place of articulation: How the airstream is obstructed. Bilabial, labiodental, etc.
	- Manner of articulation: How the speech organs interact with the airstream. Plosive, fricative, etc.

### Classification of Speech Sounds

- Speech sounds can be broadly classified into vowels and consonants.
- Vowels are produced with a relatively open vocal tract. Consonants are produced with a constriction or closure at one or more points in the vocal tract.
- Consonants can be further classified based on voicing, place of articulation and manner of articulation.
- For example, /p/, /t/, /k/ are unvoiced, bilabial, plosive consonants. /z/, /v/, /??/ are voiced, fricative consonants.
- A standard set of symbols called the International Phonetic Alphabet (IPA) is used to represent different speech sounds.

**Mnemonics:**

- For place of articulation: Please Do Not Kill Sam - PDNKS (Bilabial, Dental, Alveolar, Palatal, Velar)
- For manner of articulation: Please Do Come Over For Pizza Tonight - PDC(O)FT (Plosive, Fricative, Affricate, Nasal, Liquid)

**Advantages:** Classification of speech sounds helps understand their characteristics and how they are produced. This is useful in speech synthesis and recognition applications.
