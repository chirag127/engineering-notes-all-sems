### Selectional Restrictions for the notes of the Unit 4 - Semantics and Pragmatics in the subject of Natural Language Processing

Selectional restrictions refer to constraints on the types of entities that can fill particular semantic roles in predicates. For example, the subject of 'eat' is typically constrained to animate entities (e.g. 'John ate the pizza'), and the object is constrained to food entities (e.g. 'John ate the pizza').

Mnemonics/Learning tricks:
- Think of selectional restrictions as the 'picky eaters' of semantics - they restrict what can 'eat' or fill a semantic argument.
- The ' Animacy hierarchy' is a useful concept - animate > human > male/female for subjects, and food/drink for objects of 'eat'.

Advantages:
- Help prune the unrealistic interpretations of sentences and reduce ambiguity.
- Aid inference and question answering by expecting certain types of entities in semantic roles.

Disadvantages:
- Can be too strict - some verbs are flexible (e.g. 'drink' can take non-drink entities as objects in metaphors like 'drink in the scenery').
- Do not always correspond to real-world probabilities and can reflect biases in data.

Examples:
- 'The cat chased the dog' (animate subject, animate object)
- 'The girl ate the pizza' (animate subject, food object)

Applications:
- Core part of semantic role labelling and other semantic parsing tasks which aim to identify semantic arguments and their roles.
- Help improve machine translation and text simplification by flagging unlikely semantic interpretations.
