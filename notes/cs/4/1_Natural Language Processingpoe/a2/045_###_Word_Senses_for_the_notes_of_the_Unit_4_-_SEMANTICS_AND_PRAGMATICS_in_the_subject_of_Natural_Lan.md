### Word Senses for the notes of the Unit 4 - SEMANTICS AND PRAGMATICS in the subject of Natural Language Processing

- The meaning of a word can vary based on the context in which it is used. These different meanings are known as **word senses**. Identifying the correct sense of a word in context is called **word sense disambiguation**.
- For example, the word *bank* can refer to a financial institution or the land alongside a river. The correct sense can be determined based on the context.
- Some learning tricks for remembering word senses:
    - Think of the different contexts the word can be used in and what it would mean in those contexts. For example, think of a bank transaction vs going to the bank of a river.
    - Look for words around the target word that can indicate its sense. For example, words like *money*, *accounts*, *transactions* would indicate the financial institution sense of *bank* rather than the river bank sense.
    - Group together words that have similar senses. This can make them easier to remember. For example, *bank* (financial), *loan*, *interest*, *credit*, etc. have related senses.
- However, mnemonics and tricks are not always easy to formulate or remember for all words and their senses. It is important to gather the sense of a word from context rather than relying only on memory techniques.
- Detailed diagrams or examples can be helpful in some cases to visually depict the different senses or contexts of a word. But including them is not necessary if the points can convey the necessary understanding.
- Applications of word sense disambiguation include improving accuracy of machine translation, sentiment analysis, speech recognition, etc. However, it is a challenging task and current systems do not achieve high accuracy. More advanced NLP and deep learning techniques are being explored to better model word senses.
