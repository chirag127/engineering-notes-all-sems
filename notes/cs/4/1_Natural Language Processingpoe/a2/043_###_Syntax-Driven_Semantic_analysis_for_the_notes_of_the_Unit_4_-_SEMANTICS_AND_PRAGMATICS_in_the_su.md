### Syntax-Driven Semantic analysis for the notes of the Unit 4 - SEMANTICS AND PRAGMATICS in the subject of Natural Language Processing

- Syntax-driven semantic analysis uses the syntactic structure of a sentence to infer its meaning. The syntactic structure is analyzed to identify the semantic roles of constituents and the semantic relations between them.
- For example, in the sentence "John gave Mary a book", the syntactic structure indicates that "John" is the subject, "gave" is the verb, and "Mary" and "a book" are objects. The semantic roles can then be identified as Agent for the subject, Action for the verb, and Theme/Recipient for the objects.
- The syntactic structure constrains the possible semantic relationships and roles. This approach is efficient but limited by the fact that syntactic structure alone is often insufficient to fully determine semantic relationships. Additional world knowledge and disambiguation is required.
- Mnemonics: "Syntax shows semantics the way" - meaning syntax guides the interpretation of semantics.
- Advantages: Efficient and syntax-guided. Disadvantages: Incomplete - world knowledge needed. Limited by syntactic analysis accuracy.
- Examples: Sentence diagramming, syntactic constituent identification. Applications: Machine translation, question answering.
