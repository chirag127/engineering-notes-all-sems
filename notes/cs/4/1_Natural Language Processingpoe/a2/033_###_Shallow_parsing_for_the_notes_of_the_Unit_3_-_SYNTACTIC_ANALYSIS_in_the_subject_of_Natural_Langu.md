### Shallow parsing for the notes of the Unit 3 - SYNTACTIC ANALYSIS in the subject of Natural Language Processing

- Shallow parsing is also known as lexical analysis. It breaks down the input into word tokens. Each word is assigned a part-of-speech tag like noun, verb, adjective, etc.
- It does not require any understanding of the sentence structure or semantics. It just groups the words into phrases and extracts named entities.
- Mnemonic: Think of shallow parsing as just skimming through the surface of the text to get word tokens and their categories. No deep diving into phrases or semantics.
- Advantages: Fast, simple, can be used as a pre-processing step for deep parsing.
- Disadvantages: Does not provide full syntactic analysis. Cannot handle semantic ambiguities.
- Examples: Part-of-speech tagging, Named entity recognition.
- Applications: Information retrieval, machine translation, speech recognition, etc.

Below are some tips to learn shallow parsing:

- Understand the goal is just to get words and their categories, nothing more.
- Know the common parts-of-speech tags and what they represent. Practice assigning tags to words.
- Go through named entity recognition techniques and resources like gazetteers and context patterns.
- Implement a basic shallow parser and test it on some sentences to get a hands-on feel.
- Compare shallow parsing with deep parsing to understand the key differences.
