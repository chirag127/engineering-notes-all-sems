### Ambiguity for the notes of the Unit 3 - SYNTACTIC ANALYSIS in the subject of Natural Language Processing

1. Ambiguity: Ambiguity refers to multiple possible interpretations of a word, phrase, or sentence. Ambiguity can occur at various levels in natural language processing - lexical, syntactic, and semantic.
- Lexical ambiguity: A word having multiple meanings (e.g. 'bank' can refer to a financial institution or the land alongside a river)
- Syntactic ambiguity: A sentence having multiple possible syntactic structures (e.g. 'Visiting relatives can be boring' can have two meanings depending on which word is the verb)
- Semantic ambiguity: A sentence having multiple meanings (e.g. 'I saw the man with a telescope' can mean two different things depending on which noun 'telescope' modifies)

2. Types of syntactic ambiguity:
- Structural ambiguity: When a sentence has multiple possible parse trees (e.g. 'The horse raced past the barn fell')
- Attachment ambiguity: When a phrase can be attached to more than one other phrase (e.g. 'The girl hit by the bus was taken to the hospital' - does 'by the bus' modify 'The girl' or 'was taken to the hospital')
- Reference ambiguity: When a pronoun can refer to more than one noun (e.g. 'John agreed with Steve because he was also angry')

3. Resolving ambiguity: Ambiguity can be resolved using context, world knowledge, and discourse. Some NLP models use multiple parses and choose the most likely one based on probabilities. The ultimate goal is to produce the interpretation that a human would most likely infer.

4. Applications: Understanding and resolving ambiguity is crucial for various NLP applications like machine translation, question answering, summarization, etc. so that the system can produce the most appropriate output.
