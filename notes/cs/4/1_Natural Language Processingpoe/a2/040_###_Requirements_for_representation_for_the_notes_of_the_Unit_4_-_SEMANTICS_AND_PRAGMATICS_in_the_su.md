### Requirements for representation for the notes of the Unit 4 - SEMANTICS AND PRAGMATICS in the subject of Natural Language Processing

1. Representation of meaning:
- Semantic networks: Graphical models consisting of nodes representing concepts and edges representing relationships between concepts. Used to represent knowledge and infer new relationships.
- Frame semantics: Representing meaning through the notion of frames which are structures for conceptualizing situations. Frames consist of slots which are filled with values.
- Conceptual metaphors: Understanding one concept in terms of another, e.g. "Love is a journey". Can be used to represent abstract concepts.

2. Ambiguity:
- Lexical ambiguity: A word having multiple meanings (e.g. "bank" - river bank vs. financial bank). Context is required to determine the correct meaning.
- Syntactic ambiguity: A sentence having multiple possible interpretations due to different parse trees (e.g. "Visiting relatives can be boring"). Context and world knowledge required for disambiguation.

3. Vagueness: Fuzzy meanings where it is difficult to delineate precise boundaries (e.g. "tall", "expensive"). Handled through logical approaches or probability distributions in NLP.

4. Implicature: Meaning implied through conversational inferences as proposed by the philosopher Paul Grice, e.g. quantity, quality, relevance, manner. Important for pragmatics and interpreting utterance meaning.

5. Presupposition: Background assumptions required for an utterance to make sense (e.g. "Have you stopped beating your wife?" presupposes that you were beating your wife previously). Need to be properly handled to avoid logical errors in NLP systems.
