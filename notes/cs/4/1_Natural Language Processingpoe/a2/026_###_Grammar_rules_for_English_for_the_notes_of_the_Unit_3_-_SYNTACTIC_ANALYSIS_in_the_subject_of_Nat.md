### Grammar rules for English for the notes of the Unit 3 - SYNTACTIC ANALYSIS in the subject of Natural Language Processing

1. Subject-Verb Agreement: The verb must agree with the subject in number (singular/plural). For example: "The boy walks to school" (singular subject, singular verb), "The boys walk to school" (plural subject, plural verb).

2. Noun-Pronoun Agreement: Pronouns must agree with the nouns they refer to in number, gender, and person. For example: "Each student must bring their book" is incorrect because "student" is singular but "their" is plural. It should be "Each student must bring his/her book".

3. Misplaced Modifiers: Modifiers should be placed next to the words they modify to avoid ambiguity. For example: "While running down the street, a tree fell and hit me" is incorrect because it implies that the tree was running down the street. It should be: "While I was running down the street, a tree fell and hit me".

4. Dangling Modifiers: Modifiers should clearly modify specific words in the sentence, and should not "dangle". For example: "After finishing my work, the TV was turned on" is incorrect because "the TV" was not doing the finishing. It should be: "After finishing my work, I turned on the TV".

5. Comma Splices: Two independent clauses cannot be joined by just a comma. Use a coordinating conjunction (e.g. "and", "but") or separate into two sentences. For example: "I like reading books, I find it very relaxing" is incorrect. It should be: "I like reading books, and I find it very relaxing" or "I like reading books. I find it very relaxing".
