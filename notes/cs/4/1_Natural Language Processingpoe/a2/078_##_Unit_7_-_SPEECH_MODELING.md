## Unit 7 - SPEECH MODELING

Speech Modeling is the process of representing speech signals in a mathematical form. It helps in understanding and synthesizing human speech. Some of the commonly used Speech Models are:

1. Source-Filter Model: It represents speech as the output of a sound source (vocal folds) which is filtered by the vocal tract. The vocal tract is modeled as an acoustic tube with varying cross-sectional areas. This model helps in speech synthesis and understanding formant frequencies.
2. Articulatory Phonetic Model: It represents speech production in terms of positions and movements of speech articulators like lips, tongue, jaw, etc. This model is useful for speech recognition and synthesis based on articulatory movements.
3. Markov Model: It represents speech as a sequence of discrete states which follow a Markov process. The transitions between states are determined by transition probabilities. Hidden Markov Models are widely used in speech recognition systems. They model the statistical patterns of speech and help in determining the most likely sequence of words/phonemes.
4. Linear Prediction Coding (LPC) Model: It models speech as the output of an all-pole infinite impulse response digital filter. The filter coefficients are estimated using linear predictive analysis which gives important speech parameters like formants and pitch. LPC is widely used for low-bit-rate speech coding applications.

Some useful mnemonics and learning tricks for Speech Modeling:

- Think of speech as the sound from a 'whistle' (source) which is 'molded' by the vocal tract (filter)
- Articulators are like 'players' whose 'movements' produce speech
- Markov Models determine the 'next state' based on 'current state' and 'transition probabilities'
- LPC models speech as the output of an 'predictive' filter giving 'parameters' like formants
