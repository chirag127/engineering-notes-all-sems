## Unit 4 - SEMANTICS AND PRAGMATICS

Semantics refers to the meaning of words, phrases, and sentences. Pragmatics refers to how meaning is conveyed through speech acts and contextual information.

Some key points about semantics and pragmatics:

- Semantics deals with the literal meaning of words and sentences, independent of context. Pragmatics deals with how context influences meaning.
- Semantics is concerned with truth conditions and denotation. Pragmatics deals with how we interpret utterances in context, including implicatures and indirect speech acts.
- Semantics focuses on compositionality and how words are combined to form phrases and sentences. Pragmatics focuses on how we derive meaning not just from sentences but from the context in which they are uttered.
- Semantic meaning can often be incomplete without pragmatic enrichment. We rely on context and inferences to fully understand meaning.
- Ambiguity can arise on both semantic and pragmatic levels. Semantic ambiguity stems from having multiple literal meanings, while pragmatic ambiguity comes from having multiple implied meanings.

Some examples to understand the distinction:

- "I'm reading a book." The semantic meaning refers to the literal act of reading a book. The pragmatic meaning would depend on context, e.g. whether the speaker is implying they are busy or unavailable.
- "It's cold in here." The semantic meaning refers to the temperature. The pragmatic meaning could be a request to close the window or adjust the thermostat.
- Ironic statements like "Yeah, great job." The semantic meaning is positive but the pragmatic meaning is negative, which is derived from contextual cues.
