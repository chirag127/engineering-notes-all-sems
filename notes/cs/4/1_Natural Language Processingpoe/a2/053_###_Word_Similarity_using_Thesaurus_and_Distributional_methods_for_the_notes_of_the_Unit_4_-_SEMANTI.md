### Word Similarity using Thesaurus and Distributional methods

Thesaurus based methods:
- Uses a thesaurus to find synonyms and measures similarity based on shared synonyms.
- Ex: Two words are similar if they have many synonyms in common.
- Advantage: Simple and intuitive.
- Disadvantage: Depend on the coverage and quality of the thesaurus. Limited by the knowledge encoded in the thesaurus.

Distributional methods:
- Words that occur in similar contexts tend to have similar meanings.
- Ex: Two words are similar if they appear near the same words in text corpora.
- Compute co-occurrence statistics from corpus to capture semantic similarity.
- Advantage: Directly data-driven, can capture contextual nuances.
- Disadvantage: Require large amounts of data, high dimensionality and sparsity.

Possible ways to combine thesaurus and distributional methods to overcome individual disadvantages and achieve better performance.

Applications: Word sense disambiguation, information retrieval, machine translation, etc.

Mnemonics:
- Shared synonyms -> Thesaurus based similarity
- Shared contexts -> Distributional similarity
