### Feature Extraction And Pattern Comparison Techniques

- Feature extraction is a key step in speech analysis. It involves extracting distinct properties (features) of the speech signal that are useful for further analysis or processing.
- Some common feature extraction techniques for speech analysis are:
- Mel-Frequency Cepstral Coefficients (MFCC): Captures the short-term power spectrum of a sound, emphasizing perceptually relevant frequencies. Useful for speech recognition.
- Linear Predictive Coding (LPC): Models the vocal tract as an all-pole filter to get coefficients representing the filter. Useful for speech synthesis and recognition.
- Pitch: The perceived fundamental frequency of a sound. Can be used to extract features like pitch contours to analyze emotion in speech.
- Spectral features: Features derived from the frequency spectrum of the signal like formants, bandwidths, etc. Can be used for speaker recognition or speech recognition.
- Pattern comparison techniques are then used to analyze and classify speech samples based on the extracted features. Some common techniques are:
- Dynamic Time Warping (DTW): Aligns two sequences (like speech samples) by warping the time dimension to find the optimal match. Useful for speech recognition or speaker verification.
- Hidden Markov Models (HMMs): Models speech as a Markov process with hidden states to classify or recognize speech samples.
- Neural networks: Can be used to learn complex patterns in speech features and classify/recognize speech. Often used with deep learning techniques.
