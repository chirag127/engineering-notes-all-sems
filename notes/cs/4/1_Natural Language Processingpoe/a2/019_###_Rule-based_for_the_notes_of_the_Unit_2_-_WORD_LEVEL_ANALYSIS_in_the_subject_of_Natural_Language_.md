### Rule-based for the notes of the Unit 2 - WORD LEVEL ANALYSIS in the subject of Natural Language Processing

* Rule-based methods use handcrafted rules to analyze words. Some of the popular rule-based methods are:
* Stemming: It is a process of reducing related words to their word stem, base or root form. For example: 'fishing', 'fished', 'fisher' can be stemmed to 'fish'.
* Advantages: It is simple and fast.
* Disadvantages: It can produce erroneous results like 'king' and 'kings' can be stemmed to 'king'.
* Examples: Porter Stemmer, Lovins Stemmer, Paice/Husk Stemmer, etc.
* Lemmatization: It is a process of grouping together the inflected forms of a word so that they can be analyzed as a single item. For example: 'am', 'is', 'are' can be lemmatized to 'be'.
* Advantages: It produces better results than stemming.
* Disadvantages: It requires detailed dictionaries and is computationally expensive.
* Stop word removal: It is a process of filtering out common words like 'the', 'a', 'an', etc. that do not contain much meaning.
* Advantages: It reduces the size of the vocabulary and decreases processing time.
* Disadvantages: It may remove words that are relevant to the context.

Some mnemonics and learning tricks for rule-based methods:

* Stemming can be remembered as 'chopping off' the ends of the words.
* Lemmatization can be remembered as 'grouping related forms' of a word.
* Stop words can be remembered as 'common words' that are 'stopped' or 'removed'.
