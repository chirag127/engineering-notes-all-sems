### First-Order Logic

- First-order logic (FOL) is a formal logical system used in mathematics, philosophy, linguistics, and computer science. It goes by many names, including: first-order predicate calculus, the lower predicate calculus, quantified predicate logic.
- FOL describes first-order predicate logic, which is distinguished from propositional logic in that it permits quantifiers, variables and predicate symbols. It allows to express facts about objects and to reason about objects and their properties.
- In FOL, variables represent objects in a domain of discourse. Predicate symbols represent properties and relations, and are used together with variables to form atomic formulae (or atoms), expressions that are either true or false.
- Quantifiers allow to express how many objects in the domain of discourse have a property expressed by a predicate symbol. Universal quantification states that all objects have a property, existential quantification states that some object has a property.
- Examples of statements in FOL:
› For all x, if x is a person, then x is mortal. (universal quantification)
› There exists x such that x is a father of y. (existential quantification)
› Brother(x, y) (atomic formula with predicate symbol)

- Advantages: Precise and unambiguous. Widely used in AI, linguistics and automated reasoning. Basis for query languages and knowledge representation formalisms.
- Disadvantages: Limited expressiveness. Cannot directly represent statements about provability or infinity. Needs additional axioms and rules to be usable.
- Applications: Automated theorem proving, semantic analysis of natural language, knowledge representation and reasoning.

- Mnemonics:
› predIcate calcULus QUantifies varaibles (I, U, Q, V)
› For aLl, theRE Exists (universal, existential quantification)
