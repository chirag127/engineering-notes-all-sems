### Articulatory Phonetics for the notes of the Unit 5 - BASIC CONCEPTS of Speech Processing in the subject of Natural Language Processing

Articulatory phonetics is the study of how the vocal organs are used to produce speech sounds. The main vocal organs used to produce speech sounds are:

- Lungs: Provide air stream required to produce speech sounds
- Larynx: Contains vocal cords which produce voice
- Pharynx: Acts as a resonance chamber for speech sounds
- Tongue: Manipulates shape to produce different speech sounds
- Lips: Manipulate shape and tension to produce speech sounds
- Nasal cavity: Acts as a resonance chamber for nasal sounds

The positions and movements of these speech organs in the production of speech sounds can be depicted using vocal tract diagrams. Some key terms used in articulatory phonetics are:

- Voiced: Sounds produced with vibrating vocal cords
- Unvoiced: Sounds produced without vibrating vocal cords
- Nasal: Sounds produced with lowered velum allowing air to flow through the nose
- Plosive: Sounds produced by complete blockage followed by release of air
- Fricative: Sounds produced by constriction causing turbulence
- Affricate: Sounds beginning like plosives but ending like fricatives
- Approximant: Sounds produced with little obstruction to air flow
- Vowel: Sounds produced with little to no constriction in vocal tract
- Consonant: Sounds produced with constriction or blockage in vocal tract

Some useful mnemonics and learning tricks for articulatory phonetics:

- For classification of consonants: 'Please Pay For Coke' representing Plosive, Fricative, Affricate, Approximant, Nasal
- For places of articulation: 'Have To Touch Lips Constantly' representing Bilabial, Labiodental, Dental, Alveolar, Palatal, Velar
- For manners of articulation: 'Block Fric Sizzle Hiss Buzz' representing Plosive, Fricative, Sibilant, Hiss, Approximant

The details and diagrams would help in understanding the articulation of different speech sounds which is key to understanding accents and speech disorders. The classification of speech sounds is crucial for automatic speech recognition and speech synthesis systems.
