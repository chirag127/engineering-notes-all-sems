### Grammar-based LM for the notes of the Unit 1 - INTRODUCTION in the subject of Natural Language Processing

Grammar-based language models use the syntactic structure of sentences to predict the next word. They use grammar rules to compute the probability of sequences of words.

Advantages:

- Captures structural dependencies between words.
- Handles unseen words and phrases well.

Disadvantages:

- Requires development of complex grammar rules.
- Rules can be difficult to develop for real-world languages which have many exceptions.

Examples:

- Chomsky grammars (context-free grammars)
- Probabilistic context-free grammars (PCFGs)

Applications:

- Speech recognition systems
- Machine translation systems

Mnemonics:

- Think of grammar as a set of rules (like in English grammar) to predict next words.
- Rules can handle new phrases but can be hard to create due to language complexity.

Learning tricks:

- Understand how grammar rules work for your native language. Then think of extending them to handle probabilities and unseen cases.
- Implement a simple grammar-based model to understand the concepts in depth.
