### English Morphology for the notes of the Unit 1 - INTRODUCTION in the subject of Natural Language Processing

- Morphology is the study of word formation and structure. In English, words are formed by stringing together roots and affixes.
- Roots are bases to which prefixes and suffixes are attached. They are usually free morphemes that can occur on their own. For example, 'break', 'read', 'act', etc.
- Affixes are bound morphemes that must be attached to roots or stems to form words. They are of two types:
-- Prefixes: Attach before the root. For example, 'un-', 'pre-', 'mis-', etc.
-- Suffixes: Attach after the root. For example, '-s', '-es', '-ed', '-ing', '-er', '-est', '-ly', '-ful', '-less', etc.

Mnemonics for remembering common prefixes and suffixes -

Prefixes:
UN: opposite - unhappy, unlock
PRE: before - preview, predate
MIS: wrong - misjudge, mislead

Suffixes:
-S: plural - books, dogs
-ED: past tense - walked, talked
-ING: present participle - walking, talking
-ER: comparative - taller, faster
-EST: superlative - tallest, fastest
-LY: adverb - quickly, loudly
-FUL: full of - helpful, forgetful
-LESS: without - careless, fearless

Advantages of morphology - Useful for spelling correction, root word extraction, language modeling, etc.
Disadvantages - Complex morphology leads to data sparseness problem. Ambiguity in analysis.

Examples of morphological analysis -

Input: workers
Analysis: root: work; suffix: -ers (plural)

Input: talked
Analysis: root: talk; suffix: -ed (past tense)

Input: happier
Analysis: root: happy; suffix: -er (comparative)
