### Acoustic Phonetics for the notes of the Unit 5 - BASIC CONCEPTS of Speech Processing in the subject of Natural Language Processing

Acoustic phonetics is the study of speech sounds based on acoustic properties of the sound waves produced. It focuses on the physical properties of speech sounds or phones.

Some key concepts in acoustic phonetics are:

- Frequency: The frequency of a sound wave determines the pitch of the sound. Vowels are produced with a relatively steady frequency while consonants may have transient changes in frequency.
- Amplitude: The amplitude or loudness of a sound wave corresponds to the intensity or volume of the sound. Variations in amplitude are used to produce emphasis and prosodic features like stress and intonation.
- Spectral characteristics: The spectral content or distribution of energy at different frequencies is an important characteristic used to distinguish speech sounds. For example, consonants are classified based on whether they have concentrated spectral energy (periodic) or dispersed spectral energy (aperiodic or noise-like).
- Time characteristics: The duration and time-changing properties of a sound are used to distinguish speech sounds and recognize syllables and words. For example, stop consonants are characterized by temporal gaps or closures in the speech signal.

Some mnemonics and learning tricks for acoustic phonetics:

- Think of frequency as pitch - high frequency sounds are high pitch and low frequency sounds are low pitch
- Remember amplitude affects loudness - high amplitude is loud and low amplitude is soft
- Spectral characteristics relate to timbre - periodic sounds have definite pitch while aperiodic sounds are noise-like
- Time characteristics include onsets, closures, and durations of speech sounds
