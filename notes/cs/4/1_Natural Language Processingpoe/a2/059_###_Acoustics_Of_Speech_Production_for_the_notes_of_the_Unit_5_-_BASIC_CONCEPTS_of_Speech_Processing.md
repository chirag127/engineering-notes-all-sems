### Acoustics Of Speech Production

The production of speech sounds can be thought of as a source-filter process:

- Source: The vocal folds (vocal cords) in the larynx act as a source of quasi- periodic excitation (vibration). The rate of vibration determines the pitch of the voice.
- Filter: The vocal tract above the larynx acts as a time-varying acoustic filter. The shape of the vocal tract determines the formant frequencies of the speech signal, which characterize the individual speech sounds (phonemes).

The key stages in speech production are:

1. Breath released from the lungs passes through the trachea and sets the vocal folds in the larynx vibrating.

2. The rate of vocal fold vibration determines the pitch (F0) of the voice. A higher rate gives a higher pitch.

3. The vibrating air from the larynx then passes through the vocal tract, which acts as an acoustic filter. The shape of the vocal tract is controlled by the positions of the lips, tongue, jaw, and other articulators.

4. The formant frequencies of the speech signal are determined by the shape of the vocal tract. Different configurations of the articulators give different formant frequencies, which characterize the different speech sounds.

**Mnemonics:**

- Think of the larynx and vocal folds as the *source* of speech, like a sound source.
- Think of the vocal tract as an *acoustic filter* that filters the sound from the source.
- The *rate of vocal fold vibration* determines *pitch*, a higher rate means a higher pitch.
- The *shape of the vocal tract* determines the *formant frequencies* which characterize different speech sounds.

**Advantages:** Producing a wide range of sounds using a time-varying acoustic filter allows for the articulation of vowel and consonant sounds. The source-filter model provides a useful framework for understanding the basic mechanisms of speech production.

**Disadvantages:** The source-filter model is a simplified view of speech production. It does not account for all aspects of how speech sounds are produced in practice. The positions and movements of the articulators are highly complex and coordinated.
