### Relations between Senses for the notes of the Unit 4 - SEMANTICS AND PRAGMATICS in the subject of Natural Language Processing

The senses or meanings of words are related to each other in the following ways:

1. Synonymy: Words that have the same or similar meanings are synonyms e.g. big and large, said and tell, car and automobile. Synonyms are useful as they allow speakers to avoid repetition and make their language more interesting.
2. Antonymy: Words that have opposite meanings are antonyms e.g. good and bad, up and down, in and out. Antonyms are useful for conveying contrast and creating clarity.
3. Hyponymy: A hyponym is a word whose meaning is included within that of another word. For example, the words tree, oak and pine are hyponyms of the hypernym plant. The hypernym is a more general word and the hyponyms are more specific words that fall under the hypernym in a hierarchical classification. Hyponymy relations are useful for creating classifications and hierarchies.
4. Meronymy: A meronym is a word that refers to a part of the whole denoted by another word. For example, wheel is a meronym of car, and leg is a meronym of table. Meronymy relations are useful for identifying parts and wholes.

*Some mnemonics and learning tricks for remembering the relations:

Synonymy: Use a thesaurus to find synonyms - think of synonyms as "same/similar meaning" words.
Antonymy: Think of antonyms as "opposite meaning" words - the prefixes "anti", "contra" and "counter" indicate antonymy.
Hyponymy: Think of hypernyms as superordinate categories and hyponyms as subordinate categories in a hierarchical tree structure. The mnemonic "the tree hierarchy" can help remember hypernymy.
Meronymy: Think of meronyms as "part-whole" relations - the mnemonic "the whole is greater than the part" can help remember meronymy.

The relations between senses are important as they allow us to understand the meanings of words in context, avoid repetition, create clarity, classifications and hierarchies, and identify parts and wholes. Memorizing the mnemonics and applying the learning tricks can help in remembering the semantic relations. However, they should only be used if they are easy to remember and do not add to the cognitive load.
