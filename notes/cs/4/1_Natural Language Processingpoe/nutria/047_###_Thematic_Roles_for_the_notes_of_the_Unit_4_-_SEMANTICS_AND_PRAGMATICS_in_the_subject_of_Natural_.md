Thematic roles are a type of syntactic structure that helps to identify the relationship between a verb and its arguments. They are used in natural language processing (NLP) to identify the semantic relationships between words and phrases in a sentence.

Thematic roles can be divided into two categories: syntactic and semantic. Syntactic roles refer to the position of a word or phrase in a sentence, such as subject, object, or indirect object. Semantic roles refer to the meaning of the word or phrase, such as agent, patient, recipient, theme, or instrument.

Thematic roles are important for understanding the meaning of a sentence. For example, in the sentence "John gave Mary a book," the agent (John) is the person performing the action, the patient (Mary) is the person receiving the action, and the theme (book) is the object being given.

In NLP, thematic roles are used to identify the semantic relationships between words and phrases in a sentence. This can be used to determine the subject, object, and other parts of speech in a sentence. This can also help to identify the sentiment of a sentence, as well as the sentiment of a conversation.
