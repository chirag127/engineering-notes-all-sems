
### Language Modeling for the notes of the Unit 1 - INTRODUCTION in the subject of Natural Language Processing

Language modeling is the task of predicting the probability of a sequence of words in a text. It is an important part of natural language processing (NLP) and is used in many applications such as machine translation, speech recognition, and text generation.

Language models are based on the assumption that given a sequence of words, the next word in the sequence is more likely to be one that has been seen before in similar contexts. For example, if the previous words in a sentence are “the cat”, then the next word is more likely to be “sat” than “ate”.

Language models can be built using different techniques such as n-grams, neural networks, and recurrent neural networks (RNNs). N-grams are the most basic technique and are based on the assumption that the probability of a word in a sequence depends on the previous n words. Neural networks are more powerful and can learn more complex relationships between words. RNNs are the most powerful technique and can take into account the context of words in a longer sequence.

In addition to predicting the probability of words, language models can also be used to generate text. This is done by sampling from the language model, i.e. randomly selecting words based on their probability. This can be used to generate realistic-looking text that is based on real data.

Language models are used in many applications such as machine translation, speech recognition, text summarization, and text generation. They are also used to improve the accuracy of search engines, as well as to detect spam and other malicious content.
