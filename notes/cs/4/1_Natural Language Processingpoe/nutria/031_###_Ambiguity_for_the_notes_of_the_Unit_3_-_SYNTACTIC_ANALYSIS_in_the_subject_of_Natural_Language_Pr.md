Ambiguity is a common problem encountered in natural language processing. It occurs when a natural language expression has more than one possible meaning. This can lead to confusion and errors in the interpretation of the expression.

In syntactic analysis, ambiguity can arise when the same word or phrase can have different syntactic structures. For example, the sentence "John saw the man with the telescope" could be interpreted in two different ways:

1. John saw a man who was holding a telescope.
2. John saw a man through the telescope.

To resolve this ambiguity, syntactic analysis must determine which interpretation is correct. This is done by looking at the context of the sentence, as well as the syntactic structure of the sentence.

Another type of ambiguity that can arise in syntactic analysis is homonymy. This occurs when two different words have the same spelling or pronunciation, but different meanings. For example, the word "bank" can mean either a financial institution or the edge of a river.

To resolve homonymy, syntactic analysis must determine which meaning of the word is intended. This is done by looking at the context of the sentence, as well as the syntactic structure of the sentence.

In addition to ambiguity, syntactic analysis must also identify any errors in the sentence. This can include incorrect word order, incorrect verb forms, incorrect noun forms, and incorrect punctuation. By identifying errors, the syntactic analysis can help to ensure that the sentence is interpreted correctly.
