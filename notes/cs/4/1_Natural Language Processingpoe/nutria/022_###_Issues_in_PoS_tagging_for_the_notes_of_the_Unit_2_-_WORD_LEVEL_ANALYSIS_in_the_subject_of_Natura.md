
### Issues in PoS tagging for the notes of the Unit 2 - WORD LEVEL ANALYSIS in the subject of Natural Language Processing

1. One of the major issues in Part of Speech (POS) tagging is the ambiguity of words. For example, the word "bank" can have different meanings depending on the context. In one context, it could refer to the side of a river, while in another context it could refer to a financial institution. This makes it difficult for algorithms to accurately assign the correct POS tag to a word.

2. Another issue is the complexity of language. Natural language is full of irregularities, exceptions, and nuances that make it difficult for algorithms to accurately assign POS tags. For example, the words "read" and "read" have the same pronunciation, but they have different meanings and thus require different POS tags.

3. Additionally, POS tagging algorithms often struggle with unknown words or words that are not in the training data set. This can lead to incorrect POS tags being assigned to these words.

4. Finally, POS tagging algorithms can be computationally expensive, as they require a large amount of data to be processed in order to accurately assign POS tags. This can be a problem for applications that need to process large amounts of data quickly.

To help with the issues of POS tagging, there are a few mnemonic devices that can be used. For example, for the words “read” and “read”, one can remember that the former is a verb and the latter is a noun by using the mnemonic “Verbs end in ‘d’”. Additionally, for unknown words, one can use context clues to determine the POS tag. For example, if the sentence is “The bank was robbed”, one can infer that “bank” is referring to a financial institution, rather than the side of a river.
