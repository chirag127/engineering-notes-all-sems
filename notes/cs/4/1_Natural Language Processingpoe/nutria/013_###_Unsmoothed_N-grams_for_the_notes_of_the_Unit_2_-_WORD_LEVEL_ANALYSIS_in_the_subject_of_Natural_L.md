
### Unsmoothed N-grams for the notes of the Unit 2 - WORD LEVEL ANALYSIS in the subject of Natural Language Processing

- Unsmoothed N-grams are a type of language model used in Natural Language Processing (NLP). 
- They are used to predict the next word in a sentence based on the previous words. 
- Unsmoothed N-grams use the exact counts of the words that appear in a given sentence. 
- The N-grams are calculated by counting the number of times each combination of words appears in a given corpus. 
- For example, if the corpus contains the sentence "the cat sat on the mat", then the N-grams for this sentence would be: 
  - Unigram: "the", "cat", "sat", "on", "mat"
  - Bigram: "the cat", "cat sat", "sat on", "on the", "the mat"
  - Trigram: "the cat sat", "cat sat on", "sat on the", "on the mat"
- Unsmoothed N-grams are used in many NLP applications, such as speech recognition, machine translation, and text summarization. 
- They are also used in sentiment analysis and topic modeling. 
- Unsmoothed N-grams can be used to identify the most likely next word in a sentence, given the preceding words. 
- They can also be used to identify the most likely topic or sentiment in a given text. 
- Advantages of using Unsmoothed N-grams include the ability to capture long-term dependencies, the ability to capture rare words, and the ability to capture the context of the words. 
- Disadvantages of Unsmoothed N-grams include the fact that they can be computationally expensive and that they can be prone to overfitting. 
- Unsmoothed N-grams can be used in combination with other language models, such as smoothed N-grams, to improve the accuracy of predictions.
