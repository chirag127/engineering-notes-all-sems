### Consensus protocols for Permissioned Blockchains for the notes of the Unit 3 - Permissioned Blockchains in the subject of Block chain Architecture Design

Consensus protocols are the algorithms used in permissioned blockchains to reach agreement on the state of the blockchain and validate transactions. They play a crucial role in ensuring the security, reliability, and scalability of the blockchain.

1. Practical Byzantine Fault Tolerance (PBFT): A consensus protocol that uses a voting mechanism to reach agreement on the state of the blockchain. PBFT is designed to be highly secure, even in the presence of malicious actors.

2. Tendermint: A consensus protocol that uses a voting mechanism to reach agreement on the state of the blockchain. Tendermint is designed to be fast and efficient, making it well suited for use in high-performance blockchain applications.

3. Delegated Proof of Stake (DPoS): A consensus protocol that uses a voting mechanism to delegate the responsibility of validating transactions to a select group of nodes. DPoS is designed to be fast and efficient, making it well suited for use in high-performance blockchain applications.

4. Proof of Authority (PoA): A consensus protocol that uses a trusted set of nodes to validate transactions. PoA is designed for use in permissioned blockchains where the identity of nodes is known and trusted.

5. Proof of Stake (PoS): A consensus protocol that uses a voting mechanism to validate transactions. PoS is designed to be more energy-efficient than other consensus protocols, making it well suited for use in resource-constrained environments.

The choice of consensus protocol for a permissioned blockchain will depend on the specific requirements of the blockchain, including security, performance, and scalability. It is important to carefully consider the trade-offs between different consensus protocols when designing a permissioned blockchain.
