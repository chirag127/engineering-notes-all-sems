### KYC for the notes of the Unit 6 - Use case 1 in the subject of Block chain Architecture Design

- KYC stands for Know Your Customer, a process of verifying the identity of customers and assessing their suitability, along with the potential risks of illegal intentions towards the business relationship.
- KYC is a mandatory requirement for many financial institutions, such as banks, insurance companies, and investment firms, to comply with anti-money laundering (AML) and counter-terrorism financing (CTF) regulations.
- KYC involves collecting and verifying various types of information and documents from customers, such as name, address, date of birth, nationality, occupation, source of funds, etc. This can be a time-consuming, costly, and inefficient process, especially when done manually and repeatedly for each service provider.
- Blockchain is a distributed ledger technology that allows storing and sharing data in a secure, transparent, and immutable way, without the need for a central authority or intermediary. Blockchain can offer several benefits for KYC, such as:

  - Reducing duplication of efforts and costs by enabling customers to create and manage their own digital identity on the blockchain, and share it with multiple service providers with their consent.
  - Enhancing data security and privacy by encrypting and hashing the data on the blockchain, and allowing customers to control who can access their data and for what purpose.
  - Improving data quality and accuracy by ensuring that the data is verified and updated by multiple parties, and that any changes are recorded and traceable on the blockchain.
  - Increasing customer satisfaction and trust by simplifying and speeding up the KYC process, and providing customers with more transparency and visibility into their data and transactions.

- Some examples of blockchain KYC use cases are:

  - IBM Blockchain Trusted Identity: A decentralized platform for identification processes based on the blockchain and biometric technologies, that allows users to create and manage their own digital identity, and share it with various service providers across different sectors and geographies.
  - ASEAN Association Project: A collaborative project between three banks (OCBC Bank, HSBC Singapore and Mitsubishi UFJ Financial Group) and the Infocomm Media Development Authority of Singapore, that aims to create a blockchain-based KYC platform for corporate customers in the ASEAN region, that will reduce the time and cost of KYC verification, and improve data security and compliance.
  - uPort: An open identity system that allows users to register their own identity on the Ethereum blockchain, and use it to access various applications and services, such as voting, healthcare, banking, etc. Users can also share their data and credentials with other parties, and revoke access at any time.
  - Civic: A secure identity platform that leverages blockchain and biometrics to provide users with a reusable digital identity, and enable them to verify and share their identity with service providers, such as banks, exchanges, e-commerce sites, etc. Civic also offers a decentralized identity verification marketplace, where users can get verified by trusted third parties, and earn tokens for sharing their data.
  - SelfKey: A self-sovereign identity system that allows users to create and manage their own identity wallet on the blockchain, and store their personal and professional data and documents. Users can also access a marketplace of services that require identity verification, such as bank accounts, residency, citizenship, etc., and apply for them with one click.

- A possible mnemonic to remember the benefits of blockchain KYC is:

  - **B**etter data security and privacy
  - **L**ower costs and efforts
  - **O**ptimized data quality and accuracy
  - **C**ustomer satisfaction and trust
  - **K**eep up with compliance and regulations

  - In the traditional KYC process, you would have to fill out a lengthy online form, upload scanned copies of your identity documents, such as passport, driver's license, utility bill, etc., and wait for the bank to verify your information and approve your account.
