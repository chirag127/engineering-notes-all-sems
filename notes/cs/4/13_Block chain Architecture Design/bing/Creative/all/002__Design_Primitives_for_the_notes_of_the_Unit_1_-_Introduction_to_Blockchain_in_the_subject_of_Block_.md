### Design Primitives for the notes of the Unit 1 - Introduction to Blockchain in the subject of Block chain Architecture Design

- Design primitives are the basic building blocks of a blockchain system, such as transactions, blocks, and consensus protocols.
- Design primitives can be used to categorize, compare, and analyze different blockchain systems based on their properties, trade-offs, and performance.
- Design primitives can also help to derive the blockchain theory from other computer science fields, such as distributed systems, cryptography, and game theory.
- The main design primitives of a blockchain system are:

  - **Transaction Design**: This defines how the data and operations are encoded and executed on the blockchain. Transactions are the inputs and outputs of a blockchain system, and they can have different formats, structures, and semantics depending on the application domain and the blockchain platform. For example, Bitcoin transactions are simple transfers of value between addresses, while Ethereum transactions can contain complex smart contracts and logic. Transactions can also have different levels of privacy, anonymity, and verifiability, depending on the cryptographic primitives used to encrypt, sign, and prove them. Some examples of cryptographic primitives are hash functions, digital signatures, public-key encryption, zero-knowledge proofs, etc.
  - **Consensus Design**: This defines how the nodes in a blockchain network agree on a common state and history of the blockchain. Consensus is essential for maintaining the consistency, integrity, and security of a blockchain system, and it can be achieved by different algorithms and protocols depending on the network assumptions, threat models, and performance requirements. For example, Bitcoin uses a proof-of-work (PoW) protocol, which requires nodes to solve a hard cryptographic puzzle to create and validate new blocks. Ethereum uses a proof-of-stake (PoS) protocol, which requires nodes to stake some value to participate in the consensus process. Other consensus protocols include proof-of-authority (PoA), proof-of-elapsed-time (PoET), practical Byzantine fault tolerance (PBFT), Raft, etc.
  - **Block Design**: This defines how the transactions are grouped, ordered, and linked together to form a blockchain. Blocks are the outputs and records of a blockchain system, and they can have different sizes, frequencies, and structures depending on the consensus protocol, the network conditions, and the application requirements. For example, Bitcoin blocks have a fixed size of 1 MB and a variable frequency of 10 minutes, while Ethereum blocks have a variable size and a fixed frequency of 15 seconds. Blocks can also have different levels of finality, immutability, and scalability, depending on the block validation rules, the block reward mechanism, and the block pruning strategy.

- A blockchain system can be designed and optimized by choosing and combining different design primitives according to the specific needs and goals of the application domain and the target users. For example, a blockchain system for financial services may require high transaction throughput, low transaction latency, and strong transaction privacy, while a blockchain system for supply chain management may require high data visibility, low data redundancy, and strong data provenance. Therefore, different design primitives may be suitable for different blockchain use cases and scenarios.
