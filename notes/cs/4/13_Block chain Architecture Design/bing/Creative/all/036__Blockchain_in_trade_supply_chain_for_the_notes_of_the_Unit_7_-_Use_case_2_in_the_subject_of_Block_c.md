### Blockchain in trade/supply chain for the notes of the Unit 7 - Use case 2 in the subject of Block chain Architecture Design

- Blockchain is a decentralized ledger that records and protects transaction data shared among multiple parties.
- Blockchain can be used in the supply chain to improve transparency, traceability, efficiency, and security of the transactions and the products involved.
- Blockchain can help participants record price, date, location, quality, certification, and other relevant information to more effectively manage the supply chain.
- Blockchain can also help reduce administrative costs, eliminate intermediaries, ensure contract compliance, and prevent fraud and counterfeiting.
- Blockchain can provide increased supply chain transparency, as well as reduced cost and risk across the supply chain.

![Supply chain blockchain](https://www2.deloitte.com/content/dam/Deloitte/us/Images/infographics/us-blockchain-supply-chain-innovation.jpg)

- Some examples of blockchain applications in the supply chain are:

  - Provenance, a supply chain transparency start-up, tracks the responsible sourcing of tuna in Indonesia via blockchain.
  - Skuchain, a blockchain-based B2B trade and supply chain finance platform, enables collaboration, visibility, and control among buyers, sellers, logistics providers, banks, customs, and third parties.
  - IBM Food Trust, a blockchain network that connects growers, processors, distributors, and retailers, enhances food safety, freshness, sustainability, and authenticity.
  - TradeLens, a blockchain-enabled digital shipping platform, facilitates global trade by providing end-to-end supply chain information, documentation, and visibility.

- Some advantages of blockchain in the supply chain are:

  - It increases trust and accountability among the supply chain stakeholders.
  - It reduces errors, delays, and disputes in the transactions and the product flows.
  - It enhances the quality and performance of the products and the processes.
  - It enables real-time tracking and verification of the product status and history.
  - It supports sustainability and social responsibility initiatives by ensuring ethical and environmental compliance.

- Some challenges and limitations of blockchain in the supply chain are:

  - It requires a high level of technical expertise and infrastructure to implement and maintain.
  - It faces regulatory and legal uncertainties and barriers in different jurisdictions and industries.
  - It depends on the quality and accuracy of the data input and the consensus mechanisms.
  - It may raise privacy and security concerns due to the exposure and sharing of sensitive data.
  - It may encounter scalability and interoperability issues due to the diversity and complexity of the supply chain networks.

- A possible mnemonic to remember the advantages of blockchain in the supply chain is:

  - **T**rust
  - **R**eliability
  - **A**ccuracy
  - **C**ost-effectiveness
  - **E**fficiency
  - **S**ustainability

- A possible learning trick to understand the concept of blockchain in the supply chain is to compare it to a game of Scrabble:

  - Each player (supply chain participant) has a board (ledger) where they place tiles (transactions) to form words (products).
  - The tiles have letters (data) and numbers (tokens) that indicate their value and identity.
  - The players agree on the rules (consensus) of the game and verify each other's moves (validation).
  - The players cannot change or remove the tiles once they are placed on the board (immutability).
  - The players can see the entire board and the tiles of each player (transparency).
  - The players can score points (benefits) by forming valid and meaningful words (products).
