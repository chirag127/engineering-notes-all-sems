## Unit 1 - Introduction to Blockchain

- Blockchain is a specific type of database that stores data in blocks that are linked together by cryptographic hashes. 
- Each block contains a hash of the previous block, a timestamp, and transaction data (usually represented as a Merkle tree). 
- Blockchain is decentralized, distributed, and often public, meaning that no single entity has control over the data and anyone can access it. 
- Blockchain was invented by Satoshi Nakamoto in 2008 to serve as the public ledger for the bitcoin cryptocurrency, based on previous work by Haber, Stornetta, and Bayer. 
- Blockchain solves the problem of double-spending, which is the risk that a digital asset can be spent more than once, by using a consensus algorithm to validate new transactions and blocks. 
- Blockchain has many potential applications beyond cryptocurrencies, such as smart contracts, supply chain management, digital identity, voting systems, and more. 

Some possible mnemonics and learning tricks for this unit are:

- To remember the components of a block, think of **HAT**: Hash, Timestamp, and Transaction data.
- To remember the difference between decentralized and centralized systems, think of **DICE**: Decentralized means Independent, Collaborative, and Equal, while Centralized means Dependent, Isolated, and Hierarchical.
- To remember the name of the inventor of blockchain, think of **SATO**: SAtoshi naTOmoto.
