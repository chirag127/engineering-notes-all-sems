### Land records and other kinds of record keeping between government entities for the notes of the Unit 8 - Use case 3 in the subject of Block chain Architecture Design

- Land records are documents that contain information about the ownership, rights, and obligations of land properties. They are essential for ensuring legal certainty, facilitating transactions, and preventing disputes and frauds.
- However, the current land records system in many countries is inefficient, outdated, and vulnerable to corruption, human error, and tampering. Some of the problems include:
  - Lack of standardization and interoperability among different agencies and jurisdictions that manage land records
  - High costs and delays in accessing and verifying land records
  - Incomplete, inaccurate, or outdated information in land records
  - Risk of loss or damage of physical records due to natural disasters, fire, theft, etc.
  - Lack of transparency and accountability in land administration and governance
- Blockchain technology can offer a potential solution to these problems by creating a distributed, immutable, and transparent ledger of land records that can be accessed and verified by multiple parties in a secure and efficient way. Some of the benefits of blockchain for land records are:
  - Enhanced security and trust: Blockchain records are encrypted, timestamped, and linked to previous records, making them tamper-proof and verifiable. Blockchain also eliminates the need for intermediaries and central authorities, reducing the risk of fraud and corruption.
  - Improved efficiency and cost-effectiveness: Blockchain records can be updated and shared in real-time, reducing the time and cost of land transactions and disputes. Blockchain also enables automation of processes and smart contracts, simplifying and streamlining land administration and governance.
  - Increased transparency and accountability: Blockchain records are visible and auditable by all authorized parties, increasing the transparency and accountability of land transactions and policies. Blockchain also enables citizen participation and feedback, enhancing the social and environmental impact of land management.
- Several countries have experimented or implemented blockchain for land records, such as Georgia, Sweden, Ukraine, India, etc. Some of the use cases and examples are:
  - Georgia: In 2016, Georgia partnered with Bitfury, a blockchain company, to create a blockchain-based land registry system. The system allows the National Agency of Public Registry (NAPR) to record and verify land titles on the Bitcoin blockchain, making them secure and accessible. The system also allows citizens to view and validate land records online, reducing the need for physical visits and paperwork. The project has been successful in improving the efficiency, transparency, and trust of land administration in Georgia.
  - Sweden: In 2017, Sweden's land registry authority, Lantmäteriet, conducted a pilot project to test blockchain for land transactions. The project involved transferring the ownership of a property on a blockchain platform, using smart contracts to automate the process and digital signatures to verify the identities of the parties. The project demonstrated that blockchain can reduce the time and cost of land transactions, as well as increase the security and accuracy of land records.
  - Ukraine: In 2018, Ukraine launched a blockchain-based land registry system, called Auctus, to digitize and secure land records. The system allows the State Service of Ukraine for Geodesy, Cartography and Cadastre (SSUGCC) to store and update land records on a private blockchain network, using cryptographic hashes to link them to the public Bitcoin blockchain. The system also allows citizens to access and verify land records online, as well as to participate in land auctions and tenders. The project aims to improve the transparency, efficiency, and reliability of land administration in Ukraine.
  - India: In 2019, the state of Telangana in India announced a pilot project to use blockchain for land records. The project involves creating a blockchain-based land registry system, called Dharani, to digitize and secure land records. The system allows the Revenue Department to record and update land records on a blockchain platform, using biometric authentication and digital signatures to verify the identities of the parties. The system also allows citizens to access and verify land records online, as well as to pay land taxes and fees. The project intends to improve the quality, accessibility, and affordability of land services in Telangana.
