## Unit 3 - Permissioned Blockchains

- Permissioned blockchains are blockchains that are closed (i.e., not publicly accessible) or have an access control layer. This additional layer of security means that the blockchain can only be accessed by users with permissions .
- Permissioned blockchains are different from permissionless blockchains, which are open, decentralized networks that anyone can join and possess a copy of the ledger. Examples of permissionless blockchains are Bitcoin and Ethereum .
- Permissioned blockchains are favored by entities who require security, identity, and role definition within the blockchain. They are ideal for business applications that involve sensitive data or transactions .
- Permissioned blockchains have the following key aspects:
  - Controlled transparency: The identity of the members is known by the operators of the blockchain, but not necessarily by all participants. The privacy of the data and transactions is based on the governance decision of the network.
  - Development by private entities: The blockchain is created and maintained by a specific organization or a consortium of organizations that have a common interest or goal. The blockchain may or may not involve digital assets or tokens.
  - Incremental decentralization: The blockchain can have different degrees of decentralization depending on the consensus mechanism and the number of nodes. The consensus mechanism depends on knowing who the members are and what roles they have. The number of nodes is usually less than in permissionless blockchains, allowing for greater performance and scalability.
  - Customizable: The blockchain can be tailored to the specific needs and requirements of the network participants. The blockchain can have different features and functionalities depending on the use case and the business logic.
- Permissioned blockchains have the following advantages :
  - Strong privacy: The data and transactions are only visible to the authorized parties, and the identity of the participants is verified and authenticated. The blockchain can also implement encryption and zero-knowledge proofs to enhance the privacy and confidentiality of the network.
  - Faster, scalable: The blockchain can process more transactions per second and handle higher volumes of data than permissionless blockchains. This is because the consensus mechanism is more efficient and less resource-intensive, and the number of nodes is smaller and more manageable.
  - Customizable: The blockchain can be adapted to the specific needs and requirements of the network participants. The blockchain can have different features and functionalities depending on the use case and the business logic.
- Permissioned blockchains have the following disadvantages :
  - Less transparent: The data and transactions are not publicly verifiable and auditable, and the identity of the participants is not always known by all. The blockchain may be subject to manipulation or corruption by the operators or the members of the network.
  - Less decentralized: The blockchain is controlled by a specific organization or a consortium of organizations that have the authority to grant or revoke permissions, change the rules, or modify the data. The blockchain may be vulnerable to attacks or failures by the central authority or the nodes.
  - Less innovative: The blockchain is limited by the design and development of the private entities that create and maintain it. The blockchain may not benefit from the open source and collaborative nature of permissionless blockchains, which foster innovation and experimentation.
- Permissioned blockchains are becoming more common as businesses realize their benefits. Some examples of permissioned blockchains are :
  - Ripple: A blockchain network that facilitates cross-border payments and remittances between banks and financial institutions. The network uses a consensus protocol called the Ripple Protocol Consensus Algorithm (RPCA), which requires validators to be identified and trusted by the network.
  - Hyperledger Fabric: A blockchain framework that enables the development of enterprise-grade applications that involve multiple organizations and complex business processes. The framework uses a modular architecture that allows for different components and plug-ins, such as consensus mechanisms, membership services, and smart contracts.
  - Corda: A blockchain platform that enables the creation of interoperable applications that facilitate transactions and agreements between businesses. The platform uses a peer-to-peer network that relies on notaries to validate and record transactions, and flows to coordinate the business logic and communication between parties.
