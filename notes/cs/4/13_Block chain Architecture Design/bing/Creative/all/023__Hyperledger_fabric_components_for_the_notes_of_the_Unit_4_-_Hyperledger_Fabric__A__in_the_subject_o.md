### Hyperledger fabric components for the notes of the Unit 4 - Hyperledger Fabric (A) in the subject of Block chain Architecture Design

Hyperledger Fabric is a permissioned blockchain platform that allows multiple organizations to collaborate and execute transactions without intermediaries. Hyperledger Fabric has a modular architecture that supports pluggable components, such as consensus algorithms, membership services, and smart contracts. Hyperledger Fabric also supports private channels, which enable confidential transactions among a subset of participants.

The main components of Hyperledger Fabric are:

- **Assets**: An asset is anything that has value and can be represented as a collection of key-value pairs. Assets can be tangible or intangible, such as money, land, or intellectual property. Assets have state and ownership, which are recorded on the ledger.
- **Shared ledger**: The ledger records the state and ownership of assets, as well as the history of transactions that modify them. The ledger consists of two components: the world state and the transaction log. The world state is a database that stores the current state of assets, while the transaction log is an append-only record of all transactions that have occurred on the ledger. The ledger is replicated on every peer node in the network, ensuring consistency and transparency.
- **Smart contract**: A smart contract is a program that defines the business logic and rules for modifying the state of assets. Hyperledger Fabric smart contracts are called chaincode and are written in general-purpose programming languages, such as Go, Java, or Node.js. Chaincode is deployed on peer nodes and executed by them when invoked by clients. Chaincode can also interact with other chaincode on the same or different channels.
- **Peer nodes**: Peers are the fundamental elements of the network, as they host ledgers and chaincode. Peers can have different roles and responsibilities, such as endorsing transactions, validating transactions, committing transactions, or serving as leaders or followers. Peers can also belong to different organizations and channels, depending on the network configuration.
- **Channel**: A channel is a private communication mechanism that allows a subset of network participants to conduct confidential transactions. A channel is defined by a set of peers, a ledger, and a chaincode. Channels isolate transactions and state from other channels, providing privacy and scalability. Channels can also be used to implement different governance models and policies for different business scenarios.
- **Organization**: An organization is a logical grouping of network participants, such as companies, institutions, or individuals. Organizations have identities and certificates that are issued by a Membership Services Provider (MSP). Organizations can join or leave channels, deploy or invoke chaincode, and endorse or validate transactions, depending on their roles and permissions.
- **Membership Services Provider (MSP)**: An MSP is a component that manages the identities and certificates of network participants. An MSP defines who are the members of an organization, what are their roles and permissions, and how they are authenticated and authorized. An MSP can use different mechanisms to issue and validate certificates, such as a Certificate Authority (CA) or a pre-shared key.
- **Ordering service**: The ordering service is a component that ensures the consistency and finality of transactions on the ledger. The ordering service receives transactions from peer nodes, orders them into blocks, and delivers them to peer nodes for validation and commitment. The ordering service can use different consensus algorithms, such as Solo, Kafka, or Raft, to achieve fault tolerance and scalability. The ordering service is a logical entity that can consist of one or more ordering nodes.
