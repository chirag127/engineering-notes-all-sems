### Visibility

- Visibility refers to the ability of different participants in a blockchain network to see and verify the transactions and data stored on the ledger.
- Visibility is an important aspect of blockchain design, as it affects the security, privacy, scalability, and performance of the system.
- Visibility can be classified into two types: **public** and **private**.
- Public visibility means that anyone can access and view the transactions and data on the ledger, without needing any permission or authentication. This is the case for most public blockchains, such as Bitcoin and Ethereum.
- Private visibility means that only authorized participants can access and view the transactions and data on the ledger, based on their roles and permissions. This is the case for most private or permissioned blockchains, such as Hyperledger Fabric and Corda.
- Visibility can also be customized and fine-tuned according to the specific needs and requirements of the use case and the stakeholders involved. For example, some transactions or data can be encrypted, hashed, or masked to protect sensitive information, while still allowing verification and auditability. Some transactions or data can be shared selectively with certain parties, based on their identities or attributes. Some transactions or data can be aggregated or anonymized to preserve privacy, while still providing useful insights and statistics.
- Visibility can be achieved and implemented using various techniques and mechanisms, such as cryptography, consensus protocols, smart contracts, access control policies, channels, and zero-knowledge proofs. These techniques and mechanisms can be combined and integrated to create different levels and modes of visibility, such as full, partial, conditional, or zero visibility.
