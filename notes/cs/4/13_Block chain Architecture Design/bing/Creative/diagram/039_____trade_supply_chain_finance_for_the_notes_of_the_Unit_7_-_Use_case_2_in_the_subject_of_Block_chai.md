### Trade/Supply Chain Finance

Trade finance is the process of financing international trade transactions, such as the exchange of goods and services across borders. Trade finance involves various intermediaries, such as banks, exporters, importers, insurers, and logistics providers, who facilitate the trade process and mitigate the risks involved.

Supply chain finance is a subset of trade finance that focuses on optimizing the cash flow and working capital of the parties involved in a supply chain, such as suppliers, buyers, and financiers. Supply chain finance aims to improve the liquidity and efficiency of the supply chain by offering various financing solutions, such as invoice discounting, reverse factoring, inventory financing, and pre-shipment financing.

Blockchain is a distributed ledger technology that enables secure and transparent transactions among multiple parties without the need for a central authority or intermediary. Blockchain can offer various benefits for trade and supply chain finance, such as:

- Increased security and efficiency: Blockchain can digitize the entire trade finance lifecycle with increased security and efficiency. It can enable more transparent governance, decreased processing times, lower capital requirements and reduced risks of fraud, human error, and overall counterparty risk.
- Enhanced trust and collaboration: Blockchain can foster trust and collaboration among the trade and supply chain participants by providing a single source of truth and a shared record of transactions that is immutable and verifiable. Blockchain can also enable smart contracts, which are self-executing agreements that can automate the execution and enforcement of trade and supply chain contracts.
- New business models and opportunities: Blockchain can unlock new business models and opportunities for trade and supply chain finance by enabling peer-to-peer transactions, tokenization of assets, and access to new markets and customers. Blockchain can also facilitate the integration of other technologies, such as the Internet of Things (IoT), artificial intelligence (AI), and cloud computing, to enhance the visibility and traceability of the trade and supply chain processes.

Some of the use cases of blockchain in trade and supply chain finance are:

- Letters of credit: Letters of credit are instruments issued by banks that guarantee the payment of an exporter by the importer upon the fulfillment of certain conditions. Blockchain can streamline the process of issuing, verifying, and settling letters of credit by reducing the paperwork, costs, and delays involved. For example, Barclays has claimed to be the first bank to use blockchain in trade finance by issuing a letter of credit for a trade transaction between Ornua and Seychelles Trading Company.
- Invoice financing: Invoice financing is a form of short-term borrowing where a business sells its outstanding invoices to a third-party financier at a discount in exchange for immediate cash. Blockchain can improve the process of invoice financing by eliminating the need for intermediaries, reducing the risk of fraud and duplication, and increasing the transparency and liquidity of the invoices. For example, Populous is a platform that uses blockchain and smart contracts to connect invoice sellers and buyers in a peer-to-peer marketplace.
- Trade asset exchange: Trade asset exchange is a process where trade finance assets, such as invoices, purchase orders, and bills of lading, are exchanged among different parties for financing purposes. Blockchain can enable the seamless transfer of value across the trade network by removing process inefficiencies in asset issuance, exchange, and redemption. Blockchain’s consensus mechanism enables this by providing a single source of truth to all parties to prevent double spend problem. For example, TradeIX is a platform that uses blockchain to facilitate the trade asset exchange among banks, corporates, and alternative funders.
