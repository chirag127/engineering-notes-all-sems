### Permissions for the notes of the Unit 1 - Introduction to Blockchain

- A blockchain is a distributed ledger that records transactions among multiple parties in a secure and verifiable way.
- A blockchain can be classified into three types based on the permissions required to access and participate in the network: public, private, and consortium.
- A public blockchain is open to anyone who wants to join and validate transactions. Anyone can read, write, and audit the ledger. Examples of public blockchains are Bitcoin and Ethereum.
- A private blockchain is controlled by a single entity or a group of entities that have the authority to grant or revoke access to the network. Only authorized participants can read, write, and audit the ledger. Examples of private blockchains are Hyperledger Fabric and Corda.
- A consortium blockchain is a hybrid of public and private blockchains. It is governed by a group of entities that agree on the rules and permissions of the network. Only authorized participants can write and audit the ledger, but anyone can read it. Examples of consortium blockchains are Quorum and R3.
- The permissions of a blockchain affect its performance, security, scalability, and governance. Public blockchains are more decentralized, transparent, and resilient, but also slower, more costly, and less scalable. Private and consortium blockchains are more centralized, efficient, and scalable, but also less transparent and resilient.
