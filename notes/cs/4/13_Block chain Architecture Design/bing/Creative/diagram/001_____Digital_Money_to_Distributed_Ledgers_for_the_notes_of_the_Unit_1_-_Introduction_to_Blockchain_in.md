### Digital Money to Distributed Ledgers

- Digital money is a form of electronic money that can be used as a medium of exchange, a unit of account, and a store of value. Digital money can be transferred over the internet, mobile devices, or other electronic channels, without the need for physical cash or intermediaries.
- Distributed ledgers are systems that store and update records of transactions or other data across a network of nodes, without relying on a central authority or intermediary. Distributed ledgers can use cryptography, consensus algorithms, and smart contracts to ensure the validity, integrity, and security of the data.
- One of the most prominent examples of digital money that uses distributed ledger technology is Bitcoin, a decentralized cryptocurrency that was launched in 2009. Bitcoin uses a peer-to-peer network of nodes to maintain a public ledger of transactions, called the blockchain, that prevents double-spending and fraud.
- Other examples of digital money that use distributed ledger technology include Ethereum, Ripple, Litecoin, and stablecoins. These digital currencies have different features, such as programmability, scalability, interoperability, and stability, that aim to address the limitations or challenges of Bitcoin.
- Distributed ledger technology can also be applied to other domains, such as supply chain management, trade finance, identity verification, digital voting, and health records. These applications can benefit from the advantages of distributed ledger technology, such as transparency, immutability, efficiency, and resilience.
