### Blockchain in trade/supply chain

- Blockchain is a decentralized ledger technology that records and protects transaction data shared among multiple parties in a network.
- Blockchain can improve supply chain transparency and traceability by enabling participants to record and verify product information such as price, date, location, quality, and certification at every stage of the product lifecycle .
- Blockchain can also reduce administrative costs and inefficiencies by automating data collection and eliminating intermediaries and manual processes .
- Blockchain can enhance supply chain resilience and security by preventing fraud, tampering, and cyberattacks, as well as ensuring responsible and ethical sourcing .
- Some examples of blockchain applications in trade/supply chain are:
  - IBM Food Trust: A platform that connects farmers, processors, distributors, and retailers to share and trace food products across the supply chain.
  - TradeLens: A platform that connects shipping companies, ports, customs, and other stakeholders to digitize and streamline global trade processes.
  - Everledger: A platform that tracks and certifies the provenance and quality of diamonds, gemstones, and other high-value assets.
