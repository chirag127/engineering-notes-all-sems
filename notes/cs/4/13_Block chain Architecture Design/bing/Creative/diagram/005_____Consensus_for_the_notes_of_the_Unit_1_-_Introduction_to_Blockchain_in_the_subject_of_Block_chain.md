### Consensus

- Consensus is the process of reaching agreement among a group of participants on a shared state of data or a common decision.
- Consensus is essential for blockchain systems, as they are distributed, decentralized, and trustless networks that need to maintain consistency and validity of transactions and data.
- Consensus algorithms are the rules and mechanisms that enable the participants of a blockchain network to reach consensus, despite the possibility of failures, errors, or malicious attacks.
- Consensus algorithms can be classified into two broad categories: **permissionless** and **permissioned**.
  - Permissionless consensus algorithms are used in public blockchains, where anyone can join and participate in the network. They are designed to be resilient to censorship, collusion, and Sybil attacks, but they often sacrifice scalability and efficiency. Examples of permissionless consensus algorithms are **Proof-of-Work (PoW)**, **Proof-of-Stake (PoS)**, and **Delegated Proof-of-Stake (DPoS)**.
  - Permissioned consensus algorithms are used in private or consortium blockchains, where the participants are known and authorized by a central authority or a group of trusted entities. They are designed to achieve high performance, scalability, and finality, but they often sacrifice decentralization and security. Examples of permissioned consensus algorithms are **Practical Byzantine Fault Tolerance (PBFT)**, **Raft**, and **Tendermint**.
- Consensus algorithms can also be classified based on the type of **fault tolerance** they provide, which is the ability to withstand failures or errors in the network.
  - **Crash fault tolerance (CFT)** is the ability to tolerate nodes that stop functioning or crash, but do not behave maliciously. CFT algorithms can tolerate up to **n/2** faulty nodes, where **n** is the total number of nodes in the network. Examples of CFT algorithms are **Raft** and **Paxos**.
  - **Byzantine fault tolerance (BFT)** is the ability to tolerate nodes that behave arbitrarily or maliciously, such as sending conflicting or invalid messages. BFT algorithms can tolerate up to **n/3** faulty nodes, where **n** is the total number of nodes in the network. Examples of BFT algorithms are **PBFT**, **Tendermint**, and **HotStuff**.
  - **Nakamoto consensus** is a term coined by Vitalik Buterin to describe the probabilistic and asynchronous consensus achieved by PoW-based blockchains, such as Bitcoin and Ethereum. Nakamoto consensus relies on the longest chain rule, where the chain with the most accumulated proof-of-work is considered the valid one. Nakamoto consensus can tolerate up to **n/2** faulty nodes, where **n** is the total number of nodes in the network, but it does not guarantee finality, as forks or reorganizations can occur.
