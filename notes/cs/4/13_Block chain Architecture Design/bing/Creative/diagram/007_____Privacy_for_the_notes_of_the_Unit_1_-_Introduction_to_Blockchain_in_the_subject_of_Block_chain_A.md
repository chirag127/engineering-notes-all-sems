### Privacy for the notes of the Unit 1 - Introduction to Blockchain in the subject of Block chain Architecture Design

- Privacy is the ability to control the access and disclosure of personal or sensitive information.
- Privacy in blockchains is rather complicated as it contradicts with some highly praised properties of blockchain such as immutability.
- Immutability is considered a cornerstone of blockchains’ security and, therefore, an indisputable property according to which transactional blockchain data cannot be edited nor deleted.
- However, immutability also poses a challenge for privacy, as it means that once data is recorded on the blockchain, it cannot be erased or modified, even if it contains personal or confidential information.
- Moreover, public blockchains, which are available to any node that wishes to download the network, offer little privacy, as everyone can access the history of transactions and trace the identities and activities of the participants.
- Therefore, privacy in blockchains requires the use of various techniques and mechanisms to protect the data and the identities of the users, while preserving the integrity and transparency of the system.
- Some of the techniques and mechanisms for privacy in blockchains are:

  - Cryptography: A key aspect of privacy in blockchains is the use of private and public keys. Blockchain systems use asymmetric cryptography to secure transactions between users. In these systems, each user has a public and private key. These keys are random strings of numbers and are cryptographically related. The public key is used to identify the user and to verify the authenticity of the transactions. The private key is used to sign the transactions and to decrypt the data. The private key should be kept secret and never shared with anyone, as it is the only way to access the user's funds and data. Cryptography also enables the use of other techniques such as encryption, hashing, digital signatures, zero-knowledge proofs, and homomorphic encryption, which can enhance the privacy and security of the data and the transactions on the blockchain.
  - Permissioned blockchains: Private blockchains (or permissioned blockchains) are different from public blockchains, as they are not open to anyone, but only to a selected group of participants who have been authorized to join the network. Permissioned blockchains can offer more privacy, as they can restrict the access and the visibility of the data and the transactions to the authorized nodes. Permissioned blockchains can also implement different levels of privacy, such as public, private, or consortium, depending on the degree of control and trust among the participants.
  - Data minimization: Data minimization is the principle of collecting and storing only the minimum amount of data that is necessary for the purpose of the system. Data minimization can help to reduce the privacy risks and the storage costs of the blockchain, as it can limit the exposure and the retention of the data. Data minimization can be achieved by using techniques such as pseudonymization, anonymization, aggregation, or deletion of the data.
  - Accountability: Accountability is the principle of being responsible and answerable for the actions and the consequences of the system. Accountability can help to balance privacy with transparency and trust, as it can ensure that the participants of the blockchain are compliant with the rules and the regulations of the system, and that they can be held liable for any misconduct or misuse of the data. Accountability can be achieved by using techniques such as identity verification, audit trails, smart contracts, or governance models.
