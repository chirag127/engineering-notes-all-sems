## Unit 8 - Use case 3

- Use case 3 is about how to design and implement a chatbot that can answer questions about a specific domain, such as travel, sports, or health.
- The main steps involved in use case 3 are:

  - Define the scope and purpose of the chatbot, such as the target audience, the domain knowledge, and the expected functionality.
  - Collect and analyze data from relevant sources, such as websites, documents, or user feedback, to identify the common questions and intents that the chatbot should handle.
  - Design the chatbot architecture, such as the natural language understanding (NLU) component, the dialogue management (DM) component, and the natural language generation (NLG) component.
  - Implement the chatbot using appropriate tools and frameworks, such as Microsoft Bot Framework, Rasa, or Dialogflow.
  - Test and evaluate the chatbot using various metrics, such as accuracy, usability, and user satisfaction.
  - Deploy and maintain the chatbot on a suitable platform, such as a website, a mobile app, or a messaging service.

- An example of use case 3 is a chatbot that can answer questions about travel destinations, such as attractions, weather, culture, and transportation. The chatbot could have the following features:

  - It can understand natural language queries from the user, such as "What are the best places to visit in Paris?" or "How is the weather in Tokyo in April?"
  - It can access and retrieve relevant information from a knowledge base or an external API, such as Wikipedia, TripAdvisor, or OpenWeatherMap.
  - It can generate natural language responses that are informative, concise, and engaging, such as "Paris is famous for its landmarks, such as the Eiffel Tower, the Louvre Museum, and the Notre Dame Cathedral. You can also enjoy the cuisine, the art, and the nightlife of the city." or "Tokyo has a temperate climate, with an average temperature of 15°C in April. You can expect some rain and cherry blossoms during this month."
  - It can handle multiple turns of dialogue and maintain the context, such as remembering the user's preferences, previous queries, and suggestions.
  - It can handle errors and exceptions gracefully, such as clarifying the user's intent, providing alternative options, or apologizing for any inconvenience.
