### Design goals for the notes of the Unit 3 - Permissioned Blockchains

Permissioned blockchains are a type of distributed ledger technology (DLT) that allow only authorized participants to join the network, validate transactions, and access the shared data. Permissioned blockchains are often used by private entities or consortia that want to leverage the benefits of blockchains without sacrificing the control and security of a centralized system. Some of the design goals for permissioned blockchains are:

- **Controlled transparency**: Permissioned blockchains can provide different levels of transparency based on the goals and needs of the participating organizations. For example, some transactions or data can be encrypted or hidden from certain nodes, while others can be visible to all or some of the nodes. This allows for a balance between privacy and accountability.
- **Scalability**: Permissioned blockchains can achieve higher scalability than public blockchains, as they do not rely on proof-of-work (PoW) or other resource-intensive consensus mechanisms. Instead, they can use more efficient and faster consensus algorithms, such as practical Byzantine fault tolerance (PBFT) or Raft, that can handle a large number of transactions per second (TPS) and reduce the latency .
- **Governance**: Permissioned blockchains can have a clear and predefined governance structure that defines the roles and responsibilities of the nodes, the rules and policies of the network, and the mechanisms for resolving disputes and making decisions. This can ensure a smooth and coordinated operation of the network, as well as a fair and transparent distribution of benefits and costs among the participants .
- **Security**: Permissioned blockchains can enhance the security of the data and transactions by using cryptographic techniques, such as digital signatures, encryption, and hashing, to ensure the integrity, authenticity, and confidentiality of the information. Moreover, permissioned blockchains can prevent unauthorized access and malicious attacks by enforcing strict access control and identity management protocols, as well as by detecting and isolating compromised nodes .
