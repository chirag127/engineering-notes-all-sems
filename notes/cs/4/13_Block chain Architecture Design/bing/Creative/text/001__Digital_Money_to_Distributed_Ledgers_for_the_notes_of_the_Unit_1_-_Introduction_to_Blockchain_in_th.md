### Digital Money to Distributed Ledgers

- Digital money is any form of money that exists electronically, such as bank deposits, e-money, or cryptocurrencies.
- Distributed ledgers are systems that use independent computers (nodes) to record, share and synchronize transactions in their respective electronic ledgers, without a central authority or intermediary.
- Blockchain is one type of a distributed ledger that organizes data into blocks, which are chained together in an append-only mode. Blockchain is the technology behind Bitcoin and other cryptocurrencies.
- Digital money and distributed ledgers have the potential to transform the financial sector and other sectors, by enabling peer-to-peer transactions, reducing costs, increasing efficiency, and improving transparency and security.
- However, digital money and distributed ledgers also face many challenges and risks, such as consumer protection, financial integrity, legal and regulatory issues, environmental impact, and technological limitations.

Some key points to remember are:

- Digital money and distributed ledgers are not the same thing, but they are often related. For example, Bitcoin is a digital currency that uses a blockchain as its distributed ledger.
- Digital money and distributed ledgers can have different types, features, and functions, depending on their design and purpose. For example, some digital currencies are backed by governments or central banks (central bank digital currencies or CBDCs), while others are not (cryptocurrencies or stablecoins).
- Digital money and distributed ledgers can have different impacts and implications for different stakeholders, such as governments, central banks, financial institutions, businesses, consumers, and society. For example, some benefits of digital money and distributed ledgers are financial inclusion, innovation, and competition, while some risks are fraud, cyberattacks, and illicit activities.
