### Blockchain in trade/supply chain

- Blockchain is a decentralized ledger technology that records and protects transaction data shared among multiple parties.
- Blockchain can improve supply chain transparency and traceability by recording price, date, location, quality, certification, and other relevant information of products and materials across the supply chain.
- Blockchain can also reduce administrative costs and risks by eliminating intermediaries, ensuring contract compliance, and preventing fraud and counterfeiting.
- Blockchain can enable innovation and collaboration among supply chain stakeholders by facilitating data sharing, smart contracts, and digital tokens.
- Some examples of blockchain applications in supply chain are:
  - Provenance: a platform that tracks the responsible sourcing of products such as tuna, coffee, and cotton using blockchain.
  - Skuchain: a platform that provides blockchain-based trade and supply chain finance solutions such as inventory tracking, invoice financing, and payment automation.
  - Monegraph: a platform that uses blockchain to secure the usage and sharing rights of digital media and enable revenue sharing among creators, publishers, and distributors.
