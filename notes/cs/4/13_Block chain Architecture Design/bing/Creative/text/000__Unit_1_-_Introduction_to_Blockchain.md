## Unit 1 - Introduction to Blockchain

- A blockchain is a distributed ledger that records transactions among multiple parties in a secure, transparent, and verifiable way.
- A blockchain consists of a series of blocks, each containing a cryptographic hash of the previous block, a timestamp, and a set of transactions.
- Transactions are validated by a network of nodes, each running a consensus algorithm to ensure that all copies of the ledger are consistent and synchronized.
- A blockchain can be public, private, or hybrid, depending on the level of access and trust among the participants.
- A blockchain can enable peer-to-peer transactions, smart contracts, decentralized applications, and digital assets, among other use cases.
- A blockchain can provide benefits such as immutability, traceability, security, efficiency, and transparency, but also faces challenges such as scalability, interoperability, governance, and regulation.
