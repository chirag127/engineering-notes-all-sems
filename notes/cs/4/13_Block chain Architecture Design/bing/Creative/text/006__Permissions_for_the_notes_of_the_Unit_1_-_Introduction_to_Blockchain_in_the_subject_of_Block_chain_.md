### Permissions for the notes of the Unit 1 - Introduction to Blockchain in the subject of Block chain Architecture Design

- Permissions are the rules that define who can access, modify, or validate the data stored in a blockchain network.
- Permissions can be classified into three types: public, private, and consortium.
- Public permissions allow anyone to join the network, read the data, and participate in the consensus process. Examples of public blockchains are Bitcoin and Ethereum.
- Private permissions restrict the access to the network and the data to a predefined set of participants, usually belonging to a single organization or a trusted group. Examples of private blockchains are Hyperledger Fabric and Corda.
- Consortium permissions are a hybrid of public and private permissions, where the network is composed of multiple organizations that agree on the rules and the governance of the network. Examples of consortium blockchains are Quorum and R3.
- Permissions affect the design and the performance of a blockchain network, as they determine the level of trust, security, scalability, and efficiency of the system.
- Permissions also have implications for the legal and regulatory aspects of a blockchain network, as they define the rights and responsibilities of the participants and the authorities.
