### Protocols for the notes of the Unit 1 - Introduction to Blockchain in the subject of Block chain Architecture Design

- Protocols are basic sets of rules that ensure data sharing between computers or computing systems.
- In the case of cryptocurrencies, they set out the structure of the blockchain and the rules for managing the distributed database, which will guarantee that assets are traded securely online.
- Blockchain protocols are a set of protocols used to govern the blockchain network. The rules define the interface of the network, interaction between the computers, incentives, kind of data, etc.
- Blockchain protocols aim to address the four principles: security, decentralization, consistency, and scalability.
- Security: Protocols maintain the security of the whole crypto network. Since the network involves the transfer of money, protocols define the structure of data and also secure data from malicious users.
- Decentralization: Blockchain is a decentralized network. There is no involvement of any central authority. So the protocols authorize the whole network.
- Consistency: Whenever a transaction occurs, protocols update the whole database at each step so that each user is well versed with the whole crypto network.
- Scalability: Scalability means an increase in the number of transactions. Earlier scalability was an issue in the blockchain. But nowadays most protocols handle the issue of an increasing number of transactions in the network and the addition of nodes to the network.
- Blockchain protocols are also known as consensus methods. These methods are different systems that are implemented to reach consensus and validate transactions within a blockchain network.
- Some of the common blockchain protocols are:
  - Proof of Work (PoW): This protocol requires miners to solve complex mathematical problems to verify transactions and create new blocks. The first miner to solve the problem gets rewarded with some cryptocurrency. This protocol is used by Bitcoin, Ethereum, and Litecoin.
  - Proof of Stake (PoS): This protocol requires validators to stake some amount of cryptocurrency to participate in the consensus process. The validators are randomly selected to verify transactions and create new blocks. The validators get rewarded with transaction fees or newly created coins. This protocol is used by Cardano, Polkadot, and Binance Smart Chain.
  - Proof of Authority (PoA): This protocol requires validators to have a reputation or identity that can be verified by the network. The validators are trusted entities that are authorized to verify transactions and create new blocks. The validators do not get rewarded with coins, but with the power and influence they have in the network. This protocol is used by VeChain, xDai, and POA Network.
  - Proof of Burn (PoB): This protocol requires miners to burn some amount of cryptocurrency to prove their commitment to the network. The more coins they burn, the higher their chances of being selected to verify transactions and create new blocks. The miners do not get rewarded with coins, but with the right to mine. This protocol is used by Slimcoin, Counterparty, and Bitcoin Cash.
  - Proof of Capacity (PoC): This protocol requires miners to use their hard disk space to store some data that can be used to verify transactions and create new blocks. The more disk space they have, the higher their chances of being selected to mine. The miners get rewarded with coins for their contribution to the network. This protocol is used by Burstcoin, Chia, and SpaceMint.
