### Basic crypto primitives for the notes of the Unit 1 - Introduction to Blockchain in the subject of Block chain Architecture Design

- Cryptographic primitives are the low-level algorithms that are used to build cryptographic protocols for a strong secured network. They are the basic building blocks of the cryptosystem.
- Cryptographic primitives are important for blockchain because they provide security, encryption, decryption, validation, and specificity for the transactions and data in the network.
- Cryptographic primitives are very specific in nature and cannot be easily developed or modified. Cryptographic designers combine the cryptographic primitives to form a strong security protocol.
- Cryptographic primitives in blockchain can be classified into two categories: basic and optional.
- Basic cryptographic primitives are the ones that are essential for the functioning of the blockchain, such as hash functions, digital signatures, and encryption algorithms.
- Optional cryptographic primitives are the ones that are used to enhance the security or functionality of the blockchain, such as accumulators, commitments, zero-knowledge proofs, and ring signatures.
- Some of the common basic cryptographic primitives in blockchain are:

  - One-way hash functions: They are mathematical functions that map variable-length data to fixed-length binary data. They are irreversible, meaning that the original data cannot be recovered from the hash value. They are also collision-resistant, meaning that it is hard to find two different inputs that produce the same hash value. They are used to generate unique identifiers for blocks and transactions, to verify the integrity of data, and to implement proof-of-work consensus mechanisms  . Examples of hash functions used in blockchain are SHA-256, SHA-512, and Ethash.
  - Digital signatures: They are schemes that allow a sender to sign a message with a private key and allow a receiver to verify the signature with a public key. They are used to authenticate the identity of the sender, to ensure the non-repudiation of the message, and to prevent tampering or forgery of the message . Examples of digital signature schemes used in blockchain are ECDSA, EdDSA, and Schnorr signatures.
  - Symmetric-key encryption: It is a type of encryption that uses the same key for both encryption and decryption. It is used to encrypt and decrypt data in a fast and efficient way. It is also used to generate pseudorandom numbers and to implement stream ciphers . Examples of symmetric-key encryption algorithms used in blockchain are AES, ChaCha20, and Salsa20.
  - Asymmetric-key encryption: It is a type of encryption that uses a pair of keys: a public key and a private key. The public key can be used to encrypt data, and the private key can be used to decrypt data. It is also known as public-key encryption or public-key cryptography. It is used to encrypt and decrypt data in a secure and flexible way. It is also used to implement public-key infrastructure and key exchange protocols . Examples of asymmetric-key encryption algorithms used in blockchain are RSA, ElGamal, and ECC.
