### Consensus protocols for Permissioned Blockchains

- Consensus protocols are algorithms that enable all the nodes in a blockchain network to agree on the validity and order of transactions and blocks.
- Consensus protocols are essential for maintaining the security, integrity, and consistency of the distributed ledger in a decentralized and trustless environment.
- Consensus protocols can be classified into two broad categories: permissionless and permissioned.
- Permissionless consensus protocols are used in public blockchains, where anyone can join and participate in the network without any authorization or identity verification. Examples of permissionless consensus protocols are Proof of Work (PoW), Proof of Stake (PoS), and Delegated Proof of Stake (DPoS).
- Permissioned consensus protocols are used in private or consortium blockchains, where only authorized and identified nodes can join and participate in the network. Examples of permissioned consensus protocols are Practical Byzantine Fault Tolerance (PBFT), Raft, and Proof of Elapsed Time (PoET).
- Permissioned consensus protocols are more suitable for applications that require high performance, scalability, privacy, and compliance. Permissioned consensus protocols are also more resilient to attacks such as 51% attack, Sybil attack, and selfish mining.
- Permissioned consensus protocols can be further divided into two subcategories: leader-based and leaderless.
- Leader-based consensus protocols rely on a designated node or a group of nodes to propose and validate blocks. Leader-based consensus protocols are faster and simpler, but they introduce a single point of failure and a potential bottleneck. Examples of leader-based consensus protocols are PBFT, Raft, and Paxos.
- Leaderless consensus protocols do not rely on any specific node or group of nodes to propose and validate blocks. Leaderless consensus protocols are more decentralized and fault-tolerant, but they are more complex and slower. Examples of leaderless consensus protocols are PoET, Tendermint, and HoneyBadgerBFT.
