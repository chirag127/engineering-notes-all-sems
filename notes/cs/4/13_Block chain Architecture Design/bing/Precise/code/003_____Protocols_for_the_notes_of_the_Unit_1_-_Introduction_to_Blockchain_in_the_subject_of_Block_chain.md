### Protocols for the notes of the Unit 1 - Introduction to Blockchain in the subject of Block chain Architecture Design

1. Blockchain is a distributed database that maintains a continuously growing list of records, called blocks, which are linked and secured using cryptography.
2. Each block contains a cryptographic hash of the previous block, a timestamp, and transaction data.
3. By design, a blockchain is resistant to modification of the data.
4. Once recorded, the data in any given block cannot be altered retroactively without the alteration of all subsequent blocks, which requires consensus of the network majority.
5. Blockchain technology is the backbone of cryptocurrencies such as Bitcoin and Ethereum.
6. Blockchain technology has the potential to revolutionize many industries by enabling secure, decentralized, and transparent record-keeping.
7. Some common use cases for blockchain technology include supply chain management, voting systems, and identity management.
8. There are several different types of blockchain protocols, including public, private, and consortium blockchains.
9. Public blockchains, such as Bitcoin and Ethereum, are open to anyone and are secured by a decentralized network of nodes.
10. Private blockchains, on the other hand, are only accessible to a select group of users and are often used by businesses for internal record-keeping.
11. Consortium blockchains are a hybrid of public and private blockchains and are governed by a group of pre-selected nodes.
12. The choice of blockchain protocol depends on the specific use case and the desired level of security, transparency, and accessibility.
