## Unit 3 - Permissioned Blockchains

1. **Definition**: A permissioned blockchain is a type of blockchain where access to the network is restricted to a specific group of participants. 
2. **Access Control**: In a permissioned blockchain, the network administrator controls who can join the network, and what actions they are allowed to perform. 
3. **Use Cases**: Permissioned blockchains are commonly used in enterprise and business settings, where there is a need for privacy and control over the network. 
4. **Examples**: Some examples of permissioned blockchains include Hyperledger Fabric, R3 Corda, and Quorum. 
5. **Advantages**: Permissioned blockchains offer several advantages over public blockchains, including faster transaction speeds, greater scalability, and enhanced privacy. 
6. **Disadvantages**: One disadvantage of permissioned blockchains is that they are not as decentralized as public blockchains, which can lead to concerns about trust and security.
