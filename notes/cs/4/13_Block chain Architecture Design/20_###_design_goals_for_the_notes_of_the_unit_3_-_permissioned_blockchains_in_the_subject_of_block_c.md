### Design goals for the notes of the Unit 3 - Permissioned Blockchains in the subject of Block chain Architecture Design

Design goals are the objectives that a blockchain architecture should aim to achieve. The design goals for permissioned blockchains are:

1. Scalability: The ability of the blockchain to handle a large number of transactions and users without sacrificing performance.

2. Privacy: The ability to protect the confidentiality of transactions and user data.

3. Security: The ability to protect the blockchain from malicious actors and ensure the integrity of the data.

4. Interoperability: The ability of the blockchain to integrate with other systems and technologies, such as traditional databases and cloud computing systems.

5. Consensus: The ability to achieve consensus among participants on the state of the blockchain, without relying on a centralized authority.

6. Decentralization: The ability to distribute control of the blockchain among multiple participants, reducing the risk of single points of failure and increasing resilience.

7. Flexibility: The ability to adapt to changing requirements and support new use cases and applications.

These design goals are essential for ensuring that permissioned blockchains are secure, scalable, and flexible, and that they meet the needs of the organizations and users that rely on them. The specific design goals will vary depending on the specific requirements and use cases of each blockchain architecture.
