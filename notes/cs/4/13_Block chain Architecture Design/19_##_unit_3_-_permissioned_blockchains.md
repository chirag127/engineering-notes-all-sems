## Unit 3 - Permissioned Blockchains

Unit 3 - Permissioned Blockchains refers to a type of blockchain technology that restricts access to the network to a predetermined set of participants. Unlike public blockchains, such as Bitcoin and Ethereum, permissioned blockchains have a centralized authority that controls access to the network and manages the consensus process.

Some key features of permissioned blockchains include:

1. Access control: Only authorized participants can access the network and participate in the consensus process.

2. Scalability: Permissioned blockchains can be optimized for performance, allowing for faster transaction processing and increased scalability.

3. Privacy: Permissioned blockchains can provide greater privacy, as only authorized participants have access to the network and the data stored on it.

4. Compliance: Permissioned blockchains can be designed to meet specific regulatory requirements, such as data privacy laws.

Permissioned blockchains are often used in industries such as finance, healthcare, and government, where security, privacy, and compliance are critical concerns. They are also used for applications such as supply chain management, digital identity, and asset management.

Permissioned blockchains offer several advantages over public blockchains, including improved security, greater privacy, and the ability to meet specific regulatory requirements. However, they also have some limitations, such as a reduced level of decentralization and a dependence on a centralized authority.
