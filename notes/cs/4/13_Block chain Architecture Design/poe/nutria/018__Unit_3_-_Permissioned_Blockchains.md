
## Unit 3 - Permissioned Blockchains

1. Permissioned blockchains, also known as private blockchains, are distributed ledger networks that require permission to access and participate in the network. 
2. Permissioned blockchains are typically used in enterprise settings to track and store data. 
3. Permissioned blockchains are typically faster and more secure than public blockchains, as the network is managed and monitored by a trusted group of participants. 
4. Permissioned blockchains allow for more control over who can access the network and what data can be stored on the network. 
5. Permissioned blockchains can also be used to facilitate transactions, such as payments and asset transfers. 
6. Permissioned blockchains are more secure than public blockchains, as they are managed by a trusted group of participants, and the data stored on the network is more secure. 
7. Permissioned blockchains are also more efficient than public blockchains, as they require less computing power and can process transactions faster. 
8. Permissioned blockchains are typically used in enterprise settings, as they provide a more secure and efficient way to store and manage data.
