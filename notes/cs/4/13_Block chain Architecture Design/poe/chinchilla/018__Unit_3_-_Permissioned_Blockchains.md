## Unit 3 - Permissioned Blockchains

In this unit, we will learn about permissioned blockchains, which are a type of blockchain that restricts access to certain entities. Unlike public blockchains, where anyone can participate and access the data, permissioned blockchains have a predetermined set of nodes that have the authority to access and modify the data.

Here are some important points to understand about permissioned blockchains:

- Permissioned blockchains are also known as private blockchains or consortium blockchains.
- They are used primarily in enterprise settings, where privacy and security are paramount concerns.
- Participants in a permissioned blockchain are identified and verified before they are granted access to the network.
- Access controls are implemented to ensure only authorized entities can read, write, and modify the data.
- Permissioned blockchains are typically faster and more scalable than public blockchains because the number of nodes is smaller and more tightly controlled.
- They are less decentralized than public blockchains because the authority to modify the ledger is concentrated in the hands of a few entities.
- Permissioned blockchains can be either open or closed. In an open permissioned blockchain, anyone can join the network, but they must be approved by the existing members. In a closed permissioned blockchain, only pre-approved entities are allowed to participate.
- Examples of permissioned blockchains include Hyperledger Fabric, Corda, and Quorum.

To summarize, permissioned blockchains are a type of blockchain used in enterprise settings where privacy, security, and centralized control are important considerations. They restrict access to a predetermined set of entities and implement access controls to ensure only authorized parties can modify the data. While they are less decentralized than public blockchains, they are faster, more scalable, and better suited for certain use cases.
