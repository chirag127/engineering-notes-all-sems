# Introduction to security attacks

## What is a security attack?

- A security attack is any action that compromises the confidentiality, integrity, or availability of a system or its data.
- Security attacks can be classified into two types: passive attacks and active attacks.

## What is a passive attack?

- A passive attack is an attack that does not alter the system or its data, but only observes or analyzes the information that is transmitted or stored.
- Passive attacks are hard to detect, but can be prevented by using encryption or other techniques to hide the information.
- Examples of passive attacks are eavesdropping, traffic analysis, and ciphertext-only attack.

## What is an active attack?

- An active attack is an attack that modifies the system or its data, or creates false or misleading information to deceive the system or its users.
- Active attacks are easier to detect, but can cause more damage or disruption to the system or its services.
- Examples of active attacks are masquerade, replay, modification, and denial of service.

## What are the security services and mechanisms?

- Security services are the goals or objectives that a system should provide to protect itself and its data from security attacks.
- Security services can be categorized into five types: confidentiality, integrity, availability, authentication, and non-repudiation.
- Security mechanisms are the methods or tools that are used to implement the security services.
- Security mechanisms can be categorized into two types: preventive mechanisms and reactive mechanisms.

## What are the classical encryption techniques?

- Classical encryption techniques are the methods of encryption that were used before the advent of modern cryptography.
- Classical encryption techniques can be divided into two types: substitution ciphers and transposition ciphers.

## What are substitution ciphers?

- Substitution ciphers are the ciphers that replace each letter or symbol of the plaintext with another letter or symbol of the ciphertext, according to a fixed rule or key.
- Substitution ciphers can be further classified into two types: monoalphabetic ciphers and polyalphabetic ciphers.

## What are transposition ciphers?

- Transposition ciphers are the ciphers that rearrange the order of the letters or symbols of the plaintext, according to a fixed rule or key, to form the ciphertext.
- Transposition ciphers can be further classified into two types: simple transposition ciphers and complex transposition ciphers.

## What is cryptanalysis?

- Cryptanalysis is the science of breaking or analyzing the encryption techniques, to recover the plaintext from the ciphertext, or to find the key or the rule used for encryption.
- Cryptanalysis can be performed by using various methods, such as brute force, frequency analysis, known-plaintext attack, chosen-plaintext attack, chosen-ciphertext attack, etc.

## What is steganography?

- Steganography is the art of hiding information within another information, such as an image, a sound, a text, etc., so that the existence of the hidden information is not noticeable.
- Steganography can be used to enhance the security of encryption, by concealing the ciphertext within another medium, or to bypass the encryption, by hiding the plaintext within another medium.

## What are stream and block ciphers?

- Block ciphers are more secure and complex, but can be slower and require padding or modes of operation.
