### Classical encryption techniquessubstitution ciphers and transposition ciphers

- Substitution ciphers are a type of encryption technique that replace each plaintext symbol with a different ciphertext symbol, according to a fixed rule or key.
- Transposition ciphers are a type of encryption technique that rearrange the order of the plaintext symbols, according to a fixed rule or key.
- Cryptanalysis is the science of breaking encryption techniques, by finding the key or the plaintext from the ciphertext, without knowing the encryption algorithm or the key.
- Steganography is the art of hiding information in plain sight, by embedding it in another medium, such as an image, a video, or a text, without altering the appearance or the functionality of the medium.
- Stream ciphers are a type of encryption technique that encrypt each plaintext symbol with a different key, generated by a pseudorandom key stream.
- Block ciphers are a type of encryption technique that encrypt a fixed-length block of plaintext with a fixed-length key, producing a block of ciphertext of the same length.

### Modern Block Ciphers: Block ciphers principles, Shannon’s theory of confusion and diffusion, fiestal structure, Data encryption standard(DES), Strength of DES, Idea of differential cryptanalysis, block cipher modes of operations, Triple DES

- Block ciphers principles are the basic design elements and properties of block ciphers, such as the block size, the key size, the number of rounds, the round function, the key schedule, and the security goals.
- Shannon’s theory of confusion and diffusion are two concepts that describe how to achieve secure encryption, by making the relationship between the plaintext, the ciphertext, and the key as complex and as random as possible.
- Confusion means that each ciphertext symbol should depend on several plaintext symbols and several key symbols, in a nonlinear way, so that the key cannot be easily derived from the ciphertext.
- Diffusion means that each plaintext symbol should affect many ciphertext symbols, in a uniform way, so that the plaintext cannot be easily recovered from the ciphertext.
- Fiestal structure is a common type of round function for block ciphers, that consists of dividing the block into two halves, applying a nonlinear function to one half using the round key, and then XORing the result with the other half, and swapping the halves.
- Data encryption standard (DES) is a widely used block cipher, that encrypts 64-bit blocks of plaintext with 56-bit keys, using 16 rounds of a fiestal structure, and a predefined set of permutations and substitutions.
- Strength of DES is measured by its resistance to various types of attacks, such as brute force, differential cryptanalysis, linear cryptanalysis, and related-key attacks. DES is considered insecure today, due to its small key size and its vulnerability to some attacks.
- Idea of differential cryptanalysis is a type of attack on block ciphers, that exploits the statistical properties of the round function, by analyzing the differences between pairs of plaintexts and ciphertexts, and deducing information about the key.
- Block cipher modes of operations are different ways of using block ciphers to encrypt messages of arbitrary length, by dividing the message into blocks and applying the block cipher to each block, with some variations, such as padding, chaining, feedback, or counter.
- Triple DES is a variant of DES, that encrypts each block three times with three different keys, to increase the effective key size and the security level. Triple DES is more secure than DES, but also slower and less efficient.
