## Security Attacks
- Security attacks are any actions that compromise the security of information or systems.
- Security attacks can be classified into two categories: passive attacks and active attacks.
- Passive attacks are those that do not alter the data or system, but only observe or analyze it. Examples of passive attacks are eavesdropping, traffic analysis, and cryptanalysis.
- Active attacks are those that modify the data or system, or create false data or system. Examples of active attacks are masquerade, replay, modification, and denial of service.

## Security Services
- Security services are the countermeasures that protect the information or system from security attacks.
- Security services can be classified into five categories: confidentiality, integrity, availability, authentication, and non-repudiation.
- Confidentiality is the service that ensures that the data or system is accessible only to authorized parties. Examples of confidentiality mechanisms are encryption, access control, and data hiding.
- Integrity is the service that ensures that the data or system is not modified or corrupted by unauthorized parties. Examples of integrity mechanisms are checksums, digital signatures, and hash functions.
- Availability is the service that ensures that the data or system is accessible and usable by authorized parties when needed. Examples of availability mechanisms are redundancy, backup, and fault tolerance.
- Authentication is the service that ensures that the identity of the parties involved in the communication or transaction is verified. Examples of authentication mechanisms are passwords, biometrics, and certificates.
- Non-repudiation is the service that ensures that the parties involved in the communication or transaction cannot deny their participation or actions. Examples of non-repudiation mechanisms are digital signatures, timestamps, and audit logs.

## Security Mechanisms
- Security mechanisms are the methods or tools that implement the security services.
- Security mechanisms can be classified into two categories: preventive mechanisms and detective mechanisms.
- Preventive mechanisms are those that prevent or deter the security attacks from happening. Examples of preventive mechanisms are encryption, access control, and firewalls.
- Detective mechanisms are those that detect or identify the security attacks that have happened or are happening. Examples of detective mechanisms are intrusion detection systems, antivirus software, and audit logs.

## Classical Encryption Techniques
- Classical encryption techniques are the methods of encrypting and decrypting data using simple mathematical operations and transformations.
- Classical encryption techniques can be classified into two categories: substitution ciphers and transposition ciphers.
- Substitution ciphers are those that replace each symbol or group of symbols in the plaintext with another symbol or group of symbols in the ciphertext. Examples of substitution ciphers are Caesar cipher, monoalphabetic cipher, and polyalphabetic cipher.
- Transposition ciphers are those that rearrange the symbols or groups of symbols in the plaintext to form the ciphertext. Examples of transposition ciphers are rail fence cipher, columnar transposition cipher, and permutation cipher.

## Cryptanalysis
- Cryptanalysis is the art and science of breaking the encryption and decryption methods and revealing the plaintext or the key.
- Cryptanalysis can be classified into two categories: ciphertext-only attack and known-plaintext attack.
- Ciphertext-only attack is the attack where the cryptanalyst only has access to the ciphertext and tries to find the plaintext or the key. Examples of ciphertext-only attack methods are frequency analysis, brute force, and pattern matching.
- Known-plaintext attack is the attack where the cryptanalyst has access to some pairs of plaintext and ciphertext and tries to find the key or the encryption method. Examples of known-plaintext attack methods are linear cryptanalysis, differential cryptanalysis, and chosen-plaintext attack.
