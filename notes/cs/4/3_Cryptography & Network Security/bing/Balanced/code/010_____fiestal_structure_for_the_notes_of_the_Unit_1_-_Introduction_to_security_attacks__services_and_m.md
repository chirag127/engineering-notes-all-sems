## Security Attacks, Services and Mechanism
- Security attacks are any actions that compromise the security of information or systems.
- Security services are the countermeasures that provide protection against security attacks.
- Security mechanisms are the methods or tools that implement security services.
- Security attacks can be classified into two categories: passive attacks and active attacks.
  - Passive attacks are those that do not alter the data or system, but only observe or analyze them. Examples are eavesdropping, traffic analysis, or ciphertext-only attack.
  - Active attacks are those that modify the data or system, or create false data or messages. Examples are replay, modification, deletion, insertion, or man-in-the-middle attack.
- Security services can be classified into five categories: confidentiality, integrity, availability, authentication, and non-repudiation.
  - Confidentiality is the service that ensures that the data or system is accessible only to authorized parties. Examples are encryption, access control, or secret sharing.
  - Integrity is the service that ensures that the data or system is not modified or corrupted by unauthorized parties. Examples are checksum, digital signature, or hash function.
  - Availability is the service that ensures that the data or system is accessible and usable by authorized parties when needed. Examples are redundancy, backup, or fault tolerance.
  - Authentication is the service that ensures that the identity of a party or the origin of a message is verified. Examples are password, biometric, or digital certificate.
  - Non-repudiation is the service that ensures that a party cannot deny having sent or received a message. Examples are digital signature, timestamp, or audit trail.
- Security mechanisms can be classified into two categories: preventive mechanisms and detective mechanisms.
  - Preventive mechanisms are those that prevent or deter security attacks from happening. Examples are encryption, access control, or firewall.
  - Detective mechanisms are those that detect or identify security attacks that have happened or are happening. Examples are intrusion detection, audit, or alarm.

## Classical Encryption Techniques
- Encryption is the process of transforming plaintext (readable data) into ciphertext (unreadable data) using a secret key.
- Decryption is the reverse process of encryption, transforming ciphertext back into plaintext using the same or a different secret key.
- Cryptography is the study of encryption and decryption techniques and their applications.
- Cryptanalysis is the study of breaking encryption techniques and recovering plaintext or key from ciphertext.
- Cryptology is the combination of cryptography and cryptanalysis.
- Classical encryption techniques are the encryption techniques that were developed before the advent of computers. They are based on simple mathematical operations and can be performed by hand or simple machines.
- Classical encryption techniques can be classified into two categories: substitution ciphers and transposition ciphers.
  - Substitution ciphers are those that replace each letter or symbol of the plaintext with a different letter or symbol of the ciphertext according to a fixed rule or a key. Examples are Caesar cipher, monoalphabetic cipher, or polyalphabetic cipher.
  - Transposition ciphers are those that rearrange the order of the letters or symbols of the plaintext to form the ciphertext according to a fixed rule or a key. Examples are rail fence cipher, columnar cipher, or permutation cipher.
- Cryptanalysis of classical encryption techniques can be performed by various methods, such as frequency analysis, known-plaintext attack, chosen-plaintext attack, or brute-force attack.
