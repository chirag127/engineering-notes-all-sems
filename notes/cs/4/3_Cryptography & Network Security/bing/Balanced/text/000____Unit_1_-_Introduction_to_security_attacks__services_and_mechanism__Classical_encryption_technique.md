- Security attacks are any actions that compromise the confidentiality, integrity, or availability of information or resources in a system.
- Security services are the countermeasures that protect the system from security attacks, such as authentication, encryption, digital signatures, access control, etc.
- Security mechanisms are the specific methods or algorithms that implement the security services, such as cryptographic algorithms, protocols, firewalls, etc.
- Classical encryption techniques are the methods of transforming plaintext into ciphertext using simple mathematical operations, such as substitution and transposition.
- Substitution ciphers are the methods of replacing each letter or symbol in the plaintext with another letter or symbol, according to a fixed rule or key. For example, Caesar cipher, monoalphabetic cipher, polyalphabetic cipher, etc.
- Transposition ciphers are the methods of rearranging the order of the letters or symbols in the plaintext, according to a fixed rule or key. For example, rail fence cipher, columnar transposition cipher, etc.
- Cryptanalysis is the science of breaking the encryption schemes and recovering the plaintext from the ciphertext, without knowing the key or the algorithm. Cryptanalysis can be based on different types of attacks, such as ciphertext-only, known-plaintext, chosen-plaintext, chosen-ciphertext, etc.
- Steganography is the art of hiding the existence of a message within another message or medium, such as an image, audio, video, etc. Steganography can be combined with encryption to provide more security.
- Stream ciphers are the methods of encrypting each bit or symbol of the plaintext with a bit or symbol of a keystream, which is generated by a pseudorandom number generator (PRNG) using a secret key. Stream ciphers are fast and suitable for continuous data streams, such as voice or video. For example, RC4, A5/1, etc.
- Block ciphers are the methods of encrypting a fixed-length block of plaintext with a secret key, producing a block of ciphertext of the same length. Block ciphers are more complex and suitable for discrete data, such as text or files. For example, DES, AES, etc.
- Block cipher principles are the basic concepts and properties that a block cipher should have, such as block size, key size, number of rounds, round function, key schedule, etc.
- Shannon’s theory of confusion and diffusion is a framework for designing secure block ciphers, proposed by Claude Shannon. Confusion means that the relationship between the plaintext and the ciphertext should be complex and obscure, making it hard to find the key. Diffusion means that each bit of the plaintext should affect many bits of the ciphertext, making it hard to find the plaintext.
- Fiestel structure is a common design for block ciphers, proposed by Horst Feistel. It consists of dividing the block into two halves, and applying a round function to one half, which depends on the key and the other half. Then, the two halves are swapped. This process is repeated for a number of rounds, and the final swap is undone. For example, DES, Blowfish, etc.
- Data Encryption Standard (DES) is a widely used block cipher, standardized by NIST in 1977. It has a block size of 64 bits, a key size of 56 bits, and 16 rounds of fiestel structure. DES is considered insecure today, due to its small key size and the advances in cryptanalysis and computing power.
- Strength of DES depends on the security of the key and the resistance to various attacks. The key space of DES is 2^56, which is too small for brute force attacks. DES is also vulnerable to differential cryptanalysis, linear cryptanalysis, and other techniques that exploit its weaknesses.
- Idea of differential cryptanalysis is a method of breaking block ciphers, based on analyzing the differences between pairs of plaintexts and ciphertexts, and finding patterns that reveal the key. Differential cryptanalysis can be applied to DES and other fiestel ciphers, as well as some stream ciphers.
