### Classical encryption techniques: substitution ciphers and transposition ciphers

- Classical encryption techniques are based on two basic components: substitution and transposition.
- Substitution ciphers replace each plaintext symbol with a different ciphertext symbol, according to a fixed rule or key. For example, Caesar cipher, monoalphabetic cipher, polyalphabetic cipher, etc.
- Transposition ciphers rearrange the order of the plaintext symbols, according to a fixed rule or key. For example, rail fence cipher, columnar transposition cipher, etc.
- Both substitution and transposition ciphers can be combined to create more complex encryption schemes, such as product ciphers.
- The main goal of classical encryption techniques is to provide confidentiality, i.e., to prevent unauthorized parties from reading the plaintext message.
- The main drawback of classical encryption techniques is that they are vulnerable to cryptanalysis, i.e., the process of finding the key or the plaintext from the ciphertext, using various techniques such as frequency analysis, brute force, known plaintext, chosen plaintext, etc.

### Cryptanalysis

- Cryptanalysis is the science of breaking encryption schemes, i.e., finding the key or the plaintext from the ciphertext, without knowing the encryption algorithm or the key.
- Cryptanalysis can be classified into two types: ciphertext-only attack and known-plaintext attack.
- Ciphertext-only attack is when the attacker only has access to the ciphertext, and tries to find the key or the plaintext by analyzing the statistical properties of the ciphertext, such as frequency distribution, patterns, repetitions, etc.
- Known-plaintext attack is when the attacker has access to some pairs of plaintext and ciphertext, and tries to find the key or the plaintext by exploiting the relationship between them, such as algebraic equations, linear equations, etc.
- Cryptanalysis can be further classified into two types: classical cryptanalysis and modern cryptanalysis.
- Classical cryptanalysis is based on the mathematical properties of the encryption algorithm, such as modular arithmetic, linear algebra, number theory, etc. For example, Caesar cipher can be broken by frequency analysis, monoalphabetic cipher can be broken by Kasiski test, etc.
- Modern cryptanalysis is based on the computational complexity of the encryption algorithm, such as time complexity, space complexity, algorithmic complexity, etc. For example, DES can be broken by differential cryptanalysis, AES can be broken by linear cryptanalysis, etc.

### Steganography

- Steganography is the art of hiding information in another medium, such as text, image, audio, video, etc., without altering the appearance or functionality of the medium.
- Steganography can be used to conceal the existence of a message, rather than its content, from unauthorized parties.
- Steganography can be combined with encryption to provide both confidentiality and secrecy, i.e., to prevent unauthorized parties from reading or detecting the message.
- Steganography can be classified into two types: technical steganography and linguistic steganography.
- Technical steganography is based on the physical properties of the medium, such as noise, redundancy, compression, etc. For example, LSB (least significant bit) steganography, DCT (discrete cosine transform) steganography, etc.
- Linguistic steganography is based on the semantic properties of the medium, such as syntax, grammar, vocabulary, etc. For example, acrostic steganography, null cipher steganography, etc.

### Stream and block ciphers

- Stream and block ciphers are two types of symmetric-key encryption schemes, i.e., schemes that use the same key for encryption and decryption.
- Stream ciphers encrypt each plaintext symbol (bit or byte) with a different key symbol, generated by a pseudorandom number generator (PRNG) or a keystream generator, according to a fixed rule or algorithm. For example, RC4, A5/1, etc.
- Block ciphers encrypt each plaintext block (fixed-length group of symbols) with the same key, according to a fixed rule or algorithm. For example, DES, AES, etc.
- The main advantage of stream ciphers is that they are fast and simple to implement, and can provide high security if the keystream is truly random and never reused.
- The main disadvantage of stream ciphers is that they are vulnerable to bit-flipping attacks, i.e., changing a single bit in the ciphertext can change the corresponding bit in the plaintext, without affecting the rest of the message.
- The main advantage of block ciphers is that they are more resistant to bit-flipping attacks, and can provide various modes of operation, such as ECB (electronic codebook), CBC (cipher block chaining), CTR (counter), etc., to enhance the security and functionality of the encryption scheme.
