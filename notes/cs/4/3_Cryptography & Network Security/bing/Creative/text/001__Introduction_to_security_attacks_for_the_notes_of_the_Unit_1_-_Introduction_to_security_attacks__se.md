### Introduction to security attacks

- A security attack is any action that compromises the security of information owned by an organization or an individual.
- Security attacks can be classified into two categories: passive attacks and active attacks.
- Passive attacks are those that do not affect the normal functioning of the system, but only attempt to access or monitor the information. Examples of passive attacks are eavesdropping, traffic analysis, and ciphertext-only attacks.
- Active attacks are those that alter or disrupt the normal functioning of the system, or create false information. Examples of active attacks are modification, replay, masquerade, denial of service, and chosen-plaintext attacks.
- Security attacks can also be classified based on the source of the attack: insider attacks and outsider attacks.
- Insider attacks are those that are launched by authorized users of the system, who abuse their privileges or exploit their knowledge of the system. Examples of insider attacks are disgruntled employees, malicious insiders, or compromised accounts.
- Outsider attacks are those that are launched by unauthorized users of the system, who exploit the vulnerabilities or weaknesses of the system. Examples of outsider attacks are hackers, crackers, or cybercriminals.
- Security attacks can have various impacts on the system, such as confidentiality breach, integrity breach, availability breach, authenticity breach, or non-repudiation breach.
- Confidentiality breach is the unauthorized disclosure of information to an unauthorized party.
- Integrity breach is the unauthorized modification or deletion of information by an unauthorized party.
- Availability breach is the unauthorized prevention of access to or use of information or resources by an authorized party.
- Authenticity breach is the unauthorized impersonation of an authorized party or the creation of false information by an unauthorized party.
- Non-repudiation breach is the unauthorized denial of involvement in a communication or transaction by an authorized party.
