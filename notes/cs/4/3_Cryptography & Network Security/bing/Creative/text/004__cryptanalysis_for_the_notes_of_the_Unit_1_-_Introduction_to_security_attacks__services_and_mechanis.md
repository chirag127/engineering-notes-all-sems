### Cryptanalysis

Cryptanalysis is the study of ciphertext, ciphers and cryptosystems with the aim of understanding how they work and finding and improving techniques for defeating or weakening them. Cryptanalysis is also the art or process of deciphering coded messages without being told the key. Cryptanalysis is an important aspect of cryptology, the mathematical study of codes, ciphers and related algorithms.

Some of the topics covered in cryptanalysis are:

- Classical encryption techniques: These include substitution ciphers and transposition ciphers, which are methods of encrypting plaintext by replacing or rearranging the letters or symbols. Cryptanalysis of these techniques involves finding patterns, frequencies, anagrams, or other clues that can reveal the key or the plaintext.
- Steganography: This is the practice of hiding information within other information, such as images, audio, or text. Cryptanalysis of steganography involves detecting the presence of hidden information and extracting it.
- Stream and block ciphers: These are modern encryption techniques that use binary sequences or blocks of bits to encrypt plaintext. Stream ciphers encrypt each bit of plaintext with a bit of a key stream, while block ciphers encrypt fixed-size blocks of plaintext with a key. Cryptanalysis of these techniques involves finding weaknesses in the design or implementation of the ciphers, such as linear or differential cryptanalysis, or exploiting modes of operation, such as chosen-plaintext or ciphertext-only attacks.
- Modern block ciphers: These are block ciphers that use complex mathematical functions and structures to achieve high security and efficiency. Some examples are Data Encryption Standard (DES), Triple DES, and Advanced Encryption Standard (AES). Cryptanalysis of these ciphers involves finding attacks that can reduce the effective key size, break the fiestal structure, or exploit the confusion and diffusion properties of the ciphers.
