### Services and Mechanisms

- Services and mechanisms are two important concepts in cryptography and network security.
- A **service** is a functionality or a feature that enhances the security of the data processing systems and the information transfers of an organization. It is intended to counter security attacks and to provide protection to the users and applications.
- A **mechanism** is a method, tool, or procedure that is designed to detect, prevent, or recover from a security attack. It is used to implement one or more security services.
- There are different types of security services and mechanisms, depending on the goals and requirements of the security system. Some of the common ones are:

  - **Confidentiality**: It ensures that the information in a computer system and transmitted information are accessible only for reading by authorized parties. It prevents unauthorized disclosure of data. Encryption and decryption are the main mechanisms for providing confidentiality.
  - **Authentication**: It ensures that the origin of a message or electronic document is correctly identified, with an assurance that the identity is not false. It verifies the identity of the communicating entities. Passwords, digital signatures, certificates, and biometrics are some of the mechanisms for providing authentication.
  - **Integrity**: It ensures that only authorized parties are able to modify computer system assets and transmitted information. It prevents unauthorized alteration of data. Hash functions, message authentication codes, and digital signatures are some of the mechanisms for providing integrity.
  - **Non-repudiation**: It requires that neither the sender nor the receiver of a message be able to deny the transmission. It provides proof of the origin and delivery of data. Digital signatures, timestamps, and receipts are some of the mechanisms for providing non-repudiation.
  - **Access control**: It requires that access to information resources may be controlled by or the target system. It prevents unauthorized use of a resource. Access control lists, firewalls, and encryption are some of the mechanisms for providing access control.
  - **Availability**: It requires that computer system assets be available to authorized parties when needed. It prevents denial of service attacks. Backup, redundancy, and load balancing are some of the mechanisms for providing availability.

- As shown in the figure, a security service may use one or more security mechanisms to achieve its goal. For example, confidentiality may use encryption and decryption, while authentication may use passwords and digital signatures. A security mechanism may also support more than one security service. For example, digital signatures can provide both authentication and non-repudiation.
