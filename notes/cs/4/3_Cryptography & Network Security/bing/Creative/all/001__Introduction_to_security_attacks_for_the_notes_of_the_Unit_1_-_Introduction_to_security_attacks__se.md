- Security attacks are any actions that compromise the security of information or systems. They can be classified into two types: passive attacks and active attacks.
  - Passive attacks are those that do not affect the normal functioning of the system, but only attempt to eavesdrop or analyze the data. Examples are traffic analysis, monitoring of transmissions, and ciphertext-only attacks.
  - Active attacks are those that involve some modification of the data or the creation of false data. Examples are replay, masquerade, modification of messages, and denial of service.
- Security services are the measures that are taken to counter security attacks and provide protection to the information and systems. They can be classified into five categories: confidentiality, integrity, availability, authentication, and non-repudiation.
  - Confidentiality is the service that ensures that the information is accessible only to those who are authorized to access it. It can be achieved by encryption, access control, and physical protection.
  - Integrity is the service that ensures that the information is not altered or destroyed in an unauthorized manner. It can be achieved by checksums, digital signatures, and backup systems.
  - Availability is the service that ensures that the information and systems are accessible and usable by the authorized parties when needed. It can be achieved by redundancy, fault tolerance, and recovery mechanisms.
  - Authentication is the service that ensures that the identity of a party is verified before granting access or exchanging information. It can be achieved by passwords, biometrics, certificates, and challenge-response protocols.
  - Non-repudiation is the service that ensures that a party cannot deny having performed an action or having sent or received a message. It can be achieved by digital signatures, timestamps, and audit trails.
- Security mechanisms are the tools or methods that are used to implement security services. They can be classified into two types: preventive mechanisms and detective mechanisms.
  - Preventive mechanisms are those that aim to prevent security attacks from happening or succeeding. Examples are encryption, authentication, access control, and firewalls.
  - Detective mechanisms are those that aim to detect security attacks that have occurred or are occurring. Examples are intrusion detection systems, antivirus software, and audit logs.
- Classical encryption techniques are the methods of encryption that were used before the advent of modern cryptography. They are based on simple mathematical operations and can be divided into two types: substitution ciphers and transposition ciphers.
  - Substitution ciphers are those that replace each letter or symbol of the plaintext with another letter or symbol, according to a fixed rule or key. Examples are Caesar cipher, monoalphabetic cipher, and polyalphabetic cipher.
  - Transposition ciphers are those that rearrange the order of the letters or symbols of the plaintext, according to a fixed rule or key. Examples are rail fence cipher, columnar transposition cipher, and permutation cipher.
- Cryptanalysis is the science of breaking encryption schemes, either by finding the key or by finding a weakness in the algorithm. It can be classified into four types: ciphertext-only attack, known-plaintext attack, chosen-plaintext attack, and chosen-ciphertext attack.
  - Ciphertext-only attack is the type of attack where the attacker only has access to one or more ciphertexts, and tries to find the key or the plaintext. This is the most difficult type of attack, and requires a lot of computational power and statistical analysis.
  - Known-plaintext attack is the type of attack where the attacker has access to one or more pairs of plaintext and ciphertext, and tries to find the key or the algorithm. This type of attack is easier than ciphertext-only attack, and requires less computational power and statistical analysis.
  - Chosen-plaintext attack is the type of attack where the attacker can choose one or more plaintexts and obtain the corresponding ciphertexts, and tries to find the key or the algorithm. This type of attack is easier than known-plaintext attack, and requires less computational power and statistical analysis.
  - Chosen-ciphertext attack is the type of attack where the attacker can choose one or more ciphertexts and obtain the corresponding plaintexts, and tries to find the key or the algorithm.
