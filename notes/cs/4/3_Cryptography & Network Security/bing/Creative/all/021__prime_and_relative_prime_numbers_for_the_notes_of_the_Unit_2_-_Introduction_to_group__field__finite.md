### Prime and Relative Prime Numbers

- A prime number is a natural number greater than 1 that has no positive divisors other than 1 and itself. For example, 2, 3, 5, 7, 11, 13, 17, etc. are prime numbers.
- A relative prime number (or a coprime number) is a natural number that shares no common positive factors (except 1) with another natural number. For example, 8 and 15 are relative prime numbers, because their only common factor is 1. However, 8 and 12 are not relative prime numbers, because they have common factors 2 and 4.
- Prime and relative prime numbers are important in cryptography, especially in public key cryptography, because they have some special properties that make them hard to break.
- One of the most widely used public key cryptography algorithms is RSA, which relies on the fact that it is easy to multiply two large prime numbers, but very hard to factor their product into the original primes. This is called the integer factorization problem, and there is no known efficient algorithm to solve it for large numbers.

This number n is part of the public key, along with another number e that is chosen to be relatively prime to (p-1) * (q-1). The private key consists of another number d that is the multiplicative inverse of e modulo (p-1) * (q-1), meaning that e * d = 1 (mod (p-1) * (q-1)).

To decrypt the ciphertext c using the private key d, one has to compute m = c^d (mod n). Therefore, the prime numbers p and q have to be chosen to be large enough (typically hundreds of digits) to resist factorization attacks.
