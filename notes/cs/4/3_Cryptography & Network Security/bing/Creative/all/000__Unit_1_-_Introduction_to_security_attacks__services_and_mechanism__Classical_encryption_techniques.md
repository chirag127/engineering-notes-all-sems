- Security attacks are any actions that compromise the security of information owned by an organization or an individual. They can be classified as passive or active, depending on whether they attempt to read or modify the data. Examples of passive attacks are eavesdropping, traffic analysis, and ciphertext-only attacks. Examples of active attacks are replay, modification, masquerade, and chosen-plaintext attacks.
- Security services are the capabilities that are provided by a system to counter security attacks and enhance the security of data processing systems and information transfers. They can be classified into five categories: confidentiality, integrity, availability, authentication, and non-repudiation. Examples of security services are encryption, digital signatures, access control, firewalls, and backups.
- Security mechanisms are the methods, tools, or procedures that are used to implement security services. They can be classified into two types: preventive and reactive. Preventive mechanisms are designed to deter or prevent security attacks from happening, such as encryption, authentication, and access control. Reactive mechanisms are designed to detect, recover, or correct security violations that have occurred, such as intrusion detection, auditing, and backups.
- Classical encryption techniques are the methods of encrypting plaintext messages using simple mathematical operations and transformations. They can be divided into two types: substitution ciphers and transposition ciphers. Substitution ciphers replace each letter or symbol of the plaintext with another letter or symbol, according to a fixed rule or key. Transposition ciphers rearrange the order of the letters or symbols of the plaintext, according to a fixed pattern or key. Examples of substitution ciphers are Caesar cipher, monoalphabetic cipher, and polyalphabetic cipher. Examples of transposition ciphers are rail fence cipher, columnar transposition cipher, and permutation cipher.
- Cryptanalysis is the science of breaking ciphertexts, that is, recovering the plaintext or the key from a given ciphertext, without knowing the encryption algorithm or the key. Cryptanalysis can be performed using various techniques, such as frequency analysis, brute force, known-plaintext, chosen-plaintext, chosen-ciphertext, and differential cryptanalysis. The strength of an encryption algorithm depends on its resistance to cryptanalysis.
- Steganography is the art of hiding information in other information, such as images, audio, video, or text. The goal of steganography is to conceal the existence of the hidden information, so that only the intended recipient can detect and extract it. Steganography can be used to enhance the security and confidentiality of information, or to bypass censorship and surveillance. Examples of steganography techniques are least significant bit (LSB) insertion, masking and filtering, and spread spectrum.
- Stream and block ciphers are two types of symmetric encryption algorithms, that is, algorithms that use the same key for encryption and decryption. Stream ciphers encrypt each bit or byte of the plaintext individually, using a keystream that is generated from the key and an initialization vector (IV). Block ciphers encrypt each block of the plaintext, usually 64 or 128 bits, using a key and a mode of operation. Examples of stream ciphers are RC4, A5/1, and Salsa20. Examples of block ciphers are DES, AES, and Blowfish.

  - Confusion and diffusion: These are the two properties that a block cipher should have, according to Shannon's theory.
