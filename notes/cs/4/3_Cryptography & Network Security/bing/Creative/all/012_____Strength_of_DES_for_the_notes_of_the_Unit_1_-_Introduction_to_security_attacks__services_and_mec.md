# Strength of DES

Data Encryption Standard (DES) is a symmetric key block cipher algorithm that was adopted as a federal standard in 1977. It uses a 56-bit key to encrypt or decrypt 64-bit blocks of data. DES is based on a Feistel structure, which involves repeated application of a function that operates on half of the data block and uses a subkey derived from the main key. DES performs 16 rounds of this function, each with a different subkey. The algorithm also involves an initial and a final permutation of the data block.

The strength of DES depends on two factors: the key size and the nature of the algorithm.

- The key size of 56 bits is considered too small by today's standards, as it can be brute-forced by modern computers in a reasonable amount of time. For example, in 1998, a machine called DES Cracker was built by the Electronic Frontier Foundation (EFF) that could break a DES key in 56 hours. In 1999, a distributed computing project called distributed.net broke a DES key in 22 hours and 15 minutes. The key size of DES can be increased by using multiple keys, such as in Triple DES (3DES), which applies DES three times with two or three different keys, resulting in an effective key size of 112 or 168 bits. However, 3DES is also considered obsolete, as it is slower and more vulnerable to certain attacks than newer algorithms.

- The nature of the algorithm refers to the design and implementation of the function, the subkey generation, the permutations, and the Feistel structure. The algorithm has been extensively analyzed and tested for various types of attacks, such as differential cryptanalysis, linear cryptanalysis, related-key attacks, and chosen-plaintext attacks. The algorithm has been found to be resistant to most of these attacks, as long as the key is not weak or semi-weak. A weak key is a key that produces the same subkeys for encryption and decryption, resulting in no encryption at all. A semi-weak key is a key that produces a pair of subkeys that are inverses of each other, resulting in easy decryption. There are 16 weak keys and 48 semi-weak keys in DES, out of a total of 2^56 possible keys. These keys should be avoided when using DES.

In conclusion, DES is a historic and influential algorithm that has been widely used and studied for decades, but it is no longer considered secure or efficient by modern standards. Newer algorithms, such as Advanced Encryption Standard (AES), offer higher security and performance than DES.
