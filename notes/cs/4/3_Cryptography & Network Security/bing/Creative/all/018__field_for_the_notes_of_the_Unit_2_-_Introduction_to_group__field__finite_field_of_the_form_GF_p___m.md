- A field is a mathematical structure that allows us to perform four basic operations: addition, subtraction, multiplication and division, with some properties such as closure, associativity, commutativity, distributivity, identity and inverse .
- A field is useful in cryptography because it provides a way to manipulate data and create hard problems that can be used for encryption, decryption, digital signatures, key exchange and other cryptographic tasks .
- A finite field is a field that has a finite number of elements. A finite field of order p, where p is a prime number, is denoted by GF(p) or Fp. It consists of the integers from 0 to p-1, with addition and multiplication performed modulo p .
- A finite field of order p^n, where n is a positive integer, is denoted by GF(p^n) or Fp^n. It consists of polynomials of degree less than n, with coefficients from GF(p), with addition and multiplication performed modulo a fixed irreducible polynomial of degree n over GF(p) .
- A finite field is useful in cryptography because it can be efficiently implemented in hardware and software, and it has many hard problems such as discrete logarithm, elliptic curve and lattice problems that can be used for public key cryptography .
- Modular arithmetic is a system of arithmetic where numbers wrap around after reaching a certain value, called the modulus. For example, in modulo 12 arithmetic, 10 + 5 = 3, because 15 is equivalent to 3 modulo 12.
- Modular arithmetic is useful in cryptography because it allows us to perform operations on large numbers without overflow, and it also creates hard problems such as factoring and RSA problem that can be used for public key cryptography .
- A prime number is a natural number greater than 1 that has no positive divisors other than 1 and itself. For example, 2, 3, 5, 7, 11 are prime numbers.
- A relative prime number or a coprime number is a natural number that has no common positive divisors with another natural number other than 1. For example, 8 and 15 are relative prime numbers, because their only common divisor is 1.
- Prime numbers and relative prime numbers are useful in cryptography because they are the building blocks of many finite fields and groups, and they also create hard problems such as primality testing and finding large primes that can be used for public key cryptography .
- The Extended Euclidean Algorithm is an algorithm that computes the greatest common divisor (gcd) of two natural numbers, and also finds two integers such that their linear combination equals the gcd. For example, the gcd of 30 and 18 is 6, and the Extended Euclidean Algorithm can find x = 2 and y = -3 such that 30x + 18y = 6.
- The Extended Euclidean Algorithm is useful in cryptography because it can be used to find the multiplicative inverse of an element in a finite field or a group, which is needed for many cryptographic operations such as decryption and signature verification .
- The Advanced Encryption Standard (AES) is a symmetric-key block cipher that encrypts and decrypts data in blocks of 128 bits, using a secret key of 128, 192 or 256 bits. It consists of several rounds of substitution, permutation, mixing and key addition operations.
- The AES is useful in cryptography because it is a fast and secure cipher that can be used for data confidentiality, integrity and authentication. It is also resistant to many known attacks such as linear and differential cryptanalysis .
