### Discrete Logarithmic Problem for the notes of the Unit 2 - Introduction to group, field, finite field of the form GF(p), modular arithmetic, prime and relative prime numbers, Extended Euclidean Algorithm, Advanced Encryption Standard (AES) encryption and decryptionFermat

- The discrete logarithmic problem is defined as: given a group G, a generator g of the group and an element h of G, to find the discrete logarithm to the base g of h in the group G.
- A discrete logarithm is an integer k that solves the equation g^k = h, where g and h are elements of a group G.
- The group operation is denoted by multiplication and the identity element by 1. For example, in the group Z_p*, where p is a prime number, the group operation is modular multiplication and the identity element is 1.
- A generator of a group G is an element g that can generate all the other elements of G by raising g to different powers. For example, in the group Z_7*, the element 3 is a generator, because 3^0 = 1, 3^1 = 3, 3^2 = 2, 3^3 = 6, 3^4 = 4, 3^5 = 5, and 3^6 = 1 (mod 7).
- The discrete logarithm problem is not always hard. The hardness of finding discrete logarithms depends on the groups. For some groups, such as the group of powers of 10, the discrete logarithm problem is easy to solve. For example, log_10 1000 = 3, and log_10 0.001 = -3.
- However, for some other groups, such as the group Z_p*, where p is a large prime number, the discrete logarithm problem is considered to be computationally intractable. That is, no efficient classical algorithm is known for computing discrete logarithms in general .
- Several important algorithms in public-key cryptography, such as ElGamal, base their security on the assumption that the discrete logarithm problem over carefully chosen groups has no efficient solution.
- A mnemonic to remember the definition of the discrete logarithm problem is: **G**ive me **g**roup **G**, **g**enerator **g** and element **h**, find **k** such that **g** to the power of **k** equals **h**. The bold letters correspond to the symbols in the definition.
- A learning trick to understand the discrete logarithm problem is to compare it with the inverse of exponentiation. Exponentiation is the operation of raising a base to a power, such as 2^3 = 8. The inverse of exponentiation is finding the power given the base and the result, such as log_2 8 = 3. The discrete logarithm problem is similar, but instead of using real numbers, it uses elements of a group, such as 3^2 = 2 (mod 7). The inverse of this operation is finding the power given the generator and the element, such as log_3 2 = 2 (mod 7).
