## Unit 1 - Introduction, issues in mobile computing, overview of wireless telephony: cellular concept, GSM:

- Mobile computing is the ability to use computing devices and applications that can move with the user, communicate wirelessly, and access data and services over the internet or a network.
- Some of the issues in mobile computing are:
  - Limited battery life and power consumption of mobile devices.
  - Limited bandwidth and network coverage of wireless networks.
  - Security and privacy of data and communication over wireless channels.
  - Heterogeneity and interoperability of mobile devices, platforms, and standards.
  - Mobility management and location awareness of mobile users and devices.
  - Adaptation and optimization of applications and services for mobile environments.
- Wireless telephony is the technology that enables voice communication over wireless networks, such as cellular networks, satellite networks, and radio networks.
- Cellular concept is the idea of dividing a geographical area into smaller cells, each served by a base station, to increase the capacity and coverage of wireless networks.
- GSM (Global System for Mobile Communications) is a standard for digital cellular networks that operates in the 900 MHz and 1800 MHz bands. It supports voice, data, and multimedia services, and uses a combination of frequency division multiple access (FDMA) and time division multiple access (TDMA) to allocate channels to users.
- Some of the features of GSM are:
  - SIM (Subscriber Identity Module) card that stores the user's identity, authentication, and subscription information.
  - IMEI (International Mobile Equipment Identity) number that uniquely identifies the mobile device.
  - MSISDN (Mobile Station International Subscriber Directory Number) that is the user's phone number.
  - PLMN (Public Land Mobile Network) that is the network operator that provides the GSM service.
  - HLR (Home Location Register) that is the database that stores the user's profile and location information.
  - VLR (Visitor Location Register) that is the database that temporarily stores the user's information when roaming in another network.
  - MSC (Mobile Switching Center) that is the node that connects the base stations to the core network and handles the call routing and switching.
  - BSC (Base Station Controller) that is the node that controls and manages the base stations in a cell cluster.
  - BTS (Base Transceiver Station) that is the node that transmits and receives the radio signals to and from the mobile devices in a cell.
  - EIR (Equipment Identity Register) that is the database that stores the IMEI numbers of the valid and blacklisted devices.
  - AuC (Authentication Center) that is the node that performs the authentication and encryption of the user and the device.
