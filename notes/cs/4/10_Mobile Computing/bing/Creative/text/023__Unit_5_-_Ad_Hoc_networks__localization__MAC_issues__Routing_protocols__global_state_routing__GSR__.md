## Unit 5 - Ad Hoc networks, localization, MAC issues, Routing protocols, global state routing (GSR)

- Ad hoc networks are wireless networks that do not rely on any fixed infrastructure or centralized control. They consist of mobile nodes that communicate with each other over wireless links.
- Localization is the process of determining the position of a node in an ad hoc network, either relative to other nodes or to a global coordinate system. Localization can be achieved by using GPS, anchor nodes, or distributed algorithms based on distance or angle measurements.
- MAC issues refer to the challenges of designing medium access control protocols for ad hoc networks, such as collision avoidance, channel access, power control, and fairness. MAC protocols can be classified into contention-based, reservation-based, or hybrid schemes.
- Routing protocols are algorithms that enable nodes to discover and maintain routes to other nodes in an ad hoc network. Routing protocols can be categorized into proactive, reactive, or hybrid approaches.
  - Proactive or table-driven protocols maintain fresh lists of destinations and their routes by periodically distributing routing tables throughout the network. Examples of proactive protocols are DSDV, OLSR, and GSR.
  - Reactive or on-demand protocols find a route on demand by flooding the network with Route Request packets. Examples of reactive protocols are DSR, AODV, and TORA.
  - Hybrid protocols combine the features of proactive and reactive protocols to achieve a balance between routing overhead and route freshness. Examples of hybrid protocols are ZRP, CEDAR, and EIGRP.
- Global state routing (GSR) is a proactive routing protocol that uses link state information to construct routing tables. Each node maintains a global view of the network topology and periodically broadcasts its link state to all other nodes. GSR reduces the routing overhead by using a hierarchical structure and a reliable flooding mechanism. However, GSR suffers from scalability issues and frequent link state updates due to node mobility.
