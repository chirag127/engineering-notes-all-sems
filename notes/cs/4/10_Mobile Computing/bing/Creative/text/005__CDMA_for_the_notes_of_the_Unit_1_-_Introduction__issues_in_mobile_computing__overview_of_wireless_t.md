### CDMA

CDMA stands for Code Division Multiple Access. It is one of the two second-generation systems for cellular phones available today. The other one is GSM (Global System for Mobiles). CDMA uses a "spread-spectrum" technique to allow multiple users to share the same frequency band. CDMA has some advantages over GSM, such as higher data speeds and better coverage in rural areas, but also some disadvantages, such as less compatibility with other networks and devices.

Some of the main points about CDMA are:

- CDMA was originally designed by Qualcomm in the U.S. and is primarily used in the U.S. and portions of Asia by carriers such as Verizon, Sprint and Virgin Mobile .
- CDMA uses a "spread-spectrum" technique whereby electromagnetic energy is spread to allow for a signal with a wider bandwidth. This approach allows several people on different cell phones to be "multiplexed" over the same channel to share a bandwidth of frequencies  .
- With CDMA technology, data and voice packets are separated using codes and then transmitted using a wide frequency range. Since more space is often allocated for data with CDMA, this standard became attractive for high-speed mobile Internet use  .
- CDMA is a form of multiplexing, which allows numerous signals to occupy a single transmission channel, optimizing the use of available bandwidth.
- CDMA networks use information on the carrier's server to verify the user's identity and subscription, while GSM networks use removable SIM cards to store this information. This means that CDMA phones are not easily interchangeable with other networks and devices, while GSM phones are more flexible .
- CDMA networks are gradually phasing out as carriers worldwide switch to LTE, a single, global 4G standard that supports simultaneous voice and data use. LTE is also the basis for 5G, the next generation of wireless communications  .
