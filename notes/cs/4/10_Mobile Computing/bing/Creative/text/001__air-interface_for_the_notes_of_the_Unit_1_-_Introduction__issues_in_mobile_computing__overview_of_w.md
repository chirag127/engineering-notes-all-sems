### Air-interface

- Air-interface is the communication link between the two stations in mobile or wireless communication.
- Air-interface involves both the physical and data link layers (layer 1 and 2) of the OSI model for a connection.
- Air-interface is also called a "radio interface".

#### Physical layer

- The physical connection of an air-interface is generally radio-based.
- This is usually a point to point link between an active base station and a mobile station.
- Some types of wireless connections possess the ability to broadcast or multicast.
- Multiple links can be created in limited spectrum through FDMA, TDMA, or SDMA.
- Some advanced forms of transmission multiplexing combine frequency- and time-division approaches like OFDM or CDMA.
- The air-interface defines the frequency, channel bandwidth and modulation scheme.
- For example, TDMA and CDMA modulation are used in GSM and CDMA cellular networks respectively, while OFDMA is used for LTE.
- The air-interface also involves the use of antennas, which convert electric power into radio waves and vice versa.
- Antennas focus the radio waves in a certain direction and have a certain gain, which is the power in the strongest direction divided by the power that would be transmitted by an isotropic antenna.

#### Data link layer

- The data link layer in an air-interface is often divided farther than the simple Media access control (MAC) and Logical link control (LLC) sublayers found in other OSI terminology.
- The data link layer is responsible for error detection and correction, flow control, and framing of the data packets.
- The data link layer also handles the handoff process, which is the changeover of the active base station as a subscriber moves from one cell to another in the system.
- The data link layer may use different protocols depending on the air-interface technology, such as LAPDm for GSM, RLP for CDMA, or RLC for LTE.
