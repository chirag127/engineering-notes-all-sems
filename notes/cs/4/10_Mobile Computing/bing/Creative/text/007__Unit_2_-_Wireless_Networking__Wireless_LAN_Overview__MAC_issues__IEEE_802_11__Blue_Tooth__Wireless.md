## Unit 2 - Wireless Networking, Wireless LAN Overview: MAC issues, IEEE 802.11, Blue Tooth, Wireless

- Wireless networking is the communication of data between devices without using wires or cables. Wireless networking can provide mobility, flexibility, scalability, and cost-effectiveness for various applications and scenarios.
- Wireless LAN (WLAN) is a type of wireless network that connects devices within a limited area, such as a home, office, or campus. WLANs use radio waves or infrared signals to transmit and receive data over the air.
- MAC (Medium Access Control) issues are the challenges and solutions for coordinating the access of multiple devices to a shared wireless medium. MAC issues include how to avoid or resolve collisions, how to allocate bandwidth fairly and efficiently, how to handle hidden and exposed terminals, and how to support quality of service and security.
- IEEE 802.11 is a family of standards that define the physical and MAC layers of WLANs. IEEE 802.11 specifies various modulation schemes, data rates, frequency bands, and channel widths for different WLAN technologies, such as 802.11a, 802.11b, 802.11g, 802.11n, 802.11ac, and 802.11ax. IEEE 802.11 also defines the operation modes, frame formats, and management functions of WLANs.
- Blue Tooth is a wireless technology that enables short-range communication between devices, such as phones, headsets, keyboards, mice, printers, and speakers. Blue Tooth uses a frequency-hopping spread spectrum technique to avoid interference and provide security. Blue Tooth also defines various profiles and protocols for different applications and services, such as audio, file transfer, networking, and health.
- Wireless is a general term that refers to any type of communication that does not use wires or cables. Wireless can include WLANs, Blue Tooth, cellular networks, satellite networks, radio networks, and optical networks. Wireless can also refer to the devices, technologies, standards, and protocols that enable wireless communication.
