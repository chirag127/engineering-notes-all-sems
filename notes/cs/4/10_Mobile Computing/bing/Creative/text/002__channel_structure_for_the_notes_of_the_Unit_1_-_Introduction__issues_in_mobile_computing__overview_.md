### Channel structure

- A channel is a communication path between a cell phone and a base station in a cellular system.
- A channel can be divided into two types: downstream and upstream. Downstream channels carry signals from the base station to the cell phone, while upstream channels carry signals from the cell phone to the base station.
- Alternatively, a channel can use time division duplexing (TDD), which means that the same channel is used for both downstream and upstream signals, but at different times.
- A channel can also be divided into two categories: physical and logical. A physical channel is a set of radio frequencies or time slots that are used to transmit or receive signals. A logical channel is a function or purpose of a physical channel, such as carrying voice, data, or control information.
- A channel can be further divided into subchannels, which are smaller units of a channel that can carry different types of information. For example, a control channel can have subchannels for paging, access, and broadcast.
- A channel structure is the way that channels are organized and allocated in a cellular system. A channel structure can vary depending on the cellular technology, such as GSM, CDMA, or LTE.
- A channel structure can affect the capacity, quality, and efficiency of a cellular system. A channel structure should minimize interference, maximize frequency reuse, and optimize resource allocation.
- A channel structure can be based on different methods of multiplexing, which is the technique of combining multiple signals into one channel. Some common methods of multiplexing are frequency division multiplexing (FDM), time division multiplexing (TDM), code division multiplexing (CDM), and space division multiplexing (SDM).
