### Wireless applications for the notes of the Unit 2 - Wireless Networking, Wireless LAN Overview: MAC issues, IEEE 802.11, Blue Tooth, Wireless

Wireless applications are the software programs that run on wireless devices, such as mobile phones, tablets, laptops and smart watches, and use wireless networks, such as cellular, Wi-Fi and Bluetooth, to communicate with other devices and services. Wireless applications can provide various functions, such as voice, data, video, messaging, gaming, navigation, social networking, e-commerce and entertainment.

Some of the wireless applications that are relevant for the notes of the Unit 2 are:

- **Wireless Application Protocol (WAP)**: WAP is a specification for a set of communication protocols to standardize the way wireless devices can access internet resources, such as web pages, email, newsgroups and instant messaging. WAP uses a markup language called Wireless Markup Language (WML) to format web content for small screens and low bandwidth. WAP also uses a gateway server to translate WML into HTML and vice versa, and to compress and encrypt the data. WAP was introduced in 1999 and was widely used in the early 2000s, but it is now considered obsolete, as modern devices use more advanced web browsers and network technologies .

- **IEEE 802.11**: IEEE 802.11 is a family of standards for wireless local area networks (WLANs) that use radio waves to transmit data between devices within a limited range, such as a home, office or campus. IEEE 802.11 defines the physical layer and the medium access control (MAC) layer of the WLAN architecture, and specifies various parameters, such as frequency bands, modulation schemes, data rates, security protocols and quality of service. IEEE 802.11 has several versions, such as 802.11a, 802.11b, 802.11g, 802.11n, 802.11ac and 802.11ax, each with different features and performance. IEEE 802.11 is the most widely used wireless network technology, and it enables devices to access the internet, share files, stream media and perform other tasks wirelessly.

- **Bluetooth**: Bluetooth is a wireless technology for short-range personal area networks (PANs) that use radio waves to connect devices, such as headphones, speakers, keyboards, mice, printers, cameras and smartphones. Bluetooth enables devices to exchange data, voice and audio without cables or wires, and to form networks of up to eight devices, called piconets. Bluetooth also supports various profiles, such as hands-free, audio, object exchange, file transfer and human interface device, to provide specific functions and services. Bluetooth has several versions, such as Bluetooth 1.0, Bluetooth 2.0, Bluetooth 3.0, Bluetooth 4.0 and Bluetooth 5.0, each with different features and performance. Bluetooth is widely used for wireless communication, entertainment, health, fitness and smart home applications.
