## Unit 3 - Data management issues, data replication for mobile computers, adaptive clustering for mobile

- Data management issues in mobile computing arise due to the characteristics of wireless networks and mobile devices, such as limited bandwidth, high latency, frequent disconnections, limited battery power, and limited storage capacity.
- Data replication is a technique to improve data availability and performance in mobile computing by creating and maintaining multiple copies of data items on different devices or servers.
- Data replication can be classified into two types: server-initiated and client-initiated. Server-initiated replication is when the server decides which data items to replicate and where to place them, based on the access patterns and preferences of the clients. Client-initiated replication is when the client requests a copy of a data item from the server and caches it locally for future use.
- Data replication can also be classified into two modes: eager and lazy. Eager replication is when the updates to a data item are propagated to all its replicas as soon as possible, ensuring strong consistency. Lazy replication is when the updates to a data item are propagated to its replicas only when the network conditions are favorable, allowing temporary inconsistency.
- Data replication involves several challenges and trade-offs, such as how to select the data items to replicate, where to place the replicas, how to synchronize the replicas, how to handle conflicts, and how to balance the communication cost and the data consistency.
- Adaptive clustering is a technique to organize mobile devices into groups based on their location, mobility, and connectivity, in order to facilitate data replication and dissemination in mobile computing.
- Adaptive clustering can be classified into two types: proactive and reactive. Proactive clustering is when the mobile devices form clusters in advance, based on their predicted movement and network conditions. Reactive clustering is when the mobile devices form clusters on demand, based on their current situation and needs.
- Adaptive clustering can also be classified into two modes: centralized and distributed. Centralized clustering is when a single device acts as the cluster head and coordinates the cluster formation and maintenance. Distributed clustering is when all the devices participate in the cluster formation and maintenance, without relying on a central authority.
- Adaptive clustering involves several challenges and trade-offs, such as how to select the cluster head, how to form and maintain the clusters, how to handle cluster membership changes, how to balance the cluster size and stability, and how to optimize the data replication and dissemination within and across the clusters.
