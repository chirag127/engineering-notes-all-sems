### QoS in Ad Hoc Networks for the notes of the Unit 5 - Ad Hoc networks, localization, MAC issues, Routing protocols, global state routing (GSR), in the subject of Mobile Computing

- Quality of Service (QoS) is the performance level of service offered by a network to the user. The goal of QoS is to achieve a more deterministic network behavior so that the information carried by the network can be better delivered and the resources can be better utilized.
- QoS is essential for ad hoc networks, which are characterized by a set of mobile nodes communicating using wireless channels. Ad hoc networks face several challenges such as node mobility, limited computation power, wireless link characteristics, interference, and dynamic topology .
- QoS requirements arise at the application layer in the form of restrictions on values of certain QoS metrics. The most commonly studied QoS metrics are bandwidth, delay, jitter, packet loss, and reliability.
- QoS routing is the process of finding a route that satisfies the QoS requirements of a given flow. QoS routing in ad hoc networks is very challenging due to the following reasons :
  - The network topology changes frequently due to node mobility, which makes it difficult to maintain consistent and accurate routing information.
  - The wireless channel is unreliable and prone to errors, fading, and interference, which affects the QoS metrics on each link and the whole end-to-end route.
  - The network resources are limited and shared among multiple nodes, which creates contention and congestion, and reduces the available bandwidth and energy.
  - The QoS requirements may be heterogeneous and conflicting, which makes it hard to find a feasible route that satisfies all the constraints.
- QoS routing in ad hoc networks can be classified into three broad categories: proactive, reactive, and hybrid .
  - Proactive QoS routing protocols maintain QoS information for all possible routes in the network in the form of routing tables. These tables are periodically updated to reflect the current network state. Proactive QoS routing protocols can provide low latency and high reliability, but they incur high overhead and may not be scalable. Examples of proactive QoS routing protocols are QOLSR, A-OLSR, and Q-DSDV.
  - Reactive QoS routing protocols discover QoS routes on demand, when a source node needs to communicate with a destination node. Reactive QoS routing protocols can reduce the overhead and adapt to the network dynamics, but they may cause high delay and low reliability. Examples of reactive QoS routing protocols are Q-AODV, Q-TORA, and Q-DSR.
  - Hybrid QoS routing protocols combine the advantages of both proactive and reactive QoS routing protocols. Hybrid QoS routing protocols divide the network into zones or clusters, and use proactive QoS routing within each zone or cluster, and reactive QoS routing between different zones or clusters. Hybrid QoS routing protocols can balance the trade-off between overhead and latency, and provide scalability. Examples of hybrid QoS routing protocols are Q-ZRP, Q-CBRP, and Q-HYBRID.
- QoS routing in ad hoc networks can also be classified into two types: hard QoS routing and soft QoS routing.
  - Hard QoS routing protocols guarantee that the QoS requirements of a flow are met by the route. Hard QoS routing protocols are suitable for real-time applications that have strict QoS constraints. However, hard QoS routing protocols may not be feasible or efficient in ad hoc networks, due to the high variability and unpredictability of the network conditions. Examples of hard QoS routing protocols are Q-AODV, Q-TORA, and Q-DSDV.
  - Soft QoS routing protocols try to optimize the QoS metrics of a route, but do not guarantee that the QoS requirements of a flow are met by the route. Soft QoS routing protocols are suitable for non-real-time applications that have flexible QoS constraints. Soft QoS routing protocols can be more feasible and efficient in ad hoc networks, due to the low variability and predictability of the network conditions. Examples of soft QoS routing protocols are Q-OLSR, A-OLSR, and Q-DSR.
