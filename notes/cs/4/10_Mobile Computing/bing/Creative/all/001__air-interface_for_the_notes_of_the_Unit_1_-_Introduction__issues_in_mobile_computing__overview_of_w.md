### Air-interface for the notes of the Unit 1 - Introduction, issues in mobile computing, overview of wireless telephony: cellular concept, GSM: in the subject of Mobile Computing

- Air-interface is the communication link between the two stations in mobile or wireless communication.
- Air-interface involves both the physical and data link layers (layer 1 and 2) of the OSI model for a connection.
- Air-interface is also called a "radio interface" as it defines the frequency, channel bandwidth and modulation scheme for the radio transmission between mobile devices and the base station in a cellular network .
- Air-interface can use different technologies for multiple access, such as FDMA, TDMA, CDMA, OFDMA, etc. to allow multiple users to share the same spectrum.
- Air-interface can also support broadcast or multicast transmission for some wireless connections.
- Air-interface can vary depending on the cellular standard, such as GSM, CDMA, LTE, WiMAX, etc. Each standard has its own specifications for the air-interface parameters, such as frequency band, channel spacing, modulation type, coding scheme, etc .
- Air-interface is the radio-frequency portion of the circuit between the cellular phone set or wireless modem (usually portable or mobile) and the active base station in a cellular network.
- Air-interface requires the use of antennas to convert electric power into radio waves and vice versa. Antennas can have different shapes, sizes and characteristics depending on the frequency, direction and gain of the radio waves.
- Air-interface also requires the mechanism of handoff, which is the changeover of the active base station as a subscriber moves from one cell to another in the system. Handoff can be initiated by the mobile station, the base station or the network controller.
- Air-interface can be affected by various factors, such as interference, noise, fading, multipath, shadowing, etc. that can degrade the quality and reliability of the wireless communication.

Some mnemonics and learning tricks for the air-interface are:

- To remember the OSI model layers, use the phrase "Please Do Not Throw Sausage Pizza Away", where each word corresponds to a layer: Physical, Data link, Network, Transport, Session, Presentation, Application.
- To remember the different multiple access technologies, use the acronym F-T-CODE, where each letter corresponds to a technology: FDMA, TDMA, CDMA, OFDMA, etc.
- To remember the different cellular standards, use the acronym G-C-L-W, where each letter corresponds to a standard: GSM, CDMA, LTE, WiMAX, etc.
