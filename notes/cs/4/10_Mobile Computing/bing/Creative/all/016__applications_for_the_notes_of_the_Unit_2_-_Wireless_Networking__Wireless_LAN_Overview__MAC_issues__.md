### Applications for the notes of the Unit 2 - Wireless Networking, Wireless LAN Overview: MAC issues, IEEE 802.11, Blue Tooth, Wireless in the subject of Mobile Computing

- Wireless networking is the technology that enables devices to communicate without wires or cables. Wireless networking has many applications, such as:

  - Providing internet access to mobile devices, such as laptops, smartphones, tablets, etc.
  - Creating wireless local area networks (WLANs) within homes, offices, schools, etc.
  - Connecting devices to each other using wireless personal area networks (WPANs), such as Bluetooth, Zigbee, etc.
  - Extending the coverage of wired networks using wireless bridges, repeaters, access points, etc.
  - Supporting wireless metropolitan area networks (WMANs) and wireless wide area networks (WWANs) that span large geographic areas, such as cities, countries, etc.
  - Enabling wireless sensor networks (WSNs) and internet of things (IoT) applications that involve collecting and processing data from distributed devices.
  - Facilitating wireless ad hoc networks (WANETs) and mesh networks that are self-organizing and dynamic.

- Wireless LAN (WLAN) is a type of wireless network that connects devices within a limited area, such as a room, a building, or a campus. WLANs use radio waves to transmit and receive data. WLANs have the following characteristics:

  - WLANs are based on the IEEE 802.11 family of standards, which define the physical and data link layer specifications for wireless communication. The most common variants of IEEE 802.11 are 802.11a, 802.11b, 802.11g, 802.11n, 802.11ac, and 802.11ax, which differ in terms of frequency, bandwidth, modulation, data rate, range, and compatibility.
  - WLANs use a media access control (MAC) protocol to coordinate the access of multiple devices to the shared wireless medium. The MAC protocol for WLANs is called carrier sense multiple access with collision avoidance (CSMA/CA), which is similar to CSMA/CD used in Ethernet, but with some modifications to avoid collisions and hidden terminal problems.
  - WLANs can operate in two modes: infrastructure mode and ad hoc mode. In infrastructure mode, WLAN devices communicate with each other through a central device called an access point (AP), which acts as a bridge between the wireless and wired networks. In ad hoc mode, WLAN devices communicate with each other directly without any AP, forming a peer-to-peer (P2P) network.
  - WLANs can provide various services and features, such as authentication, encryption, quality of service (QoS), roaming, power management, etc. WLANs can also support different network architectures, such as basic service set (BSS), extended service set (ESS), independent basic service set (IBSS), and mesh network.

- Bluetooth is a type of wireless technology that enables short-range communication between devices, such as headphones, keyboards, mice, speakers, printers, etc. Bluetooth has the following characteristics:

  - Bluetooth is based on the IEEE 802.15.1 standard, which covers the MAC and physical layer specifications for wireless communication. Bluetooth also defines the higher layer protocols and profiles for different applications and services.
  - Bluetooth operates in the 2.4 GHz industrial, scientific, and medical (ISM) band, which is divided into 79 channels, each with a bandwidth of 1 MHz. Bluetooth uses frequency hopping spread spectrum (FHSS) to switch between different channels to avoid interference and enhance security.
  - Bluetooth devices can form a network called a piconet, which consists of one master device and up to seven active slave devices. Multiple piconets can be interconnected to form a scatternet, which can support more devices and increase the coverage area.
  - Bluetooth devices can communicate using two types of links: synchronous connection-oriented (SCO) and asynchronous connectionless (ACL). SCO links are used for voice transmission, which require constant and fixed bandwidth and low latency. ACL links are used for data transmission, which can tolerate variable and bursty bandwidth and higher latency.
  - Bluetooth devices can support various profiles, such as advanced audio distribution profile (A2DP), hands-free profile (HFP), human interface device profile (HID), object push profile (OPP), etc. These profiles define the roles, capabilities, and protocols for different applications and services.
