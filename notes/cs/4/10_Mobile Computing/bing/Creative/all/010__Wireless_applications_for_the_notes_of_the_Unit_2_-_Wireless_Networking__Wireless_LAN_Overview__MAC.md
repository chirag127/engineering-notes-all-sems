### Wireless applications for the notes of the Unit 2 - Wireless Networking, Wireless LAN Overview: MAC issues, IEEE 802.11, Blue Tooth, Wireless in the subject of Mobile Computing

- Wireless applications are the software programs that run on wireless devices, such as smartphones, tablets, laptops, and wearable devices. Wireless applications can communicate with other devices or networks using wireless technologies, such as Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, cellular, or satellite.
- Wireless applications can benefit from user mobility, higher reliability, and lower cost compared to wired applications. Wireless applications can also enable new services and functionalities that are not possible with wired applications, such as location-based services, augmented reality, and mobile payments.
- Wireless applications can be classified into different categories based on their purpose, functionality, and network architecture. Some common categories are:

  - Wireless personal area network (WPAN) applications: These are the applications that use short-range wireless technologies, such as Bluetooth, Zigbee, or infrared, to connect devices within a personal area, such as a room or a car. Examples of WPAN applications are wireless headphones, wireless keyboards, wireless mice, and smart watches.
  - Wireless local area network (WLAN) applications: These are the applications that use medium-range wireless technologies, such as Wi-Fi, to connect devices within a local area, such as a home or an office. Examples of WLAN applications are wireless printers, wireless scanners, wireless cameras, and wireless speakers.
  - Wireless metropolitan area network (WMAN) applications: These are the applications that use long-range wireless technologies, such as WiMAX or 5G, to connect devices within a metropolitan area, such as a city or a region. Examples of WMAN applications are wireless internet access, wireless video streaming, and wireless telephony.
  - Wireless wide area network (WWAN) applications: These are the applications that use global wireless technologies, such as cellular or satellite, to connect devices across the world. Examples of WWAN applications are wireless email, wireless web browsing, and wireless navigation.

- Wireless networking is the process of establishing and maintaining wireless connections between devices or networks using wireless technologies. Wireless networking can be divided into two main components: the physical layer and the data link layer.
- The physical layer is responsible for transmitting and receiving wireless signals using radio waves, microwaves, or infrared waves. The physical layer defines the frequency, bandwidth, modulation, and coding schemes of the wireless signals. The physical layer also deals with the issues of interference, noise, and attenuation of the wireless signals.
- The data link layer is responsible for controlling the access and flow of data over the wireless medium. The data link layer defines the protocols and algorithms for sharing the wireless medium among multiple devices or networks. The data link layer also deals with the issues of collision, congestion, and security of the wireless data.
- The most common standard for wireless networking is IEEE 802.11, also known as Wi-Fi. IEEE 802.11 is a family of standards that cover the physical and data link layer specifications for wireless LANs. IEEE 802.11 defines various versions of Wi-Fi, such as 802.11a, 802.11b, 802.11g, 802.11n, 802.11ac, and 802.11ax, that differ in their frequency, bandwidth, data rate, and range capabilities.
- IEEE 802.11 also splits the data link layer into two sublayers: the logical link control (LLC) sublayer and the media access control (MAC) sublayer. The LLC sublayer provides a common interface for different network layer protocols, such as IP, to communicate over the wireless medium. The MAC sublayer provides the mechanisms for coordinating the access and transmission of data over the wireless medium among multiple devices or networks.
- The MAC sublayer of IEEE 802.11 uses the Ethernet protocol and the carrier sense multiple access with collision avoidance (CSMA/CA) algorithm to share the wireless medium. CSMA/CA is a distributed algorithm that allows each device or network to sense the wireless medium before transmitting data. If the medium is busy, the device or network defers its transmission until the medium is idle. If the medium is idle, the device or network transmits a short request-to-send (RTS) frame to the intended receiver. The receiver replies with a clear-to-send (CTS) frame if it is ready to receive data. The sender then transmits the data frame and waits for an acknowledgment (ACK) frame from the receiver.
