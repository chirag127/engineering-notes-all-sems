### CDMA for the notes of the Unit 1 - Introduction, issues in mobile computing, overview of wireless telephony: cellular concept, GSM: in the subject of Mobile Computing

- CDMA stands for Code Division Multiple Access, which is a technique for multiplexing multiple signals over a single channel using spread spectrum technology  .
- CDMA is a form of 2G and 3G wireless communication standard that competes with GSM . CDMA is mainly used in the U.S. and parts of Asia by carriers such as Sprint, Verizon and Virgin Mobile.
- CDMA works by converting audio input into digital bits and then spreading the bits over a wide frequency range using a predefined code . The receiver must have the same code as the transmitter to decode the signal and recover the original bits .
- CDMA has some advantages over GSM, such as:
  - Higher spectral efficiency, which means more users can share the same bandwidth .
  - Better voice quality and less interference, as CDMA signals are less prone to fading and multipath effects .
  - Greater security and privacy, as CDMA signals are harder to intercept and clone due to the use of unique codes .
  - Soft handoff, which means seamless transition between cells without dropping calls  .
- CDMA also has some disadvantages over GSM, such as:
  - Less global coverage and compatibility, as GSM is more widely used and supported by roaming agreements .
  - More complex and expensive hardware and software, as CDMA requires sophisticated signal processing and power control schemes .
  - Less flexibility and scalability, as CDMA has fixed channel capacity and bandwidth allocation .
  - No removable SIM cards, which means CDMA phones are tied to specific carriers and networks .
- CDMA has evolved over time to support higher data rates and features. The original CDMA standard, also known as CDMA One, can deliver up to 14.4 Kbps in single channel mode and up to 115 Kbps in eight channel mode . CDMA2000 is a family of standards that includes 1xRTT, EVDO and EVDO Rev. B, which can offer up to 3.1 Mbps. W-CDMA is another standard that is based on CDMA but compatible with GSM, and is used for 3G and 4G networks such as UMTS and LTE .
- CDMA is gradually being phased out by newer technologies such as 4G LTE and 5G, which offer faster speeds, lower latency and more capacity . However, CDMA still has some applications in satellite communication, military communication and IoT devices .

Some mnemonics and learning tricks for CDMA are:

- To remember the meaning of CDMA, think of **C**ode **D**ivides **M**ultiple **A**ccess, which means using codes to divide the access to a single channel among multiple users.
- To remember the difference between CDMA and GSM, think of **C**DMA as **C**omplex and **C**losed, and **G**SM as **G**lobal and **G**eneric.
- To remember the advantages of CDMA, think of **S**ecurity, **S**pectrum, **S**ound and **S**oft handoff, which are the four S's of CDMA .
- To remember the disadvantages of CDMA, think of **C**overage, **C**ost, **C**apacity and **C**ompatibility, which are the four C's of CDMA .
- To remember the evolution of CDMA, think of **O**ne, **T**wo, **T**hree, which are the numbers of CDMA One, CDMA2000 and W-CDMA respectively .
