## Unit 1 - Introduction, issues in mobile computing, overview of wireless telephony: cellular concept, GSM:

- Mobile computing is the field of wireless communication and carry-around computers, such as laptop computers, tablets, smartphones, and other mobile devices.
- Mobile computing enables users to access computation, information, and related resources and capabilities anytime and anywhere .
- Mobile computing involves three aspects: mobile communication, mobile hardware, and mobile software.
- Mobile communication refers to the set of technologies, protocols, and standards that enable wireless data transmission over a network, such as Wi-Fi, cellular, satellite, or Bluetooth.
- Mobile hardware refers to the devices that are used for mobile computing, such as laptops, tablets, smartphones, and other specialized devices for medical, surveillance, security, and telemetry applications .
- Mobile software refers to the applications and services that run on mobile devices, such as web browsers, mobile apps, streaming media, and cloud-based services .
- Some of the issues and challenges in mobile computing are:
  - Limited battery life and power consumption of mobile devices .
  - Limited bandwidth and network coverage of wireless networks .
  - Security and privacy of data and communication over wireless networks .
  - Heterogeneity and interoperability of mobile devices and platforms .
  - Mobility and location management of mobile users and devices .
  - Quality of service and reliability of mobile applications and services .
  - User interface and usability of mobile devices and applications .
- Wireless telephony is the technology that enables voice and data communication over wireless networks, such as cellular, satellite, or cordless phones.
- Cellular concept is the principle of dividing a geographic area into smaller cells, each served by a base station, to provide wireless communication services to mobile users.
- GSM (Global System for Mobile Communications) is a standard for digital cellular communication that uses the cellular concept and supports voice, data, and multimedia services.
- GSM has four main components: mobile stations, base station subsystems, network and switching subsystems, and operation and support subsystems.
- GSM uses frequency division multiple access (FDMA) and time division multiple access (TDMA) to allocate radio channels to multiple users in a cell.
- GSM uses a hierarchical cellular network structure, consisting of cells, clusters, location areas, and public land mobile networks.
- GSM supports roaming, handover, authentication, encryption, and short message service features.
