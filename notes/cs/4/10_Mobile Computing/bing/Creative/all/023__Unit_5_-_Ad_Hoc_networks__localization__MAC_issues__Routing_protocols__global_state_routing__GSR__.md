## Unit 5 - Ad Hoc networks, localization, MAC issues, Routing protocols, global state routing (GSR)

- Ad hoc networks are wireless networks that do not rely on any fixed infrastructure or centralized control. They consist of mobile nodes that communicate with each other over wireless links, forming dynamic and self-organizing topologies.
- Localization is the process of determining the position of a node in an ad hoc network, either relative to other nodes or to a global coordinate system. Localization is important for many applications, such as navigation, tracking, and geographic routing.
- MAC issues refer to the challenges of designing medium access control protocols for ad hoc networks, such as collision avoidance, channel access, fairness, power control, and synchronization. MAC protocols can be classified into contention-based and reservation-based schemes, depending on whether they use random or deterministic access methods.
- Routing protocols are algorithms that enable nodes to discover and maintain routes to other nodes in an ad hoc network. Routing protocols can be categorized into proactive and reactive protocols, depending on whether they maintain routing tables periodically or on demand. Some examples of routing protocols are DSR, AODV, OLSR, and DSDV.
- Global state routing (GSR) is a proactive routing protocol that is based on link state routing. In GSR, each node maintains a complete view of the network topology and periodically broadcasts its link state information to all other nodes. GSR reduces the overhead of global flooding by using a hierarchical structure and a reliable dissemination mechanism. GSR is suitable for networks with low mobility and high bandwidth.
