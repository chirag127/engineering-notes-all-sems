### Wireless Networks

Wireless networks are computer networks that use radio frequency (RF) connections between nodes in the network, instead of cables. Wireless networks can provide mobility, flexibility, and scalability for users and devices. Wireless networks can be classified into different types based on their coverage area, such as personal-area network (PAN), local-area network (LAN), metropolitan-area network (MAN), and wide-area network (WAN).

The most common wireless network architecture is the IEEE 802.11 standard, also known as Wi-Fi. Wi-Fi networks use access points (APs) to provide wireless connectivity to wireless devices, such as laptops, smartphones, and tablets. APs are connected to a wired network, such as Ethernet, and act as bridges between the wireless and wired domains. APs can operate in different modes, such as autonomous, controller-based, or cloud-based, depending on the management and configuration of the network.
