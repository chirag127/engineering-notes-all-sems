### Channel Structure

Unit 1 - Introduction, Issues in Mobile Computing, Overview of Wireless Telephony: Cellular Concept, GSM

1. A channel in mobile computing refers to the communication pathway between two devices.
2. The channel structure is determined by the underlying wireless technology and can vary depending on the specific implementation.
3. In cellular networks, the channel structure is typically divided into control channels and traffic channels.
4. Control channels are used for signaling and management purposes, such as setting up and maintaining calls.
5. Traffic channels are used for transmitting voice and data between the mobile device and the network.
6. In the Global System for Mobile Communications (GSM), the channel structure is based on a combination of time division multiple access (TDMA) and frequency division multiple access (FDMA).
7. This means that the available frequency spectrum is divided into multiple channels, and each channel is further divided into time slots.
8. Each mobile device is assigned a specific time slot on a specific frequency channel for communication.
9. This allows multiple devices to share the same frequency channel without interfering with each other.
10. The specific details of the channel structure can vary depending on the specific implementation of the GSM standard.
