### air-interface for the notes of the Unit 1 - Introduction, issues in mobile computing, overview of wireless telephony: cellular concept, GSM: in the subject of Mobile Computing
Air Interface:
- Refers to the physical and data link layer protocols used for communication between mobile devices and cell towers in a cellular network
- Defines the radio frequency (RF) specifications, modulation techniques, and channel access methods used for transmitting data over the airwaves

Unit 1 - Introduction:
- Covers the basics of mobile computing, including its history, evolution, and current state
- Discusses the challenges and limitations of mobile computing, such as battery life, network connectivity, and security

Issues in Mobile Computing:
- Battery life: Mobile devices have limited battery capacity, which can impact their usability and performance
- Network Connectivity: Mobile devices may experience connectivity issues, such as dropped calls or slow data speeds, due to network congestion or limited coverage
- Security: Mobile devices are vulnerable to security threats, such as hacking, malware, and data theft, due to their small size and constant connectivity to the internet

Overview of Wireless Telephony:
- Describes the history and evolution of wireless telephony, from analog to digital systems
- Discusses the different types of wireless telephony systems, such as 1G, 2G, 3G, 4G, and 5G

Cellular Concept:
- Refers to the use of multiple, interconnected cells to provide wireless coverage over a large geographic area
- Each cell covers a small area and is served by a cell tower, which communicates with mobile devices in the cell

GSM (Global System for Mobile Communications):
- A widely used 2G mobile telephony system that provides voice and data services
- Defines the air interface and network architecture used for communication between mobile devices and cell towers in a GSM network
