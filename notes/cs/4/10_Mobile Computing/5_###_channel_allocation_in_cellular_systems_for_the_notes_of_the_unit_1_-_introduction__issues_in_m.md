### channel allocation in cellular systems for the notes of the Unit 1 - Introduction, issues in mobile computing, overview of wireless telephony: cellular concept, GSM: in the subject of Mobile Computing
Channel allocation in cellular systems refers to the process of assigning frequency bands to various users in a cellular network. The goal is to maximize network capacity and minimize interference between users. There are several methods for channel allocation, including static allocation, dynamic allocation, and frequency reuse.

Static allocation assigns a fixed number of channels to each cell, regardless of the number of users. This method is simple and efficient, but can lead to underutilization of resources in areas with low user density.

Dynamic allocation assigns channels to users as needed, based on the current network load. This method is more efficient, but requires more complex algorithms and can lead to increased latency.

Frequency reuse involves assigning the same frequency band to multiple cells, with the cells separated by a sufficient distance to minimize interference. This method allows for efficient use of limited frequency resources, but requires careful planning to ensure that cells do not interfere with each other.

In GSM, the channel allocation process is managed by the Base Station Controller (BSC), which assigns frequency bands to individual cells based on network load and other factors. The BSC also manages the handover process, which allows users to seamlessly switch between cells as they move through the network.
