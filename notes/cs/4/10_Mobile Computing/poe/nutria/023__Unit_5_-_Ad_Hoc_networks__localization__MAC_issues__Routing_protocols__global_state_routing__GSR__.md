
## Unit 5 - Ad Hoc networks, localization, MAC issues, Routing protocols, global state routing (GSR)

* Ad Hoc networks are wireless networks that consist of mobile nodes that communicate with each other without the need of an access point or other centralized infrastructure.
* Localization is the process of determining the geographical location of a node in an Ad Hoc network. This is typically done by measuring the signal strength of nearby nodes.
* MAC (Medium Access Control) issues in Ad Hoc networks include contention, hidden node, exposed node, and capture effects.
* Routing protocols in Ad Hoc networks are used to find routes between nodes. Examples of routing protocols include AODV, DSR, and DSDV.
* Global State Routing (GSR) is a reactive routing protocol for Ad Hoc networks. GSR uses a distributed algorithm to maintain a global view of the network.
