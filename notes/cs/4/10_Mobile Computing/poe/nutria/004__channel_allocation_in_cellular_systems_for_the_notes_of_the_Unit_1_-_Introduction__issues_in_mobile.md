
### Channel Allocation in Cellular Systems

1. Mobile computing is a field of technology that enables people to access information and services from any location, at any time and on any device.
2. Cellular concept is a type of wireless telephony that uses multiple cells to provide coverage over a wide area. Each cell is served by a base station which handles the call and data connections for the users in that cell. 
3. GSM (Global System for Mobile Communications) is a widely used standard for mobile phones and is the most popular type of cellular network. It is based on the concept of frequency reuse, where each cell is assigned a unique frequency to avoid interference between cells.
4. Channel allocation is the process of assigning frequencies to cells in a cellular network. It is an important process in order to ensure proper communication between the cells and to maximize the capacity of the network.
5. There are various channel allocation algorithms used in cellular networks such as fixed channel allocation (FCA), dynamic channel allocation (DCA), and frequency reuse techniques.
6. FCA is a simple algorithm where each cell is assigned a fixed set of frequencies. This technique is suitable for small networks with low traffic.
7. DCA is a more complex algorithm which dynamically assigns frequencies to cells based on the traffic load. This technique is suitable for large networks with high traffic.
8. Frequency reuse techniques are used to increase the capacity of the network by reusing the same frequencies in different cells. This technique is suitable for large networks with high traffic.
