

### Applications for the Notes of the Unit 5 - Ad Hoc Networks, Localization, MAC Issues, Routing Protocols, Global State Routing (GSR), in the Subject of Mobile Computing

1. Ad Hoc Networks: Ad hoc networks are wireless networks that are formed on-the-fly without the need for a centralized access point. They are self-organizing and self-configuring, making them ideal for situations where a traditional network infrastructure is not available. Ad hoc networks are used in a variety of applications, such as emergency response, military operations, and sensor networks.

2. Localization: Localization is the process of determining the physical location of a device or node in an ad hoc network. It is an important component of ad hoc networks, as it allows nodes to determine their position relative to other nodes in the network. Localization techniques include signal strength-based methods, angle of arrival-based methods, and time difference of arrival-based methods.

3. MAC Issues: Medium Access Control (MAC) protocols are used to manage the access of nodes to the shared wireless medium. They are responsible for controlling the access of nodes to the medium, as well as for coordinating the transmission of data by nodes. Common MAC protocols used in ad hoc networks include IEEE 802.11, CSMA/CA, and TDMA.

4. Routing Protocols: Routing protocols are used to determine the best path between two nodes in an ad hoc network. They are responsible for finding the most efficient route between two nodes, as well as for maintaining the routes. Common routing protocols used in ad hoc networks include AODV, DSR, and OLSR.

5. Global State Routing (GSR): Global State Routing (GSR) is a routing protocol that uses global knowledge to find routes between nodes in an ad hoc network. It is a distributed routing protocol that uses a distributed database of network topology information to determine the best route between two nodes. GSR is used in applications where scalability and robustness are important.
