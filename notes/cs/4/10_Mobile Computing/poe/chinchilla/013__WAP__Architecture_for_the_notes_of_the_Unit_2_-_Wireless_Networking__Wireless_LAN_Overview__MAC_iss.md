### WAP: Architecture for the notes of the Unit 2 - Wireless Networking, Wireless LAN Overview: MAC issues, IEEE 802.11, Blue Tooth, Wireless in the subject of Mobile Computing

Wireless networking has become an integral part of mobile computing. In this unit, we will cover the architecture of Wireless Access Points (WAPs) and the different wireless LAN technologies such as IEEE 802.11 and Bluetooth. Here are some key points to remember:

#### WAP Architecture

- WAP is a device that provides wireless connectivity to devices on a network.
- It acts as a bridge between wired and wireless networks.
- WAP consists of two main components: radio and baseband processor.
- The radio component is responsible for transmitting and receiving data wirelessly.
- The baseband processor handles the data link layer functions such as packet framing, error detection, and correction.

#### Wireless LAN Overview

- Wireless LANs (WLANs) are networks that use wireless communication to connect devices.
- WLANs use the same protocols as wired LANs, but with additional protocols for wireless communication.
- WLANs are based on the IEEE 802.11 standard.
- The standard defines two types of architecture: infrastructure and ad-hoc.
- Infrastructure architecture uses a WAP to connect wireless devices to a wired network.
- Ad-hoc architecture allows wireless devices to connect directly to each other without the need for a WAP.

#### MAC Issues in Wireless LANs

- The medium access control (MAC) layer in wireless LANs is responsible for managing access to the wireless medium.
- The MAC layer uses a protocol called Carrier Sense Multiple Access with Collision Avoidance (CSMA/CA) to avoid collisions.
- CSMA/CA works by listening to the medium before transmitting and waiting for a random period of time before attempting to transmit again.
- The hidden node problem and the exposed node problem are two MAC layer issues that can occur in wireless LANs.

#### IEEE 802.11

- IEEE 802.11 is a set of standards for wireless LANs.
- The standard defines the physical layer (PHY) and the MAC layer.
- The PHY layer defines the radio frequency, modulation scheme, and other physical characteristics of the wireless signal.
- The MAC layer defines the data link layer functions and the CSMA/CA protocol.
- The standard defines several modes of operation such as ad-hoc, infrastructure, and mesh.

#### Bluetooth

- Bluetooth is a wireless technology that allows devices to communicate with each other over short distances.
- Bluetooth uses frequency-hopping spread spectrum (FHSS) to avoid interference.
- Bluetooth devices form a piconet, which consists of one master device and up to seven slave devices.
- Bluetooth supports several profiles such as the Hands-Free Profile (HFP) and the Advanced Audio Distribution Profile (A2DP).

In conclusion, understanding the architecture of WAPs and the different wireless LAN technologies such as IEEE 802.11 and Bluetooth is essential for mobile computing. Remembering the key points mentioned above will help you prepare for exams and improve your knowledge in this area.
