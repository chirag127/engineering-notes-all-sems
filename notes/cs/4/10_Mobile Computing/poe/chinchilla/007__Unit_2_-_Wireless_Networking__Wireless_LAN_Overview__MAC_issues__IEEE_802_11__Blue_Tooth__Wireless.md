## Unit 2 - Wireless Networking, Wireless LAN Overview: MAC issues, IEEE 802.11, Blue Tooth, Wireless

Wireless networking has become an integral part of our lives, and it is crucial to understand the fundamentals of wireless networking to deploy and manage wireless networks effectively. This unit covers the following topics:

### MAC issues

- MAC (Media Access Control) is a crucial part of wireless networking, and it determines how devices communicate with each other in a wireless network.
- One of the significant issues related to MAC is contention, which occurs when multiple devices attempt to access the wireless medium simultaneously, leading to interference and reduced network performance.
- Another issue is the hidden node problem, where two devices cannot communicate with each other due to the presence of an obstacle, resulting in reduced network performance.

### IEEE 802.11

- IEEE 802.11 is a set of standards that define how wireless networks operate, and it includes several sub-standards such as 802.11b, 802.11a, 802.11g, etc.
- IEEE 802.11 uses CSMA/CA (Carrier Sense Multiple Access with Collision Avoidance) to manage the wireless medium and avoid collisions.
- IEEE 802.11 also includes security mechanisms such as WPA2 (Wi-Fi Protected Access II) and AES (Advanced Encryption Standard) to secure wireless networks.

### Bluetooth

- Bluetooth is a wireless technology that uses radio waves to exchange data between devices over short distances.
- Bluetooth operates in the 2.4 GHz frequency band and can support data rates up to 24 Mbps.
- Bluetooth uses frequency hopping spread spectrum (FHSS) to avoid interference from other wireless devices.

### Wireless

- Wireless networking refers to the use of wireless technology to exchange data between devices over a network.
- Wireless networks can be classified into two types: infrastructure networks and ad-hoc networks.
- Infrastructure networks use access points (APs) to manage wireless communications, while ad-hoc networks do not require any centralized management.

In conclusion, understanding the MAC issues, IEEE 802.11 standards, Bluetooth technology, and wireless networking fundamentals is essential for deploying and managing wireless networks effectively.
