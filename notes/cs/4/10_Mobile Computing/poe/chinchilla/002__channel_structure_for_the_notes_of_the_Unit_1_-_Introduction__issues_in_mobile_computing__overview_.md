### Channel Structure for the Notes of Unit 1 - Introduction, Issues in Mobile Computing, Overview of Wireless Telephony: Cellular Concept, GSM

The following are the important points to consider while studying the channel structure for the notes of Unit 1 - Introduction, Issues in Mobile Computing, Overview of Wireless Telephony: Cellular Concept, GSM in the subject of Mobile Computing:

- The mobile communication system is based on radio communication technology, which means that the communication takes place wirelessly through radio waves.
- The radio frequency spectrum is divided into several frequency bands, and each band is subdivided into channels.
- The channel is the basic unit of communication and is used to transmit information between the mobile devices and the base station.
- In a cellular system, the service area is divided into small geographical areas called cells, and each cell is served by a base station.
- The base station is responsible for transmitting and receiving signals to and from the mobile devices within its coverage area.
- The channel structure in a cellular system is divided into two categories: control channels and traffic channels.
- Control channels are used for signaling and control purposes, such as handover, paging, and registration. These channels are always active and are used for short messages.
- Traffic channels are used for carrying voice and data traffic between the mobile device and the base station. These channels are allocated on demand and are released when the communication is complete.
- In GSM (Global System for Mobile Communications), the channel structure consists of two types of traffic channels: Full Rate (FR) channels and Half Rate (HR) channels.
- The FR channel has a data rate of 13 kbps and is used for full-rate speech transmission. The HR channel has a data rate of 6.5 kbps and is used for half-rate speech transmission.
- The control channels in GSM include the Broadcast Control Channel (BCCH), the Paging Control Channel (PCH), the Random Access Channel (RACH), and the Access Grant Channel (AGCH).
- The BCCH is used for broadcasting system information to the mobile devices, the PCH is used for paging the mobile devices, the RACH is used for initiating a call setup, and the AGCH is used for allocating a traffic channel to the mobile device.
- The channel structure in GSM is designed to provide efficient use of the radio frequency spectrum and to support multiple users simultaneously.

By understanding the channel structure in mobile communication systems, such as GSM, one can gain a better understanding of how the communication takes place between the mobile devices and the base station. This knowledge is essential for designing and optimizing the mobile communication system to provide reliable and efficient communication services to the users.
