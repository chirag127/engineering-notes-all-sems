### WAP: Architecture for the notes of the Unit 2 - Wireless Networking, Wireless LAN Overview: MAC issues, IEEE 802.11, Blue Tooth, Wireless in the subject of Mobile Computing

Here are some key points to understand the architecture of Wireless Access Point (WAP) and related topics in Unit 2 of Mobile Computing:

- WAP is a device that connects wireless devices to a wired network using Wi-Fi or other wireless protocols. It acts as a bridge between the wireless and wired networks.
- WAP architecture consists of two main components: Access Point (AP) and Station (STA). AP is the central point that connects wireless devices to the wired network, while STA is a wireless device that communicates with AP.
- MAC (Media Access Control) issues are a set of challenges related to accessing the wireless medium, such as interference, collision, and congestion. These issues are addressed by using various MAC protocols, such as CSMA/CA, TDMA, and FDMA.
- IEEE 802.11 is a set of standards for wireless LAN (Local Area Network) that defines the physical and MAC layer specifications. It includes several variants, such as 802.11a, 802.11b, 802.11g, 802.11n, 802.11ac, and 802.11ax, each with different speed, range, and frequency band.
- Blue Tooth is a wireless protocol for short-range communication between devices, such as smartphones, laptops, and IoT devices. It uses radio waves to transmit data and supports various profiles, such as hands-free, stereo audio, and low energy.
- Wireless technology has several advantages over wired technology, such as mobility, flexibility, scalability, and cost-effectiveness. However, it also has some challenges, such as security, reliability, and performance.

By understanding the architecture of WAP and related topics, you will be able to design, implement, and troubleshoot wireless networks and devices in Mobile Computing.
