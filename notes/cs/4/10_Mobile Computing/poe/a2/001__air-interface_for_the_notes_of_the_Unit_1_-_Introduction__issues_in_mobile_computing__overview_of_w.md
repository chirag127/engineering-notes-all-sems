### Air Interface

- The air interface refers to the radio frequency spectrum through which wireless communication takes place between the mobile device and the base station.
- In mobile computing, the air interface is what enables the wireless connectivity and transmission of voice and data between the mobile device and the cellular network.
- The air interface uses a specific frequency band and a defined set of protocols to enable the wireless communication.
- The frequency band allocated to the air interface determines the maximum bandwidth and data speeds that can be achieved. Different cellular technologies use different frequency bands and protocols for their air interface.
- For example, 2G GSM uses the 900 MHz and 1800 MHz bands while 3G UMTS uses 2100 MHz band and LTE uses 700 MHz, 1900 MHz and 2500 MHz bands for the air interface.
- The air interface protocols define the mechanisms and procedures for wireless connectivity establishment, authentication, data encryption, handoffs, etc. These protocols vary based on the cellular technology being used.
- The air interface, thus, constitutes the vital technological components that enable ubiquitous wireless access to voice and data services through mobile computing devices. The capabilities and performance of the air interface determine the key aspects of the user experience with cellular networks.
