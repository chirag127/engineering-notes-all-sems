### QoS in Ad Hoc Networks for the notes of the Unit 5 - Ad Hoc networks, localization, MAC issues, Routing protocols, global state routing (GSR), in the subject of Mobile Computing
Quality of Service (QoS) in Ad Hoc Networks refers to the ability of the network to deliver consistent and reliable service levels to its users. It is a key factor for ensuring that applications run smoothly and efficiently. QoS is achieved through the use of various techniques such as resource reservation, traffic prioritization, and congestion control. In Ad Hoc networks, QoS is particularly challenging due to the dynamic and decentralized nature of the network. The following are some of the key issues related to QoS in Ad Hoc networks:

1. Resource Constraints: Ad Hoc networks are typically composed of low-power, resource-constrained devices, which makes it difficult to provide consistent QoS.

2. Bandwidth Limitations: Ad Hoc networks have limited bandwidth, which can lead to congestion and reduced QoS.

3. Interference: Interference from other devices can impact the performance of Ad Hoc networks and reduce QoS.

4. Mobility: The mobility of nodes in Ad Hoc networks can lead to frequent changes in network topology, which can impact QoS.

5. Security: The lack of a centralized infrastructure in Ad Hoc networks can make it difficult to secure the network and protect against attacks that can impact QoS.

To address these challenges, various QoS-aware routing protocols have been proposed for Ad Hoc networks. These protocols aim to provide consistent and reliable service levels by dynamically adjusting the routing path based on network conditions. Examples of QoS-aware routing protocols for Ad Hoc networks include global state routing (GSR) and differentiated services (DiffServ).
