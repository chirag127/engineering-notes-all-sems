### Atomic Commit protocols for the notes of the Unit 9 - Distributed Transactions in the subject of DISTRIBUTED SYSTEM

- Atomic commit protocols are protocols that guarantee the atomicity property of a transaction, which means that either all the operations of the transaction are executed successfully, or none of them are executed at all.
- Atomicity is important for maintaining the consistency and integrity of the data in a distributed system, where a transaction may involve multiple servers or nodes that may fail or crash independently .
- Atomic commit protocols can be classified into two categories: blocking and non-blocking.
  - Blocking protocols are protocols that may block indefinitely if some of the nodes fail or crash during the commit process. Examples of blocking protocols are two-phase commit (2PC) and three-phase commit (3PC).
  - Non-blocking protocols are protocols that guarantee to terminate in a finite number of steps, regardless of node failures or crashes. Examples of non-blocking protocols are Paxos commit, consensus commit, and parallel commit .
- Two-phase commit (2PC) is the most widely used blocking protocol for atomic commit. It consists of two phases: prepare and commit.
  - In the prepare phase, a coordinator node sends a prepare message to all the participant nodes, asking them to vote on whether to commit or abort the transaction. Each participant node replies with a yes or no vote, after locking the resources involved in the transaction and writing the undo and redo logs to disk.
  - In the commit phase, the coordinator node collects the votes from the participant nodes and decides whether to commit or abort the transaction based on the majority rule. If all the votes are yes, the coordinator sends a commit message to all the participant nodes, asking them to commit the transaction and release the locks. If any of the votes is no, the coordinator sends an abort message to all the participant nodes, asking them to abort the transaction and undo the changes.
  - The 2PC protocol ensures atomicity as long as no node fails or crashes during the commit process. However, if the coordinator or any of the participant nodes fails or crashes, the protocol may block indefinitely, waiting for the failed node to recover. This may cause the transaction to be in an uncertain state, and the locks to be held for a long time, affecting the availability and performance of the system.
- Three-phase commit (3PC) is a blocking protocol that tries to improve the fault tolerance of 2PC by adding a third phase: pre-commit.
  - In the pre-commit phase, the coordinator node sends a pre-commit message to all the participant nodes that voted yes in the prepare phase, asking them to confirm their readiness to commit. Each participant node replies with an ack message, after writing a pre-commit record to disk.
  - In the commit phase, the coordinator node collects the ack messages from the participant nodes and decides whether to commit or abort the transaction based on the majority rule. If all the ack messages are received, the coordinator sends a commit message to all the participant nodes, asking them to commit the transaction and release the locks. If any of the ack messages is missing, the coordinator sends an abort message to all the participant nodes, asking them to abort the transaction and undo the changes.
  - The 3PC protocol ensures atomicity as long as no node fails or crashes in the pre-commit or commit phases. However, if the coordinator or any of the participant nodes fails or crashes in these phases, the protocol may still block indefinitely, waiting for the failed node to recover. Moreover, the 3PC protocol introduces an extra round of communication, which increases the latency and overhead of the commit process.
- Paxos commit is a non-blocking protocol that uses the Paxos consensus algorithm to achieve atomic commit. It consists of two phases: propose and decide.
  - In the propose phase, a proposer node sends a propose message to a quorum of acceptor nodes, asking them to accept a proposal to commit or abort the transaction. Each acceptor node replies with a promise message, indicating whether it accepts the proposal or not, based on the Paxos rules.
  - In the decide phase, the proposer node collects the promise messages from the acceptor nodes and decides whether to commit or abort the transaction based on the Paxos rules. The proposer then sends a decide message to a quorum of learner nodes, asking them to learn the decision and execute the transaction accordingly
