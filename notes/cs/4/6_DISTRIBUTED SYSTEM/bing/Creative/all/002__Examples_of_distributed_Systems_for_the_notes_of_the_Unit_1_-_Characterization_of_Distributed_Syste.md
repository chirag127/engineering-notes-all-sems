### Examples of distributed Systems for the notes of the Unit 1 - Characterization of Distributed Systems in the subject of DISTRIBUTED SYSTEM

- A distributed system is a collection of independent components located on different machines that share messages with each other in order to achieve common goals.
- Distributed systems can have different architectures, such as client-server, peer-to-peer, or publish-subscribe, depending on how the components communicate and coordinate with each other.
- Distributed systems can have different benefits, such as resource sharing, openness, concurrency, scalability, fault tolerance, and transparency.
- Distributed systems can also have different challenges, such as network latency, partial failures, security, consistency, and coordination.
- Some examples of distributed systems are:

  - **The Internet**: The Internet is the largest and most widely used distributed system in the world. It consists of millions of computers, routers, and other devices that are connected by various communication protocols, such as TCP/IP, HTTP, and DNS. The Internet enables users to access information and services from anywhere in the world, such as web pages, email, online games, and streaming media. The Internet is also an example of a heterogeneous distributed system, as it supports different data models and database management systems.
  - **Telecommunication networks**: Telecommunication networks are another early example of distributed systems, such as telephone networks and cellular networks. They use peer-to-peer or client-server architectures to enable users to communicate with each other by voice, text, or video. They also use various technologies, such as circuit switching, packet switching, and wireless transmission, to route and deliver messages across different nodes and regions. Telecommunication networks are also examples of real-time distributed systems, as they have strict requirements for latency and reliability.
  - **Distributed database systems**: Distributed database systems are systems that store and manage data across multiple servers, physical locations, or both. They can use either a homogenous or a heterogeneous approach, depending on whether they share the same data model and database management system or not. They can also use different strategies, such as replication, fragmentation, or federation, to distribute and synchronize data among different nodes. Distributed database systems can improve performance, availability, and scalability of data-intensive applications, such as online shopping, social media, and cloud computing.
  - **Distributed pervasive systems**: Distributed pervasive systems are systems that use embedded computer devices, such as sensors, actuators, mobile devices, and wearable devices, to interact with the physical environment and provide context-aware services to users. They can use wireless communication, such as Bluetooth, Wi-Fi, or RFID, to connect and exchange data with each other or with a central server. They can also use different techniques, such as data aggregation, data fusion, or data mining, to process and analyze the data collected from the environment. Distributed pervasive systems can enable applications such as smart homes, smart cities, health monitoring, and environmental monitoring.
