### Resource sharing for the notes of the Unit 1 - Characterization of Distributed Systems in the subject of DISTRIBUTED SYSTEM

- Resource sharing is one of the main goals and challenges of distributed systems, which are collections of independent components located on different machines that communicate and coordinate to achieve common objectives.
- Resource sharing can involve hardware, software, or data resources that can be accessed and utilized by the components of a distributed system.
- Resource sharing can be implemented in different ways, such as:
  - Data migration: the process of transferring data from one location to another in the system, for example, by using a file transfer protocol or a database replication mechanism.
  - Computation migration: the process of transferring computation from one location to another in the system, for example, by using a remote procedure call or a code mobility technique.
  - Process migration: the process of transferring a running process from one location to another in the system, for example, by using a checkpointing and restart mechanism or a process migration protocol.
- Resource sharing can have various benefits, such as:
  - Improved performance: by distributing the workload among multiple machines, the system can achieve higher throughput, lower latency, and better load balancing.
  - Increased reliability: by replicating the resources among multiple machines, the system can tolerate failures and provide fault tolerance and availability.
  - Enhanced scalability: by adding or removing machines, the system can adjust to the changes in demand and provide elasticity and adaptability.
  - Reduced cost: by sharing the resources among multiple machines, the system can reduce the hardware and software expenses and provide economy and efficiency.
- Resource sharing can also have various challenges, such as:
  - Coordination: the system needs to ensure that the components can communicate and synchronize with each other, and that the shared resources are consistent and coherent.
  - Security: the system needs to ensure that the shared resources are protected from unauthorized access and malicious attacks, and that the components can trust each other and the system.
  - Transparency: the system needs to ensure that the components and the users are unaware of the distribution and the heterogeneity of the resources, and that the system appears as a single and integrated entity.
  - Management: the system needs to ensure that the shared resources are allocated and scheduled efficiently and fairly, and that the system can handle the complexity and the dynamics of the resources.
