## Unit 5 - Distributed Resource Management

- Distributed Resource Management (DRM) is an evolving discipline that consists of a set of software, hardware, network tools, procedures and policies for enabling distributed enterprise systems to operate effectively in production.
- DRM aims to optimize the workloads and resources in a distributed networked computing environment, such as a cluster, a grid, a cloud, or an edge system.
- DRM involves the following main tasks:
  - Resource discovery: finding and identifying the available resources in the distributed system, such as processors, memory, disk, network, etc.
  - Resource scheduling: allocating and coordinating the resources to the workloads, such as applications, containers, services, etc., according to some criteria, such as performance, throughput, cost, quality of service, etc.
  - Resource monitoring: collecting and analyzing the resource utilization and performance data, such as CPU usage, memory consumption, network traffic, etc.
  - Resource management: adjusting and controlling the resource allocation and configuration, such as adding, removing, or migrating resources, according to the changing workload demands and system conditions.
- DRM can benefit from the following features and techniques:
  - Advanced GPU support: supporting new frameworks and maximizing the use of GPU resources on-premises and in the cloud.
  - Cloud-friendly features: enabling the easy deployment and scaling of dedicated and hybrid HPC clusters in the cloud.
  - Extreme performance and scale: supporting large-scale distributed systems with millions of cores and vCPUs.
  - Monitoring and reporting: providing comprehensive and customizable reports on the resource utilization and performance.
  - License-aware scheduling: sharing expensive application licenses among the workloads with optional License Orchestrator.
  - Broadest OS and hardware support: supporting various Linux, Windows, and other OS distributions on x86, Power, and Arm systems.
- DRM can be applied to various domains and scenarios, such as:
  - Distributed Energy Resource Management Systems (DERMS): software solutions that control and coordinate a variety of distributed energy resources (DERs), such as solar, wind, and battery storage, to improve the grid efficiency and reliability.
  - Distributed Database Management Systems (DDBMS): software systems that manage and access data that are distributed across multiple nodes, such as relational, NoSQL, or graph databases, to improve the data availability and scalability.
  - Distributed Machine Learning and AI: software frameworks and platforms that enable the distributed training and inference of machine learning and AI models, such as TensorFlow, PyTorch, or Spark MLlib, to improve the model accuracy and performance.
