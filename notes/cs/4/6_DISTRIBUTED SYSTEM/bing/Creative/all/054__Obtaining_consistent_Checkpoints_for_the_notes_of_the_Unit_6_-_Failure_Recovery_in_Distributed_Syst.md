### Obtaining consistent Checkpoints for the notes of the Unit 6 - Failure Recovery in Distributed Systems in the subject of DISTRIBUTED SYSTEM

- Checkpointing is a technique to save the state of a distributed system periodically on stable storage, so that it can be recovered in case of failures .
- A checkpoint is a snapshot of the local state of a process or a component in the system .
- A consistent checkpoint is a set of checkpoints that represents a global state of the system that could have occurred during the normal execution of the system .
- A consistent checkpoint is desirable because it preserves the causal dependencies among the events in the system and avoids the domino effect, which is the situation where a process has to roll back to an earlier checkpoint because of the rollback of another process .
- There are two main approaches for obtaining consistent checkpoints in a distributed system:
  - Coordinated checkpointing: In this approach, all the processes in the system coordinate with each other to take checkpoints at the same time or in a consistent order. This ensures that the checkpoints are consistent by construction and no communication is needed during recovery. However, this approach may introduce synchronization overhead and blocking time for the processes .
  - Uncoordinated checkpointing: In this approach, each process takes checkpoints independently and asynchronously, without any coordination with other processes. This reduces the synchronization overhead and blocking time, but may result in inconsistent checkpoints. Therefore, communication is needed during recovery to find a consistent set of checkpoints among the saved ones, using algorithms such as the Z-paths algorithm or the minimum-process set algorithm .
- A hybrid approach that combines the advantages of both coordinated and uncoordinated checkpointing is also possible, such as the communication-induced checkpointing, which triggers checkpoints based on the message exchange among the processes.

- A mnemonic to remember the two main approaches for obtaining consistent checkpoints is: **C**oordinated checkpointing is **C**onsistent by **C**onstruction, while **U**ncoordinated checkpointing is **U**nsynchronized but may be **U**nconsistent.
- A learning trick to understand the concept of consistent checkpoints is to imagine a distributed system as a group of people who are working on a project and need to save their work periodically. A consistent checkpoint is like a snapshot of the project that shows the progress and the contributions of each person at a certain point in time. A coordinated checkpointing is like asking everyone to stop working and save their work at the same time, while an uncoordinated checkpointing is like letting everyone save their work whenever they want, without telling others. The former ensures that the snapshot is consistent, but may interrupt the workflow, while the latter allows more flexibility, but may result in a snapshot that does not reflect the actual state of the project.
