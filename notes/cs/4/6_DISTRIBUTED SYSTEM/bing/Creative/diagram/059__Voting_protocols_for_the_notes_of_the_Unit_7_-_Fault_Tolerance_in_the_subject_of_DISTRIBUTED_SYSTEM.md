Voting protocols are a technique for achieving fault tolerance in distributed systems, where a set of nodes have to agree on a common value or action. Voting protocols can also provide security against malicious attacks or faulty nodes. There are different types of voting protocols, such as exact voting, where all nodes have to agree on the same value, or inexact voting, where nodes can tolerate some deviation from the exact value. Voting protocols typically involve two phases: a voting phase, where nodes send their votes to a coordinator or to each other, and a decision phase, where the coordinator or the nodes decide on the final value based on the votes received.
