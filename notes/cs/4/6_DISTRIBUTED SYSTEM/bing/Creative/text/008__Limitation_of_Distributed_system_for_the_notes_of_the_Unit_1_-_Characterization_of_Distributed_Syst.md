### Limitation of Distributed System

A distributed system is a collection of independent and autonomous computers that communicate and cooperate with each other through message passing. To an external observer, a distributed system appears as a single coherent system. However, distributed systems face some challenges and limitations that affect their design and implementation. Some of the major limitations of distributed systems are:

- **Absence of a Global Clock**: In a distributed system, there is no single clock that synchronizes all the computers. Each computer has its own local clock that may run at a different rate or granularity, leading to asynchronous behavior. This makes it difficult to order events, collect consistent information, and schedule processes in a distributed system. Although some algorithms exist to synchronize clocks in a distributed system, they are not perfect and have some overhead and error. The absence of a global clock also makes it hard to design and debug distributed algorithms.

- **Absence of Shared Memory**: In a distributed system, there is no physically shared memory among the computers. Each computer has its own private memory that is not accessible by other computers. This means that there is no global state or view of the system that is shared by all the computers. To share information, computers have to exchange messages, which may be delayed, lost, or corrupted by the network. The absence of shared memory also makes it challenging to detect and resolve conflicts, ensure consistency, and verify global properties of the system. The global state of the system is distributed among many computers, and each computer only has a partial and possibly outdated view of the system.
