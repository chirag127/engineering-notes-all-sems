### Architectural models for the notes of the Unit 1 - Characterization of Distributed Systems in the subject of DISTRIBUTED SYSTEM

Architectural models are a type of system model that describe the organization of components across the network and their interrelationship. They help to design and analyze distributed systems by abstracting away the details of implementation and communication. Some common architectural models are:

- **Client-server model**: This model consists of two types of components: clients and servers. Clients request services from servers, and servers provide services to clients. Servers are usually more powerful and reliable than clients, and can handle multiple concurrent requests. Clients and servers communicate using a request-reply protocol. Examples of client-server systems are web applications, email systems, and online banking systems.
- **Peer-to-peer model**: This model consists of a set of peers that are both clients and servers. Peers can request and provide services to each other, without relying on a central authority or coordinator. Peers communicate using a message-passing protocol, and can join and leave the network dynamically. Peers are usually less powerful and reliable than servers, and have limited resources. Examples of peer-to-peer systems are file-sharing systems, distributed hash tables, and blockchain systems.
- **Multi-tier model**: This model consists of multiple layers of components, each providing a different level of abstraction and functionality. The components in each layer communicate with the components in the adjacent layers, and hide the details of the lower layers from the higher layers. The most common multi-tier model is the three-tier model, which consists of a presentation layer, a business logic layer, and a data layer. Examples of multi-tier systems are e-commerce systems, social media systems, and online gaming systems.
- **Service-oriented model**: This model consists of a set of loosely coupled and interoperable services that provide functionality to other services or applications. Services are self-contained, self-describing, and reusable components that communicate using a standard protocol, such as SOAP or REST. Services can be composed and orchestrated to create complex applications, and can be discovered and accessed using a service registry. Examples of service-oriented systems are web services, cloud computing systems, and microservices systems.
