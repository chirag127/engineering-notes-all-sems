### Transaction recovery for the notes of the Unit 9 - Distributed Transactions in the subject of DISTRIBUTED SYSTEM

- Transaction recovery is the process of restoring the consistency and correctness of a distributed database after a transaction failure or a system crash.
- Transaction recovery involves two main steps: undoing the effects of incomplete or aborted transactions, and redoing the effects of committed transactions that were not reflected in the database due to the crash.
- Transaction recovery requires the use of logs, which are records of the actions performed by transactions on the database. Logs can be stored either locally at each site, or globally at a central site, or both.
- Transaction recovery can be classified into two types: backward recovery and forward recovery.
- Backward recovery, also known as undo recovery, is the process of restoring the database to a consistent state before the failure by undoing the effects of incomplete or aborted transactions. Backward recovery requires the use of undo logs, which store the old values of the data items modified by transactions.
- Forward recovery, also known as redo recovery, is the process of restoring the database to a consistent state after the failure by redoing the effects of committed transactions that were not reflected in the database due to the crash. Forward recovery requires the use of redo logs, which store the new values of the data items modified by transactions.
- Transaction recovery can also be classified into two types based on the granularity of the recovery: local recovery and global recovery.
- Local recovery is the process of recovering a single site or a single transaction from a failure. Local recovery can be performed independently by each site, without the need for coordination with other sites.
- Global recovery is the process of recovering the entire distributed database from a failure. Global recovery requires the coordination of all the sites involved in the distributed transaction, and the use of a global commit protocol, such as the two-phase commit protocol, to ensure atomicity and durability of the transaction.
