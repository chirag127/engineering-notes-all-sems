### Fundamental Models for the notes of the Unit 1 - Characterization of Distributed Systems in the subject of DISTRIBUTED SYSTEM

Fundamental models are descriptions of properties that are present in all distributed systems. They help us to understand the basic characteristics, challenges and solutions of distributed systems. There are four main types of fundamental models:

- Interaction models: These models deal with the issues of communication and coordination between processes in a distributed system. They include aspects such as performance, timing, ordering and synchronization of events. Interaction models can be classified into two categories: synchronous and asynchronous. In a synchronous model, there are known bounds on the time to execute a step, the message transmission delay and the clock drift rate. In an asynchronous model, there are no such assumptions and events can occur in any order. Synchronous models are simpler to design and reason about, but they are less realistic and less efficient than asynchronous models.

- Failure models: These models specify the types of faults that can occur in processes and communication channels in a distributed system. They include aspects such as detection, recovery and tolerance of failures. Failure models can be classified into two categories: omission and arbitrary. In an omission failure, a process or a channel fails to perform an expected action, such as sending or receiving a message. In an arbitrary failure, a process or a channel behaves in an unpredictable and malicious way, such as sending incorrect or inconsistent messages. Omission failures are easier to handle than arbitrary failures, but they are less realistic and less robust than arbitrary failures.

- Security models: These models describe the threats and attacks that can compromise the integrity, confidentiality and availability of processes and communication channels in a distributed system. They include aspects such as authentication, authorization, encryption and auditing. Security models can be classified into two categories: passive and active. In a passive attack, an adversary observes or eavesdrops on the communication without modifying it. In an active attack, an adversary modifies or injects messages into the communication. Passive attacks are harder to detect than active attacks, but they are less damaging and less disruptive than active attacks.

- Architectural models: These models define the structure and organization of processes and communication channels in a distributed system. They include aspects such as distribution, replication, coordination and abstraction. Architectural models can be classified into two categories: software and system. In a software architectural model, the processes are organized into logical layers, objects, data or events. In a system architectural model, the processes are organized into physical nodes, such as clients, servers or peers. Software architectural models are more abstract and flexible than system architectural models, but they are less concrete and less efficient than system architectural models.
