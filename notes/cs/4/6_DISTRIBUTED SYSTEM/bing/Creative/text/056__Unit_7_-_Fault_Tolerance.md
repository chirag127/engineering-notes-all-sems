## Unit 7 - Fault Tolerance

- Fault tolerance is the ability of a system to continue functioning correctly in the presence of failures.
- Fault tolerance can be achieved by using techniques such as redundancy, replication, checkpointing, recovery, and reconfiguration.
- Redundancy is the provision of extra components or resources that can take over the functionality of a failed component or resource.
- Replication is the creation of multiple copies of data or processes that can be accessed or executed in parallel or in case of failure.
- Checkpointing is the saving of the state of a process or a system at regular intervals, so that it can be restored to a consistent state in case of failure.
- Recovery is the process of restoring the state or functionality of a system after a failure, by using checkpoints, backups, or other methods.
- Reconfiguration is the process of changing the structure or configuration of a system after a failure, by adding, removing, or replacing components or resources.
