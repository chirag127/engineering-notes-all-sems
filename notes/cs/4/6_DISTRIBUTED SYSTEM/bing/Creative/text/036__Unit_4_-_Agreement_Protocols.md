## Unit 4 - Agreement Protocols

- Agreement protocols are a class of distributed algorithms that allow a set of processes to reach a consensus on a value or a decision, despite the possibility of failures or malicious behavior.
- Agreement protocols are useful for solving problems such as leader election, distributed commit, atomic broadcast, and Byzantine fault tolerance.
- Agreement protocols can be classified into different types based on the assumptions they make about the system model, such as the number of processes, the communication channels, the failure modes, and the synchrony.
- Some common types of agreement protocols are:
  - **Majority voting**: Each process proposes a value and collects the votes from a majority of processes. The value with the most votes is chosen as the consensus value. This protocol can tolerate up to (n-1)/2 crash failures, where n is the number of processes.
  - **Paxos**: Each process acts as a proposer, an acceptor, or a learner. A proposer proposes a value and tries to get a majority of acceptors to accept it. An acceptor can accept at most one value per round. A learner learns the accepted value from the acceptors. This protocol can tolerate up to (n-1)/2 crash failures and ensures safety and liveness under asynchronous and partially synchronous models.
  - **Raft**: Each process acts as a leader, a follower, or a candidate. A leader is elected by a majority of processes and proposes a value to the followers. A follower can accept or reject the value from the leader. A candidate can initiate a leader election if it does not hear from the leader for a timeout period. This protocol can tolerate up to (n-1)/2 crash failures and ensures safety and liveness under a partially synchronous model.
  - **Two-phase commit**: Each process acts as a coordinator or a participant. A coordinator initiates a transaction and sends a request to the participants. A participant can vote to commit or abort the transaction. The coordinator collects the votes and decides to commit or abort the transaction. This protocol can tolerate up to one crash failure and ensures atomicity and consistency under a synchronous model.
  - **Three-phase commit**: Each process acts as a coordinator or a participant. A coordinator initiates a transaction and sends a request to the participants. A participant can vote to commit or abort the transaction. The coordinator collects the votes and sends a pre-commit or abort message to the participants. The participants acknowledge the pre-commit or abort message. The coordinator sends a commit or abort message to the participants. This protocol can tolerate up to one crash failure and ensures atomicity and consistency under an asynchronous model.
  - **Byzantine agreement**: Each process proposes a value and sends it to all other processes. Each process collects the values from all other processes and applies a deterministic function to compute the consensus value. This protocol can tolerate up to (n-1)/3 Byzantine failures, where n is the number of processes, and ensures validity and agreement under a synchronous model.
