### Group
- A group is a set of elements with an operation that combines two elements to produce another element.
- The operation must be associative, there must be an identity element, and each element must have an inverse.
- Examples: Integer addition, multiplication, and modular arithmetic.

### Field
- A field is a set of elements on which two binary operations addition, subtraction, multiplication, and division are defined. They satisfy certain axioms similar to the real numbers.
- Finite fields consist of a finite set of elements and are denoted as GF(p) where p is a prime number. They have applications in number theory and cryptography.

**Mnemonic:** Think of fields as flat platforms where you can perform all the usual operations - then realize that finite fields have limited elements (because they are flat plains with fences around them).

### Modular Arithmetic
- In modular arithmetic, numbers "wrap around" upon reaching a certain value called the modulo.
- To find a modulo b, you find the remainder when a is divided by b.
- Modular arithmetic follows similar rules of addition, subtraction, and multiplication as regular arithmetic but the remainders are the results.
- Important in cryptography for encryption schemes and generating random numbers.

### Prime and Relative Prime Numbers
- A prime number is an integer greater than 1 that cannot be formed by multiplying two smaller natural numbers.
- Two numbers are relative prime if they share no common factors other than 1.
- The Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic states that every integer greater than 1 can be written as a unique product of prime numbers.
- Relative prime numbers are important in number theory for proving theorems and in cryptography for the security of some encryption schemes.
