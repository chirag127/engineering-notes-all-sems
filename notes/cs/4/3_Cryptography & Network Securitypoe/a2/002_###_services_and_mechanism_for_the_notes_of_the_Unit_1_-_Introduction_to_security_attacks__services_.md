### Services and mechanism for the notes of the Unit 1 - Introduction to security attacks, services and mechanism, Classical encryption techniquessubstitution ciphers and transposition ciphers, cryptanalysis, steganography, Stream and block ciphers. Modern Block Ciphers:

1. Substitution ciphers: In substitution ciphers, each letter of the plain text is replaced by another letter or symbol. e.g. Caesar cipher. Mnemonic: Think of substituting each letter with another letter.
2. Transposition ciphers: In transposition ciphers, the position of the letters are changed according to a permutation. e.g. Rail fence cipher. Mnemonic: Think of changing the position of letters, transposing their order.
3. Cryptanalysis: The study of methods to obtain the meaning of encrypted information without access to the secret information which is normally required to do so. e.g. Frequency analysis, brute force attack.
4. Steganography: The practice of hiding a secret message within a larger one in such a way that others cannot discern the presence or contents of the hidden message. e.g. Invisible ink, digital images.
5. Stream ciphers: In stream ciphers, each plaintext digit is encrypted one at a time with the corresponding digit of a pseudorandom cipher digit stream. e.g. Vernam cipher.
6. Block ciphers: In block ciphers, the plain text is divided into blocks of letters and each block is encrypted separately. e.g. DES, AES. Mnemonic: Think of encrypting blocks of letters together.
7. Shannon’s theory of confusion and diffusion: Confusion disguises the relationship between the plaintext and cipher text. Diffusion spreads out the redundancy of the plaintext over the cipher text. A secure cipher must have both confusion and diffusion.
8. Feistel structure: A design for block ciphers where the cipher text block is divided into two halves and the right half is encrypted using a function of the left half and the key. It is used in DES.
9. Data Encryption Standard (DES): A block cipher with a 64-bit block size and a 56-bit key. It uses 16 rounds of fiestel structure but is vulnerable to brute force attacks due to small key size.
10. Triple DES (3DES): A variant of DES that applies DES three times to each block with two/three different keys to strengthen it against attacks. It has a 112/168-bit key.
