### Finite Field of the Form GF(p)
- A finite field is a field with a finite number of elements. We denote a finite field with p elements as GF(p) where p is a prime number.
- In a finite field GF(p), the elements are the numbers {0, 1, 2, ..., p-1} and the field operations are performed modulo p. That is, for any two elements a and b in GF(p), a+b is computed as (a+b)%p and ab is computed as (a*b)%p.
- Some properties of finite fields:
-- GF(p) contains p elements.
-- Addition, subtraction, multiplication and division are defined modulo p.
-- Multiplication is commutative and associative.
-- There exists an additive and multiplicative identity (0 and 1 respectively).
-- Every element has an additive inverse.
-- The field is closed under addition, subtraction and multiplication.
-- Not every element has a multiplicative inverse. Only elements relatively prime to p have inverses.
- Applications of finite fields:
-- Used in constructing linear feedback shift registers which are used in stream ciphers.
-- Used in Reed-Solomon codes which are used for error correction.
-- Used in cryptography algorithms like elliptic curve cryptography and AES.
