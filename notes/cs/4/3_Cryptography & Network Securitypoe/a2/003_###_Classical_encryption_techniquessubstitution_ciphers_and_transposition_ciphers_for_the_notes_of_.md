Classical Encryption Techniques
Substitution Ciphers
- In substitution ciphers, each letter of the plaintext is replaced by another letter or symbol.
- Examples: Caesar cipher, monoalphabetic cipher, homophonic substitution cipher, etc.
- Mnemonics: Think of substituting each letter of the plaintext with another letter or symbol.

Transposition Ciphers
- In transposition ciphers, the positions of the letters are rearranged (permuted) to encrypt the plaintext.
- Example: Rail fence cipher, route cipher, etc.
- Mnemonics: Think of shuffling or rearranging the positions of the letters of the plaintext to encrypt.

Cryptanalysis
- Cryptanalysis refers to methods of obtaining the meaning of encrypted information, without access to the secret key.
- Methods: Ciphertext-only attack, Known-plaintext attack, Chosen-plaintext attack, etc.
- Steganography refers to hiding the existence of a secret message.

Stream and Block Ciphers
- Stream ciphers encrypt plaintext digit-by-digit or bit-by-bit using a pseudorandom cipher digit/bit stream.
- Block ciphers encrypt plaintext in blocks of bits of a fixed size.

Modern Block Ciphers
- Block ciphers principles: Confusion and diffusion
- Shannon's theory of confusion and diffusion
- Feistel structure:Encrypts blocks of plaintext bits in multiple rounds
- Data Encryption Standard (DES): A popular block cipher with 64-bit block size and 56-bit key
- Strength of DES: Can be cracked easily due to small key size
- Idea of differential cryptanalysis: Analyze how differences in plaintext propagate to differences in ciphertext
- Block cipher modes of operation: ECB, CBC, CFB, OFB, CTR modes
- Triple DES (3DES): Applies DES cipher 3 times with 2/3 different keys to strengthen DES
