## Unit 1 - Introduction to security attacks, services and mechanisms

### Classical encryption techniques
- Substitution ciphers: In substitution ciphers, each letter of the plaintext is replaced by another letter or symbol. Examples: Caesar cipher, Monoalphabetic cipher. Mnemonics: Think of substituting each letter with another letter or symbol.
- Transposition ciphers: In transposition ciphers, the positions of the letters in the plaintext are rearranged to form the ciphertext. Example: Rail fence cipher. Mnemonics: Think of changing the positions of letters like arranging rails in a fence.

### Cryptanalysis
- Techniques to analyze and break ciphers without knowing the key. Example: Frequency analysis.
- Steganography: Hiding the existence of a message. Examples: Invisible ink, Digital watermarking.

### Stream and block ciphers
- Stream ciphers: Each plaintext digit is encrypted one at a time with the corresponding digit of a keystream. Example: Vernam cipher.
- Block ciphers: The plaintext is divided into blocks and each block is encrypted. Examples: DES, AES.

### Modern block ciphers
- Block cipher principles: Confusion and diffusion by Shannon. Feistel structure.
- Data Encryption Standard (DES): Algorithm, encryption process, key sizes, strength.
- Idea of differential cryptanalysis: How block ciphers can be attacked by analyzing differences in plaintexts and ciphertexts.
- Block cipher modes of operation: ECB, CBC, CFB, OFB, CTR. Usage and security.
- Triple DES: Encrypt-decrypt-encrypt method to increase strength of DES.
