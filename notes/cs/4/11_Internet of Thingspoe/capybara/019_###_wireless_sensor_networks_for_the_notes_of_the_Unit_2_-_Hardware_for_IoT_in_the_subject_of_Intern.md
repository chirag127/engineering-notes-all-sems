### Wireless Sensor Networks for the Notes of Unit 2 - Hardware for IoT in the Subject of Internet of Things

Wireless Sensor Networks (WSNs) are an important component of the Internet of Things (IoT) ecosystem. WSNs consist of small, low-cost, low-power sensors that are distributed in a network to monitor and collect data from the environment. This data is then transmitted wirelessly to a central location for processing and analysis in real-time.

#### Advantages of Wireless Sensor Networks

- WSNs are cost-effective as they require less wiring and infrastructure compared to traditional wired networks.
- They are easy to deploy and can be used in remote and inaccessible areas.
- WSNs are scalable and can be expanded by adding more sensors to the network.
- They provide real-time monitoring and data analysis, which helps in making timely decisions.
- They are capable of collecting data from multiple sources and can be integrated with other systems for data analysis.

#### Disadvantages of Wireless Sensor Networks

- The limited range of wireless sensors can be a challenge in large-scale deployments.
- The battery life of wireless sensors is limited, which requires frequent replacement or recharging.
- Wireless sensors are vulnerable to interference from other wireless devices, which can affect the quality of data transmission.

#### Applications of Wireless Sensor Networks

- Environmental Monitoring: WSNs can be used to monitor air quality, water quality, and soil conditions in remote areas.
- Industrial Automation: WSNs can be used to monitor industrial processes and equipment, reducing downtime and improving efficiency.
- Healthcare: WSNs can be used in healthcare to monitor patient health, track medication adherence, and detect falls or accidents.
- Smart Agriculture: WSNs can be used in agriculture to monitor soil moisture, temperature, and humidity, and to automate irrigation systems.
- Smart Homes and Buildings: WSNs can be used to monitor energy consumption, temperature, and humidity in homes and buildings, enabling energy-efficient management.

#### Learning Trick for Wireless Sensor Networks

One useful mnemonic for remembering the advantages of wireless sensor networks is "CRES": cost-effective, remote, expandable, and real-time.
