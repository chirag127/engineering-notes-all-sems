### Radio Frequency Identification (RFID) Technology for the Notes of Unit 2 - Hardware for IoT in the Subject of Internet of Things

Radio Frequency Identification (RFID) is a wireless communication technology that uses radio waves to automatically identify and track objects or people. RFID technology has become an essential component of the Internet of Things (IoT) ecosystem, enabling devices to communicate and exchange information seamlessly.

#### How Does RFID Technology Work?

RFID technology consists of two main components: a reader and a tag. The reader emits a radio frequency signal that is received by the tag, which responds with its unique identification information. The reader then captures the information and sends it to a backend system for processing and analysis.

#### Types of RFID Technology

There are two types of RFID technology: active and passive.

- Active RFID: In active RFID technology, the tag has its own power source, which enables it to transmit signals to the reader from a longer distance. Active RFID tags are commonly used for tracking high-value assets, such as vehicles, containers, and equipment.

- Passive RFID: In passive RFID technology, the tag does not have its own power source and relies on the energy transmitted by the reader to power it up. Passive RFID tags are typically used for tracking low-value items, such as retail products, documents, and inventory.

#### Advantages of RFID Technology

There are several advantages of RFID technology, including:

- Improved inventory management: RFID technology enables real-time tracking of inventory, reducing the need for manual counting and improving accuracy.

- Enhanced security: RFID technology can be used to monitor and control access to restricted areas, improving security and reducing the risk of unauthorized entry.

- Increased efficiency: RFID technology enables faster and more efficient data collection, reducing the time and cost associated with manual data entry.

#### Applications of RFID Technology

RFID technology has a wide range of applications across various industries, including:

- Retail: RFID technology is used for inventory management, reducing stock-outs and improving customer satisfaction.

- Manufacturing: RFID technology is used for asset tracking, reducing the risk of lost or stolen equipment and improving efficiency.

- Healthcare: RFID technology is used for patient tracking, reducing the risk of medication errors and improving patient safety.

#### Learning Tricks for RFID Technology

To remember the difference between active and passive RFID technology, you can use the following mnemonic:

- Active RFID: Active tags have their own power source, which means they can "act" independently of the reader.

- Passive RFID: Passive tags rely on the energy transmitted by the reader, which means they are "passive" in their communication with the reader.
