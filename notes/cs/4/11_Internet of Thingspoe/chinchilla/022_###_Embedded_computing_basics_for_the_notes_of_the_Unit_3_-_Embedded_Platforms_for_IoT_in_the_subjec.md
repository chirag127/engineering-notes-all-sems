### Embedded Computing Basics for the Notes of Unit 3 - Embedded Platforms for IoT in the Subject of Internet of Things

Embedded computing is the foundation of the Internet of Things (IoT) and involves the use of hardware and software in a small, specialized computer system. In this unit, we will learn about the basics of embedded computing and how it relates to IoT.

Here are some key points to remember:

1. Definition of Embedded Computing: Embedded computing refers to the use of hardware and software in a small, specialized computer system that is designed to perform a specific function or set of functions.

2. Characteristics of Embedded Computing: Embedded computing systems are typically characterized by their small size, low power consumption, and real-time operation. They are designed to perform specific tasks, and therefore have limited functionality compared to a general-purpose computer.

3. Embedded Systems Architecture: An embedded system consists of three main components: the hardware, the software, and the interface. The hardware includes the processor, memory, input/output (I/O) devices, and other components. The software includes the operating system and application software. The interface is the way in which the user interacts with the system.

4. Programming Languages: Embedded systems can be programmed using a variety of languages, including C, C++, and assembly language. C is the most commonly used language for embedded systems programming, due to its efficiency and flexibility.

5. Real-Time Operating Systems: Real-time operating systems (RTOS) are used in embedded systems to provide predictable and deterministic behavior. RTOSs allow the system to respond to external events in a timely manner, which is critical for many embedded applications.

6. Hardware Design Considerations: When designing an embedded system, several hardware factors must be considered, including power consumption, form factor, cost, and performance. The choice of processor, memory, and I/O devices will depend on the specific requirements of the application.

7. Advantages of Embedded Systems: Embedded systems offer several advantages over traditional computing systems, including lower cost, smaller size, and lower power consumption. They are also more reliable and can operate in harsh environments.

8. Applications of Embedded Systems: Embedded systems are used in a wide range of applications, including consumer electronics, medical devices, automotive systems, and industrial control systems.

Mnemonics and Learning Tricks:

- To remember the components of an embedded system, think of the acronym HIS (Hardware, Interface, Software).
- To remember the characteristics of embedded systems, think of the acronym SLP (Small size, Low power consumption, Real-time operation).
- To remember the advantages of embedded systems, think of the acronym CRL (Cost-effective, Reliable, Low power consumption).

In conclusion, understanding the basics of embedded computing is essential for anyone interested in the field of IoT. By learning about the hardware, software, and design considerations of embedded systems, as well as their advantages and applications, you will be better equipped to design and develop your own IoT devices.
