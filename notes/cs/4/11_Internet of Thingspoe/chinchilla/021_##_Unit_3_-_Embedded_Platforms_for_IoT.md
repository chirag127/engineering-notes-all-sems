## Unit 3 - Embedded Platforms for IoT

Embedded platforms refer to the hardware and software systems that are designed to perform specific functions within an IoT system. These platforms are critical components of an IoT system, as they provide the necessary computing power, connectivity, and storage to enable devices to interact with each other and with the cloud.

In this unit, we will explore the different types of embedded platforms that are commonly used in IoT systems, along with their advantages, disadvantages, and applications. We will also discuss some of the important considerations that need to be taken into account when selecting an embedded platform for an IoT system.

### Types of Embedded Platforms

1. Microcontrollers: Microcontrollers are small, low-power computing devices that are designed to control specific functions within an IoT system. They typically have limited processing power and memory, but are highly efficient and can be programmed to perform a wide range of tasks.

2. Single-board computers: Single-board computers are more powerful than microcontrollers and are capable of running more complex software applications. They are often used in IoT systems that require more advanced processing capabilities, such as machine learning or computer vision.

3. System-on-chip (SoC) devices: SoC devices are highly integrated circuits that combine multiple components, such as processors, memory, and connectivity, into a single package. They are commonly used in IoT systems that require high-performance computing and connectivity capabilities.

### Advantages of Embedded Platforms

- Low power consumption: Embedded platforms are designed to consume minimal power, which makes them ideal for IoT systems that rely on battery power or have limited access to power sources.

- Scalability: Embedded platforms can be easily scaled up or down depending on the requirements of the IoT system. This makes them highly flexible and adaptable to changing demands.

- Cost-effective: Embedded platforms are typically less expensive than traditional computing systems, which makes them a cost-effective solution for IoT systems.

### Disadvantages of Embedded Platforms

- Limited processing power: Embedded platforms have limited processing power and memory, which can limit their ability to perform complex tasks.

- Limited connectivity options: Some embedded platforms may have limited connectivity options, which can restrict their ability to communicate with other devices or the cloud.

### Important Considerations for Selecting an Embedded Platform

1. Processing power and memory: The processing power and memory of an embedded platform should be sufficient to perform the required tasks within the IoT system.

2. Connectivity options: The embedded platform should have the necessary connectivity options to communicate with other devices and the cloud.

3. Power consumption: The power consumption of the embedded platform should be low enough to meet the power requirements of the IoT system.

4. Cost: The cost of the embedded platform should be within the budget of the IoT system.

### Mnemonic

A helpful mnemonic to remember the types of embedded platforms is "MiSs SoC" - Microcontrollers, Single-board computers, and System-on-chip devices.

Overall, understanding the different types of embedded platforms and their advantages and disadvantages is crucial for developing and deploying effective IoT systems. By carefully considering the processing power, connectivity options, power consumption, and cost of different embedded platforms, developers can select the most appropriate platform for their specific IoT application.
