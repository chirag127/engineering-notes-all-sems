### Overview of IOT supported Hardware platforms such as Arduino for the notes of the Unit 3 - Embedded Platforms for IoT in the subject of Internet of Things

Internet of Things (IoT) is an emerging field that aims to connect everyday devices to the internet, allowing them to collect and share data. Hardware platforms such as Arduino have become increasingly popular for building IoT applications due to their ease of use, low cost, and versatility.

Here are some key points to keep in mind about IOT supported Hardware platforms such as Arduino:

1. Arduino is an open-source platform that consists of a hardware board and a software development environment. The board contains a microcontroller that can be programmed to control various sensors and actuators.

2. Arduino boards come in different shapes and sizes, each with its own set of features and capabilities. Some popular boards include the Arduino Uno, Mega, and Nano.

3. Arduino boards can be programmed using a variety of programming languages, including C++, Python, and Scratch. The Arduino IDE (Integrated Development Environment) is a user-friendly software tool that makes it easy to write and upload code to the board.

4. Arduino boards can be connected to a variety of sensors and actuators, such as temperature sensors, motion sensors, and motors. This allows them to collect data from the environment and perform actions based on that data.

5. Arduino boards can communicate with other devices over a variety of communication protocols, such as Bluetooth, Wi-Fi, and Ethernet. This allows them to connect to the internet and interact with other IoT devices.

6. Arduino boards are low cost, making them an affordable option for hobbyists and students. They are also easy to use, with a large community of developers and enthusiasts who share code and provide support.

Mnemonics and learning tricks:

- Remember the phrase "A Microcontroller for Every Need" to help you remember the different types of Arduino boards available (Uno, Mega, Nano, etc.).
- Use the acronym IDE to remember the name of the software tool used to program Arduino boards (Integrated Development Environment).
