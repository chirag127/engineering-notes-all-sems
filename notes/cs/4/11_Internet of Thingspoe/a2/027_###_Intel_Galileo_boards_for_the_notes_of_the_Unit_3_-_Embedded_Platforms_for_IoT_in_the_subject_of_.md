### Intel Galileo boards for the notes of the Unit 3 - Embedded Platforms for IoT in the subject of Internet of Things

- Intel Galileo is a microcontroller board designed for prototyping and learning. It is based on the Intel Quark X1000 microprocessor.
- Some key features of Intel Galileo board are -
	- 32-bit Intel Pentium-class processor (400 MHz)
	- 256 MB DRAM
	- Mini-PCIe slot
	- 100Mb Ethernet port
	- Micro-SD slot
	- USB Host port
	- Arduino shield compatibility
- Intel Galileo boards are suitable for IoT projects as they have good processing power and connectivity options like Ethernet and Wi-Fi. They can run various Real-Time Operating Systems (RTOS) and high-level languages like Python and C/C++.
- Some pros of using Intel Galileo boards are -
	- High performance with Intel processor
	- Arduino shield compatibility provides a wide range of sensors and actuators
	- Support for Windows, Linux and Open source software
	- Good options for connectivity
- Some cons are -
	- Higher cost compared to basic microcontroller boards
	- Steeper learning curve
	- Limited community support compared to popular boards like Arduino and Raspberry Pi

- Overall, Intel Galileo boards are good options for intermediate to advanced level prototyping and learning for IoT projects if higher performance is needed and Arduino shields are going to be used. They provide a good platform to learn the capabilities of Intel processors and run advanced software. However, for beginners, other popular boards may be more suitable due to their large community support and lower cost.

- Here are some tips to remember -
	- Intel Quark X1000 processor for higher performance IoT projects
	- Arduino shield compatibility
	- Multiple connectivity and OS options
	- Higher cost and steep learning curve
