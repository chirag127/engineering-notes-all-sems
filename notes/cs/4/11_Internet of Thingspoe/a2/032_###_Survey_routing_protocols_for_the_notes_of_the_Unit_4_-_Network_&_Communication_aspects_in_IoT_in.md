### Survey routing protocols for the notes of the Unit 4 - Network & Communication aspects in IoT in the subject of Internet of Things

The following are the major routing protocols used in IoT:

1. **RPL (IPv6 Routing Protocol for Low power and Lossy Networks):**
- It is a distance vector routing protocol.
- It builds a destination oriented directed acyclic graph (DODAG).
- Nodes are connected in the form of a tree with the root as the destination.
- Uses objective functions to determine the best path.
- Supports IPv6 networks.
- Suitable for low power and lossy networks.

Mnemonic: RPL routes in tree topology towards the root.

2. **MQTT (Message Queuing Telemetry Transport):**
- It is a publish-subscribe based messaging protocol.
- It works on the principle of topics and subscribers.
- Publishers publish messages to a particular topic.
- Subscribers subscribe to a particular topic to receive messages.
- It is lightweight and hence suitable for IoT.
- It supports three qualities of service - At most once, At least once, Exactly once.

Mnemonic: MQTT relies on pub-sub model for message routing.

3. **CoAP (Constrained Application Protocol):**
- It is a web transfer protocol designed for constrained networks and devices.
- It is similar to HTTP but is more lightweight.
- It uses UDP as the transport layer protocol.
- It supports four types of messages - CON, NON, ACK and RST.
- It is suitable for IoT applications due to its simplicity and minimal overhead.

Mnemonic: CoAP is like constrained HTTP over UDP for IoT.
