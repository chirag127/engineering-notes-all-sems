### Embedded computing basics

- Embedded systems are specialized computer systems that are part of a larger device or system. They are embedded as part of the complete device.
- They are designed to perform a specific task as part of the device and are not designed to be general purpose computers.
- They have software embedded into hardware. The hardware and software components are tightly coupled and the software is tailored to the hardware components.
- They typically have limited resources such as less memory and processing power as they are optimized for specific tasks.
- They must function reliably and meet strict performance constraints as they are critical parts of the device. Reliability and real-time performance are key requirements.
- They use microcontrollers or microprocessors as the core processing components. The software is written in languages suitable for resource-constrained environments such as C/C++.
- Sensing and actuation capabilities are interfaced to the embedded system to provide input and output interfaces to the system and surrounding environment.
- Examples include systems in vehicles, microwave ovens, washing machines, digital watches, etc.

**Mnemonics:**
E - Embedded; S - Specialized; L - Limited resources; R - Reliable; T - Tightly coupled hardware-software; U - Uses microcontrollers.

**Learning tips:**
- Understand key characteristics of embedded systems - specialized, limited resources, reliable, real-time, tightly coupled hardware-software.
- Know key components - microcontrollers, sensing and actuation interfaces.
- Learn by analyzing examples of embedded systems and their requirements and constraints.
- Get hands-on practice with development boards to understand embedded software development.
