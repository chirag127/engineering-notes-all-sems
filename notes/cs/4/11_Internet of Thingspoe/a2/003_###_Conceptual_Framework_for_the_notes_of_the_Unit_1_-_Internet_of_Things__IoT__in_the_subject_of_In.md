### Conceptual Framework for the notes of the Unit 1 - Internet of Things (IoT) in the subject of Internet of Things

- The Internet of Things (IoT) refers to the interconnection of everyday physical objects/devices through the internet, enabling them to send and receive data.
- IoT enables everyday objects to be connected to the internet and fitted with sensors and digital identifiers to capture/store/transmit data.
- The key components of IoT are:
    - Sensors: To sense and collect data from the environment. Eg: Temperature sensors, motion sensors, etc.
    - Microcontrollers: To process the data collected by sensors and control the devices. Eg: Arduino, Raspberry Pi, etc.
    - Connectivity: To enable the transfer of data over the internet. Eg: WiFi, Bluetooth, cellular data, etc.
    - Cloud: To store and analyze the data. Eg: AWS IoT, Azure IoT Hub, etc.
    - Applications: To access and view the data through interfaces. Eg: Mobile apps, web apps, etc.
- The potential benefits of IoT are:
    - Increased automation and optimized systems.
    - Better data-driven decision making.
    - Improved customer experiences.
    - Enhanced predictive analytics and maintenance.
- The potential risks and challenges of IoT are:
    - Data privacy and security concerns.
    - Interoperability issues between devices and platforms.
    - Reliability and downtime concerns.
    - Ethical issues surrounding data use and automation.
- Some applications of IoT are:
    - Smart homes (eg: smart thermostats, smart locks, smart speakers)
    - Smart cities (eg: smart transportation, smart utilities)
    - Industrial IoT (eg: predictive maintenance, supply chain monitoring)
    - Healthcare (eg: remote patient monitoring, smart medical devices)
