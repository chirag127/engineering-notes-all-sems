### Wireless Sensor Networks for the notes of the Unit 2 - Hardware for IoT in the subject of Internet of Things

Wireless Sensor Networks (WSNs) are networks of small, low-cost, low-power sensor nodes that collect and disseminate sensor data via wireless communications. WSNs are an important component of the Internet of Things and are used in many applications such as environmental monitoring, surveillance, and industrial monitoring.

Key points about WSNs:

- Sensor nodes are small, lightweight, and have limited power, computation, and memory resources.
- Sensor nodes collect and transmit sensor data such as temperature, motion, pressure, etc.
- Sensor nodes communicate via wireless signals and form a wireless ad-hoc network.
- Routing protocols are optimized for WSNs to efficiently route data and minimize power usage.
- Data aggregation and compression techniques are used to reduce the amount of transmitted data and save energy.
- applications: Environmental monitoring, Structural health monitoring, Smart homes, etc.

Advantages:

- Inexpensive and easy to deploy
- Can access previously inaccessible locations
- Provides fine-grained sensing and monitoring

Disadvantages:

- Limited power - networks have limited lifetime
- Limited range - wireless signals have limited range
- Vulnerable to attacks - wireless signals can be eavesdropped on or jammed
- Inaccurate sensor data - low-cost sensors can produce noisy or erroneous data

Some mnemonics/ tricks to remember:

- " Low power, low cost: WSNs are frugal "
- " Wireless signals have limited range "
- " Security challenges: wireless signal vulnerabilities "
