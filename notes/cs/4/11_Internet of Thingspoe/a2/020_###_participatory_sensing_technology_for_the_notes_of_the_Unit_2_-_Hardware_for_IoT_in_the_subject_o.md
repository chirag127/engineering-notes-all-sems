### Participatory Sensing Technology

- Participatory sensing refers to the collection of data by recruiting individuals and communities to share data from sensors, devices, and apps on their personal devices.
- The sensors in smartphones like accelerometer, gyroscope, camera, GPS, etc. are used to collect various data from the environment and the individuals.
- The collected data is then shared with the participatory sensing system which aggregates, analyzes and puts the data to use.
- Advantages:
    - Inexpensive as it utilizes the existing sensors and devices.
    - Scalable as many individuals and devices can participate.
    - Provides macro-level view by combining data from many users and devices.
- Disadvantages:
    - Data integrity and privacy can be issues as the data is crowdsourced.
    - Biases can creep in as the population of users and areas covered may not be representative.
    - Requires active participation and continued engagement from the users.
- Applications:
    - Traffic monitoring and management.
    - Noise and air pollution mapping.
    - Tracking spread of diseases.
    - Disaster management.
    - Urban planning.
- To remember:
    - Think of participatory sensing as crowd-sourced data collection.
    - The power lies in combining data from many users and devices.
    - Ensure diversity of participants and data verification for better results.
