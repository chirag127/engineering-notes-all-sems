### Definition for the notes of the Unit 1 - Internet of Things (IoT) in the subject of Internet of Things

- IoT or Internet of Things refers to the interconnection of everyday physical objects or 'things' and integration of hardware, software, sensors, and network connectivity which allows these devices to collect and exchange data.
- The core components of IoT are:
    - Sensors: They detect and respond to electrical or optical signals. They collect data from the surrounding environment. Eg: Temperature sensors, motion sensors, etc.
    - Connectivity: They connect the sensors and devices to the internet either through wired or wireless medium like Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, NFC, etc.
    - Processors: They process the data received from the sensors and determine appropriate actions to be taken. Eg: Microcontrollers.
    - Software: They help collect, store and analyze the data and control the devices. Eg: Real-time operating systems.
- The key characteristics of IoT are:
    - Always-on connectivity: The devices are always connected to the internet.
    - Ubiquitous computing: The devices are embedded in everyday physical objects.
    - Cloud computing: The data is processed and stored in the cloud.
    - Machine-to-machine communication: The devices can communicate with each other without human interaction.
- The applications of IoT include:
    - Smart homes: Smart thermostats, smart appliances, home security systems, etc.
    - Smart cities: Smart parking, smart lighting, environmental monitoring, etc.
    - Industrial IoT: Machine monitoring, predictive maintenance, remote diagnostics, etc.
    - Healthcare: Patient monitoring, medical device integration, remote surgery assistance, etc.
- The advantages of IoT are automation, convenience, data collection, and optimization while the disadvantages are security risks, technical issues, high costs, and lack of standards.
