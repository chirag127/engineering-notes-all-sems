### Radio Frequency Identification (RFID) Technology

- RFID is a wireless technology that uses tiny tags or chips to store identification data and a reader to retrieve the data wirelessly.
- The RFID tags contain an antenna to enable them to receive and respond to radio-frequency queries from an RFID reader.
- The RFID reader transmits an encoded radio signal to interrogate the tag. The tag receives the message and responds with its identification information.
- The RFID tags can be passive, active or semi-passive. Passive tags do not have an internal power source, they get activated by the energy in the radio signal from the reader. Active tags have an internal power source. Semi-passive tags have a small power source to power the tag's electronics but communicate using energy from the reader's signal.
- RFID tags can be read-write or read-only. Read-write tags can have their information modified or updated. Read-only tags contain information that cannot be modified.
- The main advantages of RFID are that it is wireless, the tags have no line of sight requirements and a large number of tags can be read simultaneously. The disadvantages are that RFID tags can be expensive, the technology can be vulnerable to interference and security risks exist with the data transmitted and stored on the tags.
- RFID has applications in access control, contactless payments, supply chain management and tracking assets. It enables fast and efficient identification, tracking and management of objects and people.
