### Design Principles for Connected Devices

The following are the key design principles for connected devices in IoT:

1. Interoperability: The devices should be able to connect and exchange data with each other seamlessly regardless of the manufacturer or technology used. Common standards and protocols should be followed for interoperability.
2. Scalability: The IoT system should be able to scale to billions of devices and exabytes of data easily without impacting performance, security or functionality. A scalable architecture and decentralized approaches are required.
3. Openness: The devices and systems should be built on open standards and APIs to encourage widespread adoption and innovation. Closed ecosystems restrict the growth of IoT.
4. Security: Security should be built-in and not bolted-on. Threats like malware, spoofing, eavesdropping, etc. should be protected against through data encryption, access control, device authentication, secure software/firmware updates, etc.
5. Privacy: The privacy of the data collected and exchanged should be ensured through appropriate policies and technologies like data anonymization and minimal data collection. Users should remain in control of their data.
6. Reliability: The IoT system should be robust and resilient against failures, errors and attacks to deliver a reliable performance and experience. Redundancy and fault-tolerance techniques are necessary.
7. Usability: The interactions with the connected devices should be simple, intuitive and seamless. The complexity should be hidden from the users to enable easy use of IoT.

The above principles would guide the designers and developers of connected devices and IoT systems to build sustainable, practical and useful IoT solutions. Following the principles would lead to interoperable, scalable, open, secure, private and reliable IoT implementations.
