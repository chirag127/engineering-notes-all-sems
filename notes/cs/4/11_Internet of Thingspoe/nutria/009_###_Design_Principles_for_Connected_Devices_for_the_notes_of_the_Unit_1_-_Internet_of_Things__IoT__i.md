
### Design Principles for Connected Devices for the notes of the Unit 1 - Internet of Things (IoT) in the subject of Internet of Things

Connected Devices are devices that are connected to a network, such as the Internet, and can interact with other devices on the network. In the context of the Internet of Things (IoT), connected devices are those that are connected to the Internet and can be used to control and monitor physical objects.

Design principles for connected devices should be based on the following:

1. Security: Security should be a top priority when designing connected devices. This includes ensuring that the device is secure from unauthorized access, and that data transmitted over the network is secure and encrypted.

2. Scalability: Connected devices should be designed to scale as the network grows. This means that the device should be able to handle a large number of requests and be able to handle a large amount of data.

3. Usability: Connected devices should be designed to be user-friendly and intuitive. This means that the user interface should be easy to understand and use.

4. Reliability: Connected devices should be reliable and should be able to withstand a variety of conditions, such as power outages, network outages, and environmental conditions.

5. Flexibility: Connected devices should be designed to be flexible and adaptable. This means that the device should be able to handle a variety of tasks and be able to be upgraded as new technologies become available.

6. Energy Efficiency: Connected devices should be designed to be energy efficient. This means that the device should use as little power as possible and should be able to run for long periods of time without needing to be recharged.

7. Cost Efficiency: Connected devices should be designed to be cost-effective. This means that the device should be able to be produced at a low cost and be able to be sold at a competitive price.

Mnemonics:

1. Secure Scalable Usable Reliable Flexible Efficient Cost Effective (SSURFCE)
