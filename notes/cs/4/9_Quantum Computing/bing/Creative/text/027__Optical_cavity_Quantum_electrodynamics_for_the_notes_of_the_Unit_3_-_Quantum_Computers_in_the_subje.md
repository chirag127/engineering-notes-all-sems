### Optical cavity Quantum electrodynamics

- Optical cavity Quantum electrodynamics (QED) is the study of the interaction between light confined in a reflective cavity and atoms or other particles, under conditions where the quantum nature of photons is significant.
- It could in principle be used to construct a quantum computer, by using the cavity field as a quantum bus to couple qubits encoded in atoms, ions, superconducting circuits or other systems.
- The simplest case of optical cavity QED is the Jaynes–Cummings model, which describes the interaction between a single two-level atom and a single cavity mode.
- The atom and the cavity mode can exchange energy and information through the process of coherent absorption and emission of photons, resulting in vacuum Rabi oscillations.
- The atom-cavity system can also be manipulated to create entangled states, such as Bell states or Schrödinger cat states, by controlling the interaction time or the cavity detuning.
- Optical cavity QED can also be extended to more complex scenarios, such as multiple atoms or modes, nonlinearity, dissipation, feedback, measurement, etc.
- Optical cavity QED has enabled many experimental breakthroughs in quantum optics, such as the observation of the Purcell effect, the strong coupling regime, the photon blockade effect, the cavity-mediated cooling and trapping of atoms, the generation of single photons and nonclassical light, the realization of quantum logic gates and algorithms, the implementation of quantum error correction and metrology, and the exploration of quantum phase transitions and many-body physics.
- Optical cavity QED has also been applied to various physical platforms, such as optical and microwave cavities, photonic crystals, plasmonic nanostructures, circuit QED, etc.
- Optical cavity QED is a rich and active field of research, with many open challenges and opportunities for advancing quantum science and technology.
