### Conditions for Quantum Computation

Quantum computation is the process of using quantum systems, such as qubits, to perform operations that are faster or more efficient than classical computation. Qubits are the basic units of quantum information, and they can exist in superposition of two states, usually denoted as |0> and |1>. Quantum computation also exploits other quantum phenomena, such as entanglement and interference, to manipulate qubits and achieve quantum speedup.

However, quantum computation faces many challenges, such as noise, decoherence, scalability, and error correction. To overcome these challenges and construct a practical quantum computer, certain conditions must be met. These conditions are known as the DiVincenzo criteria, proposed by the theoretical physicist David P. DiVincenzo in 2000. The DiVincenzo criteria consist of seven requirements that an experimental setup must satisfy to successfully implement quantum algorithms and quantum communication. They are:

1. **A scalable physical system with well-characterized qubits.** This means that the physical system that implements the qubits must be able to support a large number of qubits, and that the properties and interactions of the qubits must be well understood and controlled. For example, some possible physical systems for qubits are superconducting circuits, trapped ions, nuclear magnetic resonance, or photons.
2. **The ability to initialize the state of the qubits to a simple fiducial state.** This means that the qubits must be able to be prepared in a known and simple state, such as |0> or |1>, before the quantum computation begins. This is necessary to ensure that the input of the quantum algorithm is well defined and that the qubits are not affected by unwanted noise or previous operations.
3. **Long relevant decoherence times.** This means that the qubits must maintain their quantum coherence for a sufficiently long time, compared to the duration of the quantum computation. Quantum coherence is the property that allows qubits to exist in superposition and entanglement, and it is essential for quantum computation. However, quantum coherence is fragile and can be destroyed by interactions with the environment or other qubits. This process is called decoherence, and it limits the performance and accuracy of quantum computation. Therefore, the qubits must be isolated from sources of decoherence as much as possible, and the quantum computation must be completed before the qubits lose their coherence.
4. **A "universal" set of quantum gates.** This means that the qubits must be able to undergo a set of quantum operations, called quantum gates, that can perform any possible quantum computation. Quantum gates are the analogues of classical logic gates, and they act on one or more qubits to change their states. A universal set of quantum gates is a minimal set of quantum gates that can be combined to approximate any other quantum gate with arbitrary precision. For example, one possible universal set of quantum gates is the Hadamard gate, the phase gate, and the controlled-NOT gate.
5. **A qubit-specific measurement capability.** This means that the qubits must be able to be measured individually and accurately, without disturbing the other qubits or the quantum computation. Measurement is the process of extracting information from the qubits, and it is necessary to obtain the output of the quantum algorithm. However, measurement also collapses the quantum state of the qubits, and it can introduce errors or noise. Therefore, the measurement must be done carefully and selectively, and only at the end of the quantum computation.
6. **Interconverting stationary and flying qubits and faithfully transmitting flying qubits between specified locations.** This means that the qubits must be able to be converted between two types: stationary qubits, which are fixed in the physical system and used for quantum computation, and flying qubits, which are mobile and used for quantum communication. Moreover, the flying qubits must be able to be transmitted between different locations, such as different quantum computers or quantum devices, without losing their quantum information or coherence. This is necessary to enable quantum communication, which is the process of sending and receiving quantum information using quantum channels, such as photons or quantum entanglement.
7. **Faithfully transmitting flying qubits between specified locations.** This means that the flying qubits must be able to be transmitted between different locations, such as different quantum computers or quantum devices, without losing their quantum information or coherence.
