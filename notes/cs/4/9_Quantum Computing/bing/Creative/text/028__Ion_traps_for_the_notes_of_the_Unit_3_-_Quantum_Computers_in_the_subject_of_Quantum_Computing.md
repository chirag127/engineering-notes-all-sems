### Ion traps for the notes of the Unit 3 - Quantum Computers in the subject of Quantum Computing

- Ion traps are devices that can confine and manipulate charged atomic particles (ions) using electromagnetic fields.
- Ion traps can be used as a platform for quantum computing, where qubits are stored in stable electronic states of each ion, and quantum information can be transferred through the collective quantized motion of the ions in a shared trap.
- Ion traps can perform universal quantum computation by applying lasers to induce coupling between the qubit states (for single qubit operations) or coupling between the internal qubit states and the external motional states (for entanglement between qubits).
- Ion traps have demonstrated high accuracy and coherence in quantum operations, and are one of the most promising architectures for scalable and universal quantum computing.
- Ion traps can be scaled up by transporting ions to different locations in an array of traps, building large entangled states via photonically connected networks of remotely entangled ion chains, or combining these two approaches.
- Ion traps have achieved the largest number of controllably entangled qubits (20) as of April 2018, and have also implemented various quantum algorithms using few-ion-qubit systems.
- Ion traps face challenges such as decoherence, control errors, heating, and cross-talk, which require further research and development to overcome.
- Ion traps have been invented in the 1950s by Wolfgang Paul, who received the Nobel Prize for his work in 1989. The first proposal for a controlled-NOT quantum gate using ion traps was made by Ignacio Cirac and Peter Zoller in 1995, and the first experimental realization was achieved at NIST Ion Storage Group in the same year.
