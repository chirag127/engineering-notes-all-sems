### Measurement for the notes of the Unit 2 - Quantum Computation in the subject of Quantum Computing

- Measurement is a fundamental concept in quantum mechanics, where the outcome of an observation on a quantum system is generally probabilistic and non-deterministic.
- Measurement can also be used as a tool for quantum computation, where the processing of quantum information is achieved by performing local measurements on qubits that are prepared in a highly entangled state.
- Measurement-based quantum computation (MQC) is a framework that encompasses various models of quantum computation that rely on measurements as the main computational resource, such as the one-way quantum computer and the teleportation-based model.
- In MQC, the computation is driven by the choice of measurement bases and the adaptive feedback of the measurement outcomes, which determine the next measurements to be performed.
- MQC has several advantages over other models of quantum computation, such as the quantum circuit model, which requires the implementation of unitary transformations on qubits. Some of these advantages are:

  - MQC can be more robust against noise and errors, as the entangled state is prepared before the computation and the measurements are performed in parallel, reducing the decoherence time and the need for error correction.
  - MQC can be more efficient in terms of resources, as the entangled state can be reused for different computations and the measurements can be performed on any qubit, regardless of its location or connectivity.
  - MQC can provide new insights into the nature and power of quantum computation, as it reveals the role of entanglement as a computational resource and the connection between quantum computation and other branches of physics and mathematics.

- The standard process of MQC consists of three steps: entangle the qubits, measure the ancillae (auxiliary qubits) and correct the outputs. In the first step, the qubits are entangled in order to prepare the source state, which is usually a cluster state or a graph state. In the second step, the ancillae are measured in different bases, depending on the desired computation. In the third step, the outputs are corrected by applying classical operations on the remaining qubits, based on the measurement outcomes of the ancillae.
