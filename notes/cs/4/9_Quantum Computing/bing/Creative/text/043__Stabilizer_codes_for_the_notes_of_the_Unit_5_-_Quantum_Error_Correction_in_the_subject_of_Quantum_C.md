### Stabilizer codes for the notes of the Unit 5 - Quantum Error Correction in the subject of Quantum Computing

- Stabilizer codes are a class of quantum error-correcting codes that are based on the notion of stabilizer groups, which are subgroups of the Pauli group that commute with each other and do not contain -I.
- Stabilizer codes can be used to protect quantum information from errors due to noise, decoherence, or faulty operations by encoding a logical qubit into a larger number of physical qubits, such that the logical state is invariant under the action of the stabilizer group.
- Stabilizer codes can be characterized by their parameters [[n, k, d]], where n is the number of physical qubits, k is the number of logical qubits, and d is the minimum distance, which is the minimum weight of a non-trivial logical operator that can cause an error on the encoded state.
- Stabilizer codes can be constructed from classical linear codes by using the CSS construction, which maps a pair of classical codes C1 and C2 to a quantum code with the generator matrix G = [H2 | -H1], where H1 and H2 are the parity check matrices of C1 and C2, respectively.
- Stabilizer codes can also be represented by stabilizer circuits, which are quantum circuits that consist of Clifford gates and prepare the encoded state from the |0> state. Alternatively, stabilizer codes can be represented by stabilizer tables, which are matrices that list the generators of the stabilizer group and the logical operators in terms of Pauli matrices.
- Stabilizer codes can be decoded by using syndrome measurement, which is a procedure that measures the eigenvalues of the stabilizer generators and determines the error syndrome, which is a binary vector that indicates which generators are violated by the error. The syndrome can then be used to look up the most likely error from a table or to run an algorithm that finds the optimal correction.
- Stabilizer codes have many applications in quantum computing, such as fault-tolerant quantum computation, quantum cryptography, quantum metrology, and quantum error mitigation. Some examples of stabilizer codes are the Shor code, the Steane code, the surface code, and the toric code.
