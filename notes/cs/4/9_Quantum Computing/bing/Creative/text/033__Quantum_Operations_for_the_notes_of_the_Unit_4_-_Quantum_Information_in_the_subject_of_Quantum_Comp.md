### Quantum Operations

Quantum operations are transformations that a quantum mechanical system can undergo. They can describe the effects of unitary evolution, measurement, noise, and interaction with an environment. Quantum operations are important for quantum computation and quantum information, as they define how quantum information can be manipulated and processed.

Some properties of quantum operations are:

- They are linear, meaning that the operation on a linear combination of states is the same as the linear combination of the operation on each state.
- They are completely positive, meaning that they preserve the positivity of the density matrix, even when the system is part of a larger system.
- They are trace-preserving or trace-nonincreasing, meaning that they do not change or decrease the total probability of the system.

Quantum operations can be represented in different ways, such as:

- Kraus operators, which are a set of operators that act on the system and satisfy a completeness relation.
- Choi-Jamiolkowski isomorphism, which is a correspondence between quantum operations and quantum states.
- Stinespring dilation, which is a way of embedding a quantum operation into a unitary evolution of a larger system.

Some examples of quantum operations are:

- Quantum gates, which are unitary operations that act on one or more qubits and implement basic logic operations.
- Quantum measurements, which are operations that extract classical information from a quantum system and collapse its state.
- Quantum channels, which are operations that model the effects of noise and decoherence on a quantum system.
- Quantum instruments, which are operations that generalize quantum measurements and capture the classical and quantum information obtained.
