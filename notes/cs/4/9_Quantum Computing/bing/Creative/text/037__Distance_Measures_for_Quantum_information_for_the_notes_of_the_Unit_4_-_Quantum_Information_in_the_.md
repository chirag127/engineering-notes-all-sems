### Distance Measures for Quantum Information

- Distance measures for quantum information are mathematical tools that quantify how close or similar two quantum states are.
- Distance measures are useful for analyzing the performance and security of quantum information processing protocols, such as quantum communication, quantum cryptography, and quantum error correction.
- Distance measures can also reveal the properties and relations of quantum states, such as entanglement, coherence, and purity.
- There are many different distance measures for quantum information, each with its own advantages and disadvantages. Some of the most common ones are:

  - **Trace distance**: This is the quantum generalization of the Kolmogorov distance for classical probability distributions. It is defined as $$T(\rho, \sigma) = \frac{1}{2} \mathrm{Tr}|\rho - \sigma|,$$ where $|\rho - \sigma|$ is the absolute value of the difference of the density matrices. The trace distance is a metric, meaning that it satisfies the properties of positivity, symmetry, and triangle inequality. It also has a simple operational interpretation: it is the maximum probability of distinguishing two quantum states by a single measurement .
  - **Fidelity**: This is a measure of how much overlap or similarity there is between two quantum states. It is defined as $$F(\rho, \sigma) = \mathrm{Tr} \sqrt{\sqrt{\rho} \sigma \sqrt{\rho}},$$ where $\sqrt{\rho}$ is the unique positive square root of the density matrix $\rho$. The fidelity is not a metric, but it is related to several other distance measures, such as the Bures distance, the Hellinger distance, and the angle distance .
  - **Relative entropy**: This is the quantum generalization of the Kullback-Leibler divergence for classical probability distributions. It is defined as $$S(\rho \| \sigma) = \mathrm{Tr}(\rho \log \rho - \rho \log \sigma),$$ where $\log$ is the logarithm base 2. The relative entropy is not a metric, but it is non-negative and zero if and only if $\rho = \sigma$. It also has an information-theoretic interpretation: it is the difference between the von Neumann entropy of $\rho$ and the conditional entropy of $\rho$ given $\sigma$. It measures how much information is lost when $\sigma$ is used to approximate $\rho$ .
  - **Renyi entropy**: This is a family of quantum generalizations of the Renyi entropy for classical probability distributions. It is defined as $$H_a(\rho \| \sigma) = \frac{1}{a-1} \log \mathrm{Tr}(\rho^a \sigma^{1-a}),$$ where $a$ is a positive parameter. The Renyi entropy reduces to the relative entropy when $a \to 1$, and to the logarithm of the fidelity when $a \to 0$. It also has a sandwiched version, defined as $$\tilde{H}_a(\rho \| \sigma) = \frac{1}{a-1} \log \mathrm{Tr}(\sigma^{\frac{1-a}{2a}} \rho \sigma^{\frac{1-a}{2a}})^a.$$ The Renyi entropy and its sandwiched version have applications in quantum information theory, such as improving bounds on the concavity of quantum entropy .
