### Fault – Tolerant Quantum Computation

- Fault-tolerant quantum computation is the ability to perform reliable quantum computation in the presence of errors that affect the quantum system.
- Errors can arise from uncontrolled interactions with the environment (decoherence), imperfect implementations of quantum gates, or measurement errors.
- Fault-tolerant quantum computation requires the use of quantum error correction schemes, which encode quantum information in a redundant way and allow for the detection and correction of errors without disturbing the encoded information.
- Fault-tolerant quantum computation also requires the design of quantum circuits that avoid the propagation of errors and the implementation of fault-tolerant quantum gates that can operate on encoded qubits.
- Fault-tolerant quantum computation is possible if the physical error rate per gate is below a certain threshold, which depends on the quantum error correction code and the noise model. The threshold theorem states that the logical error rate can be suppressed to arbitrarily low levels by increasing the level of encoding and the number of qubits.
- Fault-tolerant quantum computation is essential for achieving large-scale quantum computation and communication, as well as for testing the fundamental limits of quantum mechanics.
