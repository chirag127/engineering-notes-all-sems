## Unit 5 - Quantum Error Correction

- Quantum error correction (QEC) is a set of methods to protect quantum information—that is, quantum states—from unwanted environmental interactions (decoherence) and other forms of noise.
- QEC is essential to achieve fault-tolerant quantum computing that can reduce the effects of noise on stored quantum information, faulty quantum gates, faulty quantum preparation, and faulty measurements.
- QEC codes do not always correctly decode logical qubits, but their use reduces the effect of noise and allows algorithms of greater circuit depth.
- QEC codes are based on spreading the information of one logical qubit onto a highly entangled state of several physical qubits.
- QEC codes can be classified into different types, such as bit flip codes, sign flip codes, Shor codes, bosonic codes, and general codes.
- QEC codes can be implemented using different models, such as the circuit model, the surface code model, the topological model, and the measurement-based model.
- QEC codes have been experimentally realized using various physical systems, such as trapped ions, superconducting qubits, photonic qubits, and nuclear magnetic resonance.
- QEC codes can also be used for quantum communication, where quantum states pass through noisy channels and need to be protected from errors.
- QEC codes are interesting objects in their own right, displaying rich forms of entanglement and quantum information theory.
