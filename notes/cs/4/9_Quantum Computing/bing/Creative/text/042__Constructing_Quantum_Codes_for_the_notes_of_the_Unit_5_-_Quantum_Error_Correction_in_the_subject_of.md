### Constructing Quantum Codes

Quantum codes are methods of encoding quantum information in such a way that errors caused by noise or decoherence can be detected and corrected. Quantum codes are essential for quantum information processing and quantum computation, as quantum systems are very sensitive to disturbances from the environment.

There are several ways of constructing quantum codes from classical codes, which are well-studied methods of encoding classical information in binary or non-binary symbols. Some of the most common methods are:

- **CSS construction**: This method, proposed by Calderbank and Shor  and Steane , allows quantum codes to be constructed from two classical linear codes that are related by orthogonality. A CSS code has the form $Q=C_1 \cap C_2^\perp$, where $C_1$ and $C_2$ are classical linear codes over $\mathbb{F}_q$ and $C_2^\perp$ is the dual code of $C_2$ with respect to the standard inner product. A CSS code can correct both bit-flip and phase-flip errors by using the syndrome of $C_1$ and $C_2$, respectively. Many examples of CSS codes can be obtained from classical codes such as Hamming codes, Reed-Muller codes, and cyclic codes.

- **Hermitian construction**: This method, proposed by Ashikhmin and Litsyn , allows quantum codes to be constructed from classical linear codes that contain their Hermitian dual codes. The Hermitian dual code of a code $C$ over $\mathbb{F}_{q^2}$ is defined as $C^\perp_H = \{x \in \mathbb{F}_{q^2}^n | x^* \cdot y = 0, \forall y \in C\}$, where $x^*$ is the conjugate of $x$ and $\cdot$ is the standard inner product. A Hermitian code has the form $Q=C \cap C^\perp_H$, where $C$ is a classical linear code over $\mathbb{F}_{q^2}$. A Hermitian code can correct both bit-flip and phase-flip errors by using the syndrome of $C$ and $C^\perp_H$, respectively. Many examples of Hermitian codes can be obtained from classical codes such as constacyclic codes, skew constacyclic codes, and Reed-Solomon codes.

- **Generalized CSS construction**: This method, proposed by Movassagh and Ouyang , allows quantum codes to be constructed from any classical code, not necessarily linear or self-orthogonal. The idea is to map the classical codewords to basis states of a quantum code space, and then use a quantum circuit to transform the basis states into a superposition of orthogonal states that can be error-corrected. The quantum circuit consists of two parts: a classical encoder that maps the classical codewords to binary strings, and a quantum encoder that maps the binary strings to quantum states. The quantum encoder can be designed using the stabilizer formalism or the CSS construction. This method can generate quantum codes with linear distance and constant rate from classical codes with linear distance and rate. It can also embed quantum codes in the ground space of local Hamiltonians, which can be useful for quantum LDPC codes and quantum error correction using physics.
