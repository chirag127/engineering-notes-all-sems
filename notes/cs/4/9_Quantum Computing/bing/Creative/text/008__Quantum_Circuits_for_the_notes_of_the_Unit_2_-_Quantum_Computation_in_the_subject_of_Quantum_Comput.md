### Quantum Circuits

- A quantum circuit is a model for quantum computation, similar to classical circuits, in which a computation is a sequence of quantum gates, measurements, initializations of qubits to known values, and possibly other actions.
- A quantum gate is a basic unitary operation that acts on one or more qubits, such as the Hadamard gate, the Pauli gates, the CNOT gate, etc. Quantum gates can be represented by matrices and can be composed to form larger quantum circuits.
- A quantum wire is a line that carries a qubit from one gate to another, or to a measurement device. Quantum wires can be entangled, meaning that the states of the qubits they carry are correlated in a non-classical way.
- A quantum circuit can be represented by a diagram, in which the horizontal lines are quantum wires and the boxes or symbols are quantum gates. The input qubits are on the left and the output qubits are on the right. Optionally, the diagram can also show the measurements and the classical bits that result from them.
- A quantum circuit can also be represented by a mathematical expression, in which the quantum gates are denoted by their matrix symbols and the qubits are denoted by their basis vectors. The expression is read from right to left, following the order of the gates in the diagram.
- A quantum circuit can be simulated by a classical computer, using linear algebra and complex numbers, but the computational cost grows exponentially with the number of qubits and gates. Alternatively, a quantum circuit can be implemented by a quantum computer, using physical systems that can realize qubits and quantum gates, such as superconducting circuits, trapped ions, photons, etc.
- Quantum circuits are designed to perform quantum algorithms, which are computational tasks that can benefit from the quantum phenomena of superposition and entanglement. Some examples of quantum algorithms are Shor's algorithm for factoring large numbers, Grover's algorithm for searching unsorted databases, quantum error correction codes for protecting quantum information, etc.
- Quantum circuits are also studied as models of quantum many-body physics, in which the quantum gates and measurements can induce complex and universal collective phenomena, such as thermalization, chaos, entanglement growth, phase transitions, etc. Random quantum circuits are a special class of quantum circuits that use random gates and measurements, and can be analyzed using tools from statistical mechanics, information theory, and quantum field theory.
