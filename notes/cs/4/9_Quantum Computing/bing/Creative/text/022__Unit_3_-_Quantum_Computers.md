## Unit 3 - Quantum Computers

- A quantum computer is a computer that exploits quantum mechanical phenomena. At small scales, physical matter exhibits properties of both particles and waves, and quantum computing leverages this behavior using specialized hardware. 
- A quantum computer uses qubits (quantum bits) to store and manipulate information. Unlike classical bits, which can only be in one of two states (0 or 1), qubits can be in a superposition of both states simultaneously, until they are measured. 
- Qubits can also exhibit quantum entanglement, which means that two or more qubits can share a quantum state and influence each other, even when they are physically separated. 
- Quantum computers can perform certain tasks faster and more efficiently than classical computers, by exploiting quantum parallelism and interference. For example, quantum computers can factor large numbers, search large databases, and simulate quantum systems much faster than classical computers.  
- Quantum computers are still in the early stages of development, and face many challenges, such as maintaining quantum coherence, minimizing noise and errors, and scaling up the number and quality of qubits.  
- Quantum computers are expected to have a significant impact on various fields, such as cryptography, artificial intelligence, chemistry, physics, and medicine.
