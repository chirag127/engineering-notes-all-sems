### Theory of Quantum Error –Correction

- Quantum error correction (QEC) is used in quantum computing to protect quantum information from errors due to decoherence and other quantum noise.
- QEC is essential to achieve fault tolerant quantum computing that can reduce the effects of noise on stored quantum information, faulty quantum gates, faulty quantum preparation, and faulty measurements.
- QEC codes do not always correctly decode logical qubits, but their use reduces the effect of noise.
- QEC codes are based on encoding states into larger Hilbert spaces subject to known interactions.
- QEC codes have necessary and sufficient conditions for the perfect recovery of an encoded state after its degradation by an interaction.
- QEC codes depend only on the behavior of the logical states.
- QEC codes cannot use the classical method of copying information due to the no-cloning theorem.
- QEC codes use entanglement to spread the information of one qubit onto a highly entangled state of several qubits.
- QEC codes use ancilla qubits to protect the logical qubit, or use a lattice of qubits arranged on a surface such as a torus.
- QEC codes can be classified into different classes, such as stabilizer codes, which use Pauli operators to detect and correct errors, or topological codes, which use geometric properties to encode and manipulate information.
- QEC codes face various challenges in practical implementation, such as physical limitations, resource constraints, error models, and decoding algorithms.
