### Entanglement as a physical resource for the notes of the Unit 5 - Quantum Error Correction in the subject of Quantum Computing

- Quantum entanglement is a phenomenon in which two or more quantum systems, such as qubits, are correlated in such a way that their quantum states cannot be described independently, even when they are physically separated.
- Quantum entanglement is a physical resource, like energy, that can be measured, transformed, and purified. It can be used to perform tasks that are impossible or inefficient with classical resources, such as quantum cryptography, superdense coding, and quantum teleportation.
- Quantum entanglement is essential for quantum computing, as it enables the creation and manipulation of quantum superpositions and quantum interference, which are the basis of quantum algorithms and quantum error correction.
- Quantum entanglement can be generated and detected in various physical systems, such as superconducting qubits, trapped ions, photons, and spins. The degree and type of entanglement present in a quantum state can be quantified by various entanglement measures, such as entanglement entropy, concurrence, and negativity.
- Quantum entanglement can be affected by noise and decoherence, which reduce the coherence and fidelity of the quantum state. Therefore, quantum error correction techniques are needed to protect and restore the entanglement in quantum computing.

Some possible mnemonics and learning tricks for entanglement as a physical resource are:

- To remember the definition of quantum entanglement, think of the acronym QEC: Quantum Entanglement is when Quantum systems are correlated Even when they are separated by a large distance.
- To remember some of the applications of quantum entanglement, think of the acronym CTS: Quantum entanglement enables Cryptography, Teleportation, and Superdense coding.
- To remember some of the physical systems that can exhibit quantum entanglement, think of the acronym SIPS: Quantum entanglement can be generated and detected in Superconducting qubits, Ions, Photons, and Spins.
- To remember some of the entanglement measures, think of the acronym ENC: Entanglement measures include Entropy, Negativity, and Concurrence.
