## Unit 2 - Quantum Computation

- Quantum computation is a rapidly-emerging technology that harnesses the laws of quantum mechanics to solve problems too complex for classical computers.
- Quantum computation involves qubits, which are quantum systems that can exist in a superposition of two states, such as 0 and 1 .
- Unlike classical bits, which can only store one value at a time, qubits can store and process both values simultaneously, enabling parallel computation and exponential speedup .
- Quantum computation also exploits another quantum phenomenon called entanglement, which is a special correlation between two or more qubits that allows them to share information and act as a single system.
- Quantum computation also relies on another quantum principle called interference, which is the constructive and destructive combination of quantum states that can enhance or cancel out certain outcomes.
- Quantum computation can be used for various applications, such as quantum simulation, cryptography, quantum machine learning, and search problems.
- Quantum simulation is the use of quantum computers to model and study complex quantum systems, such as molecules, materials, and chemical reactions.
- Cryptography is the use of quantum computers to perform secure communication and computation, such as quantum key distribution, quantum encryption, and quantum digital signatures.
- Quantum machine learning is the use of quantum computers to enhance the performance and capabilities of machine learning algorithms, such as quantum neural networks, quantum support vector machines, and quantum clustering.
- Search problems are the use of quantum computers to find a specific item or solution among a large number of possibilities, such as Grover's algorithm, quantum walk, and quantum annealing.

Some mnemonics and learning tricks for Unit 2 - Quantum Computation are:

- To remember the three basic properties of quantum computation, use the acronym SEI: Superposition, Entanglement, and Interference.
- To remember the difference between classical bits and qubits, use the analogy of a coin: a classical bit is like a coin that can only show heads or tails, while a qubit is like a coin that can show both heads and tails at the same time.
- To remember the applications of quantum computation, use the acronym SCQS: Simulation, Cryptography, Quantum machine learning, and Search problems.
