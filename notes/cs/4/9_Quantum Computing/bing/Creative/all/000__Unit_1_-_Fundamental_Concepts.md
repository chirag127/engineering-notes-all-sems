## Unit 1 - Fundamental Concepts

This unit covers the basic concepts of computer science, such as data types, algorithms, abstraction, and complexity.

- Data types are the categories of values that can be stored and manipulated by a computer program. Examples of data types are integers, floating-point numbers, strings, booleans, lists, and dictionaries.
- Algorithms are the step-by-step instructions that describe how to solve a problem or perform a task. Algorithms can be expressed in natural language, pseudocode, flowcharts, or programming languages.
- Abstraction is the process of hiding or simplifying the details of a system or problem to focus on the essential features or properties. Abstraction can be applied at different levels, such as data abstraction, procedural abstraction, or object-oriented abstraction.
- Complexity is the measure of how difficult a problem or system is to understand, solve, or implement. Complexity can be analyzed in terms of time, space, or other resources. Some common methods of measuring complexity are asymptotic notation, empirical analysis, and recursion trees.

Some mnemonics and learning tricks for this unit are:

- To remember the common data types, you can use the acronym **IF SLAB** (Integer, Float, String, List, Boolean, Dictionary).
- To remember the steps of designing an algorithm, you can use the acronym **PAC** (Problem, Algorithm, Code).
- To remember the levels of abstraction, you can use the acronym **DPO** (Data, Procedure, Object).
- To remember the types of asymptotic notation, you can use the acronym **OBT** (Big-O, Big-Theta, Big-Omega).
