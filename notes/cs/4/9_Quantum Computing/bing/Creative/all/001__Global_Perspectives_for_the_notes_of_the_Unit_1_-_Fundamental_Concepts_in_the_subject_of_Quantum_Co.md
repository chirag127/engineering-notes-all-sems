### Global Perspectives for the notes of the Unit 1 - Fundamental Concepts in the subject of Quantum Computing

- Quantum computing is a rapidly evolving field that promises to transform various industries, such as finance, health care, chemistry, cybersecurity, and more, by solving complex problems that are beyond the reach of classical computers.
- Quantum computing is based on the principles of quantum mechanics, which describe the behavior of subatomic particles, such as electrons, photons, and atoms, that can exist in superposition (a combination of two or more states) and entanglement (a correlation between two or more particles).
- Quantum computers use quantum bits, or qubits, as the basic unit of information, which can be in a superposition of 0 and 1, unlike classical bits that can only be either 0 or 1. Qubits can also interact with each other through quantum interference, which can enhance or cancel out the probability of observing a certain state.
- Quantum computers can perform parallel operations on multiple qubits, which can exponentially increase the computational power and speed compared to classical computers. However, quantum computers also face challenges, such as noise, decoherence, and error correction, that limit the number and reliability of qubits available for computation.
- Quantum computing is measured by quantum volume, which is a metric that captures the number of qubits, the connectivity between them, the error rate, and the gate fidelity of a quantum system. Quantum volume is expected to double every year, according to several companies making quantum computers.
- Quantum computing is currently in the NISQ (noisy intermediate-scale quantum) era, which means that quantum computers have a few tens to hundreds of qubits, but are still noisy and prone to errors. NISQ devices can still demonstrate quantum advantage, which is the ability to outperform classical computers on certain tasks, such as optimization, simulation, machine learning, and cryptography.
- Quantum computing is expected to reach the fault-tolerant era, which means that quantum computers will have thousands to millions of qubits, and will be able to correct errors and run large-scale algorithms reliably. Fault-tolerant quantum computers could solve problems that are intractable for classical computers, such as factoring large numbers, simulating quantum systems, and breaking current encryption schemes.
- Quantum computing is a global endeavor, with many countries, companies, universities, and research institutions investing in quantum research and development. Some of the leading players in the quantum computing landscape include Google, IBM, Microsoft, Intel, Amazon, Alibaba, Tencent, Baidu, Huawei, Rigetti, IonQ, Xanadu, D-Wave, and more.
- Quantum computing is also a multidisciplinary field, that requires collaboration and innovation from various domains, such as physics, mathematics, computer science, engineering, chemistry, biology, and more. Quantum computing also faces a talent gap, which means that there is a shortage of skilled and trained professionals who can design, build, program, and use quantum computers.
- Quantum computing is a disruptive and transformative technology, that could have significant impacts on various sectors of society, economy, and environment. Quantum computing could enable new discoveries and applications, such as drug design, climate modeling, artificial intelligence, quantum internet, and more. Quantum computing could also pose new challenges and risks, such as ethical, legal, social, and security issues, that need to be addressed by policymakers, regulators, and stakeholders.
