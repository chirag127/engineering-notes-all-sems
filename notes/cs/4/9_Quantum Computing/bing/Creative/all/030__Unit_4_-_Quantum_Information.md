## Unit 4 - Quantum Information

- Quantum information is the information of the state of a quantum system. It is the basic entity of study in quantum information theory, and can be manipulated using quantum information processing techniques. 
- Quantum information refers to both the technical definition in terms of Von Neumann entropy and the general computational term. It is an interdisciplinary field that involves quantum mechanics, computer science, information theory, philosophy and cryptography among other fields. 
- Quantum information, like classical information, can be processed using digital computers, transmitted from one location to another, manipulated with algorithms, and analyzed with computer science and mathematics. Just like the basic unit of classical information is the bit, quantum information deals with qubits. 
- Qubits are quantum systems that can exist in superposition of two states, usually denoted as |0> and |1>. Unlike bits, qubits cannot be copied or cloned due to the no-cloning theorem. Qubits can also be entangled, which means that their states are correlated even when they are separated by large distances. 
- Quantum information processing is the manipulation of quantum information using quantum devices, such as quantum gates, quantum circuits, quantum algorithms, quantum error correction, and quantum cryptography. Quantum information processing can perform tasks that are impossible or intractable for classical information processing, such as factoring large numbers, simulating quantum systems, and achieving secure communication.  
- Quantum information theory is the branch of mathematics and physics that studies the fundamental limits and principles of quantum information processing. It generalizes and extends classical information theory, which deals with bits and Shannon entropy. Quantum information theory introduces concepts such as qubits, Von Neumann entropy, quantum entanglement, quantum channel capacity, quantum error correction, and quantum complexity. 
- Quantum information science also has connections and applications to other fields of physics, such as quantum gravity, black holes, and quantum thermodynamics. For example, quantum entanglement can be used to describe the geometry of space, quantum error correction can explain the information paradox of black hole evaporation, and quantum complexity can measure the stability of space. 

Some mnemonics and learning tricks for quantum information are:

- To remember the no-cloning theorem, think of the acronym CANT: Copying A qubit is Not possible.
- To remember the basic quantum gates, think of the acronym HXZ: Hadamard, X (NOT), and Z (phase) gates.
- To remember the BB84 protocol for quantum key distribution, think of the acronym BEEP: Bases, Eavesdropper, Error rate, Privacy amplification.
- To remember the difference between Von Neumann entropy and Shannon entropy, think of the acronym VINE: Von Neumann Is Non-additive for Entangled states.
