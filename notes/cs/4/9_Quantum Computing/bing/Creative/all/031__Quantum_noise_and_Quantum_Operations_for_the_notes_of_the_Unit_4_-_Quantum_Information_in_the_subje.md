### Quantum noise and Quantum Operations for the notes of the Unit 4 - Quantum Information in the subject of Quantum Computing

- Quantum noise is the uncertainty or randomness that arises from the quantum nature of matter and energy. It can affect the performance and accuracy of quantum computers, which rely on manipulating the quantum states of qubits .
- Quantum operations are the mathematical descriptions of how quantum states can be transformed by quantum gates, measurements, or other physical processes. They are usually represented by unitary matrices or superoperators that act on the Hilbert space of the qubits.
- Some examples of quantum operations are:
  - The Hadamard gate, which creates a superposition of two basis states: H|0> = (|0> + |1>)/sqrt(2) and H|1> = (|0> - |1>)/sqrt(2).
  - The CNOT gate, which flips the target qubit if the control qubit is |1>: CNOT|00> = |00>, CNOT|01> = |01>, CNOT|10> = |11>, CNOT|11> = |10>.
  - The measurement operation, which collapses the quantum state to one of the basis states with some probability: M|0> = |0> with probability |<0|0>|^2 and M|1> = |1> with probability |<1|1>|^2.
- Quantum noise can be caused by various sources, such as imperfect control signals, interference from the environment, and unwanted interactions between qubits . These sources can introduce errors or decoherence, which reduce the fidelity or coherence of the quantum state .
- Quantum noise can be mitigated by various techniques, such as:
  - Error correction, which involves encoding the logical qubits into physical qubits and applying recovery operations to correct the errors.
  - Noise adaptive search, which involves finding the most robust quantum circuit and qubit mapping for a given task using an evolutionary algorithm .
  - Quantum Zeno effect, which involves applying frequent measurements or projections to prevent the quantum state from evolving into an undesired state.
- Quantum noise and quantum operations are important topics for quantum computing, as they determine the feasibility and efficiency of quantum algorithms and applications. Some examples of quantum computing tasks that are affected by quantum noise and quantum operations are:
  - Machine learning, which involves using quantum circuits to perform classification, regression, or clustering on data .
  - Quantum chemistry, which involves using quantum circuits to simulate the electronic structure and dynamics of molecules .
  - Quantum cryptography, which involves using quantum states to generate and distribute secure keys for encryption and decryption.
