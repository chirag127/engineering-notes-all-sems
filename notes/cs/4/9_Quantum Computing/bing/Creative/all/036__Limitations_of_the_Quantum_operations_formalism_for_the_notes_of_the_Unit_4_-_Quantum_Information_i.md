### Limitations of the Quantum operations formalism for the notes of the Unit 4 - Quantum Information in the subject of Quantum Computing

- The quantum operations formalism is a general tool for describing the dynamics of quantum systems, either closed or open, in terms of linear maps on the space of density matrices.
- However, the quantum operations formalism has some limitations that need to be taken into account when applying it to physical situations.
- One limitation is that the quantum operations formalism does not account for the effects of the preparation and measurement devices on the quantum system. For example, if the system interacts with the degrees of freedom used to prepare or measure it, the dynamics may not be adequately described by a quantum operation.
- Another limitation is that the quantum operations formalism does not capture the nonlocal or contextual aspects of quantum mechanics. For example, if the system is entangled with another system, the quantum operation may not reflect the correlations or the influence of the measurement settings on the other system.
- A third limitation is that the quantum operations formalism does not provide a clear physical explanation for the origin and meaning of the quantum operators. For example, it is not clear how the basic quantum commutator [x,p]=iℏ arises from the interaction of the system with the vacuum field, or how the Heisenberg equations relate the response functions of the system to the field modes.
- A fourth limitation is that the quantum operations formalism does not reveal the limits of quantum computation and information processing. For example, it is not clear what are the fundamental physical principles that constrain the power and speed of quantum computers, or what are the optimal ways of implementing quantum algorithms and protocols using quantum operations.
