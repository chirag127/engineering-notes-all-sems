### Nuclear magnetic resonance for the notes of the Unit 3 - Quantum Computers in the subject of Quantum Computing

- Nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR) is a technique that uses the spin states of nuclei within molecules as qubits .
- NMR quantum computing can be implemented in liquid or solid state samples, with different advantages and challenges.
- Liquid state NMR (LSNMR) quantum computing uses molecules in a liquid sample as qubits, and exploits the spin-spin interactions between atoms and the external magnetic field to perform single and two-qubit gates.
- LSNMR quantum computing has the benefits of long coherence time, precise manipulation, and well-developed quantum control techniques, which enable the realization of quantum schemes with up to 12 qubits.
- LSNMR quantum computing also has the limitations of ensemble quantum processing, which requires large samples and reduces the signal-to-noise ratio, and the difficulty of initialization and measurement of the quantum state.
- Solid state NMR (SSNMR) quantum computing uses a solid state sample, such as a nitrogen vacancy diamond lattice, as qubits, and overcomes some of the problems of LSNMR quantum computing.
- SSNMR quantum computing has the advantages of avoiding molecular diffusion decoherence, achieving lower temperatures to suppress phonon decoherence, and having a greater variety of control operations for initialization and manipulation.
- SSNMR quantum computing also has the challenges of scaling up the number of qubits, maintaining the coherence time, and improving the readout fidelity.
- NMR quantum computing has been used to demonstrate various quantum algorithms, such as quantum search, quantum Fourier transform, quantum error correction, quantum machine learning, quantum simulation, and quantum cryptography.
- NMR quantum computing is one of the promising candidates for quantum information processing, but it also faces many technical and theoretical obstacles that need to be overcome .

#### Mnemonics and learning tricks

- To remember the difference between liquid and solid state NMR quantum computing, you can use the following mnemonic: **L**iquid state NMR uses **L**onger coherence time, **L**arger samples, and **L**ess decoherence, while **S**olid state NMR uses **S**maller samples, **S**tronger control operations, and **S**uppressed phonon decoherence.
- To remember the basic steps of NMR quantum computing, you can use the acronym **PIMP**:
  - **P**repare the initial state of the qubits, usually a pseudo-pure state or a thermal state.
  - **I**mplement the quantum gates by applying radiofrequency pulses and magnetic field gradients to the qubits.
  - **M**easure the final state of the qubits by detecting the NMR signal of the ensemble.
  - **P**rocess the data by using Fourier transform, signal averaging, and error correction techniques.
