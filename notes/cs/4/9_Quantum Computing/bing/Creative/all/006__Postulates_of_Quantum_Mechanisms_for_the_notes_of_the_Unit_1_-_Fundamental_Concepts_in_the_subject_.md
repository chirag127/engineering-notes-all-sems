### Postulates of Quantum Mechanics for the notes of the Unit 1 - Fundamental Concepts in the subject of Quantum Computing

Quantum mechanics is the branch of physics that describes the behavior of matter and energy at the smallest scales. Quantum mechanics is based on a set of postulates, or basic assumptions, that relate the physical quantities to the mathematical structures of Hilbert spaces, vectors and operators. Here are the main postulates of quantum mechanics:

- **Postulate 1**: The state of a quantum system is completely specified by a wave function, which is a complex-valued function of the coordinates and time. The wave function contains all the information that can be known about the system, and there are no hidden variables. The wave function must be normalized, meaning that the integral of its squared modulus over all space is equal to one. The wave function must also satisfy the Schrödinger equation, which is a partial differential equation that governs its time evolution.

- **Postulate 2**: Every physical observable, such as position, momentum, energy, angular momentum, etc., corresponds to a self-adjoint (or Hermitian) operator, which is a linear transformation that preserves the inner product of the Hilbert space. The eigenvalues of the operator are the possible outcomes of measuring the observable, and the eigenvectors of the operator form a complete basis of the Hilbert space. The operator must also satisfy the commutation relations with other operators, which reflect the uncertainty principle.

- **Postulate 3**: The outcome of a measurement of an observable is one of its eigenvalues, and the probability of obtaining a particular eigenvalue is given by the Born rule, which states that the probability is equal to the squared modulus of the inner product of the wave function and the corresponding eigenvector. The measurement also causes the collapse of the wave function, meaning that the state of the system after the measurement is the eigenvector corresponding to the observed eigenvalue, normalized by the probability.

- **Postulate 4**: If a system is composed of two or more subsystems, the state of the composite system is given by the tensor product of the states of the subsystems. The tensor product is a way of combining two Hilbert spaces into a larger one, such that the basis vectors of the composite space are the products of the basis vectors of the subsystems. The tensor product also allows the definition of entangled states, which are states that cannot be written as the product of the states of the subsystems.

These postulates form the foundation of quantum mechanics, and they can be used to derive many results and applications of quantum theory. However, they also raise some conceptual and philosophical questions, such as the interpretation of the wave function, the nature of measurement and collapse, the role of entanglement and nonlocality, and the compatibility of quantum mechanics with relativity and gravity. These questions are still the subject of active research and debate among physicists and philosophers.
