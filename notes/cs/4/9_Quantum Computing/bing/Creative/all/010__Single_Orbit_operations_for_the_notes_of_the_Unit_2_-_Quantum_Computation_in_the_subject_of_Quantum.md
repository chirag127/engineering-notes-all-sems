### Single Qubit Operations for the Notes of the Unit 2 - Quantum Computation in the Subject of Quantum Computing

- Single qubit operations, or single qubit quantum gates, are transformations that act on a single quantum bit (qubit) and change its quantum state.
- Single qubit operations can be classified into two categories: Clifford gates and non-Clifford gates.
- Clifford gates are those that map the computational basis states |0> and |1> to other states in the same basis, up to a global phase. They include the identity gate I, the Pauli gates X, Y, Z, the Hadamard gate H, and the phase gate S.
- Non-Clifford gates are those that map the computational basis states to states outside the computational basis, such as the T gate, which adds a pi/4 phase to the |1> state, or the R gate, which rotates the qubit by an arbitrary angle around the Z axis.
- Single qubit operations can be represented by 2x2 unitary matrices, which preserve the norm of the quantum state vector. For example, the X gate can be represented by the matrix [[0, 1], [1, 0]], which flips the qubit from |0> to |1> and vice versa.
- Single qubit operations can also be visualized using the Bloch sphere, which is a spherical representation of the qubit state space. The computational basis states |0> and |1> correspond to the north and south poles of the sphere, respectively, and any other state can be represented by a point on the surface of the sphere, with its latitude and longitude determined by the relative amplitudes and phases of the qubit state vector. For example, the state |+> = (|0> + |1>)/sqrt(2) corresponds to the point on the equator with longitude zero, and the state |-> = (|0> - |1>)/sqrt(2) corresponds to the point on the equator with longitude pi.
- Single qubit operations can be interpreted as rotations of the qubit state vector on the Bloch sphere. For example, the X gate rotates the qubit by pi radians around the X axis, the Y gate rotates the qubit by pi radians around the Y axis, and the Z gate rotates the qubit by pi radians around the Z axis. The H gate rotates the qubit by pi/2 radians around the Y axis, followed by pi radians around the Z axis, mapping the poles to the equator and vice versa. The S gate rotates the qubit by pi/2 radians around the Z axis, adding a phase of i to the |1> state. The T gate rotates the qubit by pi/4 radians around the Z axis, adding a phase of exp(i*pi/4) to the |1> state. The R gate rotates the qubit by an arbitrary angle theta around the Z axis, adding a phase of exp(i*theta/2) to the |1> state.
- Single qubit operations are sufficient to construct a universal quantum computer, together with Bell-basis measurements and certain entangled quantum states such as Greenberger–Horne–Zeilinger (GHZ) states. This can be achieved by using a technique called quantum teleportation, which allows the state of a qubit to be transferred from one location to another by communicating just two classical bits, provided that the sender and the receiver have previously shared halves of an EPR state, which is a maximally entangled state of two qubits. By applying single qubit operations to the EPR state before or after the teleportation, any desired quantum gate can be implemented on the teleported qubit. This method reduces the resource requirements for quantum computers and unifies known protocols for fault-tolerant quantum computation.
