Shannon entropy is a measure of the uncertainty or information content of a random variable. In quantum information theory, the von Neumann entropy is the quantum analogue of the Shannon entropy, defined as H(ρ) = -Tr(ρ log ρ), where ρ is the density matrix of a quantum system.

Quantum error correction is the process of encoding quantum information in such a way that errors due to noise or decoherence can be detected and corrected without disturbing the encoded information. Quantum error correction codes are based on the Pauli group, which consists of the identity matrix I and the three Pauli matrices X, Y, and Z, along with their tensor products.

A quantum error correction code can be described by a set of stabilizer operators, which are elements of the Pauli group that commute with each other and have eigenvalue +1 on the encoded states. The stabilizer operators form an Abelian subgroup of the Pauli group, called the stabilizer group. The stabilizer group can be generated by a set of independent stabilizer generators, which are usually denoted by S1, S2, ..., Sk.
