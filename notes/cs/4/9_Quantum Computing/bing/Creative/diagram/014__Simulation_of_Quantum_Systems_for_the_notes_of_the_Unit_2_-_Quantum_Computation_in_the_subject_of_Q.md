Simulation of quantum systems is the process of using a quantum device to mimic the behavior of another quantum system that is difficult to access or control. A quantum simulator can be used to study various quantum phenomena, such as quantum phase transitions, quantum transport, quantum entanglement, and quantum algorithms. A quantum simulator can be either analog or digital, depending on whether it uses continuous or discrete variables to encode the quantum information.

A possible diagram for the simulation of quantum systems is shown below. It consists of three main components: the quantum system of interest, the quantum simulator, and the classical computer. The quantum system of interest is the target system that we want to understand or manipulate. The quantum simulator is the controllable quantum device that can emulate the dynamics of the target system. The classical computer is the device that can program the quantum simulator, process the measurement data, and compare the results with the theoretical predictions.
