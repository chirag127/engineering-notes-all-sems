# Single Orbit operations

Single-qubit operations are the basic building blocks of quantum computation. These operations are performed using single-qubit gates, which allow for control over the state of a qubit. For example, the X-gate, also known as the 'bit flip', switches the amplitudes of |1⟩ and |0⟩ in a qubit.

Single-qubit operations can be classified into two categories: Clifford gates and non-Clifford gates. Non-Clifford gates consist only of the T-gate (also known as the π/8 gate).

It has been demonstrated that any quantum computation can be performed using single-qubit operations and CNOT gates, providing an alternative universal set of operations for quantum computation.

In summary, single-qubit operations are fundamental to quantum computation and provide a means of controlling the state of a qubit. These operations can be performed using single-qubit gates, which can be classified into Clifford and non-Clifford gates. Any quantum computation can be performed using single-qubit operations and CNOT gates.
