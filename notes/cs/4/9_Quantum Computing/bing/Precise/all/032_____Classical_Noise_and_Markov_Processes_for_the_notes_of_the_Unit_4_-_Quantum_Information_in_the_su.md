# Classical Noise and Markov Processes

Classical noise in systems can be described using the theory of stochastic processes. In the analysis of multi-stage processes, it is often a good assumption to use Markov processes. A Markov process is a type of stochastic process where the probability of each event depends only on the state attained in the previous event. This is known as the Markov property.

A classical Markov semigroup of transition operators can be generated from a classical Markov process. On the other hand, given a Markov semigroup, a Markov process can be constructed based on the Markov semigroup using the Kolmogorov consistency theorem. The theory of classical Markov processes is well known in the probability community.

In the context of quantum information, it is important to note that processes that cannot produce coherence and destroy any coherence that is fed in form a strict subset of processes that appear classical when sequentially probed in the computational basis.
