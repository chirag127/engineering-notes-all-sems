# Strong Subadditivity

Strong subadditivity is a property of the von Neumann entropy that is used in quantum information theory. It states that for any tripartite quantum state, the sum of the entropies of any two of the subsystems is greater than or equal to the sum of the entropies of the remaining subsystem and the whole system.

Mathematically, for any tripartite state ρABC, the strong subadditivity property is expressed as:

S(ρAB) + S(ρBC) ≥ S(ρB) + S(ρABC)

where S(ρ) denotes the von Neumann entropy of the state ρ.

This property is useful in the study of quantum error correction as it provides a way to bound the amount of information that can be transmitted through a noisy quantum channel. It also plays a role in the study of entanglement and quantum correlations.

Some key points to remember about strong subadditivity are:
- It is a property of the von Neumann entropy.
- It applies to tripartite quantum states.
- It provides a bound on the amount of information that can be transmitted through a noisy quantum channel.
- It is useful in the study of entanglement and quantum correlations.
