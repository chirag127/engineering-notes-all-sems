### Entropy and Information in Quantum Computing

1. Entropy is a central concept in both classical and quantum information theory, measuring the uncertainty and the information content in the state of a physical system.
2. Classical information theory is reviewed and then generalized into quantum information theory.
3. Both Shannon and Von Neumann entropy are discussed, making the connection to compressibility of a message stream and the generalization of compressibility in a quantum system.
4. The application of Von Neumann entropy in entanglement of formation for both pure and mixed bipartite quantum states is considered.
5. Entropy Quantum Computing is rooted in the openness of natural quantum systems.
6. The EQC does not try to eliminate or protect the interaction with the environment. It uses controlled feedback from the environment to drive the quantum information results.
7. The Belavkin–Staszewski (BS) relative entropy is an enticing and crucial entropy used to process quantum information tasks, which can be used to describe the effects of possible noncommutativity of the quantum states.
8. Quantum verification has been highlighted as a significant challenge on the road to scalable technology, especially with the rapid development of quantum computing.
9. To verify quantum states, self-testing is proposed as a device-independent concept, which is based only on the observed statistics.
