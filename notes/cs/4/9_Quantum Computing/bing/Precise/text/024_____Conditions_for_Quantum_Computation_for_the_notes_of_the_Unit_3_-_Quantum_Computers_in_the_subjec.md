### Conditions for Quantum Computation

Quantum computation is a rapidly developing field that exploits the properties of quantum mechanics to perform calculations that are impossible or impractical for classical computers. There are several conditions that must be met in order to build a functional quantum computer. These conditions are known as the DiVincenzo criteria, named after the theoretical physicist David P. DiVincenzo, who proposed them in 2000 .

1. **Long Coherence Time**: Long qubit coherence times are a prerequisite for quantum computing. This means that the qubits must maintain their quantum state for a sufficient amount of time to perform calculations .

2. **High Scalability**: A quantum computer must be able to scale up to a large number of qubits in order to perform complex calculations .

3. **High Fault Tolerance and Quantum Error Correction**: Quantum computers must be able to correct errors that occur during computation. This is achieved through the use of quantum error correction codes .

4. **Ability to Initialize Qubits**: A quantum computer must be able to initialize its qubits into a known state before computation begins .

5. **Universal Quantum Gates**: A quantum computer must be able to perform a universal set of quantum gates, which are the building blocks of quantum algorithms .

6. **Efficient Qubit-state Measurement Capability**: A quantum computer must be able to efficiently measure the state of its qubits .

7. **Faithful Transmission of Flying Qubits and Interconversion between Stationary and “Flying” Qubits**: A quantum computer must be able to transmit qubits between different parts of the computer, and convert between stationary and “flying” qubits .

These are the seven key requirements for designing effective quantum computing systems .
