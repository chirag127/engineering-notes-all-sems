### Examples of Quantum noise and Quantum Operations for the notes of the Unit 4 - Quantum Information in the subject of Quantum Computing

1. Quantum noise
- Decoherence: Interaction of the quantum system with its surrounding environment which causes the superposition state to collapse into a probabilistic mixture of states. This leads to loss of quantum information.
- Amplitude damping: The probability of finding a qubit in the excited state decays exponentially with time. This causes the quantum state to lose coherence.
- Bit-flip errors: The quantum state of a qubit gets flipped due to noise. For example, |0> may get flipped to |1>.
- Phase-flip errors: The relative phase between the |0> and |1> states of a qubit gets flipped due to noise.

2. Quantum Operations
- State preparation: Preparing a desired initial quantum state. For example, preparing an input qubit in the state (|0> + |1>)/sqrt(2).

- Measurement: Measuring an observable property of a quantum system and collapsing its state to an eigenstate of the measurement operator. For example, measuring the Z-spin of an electron.
