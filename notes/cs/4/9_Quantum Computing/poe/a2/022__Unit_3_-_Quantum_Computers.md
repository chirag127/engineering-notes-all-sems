## Unit 3 - Quantum Computers

1. Qubits: Quantum bits or 'Qubits' are the basic units of information in a quantum computer. They can hold values of 1, 0, or a superposition of both. Qubits can become entangled so their states are dependent on each other, even if they are separated.
2. Superposition: Qubits can be in a superposition of multiple states until they are measured. This means that a quantum computer can perform many computations in parallel. This allows quantum computers to solve certain problems much faster than traditional computers.
3. Entanglement: When two or more qubits become 'entangled', their states are dependent on each other, even if they are separated. If you measure a property of one qubit, such as spin, you instantly know the corresponding property of the other qubit. No matter how far apart the qubits are, they will always be in this quantum connection.
4. Decoherence: The delicate quantum states of qubits can easily be disrupted by interactions with the environment. This is known as decoherence and it results in the loss of quantum properties like superposition and entanglement. Quantum error correction techniques are used to prevent decoherence and preserve the quantum nature of the qubits.
5. Quantum Algorithms: Quantum algorithms like Simon's algorithm and Shor's algorithm can be used to solve certain problems much more efficiently than traditional algorithms. Shor's algorithm can be used to break public-key cryptography and Simon's algorithm can find the period of a function. Quantum algorithms provide the key speedups that make quantum computers superior for certain types of calculations.
