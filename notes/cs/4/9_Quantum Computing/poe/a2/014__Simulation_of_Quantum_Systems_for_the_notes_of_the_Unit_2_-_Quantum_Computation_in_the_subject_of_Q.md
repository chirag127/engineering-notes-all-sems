### Simulation of Quantum Systems

- Quantum systems can be simulated on a classical computer to study their behavior and dynamics.
- However, the computational resources required to simulate quantum systems grow exponentially with the number of quantum particles. This is known as the quantum complexity barrier.
- There are various methods to simulate quantum systems:
-- The exact simulation that follows the time evolution of the quantum state. This becomes infeasible for systems with more than a few quantum particles due to the exponential growth of required resources.
-- Approximate and variational methods that provide an approximate solution to the time evolution with less computational cost but some loss of accuracy. Examples include the variational quantum eigensolver.
-- Simulation of the time evolution of observables instead of the quantum state. This can be more efficient but depends on the observable being measured.
- Quantum simulation is a promising application of quantum computers as they can overcome the quantum complexity barrier and efficiently simulate quantum systems. This could enable the study of quantum systems that are intractable for classical computers.
