### Optical Photon Quantum Computer

1. Optical photon quantum computers use photons (light particles) to represent quantum bits or 'qubits'. The qubits are encoded in the polarization state of the photons.
2. The photons are made to interact with each other through nonlinear optical processes to realize quantum logic gates. The interactions are controlled by applying strong laser pulses.
3. Some of the key components required to build an optical photon quantum computer are:
- Single photon sources to produce polarized photons
- Beam splitters and mirrors to manipulate the photon paths
- Nonlinear crystals to realize quantum logic gates via interaction of photons
- Photodetectors to read the output state of the photons
4. The key advantages of an optical photon quantum computer are:
- Photons have negligible interaction with the environment, so the quantum coherence is maintained for a relatively long time. This leads to a high number of operations that can be performed before decoherence effects set in.
- The photons can be made to interact strongly via nonlinear processes, enabling fast gate operations.
- The components required are based on established optical technology, so scaling to larger systems is feasible.
5. However, some of the challenges in building an optical quantum computer are:
- It is difficult to generate single photons on demand with high accuracy. The single photon sources have limited efficiency and can introduce errors.
- The photon detection process can also introduce errors. It is challenging to build efficient photodetectors that can distinguish between single photons.
- The optical components need to be highly precise and stable to control the photons effectively. This requires advanced engineering controls and can make the system quite complex.
