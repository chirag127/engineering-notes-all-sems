### Classical Noise and Markov Processes

1. Classical noise refers to unwanted disturbances that affect a quantum system and decohere its quantum state. Some examples are:
- Thermal fluctuations: At finite temperature, the quantum system interacts with its thermal environment causing decoherence.
- Vibrations and collisions: The quantum system interacts with its surroundings leading to decoherence.
- Electrical interference: Additional electromagnetic fields can interact with the quantum system and destroy its quantum coherence.

2. The effect of classical noise on a quantum system can be modeled as a Markov process. A Markov process is a random process where the probability of transitioning to a future state depends only on the present state and not on the sequence of events that led to the present state. The evolution of the quantum system under classical noise can be described by a completely positive trace-preserving (CPTP) map that is also Markovian. The Lindblad equation is a popular choice to describe such open system dynamics of a quantum system under Markovian noise.

3. To protect quantum coherence and enable quantum technologies, it is crucial to isolate the quantum system from classical noise in the environment. This can be achieved using quantum error correction codes and by employing decoherence-free subspaces. The field of quantum control also investigates strategies to counteract decoherence by means of opportune manipulations of the system-environment coupling.
