### Optical cavity Quantum electrodynamics for the notes of the Unit 3 - Quantum Computers

- Optical cavity Quantum electrodynamics (QED) is the study of how light and matter interact within an optical cavity.
- An optical cavity is a region of space between two mirrors, where light can bounce back and forth, reflecting between the mirrors.
- When an atom is placed inside the optical cavity, the interaction between the light field and the atom can be greatly enhanced due to the cavity causing the light to reflect and interact with the atom multiple times.
- This cavity QED system allows for precise control and measurement of the interaction between single atoms and single photons, enabling various applications like single-atom lasers, quantum memories, and quantum computing components.
- The strong coupling regime is when the atom-photon interaction rate becomes faster than the dissipation rates of the atom and the cavity. New quantum effects can be observed in this regime, enabling quantum applications.
- Weak measurements can be used to obtain information about a quantum system without significantly disturbing it. By repeated weak measurements of a cavity QED system, the quantum state of the atom or light field can be determined. This is useful for monitoring quantum systems to check for quantum behavior or errors.
- Cavity QED has promising applications for quantum computing, including proposals for quantum logic gates, quantum memories, and single-photon sources. The strong atom-photon coupling possible in cavity QED could enable faster quantum gates and longer-lived quantum states for more powerful quantum computers.
