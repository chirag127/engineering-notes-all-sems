## Unit 2 - Quantum Computation

1. Qubits: Quantum bits or 'Qubits' are the basic units of information in a quantum computer. They can hold values of 0, 1, or a superposition of both.
2. Superposition: Qubits can be in a superposition of states where the qubit is in both 0 and 1 at the same time. This allows a quantum computer to perform many computations in parallel.
3. Entanglement: When two or more qubits are entangled, their states are linked. If you measure a property of one qubit, such as spin, you instantly know the corresponding property of the other qubit. No matter how far apart the qubits are.
4. Measurement: When a qubit is measured, it gives a random output of 0 or 1. The superposition collapses into a single classical state. This causes the loss of quantum nature of the system.
5. Quantum Gates: They are the quantum analog of classical logic gates. They perform operations on the qubits by manipulating their quantum states. Examples are Hadamard gate, Phase gate, etc.
6. Quantum Algorithms: Algorithms that harness the quantum mechanical properties of superposition and entanglement to solve complex problems that are intractable for classical computers. Examples are Quantum Fourier Transform and Shor's algorithm.
