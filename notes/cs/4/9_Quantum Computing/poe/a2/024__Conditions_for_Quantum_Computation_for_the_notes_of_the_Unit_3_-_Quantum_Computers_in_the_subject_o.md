### Conditions for Quantum Computation

* Isolation from Environment: The quantum system must be isolated from its surrounding environment to prevent decoherence i.e. the loss of quantum nature. This is difficult to achieve in practice.
* Precise Control: The quantum system must be under precise control. The controls like magnetic or electric fields must be precisely tuned to perform the required operations. Even a small error can lead to incorrect results.
* Low Temperature: The quantum system must be maintained at extremely low temperatures close to absolute zero. This is required to reduce thermal excitations that can cause decoherence. Cooling the system to such low temperatures requires advanced cryogenic engineering.
* Error Correction: As decoherence cannot be completely avoided, techniques to detect and correct errors must be implemented. This requires additional quantum bits and more complex controls and measurements. This leads to a large-scale engineering challenge.

The above points cover the key conditions required for a quantum computer to function properly and give the desired results. Achieving all these conditions simultaneously is a significant challenge from an engineering perspective making quantum computing a difficult technological goal to achieve.
