### Guiding Principles for the notes of the Unit 3 - Quantum Computers

1. Quantum bits or 'qubits' are the basic units of information in a quantum computer. They can hold values of 0, 1, or a superposition of both.

2. Qubits can be represented by particles, photons, ions, or electrons and can be in a superposition of states.

3. Qubits can become entangled, so their states are dependent on each other, even if they are separated. This entanglement is a key feature for the power of quantum computers.

4. The state of qubits is fragile and can easily become decoherent, so they need to be isolated from the environment. Error correction codes are used to deal with decoherence and errors.

5. Quantum algorithms like Simon's algorithm and Shor's algorithm can be used to solve certain problems much faster than traditional algorithms. They utilize the superposition and entanglement of qubits.

6. The quantum Turing machine is the theoretical model of a quantum computer, just as the classical Turing machine models a conventional computer. It can take advantage of quantum mechanical effects to solve problems.

7. There are still many technical challenges to building a large-scale quantum computer. It is an active area of research in computer science and physics. Real-world quantum computers today are limited to a small number of qubits.
