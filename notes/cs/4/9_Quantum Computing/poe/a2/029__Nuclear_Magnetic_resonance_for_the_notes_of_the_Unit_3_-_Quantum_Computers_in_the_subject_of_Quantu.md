### Nuclear Magnetic Resonance

- Nuclear Magnetic Resonance (NMR) is a physical phenomenon in which nuclei in a magnetic field absorb and re-emit electromagnetic radiation.
- This is used to study the structure, dynamics, reaction state, and chemical environment of molecules.
- In NMR, a sample is placed in a magnetic field and exposed to electromagnetic radiation. The nuclei of atoms in the sample absorb and re-emit the radiation, which is detected to produce an NMR signal.
- The frequency of the absorbed radiation depends on the strength of the magnetic field and the magnetic properties of the nuclei. By scanning the frequency of the radiation, an NMR spectrum can be recorded that provides information about the local chemical environment of the nuclei.
- NMR spectroscopy is widely used in physics, chemistry, materials science, and medicine for studying molecular structure, dynamics and interactions.
