### Quantum Operations for the notes of the Unit 4 - Quantum Information in the subject of Quantum Computing

1. Quantum gates: Quantum gates are the basic operations that can be performed on quantum bits or qubits. Some of the important quantum gates are:
- Hadamard gate: It puts a qubit into a superposition of states. It converts |0> to (|0> + |1>)/sqrt(2) and vice versa.
- Pauli gates: These include operations like bit flip (X), phase flip (Z) and bit-phase flip (Y). They operate on one qubit.
- CNOT gate: It is a controlled not gate that flips the target qubit if the control qubit is 1. It operates on two qubits.
- Toffoli gate: It is a controlled-controlled not gate. It flips the target qubit if both the control qubits are 1. It operates on three qubits.

2. Quantum circuits: Quantum circuits are made up of quantum gates arranged in a sequence to perform a quantum algorithm. The output of one gate is connected to the input of the next gate. The quantum state passes through the gates, gets modified by the operations performed by the gates and we get the final output.

3. Reversibility: Quantum operations must be reversible. The quantum gates do not lose information and there is a corresponding inverse gate for every gate. The inverse gate can undo the effect of the gate. This is necessary to preserve the quantum nature of the system.

4. Measurements: Measurements in quantum mechanics are fundamentally probabilistic. When we measure a quantum system, it collapses from a superposition of states to a single state. The probability of getting a particular state depends on the quantum state before measurement. Measurements can provide information about the quantum state but also disturb it.
