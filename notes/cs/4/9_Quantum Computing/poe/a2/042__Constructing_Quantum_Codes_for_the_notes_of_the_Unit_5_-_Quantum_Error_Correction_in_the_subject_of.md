### Constructing Quantum Codes

1. Introduction
- Quantum error correction codes are used to detect and correct errors in quantum states.
- They encode quantum information in a redundant way so that it can be recovered even after some errors occur.
- The construction of good quantum error correcting codes is an important problem in quantum information theory.

2. The stabilizer formalism
- The stabilizer formalism provides a framework to construct and study quantum error correcting codes.
- In this formalism, a quantum code is defined by its stabilizer, which is an Abelian subgroup of the Pauli group. The codewords are the joint eigenvectors of the stabilizer generators with eigenvalue 1.
- The stabilizer formalism allows us to translate problems about quantum codes into problems about combinations of Pauli matrices, which can often be handled more easily.

3. Examples of stabilizer codes
- We briefly discuss a few important examples of stabilizer codes:
-- The [[5,1,3]] code, which is the smallest nontrivial quantum error correcting code.
-- The [[7,1,3]] Steane code, which is a Calderbank-Shor-Steane (CSS) code that admits an efficient decoding algorithm.
-- The surface code, which is a two-dimensional topological code with a high error threshold. It is promising for fault-tolerant quantum computing.
