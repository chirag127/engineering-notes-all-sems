
### Quantum Computation for the notes of the Unit 1 - Fundamental Concepts in the subject of Quantum Computing

1. Quantum computation is the use of quantum-mechanical phenomena such as superposition and entanglement to perform computation. 
2. Quantum computers are different from digital computers based on transistors. Whereas digital computers require data to be encoded into binary digits (bits), each of which is always in one of two definite states (0 or 1), quantum computation uses quantum bits, which can be in superpositions of states.
3. A quantum Turing machine is a theoretical model of such a computer, and is also known as the universal quantum computer.
4. Quantum algorithms are algorithms which run on a realistic model of quantum computation, the most commonly used model being the quantum circuit model.
5. Quantum algorithms are often probabilistic, in that they provide the correct solution only with a certain known probability.
6. The most well-known example of a quantum algorithm is Shor's algorithm for factoring integers.
7. Quantum computers can also be used to search unsorted databases faster than classical computers. This is known as Grover's algorithm.
8. Quantum computers can also be used to solve optimization problems, such as the traveling salesman problem, faster than classical computers. This is known as the quantum approximate optimization algorithm (QAOA).
