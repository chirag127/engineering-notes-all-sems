### Consensus protocols for Permissioned Blockchains

Consensus protocols play a crucial role in the functioning of permissioned blockchains. They are responsible for ensuring that all nodes in the network agree on the current state of the blockchain. In this section, we will discuss some of the most commonly used consensus protocols for permissioned blockchains.

#### Practical Byzantine Fault Tolerance (PBFT)

PBFT is a popular consensus protocol for permissioned blockchains. It is designed to be fault-tolerant and can handle up to one-third of the nodes in the network being faulty. PBFT works by dividing the nodes in the network into three roles: client, primary, and backup. The primary node is responsible for collecting transactions and proposing a block to the network. The backup nodes validate the proposed block and ensure that it is added to the blockchain.

#### Proof of Authority (PoA)

PoA is a consensus protocol that relies on a group of pre-approved validators to validate transactions and add them to the blockchain. It is a popular consensus protocol for permissioned blockchains because it is energy-efficient and can handle high transaction throughput. In PoA, the validators are chosen based on their reputation and identity, which ensures that only trusted nodes participate in the consensus process.

#### Proof of Stake (PoS)

PoS is a consensus protocol that relies on a group of validators who hold a stake in the blockchain to validate transactions and add them to the blockchain. In PoS, the validators are chosen based on the amount of cryptocurrency they hold. The more cryptocurrency a validator holds, the more likely they are to be chosen to validate transactions. PoS is energy-efficient and has a lower barrier to entry than PoW, which makes it a popular consensus protocol for permissioned blockchains.

#### Delegated Proof of Stake (DPoS)

DPoS is a consensus protocol that is similar to PoS, but instead of all validators participating in the consensus process, they elect a smaller group of nodes to act as delegates on their behalf. The delegates are responsible for validating transactions and adding them to the blockchain. DPoS is energy-efficient and has a low barrier to entry, which makes it a popular consensus protocol for permissioned blockchains.

#### Learning tricks and Mnemonics

- PBFT: "Primary, Backup, Fault-Tolerant"
- PoA: "Pre-Approved Validators"
- PoS: "Validators with a Stake"
- DPoS: "Delegates with Proof of Stake"

In conclusion, choosing the right consensus protocol for a permissioned blockchain depends on various factors such as energy efficiency, scalability, security, and transaction throughput. Each consensus protocol has its advantages and disadvantages, and it is essential to understand their working and use cases to make an informed decision.
