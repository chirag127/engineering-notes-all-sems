### Provenance of goods for the notes of the Unit 7 - Use case 2 in the subject of Block chain Architecture Design

Provenance of goods refers to the history of a product, including its origin, ownership, and any changes in its condition or location throughout its lifecycle. In the context of blockchain architecture design, provenance can be tracked using distributed ledger technology, providing transparency and accountability in the supply chain.

Here are some key points to understand the provenance of goods in the context of blockchain architecture design:

- Blockchain technology can be used to create a tamper-proof record of a product's journey from its origin to its final destination. This record can include information about the product's origin, the parties involved in its production and distribution, and any changes in ownership or condition along the way.

- By tracking the provenance of goods using blockchain technology, businesses can ensure that their products are ethically sourced and meet regulatory requirements. This can be particularly important in industries such as food and pharmaceuticals, where safety and quality are critical.

- One of the key benefits of using blockchain technology to track provenance is that it provides a single source of truth for all parties involved in the supply chain. This can help to reduce disputes and increase trust between parties, as everyone has access to the same information.

- To track provenance using blockchain technology, businesses can create a unique digital identity for each product and record its movement on the blockchain using smart contracts. These smart contracts can be programmed to trigger certain actions, such as releasing payment or notifying parties of a change in ownership.

- Mnemonic: To remember the key points of provenance of goods, you can use the acronym "PACTS" - Provenance, Accountability, Compliance, Transparency, and Safety. 

In conclusion, tracking the provenance of goods using blockchain technology can help to increase transparency, accountability, and trust in the supply chain. By creating a tamper-proof record of a product's journey from its origin to its final destination, businesses can ensure that their products are ethically sourced, compliant with regulations, and of high quality.
