## Unit 3 - Permissioned Blockchains

Permissioned blockchains are also known as private blockchains, where the network access is restricted to a certain group of participants. In contrast to public blockchains, permissioned blockchains have more control over the participants and the validation of transactions. In this unit, we will explore the following topics:

### 1. Permissioned Blockchain Overview:

- Definition and characteristics of permissioned blockchains.
- Differences between permissioned and public blockchains.
- Advantages and disadvantages of permissioned blockchains.

### 2. Nodes and Consensus Mechanisms:

- Node types in permissioned blockchains, such as validators, endorsers, and auditors.
- Consensus mechanisms used in permissioned blockchains, such as Proof of Authority (PoA) and Byzantine Fault Tolerance (BFT).
- Comparison of different consensus mechanisms and their suitability for different use cases.

### 3. Permissioned Blockchain Architecture:

- Components of permissioned blockchain architectures, such as the network layer, consensus layer, and application layer.
- Detailed explanation of the Hyperledger Fabric permissioned blockchain architecture, including its components and how they interact with each other.

### 4. Smart Contracts and Chaincode:

- Smart contracts and their importance in permissioned blockchains.
- Chaincode, which is the smart contract implementation in Hyperledger Fabric.
- How to write and deploy chaincode in Hyperledger Fabric using the Go programming language.

### 5. Applications of Permissioned Blockchains:

- Use cases of permissioned blockchains, such as supply chain management, healthcare, and finance.
- Advantages of using permissioned blockchains in these use cases, such as increased efficiency, transparency, and security.

To remember the different consensus mechanisms used in permissioned blockchains, you can use the mnemonic "PoA, BFT, and More". This stands for Proof of Authority (PoA), Byzantine Fault Tolerance (BFT), and other consensus mechanisms that may be used in permissioned blockchains. Another mnemonic for remembering the components of the Hyperledger Fabric permissioned blockchain architecture is "NCA", which stands for Network layer, Consensus layer, and Application layer.
