
### Protocols for the notes of the Unit 1 - Introduction to Blockchain in the subject of Block chain Architecture Design

Protocols are the rules and regulations that govern the functioning of a blockchain network. They are responsible for the secure and reliable operation of the network and are designed to incentivize the participants to behave in a certain manner. 

The following are some of the most common protocols used in blockchain networks:

- Proof-of-Work (PoW): This is the most widely used protocol in blockchain networks. It is a consensus mechanism that requires miners to solve complex mathematical puzzles in order to validate transactions and create new blocks.

- Proof-of-Stake (PoS): This is an alternative consensus mechanism to PoW. It requires participants to stake or “lock” a certain amount of their tokens in order to validate transactions and create new blocks.

- Byzantine Fault Tolerance (BFT): This is a consensus algorithm that is used to ensure that the network remains secure and reliable even in the presence of malicious actors.

- Delegated Proof-of-Stake (DPoS): This is a variant of PoS that allows participants to vote for a set of delegates who will be responsible for validating transactions and creating new blocks.

- Consensus by Betting (CbB): This is a consensus algorithm that requires participants to bet on the outcome of a transaction in order to validate it.

- Proof-of-Authority (PoA): This is a consensus algorithm that requires participants to prove their identity in order to validate transactions and create new blocks.

- Proof-of-Elapsed-Time (PoET): This is a consensus algorithm that requires participants to wait a certain amount of time before they can validate transactions and create new blocks.

- Practical Byzantine Fault Tolerance (PBFT): This is a consensus algorithm that is used to ensure that the network remains secure and reliable even in the presence of malicious actors.
