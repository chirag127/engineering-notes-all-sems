
### Consensus protocols for Permissioned Blockchains 

Permissioned blockchains are those blockchains where the participants in the network are known and trusted. The consensus protocols used in these networks are different from those used in public blockchains. The consensus protocols used in permissioned blockchains need to be secure, efficient and scalable.

##### Types of Consensus Protocols

1. Proof of Authority (PoA): In PoA, network participants are identified and authorized by a set of validators. The validators are responsible for verifying transactions and maintaining the network. This type of consensus protocol is used in private networks and is suitable for low-latency applications.

2. Practical Byzantine Fault Tolerance (PBFT): PBFT is a consensus protocol that is used in permissioned blockchains. It is based on the Byzantine Generals Problem and ensures that the consensus is reached even if some of the validators are malicious. PBFT is suitable for networks with high throughput and low latency.

3. Delegated Proof of Stake (DPoS): DPoS is a consensus protocol that is used in permissioned blockchains. In this protocol, the validators are selected by the network participants. The validators are responsible for verifying transactions and maintaining the network. DPoS is suitable for networks with high throughput and low latency.

##### Mnemonics 

A mnemonic for remembering the types of consensus protocols used in permissioned blockchains is: 

PoA - Proof of Authority, PBFT - Practical Byzantine Fault Tolerance, DPoS - Delegated Proof of Stake.
