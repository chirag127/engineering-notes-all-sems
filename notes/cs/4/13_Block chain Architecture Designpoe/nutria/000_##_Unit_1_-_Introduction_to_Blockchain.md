
## Unit 1 - Introduction to Blockchain

Blockchain is a distributed ledger technology that allows for secure, transparent, and immutable transactions. It is a decentralized system that can be used to store and transfer data, as well as verify the authenticity of transactions.

**Mnemonic:** BLOCKCHAIN = **B**lockchain is **L**edger **O**f **C**onfirmed **H**istory **A**nd **I**mmutable **N**etwork

**Advantages:**
- Increased security: Transactions are encrypted and stored on a distributed ledger, making it difficult for malicious actors to tamper with or alter data.
- Transparency: All transactions are visible to everyone on the network, making it easier to detect fraudulent activity.
- Immutability: Once a transaction has been added to the ledger, it cannot be changed or removed.
- Low cost: Transactions are processed quickly and without the need for intermediaries, resulting in lower transaction costs.

**Disadvantages:**
- Scalability: As the network grows, it becomes more difficult to process transactions in a timely manner.
- Lack of regulation: As the technology is still relatively new, there are no established regulations or standards that govern its use.
- Energy consumption: The process of verifying transactions requires a lot of energy, which can be costly.

**Applications:**
- Cryptocurrency: Blockchain technology is used to create and manage digital currencies such as Bitcoin and Ethereum.
- Smart contracts: Smart contracts are self-executing contracts that can be used to automate processes such as payments, asset transfers, and more.
- Supply chain management: Blockchain can be used to track the movement of goods and materials, ensuring that all transactions are secure and transparent.

**Examples:**
- Bitcoin: The world’s first decentralized digital currency, created in 2009.
- Ethereum: A blockchain-based platform that allows users to create and execute smart contracts.
- Hyperledger Fabric: An open source platform for developing enterprise-grade blockchain applications.
