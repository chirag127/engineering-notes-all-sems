
## Unit 3 - Permissioned Blockchains

Permissioned blockchains are distributed ledger networks that require permission to access and participate in the network. Unlike public blockchains, such as Bitcoin, permissioned blockchains are not open to everyone. This means that users must be approved and given permission to join the network.

### Advantages

* Increased privacy: Permissioned blockchains only allow approved users to access the network, making it much more secure and private.
* Faster transactions: Permissioned blockchains can process transactions much faster than public blockchains.
* Lower cost: The cost of running a permissioned blockchain is much lower than running a public blockchain.

### Disadvantages

* Centralized control: Permissioned blockchains are typically controlled by a single entity or group, which can lead to issues with centralization.
* Lack of decentralization: Permissioned blockchains lack the decentralization that public blockchains have, which can lead to issues with trust.
* Limited scalability: Permissioned blockchains are limited in their scalability, as they require permission to join the network.

### Examples

* Hyperledger Fabric: Hyperledger Fabric is an open source permissioned blockchain platform developed by the Linux Foundation. It is designed for enterprise-level applications and supports multiple consensus algorithms.
* R3 Corda: R3 Corda is an open source distributed ledger platform designed for financial services. It is based on a permissioned blockchain and allows users to build applications on top of it.

### Applications

* Supply chain management: Permissioned blockchains can be used to track and manage the supply chain, from raw materials to finished products.
* Identity management: Permissioned blockchains can be used to securely store and manage digital identities.
* Healthcare: Permissioned blockchains can be used to securely store and share medical data.
