## Unit 1 - Introduction to Blockchain

### What is Blockchain?

- Blockchain is a distributed ledger that maintains a continuously growing list of records called blocks.
- Each block contains a timestamp and a link to the previous block.
- By design, blockchain is resistant to modification of the data. So, the data in it is highly secure and tamper-proof.
- Blockchain is decentralized, so no single entity controls the data. Multiple parties have a copy of the entire blockchain and comes to a consensus on the validity of the records.

### How does Blockchain work?

- New transactions are recorded in a new block that is added to the blockchain in a linear, chronological order.
- Each block contains a timestamp and a link to the previous block in the chain.
- The blocks are all secured and bound to each other using cryptographic hashes.
- The blockchain is decentralized across many nodes in a peer-to-peer network.
- The peers constantly verify and reconcile transactions via the network.
- This makes the records more transparent, secure, and harder to fraud as there is no central point of failure.

### Key characteristics of Blockchain

- Decentralization - The blockchain network has no central authority or single administrator.
- Transparency - The blockchain is an open, distributed ledger that can be viewed by anyone.
- Immutability - Records in the blockchain cannot be altered or removed, only new records can be added.
- Security - blockchain is secure through encryption and decentralization.
- Consensus - The blockchain network uses consensus mechanisms to verify and validate transactions.
- Smart contracts - blockchain can execute programmed transactions called smart contracts.
