### Invoice Management Discounting for the notes of the Unit 7 - Use case 2 in the subject of Block chain Architecture Design

- Invoice discounting is a method of financing in which a company sells its accounts receivable to a finance company, known as a factor, at a discount. The factor analyzes the credit-worthiness of the accounts receivable and advances the company a portion of the accounts receivable amount, retaining a percentage, known as a discount fee, as profit.
- The company gets immediate cash to support its operations, while the factor assumes the risk that the accounts receivable may not be fully collectible. Once the account receivable is paid, the factor forwards the remainder of the payment, less the discount fee, to the company.
- Advantages: Provides immediate cash flow, frees up working capital, company avoids debt, factor assumes credit risk.
- Disadvantages: Loss of control over receivables, fees reduce amount received, reliance on factor, disclosure of financials.
- Example: Company has $100,000 in accounts receivable and sells them to a factor for $90,000. Once collected, factor returns $95,000 ($100,000 - $5,000 discount fee) to the company.
- Applications: Companies with inconsistent cash flow, rapid growth, or maxed-out credit lines. Provides an alternative source of financing.
- In block chain, the invoice details and accounts receivable details can be stored in an immutable and transparent manner. The factor and company can track the collections and payments in real-time. This increases the efficiency, reduces fraud and speeds up the process. The fees and discount rates can also be pre-defined in smart contracts for automatic settlements.
