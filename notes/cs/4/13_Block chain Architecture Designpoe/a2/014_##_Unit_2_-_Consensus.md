## Unit 2 - Consensus

Consensus is a crucial concept in blockchain networks. It refers to the agreement on the validity of transactions and the order in which they are recorded (chronological order) into the blockchain.

- Consensus protocols are algorithms that allow all the nodes in a blockchain network to agree on the updated state of the blockchain. The state includes:
-- The order of transactions
-- The number of coins owned by each user
-- Other data that needs to be kept in sync across all nodes

- The most popular consensus protocols are:
-- Proof-of-Work (PoW): Used in Bitcoin and Ethereum 1.0. Miners solve complex puzzles to add blocks to the blockchain and are rewarded with cryptocurrency. Pros: secure, simple. Cons: resource intensive, centralizing tendency.
-- Proof-of-Stake (PoS): Validators stake coins to verify transactions and are chosen to add blocks based on their stake size ("stake weight"). Pros: energy efficient, decentralized. Cons: Complex, security concerns. Used in: Ethereum 2.0, Algorand, Tezos, etc.
-- Practical Byzantine Fault Tolerance (PBFT): Validators communicate with each other to agree on the blockchain state. Pros: Fast, energy efficient. Cons: Complex, limited scalability. Used in: Hyperledger Fabric, Ripple, Stellar, etc.
-- Delegated Proof-of-Stake (DPoS): Token holders vote for "witnesses" to maintain the blockchain. Pros: Fast, energy efficient, decentralized. Cons: Voters have significant influence, risk of centralization. Used in: EOS, Steem, Ardor, etc.

Tips to remember consensus:
- Consensus is crucial for ensuring all nodes agree on the state of the blockchain
- Different protocols have various pros and cons (security vs efficiency trade-offs)
- The most popular protocols are PoW, PoS, PBFT and DPoS
