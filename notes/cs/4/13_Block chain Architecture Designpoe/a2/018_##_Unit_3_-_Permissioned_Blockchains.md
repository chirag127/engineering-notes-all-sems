## Unit 3 - Permissioned Blockchains

- Permissioned blockchains are private blockchain networks which place restrictions on who can participate in the network, execute transactions, and mine blocks.
- They are centered around the concept of permissions, where network participants must be explicitly authorized to join the network. This enables greater control, privacy, and efficiency.
- Some key characteristics of permissioned blockchains are:
    - Only authorized nodes can participate in the consensus process.
    - The identity of nodes is known and verified.
    - Access rights are controlled.
    - The blockchain network is not fully decentralized.
- The primary benefits of permissioned blockchains are:
    - Greater transaction throughput due to smaller node networks.
    - Stronger privacy since access is restricted.
    - Ability to integrate with existing systems and processes.
- However, the main drawbacks are:
    - Reduced decentralization as there are limitations around who can participate.
    - Single points of failure with centralized administration of the network.
    - Complete trust in the entities controlling permissions.
- Examples of permissioned blockchain networks include Hyperledger Fabric, R3 Corda, and Quorum.
- They are best suited for enterprise and banking applications where efficiency, privacy, and control are priorities over full decentralization.
