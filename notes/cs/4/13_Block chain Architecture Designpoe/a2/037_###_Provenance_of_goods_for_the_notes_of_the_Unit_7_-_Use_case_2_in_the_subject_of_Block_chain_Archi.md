### Provenance of goods for the notes of the Unit 7 - Use case 2 in the subject of Block chain Architecture Design

Provenance of goods refers to the origin and ownership of a good as it moves through a supply chain. Blockchain can be used to track the provenance of goods and detect fraud.

- Each participant in the supply chain maintains a copy of the blockchain.
- Asset transfers between participants are recorded on the blockchain with timestamps and digital signatures.
- This creates an immutable, trusted record of the full history of the asset.
- Any attempts to fraudulently modify ownership records would be detected by discrepancies between copies of the blockchain.
- This use case enables verification of ethical production, verification of authenticity/counterfeits, and improved recalls.

Mnemonics: "Blockchain tracks goods like a ledger, ensuring ethical sourcing with time-stamped records."

Advantages:
- Provides transparency and accountability across supply chains.
- Detects and prevents fraud.
- Enables verification of ethical production/sourcing.
- Improves product recalls and safety.

Disadvantages:
- Requires participation and buy-in from all supply chain parties.
- Incurs costs to implement and maintain blockchain infrastructure.
- Does not prevent all types of fraud (e.g. collusion between supply chain parties).

Examples:
- Everledger uses blockchain to track diamond provenance and prevent fraud.
- Walmart uses blockchain to track produce and improve food safety.
- Blockchain is being explored for supply chain tracking in many industries.

Applications:
-Luxury goods
-Precious metals/gemstones
-Pharmaceuticals
-Food produce
