### KYC for the notes of the Unit 6 - Use case 1 in the subject of Block chain Architecture Design

KYC stands for Know Your Customer. It is a process by which a business verifies the identity of its clients and assesses their suitability, along with the potential risks of illegal intentions towards the business relationship.

In the context of blockchain and cryptocurrencies, KYC is important for the following reasons:

- It helps prevent fraud and money laundering activities. By verifying user identity, it becomes difficult for malicious actors to create multiple accounts or use fake identities to conduct illegal activities.
- It enables businesses to comply with regulatory requirements. Most countries have KYC and AML (Anti-Money Laundering) laws that businesses must adhere to. Implementing KYC procedures helps blockchain companies and exchanges follow these regulations.
- It builds trust in the system. When users know that everyone on the system has been verified, it improves the credibility and security of the blockchain solution or platform.

The typical KYC process includes the following steps:

1. Customer provides personal details - Name, address, date of birth, etc.
2. Customer provides government-issued ID - Passport, driver's license, national ID card, etc.
3. Customer provides proof of address - Recent utility bill, bank statement, etc.
4. Customer's information is verified against various sanctions and watchlist databases.
5. Risk assessment is performed to classify the customer in terms of risk.
6. Appropriate monitoring is put in place for higher risk customers.

Some key points to remember about blockchain KYC:

- KYC data needs to be securely stored since it contains sensitive personal information. Public blockchains are not ideal for storing KYC data due to privacy concerns. Private blockchain or off-chain storage is preferable.
- KYC needs to be performed only once as long as the customer information does not change. The immutable nature of blockchains can aid in ensuring KYC data integrity.
- KYC compliance needs to be an ongoing process to account for changing regulations and to monitor risk profiles of customers. So, KYC procedures should be robust, automated, and adaptable.
