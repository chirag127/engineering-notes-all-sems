## Digital Money to Distributed Ledgers

- Digital money refers to the electronic transfer of funds versus physical cash transactions. Banks maintain digital records of account balances and transactions.
- With distributed ledgers like blockchain, this digital record keeping is decentralized across many nodes in a peer-to-peer network. There is no central authority or single institution maintaining the ledger.
- The distributed ledger is synchronized across the network, with consensus achieved through proof-of-work or proof-of-stake. This makes the ledger transparent, secure, and tamper-resistant.
- Bitcoin pioneered the first blockchain-based distributed ledger to track ownership and transactions of the digital currency. The blockchain ensured that Bitcoin transactions are secure, legitimate, and transparent without the need for a central authority.
- Blockchain's distributed ledger technology has applications beyond cryptocurrencies. It can be used to track and verify digital records and transactions of other assets like stocks, bonds, property records, medical records, supply chain records, etc. in a secure and trusted way.
- Some pros of distributed ledgers are decentralization, security, transparency. Some cons are scalability, interoperability, energy use, and adoption challenges.

Mnemonics:
Digital cash -> Digital distributed consensus
Central bank -> Distributed network
Ledger at one institution -> Ledger across many nodes

Learning tricks:
- Understand how digital money works with bank ledgers
- Think of how a decentralized blockchain distributes the ledger across many nodes
- This makes the ledger transparent, secure, and robust without central points of failure
