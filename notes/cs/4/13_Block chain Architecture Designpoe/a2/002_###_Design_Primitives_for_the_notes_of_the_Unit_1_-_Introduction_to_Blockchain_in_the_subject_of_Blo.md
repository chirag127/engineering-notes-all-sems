### Design Primitives for the notes of the Unit 1 - Introduction to Blockchain in the subject of Block chain Architecture Design

1. Peer-to-Peer Network: Blockchain is a peer-to-peer network where nodes are interconnected and transactions are broadcasted to all the nodes in the network. There is no central authority and nodes constantly communicate with each other.

 Mnemonic: Imagine a mesh network where nodes are communicating with each other directly.

2. Consensus: Since there is no central authority, consensus is achieved through various consensus algorithms like Proof-of-Work, Proof-of-Stake, etc. This ensures that all nodes agree on the state of the blockchain and prevents double spending.

 Mnemonic: Imagine multiple people trying to agree on something together without any mediator. Consensus helps them reach an agreement.

3. Smart Contracts: Smart contracts are programs stored on the blockchain that execute automatically when certain conditions are met. They allow precise control and automation of blockchain transactions.

 Mnemonic: Imagine a vending machine. You put in money and select a product, the machine automatically dispenses the product. Similarly, smart contracts execute automatically when certain conditions are fulfilled.

4. Decentralization: The blockchain is a decentralized system where the infrastructure is collectively owned and operated by all the participants in the network. This decentralization makes blockchains resistant to single points of failure.

 Mnemonic: Imagine a system not controlled by any single entity but operated collectively by multiple entities. There is no single point of control or failure in such a system.

5. Immutability: The data stored on the blockchain is immutable, meaning it cannot be altered or removed. This ensures data integrity and security. Previous data blocks are protected by cryptographic hashes.

 Mnemonic: Imagine data written on a permanent medium like stone. Once written, it cannot be erased or altered. The blockchain data is similar and cannot be changed.

The above points cover the key design primitives of a blockchain. Detailed diagrams and examples can be included if required. The primitives work together to enable crucial blockchain capabilities like decentralization, data integrity, automation, etc.
