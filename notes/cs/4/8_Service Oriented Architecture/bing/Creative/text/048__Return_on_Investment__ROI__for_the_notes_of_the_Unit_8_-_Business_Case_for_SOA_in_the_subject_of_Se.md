### Return on Investment (ROI) for SOA

- Return on investment (ROI) is a measure of the value or benefit that an organization can obtain from investing in a project or initiative, such as service-oriented architecture (SOA).
- ROI can be calculated by dividing the net benefit (benefit minus cost) by the cost of the investment, and multiplying by 100 to get a percentage.
- SOA can provide various benefits to an organization, such as reducing integration expense, increasing asset reuse, increasing business agility, and reducing business risk. These benefits can result in improved efficiency, quality, customer satisfaction, innovation, and revenue.
- However, SOA also involves costs, such as the initial investment in infrastructure, tools, and skills, the ongoing maintenance and governance of services, and the potential risks and challenges of implementing SOA, such as complexity, security, and organizational change.
- Therefore, calculating the ROI of SOA is not a simple or straightforward task. It requires a clear understanding of the business goals and drivers, the current and desired state of the IT environment, the expected benefits and costs of SOA, and the assumptions and uncertainties involved in the estimation process.
- There are different methods and models for estimating the ROI of SOA, such as the calculated reuse model, the business value model, the cost-benefit analysis model, and the balanced scorecard model. Each model has its own advantages and limitations, and may be more or less suitable for different types of SOA projects and scenarios.
- The calculated reuse model focuses on the value of reusing services and processes across the organization, and estimates the ROI based on the number of services available for reuse, the degree of reuse, and the service complexity. This model is useful for quantifying the cost savings and efficiency gains of SOA, but it may not capture the qualitative and strategic benefits of SOA, such as agility and innovation.
- The business value model focuses on the alignment of SOA with the business goals and drivers, and estimates the ROI based on the impact of SOA on key performance indicators (KPIs), such as revenue, profit, customer satisfaction, and market share. This model is useful for demonstrating the business relevance and competitive advantage of SOA, but it may not capture the technical and operational aspects of SOA, such as integration and governance.
- The cost-benefit analysis model focuses on the comparison of the costs and benefits of SOA over a specific time period, and estimates the ROI based on the net present value (NPV) and internal rate of return (IRR) of the SOA investment. This model is useful for evaluating the financial viability and sustainability of SOA, but it may not capture the intangible and long-term benefits of SOA, such as flexibility and adaptability.
- The balanced scorecard model focuses on the balance of the financial, customer, internal, and learning perspectives of SOA, and estimates the ROI based on the score of each perspective, which is derived from the measurement of relevant objectives, indicators, and targets. This model is useful for providing a comprehensive and holistic view of the value of SOA, but it may not capture the specific and detailed aspects of SOA, such as service design and implementation.
