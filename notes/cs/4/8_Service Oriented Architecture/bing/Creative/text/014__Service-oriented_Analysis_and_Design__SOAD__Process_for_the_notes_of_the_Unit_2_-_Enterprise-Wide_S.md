### Service-oriented Analysis and Design (SOAD) Process

- Service-oriented analysis and design (SOAD) is a methodology that refers to the modeling and design for service-oriented architecture (SOA) applications.
- SOAD aims to develop highly adaptable and reusable services that can meet the requirements of various potential clients and contexts.
- SOAD builds upon existing, well-disciplined and proven methodologies such as object-oriented analysis and design (OOAD), enterprise architecture frameworks and business process modeling (BPM).
- SOAD also considers the following important concepts and aspects:
  - Service categorization and aggregation: grouping and organizing services based on their functionality, granularity, quality and dependencies.
  - Policies and aspects: defining and enforcing non-functional requirements and cross-cutting concerns for services, such as security, reliability, performance and governance.
  - Meet-in-the middle processes: balancing the top-down and bottom-up approaches for service identification and specification, by using both business goals and existing assets as inputs.
  - Semantic brokering: resolving the semantic mismatches and interoperability issues among services, by using ontologies, vocabularies and mappings.
  - Service harvesting and knowledge brokering: discovering and reusing existing services and knowledge sources, by using registries, repositories and brokers.
- SOAD involves the following key elements:
  - Process model: defining the process and notation for orchestrating the SOAD activities, such as service analysis, service design, service implementation and service testing.
  - Instructions: providing a structured way to conceptualize services, by using techniques such as service modeling, service decomposition, service contract and service interface.
  - Standards: providing well-defined quality factors and best practices for service, capability, data and constraint granularity, as well as the roles and responsibilities of the SOAD participants, such as developers, architects and analysts.
  - Artifacts: defining the deliverables and outcomes of the SOAD activities, such as service models, service specifications, service contracts, service implementations and service tests.
  - Quality of service: facilitating end-to-end modeling and providing comprehensive tool support for the SOAD activities, such as service discovery, service composition, service orchestration and service monitoring.
