### EA and SOA for Business and IT Alignment

- EA (Enterprise Architecture) is a discipline that helps to understand, plan, and govern the alignment of business and IT in an organization. EA provides a holistic view of the current and desired state of the enterprise, and defines the principles, standards, and guidelines for achieving the desired outcomes.  
- SOA (Service Oriented Architecture) is an architectural style that promotes the design and implementation of loosely coupled, reusable, and interoperable services that can be orchestrated to deliver business value. SOA enables the alignment of business and IT by exposing business capabilities as services that can be consumed by different applications and processes.  
- EA and SOA are complementary and synergistic practices that can help to achieve strategic business and IT alignment. EA provides the vision, direction, and governance for the enterprise, while SOA provides the principles, patterns, and platform for the implementation of the solutions.  
- EA and SOA can benefit from each other in the following ways:
  - EA can leverage SOA to define the business architecture, identify the business services, and align them with the IT services. EA can also use SOA to establish the governance framework, policies, and standards for the design, development, and management of the services.  
  - SOA can leverage EA to understand the business drivers, goals, and requirements, and to align the service portfolio with the strategic objectives and priorities of the enterprise. SOA can also use EA to ensure the compliance, quality, and consistency of the services across the enterprise.  
- EA and SOA can be integrated and aligned using various methods and tools, such as:
  - SOA Reference Architecture: A generic model that defines the architectural components, patterns, standards, and best practices for developing and deploying SOA solutions. An organization can customize and adapt the SOA Reference Architecture to its specific context and needs, and use it as a guide for the design and implementation of the SOA solutions.  
  - SOA Governance: A set of processes, roles, responsibilities, and artifacts that ensure the effective and efficient management of the SOA initiatives and assets. SOA Governance helps to establish the decision-making authority, accountability, and control mechanisms for the SOA program, and to monitor and measure the performance and outcomes of the SOA solutions. SOA Governance should be aligned with the EA Governance, and follow the EA principles and standards.  
  - SOA Repository and Registry: A centralized and shared repository and registry that store and publish the metadata and information about the SOA artifacts, such as services, contracts, policies, and processes. The SOA Repository and Registry enable the discovery, reuse, and governance of the SOA assets, and facilitate the communication and collaboration among the SOA stakeholders. The SOA Repository and Registry should be integrated with the EA Repository and Registry, and follow the EA metadata and taxonomy.  
  - SOA Maturity Model: A framework that assesses the current state and the desired state of the SOA adoption and implementation in an organization, and defines the roadmap and the actions for achieving the SOA maturity. The SOA Maturity Model helps to identify the gaps, risks, and opportunities for the SOA program, and to prioritize and align the SOA initiatives and projects with the EA goals and objectives.
