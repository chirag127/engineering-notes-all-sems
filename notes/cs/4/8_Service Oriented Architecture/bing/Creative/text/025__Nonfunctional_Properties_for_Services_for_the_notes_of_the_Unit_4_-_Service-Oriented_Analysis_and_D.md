### Nonfunctional Properties for Services

Nonfunctional properties for services are the qualities and features that are desirable by the service users, but are not directly related to the functionality or behavior of the service. Nonfunctional properties can affect the performance, reliability, security, usability, and cost of a service, and can influence the service selection and composition decisions. Nonfunctional properties can also be used to specify the policies and constraints for the consumption and provision of a service, such as the price, payment, availability, and obligations of the service provider and consumer.

Some examples of nonfunctional properties for services are:

- Availability: The degree to which a service is accessible and operational at a given time and location.
- Performance: The measure of how well a service responds to the requests and demands of the service consumers, such as the response time, throughput, and scalability of the service.
- Security: The protection of a service and its data from unauthorized access, modification, or disclosure, as well as the assurance of the authenticity, integrity, and confidentiality of the service and its transactions.
- Usability: The ease of use and learnability of a service and its interface, as well as the satisfaction and trust of the service consumers.
- Cost: The amount of resources and money required to consume or provide a service, as well as the benefits and risks associated with the service.
- Quality: The degree to which a service meets the expectations and requirements of the service consumers and providers, as well as the standards and regulations of the service domain.

Nonfunctional properties for services can be formally described and specified using various methods and languages, such as XML, RDF, OWL, WS-Policy, and QoSML. Formal description of nonfunctional properties can enable the automated discovery, comparison, and evaluation of services, as well as the verification and monitoring of service level agreements (SLAs) between the service providers and consumers. Nonfunctional properties can also be assessed and measured using various techniques and tools, such as metrics, benchmarks, tests, and surveys.
