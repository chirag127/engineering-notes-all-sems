### Considerations for Service-oriented Applications

Service-oriented applications are software applications that are composed of services that communicate and interact with each other using standard protocols and interfaces. Service-oriented applications can leverage the benefits of service-orientation, such as reusability, interoperability, agility, and scalability. However, developing and maintaining service-oriented applications also requires some considerations, such as:

- The business value and strategic goals of the application should be aligned with the service inventory and the service-oriented architecture (SOA) principles.
- The application should use basic interoperability mechanisms, such as common data models, message formats, and communication protocols, to avoid unnecessary data transformation and integration complexity .
- The application should use shared services that are agnostic and reusable, rather than custom or specific implementations, to reduce redundancy and increase consistency .
- The application should support continued improvement and evolution of the services and the service inventory, rather than aiming for immediate perfection or finality .
- The application should use an enterprise service bus (ESB) or a similar architectural pattern to facilitate the integration and orchestration of the services, as well as to provide common functionalities, such as routing, security, logging, and monitoring .
- The application should follow the service contract and the service governance policies that define the interface, behavior, and lifecycle of the services, as well as the roles and responsibilities of the service providers and consumers .
