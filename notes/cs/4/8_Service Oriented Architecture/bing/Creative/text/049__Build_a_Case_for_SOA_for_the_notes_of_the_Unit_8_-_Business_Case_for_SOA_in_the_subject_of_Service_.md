### Build a Case for SOA

Service Oriented Architecture (SOA) is an architectural approach that aims to achieve loose coupling among interacting software agents. A service is a unit of work done by a service provider to achieve desired end results for a service consumer. Both provider and consumer are roles played by software agents on behalf of their owners.

SOA can offer many benefits to organizations, such as:

- Agility: SOA can enable faster and easier adaptation to changing business needs, by allowing services to be composed, orchestrated, and reused in different ways.
- Interoperability: SOA can facilitate communication and integration among heterogeneous systems, by using standard protocols and interfaces for service interactions.
- Reusability: SOA can reduce development and maintenance costs, by promoting the reuse of existing services and avoiding duplication of functionality.
- Alignment: SOA can improve the alignment between business and IT, by modeling services based on business processes and goals, and by enabling business users to monitor and control service execution.

However, SOA also involves some costs and risks, such as:

- Complexity: SOA can increase the complexity of the system architecture, by introducing additional layers, components, and dependencies among services.
- Governance: SOA can require more effort and resources for governance, by establishing policies, standards, and processes for service design, development, deployment, and management.
- Security: SOA can pose new security challenges, by exposing services to external consumers and by requiring secure and reliable service interactions across networks.
- Culture: SOA can demand a change in the organizational culture, by shifting from a siloed and project-based mindset to a service-oriented and collaborative one.

Therefore, building a case for SOA requires a careful analysis of the specific business context and objectives, and a comparison of the expected benefits and costs of SOA versus other alternatives. Some key questions that can help in this analysis are:

- What are the main business drivers and challenges that motivate the adoption of SOA?
- What are the current and desired capabilities and outcomes of the business processes and functions that SOA will support or enable?
- What are the existing and planned IT systems and assets that SOA will leverage or integrate?
- What are the key performance indicators and metrics that will measure the success and value of SOA?
- What are the risks and assumptions that may affect the feasibility and viability of SOA?
- What are the resources and skills that are needed and available for implementing and managing SOA?
