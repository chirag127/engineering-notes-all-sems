## Unit 4 - Service-Oriented Analysis and Design

- Service-oriented analysis and design (SOAD) is a methodology for developing software systems that are composed of loosely coupled, interoperable, and reusable services.
- SOAD aims to align the business and technical aspects of a system, by identifying the business processes, goals, and requirements, and then designing the services that support them.
- SOAD follows an iterative and incremental approach, where the system is developed in cycles of analysis, design, implementation, and testing.
- SOAD consists of the following phases:

  - **Service identification**: This phase involves identifying the potential services that can be part of the system, based on the business processes, goals, and requirements. The services should be cohesive, loosely coupled, reusable, and aligned with the business domain. The output of this phase is a service portfolio, which is a list of candidate services with their descriptions and categories.
  - **Service specification**: This phase involves defining the interface and behavior of each service, using a service description language such as WSDL or RAML. The service specification should include the inputs, outputs, operations, parameters, preconditions, postconditions, and quality attributes of the service. The output of this phase is a service contract, which is a formal agreement between the service provider and the service consumer.
  - **Service realization**: This phase involves designing and implementing the service logic, using a programming language or a service orchestration tool such as BPEL or BPMN. The service realization should follow the service contract and adhere to the service-oriented principles and best practices. The output of this phase is a service implementation, which is the executable code of the service.
  - **Service testing**: This phase involves verifying and validating the functionality, performance, reliability, security, and interoperability of the service, using various testing techniques and tools such as SOAPUI or Postman. The service testing should cover both the service interface and the service logic, and ensure that the service meets the expectations of the service consumer. The output of this phase is a service test report, which is a document that summarizes the test results and identifies the defects and issues of the service.

- SOAD benefits from the use of service modeling tools and standards, such as UML, SoaML, or ArchiMate, which can help to visualize, document, and communicate the service-oriented architecture and design of the system.
