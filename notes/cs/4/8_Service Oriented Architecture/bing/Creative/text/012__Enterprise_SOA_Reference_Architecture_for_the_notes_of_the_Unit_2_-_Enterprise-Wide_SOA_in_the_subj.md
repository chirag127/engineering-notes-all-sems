### Enterprise SOA Reference Architecture

- Enterprise SOA Reference Architecture (SOA RA) is a standard developed by The Open Group for designing and evaluating service-oriented architecture (SOA) solutions and enterprise architecture guidelines.
- SOA RA provides a blueprint for creating or assessing architecture, as well as insights, patterns, and building blocks for integrating fundamental elements of an SOA into a solution or enterprise architecture.
- SOA RA aims to answer some of the key questions and issues encountered by architects, such as:
  - What are the aspects, building blocks, and layers of an SOA that I need to consider in designing solutions, establishing enterprise architecture guidelines, or assessing an architecture based on service-oriented principles?
  - What are the building blocks I need to include in each layer of my solution or standardize as part of a enterprise architecture?
  - How do I ensure that my solution or enterprise architecture is aligned with the business goals, requirements, and processes?
  - How do I ensure that my solution or enterprise architecture is consistent, coherent, and compliant with the principles and best practices of SOA?
  - How do I ensure that my solution or enterprise architecture is scalable, adaptable, and resilient to changes and disruptions?
  - How do I ensure that my solution or enterprise architecture is secure, reliable, and manageable?
- SOA RA consists of nine layers representing nine key clusters of considerations and responsibilities that typically emerge in the process of designing an SOA solution or defining an enterprise architecture standard. The nine layers are:
  - Operational Systems Layer: This layer contains the existing systems and data sources that provide the functionality and information required by the business processes and services. It also includes the adapters and connectors that enable the integration and interoperability of the operational systems with the service components and services.
  - Service Component Layer: This layer contains the service components that encapsulate the business logic and data access of the operational systems and expose them as services. It also includes the service component frameworks that provide the common capabilities and infrastructure for developing, deploying, and managing the service components.
  - Services Layer: This layer contains the services that represent the business capabilities and functions that can be consumed by the business processes and consumers. It also includes the service descriptions that define the interface, contract, and policies of the services, as well as the service registry and repository that enable the discovery and governance of the services.
  - Business Process Layer: This layer contains the business processes that orchestrate the services to achieve the business goals and outcomes. It also includes the business process models that define the flow, logic, and rules of the business processes, as well as the business process engines that execute and monitor the business processes.
  - Consumer Layer: This layer contains the consumers that access and use the services and business processes to perform their tasks and roles. It also includes the consumer applications that provide the user interface and interaction for the consumers, as well as the consumer frameworks that provide the common capabilities and infrastructure for developing, deploying, and managing the consumer applications.
  - Integration Layer: This layer contains the integration components that enable the communication and coordination of the services, business processes, and consumers across different domains, platforms, and protocols. It also includes the integration patterns and standards that define the best practices and guidelines for implementing the integration components, as well as the integration infrastructure that provides the common capabilities and infrastructure for developing, deploying, and managing the integration components.
  - Quality of Service Layer: This layer contains the quality of service components that ensure the performance, availability, reliability, security, and compliance of the services, business processes, and consumers. It also includes the quality of service policies and standards that define the requirements and expectations for the quality of service components, as well as the quality of service infrastructure that provides the common capabilities and infrastructure for developing, deploying, and managing the quality of service components.
  - Information Layer: This layer contains the information components that enable the access, exchange, and analysis of the information produced and consumed by the services, business processes, and consumers. It also includes the information models and standards that define the structure, semantics, and quality of the information, as well as the information infrastructure that provides the common capabilities and infrastructure for developing, deploying, and managing the information components.
