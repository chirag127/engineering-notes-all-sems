### Concepts for the notes of the Unit 7 - Big Data and SOA in the subject of Service Oriented Architecture

- Big Data is a term that refers to the large and complex datasets that are generated from various sources and applications, such as social media, sensors, web logs, etc. Big Data has the characteristics of volume, velocity, variety, veracity, and value.
- Service Oriented Architecture (SOA) is a design paradigm that promotes the development and integration of loosely coupled, reusable, and interoperable services that can be composed to fulfill business requirements. SOA has the principles of abstraction, autonomy, reusability, statelessness, discoverability, composability, and loose coupling.
- Big Data and SOA can be combined to leverage the benefits of both approaches, such as scalability, flexibility, agility, and efficiency. Some of the common scenarios where Big Data and SOA can be applied are:
  - Data as a Service (DaaS): This is a service model that provides access to data sources and analytics through standardized interfaces and protocols, such as RESTful web services. DaaS can enable data sharing, integration, and consumption across different applications and platforms, as well as support data governance and security.
  - Analytics as a Service (AaaS): This is a service model that provides analytical capabilities and insights on demand, such as data mining, machine learning, natural language processing, etc. AaaS can enable data-driven decision making, business intelligence, and predictive analytics, as well as reduce the cost and complexity of data analysis.
  - Event-Driven SOA: This is a SOA style that supports the processing and handling of events, such as messages, signals, or notifications, that are generated from various sources and applications. Event-Driven SOA can enable real-time and reactive systems, such as complex event processing, stream processing, and event sourcing, as well as support event correlation, filtering, and aggregation.
  - Microservices: This is a SOA style that decomposes the application into small and independent services that communicate through lightweight protocols, such as HTTP or messaging. Microservices can enable high scalability, availability, and resilience, as well as support continuous delivery, deployment, and testing.
