### Stakeholder Objectives for the Business Case for SOA

- Stakeholders are the individuals or groups who have an interest or a stake in the project and its outcomes. They may include business owners, end users, developers, architects, testers, managers, vendors, regulators, policymakers, and the general public  .
- Stakeholder objectives are the goals or expectations that the stakeholders have for the project and how it will benefit them or address their needs or problems. Stakeholder objectives may vary depending on their roles, perspectives, and interests in the project.
- The business case for SOA is a document that explains how the benefits of a service-oriented architecture (SOA) approach outweigh its costs and risks, and why it should be adopted for the project. The business case for SOA should include the following elements :
  - The business problem or opportunity that the project aims to solve or create, and the context and background of the situation.
  - The proposed solution and how it leverages the principles and benefits of SOA, such as agility, interoperability, reusability, and alignment with business goals.
  - The costs and risks of implementing SOA, such as technical complexity, organizational change, governance, and security issues, and how they will be managed or mitigated.
  - The expected outcomes and benefits of SOA, such as improved efficiency, quality, customer satisfaction, innovation, and competitive advantage, and how they will be measured and evaluated.
  - The alternatives and options to SOA, such as traditional architectures or other architectural approaches, and why they are less desirable or feasible.
  - The recommendations and action plan for adopting SOA, such as the scope, timeline, resources, roles, and responsibilities of the project.
- The stakeholder objectives for the business case for SOA are the specific and measurable goals that the stakeholders want to achieve or see from the project and its SOA solution. The stakeholder objectives should be aligned with the business problem or opportunity, the proposed solution, and the expected outcomes and benefits of SOA. The stakeholder objectives should also be realistic, achievable, and relevant to the project and the organization.
- Some examples of stakeholder objectives for the business case for SOA are:
  - Business owners: Increase revenue, market share, and customer loyalty by offering more flexible and personalized services and products to customers.
  - End users: Enhance user experience, satisfaction, and productivity by accessing and using services and applications that are easy, fast, and reliable.
  - Developers: Reduce development time, cost, and effort by reusing and composing existing services and components instead of creating new ones from scratch.
  - Architects: Improve architectural quality, consistency, and scalability by following SOA principles and standards and applying best practices and patterns.
  - Testers: Simplify testing and quality assurance by using service contracts and interfaces to test and verify the functionality and performance of services and applications.
  - Managers: Optimize resource allocation, coordination, and communication by establishing clear roles, responsibilities, and governance for the project and its SOA solution.
  - Vendors: Strengthen partnership and collaboration by providing and consuming services and applications that are interoperable and compatible with different platforms and technologies.
  - Regulators: Ensure compliance and accountability by adhering to the rules and regulations that govern the financial systems and markets served by the project and its SOA solution.
  - Policymakers: Support economic and social development by shaping and influencing the environment and conditions that enable and facilitate the project and its SOA solution.
  - The general public: Benefit from the value and impact of the project and its SOA solution on the society and the environment.
