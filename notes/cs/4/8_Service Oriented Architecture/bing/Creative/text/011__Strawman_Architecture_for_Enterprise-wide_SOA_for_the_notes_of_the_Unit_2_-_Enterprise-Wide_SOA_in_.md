### Strawman Architecture for Enterprise-wide SOA

- A strawman architecture is a high-level, conceptual design that illustrates the main components and interactions of a system without going into the details of implementation or technology choices.
- A strawman architecture for enterprise-wide SOA is a way of describing how different services, applications, and processes can be integrated and orchestrated across an organization using the principles and standards of service-oriented architecture (SOA).
- A strawman architecture for enterprise-wide SOA typically consists of the following layers:
  - **Business layer**: This layer defines the business goals, strategies, and processes of the organization. It also identifies the key business services that support the business processes and the business events that trigger or affect them.
  - **Service layer**: This layer defines the service contracts, interfaces, and policies that govern the interaction and communication between the business services and the consumers of those services. It also defines the service registry and repository that store and publish the service metadata and artifacts.
  - **Integration layer**: This layer defines the service bus, adapters, and mediators that enable the interoperability and integration of the business services and the applications that consume or provide them. It also defines the service orchestration and choreography that coordinate the execution and collaboration of the business services and the business processes.
  - **Application layer**: This layer defines the applications that implement or consume the business services. It also defines the application components, frameworks, and platforms that support the development, deployment, and management of the applications.
  - **Infrastructure layer**: This layer defines the physical and logical resources that support the operation and performance of the applications and the services. It also defines the infrastructure services, such as security, monitoring, logging, and auditing, that provide common functionality and quality of service for the applications and the services.
- A strawman architecture for enterprise-wide SOA can be represented using different notations and diagrams, such as UML, BPMN, or ArchiMate, depending on the level of abstraction and the audience of the design. A strawman architecture for enterprise-wide SOA can also be refined and validated using different methods and tools, such as prototyping, simulation, or testing, depending on the scope and the complexity of the design.
