## Unit 10 - EA and SOA for Business and IT Alignment

- EA stands for Enterprise Architecture, which is a framework that covers all the dimensions of IT architecture for the enterprise, such as business, data, application, and technology architectures.
- SOA stands for Service Oriented Architecture, which is an architectural strategy that uses the concept of "Services" as the underlying business-IT alignment entity. Services are self-contained, reusable, and loosely coupled components that provide functionality to support business processes.
- EA and SOA share a similar goal of bridging the gap between business and IT through business-aligned services. Both aim to achieve flexibility, agility, standardization, reuse, and innovation in the IT landscape .
- However, EA and SOA also have different scopes and perspectives. EA is a holistic and strategic view of the enterprise, while SOA is a tactical and operational view of the service layer. EA provides the principles, standards, and guidelines for the IT architecture, while SOA provides the patterns, best practices, and governance for the service design and implementation.
- Therefore, EA and SOA need to work together to effectively build an optimal IT landscape that supports the business needs and priorities. EA and SOA can complement each other in the following ways :
  - EA can provide the business architecture that defines the business services, processes, roles, and capabilities that SOA needs to support.
  - EA can provide the SOA reference architecture that defines the architectural patterns, standards, and best practices for developing SOA solutions.
  - SOA can provide the service metadata that describes the service contracts, interfaces, dependencies, and policies that EA needs to manage and govern.
  - SOA can provide the service governance framework that defines the processes, roles, responsibilities, and artifacts for governing the service lifecycle and quality.
  - EA and SOA can collaborate on the service portfolio management that identifies, prioritizes, and aligns the service requirements and investments with the business goals and strategies.
