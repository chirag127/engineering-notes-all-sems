According to the web search results, service-oriented architecture (SOA) is an architectural model that aims to enhance the agility and cost-effectiveness of an enterprise while reducing the overall burden of IT on an organization. One of the strategic benefits of SOA is increased return on investment (ROI), which represents the tangible value and cost-savings that something provides when compared to the cost of producing and governing it.

The following diagram illustrates the basic architecture of a service-oriented solution, which consists of multiple services that form a service composition to automate a particular task or business process. The services are designed according to the service-orientation design principles, such as standardized service contract, service loose coupling, service abstraction, service reusability, service autonomy, service statelessness, service discoverability, and service composability. The services are also interoperable, federated, aligned with business and technology, and vendor-neutral.

The cost savings of SOA can be derived from the following factors:

- Reduced waste and redundancy: By reusing existing services, the need for developing and maintaining duplicate functionality is reduced, which saves time and resources.
- Reduced size and operational cost: By decomposing complex systems into smaller and simpler services, the size and complexity of the solution is reduced, which lowers the operational cost and improves the performance and reliability.
- Reduced overhead associated with governance and evolution: By standardizing the service contracts and interfaces, the governance and evolution of the services is simplified, which reduces the effort and cost of managing and updating the services.
- Increased business agility: By composing and recomposing services to meet changing business needs, the solution can adapt to the market and customer demands faster and easier, which increases the revenue and customer satisfaction.
