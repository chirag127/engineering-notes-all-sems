### Emergence of MSA for the notes of the Unit 1 - Introduction: SOA and MSA Basics in the subject of Service Oriented Architecture

- Microservice Architecture (MSA) is a novel service-based architectural style for distributed software systems.
- MSA emerged as a response to the demand for more scalable, agile, and resilient software systems that can leverage the cloud computing environment.
- MSA follows the developments of Service Oriented Architecture (SOA), but puts a stronger focus on self-containment and independence of services.
- Each microservice is responsible for realizing exactly one business or technological capability that is distinct from other services' capabilities.
- Additionally, on the implementation and operation level, microservices are self-contained in that they are developed, tested, deployed and operated independently from each other.
- MSA also promotes the use of fine-grained services and the cooperation of the nodes in the cloud, which enables better performance, availability, and fault-tolerance.
- MSA differs from SOA in several aspects, such as the granularity, autonomy, communication, data management, governance, and deployment of services.

#### Mnemonic

- To remember the key aspects of MSA, you can use the acronym **FACADE**:

  - **F**ine-grained services
  - **A**utonomous services
  - **C**loud-based cooperation
  - **A**gile development and operation
  - **D**ecentralized data management
  - **E**xact capability realization

#### Learning trick

- To compare and contrast MSA and SOA, you can use the following table:

| Aspect | MSA | SOA |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Granularity | Fine-grained | Coarse-grained |
| Autonomy | High | Low |
| Communication | Synchronous or asynchronous | Mostly synchronous |
| Data management | Decentralized | Centralized |
| Governance | Lightweight | Heavyweight |
| Deployment | Independent | Dependent |
