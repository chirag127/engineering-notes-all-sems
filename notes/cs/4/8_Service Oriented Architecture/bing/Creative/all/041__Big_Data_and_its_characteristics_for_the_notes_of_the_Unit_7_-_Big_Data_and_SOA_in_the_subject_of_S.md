### Big Data and its characteristics for the notes of the Unit 7 - Big Data and SOA in the subject of Service Oriented Architecture

Big data is a term that describes large, hard-to-manage volumes of data – both structured and unstructured – that inundate businesses on a day-to-day basis. Big data can be generated from various sources like social media platforms, business processes, machines, networks, human interactions, etc. Big data can be used for various purposes like healthcare, academia, banking, manufacturing, IT, etc.

Big data is more than just a large volume of data. It has some distinctive characteristics that make it different from traditional data. The five characteristics of big data are- volume, value, variety, velocity, and veracity. These are also known as the 5 V's of big data.

- **Volume**: Volume refers to the amount of data that is generated and stored. Big data can range from terabytes to petabytes or even exabytes of data. The volume of big data is increasing exponentially as more data sources and devices are connected. The challenge is to store, process, and analyze such large volumes of data efficiently and effectively.
- **Value**: Value refers to the usefulness and relevance of the data for the business or the user. Big data can have a high potential value, but it may not be realized until it is processed and analyzed. The value of big data depends on the quality, context, and purpose of the data. The challenge is to extract meaningful insights and actionable information from the data that can improve decision making, performance, and outcomes.
- **Variety**: Variety refers to the diversity and complexity of the data types and formats. Big data can include structured, semi-structured, and unstructured data, such as text, images, audio, video, sensor data, web logs, social media posts, etc. The variety of big data poses challenges for data integration, transformation, and analysis, as different data sources and formats may require different tools and techniques.
- **Velocity**: Velocity refers to the speed and frequency at which the data is generated, collected, and processed. Big data can be produced and consumed in real-time or near-real-time, such as streaming data from sensors, web clicks, social media, etc. The velocity of big data demands fast and scalable data processing and analysis systems that can handle the data in motion and provide timely insights and responses.
- **Veracity**: Veracity refers to the accuracy, reliability, and trustworthiness of the data. Big data can have a high degree of uncertainty, inconsistency, incompleteness, and noise, due to the variety and velocity of the data sources and the potential for human or machine errors. The veracity of big data affects the quality and validity of the data analysis and the confidence and credibility of the data-driven decisions.

A possible mnemonic to remember the 5 V's of big data is:

**V**ery **V**ast **V**ariety of **V**ery **V**alid data.

Some possible learning tricks to understand the 5 V's of big data are:

- To illustrate the concept of volume, think of how many books, DVDs, or CDs you would need to store one terabyte of data. Then multiply that by a thousand to get one petabyte, and by a million to get one exabyte.
- To illustrate the concept of value, think of how a diamond is more valuable than a piece of coal, even though they are both made of carbon. Similarly, big data can have more value than traditional data, depending on how it is processed and analyzed.
- To illustrate the concept of variety, think of how many different types of data you encounter in your daily life, such as text messages, emails, photos, videos, music, etc. Then think of how many different formats and standards these data types can have, such as JPEG, MP3, PDF, etc.
- To illustrate the concept of velocity, think of how fast and frequently you receive or send data, such as streaming a video, browsing a website, updating a social media status, etc. Then think of how fast and frequently businesses or organizations need to process and analyze data, such as monitoring traffic, weather, stock prices, etc.
