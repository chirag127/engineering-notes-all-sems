### Return on Investment (ROI) for the notes of the Unit 8 - Business Case for SOA in the subject of Service Oriented Architecture

- Return on Investment (ROI) is a measure of the value or benefit that an organization can gain from implementing a project or initiative, such as Service Oriented Architecture (SOA).
- ROI can be calculated by comparing the expected costs and benefits of the project over a certain period of time, usually expressed as a percentage or a ratio.
- ROI can help organizations justify their investment in SOA and evaluate its performance and impact on the business.
- SOA can provide benefits in four basic categories: reducing integration expense, increasing asset reuse, increasing business agility, and reducing business risk.
- Reducing integration expense means that SOA can lower the complexity and cost of integrating and managing distributed computing environments by using standards-based interfaces and loosely coupled services.
- Increasing asset reuse means that SOA can enable the development and deployment of reusable services that can be shared across different systems and processes, reducing duplication and increasing efficiency .
- Increasing business agility means that SOA can improve the responsiveness and adaptability of the IT infrastructure to the changing needs and demands of the business, enabling faster and easier changes and innovations.
- Reducing business risk means that SOA can enhance the reliability, security, and compliance of the IT systems and services, minimizing the potential for errors, failures, and breaches.
- To calculate the ROI of SOA, organizations need to identify and quantify the costs and benefits of SOA in each of these categories, as well as the time frame and the discount rate for the analysis.
- One possible formula for calculating the ROI of SOA is:

`ROI = (NSR * DR * C * B) / I`

where:

  - NSR is the number of services that are available for reuse
  - DR is the degree of reuse from system to system
  - C is the complexity of each service
  - B is the benefit per unit of complexity
  - I is the investment in SOA

- For example, if an organization has 100 services available for reuse (NSR = 100), and the degree of reuse is 50% (DR = 0.5), and the average complexity of each service is 300 function points (C = 300), and the benefit per unit of complexity is $10 (B = 10), and the investment in SOA is $1,000,000 (I = 1,000,000), then the ROI of SOA is:

`ROI = (100 * 0.5 * 300 * 10) / 1,000,000`

`ROI = 0.15`

- This means that for every dollar invested in SOA, the organization can expect to gain 15 cents in return.
- Alternatively, the ROI of SOA can be expressed as a ratio or a percentage, such as:

`ROI ratio = (NSR * DR * C * B) / I - 1`

`ROI ratio = 0.15 - 1`

`ROI ratio = -0.85`

`ROI percentage = ROI ratio * 100%`

`ROI percentage = -0.85 * 100%`

`ROI percentage = -85%`

- This means that the organization can expect to lose 85% of its investment in SOA.
- However, this formula is only a simplified example and does not account for other factors that may affect the ROI of SOA, such as the time value of money, the risk and uncertainty of the project, the opportunity cost of the investment, and the intangible benefits of SOA that are difficult to quantify, such as customer satisfaction, employee morale, and competitive advantage.
- Therefore, organizations should use more sophisticated and comprehensive methods and tools to calculate the ROI of SOA, such as cost-benefit analysis, net present value, internal rate of return, payback period, and break-even point.
- Additionally, organizations should consider the social return on investment (SROI) of SOA, which is the value or impact that SOA can create for the stakeholders and the society, beyond the financial returns.
