### Service Orientation in Daily Life for the notes of the Unit 1 - Introduction: SOA and MSA Basics in the subject of Service Oriented Architecture

- Service orientation is a predisposition to being helpful, thoughtful, considerate, and cooperative.
- Service orientation is also the ability and desire to anticipate, recognize and meet others' needs, sometimes even before those needs are articulated.
- Service orientation is a key skill for future jobs, as it helps to build lasting and loyal relationships with customers, colleagues, and stakeholders.
- Service orientation can be applied in daily life in various ways, such as:

  - Checking in with your people: A phone call or short text message to check in with the folks in your life is a simple way to let them know they’re important to you.
  - If you’ve got it, give it: If you have something that someone else needs or wants, whether it’s a skill, a resource, or a connection, share it generously.
  - Volunteering at a local organization: Giving your time and energy to a cause that matters to you is a great way to serve your community and make a positive impact.
  - Doing what you’re doing, but better: Whatever you do, whether it’s your job, your hobby, or your passion, strive to do it with excellence and care. This will show your respect and appreciation for yourself and others.
  - Taking responsibility for your impact: Be aware of how your actions and words affect others, and try to minimize any negative consequences. Apologize when you make a mistake, and learn from it.
  - Understanding customer attitudes through social listening: Use tools like Sprout Social and Mention to track what people are saying about your company, product, or service online. This will help you to respond to their feedback, address their issues, and improve their satisfaction.
  - Offering tremendous perceived value to your customers: Provide your customers with more than they expect, and make them feel special and valued. This will create a memorable and positive experience that will make them loyal and happy.
  - Giving employees autonomy to uphold their service standard: Empower your employees to make decisions and take actions that will benefit your customers, without having to seek approval or permission. This will show your trust and respect for your employees, and enable them to deliver faster and better service.

- Some mnemonics and learning tricks for service orientation are:

  - Remember the four C's of service orientation: helpful, thoughtful, considerate, and cooperative.
  - Use the acronym SERVE to remind yourself of the five simple ideas to incorporate service into your daily life: check in, give it, volunteer, do better, take responsibility.
  - Think of service orientation as a way of being, not just a way of doing. It is a mindset and a attitude that guides your actions and interactions with others.
