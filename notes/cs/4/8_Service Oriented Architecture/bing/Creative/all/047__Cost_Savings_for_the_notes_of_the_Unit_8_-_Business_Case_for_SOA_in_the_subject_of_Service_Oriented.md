### Cost Savings for the notes of the Unit 8 - Business Case for SOA in the subject of Service Oriented Architecture

- SOA is a development methodology that encourages sharing of remotely invocable application functions throughout networks.
- SOA can reduce the marginal cost of building new applications by increasing the service-reuse rate.
- SOA can also reduce the total cost of ownership by consolidating silos of redundant application functionality and data throughout organizations.
- SOA requires a significant upfront investment in rethinking and redesigning the application architecture, development, integration, deployment and management processes.
- SOA can deliver both hard and soft monetary paybacks from increased efficiency, agility, scalability and quality of service.

Some examples of cost savings from SOA are:

- Thomson Learning saved over £2.8 million in development costs over the past three years based on reuse of existing functionality within the service catalog.
- Abebooks reduced the number of Oracle database licenses by consolidating servers that accompanied their SOA push.
- Standard Life Group reduced the cost of data complexity by 80% by using SOA to refine the data so it fits into their SOA.

A possible mnemonic to remember the cost savings of SOA is:

**S**hare, **R**euse, **C**onsolidate, **R**educe, **D**eliver
