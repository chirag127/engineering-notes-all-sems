### Design of Client Services for the notes of the Unit 4 - Service-Oriented Analysis and Design in the subject of Service Oriented Architecture

- Client services are the services that consume or invoke other services to provide a business functionality or a user interface.
- Client services can be classified into three types: presentation services, intermediary services, and task services.
- Presentation services are the services that provide a user interface for accessing other services, such as web pages, mobile apps, or dashboards.
- Intermediary services are the services that orchestrate, coordinate, or mediate the interactions between other services, such as business process services, composite services, or broker services.
- Task services are the services that perform a specific business task or function by invoking other services, such as validation services, calculation services, or notification services.
- The design of client services involves the following steps:
  - Identify the business requirements and goals for the client service
  - Identify the target users and stakeholders of the client service
  - Identify the services that the client service needs to consume or invoke
  - Define the service contract and interface for the client service
  - Define the service logic and behavior for the client service
  - Define the service quality attributes and non-functional requirements for the client service
  - Define the service deployment and governance strategy for the client service
- The design of client services should follow the general principles of service design, such as:
  - Focusing on the user and customer needs rather than the internal needs of the business
  - Delivering a unified and efficient system rather than component-by-component
  - Creating value for users and customers and being as efficient as possible
  - Treating special events as common events and designing processes to accommodate them
  - Involving the users and stakeholders in the design process
  - Prototyping and testing the service before developing it in full
  - Developing the service as a minimum viable service and iterating and improving it based on feedback
  - Collaborating with all relevant stakeholders in the design and delivery of the service
- The design of client services should also consider the specific characteristics and challenges of service-oriented architecture, such as  :
  - Aligning the service with the business domain and goals
  - Ensuring the service interoperability and compatibility with other services
  - Ensuring the service reusability and modularity
  - Ensuring the service scalability and performance
  - Ensuring the service reliability and availability
  - Ensuring the service security and privacy
  - Ensuring the service maintainability and evolvability
  - Ensuring the service compliance and governance
- The design of client services can benefit from using some service-oriented analysis and design (SOAD) methodologies, such as :
  - Service-Oriented Modeling Framework (SOMF)
  - Service-Oriented Modeling and Architecture (SOMA)
  - Service-Oriented Development Method (SOD-M)
  - Service-Oriented Design and Development Methodology (SODDM)
  - These methodologies provide some guidelines, techniques, and tools for identifying, modeling, designing, and developing client services and other types of services in a service-oriented architecture.
