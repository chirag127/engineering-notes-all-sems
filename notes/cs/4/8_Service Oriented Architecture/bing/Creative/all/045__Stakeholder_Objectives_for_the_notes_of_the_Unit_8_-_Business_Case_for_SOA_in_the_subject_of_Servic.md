### Stakeholder Objectives for the notes of the Unit 8 - Business Case for SOA in the subject of Service Oriented Architecture

- Stakeholders are the individuals or groups who have an interest or a stake in the outcome of a service-oriented architecture (SOA) initiative.
- Stakeholders can be classified into two broad categories: business stakeholders and technical stakeholders.
- Business stakeholders are those who are concerned with the strategic, operational, and financial aspects of the organization, such as executives, managers, customers, and partners.
- Technical stakeholders are those who are involved in the design, development, testing, deployment, and maintenance of the SOA, such as architects, developers, testers, and administrators.
- Stakeholder objectives are the goals or expectations that each stakeholder has for the SOA initiative, which may vary depending on their role, perspective, and needs.
- Stakeholder objectives can be aligned with the business case for SOA, which is the justification or rationale for adopting a SOA approach in the organization.
- The business case for SOA should demonstrate how SOA can deliver value to the organization by addressing the following aspects:
  - Business drivers: the external or internal factors that motivate the organization to pursue SOA, such as market changes, customer demands, regulatory requirements, or competitive pressures.
  - Business benefits: the positive outcomes or advantages that SOA can provide to the organization, such as increased agility, interoperability, efficiency, quality, or innovation.
  - Business costs: the negative outcomes or disadvantages that SOA can entail for the organization, such as increased complexity, risk, or investment.
  - Business risks: the uncertainties or threats that SOA can pose to the organization, such as technical challenges, organizational resistance, or cultural barriers.
  - Business metrics: the measurable indicators or criteria that can be used to evaluate the performance or success of SOA, such as return on investment, customer satisfaction, or time to market.
- Some examples of stakeholder objectives for the business case for SOA are   :
  - Business executives: to increase revenue, profit, and market share by delivering innovative and customer-centric products and services.
  - Business managers: to improve operational efficiency, productivity, and quality by streamlining and automating business processes and workflows.
  - Business customers: to enhance customer experience, satisfaction, and loyalty by providing personalized and consistent service across multiple channels and platforms.
  - Business partners: to strengthen collaboration, integration, and trust by enabling seamless and secure data exchange and transactions.
  - Technical architects: to design and implement a flexible, scalable, and reliable SOA that supports the current and future business needs and goals.
  - Technical developers: to reuse and leverage existing and new services and components that adhere to the SOA standards and principles.
  - Technical testers: to ensure the functionality, performance, and security of the SOA and its services and components.
  - Technical administrators: to monitor and manage the SOA and its services and components, and to troubleshoot and resolve any issues or problems.

- A mnemonic to remember the stakeholder objectives for the business case for SOA is:

  - **B**usiness drivers, benefits, costs, risks, and metrics
  - **E**xecutives, managers, customers, and partners
  - **A**rchitects, developers, testers, and administrators
  - **R**evenue, efficiency, experience, collaboration, flexibility, reuse, quality, and security

- The acronym is **BEAR**.
