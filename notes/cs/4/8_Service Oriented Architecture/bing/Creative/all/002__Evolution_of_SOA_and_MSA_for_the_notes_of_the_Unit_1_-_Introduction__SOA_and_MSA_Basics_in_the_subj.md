### Evolution of SOA and MSA for the notes of the Unit 1 - Introduction: SOA and MSA Basics in the subject of Service Oriented Architecture

- Service-Oriented Architecture (SOA) is an enterprise-wide approach to software development of application components that takes advantage of reusable software components, or services.
- Microservices Architecture (MSA) is an architectural style that structures an application as a collection of loosely coupled, fine-grained, and independently deployable services.
- Both SOA and MSA are service-based architectures, which means that they are architectural patterns leveraging services as the primary component for developing applications.
- However, SOA and MSA differ in several aspects, such as service granularity, service communication, service deployment, service governance, and service implementation  .
- SOA emerged in the late 1990s and represents an important stage in the evolution of application development and integration. Before SOA was an option, connecting a monolithic application to data or functionality in another system required complex point-to-point integration that developers had to recreate for each new development project. Exposing those functions through SOA eliminates the need to recreate the deep integration every time.
- SOA provides four different service types: Functional services (i.e., business services), which are critical for business applications; Enterprise services, which serve to implement functionality across multiple business domains; Application services, which provide specific functionality for a single application; and Infrastructure services, which provide common functionality for the IT environment.
- SOA relies on an Enterprise Service Bus (ESB) to facilitate communication and integration between heterogeneous applications and messaging protocols . The ESB acts as a middleware layer that provides routing, transformation, orchestration, and mediation capabilities. The ESB also enables service discovery and registry, which allows services to be published and consumed by other applications.
- SOA services are coarse-grained, which means that they encapsulate a large amount of functionality and data in a single service . This makes them reusable across multiple applications and business domains, but also increases the complexity and coupling of the service interface and implementation .
- SOA services are deployed as a single unit, which means that any change or update to a service requires redeploying the entire service and coordinating with other dependent services . This reduces the agility and scalability of the SOA applications, as well as the availability and reliability of the services .
- SOA requires strong governance of service representation and implementation, which means that there are centralized policies and standards for designing, developing, testing, and deploying services . This ensures consistency and quality of the services, but also introduces overhead and bureaucracy in the development process .
- SOA services are implemented using service orchestration, which means that they use a centralized controller or coordinator to manage the flow of logic and data between services . This provides a clear and explicit representation of the business process, but also creates a single point of failure and a performance bottleneck .

- MSA emerged in the early 2010s and represents a further refinement and evolution of the service-based architecture concept. MSA is influenced by the rise of cloud computing, DevOps, and agile methodologies, which demand faster and more frequent delivery of software applications .
- MSA does not have a predefined set of service types, but rather allows each service to be designed and implemented according to its own business domain and functionality . This is also known as the bounded context principle, which means that each service has its own responsibility and data model, and does not share them with other services .
- MSA relies on lightweight and simple communication protocols, such as RESTful APIs, to enable communication and integration between services . The communication is usually asynchronous and event-driven, which means that services do not wait for a response from other services, but rather react to events or messages that are published or consumed by other services .
- MSA services are fine-grained, which means that they encapsulate a small amount of functionality and data
