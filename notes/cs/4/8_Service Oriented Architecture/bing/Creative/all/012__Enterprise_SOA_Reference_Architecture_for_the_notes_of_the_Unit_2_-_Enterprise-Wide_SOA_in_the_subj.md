### Enterprise SOA Reference Architecture for the notes of the Unit 2 - Enterprise-Wide SOA in the subject of Service Oriented Architecture

- Enterprise SOA Reference Architecture (SOA RA) is a blueprint for creating or evaluating architecture for solutions based on service-oriented principles and techniques.
- It provides guidelines and options for making architectural, design, and implementation decisions in the implementation of solutions.
- It also provides insights, patterns, and the building blocks for integrating fundamental elements of an SOA into a solution or enterprise architecture.
- It is developed by the SOA Reference Architecture project of the SOA Work Group within The Open Group, which is a vendor-neutral and technology-neutral consortium that works with customers, suppliers, consortia and other standard bodies to capture, understand and address current and emerging requirements, establish policies and share best practices for SOA.
- It is based on the SOA Source Book, which contains material relating to SOA developed by The Open Group for the benefit of enterprise, IT, and product architects.
- It is aligned with other SOA standards and frameworks, such as the SOA Ontology, the SOA Governance Framework, the Service Integration Maturity Model (OSIMM), and the TOGAF standard for enterprise architecture.

- The SOA RA has nine layers representing nine key clusters of considerations and responsibilities that typically emerge in the process of designing an SOA solution or defining an enterprise architecture standard. They are:

  - Operational Systems Layer: This layer contains the existing systems and data sources that provide the functionality and information that are exposed and consumed as services. It also includes the adapters and connectors that enable the integration and interoperability of the operational systems with the service components and services.
  - Service Component Layer: This layer contains the service components that implement the service logic and provide the service functionality. Service components are modular units of implementation that can be composed and reused to create services. They can be implemented using various technologies and platforms, such as Java, .NET, ESB, etc.
  - Services Layer: This layer contains the services that are the core building blocks of an SOA. Services are self-contained, loosely coupled, and standardized units of functionality that can be discovered, invoked, and composed by service consumers. Services are defined by service contracts that specify the interface, behavior, quality of service, and policies of the services.
  - Business Process Layer: This layer contains the business processes that orchestrate and coordinate the services to achieve the business goals and outcomes. Business processes are defined by business process models that specify the flow, logic, rules, and events of the business processes. Business processes can be executed by business process engines that support various standards and languages, such as BPMN, BPEL, etc.
  - Consumer Layer: This layer contains the service consumers that access and use the services to fulfill their needs and expectations. Service consumers can be human users or applications that interact with the services through various channels and devices, such as web browsers, mobile phones, etc. Service consumers can also be other services that invoke and compose the services to provide higher-level functionality.
  - Integration Layer: This layer contains the integration capabilities that enable the communication, mediation, and transformation of the service messages and data across the different layers, components, and systems of the SOA. Integration capabilities can be provided by various technologies and mechanisms, such as messaging, routing, brokering, mapping, etc.
  - Quality of Service Layer: This layer contains the quality of service capabilities that ensure the reliability, availability, performance, security, and scalability of the services and the SOA. Quality of service capabilities can be provided by various technologies and mechanisms, such as monitoring, logging, auditing, caching, load balancing, encryption, authentication, authorization, etc.
  - Information Layer: This layer contains the information capabilities that enable the management, governance, and analysis of the service data and metadata across the different layers, components, and systems of the SOA. Information capabilities can be provided by various technologies and mechanisms, such as data modeling, data quality, data integration, data warehousing, business intelligence, etc.
  - Governance Layer: This layer contains the governance capabilities that enable the definition, implementation, and enforcement of the policies, standards, and best practices for the services and the SOA. Governance capabilities can be provided by various technologies and mechanisms, such as governance framework, governance processes, governance roles, governance tools, etc.
