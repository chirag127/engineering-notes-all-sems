### Enterprise Architecture for the notes of the Unit 10 - EA and SOA for Business and IT Alignment in the subject of Service Oriented Architecture

- Enterprise Architecture (EA) is the organizing logic for business processes and IT infrastructure reflecting the integration and standardization requirements of the company’s operating model.
- EA is the process of aligning a business's strategic vision with its information technology.
- EA covers all the dimensions of IT architecture for the enterprise, such as business, data, application, and technology.
- EA helps to re-align IT developments with business objectives and address the growing gap between IT and business.
- EA provides a framework for planning, designing, implementing, and governing the IT systems of an enterprise.
- EA consists of major components and sub-components, such as:
  - Enterprise Transition Strategy: a roadmap that defines the sequence of projects and initiatives to move from the current state to the desired future state of the enterprise.
  - Release Architecture: a subset of the Enterprise Transition Strategy that defines the scope, schedule, and resources for a specific release of the IT system.
  - Enterprise Target Architecture: a description of the desired future state of the enterprise in terms of business, data, application, and technology.
    - EA Strategy and Function: a statement of the purpose, goals, principles, and requirements of the EA.
    - EA Principles: a set of guidelines and rules that govern the design and implementation of the EA.
    - EA Requirements: a set of functional and non-functional specifications that define the expected behavior and quality of the EA.
    - Business Architecture: a description of the business services, processes, scenarios, controls, and requirements of the enterprise.
    - Data Architecture: a description of the data entities, relationships, flows, and quality of the enterprise.
    - Application Architecture: a description of the applications, systems, interfaces, and services that support the business processes of the enterprise.
    - Technology Architecture: a description of the hardware, software, network, security, and infrastructure components that enable the IT systems of the enterprise.
- Service Oriented Architecture (SOA) is a powerful and flexible solution architecture for systems integration, resource sharing, and enabling agility in the modern enterprise.
- SOA is an architectural strategy that uses the concept of “Services” as the underlying business-IT alignment entity.
- SOA promotes an alignment between business and IT and allows disparate domains and information systems to collaborate together as part of a cohesive enterprise.
- SOA is based on the principles of loose coupling, reusability, interoperability, abstraction, and discoverability.
- SOA consists of three main roles: service providers, service consumers, and service brokers.
  - Service providers: the entities that offer and expose services to the service consumers.
  - Service consumers: the entities that request and consume services from the service providers.
  - Service brokers: the entities that facilitate the discovery and binding of services between the service providers and consumers.
- SOA and EA share a similar goal of bridging the gap between business and IT through business-aligned services.
- SOA and EA complement each other and can be integrated to achieve a higher level of business-IT alignment and agility.
- SOA and EA integration can be done at different levels, such as:
  - Strategic level: aligning the business vision, goals, and objectives with the IT vision, goals, and objectives.
  - Tactical level: aligning the business processes, scenarios, and requirements with the IT services, systems, and interfaces.
  - Operational level: aligning the business performance, metrics, and governance with the IT performance, metrics, and governance.
- SOA and EA integration can also be done using different approaches, such as:
  - Top-down approach: starting from the business architecture and deriving the service architecture from it.
  - Bottom-up approach: starting from the existing IT systems and identifying the services that can be exposed from them.
  - Middle-out approach: starting from the core business services and expanding them to cover the business processes and IT systems.
- SOA and EA integration can provide several benefits, such as:
  - Improved business agility and responsiveness to changing market conditions and customer demands.
