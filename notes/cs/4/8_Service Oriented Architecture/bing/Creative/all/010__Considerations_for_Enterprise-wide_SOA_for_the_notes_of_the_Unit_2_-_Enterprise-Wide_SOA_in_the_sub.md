### Considerations for Enterprise-wide SOA for the notes of the Unit 2 - Enterprise-Wide SOA in the subject of Service Oriented Architecture

- Enterprise-wide SOA is an IT strategy that organizes the discrete functions contained in enterprise applications into interoperable, standards-based services that can be combined and reused quickly to meet business needs.
- Enterprise-wide SOA requires a clear vision, a realistic plan, and a consistent execution to achieve the desired benefits of greater business agility, faster time to market, and lower costs.
- Enterprise-wide SOA involves six domains that need to be considered and aligned: business, organization, governance, methodology, infrastructure, and information.
- Business domain: This domain defines the business goals, processes, and services that drive the SOA initiative. It also identifies the key performance indicators (KPIs) and metrics that measure the business value and outcomes of SOA.
- Organization domain: This domain defines the roles, responsibilities, skills, and culture of the people involved in the SOA initiative. It also establishes the communication, collaboration, and change management mechanisms that enable the SOA adoption and transformation.
- Governance domain: This domain defines the policies, standards, and best practices that guide the design, development, deployment, and management of SOA services and processes. It also defines the decision-making authority, accountability, and compliance mechanisms that ensure the quality and consistency of SOA.
- Methodology domain: This domain defines the processes, methods, and tools that support the SOA lifecycle, from service identification and modeling, to service implementation and testing, to service deployment and monitoring. It also defines the iterative and incremental approach that enables the SOA evolution and maturity.
- Infrastructure domain: This domain defines the technical architecture, platform, and components that enable the SOA service delivery and integration. It also defines the security, reliability, scalability, and performance requirements and capabilities of SOA.
- Information domain: This domain defines the data and information assets that are exposed and consumed by SOA services and processes. It also defines the data quality, consistency, and governance aspects of SOA.
- Enterprise-wide SOA requires a roadmap that captures the current state, the desired state, and the transition plan for each of the six domains. The roadmap should be a living document that is updated and refined as the SOA initiative progresses and matures.
- Enterprise-wide SOA requires a maturity model that assesses the current and desired levels of SOA sophistication and capability for each of the six domains. The maturity model should be based on objective and measurable criteria and should provide guidance and feedback for the SOA improvement and optimization.
- Enterprise-wide SOA requires a self-assessment tool that helps the organization to evaluate its readiness and suitability for SOA adoption and transformation. The self-assessment tool should provide a baseline and a gap analysis for the SOA roadmap and maturity model.

Some possible mnemonics and learning tricks for the considerations for enterprise-wide SOA are:

- To remember the six domains, use the acronym BOGMII: Business, Organization, Governance, Methodology, Infrastructure, Information.
- To remember the three components of the SOA roadmap, use the acronym CPT: Current state, Plan, Transition.
- To remember the four phases of the SOA lifecycle, use the acronym IMTD: Identification, Modeling, Testing, Deployment.
