
## Unit 8 - Business Case for SOA

1. Service-Oriented Architecture (SOA) is an architectural style that allows organizations to develop applications that are built from loosely coupled, interoperable services.

2. SOA enables organizations to create a more agile and flexible IT infrastructure that is better suited for rapidly changing business requirements. 

3. SOA offers a number of advantages over traditional monolithic architectures, such as increased scalability, improved performance, and reduced cost.

4. SOA also allows organizations to create an environment where services can be reused and integrated to create new applications quickly and efficiently.

5. SOA is not without its challenges. Organizations must have a clear understanding of the business goals and objectives before implementing SOA in order to ensure that the architecture is properly designed and implemented.

6. Security is also a key concern when implementing SOA. Organizations must ensure that all the services are properly secured and that the data is protected from unauthorized access.

7. Finally, organizations must ensure that their SOA implementation is properly managed and monitored to ensure that it is running optimally and that any changes or upgrades are properly managed.
