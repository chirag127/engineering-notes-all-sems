### Composite Applications for the notes of the Unit 3 - Service-Oriented Applications in the subject of Service Oriented Architecture

Composite applications are applications that are built by combining smaller, independent services into a larger, integrated solution. In a service-oriented architecture (SOA), composite applications are constructed by combining services that are published and managed by a service-oriented infrastructure (SOI).

Composite applications are designed to be flexible and reusable, and can be composed and recomposed as needed to meet changing business requirements. The modular nature of composite applications enables organizations to build and deploy new applications quickly and cost-effectively, and to make changes to existing applications without disrupting the overall system.

Advantages of composite applications include:

1. Reusability: Composite applications are built from reusable services, which can be combined and recombined to meet changing business requirements.

2. Flexibility: Composite applications can be easily reconfigured and recomposed to meet changing business requirements.

3. Improved time-to-market: Composite applications can be built and deployed quickly, which can improve time-to-market for new applications and changes to existing applications.

4. Improved cost-effectiveness: Composite applications can be more cost-effective to build and maintain than monolithic applications, as they are built from reusable services.

Disadvantages of composite applications include:

1. Increased complexity: Composite applications can be more complex than monolithic applications, as they are built from multiple services that must be integrated and managed.

2. Dependency management: Composite applications may depend on multiple services, which can increase the risk of service failure and the complexity of managing dependencies.

3. Integration challenges: Integrating services into a composite application can be challenging, and may require specialized skills and knowledge.

In conclusion, composite applications are applications that are built by combining smaller, independent services into a larger, integrated solution. They offer advantages such as reusability, flexibility, improved time-to-market, and improved cost-effectiveness. However, they can also be more complex than monolithic applications, and may require specialized skills and knowledge to integrate services into a composite application.
