### Strawman Architecture for Enterprise-wide SOA for the notes of the Unit 2 - Enterprise-Wide SOA in the subject of Service Oriented Architecture

Strawman Architecture is a term used in Service Oriented Architecture (SOA) to describe a high-level, preliminary design of an enterprise-wide SOA solution. It is a starting point for further discussions and is used to provide a common understanding of the proposed solution. A strawman architecture typically includes a description of the services and components that will be used, the relationships between them, and the interactions between the services and external systems.

The purpose of a strawman architecture is to provide a basis for further discussions and to identify any potential issues or limitations with the proposed solution. It is not intended to be a complete or final design, but rather a starting point for further refinement.

In an enterprise-wide SOA, the strawman architecture is typically created by a team of architects and subject matter experts who have a deep understanding of the business requirements and technology constraints. The strawman architecture is then reviewed and refined by stakeholders, including business and IT leaders, to ensure that it meets the needs of the enterprise.

In summary, Strawman Architecture is a preliminary design of an enterprise-wide SOA solution that is used to provide a common understanding of the proposed solution and to identify any potential issues or limitations. It is a starting point for further discussions and refinement.
