### Underlying Principles of Parallel and Distributed Computing for the notes of the Unit 1 - Introduction To Cloud Computing in the subject of Cloud Computing

Parallel and Distributed Computing are two fundamental concepts in Cloud Computing. Parallel Computing refers to the execution of multiple tasks simultaneously by dividing them into smaller subtasks and running them on multiple processors. Distributed Computing, on the other hand, refers to the use of multiple machines connected on a network to work together towards a common goal.

Here are some underlying principles of Parallel and Distributed Computing that are crucial to understand in the context of Cloud Computing:

1. **Scalability**: Scalability is the ability of a system to handle increasing amounts of work. Both Parallel and Distributed Computing are designed with scalability in mind. Parallel Computing achieves scalability by dividing tasks into smaller subtasks and running them on multiple processors. Distributed Computing achieves scalability by distributing tasks across multiple machines in a network.

2. **Fault Tolerance**: Fault Tolerance is the ability of a system to continue functioning even in the event of failures. In Distributed Computing, machines can fail, or network connections can be lost. To ensure fault tolerance, Distributed Computing systems use redundancy and replication to ensure that even if one machine or network connection fails, the system can continue to function.

3. **Load Balancing**: Load Balancing is the process of distributing workloads evenly across multiple processors or machines in a network. Load balancing ensures that no single processor or machine is overloaded while others are idle, leading to optimal utilization of resources.

4. **Synchronization**: Synchronization refers to the coordination of activities between different processors or machines in a network. In Parallel Computing, synchronization is required to ensure that subtasks are completed in the correct order and that the results are combined correctly. In Distributed Computing, synchronization is required to ensure that different machines are working towards a common goal and that their results are combined correctly.

5. **Communication**: Communication is the exchange of information between different processors or machines in a network. In Distributed Computing, communication is essential for coordination and collaboration between different machines. Communication can take place through various mechanisms such as message passing, remote procedure calls, and shared memory.

Mnemonics and learning tricks can be helpful in remembering these principles:

- **S**calability - Think of the letter 'S' standing for 'Size' or 'Scaling'.
- **F**ault Tolerance - Think of the letter 'F' standing for 'Failures' or 'Faults'.
- **L**oad Balancing - Think of the letter 'L' standing for 'Level' or 'Load'.
- **S**ynchronization - Think of the letter 'S' standing for 'Sequence' or 'Sync'.
- **C**ommunication - Think of the letter 'C' standing for 'Collaboration' or 'Connections'. 

Remembering these mnemonics can help you recall the underlying principles of Parallel and Distributed Computing more easily.
