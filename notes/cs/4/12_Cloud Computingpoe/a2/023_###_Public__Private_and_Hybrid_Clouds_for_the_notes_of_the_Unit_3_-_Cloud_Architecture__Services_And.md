### Public, Private and Hybrid Clouds

Public Cloud:
- Provided by a third-party cloud service provider.
- Resources are shared between multiple clients.
- Cheaper and highly scalable.
- Limited control over the infrastructure.
- Examples: AWS, Azure, Google Cloud.

Private Cloud:
- Operated solely for an organization.
- Resources are dedicated to the organization.
- Higher security and control.
- Requires investment in resources and management.
- Examples: On-premises data centers of organizations.

Hybrid Cloud:
- Combines public and private clouds.
- Sensitive data and applications are kept private.
- Remaining needs are met through the public cloud.
- Provides more flexibility and cost optimization.
- Examples: Using a private cloud for critical operations and public cloud for scalability.

Mnemonics:
- Public: Share with Public, Cheap but Less Control
- Private: Own and Dedicated but Expensive
- Hybrid: Combine Best of Public and Private

Advantages:
- Public: Cost-effective, Scalable
- Private: Secure, Control
- Hybrid: Flexible, Optimized

Disadvantages:
- Public: Less Control, Security Concerns
- Private: Expensive, Limited Scalability
- Hybrid: Complex to Manage

Applications:
- Public: Websites, Mobile Apps, Generic Servers
- Private: Critical Data and Operations
- Hybrid: Flexible Workloads
