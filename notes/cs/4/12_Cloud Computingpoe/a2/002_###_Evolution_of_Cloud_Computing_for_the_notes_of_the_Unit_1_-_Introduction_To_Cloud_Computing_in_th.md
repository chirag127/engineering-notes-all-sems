### Evolution of Cloud Computing

The evolution of cloud computing can be traced through following key phases:

1.  **Grid and utility computing**: The concept of utility computing dates back to 1960s. The idea was simple, computing resources would be available on-demand, just like other utility services such as electricity, water, etc. However, the technology wasn't ready for this vision back then. The concept re-emerged in the late 90s and early 2000s under the names 'grid computing' and 'utility computing'. The key offerings were raw computing power and storage on-demand.

2.  **Infrastructure as a Service (IaaS)**: The next phase in evolution was the introduction of virtualization technology which allowed servers and storage to be virtualized and offered as a service (IaaS) over the internet. This model gained popularity with the launch of Amazon Elastic Compute Cloud (EC2) in 2006 which offered virtual machines on-demand. The key benefits included scalability, cost-efficiency and reduced time to deploy infrastructure.

3.  **Platform as a Service (PaaS)**: The PaaS model offered a development platform in the cloud, handling infrastructure and OS requirements. This enabled developers to focus on their core product without worrying about the underlying infrastructure. Offerings such as Google AppEngine and Microsoft Azure emerged during this phase which supported various software stacks and programming languages.

4.  **Software as a Service (SaaS)**: The SaaS model offered complete business applications as a service over the internet. This phase made cloud computing more accessible to the masses, with offerings such as Google Docs, Salesforce CRM, etc. The key benefits of SaaS were zero installation and maintenance efforts, automatic updates and multi-tenancy.

The future of cloud computing promises continued advancement in cloud offerings as well as deeper integration with emerging technologies such as artificial intelligence, blockchain, serverless computing, etc. Cloud computing has truly reshaped the IT industry and continues to do so at a rapid pace.
