### Elasticity in Cloud

- Elasticity in cloud refers to the ability of a cloud infrastructure to dynamically provision and de-provision resources based on demand.
- Resources can be scaled up or down as per the requirements. This allows optimum utilization of resources and cost efficiency.
- For example, additional servers can be launched or terminated based on the traffic on a website. During peak hours, more servers can be launched to handle the traffic and these extra servers can be terminated during non-peak hours to save cost.
- This scalability and flexibility is enabled through virtualization and automation technologies in cloud. Resources can be allocated or de-allocated on-demand without any manual interference.
- The three aspects of elasticity in cloud are:
    - Scale out: Adding more resources such as servers, storage, etc. to handle increasing load.
    - Scale up: Increasing the resources of an existing system such as increasing RAM, CPU, etc. to handle increasing load.
    - Scale in: Reducing the resources by terminating underutilized servers or decreasing resources to save cost when the load decreases.
- The elastic nature of cloud makes it suitable for dynamic workload requirements and unpredictable traffic patterns. It leads to optimum resource utilization and cost efficiency which are key benefits of cloud computing.

- Some mnemonics or learning tricks for elasticity in cloud:
    - Think of a rubber band - it can stretch and shrink easily similar to scaling out and in of resources in cloud.
    - Imagine servers as balloons - you can inflate (scale up) or deflate (scale in) balloons as per requirement similar to scaling resources up or down.

- Examples of elasticity in cloud:
    - Auto-scaling groups in AWS to increase or decrease the number of EC2 instances based on load.
    - Scaling memory and CPU of Azure virtual machines up or down based on workload.
    - Google Cloud auto-scaling machine types and disks based on resource utilization.

- Advantages:
    - Optimum resource utilization and cost efficiency.
    - Handling dynamic workloads and unpredictable traffic patterns.
- Disadvantages:
    - Complex to implement and manage.
    - Over-provisioning or under-provisioning of resources can lead to inefficiencies or service issues.
