### PaaS

- PaaS stands for Platform as a Service. It provides a computing platform and a solution stack as a service.
- It allows customers to develop, run and manage applications without the complexity of building and maintaining the infrastructure typically associated with developing and launching an app.
- With PaaS, the consumer controls software deployment and configuration settings for the app, while the provider controls the underlying infrastructure including network, servers, operating system, storage, and individual application capacities.
- Examples of PaaS providers include Heroku, Microsoft Azure, Google App Engine, etc.
- Some key benefits of PaaS are:

• Reduced operational costs as infrastructure maintenance is handled by the provider.
• Increased speed of app development and deployment.
• Easy scalability as resources can be scaled up or down as required.
• Multi-tenancy and shared resources leading to cost efficiency.

- Some disadvantages of PaaS are:

• Vendor lock-in as it is difficult to migrate apps between platforms.
• Limited control over the underlying infrastructure.
• Reliance on the provider for security, updates, and availability.

- Useful mnemonics:

• Paas - Platform as a Service
• Provides platform and solution stack
• Focus on app development and deployment
• Provider handles infrastructure
• Examples - Heroku, Azure, GAE

- Learning tips:

• Understand the key characteristics of PaaS like managed infrastructure and focus on app development.
• Learn the pros and cons of PaaS and how it differs from IaaS and SaaS.
• Get familiar with popular PaaS providers and their offerings.
• Practice deploying sample apps on free tiers of PaaS providers to understand the concept better.
