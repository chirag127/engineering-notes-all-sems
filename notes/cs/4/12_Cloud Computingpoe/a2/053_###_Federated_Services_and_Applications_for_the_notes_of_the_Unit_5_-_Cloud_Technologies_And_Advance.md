### Federated Services and Applications

Federated services and applications refer to services and applications that span across multiple cloud providers and data centers. Some key examples are:

- **Hybrid clouds:** Combination of private and public clouds to share resources that can be managed and scaled together. Provides more flexibility and control.
- **Multi-cloud:** Use of multiple public cloud providers to avoid vendor lock-in, increase redundancy, and leverage price differences. Requires abstraction layers to manage the different APIs.
- **Inter-cloud:** Sharing of resources across different public cloud providers. Still an open research challenge due to technical and business challenges.
- **Distributed applications:** Applications that span multiple clouds and data centers for redundancy, reduced latency, and scaling. Require mechanisms to abstract from the underlying infrastructure.

Advantages:

- Avoid vendor lock-in
- Increased redundancy and fault-tolerance
- Scalability across multiple clouds
- Price optimization by choosing cheaper resources

Disadvantages:

- Technical challenges for unified management and monitoring
- Need for standards and APIs across providers
- Additional overhead for migration and synchronization
- Security and compliance challenges
- Difficult business relationships between competitors

Overall, federated services and applications have the potential to unleash the full power of the cloud by overcoming single-provider limitations. However, there are still many open research and business challenges to solve before they can become mainstream.
