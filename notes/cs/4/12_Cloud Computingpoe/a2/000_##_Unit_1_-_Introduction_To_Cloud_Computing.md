## Unit 1 - Introduction To Cloud Computing

Cloud computing refers to the delivery of computing services over the Internet. Instead of companies hosting software, data, and computing resources on their own in-house servers, all of that is hosted by a cloud provider in their data centers.

- **Key characteristics of cloud computing**:
  - On-demand self-service: Computing resources can be provisioned as needed automatically without requiring human interaction.
  - Broad network access: Services are available over the network and accessed through standard mechanisms.
  - Resource pooling: Resources are pooled to serve multiple consumers using a multi-tenant model.
  - Rapid elasticity: Resources can be elastically provisioned and released to scale rapidly.
  - Measured service: Resource usage can be monitored, controlled, and reported providing transparency for both the provider and consumer.
- **Advantages of cloud computing**:
  - Reduced costs: No heavy initial capital investment in hardware and software. Pay-as-you-go model.
  - Increased speed and agility: Fast and easy deployment of resources leading to increased innovation.
  - Reliability: Cloud providers have redundant resources and data backup plans resulting in greater reliability.
  - Automatic scaling: Resources can be scaled up or down as required automatically.
  - Accessibility: Access to resources anytime from anywhere over the Internet.
- **Disadvantages of cloud computing**:
  - Vendor lock-in: Difficulty in migrating data and applications from one cloud provider to another.
  - Technical issues: Potential for downtime and technical issues beyond your control.
  - Compliance and regulatory concerns: Regulations may prevent storage of certain types of data in the cloud.
  - Performance concerns: Relying on the Internet can lead to latency and other performance issues.
- **Popular cloud providers**: Amazon Web Services (AWS), Microsoft Azure, Google Cloud Platform (GCP)
