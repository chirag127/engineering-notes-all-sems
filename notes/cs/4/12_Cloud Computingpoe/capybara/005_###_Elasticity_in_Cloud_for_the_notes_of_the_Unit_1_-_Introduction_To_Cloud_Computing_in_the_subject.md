### Elasticity in Cloud

Elasticity is one of the key features of cloud computing. It refers to the ability of a cloud system to automatically and dynamically allocate or de-allocate resources based on the changing demands of the workload. 

#### Advantages of Elasticity

- Cost-Effective: Elasticity allows you to pay only for the resources that you need, when you need them. This helps to reduce costs and optimize resource utilization.

- Improved Performance: With elasticity, you can scale up or down your resources quickly and easily, to meet the demands of your workload. This helps to improve the performance and availability of your applications.

- High Availability: Elasticity helps to ensure that your applications are always available, even during peak usage periods. It allows you to automatically add resources in response to increased demand, and remove them when the demand decreases.

- Flexibility: Elasticity provides a flexible environment that can adapt to the changing needs of your business. It allows you to quickly and easily scale resources up or down, without the need for manual intervention.

#### Disadvantages of Elasticity

- Complexity: Elasticity can be complex to implement and manage, especially for large-scale systems. This requires expertise in cloud technologies and architectures.

- Cost Overruns: Elasticity can also lead to cost overruns if not managed properly. If resources are not released when they are no longer needed, it can lead to unnecessary costs.

#### Examples of Elasticity in Cloud

- Auto Scaling: Amazon Web Services (AWS) provides an Auto Scaling service that automatically adjusts the number of instances in a group based on the changing demands of the workload.

- Load Balancing: Load balancing is another example of elasticity, where incoming traffic is distributed across multiple instances to ensure high availability and performance.

#### Mnemonic

A simple mnemonic to remember elasticity in cloud computing is "Elasticity is like a rubber band - it expands and contracts based on the workload demand."
