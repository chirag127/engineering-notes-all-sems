## Unit 3 - Embedded Platforms for IoT

Embedded platforms for IoT refer to the hardware and software components that make up the foundation of Internet of Things (IoT) devices. These platforms are designed to be small, low-power, and cost-effective, while still providing the necessary processing power, connectivity, and storage to support IoT applications.

Embedded platforms for IoT typically include:

1. Microcontroller: A small, low-power computer that serves as the central processing unit (CPU) for the IoT device.

2. Sensors and actuators: Devices that measure physical parameters, such as temperature, light, or motion, and respond to these measurements by controlling other devices, such as lights or motors.

3. Connectivity: Wireless or wired connectivity options, such as Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, Zigbee, or Ethernet, that allow the IoT device to communicate with other devices and the cloud.

4. Memory: On-board storage for data and software, such as flash memory or a microSD card.

5. Power management: Components that regulate the power consumption of the device, such as voltage regulators, battery management circuits, and power-saving modes.

Embedded platforms for IoT play a critical role in enabling IoT devices to function as intended. By providing the necessary processing power, connectivity, and storage, they allow IoT devices to collect and transmit data, respond to user inputs, and interact with other devices and the cloud.

In conclusion, embedded platforms for IoT refer to the hardware and software components that make up the foundation of IoT devices. These platforms typically include a microcontroller, sensors and actuators, connectivity options, memory, and power management components, and play a critical role in enabling IoT devices to function as intended.
