## Unit 1 - Internet of Things (IoT)

1. Internet of Things (IoT) refers to the interconnection of everyday physical objects and appliances via the internet. These devices are equipped with sensors, software, and network connectivity that allows them to send and receive data.
2. IoT devices can collect useful data and automate tasks in homes, businesses, and cities. For example, smart home devices can automate lights, thermostats, and other appliances. Industrial IoT devices can monitor assets and equipment for predictive maintenance. Smart city IoT can automate traffic systems and monitor utilities.
3. The key components of IoT are:
    - Sensors: Devices that detect and measure physical phenomena like temperature, motion, pressure, etc. and convert it into data.
    - Connectivity: Technologies like Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, cellular data, NB-IoT, LoRaWAN, etc. that provide internet connectivity to devices.
    - Cloud: A network of servers that store and process data from IoT devices and handle automation and analytics.
    - Software: Apps and platforms that access device data, analyze information, and control devices.
4. IoT offers significant opportunities to improve efficiency, gain business insights, and enhance user experiences. However, it also introduces risks around security, privacy, and over-reliance on technology that must be addressed. IoT systems must have strong security controls and data privacy protections in place to avoid vulnerabilities.
