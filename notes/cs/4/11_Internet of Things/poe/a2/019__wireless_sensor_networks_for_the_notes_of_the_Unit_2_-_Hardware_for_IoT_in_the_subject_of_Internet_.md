### Wireless Sensor Networks

- A wireless sensor network (WSN) consists of spatially distributed autonomous sensors to monitor physical or environmental conditions, such as temperature, sound, vibration, pressure, motion or pollutants.
- The sensors collectively pass their data through the network to a main location or sink where the data can be observed and analysed.
- WSNs are used in many industrial and consumer applications, such as industrial process monitoring and control, machine health monitoring, and so on.
- The key components of a wireless sensor network are:
 - Sensors: Converts physical phenomena to electrical signals
 - Microcontroller: Coordinates the functions of the sensor node
 - Transceiver: Sends and receives radio frequency signals
 - Power supply: Batteries or an energy harvesting system
 - Memory: Stores data, programs, etc.
- The advantages of WSNs are:
 - Easy deployment since no cables are required
 - Low cost and small size
 - Ability to access harsh and remote environments
 - Adaptability to different applications
 - Self-organization and fault tolerance
- The limitations and challenges of WSNs are:
 - Limited power supply leading to short network lifetimes
 - Limited bandwidth and processing capabilities
 - Vulnerability to physical damage, interference and eavesdropping
 - Scalability to large numbers of nodes
 - Reliability and real-time constraints
