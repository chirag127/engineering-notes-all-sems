### E-health

E-health refers to the use of electronic means and technologies to deliver health-related services and information. It is a broad term that encompasses a wide range of applications and services, including:

1. **Telemedicine:** The use of telecommunication and information technologies to provide clinical health care from a distance. This can include remote consultations, remote monitoring, and remote diagnosis.

2. **Electronic Health Records (EHRs):** The use of electronic systems to store, manage, and exchange health information. EHRs can improve the quality of care by providing clinicians with timely and accurate information about their patients.

3. **mHealth:** The use of mobile devices and wireless technologies to deliver health-related services and information. This can include text messaging, mobile apps, and wearable devices.

4. **Health Information Systems:** The use of information and communication technologies to manage and exchange health information. This can include electronic prescribing, laboratory information systems, and disease surveillance systems.

5. **Telehealth:** The use of telecommunication technologies to deliver health-related services and information, including non-clinical services such as health education and health promotion.

E-health has the potential to improve the accessibility, quality, and efficiency of healthcare, particularly in remote and underserved areas. However, there are also challenges associated with the implementation of e-health, including issues related to privacy, security, and the digital divide.
