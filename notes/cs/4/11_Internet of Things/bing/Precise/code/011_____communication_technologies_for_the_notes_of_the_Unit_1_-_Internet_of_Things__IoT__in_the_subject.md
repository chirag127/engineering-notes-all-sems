### Communication Technologies for the Notes of the Unit 1 - Internet of Things (IoT) in the Subject of Internet of Things

1. **Wireless Sensor Networks (WSN):** A wireless sensor network is a group of specialized transducers with a communications infrastructure for monitoring and recording conditions at diverse locations. WSNs measure environmental conditions like temperature, sound, pollution levels, humidity, wind, and so on.

2. **Radio-Frequency Identification (RFID):** RFID is a wireless communication technology that uses radio waves to identify and track objects. RFID systems consist of a reader, antenna, and a tag or transponder attached to the object to be tracked.

3. **Near Field Communication (NFC):** NFC is a short-range wireless connectivity technology that enables communication between devices when they are brought close together, typically within a few centimeters. NFC is used for contactless payment systems, data exchange, and simplified setup of more complex communications such as Wi-Fi.

4. **Zigbee:** Zigbee is a wireless technology developed as an open global standard to address the unique needs of low-cost, low-power wireless IoT networks. Zigbee operates in the 2.4 GHz frequency band and is used for applications such as home automation, industrial control, and medical data collection.

5. **Bluetooth Low Energy (BLE):** BLE is a wireless personal area network technology designed for applications in the healthcare, fitness, security, and home entertainment industries. BLE is distinguished by its low power consumption, which makes it suitable for battery-operated devices.

6. **Z-Wave:** Z-Wave is a wireless communications protocol used primarily for home automation. It is a mesh network using low-energy radio waves to communicate from appliance to appliance, allowing for wireless control of residential appliances and other devices, such as lighting control, security systems, thermostats, windows, locks, swimming pools, and garage door openers.

7. **Cellular:** Cellular communication is a form of wireless communication that is most widely used with mobile phones. It operates on the basis of cells, where each cell is assigned a frequency or a set of frequencies to use within its boundaries. Cellular networks offer a number of advantages over other alternatives, including increased capacity, reduced power usage, and a large coverage area.

8. **Wi-Fi:** Wi-Fi is a wireless networking technology that uses radio waves to provide wireless high-speed Internet and network connections. Wi-Fi is commonly used in homes, offices, airports, and other public places to provide Internet access to laptops, smartphones, and other Wi-Fi enabled devices.

9. **Long Range Wide Area Network (LoRaWAN):** LoRaWAN is a protocol designed for wireless battery-operated devices in a regional, national, or global network. It targets key requirements of the Internet of Things, such as secure bi-directional communication, mobility, and localization services.

10. **Thread:** Thread is an IPv6-based, low-power, secure, and scalable mesh networking technology for IoT products. It is designed to provide reliable, low-power wireless communication for connected devices in the home. Thread networks are simple to set up and secure to use, and provide a robust and reliable network for connected devices to communicate with each other and with the cloud.
