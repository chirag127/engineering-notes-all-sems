### Beagle Bone

Beagle Bone is a series of low-cost, open-source, single-board Linux computers that can be used for embedded applications such as IoT. Some of the features and benefits of Beagle Bone are:

- It has a small size (about the size of a credit card) and a low power consumption (about 2 W).
- It has a high-performance processor (ARM Cortex-A8) that can run up to 1 GHz and support various operating systems such as Android, Ubuntu, Debian, etc.
- It has plenty of I/O interfaces and expansion capabilities, such as USB, Ethernet, HDMI, SPI, I2C, UART, ADC, PWM, etc. It can also be connected to various plug-in boards called capes that add more functionality and sensors.
- It has a large and active community of developers and users who provide support, documentation, tutorials, and projects.

Some of the examples of Beagle Bone products are:

- BeagleBone Black: The most popular and widely used Beagle Bone product. It has 512 MB of RAM, 4 GB of eMMC flash storage, and a 3D graphics accelerator. It costs about $60.
- BeagleBone Green: A variant of BeagleBone Black that replaces the HDMI port with two Grove connectors for easy sensor integration. It costs about $70.
- BeagleBone AI: A Beagle Bone product that is designed for embedded machine learning applications. It has a dual-core ARM Cortex-A15 processor, 1 GB of RAM, 16 GB of eMMC flash storage, and a neural processing unit that can perform up to 9 TOPS (trillion operations per second). It costs about $100.
- PocketBeagle: A tiny and low-cost Beagle Bone product that has a 1 GHz processor, 512 MB of RAM, and 8 MB of SPI flash storage. It costs about $25.
