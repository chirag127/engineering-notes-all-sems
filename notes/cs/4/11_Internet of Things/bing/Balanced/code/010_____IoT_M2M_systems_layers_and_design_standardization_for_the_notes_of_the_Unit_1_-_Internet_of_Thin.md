# IoT/M2M systems layers and design standardization

- IoT/M2M systems are composed of devices, networks, platforms, and applications that enable data exchange and service delivery among physical objects and other entities over the internet.
- IoT/M2M systems can be divided into three major domains: the application layer, the service layer, and the network layer. Each layer has different functions, protocols, and standards.
- The application layer is the topmost layer that provides the user interface and the business logic for IoT/M2M applications. It can be implemented using various programming languages, frameworks, and platforms. Some examples of application layer protocols are MQTT, CoAP, HTTP, and WebSocket.
- The service layer is the middle layer that provides common functionalities and services for IoT/M2M applications, such as device management, data management, security, discovery, and orchestration. It can be implemented using various architectures, such as cloud, fog, edge, or peer-to-peer. Some examples of service layer standards are oneM2M, OGC SensorThings API, and LwM2M.
- The network layer is the bottom layer that provides the connectivity and transport for IoT/M2M devices and applications. It can be implemented using various technologies, such as cellular, Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, Zigbee, LoRa, or NB-IoT. Some examples of network layer standards are 3GPP, IEEE 802.15.4, and IPv6.
- IoT/M2M systems layers and design standardization aim to promote interoperability, scalability, security, and efficiency among different IoT/M2M systems and devices. Some of the benefits of standardization are reducing complexity, enabling innovation, facilitating integration, and lowering costs. Some of the challenges of standardization are addressing diversity, ensuring compatibility, balancing trade-offs, and fostering adoption.
