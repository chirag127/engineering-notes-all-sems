### Radio Frequency Identification (RFID) Technology

- Radio Frequency Identification (RFID) technology uses radio waves to identify people or objects without making any physical contact or requiring a line of sight .
- An RFID system consists of a tag, a reader and a database .
  - A tag is a wireless device that contains an integrated circuit (IC) and an antenna. The tag can store information such as a unique identifier or other data. The tag can be passive (no battery) or active (battery-powered)  .
  - A reader is a device that has one or more antennas that emit radio waves and receive signals from the tags. The reader can be fixed or mobile, and can communicate with the database via a wired or wireless connection  .
  - A database is a system that stores the information associated with the tags, such as the identity, location, status or history of the tagged objects .
- RFID technology can be used for various applications, such as:
  - Tracking items along a supply chain, from manufacturing to distribution to retail  .
  - Managing inventory and assets, such as books, tools, vehicles or medical equipment  .
  - Enhancing security and access control, such as passports, ID cards, tickets or keys  .
  - Improving customer service and loyalty, such as payment systems, loyalty cards or smart shelves  .
  - Supporting health and safety, such as patient identification, medication verification or food safety  .
- RFID technology has some advantages and disadvantages, such as:
  - Advantages: fast, accurate, reliable, automated, scalable, flexible, low-cost, long-range, high-capacity, reusable  .
  - Disadvantages: interference, collision, security, privacy, standardization, regulation, environmental, ethical  .
