### Tablets for the notes of the Unit 7 - IoT Applications

- IoT applications are the use cases of IoT devices and technologies that provide value to end-users, businesses, or society.
- IoT devices are any physical objects that can connect to the internet and communicate data, such as sensors, actuators, cameras, wearables, smart appliances, etc.
- IoT technologies are the hardware, software, protocols, and platforms that enable the connectivity, data processing, and analytics of IoT devices and applications.
- Tablets are portable computing devices that have a touchscreen interface and can run various applications, such as web browsers, games, productivity tools, etc.
- Tablets can be used for IoT applications in various ways, such as:

  - Controlling and monitoring IoT devices remotely, such as smart home systems, security cameras, thermostats, etc. For example, Josh.ai is a voice-controlled home automation system that can connect to various smart devices, including tablets.
  - Collecting and displaying data from IoT sensors, such as temperature, humidity, air quality, etc. For example, SensorPush is a wireless thermometer and hygrometer that can send data to a tablet app via Bluetooth or WiFi.
  - Providing touchless transactions and interactions, such as contactless payment, digital signatures, QR codes, etc. For example, Square is a payment system that can turn a tablet into a point-of-sale device that accepts credit cards, NFC, and mobile payments.
  - Enhancing the user experience and engagement, such as augmented reality, virtual reality, gamification, etc. For example, IKEA Place is an app that lets users virtually place furniture in their homes using a tablet camera and AR technology.
  - Supporting healthcare and wellness, such as telemedicine, remote diagnosis, medication management, etc. For example, Medisafe is an app that helps users track and manage their medications, reminders, and adherence using a tablet.

- Tablets for IoT applications have some advantages and disadvantages, such as:

  - Advantages: 
    - Portability: Tablets are easy to carry and use anywhere, unlike desktops or laptops.
    - Versatility: Tablets can run various apps and perform multiple functions, unlike specialized devices.
    - User-friendliness: Tablets have a simple and intuitive touchscreen interface, unlike keyboards or mice.
    - Connectivity: Tablets can connect to the internet and other devices via WiFi, Bluetooth, NFC, etc., unlike offline devices.
  - Disadvantages:
    - Battery life: Tablets have a limited battery capacity and need to be recharged frequently, unlike wired devices.
    - Security: Tablets are vulnerable to cyberattacks and data breaches, unlike offline devices.
    - Durability: Tablets are fragile and prone to damage from drops, spills, scratches, etc., unlike rugged devices.
    - Performance: Tablets have lower processing power and memory than desktops or laptops, unlike high-end devices.
