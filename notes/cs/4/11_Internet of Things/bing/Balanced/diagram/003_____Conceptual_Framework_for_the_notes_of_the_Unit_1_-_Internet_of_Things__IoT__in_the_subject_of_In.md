### Conceptual Framework for the notes of the Unit 1 - Internet of Things (IoT) in the subject of Internet of Things

- A conceptual framework is a set of ideas, principles, and assumptions that guide the design and implementation of a system or a solution.
- A conceptual framework for IoT aims to provide a common understanding of the key concepts, components, and relationships of IoT systems and applications.
- A conceptual framework for IoT can help to address the challenges and opportunities of IoT, such as heterogeneity, scalability, interoperability, security, privacy, and value creation.
- A conceptual framework for IoT can also help to identify the gaps and needs for further research and development in the IoT domain.
- There are different conceptual frameworks for IoT proposed by various researchers and organizations, depending on the perspective, scope, and objectives of the framework.
- Some examples of conceptual frameworks for IoT are:

  - The four-layer conceptual framework for context-aware networks , which consists of the following layers:
    - Physical layer: This layer covers the heterogeneous physical entities, such as sensors, actuators, RFID tags, and smart objects, that are connected to the internet and can sense, actuate, and communicate.
    - Virtual layer: This layer covers the placement, integration, and synchronization of the physical entities with the management system, such as cloud computing, middleware, and databases, that can store, process, and analyze the data generated by the physical entities.
    - Context layer: This layer covers the context information, such as location, time, identity, preferences, and activities, that can be derived from the data and used to provide dynamic and personalized services to the users and applications.
    - Service layer: This layer covers the e-services, such as smart home, smart health, smart city, and smart industry, that can be delivered to the users and applications based on the context information and the user requirements.
  - The resource-based view conceptual framework for IoT impact on supply chain management, which consists of the following elements:
    - IoT resources: These are the tangible and intangible assets, such as devices, networks, platforms, data, and capabilities, that enable the IoT implementation and adoption in the supply chain.
    - Internal supply chain capabilities: These are the abilities, such as visibility, agility, collaboration, and innovation, that the supply chain can leverage from the IoT resources to improve its performance and efficiency.
    - External supply chain challenges: These are the factors, such as complexity, uncertainty, risk, and competition, that the supply chain faces in the dynamic and globalized market environment and that can be addressed by the IoT resources and capabilities.
    - Supply chain competitiveness: This is the outcome, such as cost, quality, service, and sustainability, that the supply chain can achieve by using the IoT resources and capabilities to overcome the external challenges and meet the customer demands.
  - The theoretical framework and conceptual model for IoT adoption and implementation, which consists of the following dimensions:
    - IoT adoption: This is the process, such as awareness, intention, decision, and use, that the individuals and organizations go through to adopt and use the IoT technologies and applications.
    - IoT implementation: This is the process, such as planning, design, development, deployment, and evaluation, that the individuals and organizations go through to implement and operate the IoT systems and solutions.
    - IoT antecedents: These are the factors, such as technology, organization, environment, and individual, that influence the IoT adoption and implementation processes and outcomes.
    - IoT outcomes: These are the results, such as benefits, challenges, and impacts, that the individuals and organizations obtain from the IoT adoption and implementation processes and outcomes.
