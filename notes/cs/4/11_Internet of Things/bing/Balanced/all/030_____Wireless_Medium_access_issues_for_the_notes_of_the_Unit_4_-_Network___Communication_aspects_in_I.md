# Wireless Medium Access Issues for the Notes of the Unit 4 - Network & Communication Aspects in IoT in the Subject of Internet of Things

- Wireless medium access is the process of coordinating the transmission and reception of data among multiple devices that share a common wireless channel.
- Wireless medium access is challenging in IoT because of the following issues:
  - **Interference**: IoT devices may operate in the same frequency band as other wireless technologies, such as WiFi, Bluetooth, ZigBee, etc. This may cause interference and degrade the performance of IoT devices. For example, IEEE 802.15.4-compliant IoT devices operate in the unlicensed 2.4 GHz band and are subject to interference caused by high-powered IEEE 802.11-compliant WiFi users.
  - **Energy efficiency**: IoT devices are often battery-powered and have limited energy resources. Therefore, wireless medium access should minimize the energy consumption of IoT devices and prolong their lifetime. For example, IoT devices may use duty cycling techniques to switch between active and sleep modes, or use wireless energy harvesting techniques to replenish their energy from the environment.
  - **Scalability**: IoT networks may consist of a large number of devices with diverse capabilities and requirements. Wireless medium access should be able to support the scalability of IoT networks and adapt to the dynamic changes in the network topology and traffic patterns. For example, IoT devices may use clustering techniques to form groups of devices with a common leader, or use content-centric networking techniques to deliver data based on the content name rather than the device address.
  - **Latency**: IoT applications may have strict latency requirements for the delivery of data. Wireless medium access should be able to reduce the delay and jitter of data transmission and ensure the reliability and quality of service of IoT applications. For example, IoT devices may use priority-based scheduling techniques to assign different priorities to different types of data, or use cognitive radio techniques to sense and exploit the spectrum opportunities in the wireless channel.
  - **Security**: IoT devices may be vulnerable to various security threats, such as eavesdropping, jamming, spoofing, etc. Wireless medium access should be able to provide security and privacy for the data transmission and the devices involved. For example, IoT devices may use encryption techniques to protect the data confidentiality and integrity, or use authentication techniques to verify the identity and legitimacy of the devices.
