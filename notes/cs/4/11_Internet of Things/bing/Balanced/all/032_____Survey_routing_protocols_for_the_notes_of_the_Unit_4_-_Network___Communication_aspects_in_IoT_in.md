# Survey routing protocols for the notes of the Unit 4 - Network & Communication aspects in IoT in the subject of Internet of Things

- Routing protocols are the rules that determine how data packets are forwarded from one node to another in a network.
- Routing protocols for Internet of Things (IoT) are designed to meet the specific requirements and challenges of IoT networks, such as low power consumption, scalability, mobility, heterogeneity, and security.
- Routing protocols for IoT can be classified into different categories based on various criteria, such as network topology, routing strategy, data flow direction, and security mechanism.
- Some of the common categories of routing protocols for IoT are:

  - Flat routing protocols: These protocols do not have any hierarchy or structure in the network. All nodes have the same role and responsibility. Examples of flat routing protocols are Ad hoc On-Demand Distance Vector (AODV), Dynamic Source Routing (DSR), and Optimized Link State Routing (OLSR).
  - Hierarchical routing protocols: These protocols organize the network into clusters or groups of nodes, where each cluster has a leader or a coordinator. The leader is responsible for collecting and aggregating data from the cluster members and forwarding it to the sink or the base station. Examples of hierarchical routing protocols are Low-Energy Adaptive Clustering Hierarchy (LEACH), Threshold-sensitive Energy Efficient sensor Network (TEEN), and Hybrid Energy-Efficient Distributed clustering (HEED).
  - Location-based routing protocols: These protocols use the geographic information of the nodes to make routing decisions. The nodes can obtain their location information from Global Positioning System (GPS) or other localization techniques. Examples of location-based routing protocols are Geographic Routing Protocol (GRP), Greedy Perimeter Stateless Routing (GPSR), and Distance Routing Effect Algorithm for Mobility (DREAM).
  - Data-centric routing protocols: These protocols use the content or the attributes of the data packets to route them to the appropriate nodes or destinations. The nodes can query the network for the data they are interested in, and the nodes that have the matching data can reply. Examples of data-centric routing protocols are Sensor Protocols for Information via Negotiation (SPIN), Directed Diffusion, and Rumor Routing.
  - Multipath routing protocols: These protocols establish multiple paths between the source and the destination nodes to increase the reliability, load balancing, and fault tolerance of the network. Examples of multipath routing protocols are Split Multipath Routing (SMR), Ad hoc On-demand Multipath Distance Vector (AOMDV), and Braided Multipath Routing (BMR).
  - QoS-aware routing protocols: These protocols consider the Quality of Service (QoS) requirements of the data packets, such as delay, bandwidth, energy, and reliability, and route them accordingly. Examples of QoS-aware routing protocols are Sequential Assignment Routing (SAR), Energy Aware Routing (EAR), and SPEED.
  - Security-aware routing protocols: These protocols provide security mechanisms to protect the data packets and the network from various attacks, such as eavesdropping, modification, replay, spoofing, and denial of service. Examples of security-aware routing protocols are Secure Efficient Ad hoc Distance vector routing protocol (SEAD), Secure Routing Protocol (SRP), and Secure Position Aided Ad hoc Routing (SPAAR).

- Routing protocols for IoT are still an active area of research and development, as new challenges and opportunities emerge with the advancement of IoT technologies and applications. Some of the open issues and future directions are:

  - Energy efficiency: Routing protocols for IoT should minimize the energy consumption of the nodes and prolong the network lifetime, as most of the IoT devices are battery-powered and have limited energy resources.
  - Scalability: Routing protocols for IoT should be able to handle the large number of nodes and data traffic in the network, as IoT networks are expected to grow exponentially in the future.
  - Mobility: Routing protocols for IoT should be able to cope with the dynamic topology and frequent link changes caused by the mobility of the nodes and the environment, as IoT networks are often deployed in mobile and unpredictable scenarios.
  - Heterogeneity: Routing protocols for IoT should be able to support the diversity of the nodes and the data in the network, as IoT networks are composed of different types of devices and applications with different capabilities and requirements.
  - Security: Routing protocols for IoT should provide security measures to ensure the confidentiality, integrity, and availability of the data and the network, as IoT networks are vulnerable to various attacks and threats.
