### Smart Cards for the Notes of the Unit 7 - IoT Applications in the Subject of Internet of Things

- A smart card is a physical device that contains a microprocessor or a microcontroller that can store and process data securely.
- A smart card can be used for various purposes, such as identification, authentication, payment, access control, data storage, and encryption.
- A smart card can communicate with other devices using contact or contactless interfaces, such as magnetic stripe, barcode, radio frequency, or near field communication.
- A smart card can be integrated with IoT devices to enhance their security, functionality, and user experience.
- Some of the applications of smart cards in IoT are:

  - Smart home devices: Smart cards can be used to authenticate and control smart devices, such as lights, thermostats, cameras, locks, and appliances, using a mobile app or a voice assistant. Smart cards can also store user preferences and settings for the smart devices.
  - Cellular failover connectivity: Smart cards can provide a backup connection for IoT devices when the primary network is unavailable or unreliable. Smart cards can switch to another available network, such as 4G LTE, using a SIM card or an embedded secure element.
  - Industrial Internet of Things: Smart cards can be used to authenticate and protect industrial IoT devices, such as sensors, actuators, machines, and robots, from unauthorized access and tampering. Smart cards can also store and encrypt data collected by the industrial IoT devices and enable secure communication with the cloud or other devices.
  - Trackers and wearable devices: Smart cards can be used to enable and monitor the location, activity, and health of trackers and wearable devices, such as smartwatches, fitness trackers, healthcare monitors, and so on. Smart cards can also store and encrypt personal and medical data of the users and enable secure communication with the cloud or other devices.
