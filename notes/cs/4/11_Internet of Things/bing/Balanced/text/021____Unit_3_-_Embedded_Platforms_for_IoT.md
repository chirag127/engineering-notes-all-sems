## Unit 3 - Embedded Platforms for IoT

- Embedded platforms for IoT are hardware and software systems that enable IoT devices to perform their functions and communicate with other devices and networks.
- Embedded platforms for IoT typically consist of:
  - An embedded device, such as a microcontroller or a microprocessor, that executes the application logic and controls the sensors and actuators.
  - An embedded operating system or firmware, such as Mbed OS or Amazon FreeRTOS, that provides the basic services and drivers for the device.
  - An embedded connectivity module, such as a Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, or cellular chip, that enables the device to connect to the internet or other networks.
  - An embedded security module, such as a secure element or a trusted platform module, that protects the device from cyber threats and ensures data integrity and privacy.
- Embedded platforms for IoT can vary in size, power, performance, and functionality depending on the requirements and constraints of the IoT application.
- Embedded platforms for IoT can be classified into three categories based on their role in the IoT system:
  - IoT gateways, which act as intermediaries between the edge devices and the cloud or other networks. They can provide data aggregation, processing, filtering, and routing functions, as well as security and management features. Examples of IoT gateways are AMD Ryzen™ Embedded processors and Cisco Embedded switches .
  - IoT edge devices, which are located at the edge of the network and perform sensing, actuation, and local processing functions. They can also provide some level of intelligence and decision making capabilities. Examples of IoT edge devices are Raspberry Pi and Arduino boards.
  - IoT end devices, which are the smallest and simplest devices that only perform sensing or actuation functions. They usually have limited processing and communication capabilities and rely on other devices for data transmission and management. Examples of IoT end devices are smart sensors and actuators.
