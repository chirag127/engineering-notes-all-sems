A MAC protocol survey for IoT is a study of the different medium access control (MAC) layer protocols that are used or proposed for Internet of Things (IoT) applications. The MAC layer protocols are responsible for coordinating the access of multiple devices to a shared wireless medium, such as radio frequency, and ensuring reliable and efficient data transmission. The MAC layer protocols can be classified into two groups based on the coverage range: short-range and long-range.

Short-range MAC protocols are designed for devices that communicate within a small area, such as a room or a building. They typically have high data rates, low latency, and low power consumption. Some examples of short-range MAC protocols are RFID, NFC, Bluetooth, Bluetooth Low Energy, IEEE 802.15.4, Wireless-HART, Z-Wave, Weightless, and IEEE 802.11 a/b/g/n/ah.

Long-range MAC protocols are designed for devices that communicate over large distances, such as a city or a country. They typically have low data rates, high latency, and high power consumption. Some examples of long-range MAC protocols are NB-IoT, LTE CAT-0, LTE CAT-M, LTE CAT-N, LoRa, and SigFox.
