The Internet of Things (IoT) is a network of physical devices that can transfer data to one another without human intervention. IoT devices can include anything with a sensor assigned a unique identifier (UID). The primary goal of the IoT is to create self-reporting devices that can communicate with each other (and users) in real time.

There are many examples of IoT devices across various industries and domains. Here are some of them:

- Connected cars: These are vehicles that can access the internet and communicate with other devices, such as smartphones, traffic lights, or parking meters. They can also receive over-the-air (OTA) updates and data management services from software providers, such as Airbiquity. Some examples of connected cars are Tesla, which offers connectivity features and an app to control the vehicle, and Zubie, which offers GPS and fleet tracking for rent and business vehicles.
- Smart appliances: These are household devices that can be controlled remotely or automatically, such as refrigerators, washing machines, or ovens. They can also monitor their own performance and energy consumption, and alert users of any issues or maintenance needs. Some examples of smart appliances are LG Electronics' ThinQ appliances, which can be connected to a smartphone app and voice assistants, and Samsung's Family Hub refrigerator, which has a touchscreen and cameras to display the contents of the fridge.
- Connected security systems: These are systems that can monitor and protect homes or businesses from intruders, fire, or other hazards. They can also send alerts and notifications to users or authorities, and allow remote access and control. Some examples of connected security systems are Ring, which offers video doorbells and cameras, and SimpliSafe, which offers wireless sensors and alarms.
- Smart agriculture equipment: These are devices that can help farmers improve crop yield and quality, reduce water and fertilizer use, and detect pests and diseases. They can also collect and analyze data from soil, weather, and plants, and provide recommendations and insights. Some examples of smart agriculture equipment are John Deere's precision agriculture solutions, which use GPS and sensors to optimize planting and harvesting, and CropX's soil sensors, which measure moisture and temperature and adjust irrigation accordingly.
- Connected retail: These are devices that can enhance the shopping experience and customer loyalty, such as smart shelves, digital signage, or payment terminals. They can also provide inventory management, supply chain optimization, and data analytics. Some examples of connected retail are Amazon Go, which uses cameras and sensors to enable cashierless checkout, and Zebra Technologies' SmartLens, which uses RFID tags and readers to track inventory and prevent theft.
- Connected healthcare monitors: These are devices that can measure and track vital signs, such as blood pressure, heart rate, or glucose levels, and transmit the data to healthcare providers or caregivers. They can also provide feedback and guidance to users, and alert them of any anomalies or emergencies. Some examples of connected healthcare monitors are Fitbit, which offers wearable devices and apps to track fitness and wellness, and Dexcom, which offers continuous glucose monitoring systems for people with diabetes.
- Connected manufacturing equipment: These are devices that can improve the efficiency and quality of production processes, such as robots, sensors, or controllers. They can also monitor and optimize the performance and maintenance of the equipment, and provide real-time data and insights. Some examples of connected manufacturing equipment are Siemens' MindSphere, which is a cloud-based platform that connects and analyzes data from industrial IoT devices, and GE's Predix, which is a software platform that enables predictive maintenance and asset optimization.
- Connected cities: These are devices that can improve the livability and sustainability of urban areas, such as smart street lights, traffic signals, or waste bins. They can also provide public services, such as transportation, energy, or safety, and collect and share data and insights. Some examples of connected cities are Barcelona, which uses IoT to manage parking, lighting, and waste, and Singapore, which uses IoT to monitor air quality, traffic, and security.
