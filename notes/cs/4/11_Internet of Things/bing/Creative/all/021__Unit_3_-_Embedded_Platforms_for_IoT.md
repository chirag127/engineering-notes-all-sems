## Unit 3 - Embedded Platforms for IoT

- Embedded platforms for IoT are hardware and software systems that provide the functionality and connectivity for IoT devices and applications.
- Embedded platforms for IoT typically consist of one or more of the following components:
  - Embedded processors: These are microcontrollers (MCUs) or microprocessors (MPUs) that execute the application logic and interface with sensors, actuators, and other peripherals. Embedded processors can range from low-power, low-cost MCUs to high-performance, multicore MPUs, depending on the requirements of the IoT application.
  - Embedded operating systems: These are software platforms that provide the basic services and abstractions for the embedded processors, such as memory management, task scheduling, inter-process communication, device drivers, and network stacks. Embedded operating systems can be either general-purpose (such as Linux or Windows) or real-time (such as FreeRTOS or Azure RTOS), depending on the timing and reliability constraints of the IoT application.
  - Embedded middleware: These are software libraries and frameworks that provide higher-level functionality and connectivity for the IoT application, such as data processing, encryption, authentication, cloud integration, and protocol support. Embedded middleware can be either vendor-specific (such as Mbed OS or AWS IoT Device SDK) or vendor-agnostic (such as MQTT or CoAP), depending on the interoperability and scalability requirements of the IoT application.
- Embedded platforms for IoT can offer several benefits for IoT developers, such as:
  - Simplifying software development and maintenance by providing ready-made and tested components and tools.
  - Enhancing performance and power efficiency by optimizing the hardware and software for the specific IoT use case and environment.
  - Improving security and reliability by implementing advanced features and mechanisms to protect the IoT devices and data from unauthorized access and threats.
  - Accelerating time to market by reducing the integration and testing efforts and enabling faster deployment and updates.
- Embedded platforms for IoT can also pose some challenges for IoT developers, such as:
  - Increasing complexity and diversity by requiring the knowledge and skills of multiple hardware and software technologies and standards.
  - Limiting flexibility and customization by imposing certain constraints and trade-offs on the hardware and software design and configuration.
  - Raising compatibility and interoperability issues by introducing potential conflicts and mismatches between different hardware and software components and platforms.
- Embedded platforms for IoT can be classified into different categories based on various criteria, such as:
  - Architecture: This refers to the type and structure of the embedded processor, such as ARM, x86, RISC-V, or MIPS, and the number and arrangement of the cores, such as single-core, multicore, or heterogeneous.
  - Functionality: This refers to the level and scope of the functionality and connectivity provided by the embedded platform, such as basic, intermediate, or advanced, and edge, gateway, or cloud.
  - Application: This refers to the specific domain and use case of the IoT application, such as industrial, automotive, medical, or consumer, and the corresponding requirements and challenges.
- Some examples of embedded platforms for IoT are:
  - AMD Embedded Solutions for IoT: These are embedded processors and platforms that offer high performance, power efficiency, and security for edge computing, IoT gateways, and cloud applications. They include the AMD EPYC Embedded and AMD Ryzen Embedded families of processors, which are based on the x86 architecture and support various operating systems and middleware.
  - Cisco IoT Embedded Networking: These are embedded routers and switches that offer secure and reliable connectivity in extreme environments. They include the Cisco ESR6300, Cisco ESS9300, Cisco 5921, Cisco 3300, and Cisco 2020 series of products, which are based on the ARM architecture and support various operating systems and middleware.
  - Azure RTOS: This is a real-time embedded operating system that offers efficient and robust services and abstractions for embedded processors. It supports various architectures, such as ARM, x86, and RISC-V, and various middleware, such as Azure IoT Device SDK and Azure IoT Hub.
