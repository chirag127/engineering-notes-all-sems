### Design Principles for Connected Devices for the notes of the Unit 1 - Internet of Things (IoT) in the subject of Internet of Things

- Design principles for connected devices are the guidelines and best practices that help to create effective and user-friendly IoT solutions.
- Design principles for connected devices cover various aspects such as interaction, communication, data, security, and scalability of IoT devices and systems.
- Some of the common design principles for connected devices are:

  - **Calm technology**: A system that does not compete for attention but has a high utility when given attention, this type of technology is referred to as calm technology. Calm technology aims to reduce the cognitive load and distraction of users by providing subtle and ambient feedback and notifications. For example, a smart thermostat that adjusts the temperature automatically and only alerts the user when there is a problem or a change in preference.
  - **System thinking**: A design principle that thinks of a device in the context of its operating environment and its interaction with other elements, rather than as an isolated product. System thinking helps to design devices that are interoperable, adaptable, and resilient to changes and failures. For example, a smart lighting system that can communicate with other devices such as sensors, switches, and mobile apps, and can adjust the brightness and color according to the time, weather, and user preferences.
  - **User-centric design**: A design principle that focuses on the needs, preferences, and expectations of the users, rather than on the technical features or specifications of the devices. User-centric design helps to create devices that are easy to use, intuitive, and satisfying for the users. For example, a smart speaker that can understand natural language commands and queries, and can provide personalized and relevant responses and suggestions.
  - **Security by design**: A design principle that incorporates security measures and best practices from the beginning of the design process, rather than as an afterthought or a patch. Security by design helps to protect the devices, data, and users from unauthorized access, manipulation, or harm. For example, a smart lock that uses encryption, authentication, and authorization to prevent unauthorized entry or access.
  - **Scalability by design**: A design principle that considers the potential growth and expansion of the devices and systems, and ensures that they can handle increased demand, complexity, and diversity. Scalability by design helps to create devices that are flexible, modular, and compatible with different standards and protocols. For example, a smart meter that can support different types of energy sources, devices, and services, and can communicate with different networks and platforms.

- A possible mnemonic to remember these design principles is **C.U.S.S.S.** (Calm, User-centric, System, Security, Scalability).
