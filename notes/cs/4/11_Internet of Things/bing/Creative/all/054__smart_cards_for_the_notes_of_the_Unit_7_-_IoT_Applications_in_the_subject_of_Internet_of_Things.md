### Smart cards for the notes of the Unit 7 - IoT Applications in the subject of Internet of Things

- A smart card is a small portable computer, usually the size of a credit card, without a display and a keyboard. It integrates a microprocessor, some memory, and some apps.
- The circular metal contact is vital to connect to the chip below and activate the card electrically. It's used with a contact or contactless card reader (POS for payments, at the ATM, or even on your mobile phone).
- Smart cards are used in a wide range of industries worldwide to support access, identity, payment and other applications. Some examples are:
  - Enterprise ID: smart cards can be used as physical and logical access control devices for employees, contractors, and visitors.
  - Financial: smart cards can be used as credit, debit, or prepaid cards for secure and convenient payments.
  - Government: smart cards can be used as national ID cards, electronic passports, driver's licenses, health cards, or residence permits.
  - Healthcare: smart cards can be used as patient ID cards, insurance cards, or electronic health records to improve patient safety and privacy.
  - Identity: smart cards can be used as personal identification devices for online authentication, digital signatures, or biometric verification.
  - Internet of Things (IoT): smart cards can be used to authenticate and encrypt communications between IoT devices and applications, as well as to manage encryption keys .
  - Telecommunications: smart cards can be used as SIM cards for mobile phones, tablets, or other connected devices.
  - Transportation: smart cards can be used as transit cards, toll cards, or car park cards for convenient and efficient mobility services.
- Smart cards provide ways to securely identify and authenticate the holder and third parties who want access to the card. For example, a cardholder can use a PIN code or biometric data for authentication. They also provide a way to securely store data on the card and protect communications with encryption.
- Smart cards are based on international standards that ensure interoperability, compatibility, and security across different platforms and applications.
- Smart cards can support multiple applications on a single card, allowing the cardholder to use the same card for different purposes and services.
- The cost of a smart card depends on various factors, such as the type, size, and features of the card, the volume and frequency of the order, and the market conditions. According to some estimates, the average cost of a smart card ranges from $0.5 to $5.
- A mnemonic to remember the main features and benefits of smart cards is: **S**ecure, **M**ulti-functional, **A**uthentic, **R**eliable, and **T**echnical.
