### Digital sensors for the notes of the Unit 2 - Hardware for IoT in the subject of Internet of Things

- Digital sensors are pieces of hardware that detect changes in an environment and collect data in a digital format .
- Digital sensors interact directly with an IoT microcontroller, eliminating the need for an analog-to-digital converter.
- Digital sensors are further differentiated based on the work they perform, such as measuring temperature, pressure, motion, light, sound, etc .
- Digital sensors can be connected to a network and share data with other devices or cloud services .
- Digital sensors are used in various IoT applications, such as smart cars, smart factories, smart homes, smart buildings, and smart things .

Some examples of digital sensors for IoT are:

- XENSIV™ 60 GHz radar sensors: These sensors use radar technology to give devices greater contextual awareness and the ability to \"see\" their surroundings. They can detect gestures, movements, presence, distance, and speed of objects or people.
- XENSIV™ MEMS microphones: These sensors use microelectromechanical systems (MEMS) technology to capture sound with high quality and low noise. They can enable voice control, audio recording, noise cancellation, and acoustic sensing in IoT devices.
- XENSIV™ PAS CO2 sensors: These sensors use photoacoustic spectroscopy (PAS) technology to measure the concentration of carbon dioxide (CO2) in the air. They can enable air quality monitoring, ventilation control, and indoor climate optimization in IoT devices.

Some advantages of digital sensors for IoT are:

- They can provide accurate and reliable data in a format that is easy to process and store .
- They can reduce the complexity and cost of the IoT system by eliminating the need for extra components or conversions.
- They can enable new and innovative IoT use cases that enhance the user experience and the device functionality .

Some disadvantages of digital sensors for IoT are:

- They may require more power and bandwidth than analog sensors, depending on the data rate and resolution.
- They may be affected by noise or interference from other digital devices or signals.
- They may face compatibility or interoperability issues with different IoT platforms or protocols.

Some mnemonics and learning tricks for digital sensors for IoT are:

- To remember the types of digital sensors, use the acronym TPLMS: Temperature, Pressure, Light, Motion, Sound .
- To remember the examples of digital sensors, use the acronym RMC: Radar, Microphone, CO2.
- To remember the advantages of digital sensors, use the acronym ARC: Accuracy, Reliability, Complexity .
- To remember the disadvantages of digital sensors, use the acronym PNI: Power, Noise, Interoperability.
