### Communication technologies for the notes of the Unit 1 - Internet of Things (IoT) in the subject of Internet of Things

Communication technologies are essential for enabling the connectivity and data exchange among IoT devices, networks, and remote infrastructures. There are various communication technologies and protocols that are used for IoT, depending on the requirements and constraints of the IoT system. Some of the common communication technologies and protocols for IoT are:

- **Bluetooth**: A short-range wireless technology that uses radio waves to connect devices within a range of 10 meters. Bluetooth is widely used for personal area networks (PANs) and wearable devices. Bluetooth has low power consumption and can support multiple devices simultaneously. Bluetooth has several versions, such as Bluetooth Low Energy (BLE), which is optimized for IoT applications. Bluetooth supports data rates up to 24 Mbps and operates in the 2.4 GHz frequency band. Bluetooth is an example of an infrastructure protocol, which provides the basic connectivity and data transfer among IoT devices .

- **Zigbee**: A low-power wireless technology that uses mesh networking to connect devices within a range of 100 meters. Zigbee is mainly used for industrial and home automation applications, such as smart lighting, smart metering, and smart security. Zigbee has low data rates (up to 250 kbps) and operates in the 2.4 GHz frequency band. Zigbee is also an example of an infrastructure protocol, which provides the routing and network management functions for IoT devices .

- **Z-Wave**: A low-power wireless technology that uses a star topology to connect devices within a range of 30 meters. Z-Wave is similar to Zigbee, but it operates in the sub-GHz frequency band (868 MHz in Europe and 908 MHz in North America). Z-Wave is mainly used for home automation applications, such as smart locks, smart thermostats, and smart sensors. Z-Wave has low data rates (up to 100 kbps) and supports up to 232 devices per network. Z-Wave is also an example of an infrastructure protocol, which provides the network formation and device discovery functions for IoT devices .

- **Wi-Fi**: A wireless technology that uses radio waves to connect devices within a range of 100 meters. Wi-Fi is widely used for local area networks (LANs) and internet access. Wi-Fi has high data rates (up to 1.3 Gbps) and operates in the 2.4 GHz and 5 GHz frequency bands. Wi-Fi is an example of a data protocol, which provides the data transmission and security functions for IoT devices. Wi-Fi has several standards, such as Wi-Fi Direct, which enables peer-to-peer communication among IoT devices, and Wi-Fi HaLow, which is a low-power variant of Wi-Fi for IoT applications .

- **Cellular**: A wireless technology that uses cellular networks to connect devices over long distances. Cellular is widely used for wide area networks (WANs) and mobile communication. Cellular has high data rates (up to 10 Gbps) and operates in various frequency bands, depending on the generation of the technology. Cellular is an example of a data protocol, which provides the data transmission and security functions for IoT devices. Cellular has several standards, such as 4G LTE, which is the current mainstream technology for mobile broadband, and 5G NR, which is the next-generation technology for IoT applications. Cellular also supports narrowband IoT (NB-IoT) and LTE-M, which are low-power and low-cost variants of cellular for IoT applications .

- **NFC**: A short-range wireless technology that uses magnetic fields to connect devices within a range of 10 centimeters. NFC is mainly used for contactless payment, identification, and authentication applications. NFC has low data rates (up to 424 kbps) and operates in the 13.56 MHz frequency band. NFC is an example of an identification protocol, which provides the device identification and data exchange functions for IoT devices. NFC supports two modes of communication: passive mode, where one device generates the magnetic field and the other device responds, and active mode, where both devices generate and respond to the magnetic field .
