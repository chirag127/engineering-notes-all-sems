### E-health for the notes of the Unit 7 - IoT Applications in the subject of Internet of Things

- E-health is the application of digital technology to healthcare practice, such as using the Internet, IoT devices, mobile apps, cloud computing, big data analytics, etc. to provide health-related services and information .
- E-health can improve the quality, efficiency, accessibility, and affordability of healthcare, as well as enhance the patient engagement and satisfaction .
- E-health can also enable remote physiological monitoring, ambient assisted living, active and healthy aging, and personalized medicine for patients with chronic diseases or special needs  .
- Some examples of e-health applications are:
  - Smart wearables, implants, and ingestible electronics that can measure vital signs, track physical activity, monitor glucose levels, etc. and send the data to the cloud or the healthcare provider .
  - Telemedicine platforms that can connect patients and doctors via video calls, chatbots, or online portals, and allow remote diagnosis, consultation, prescription, and follow-up .
  - IoT sensors and architectures that can collect and analyze health data from various sources, such as electronic health records, medical devices, environmental sensors, etc. and provide feedback, alerts, or recommendations .
  - Automatic exposure notification systems that can use Bluetooth-based proximity detection to identify and notify people who have been in contact with someone who tested positive for COVID-19.
  - Brain-computer interfaces that can use electrodes or other transducers to measure brain signals and translate them into commands or actions, such as controlling a wheelchair, a prosthetic limb, or a computer.
- Some challenges and opportunities for e-health applications are:
  - Ensuring the safety, security, privacy, and ethics of e-health data and systems, especially in the context of cyberattacks, data breaches, or unauthorized access  .
  - Achieving interoperability and standardization among different e-health devices, platforms, and protocols, and ensuring compatibility and integration with existing healthcare systems and regulations  .
  - Developing reliable and location-agnostic communication and networking technologies for e-health, such as radio frequency propagation, electric field communication, kinetic-based micro energy-harvesters, etc. that can overcome the challenges of body area networks, such as attenuation, interference, or power consumption.
  - Exploiting the potential of 5G and IoT for e-health, such as enabling low-latency, high-bandwidth, and massive connectivity for e-health applications, as well as supporting edge computing and big data analytics for e-health data processing and decision making .
  - Increasing the awareness, acceptance, and adoption of e-health among patients, healthcare providers, and policymakers, and addressing the barriers and gaps in terms of digital literacy, trust, and affordability .

- A possible mnemonic to remember the main types of e-health applications is SWITBA, which stands for Smart wearables, implants, and ingestible electronics; Telemedicine platforms; IoT sensors and architectures; Automatic exposure notification systems; and Brain-computer interfaces.
