### Overview of IOT supported Hardware platforms such as Arduino for the notes of the Unit 3 - Embedded Platforms for IoT in the subject of Internet of Things

- IoT (Internet of Things) is a concept that refers to the network of various devices that interact with each other and with their environment through the internet or other wireless communication technologies.
- IoT devices can be sensors, actuators, cameras, wearables, smart appliances, etc. that collect, process, and transmit data to other devices or cloud services.
- IoT devices require hardware platforms that enable them to connect to the internet and perform their functions. Hardware platforms are the physical components that make up an IoT device, such as microcontrollers, processors, memory, storage, input/output interfaces, wireless modules, etc.
- There are many hardware platforms available in the market that support IoT development and prototyping. Some of the most popular ones are:

  - Arduino: Arduino is an open-source platform that consists of a range of microcontroller-based development boards, software tools, and a large community of users and developers. Arduino boards are easy to use, program, and interface with various sensors and actuators. Arduino boards can communicate with the internet using Wi-Fi, Ethernet, Bluetooth, or cellular modules. Arduino boards are suitable for beginners and hobbyists who want to create simple and interactive IoT projects. Some examples of Arduino boards are Arduino Uno, Arduino Nano, Arduino Mega, Arduino Yun, Arduino MKR, etc.   
  - Raspberry Pi: Raspberry Pi is a single-board computer that runs on Linux and can perform various computing tasks. Raspberry Pi boards have USB ports, HDMI port, Ethernet port, GPIO pins, and a microSD card slot. Raspberry Pi boards can connect to the internet using Wi-Fi, Ethernet, or cellular modules. Raspberry Pi boards are suitable for advanced users who want to create complex and powerful IoT applications that require more processing power and memory. Some examples of Raspberry Pi boards are Raspberry Pi 4, Raspberry Pi 3, Raspberry Pi Zero, etc.  
  - Espressif: Espressif is a company that develops low-power and low-cost Wi-Fi and Bluetooth chips and modules for IoT applications. Espressif is most well-known for its ESP8266 and ESP32 series of chips, modules, and development boards. ESP8266 and ESP32 boards have built-in Wi-Fi and Bluetooth capabilities, as well as GPIO pins, ADC, DAC, SPI, I2C, UART, etc. ESP8266 and ESP32 boards can be programmed using Arduino IDE, MicroPython, Lua, etc. ESP8266 and ESP32 boards are suitable for users who want to create IoT devices that require wireless connectivity and low power consumption. Some examples of ESP8266 and ESP32 boards are NodeMCU, Wemos D1, ESP32 DevKit, etc.  
  - SparkFun: SparkFun is a company that sells various electronic components, modules, sensors, and development boards for IoT and other applications. SparkFun offers more than 2,000 open-source products and provides online tutorials and training for users. SparkFun also has its own line of development boards called SparkFun RedBoard, which are compatible with Arduino IDE and can be used for IoT projects. SparkFun also sells other development boards from different manufacturers, such as Arduino, Raspberry Pi, Espressif, etc. 
  - Adafruit: Adafruit is a company that sells various electronic components, modules, sensors, and development boards for IoT and other applications. Adafruit also offers online learning resources and guides for users. Adafruit has its own line of development boards called Adafruit Feather, which are designed for prototyping on the fly. Adafruit Feather boards are small, lightweight, and have various wireless options, such as Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, LoRa, etc. Adafruit Feather boards can be programmed using Arduino IDE, CircuitPython, etc. Adafruit also sells other development boards from different manufacturers, such as Arduino, Raspberry Pi, Espressif, etc. 
  - Intel: Intel is a company that develops processors, chips, and modules for various applications, including IoT. Intel has a range of development boards that support IoT development, such as Intel Galileo, Intel Edison, Intel Joule, Intel Curie, etc. Intel boards have high-performance processors, memory, storage, and wireless capabilities. Intel boards can run Linux, Windows, or other operating systems. Intel boards can be programmed using Arduino IDE, Intel System Studio
