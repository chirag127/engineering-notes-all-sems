# Embedded Computing Basics

Embedded computing is the use of computer systems to perform specific functions within larger systems or devices. Embedded systems are usually designed to have low cost, low power consumption, high reliability, and fast response time. Embedded systems can be found in various applications, such as consumer electronics, industrial automation, medical devices, automotive systems, and Internet of Things (IoT) devices.

Some of the basic concepts and characteristics of embedded computing are:

- **Hardware**: Embedded systems typically consist of a microcontroller or a microprocessor, memory, input/output devices, and peripherals. The hardware components are often customized or integrated to meet the specific requirements of the application. For example, an embedded system may have a sensor, an actuator, a communication module, or a display as part of its hardware.
- **Software**: Embedded systems usually run a single program or a set of programs that are stored in the memory. The software can be written in different languages, such as assembly, C, C++, Java, or Python. The software can also use different operating systems, such as Linux, Windows, or real-time operating systems (RTOS). The software is responsible for controlling the hardware, processing the data, and communicating with other devices or systems.
- **Development**: Embedded systems are developed using various tools and methods, such as software engineering tools, hardware design tools, debugging tools, testing tools, and simulation tools. The development process involves several steps, such as requirement analysis, design, implementation, verification, validation, and deployment. The development process also requires knowledge of the application domain, the hardware platform, the software platform, and the performance and safety constraints.
- **Types**: Embedded systems can be classified into different types, based on their functionality, complexity, connectivity, and mobility. Some of the common types of embedded systems are:

  - **Standalone embedded systems**: These are embedded systems that operate independently and do not require any external network or communication. They perform a single or a few tasks and have a fixed input and output. Examples of standalone embedded systems are calculators, digital watches, and microwave ovens.
  - **Network embedded systems**: These are embedded systems that are connected to a network or the Internet and can communicate with other devices or systems. They perform multiple tasks and have a dynamic input and output. Examples of network embedded systems are smart home devices, security systems, and web servers.
  - **Mobile embedded systems**: These are embedded systems that are portable and can move from one location to another. They have limited power and memory resources and rely on wireless communication. Examples of mobile embedded systems are smartphones, tablets, and wearable devices.
  - **Real-time embedded systems**: These are embedded systems that have strict timing constraints and must respond to events or inputs within a specified time frame. They are often used for critical applications that require high reliability and accuracy. Examples of real-time embedded systems are airbags, pacemakers, and flight control systems.
