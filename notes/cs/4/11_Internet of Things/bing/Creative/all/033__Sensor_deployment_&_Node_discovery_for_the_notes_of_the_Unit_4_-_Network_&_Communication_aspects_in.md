### Sensor deployment and node discovery for the notes of the Unit 4 - Network & Communication aspects in IoT in the subject of Internet of Things

- Sensor deployment is the process of placing and configuring sensor nodes in an IoT network to achieve a desired sensing coverage and connectivity.
- Sensor nodes are the devices that can sense, process and communicate data in an IoT network. They can be deployed in various environments, such as public spaces, healthcare, agriculture, smart cities, etc.
- Sensor deployment can be classified into two types: deterministic and random. Deterministic deployment is when the sensor nodes are placed at predefined locations, while random deployment is when the sensor nodes are scattered randomly in the area of interest.
- Sensor deployment can affect the performance of the IoT network in terms of energy efficiency, data quality, network lifetime, security, etc. Therefore, sensor deployment should consider the following factors: 
  - The sensing requirements of the application, such as the accuracy, frequency and resolution of the data.
  - The communication requirements of the application, such as the bandwidth, latency and reliability of the data transmission.
  - The environmental constraints of the deployment area, such as the terrain, obstacles, interference, etc.
  - The resource constraints of the sensor nodes, such as the battery, memory, processing power, etc.
  - The cost and availability of the sensor nodes, such as the number, type and price of the sensors.
- Sensor deployment can be optimized using various techniques, such as mathematical models, optimization algorithms, heuristic methods, etc. The objective of sensor deployment optimization is to maximize the sensing coverage and connectivity of the IoT network while minimizing the energy consumption and cost of the sensor nodes.
- Node discovery is the process of identifying and locating the sensor nodes in an IoT network. Node discovery is essential for establishing and maintaining the network topology, routing the data, managing the network resources, etc.
- Node discovery can be classified into two types: proactive and reactive. Proactive node discovery is when the sensor nodes periodically broadcast their presence and information to the network, while reactive node discovery is when the sensor nodes respond to the queries from other nodes or a central controller.
- Node discovery can be performed using various techniques, such as beaconing, neighbor discovery, localization, etc. The objective of node discovery is to provide accurate and timely information about the sensor nodes to the network, such as their ID, location, status, capabilities, etc.
- Node discovery can affect the performance of the IoT network in terms of energy efficiency, data quality, network lifetime, security, etc. Therefore, node discovery should consider the following factors: 
  - The network size and density, such as the number and distribution of the sensor nodes.
  - The network dynamics and mobility, such as the changes and movements of the sensor nodes.
  - The network heterogeneity and diversity, such as the different types and capabilities of the sensor nodes.
  - The network scalability and adaptability, such as the ability to handle the addition and deletion of the sensor nodes.
  - The network security and privacy, such as the protection of the sensor nodes from malicious attacks and unauthorized access.

- A possible mnemonic to remember the factors affecting sensor deployment and node discovery is **SCREAM**:

  - **S**ensing requirements
  - **C**ommunication requirements
  - **R**esource constraints
  - **E**nvironmental constraints
  - **A**vailability and cost
  - **M**obility and dynamics

- A possible learning trick to understand the difference between deterministic and random deployment is to imagine a chess board and a dice. Deterministic deployment is like placing the chess pieces at specific positions on the board, while random deployment is like throwing the dice and placing the pieces wherever they land.
