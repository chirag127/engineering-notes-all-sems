### Intel Galileo boards for the notes of the Unit 3 - Embedded Platforms for IoT in the subject of Internet of Things

- Intel Galileo is a development board based on Intel x86 architecture and is designed for the maker and education communities. 
- It is compatible with Arduino software and hardware, enabling re-use of existing sketches and shields. 
- It runs an open source Linux operating system with the Arduino software libraries. 
- It can be programmed through OS X, Microsoft Windows and Linux host operating software. 
- It features the Intel Quark SoC X1000, a 32-bit, single core, single-thread, Pentium instruction set architecture (ISA)-compatible CPU, operating at speeds up to 400 MHz. 
- It has 256 MB of DDR3 RAM and 8 MB of flash memory. 
- It supports both 3.3V and 5V shields. 
- It comes with several computing industry standard I/O interfaces, such as Ethernet, USB host and client, RS-232, SD card slot, mini PCI Express slot, JTAG header, etc. 
- It can use the Intel® Distribution of OpenVINO™ toolkit on an Intel® Movidius™ Vision Processing Unit (VPU) and Intel® Arria® 10 FPGA for power-efficient deep neural network inference for video analytics and computer vision applications. 
- It can use the MRAA and UPM libraries for low level and high level access to the GPIO interfaces. 
- It can be used for various IoT projects, such as smart home, wearable devices, robotics, environmental monitoring, etc.  
- Intel released two versions of Galileo, referred to as Gen 1 and Gen 2. The Gen 2 has some improvements over the Gen 1, such as better PWM, faster GPIO, power over Ethernet, and a 6-pin UART connector. 
- The board was discontinued on June 19, 2017.
