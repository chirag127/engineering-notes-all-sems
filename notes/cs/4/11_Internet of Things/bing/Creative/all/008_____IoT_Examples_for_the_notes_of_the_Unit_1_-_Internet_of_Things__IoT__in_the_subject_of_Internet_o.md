# IoT Examples

The Internet of Things (IoT) is the network of physical objects that can collect, process, exchange and utilize data interrelatedly, via the internet or other communications networks. IoT has many different applications in various domains, such as transportation, home automation, healthcare, agriculture, retail, manufacturing and smart cities. Here are some examples of IoT devices and systems that illustrate the potential and diversity of IoT:

- **Connected cars**: IoT enables vehicles to communicate with each other, with the infrastructure, and with the cloud, to provide enhanced safety, efficiency, entertainment and convenience features. For example, Tesla's cars can receive over-the-air (OTA) software updates, access real-time traffic information, and stream music and videos. Autonomous vehicles are another example of IoT in action, as they use artificial intelligence (AI)-enabled cameras, motion sensors and onboard computers to safely navigate roadways in all sorts of traffic and weather conditions.
- **Smart appliances**: IoT enables household appliances to be controlled remotely, to optimize energy consumption, and to provide personalized services. For example, smart refrigerators can monitor the food items inside, suggest recipes, and order groceries online. Smart ovens can adjust the temperature and cooking time according to the dish. Smart washing machines can detect the load size and fabric type, and choose the optimal washing cycle.
- **Connected security systems**: IoT enables home and business owners to monitor and protect their properties from anywhere, using sensors, cameras, alarms, locks and other devices. For example, Ring's doorbell camera can detect motion, record video, and allow the user to see and talk to the visitors through a smartphone app. Nest's smoke and carbon monoxide detector can alert the user and the emergency services in case of fire or gas leak.
- **Smart agriculture equipment**: IoT enables farmers to improve crop yield, reduce water and fertilizer waste, and monitor livestock health and location, using sensors, drones, GPS, and cloud computing. For example, John Deere's precision agriculture solutions can collect and analyze data from the soil, the plants, the weather, and the machines, and provide actionable insights and recommendations to the farmers. Livestock monitoring systems can track the animals' movements, behaviors, and vital signs, and alert the farmers of any anomalies or risks.
- **Connected retail**: IoT enables retailers to enhance customer experience, optimize inventory management, and increase sales and revenue, using sensors, RFID tags, beacons, digital signage, and mobile apps. For example, Amazon Go's cashierless stores use computer vision, deep learning, and sensor fusion to allow customers to shop without scanning or checking out. Smart shelves can detect the stock level and the expiration date of the products, and automatically reorder or discount them. Personalized offers and recommendations can be delivered to the customers based on their location, preferences, and purchase history.
- **Connected healthcare monitors**: IoT enables patients and healthcare providers to monitor and manage various health conditions, using wearable devices, implants, smart patches, and mobile apps. For example, Fitbit's fitness trackers can measure the user's heart rate, steps, calories, sleep quality, and other health metrics, and provide feedback and guidance. Medtronic's insulin pumps can automatically adjust the insulin dosage based on the user's blood glucose level. ECG monitors can detect and alert the user of any cardiac abnormalities.
- **Connected manufacturing equipment**: IoT enables manufacturers to improve operational efficiency, product quality, and worker safety, using sensors, actuators, robots, and cloud computing. For example, Bosch's Industry 4.0 solutions can monitor and control the production processes, and optimize the energy and resource consumption. GE's Predix platform can collect and analyze data from the industrial machines, and provide predictive maintenance and performance optimization. Smart helmets can protect the workers from hazards and provide them with real-time information and assistance.
- **Connected cities**: IoT enables urban planners and authorities to improve the quality of life, sustainability, and resilience of the cities, using sensors, cameras, smart meters, and other devices. For example, smart streetlights can adjust the brightness and color based on the traffic and weather conditions, and save energy and costs. Smart parking systems can guide the drivers to the available parking spots, and reduce congestion and pollution. Smart waste management systems can monitor the fill level of the bins, and optimize the collection routes and schedules.
