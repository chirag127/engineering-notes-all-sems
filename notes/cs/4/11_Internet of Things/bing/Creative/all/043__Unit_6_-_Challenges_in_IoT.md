## Unit 6 - Challenges in IoT

The Internet of Things (IoT) refers to the interconnectivity of physical devices, vehicles, home appliances, and other items embedded with electronics, software, sensors, and connectivity which enables these objects to connect and exchange data. The IoT concept involves extending Internet connectivity beyond traditional devices like desktop and laptop computers, smartphones and tablets to a diverse range of devices and everyday things. The ultimate goal of IoT is to offer advanced connectivity of devices, systems, and services that goes beyond machine-to-machine communications and covers a variety of protocols, domains, and applications.

However, IoT also faces many challenges that need to be addressed in order to realize its full potential. Some of the major challenges in IoT are:

- **Security and privacy**: IoT devices collect and store vast amounts of personal data, making them a prime target for hackers and cybercriminals. IoT security seeks to protect devices and networks, addressing the specific issues inherent to IoT deployments. But ensuring robust security for IoT devices is no easy task. Some of the security challenges in IoT are :
  - Lack of encryption: Many IoT devices do not encrypt communications if the data is transferred over a local Wi-Fi network. If the Wi-Fi network is unsecured, this leaves the device wide open to security risks.
  - Insufficient testing and updating: Many IoT devices and products do not get enough testing and updates and are prone to hackers and other security issues. Some devices require manual interaction to update software and firmware, while others allow automatic over the air (OTA) updates. However, even if a device is kept up-to-date, there can be holes in system security if the device manufacturer or software designer retires a product or goes out of business and stops creating updates.
  - Brute forcing and the risk of default passwords: Weak credentials and login details leave nearly all IoT devices vulnerable to password hacking and brute force. Any company that uses factory default credentials on their devices is placing both their business and its assets and the customer and their valuable information at risk of being susceptible to a brute force attack.
  - IoT malware and ransomware: IoT malware and ransomware use encryption to effectively lock out users from various devices and platforms and still use a user’s valuable data and info. For example, a hacker can hijack a computer camera and take pictures. By using malware access points, the hackers can demand ransom to unlock the device and return the data.
  - IoT botnet aiming at cryptocurrency: IoT botnet workers can manipulate data privacy, which could be massive risks for an open Crypto market. The exact value and creation of cryptocurrencies code face danger from mal-intentioned hackers. The blockchain technology itself is not particularly vulnerable, but the app development process is.
  - Inadequate device security: Inadequate device security refers to the lack of proper measures to protect electronic devices such as computers, smartphones, and IoT devices from cyber attacks, hacking, data theft, and unauthorized access. Typically, IoT devices are not equipped with the same security features that standard computers have. In many cases, it’s nearly impossible to detect malware or ransomware in a device unless you’re an expert at coding.
- **Lack of effective and informed government regulations**: IoT is a fast-developing area of technology. The wheels of government, however, often move slowly. This means that there is a gap between the pace of innovation and the pace of regulation. Many countries do not have clear or consistent laws and policies regarding IoT, which can lead to confusion, uncertainty, and inconsistency for both developers and users. For example, there may be different standards for data protection, privacy, security, liability, and ownership across different regions and jurisdictions. This can hamper the growth and adoption of IoT, as well as pose legal and ethical challenges.
- **Device compatibility**: In the context of the Internet of Things (IoT), interoperability is a critical challenge, as a large number of diverse devices are being connected to the internet. The lack of standardization in the IoT can lead to difficulties in communication and data exchange between devices, resulting in an fragmented and inefficient system. For example, a smart home device may not be compatible with a smart car device, or a smart city device may not be compatible with a smart agriculture device. To overcome this challenge, there is a need for common protocols, platforms, and frameworks that can enable seamless integration and interoperability of IoT devices.
