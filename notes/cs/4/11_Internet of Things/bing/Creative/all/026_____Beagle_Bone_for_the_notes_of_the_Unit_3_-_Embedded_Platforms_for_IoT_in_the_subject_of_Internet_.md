# Beagle Bone

Beagle Bone is a series of low-cost, open-source, single-board Linux computers that can be used for embedded applications such as Internet of Things (IoT). Some of the features and benefits of Beagle Bone are:

- It has a small size (about the size of a credit card) and a low power consumption (about 2W).
- It has a high-performance processor (ARM Cortex-A8 or ARM Cortex-A15) that can run various operating systems such as Android, Ubuntu, Debian, and Angstrom.
- It has plenty of input/output (I/O) interfaces such as USB, Ethernet, HDMI, LCD, audio, serial, SPI, I2C, and GPIO, that can be used to connect sensors, actuators, displays, cameras, and other peripherals.
- It has a large and active community of developers and users who provide support, documentation, tutorials, and software libraries for various projects and applications.
- It can be extended with cape plug-in boards that add additional functionality such as wireless connectivity, motor control, battery power, prototyping, and more.

Some of the popular models of Beagle Bone are:

- BeagleBone Black: The most widely used model, with a 1GHz ARM Cortex-A8 processor, 512MB of RAM, 4GB of eMMC flash storage, and a microSD slot. It costs around $60 and can be purchased from various online retailers such as eBay, Amazon, and Digi-Key.
- BeagleBone Green: A variant of BeagleBone Black, with two Grove connectors for easy attachment of sensors and actuators. It costs around $70 and can be purchased from Seeed Studio or Priclist.com.
- BeagleBone Blue: A model designed for robotics, with a 9-axis inertial measurement unit (IMU), a barometer, a magnetometer, four DC motor drivers, four quadrature encoder inputs, eight servo outputs, and a Wi-Fi/Bluetooth module. It costs around $80 and can be purchased from Seeed Studio or US eBay.
- BeagleBone AI: A model designed for artificial intelligence and machine learning, with a 1.5GHz ARM Cortex-A15 processor, 1GB of RAM, 16GB of eMMC flash storage, a microSD slot, a dual-core PowerVR SGX544 GPU, and a dual-core C66x DSP. It also has a Texas Instruments Deep Learning Accelerator (DLA) that can perform up to 8 TOPS (trillion operations per second) of neural network inference. It costs around $100 and can be purchased from US eBay or Digi-Key.
