# Radio Frequency Identification (RFID) Technology

- Radio Frequency Identification (RFID) technology uses radio waves to identify people or objects without making any physical contact or requiring a line of sight  .
- An RFID system consists of a tiny radio transponder, a radio receiver and transmitter. The transponder, also known as a tag, contains a unique identifier and possibly other information. The receiver and transmitter, also known as a reader, emits radio waves and receives signals from the tag.
- RFID technology can be classified into three types based on the power source and communication mode of the tag:
  - Passive RFID: The tag has no battery and relies on the reader's radio waves to power it and transmit data. Passive RFID tags are cheaper, smaller and more durable than active tags, but have a shorter range and lower memory capacity.
  - Active RFID: The tag has a battery and can initiate communication with the reader. Active RFID tags are more expensive, larger and less durable than passive tags, but have a longer range and higher memory capacity.
  - Semi-passive RFID: The tag has a battery but only uses it to power the internal circuitry, not the radio transmission. Semi-passive RFID tags are intermediate in cost, size, durability, range and memory capacity between passive and active tags.
- RFID technology has many applications in various domains, such as supply chain management, inventory control, asset tracking, access control, identification, authentication, security, payment, healthcare, transportation, etc .
- RFID technology has some advantages and disadvantages compared to other identification technologies, such as barcodes, magnetic stripes, biometrics, etc. Some of the advantages are:
  - RFID tags can store more data than barcodes or magnetic stripes.
  - RFID tags can be read from a distance and do not need to be visible or aligned with the reader.
  - RFID tags can be read simultaneously and at a high speed, increasing efficiency and productivity.
  - RFID tags can be attached to or embedded in various objects, including living beings, and can withstand harsh environments.
  - RFID tags can be updated or rewritten with new information, making them more flexible and adaptable.
- Some of the disadvantages are:
  - RFID tags are more expensive than barcodes or magnetic stripes, especially active and semi-passive tags.
  - RFID tags can interfere with other radio devices or be affected by metal or liquid objects, reducing their reliability and accuracy.
  - RFID tags can pose privacy and security risks, as they can be read by unauthorized or malicious parties, cloned, modified or destroyed.
  - RFID tags can raise ethical and social concerns, as they can be used for surveillance, tracking, profiling or discrimination of individuals or groups.
