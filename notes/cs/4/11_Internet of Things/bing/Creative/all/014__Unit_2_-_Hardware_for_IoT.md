## Unit 2 - Hardware for IoT

- Hardware for IoT refers to the physical components and devices that enable data acquisition, processing, storage, and communication in IoT applications.
- Hardware for IoT can be classified into four main categories: sensors, microcontrollers, communication modules, and power management modules.
- Sensors are devices that measure physical quantities such as temperature, humidity, pressure, light, sound, etc. and convert them into electrical signals that can be processed by microcontrollers or transmitted to other devices. Sensors are the most critical hardware in IoT applications and are used to gather information from the surroundings .
- Microcontrollers are small computers that execute a single task or application. They have a processor, memory, input/output ports, and sometimes a communication interface. Microcontrollers are used to control the sensors, process the data, and communicate with other devices or servers .
- Communication modules are devices that enable wireless or wired data transmission between IoT devices or between IoT devices and servers. Communication modules can use different protocols and technologies such as Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, ZigBee, LoRa, cellular, etc. depending on the requirements of the IoT application  .
- Power management modules are devices that provide power supply and regulate the power consumption of IoT devices. Power management modules can include batteries, solar panels, energy harvesters, converters, regulators, etc. Power management modules are important for ensuring the reliability and longevity of IoT devices, especially in remote or harsh environments .

Some examples of IoT hardware platforms and prototyping kits are:

- Raspberry Pi: A single-board computer that runs Linux and can be used for various IoT applications. Raspberry Pi has a processor, memory, input/output ports, and supports various communication interfaces such as Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, Ethernet, etc. Raspberry Pi can be connected to sensors, actuators, cameras, displays, etc. and can run various software and programming languages.
- Arduino: A family of microcontroller boards and open-source development tools that can be used for various IoT applications. Arduino has a processor, memory, input/output ports, and supports various communication interfaces such as Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, Ethernet, etc. Arduino can be connected to sensors, actuators, displays, etc. and can run various software and programming languages.
- Pycom: A company that offers modules and development kits with flexible connectivity options, such as Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, LoRa, Sigfox, LTE, etc. Pycom modules have a processor, memory, input/output ports, and can be connected to sensors, actuators, displays, etc. Pycom modules can run MicroPython, a version of Python for microcontrollers.
- Particle: A company that offers development kits and connectivity modules that work with its software platform, Particle IoT Device Management. Particle hardware supports various communication interfaces such as Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, cellular, etc. and can be connected to sensors, actuators, displays, etc. Particle hardware can run various software and programming languages.
