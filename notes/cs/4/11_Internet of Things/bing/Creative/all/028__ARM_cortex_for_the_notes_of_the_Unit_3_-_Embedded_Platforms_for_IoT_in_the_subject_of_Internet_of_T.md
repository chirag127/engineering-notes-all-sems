### ARM cortex for the notes of the Unit 3 - Embedded Platforms for IoT in the subject of Internet of Things

- ARM cortex is a family of processors designed by ARM Holdings for low-power, high-performance, and cost-effective embedded applications, such as IoT devices.
- ARM cortex processors are based on the RISC (reduced instruction set computing) architecture, which simplifies the instruction set and reduces the number of cycles per instruction, leading to faster and more efficient execution.
- ARM cortex processors are divided into three categories: Cortex-A, Cortex-R, and Cortex-M, each targeting different application domains and performance levels.
- Cortex-A processors are the most powerful and complex, supporting operating systems such as Linux and Android, and running applications such as web browsers, multimedia, and gaming. They are suitable for cloud-native edge devices, such as smart wearables, gateways, and high-end smart cameras.
- Cortex-R processors are optimized for real-time and safety-critical applications, such as automotive, industrial, and medical systems. They offer high reliability, determinism, and fault tolerance, and can handle interrupts and exceptions with low latency.
- Cortex-M processors are the smallest and most energy-efficient, designed for microcontroller and embedded applications, such as sensors, actuators, and smart meters. They offer a balance of performance, power consumption, and code density, and can run bare-metal or with a lightweight operating system, such as FreeRTOS.
- ARM cortex processors support various features and extensions, such as floating-point units, digital signal processing, memory protection, security, and machine learning.
- ARM cortex processors can be integrated with other ARM IP, such as Ethos microNPU (neural processing unit), Mali GPU (graphics processing unit), and Corstone subsystem, to create customized and differentiated SoCs (system on chips) for IoT applications.
- ARM cortex processors are widely adopted by the IoT industry, due to their scalability, compatibility, and ecosystem support. They enable developers to create innovative and diverse IoT solutions, ranging from keyword recognition and voice recognition to smart speakers and thermostats.
- ARM cortex processors can be accessed and developed using various tools and platforms, such as Arm Virtual Hardware, Arm Development Studio, Arm Keil MDK, and Arm Mbed OS. These tools and platforms simplify and accelerate the IoT and embedded development process, by providing accurate models, debuggers, compilers, libraries, and drivers.

#### Mnemonics and learning tricks

- To remember the three categories of ARM cortex processors, use the acronym **ARM**: **A** for applications, **R** for real-time, and **M** for microcontrollers.
- To remember the features and extensions of ARM cortex processors, use the acronym **FEMMES**: **F** for floating-point, **E** for Ethos, **M** for Mali, **M** for memory protection, **E** for security (enclave), and **S** for signal processing.
- To remember the tools and platforms for ARM cortex development, use the acronym **VAKM**: **V** for Virtual Hardware, **A** for Development Studio, **K** for Keil MDK, and **M** for Mbed OS.
