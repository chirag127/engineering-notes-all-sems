### Wireless Medium Access Issues for the notes of the Unit 4 - Network & Communication aspects in IoT in the subject of Internet of Things

Wireless medium access issues are the challenges and problems that arise when different wireless devices try to communicate and share data over a common channel without physical wires. Wireless medium access issues are important to consider for the design and implementation of Internet of Things (IoT) applications, which involve a large number of heterogeneous and distributed devices that need to interact and cooperate with each other.

Some of the main wireless medium access issues in IoT are:

- **Half-duplex operation**: This means that a wireless device can either send or receive data at a time, but not both simultaneously. This is because the wireless signal that is transmitted by the device may interfere with the signal that is received by the device, making it difficult to detect collisions or errors. Therefore, wireless devices need to coordinate and avoid collisions by using techniques such as time division multiple access (TDMA), frequency division multiple access (FDMA), or code division multiple access (CDMA).

- **Time-varying channel**: This means that the wireless channel may change over time due to factors such as reflection, diffraction, scattering, fading, or multipath propagation of the wireless signal. These factors may affect the signal strength, quality, delay, or reliability of the wireless communication. Therefore, wireless devices need to adapt and cope with the dynamic and unpredictable channel conditions by using techniques such as channel estimation, channel coding, modulation, or adaptive transmission. 

- **Burst channel errors**: This means that a sequence of consecutive bits or symbols in the wireless communication may be corrupted or lost due to noise, interference, or fading. This may result in data loss, retransmission, or delay for the wireless devices. Therefore, wireless devices need to detect and correct errors by using techniques such as error detection codes, error correction codes, or automatic repeat request (ARQ) protocols.

- **Interference**: This means that the wireless communication may be affected by other wireless devices or technologies that use the same or adjacent frequency bands. For example, IoT devices that use the IEEE 802.15.4 standard may suffer from interference caused by IEEE 802.11 (Wi-Fi) devices that operate in the same 2.4 GHz industrial, scientific, and medical (ISM) band. This may degrade the performance, reliability, or security of the wireless communication. Therefore, wireless devices need to avoid or mitigate interference by using techniques such as frequency hopping, spread spectrum, or cognitive radio. 

- **Privacy and security**: This means that the wireless communication may be vulnerable to eavesdropping, spoofing, jamming, or hacking by malicious or unauthorized parties. This may compromise the confidentiality, integrity, or availability of the data or devices involved in the IoT applications. Therefore, wireless devices need to protect and secure their communication by using techniques such as encryption, authentication, or key management.

A mnemonic to remember these wireless medium access issues in IoT is **HIT-BIP**, which stands for **H**alf-duplex, **I**nterference, **T**ime-varying, **B**urst, **I**nterference, and **P**rivacy.
