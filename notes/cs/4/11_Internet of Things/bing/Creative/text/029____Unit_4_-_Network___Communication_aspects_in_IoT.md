## Unit 4 - Network & Communication aspects in IoT

- IoT stands for Internet of Things, which refers to the interconnection of physical devices, sensors, actuators, and other smart objects over the internet.
- IoT devices communicate with each other and with cloud-based services using various network and communication protocols, depending on the requirements and constraints of the application domain.
- Network and communication aspects in IoT include the following topics:

  - **Components for IoT device communication**: These are the basic elements that enable data transmission and reception among IoT devices, such as:
    - IoT device: anything from the tiniest temperature sensor to a giant industrial robot
    - Local communications: the method the device uses to speak with neighboring devices, such as Bluetooth, Wi-Fi, ZigBee, etc.
    - Application protocol: the framework that defines how information content is transported, such as MQTT, CoAP, HTTP, etc.
    - Gateways: translate and re-transmit information, typically linking local device networks to the internet
  - **Communication protocols in IoT**: These are the rules and standards that govern the data exchange and ensure interoperability among heterogeneous devices, such as:
    - Internet protocol (IP): the main protocol that enables devices to have unique addresses and communicate over the internet. IoT works mostly with IPv6, which is the latest internet protocol version and it is used for transferring data and communication
    - Transport layer protocols: these are the protocols that provide reliable or unreliable data delivery services, such as TCP, UDP, etc.
    - Application layer protocols: these are the protocols that define the format and semantics of the data, such as MQTT, CoAP, HTTP, etc.
  - **Networking technologies for IoT**: These are the technologies that enable IoT devices to connect with other devices, applications, and services running in the cloud, such as:
    - Wireless technologies: these are the technologies that use radio waves or other electromagnetic signals to transmit data, such as Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, ZigBee, LoRa, etc.
    - Wired technologies: these are the technologies that use physical cables or wires to transmit data, such as Ethernet, USB, etc.
    - Cellular technologies: these are the technologies that use cellular networks to transmit data, such as 4G, 5G, etc.
  - **Security and privacy issues in IoT networks**: These are the challenges and solutions that aim to protect the data and devices from unauthorized access, modification, or disclosure, such as:
    - Vulnerabilities, threat models, and risk management: these are the methods to identify and assess the potential sources and impacts of attacks on IoT networks
    - Encryption and authentication: these are the techniques to ensure the confidentiality and integrity of the data and the identity of the devices
    - Access control and authorization: these are the mechanisms to grant or deny permissions to access or modify the data or the devices
