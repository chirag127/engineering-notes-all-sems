## Unit 4 - Network & Communication aspects in IoT

- Network and communication aspects in IoT refer to the technologies and protocols that enable IoT devices to communicate with each other, applications, and services running in the cloud or on the edge.
- IoT devices can use different types of networks and communication methods depending on their requirements, such as range, bandwidth, power consumption, reliability, and security.
- Some of the common network and communication technologies and protocols for IoT are:

  - **Local communications**: These are the methods that IoT devices use to speak with neighboring devices, such as sensors, actuators, gateways, or controllers. Examples of local communication technologies are Bluetooth, Zigbee, Z-Wave, Wi-Fi, and NFC. These technologies operate at the physical and link layers of the OSI model and use different frequencies, modulation schemes, and data rates to transmit and receive data wirelessly. Some of these technologies also define application protocols, such as Bluetooth Low Energy (BLE) GATT, Zigbee Cluster Library, and Wi-Fi Alliance protocols.
  - **Application protocols**: These are the frameworks that define how information content is transported and formatted between IoT devices and cloud or edge services. Examples of application protocols are MQTT, CoAP, HTTP, and AMQP. These protocols operate at the application layer of the OSI model and use different methods, such as request/response, publish/subscribe, or peer-to-peer, to exchange data. Some of these protocols also define data formats, such as JSON, XML, or CBOR, to encode and decode data.
  - **Internet protocols**: These are the protocols that define how IoT devices are logically addressed and how network traffic is routed and delivered between source and destination hosts. Examples of internet protocols are IPv4, IPv6, TCP, and UDP. These protocols operate at the network and transport layers of the OSI model and use different methods, such as IP addressing, packet switching, error control, and congestion control, to ensure reliable and efficient data transmission. IPv6 is commonly adopted for IoT device addressing, as it provides a larger address space and supports features such as auto-configuration, multicast, and security.
  - **Gateways**: These are devices that translate and re-transmit information, typically linking local device networks to the Internet or to other networks. Examples of gateways are routers, bridges, firewalls, and proxies. Gateways can operate at different layers of the OSI model, depending on their functionality and complexity. Gateways can perform tasks such as protocol conversion, data aggregation, security enforcement, and edge computing.
