### Communicating data with H/W units

- In IoT applications, data communication is the process of transferring data between hardware (H/W) units, such as sensors, actuators, gateways, and cloud servers.
- Data communication can be classified into two types: wired and wireless.
- Wired communication uses physical cables or wires to connect H/W units and transmit data. Examples of wired communication protocols are Ethernet, USB, Serial, and I2C.
- Wireless communication uses radio waves or other electromagnetic signals to connect H/W units and transmit data. Examples of wireless communication protocols are Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, Zigbee, and LoRaWAN.
- Data communication can also be categorized into two modes: simplex and duplex.
- Simplex communication is a one-way data transfer from a sender to a receiver. For example, a sensor sending data to a gateway is a simplex communication.
- Duplex communication is a two-way data transfer between a sender and a receiver. For example, a gateway sending data to a cloud server and receiving a response is a duplex communication.
- Data communication can involve different levels of abstraction, such as physical, data link, network, transport, and application. Each level has its own functions and protocols to ensure reliable and efficient data transfer.
- Data communication can face various challenges and issues, such as noise, interference, latency, bandwidth, security, and compatibility. These challenges and issues can affect the quality and performance of data communication and require appropriate solutions and techniques.
