### MAC protocol survey for the notes of the Unit 4 - Network & Communication aspects in IoT in the subject of Internet of Things

- MAC (Medium Access Control) protocols are responsible for coordinating the access of multiple devices to the shared wireless medium in IoT networks.
- MAC protocols can be classified into two categories: contention-based and contention-free.
- Contention-based MAC protocols allow devices to compete for the channel access, which may result in collisions and retransmissions. Examples of contention-based MAC protocols are CSMA/CA, ALOHA, and IEEE 802.11.
- Contention-free MAC protocols assign the channel access to devices in a deterministic way, which avoids collisions and improves the network performance. Examples of contention-free MAC protocols are TDMA, FDMA, CDMA, and IEEE 802.15.4.
- MAC protocols can also be classified into short-range and long-range, depending on the coverage area and the transmission data rate they provide.
- Short-range MAC protocols are suitable for applications that require low power consumption, high data rate, and small coverage area. Examples of short-range MAC protocols are RFID, NFC, Bluetooth, Bluetooth Low Energy, IEEE 802.15.4, Wireless-HART, Z-Wave, Weightless, and IEEE 802.11ah.
- Long-range MAC protocols are suitable for applications that require low data rate, large coverage area, and high scalability. Examples of long-range MAC protocols are NB-IoT, LTE CAT-0, LTE CAT-M, LTE CAT-N, LoRa, and SigFox.
- MAC protocols for IoT face various challenges, such as energy efficiency, scalability, reliability, latency, security, and interoperability.
- MAC protocols for IoT need to adapt to the diverse requirements and characteristics of different IoT applications and devices, such as sensing, actuation, control, monitoring, and communication.
- MAC protocols for IoT need to cope with the dynamic and heterogeneous nature of the wireless environment, such as interference, noise, fading, and mobility.
- MAC protocols for IoT need to be compatible with the existing standards and protocols, such as TCP/IP, IPv6, 6LoWPAN, and CoAP.
