### Arduino Platform Boards Anatomy

- Arduino is an open-source platform that consists of hardware and software components for creating interactive electronic projects.
- Arduino boards are microcontroller-based boards that can be programmed using the Arduino IDE (Integrated Development Environment) or other compatible software.
- Arduino boards have various features and specifications depending on the model, but they generally share some common elements, such as:
  - A USB port for connecting to a computer and uploading sketches (programs).
  - A power jack or connector for supplying external power to the board.
  - A reset button for restarting the board and the sketch.
  - A microcontroller chip that executes the sketch and controls the board's functions.
  - A crystal oscillator that provides a stable clock signal for the microcontroller.
  - A voltage regulator that regulates the input voltage to a suitable level for the board.
  - A set of pins that can be used for input and output of digital or analog signals, as well as communication protocols such as SPI, I2C, UART, etc.
  - A set of LEDs that indicate the board's status, such as power, TX, RX, etc.
- Some examples of Arduino boards are:
  - Arduino Uno: The most popular and widely used Arduino board, based on the ATmega328P microcontroller. It has 14 digital pins, 6 analog pins, a 16 MHz crystal oscillator, a USB port, a power jack, a reset button, and an ICSP header.
  - Arduino Nano: A small and compact Arduino board, based on the ATmega328P microcontroller. It has 14 digital pins, 8 analog pins, a 16 MHz crystal oscillator, a mini-USB port, a reset button, and an ICSP header.
  - Arduino Mega: A large and powerful Arduino board, based on the ATmega2560 microcontroller. It has 54 digital pins, 16 analog pins, a 16 MHz crystal oscillator, a USB port, a power jack, a reset button, an ICSP header, and 4 additional LEDs.
  - Arduino Due: A high-performance Arduino board, based on the SAM3X8E ARM Cortex-M3 microcontroller. It has 54 digital pins, 12 analog pins, a 84 MHz crystal oscillator, a micro-USB port, a power jack, a reset button, an erase button, an ICSP header, and 2 additional LEDs.
  - Arduino MKR1000: A small and powerful Arduino board, based on the SAMD21 Cortex-M0+ microcontroller. It has 8 digital pins, 7 analog pins, a 32.768 kHz crystal oscillator, a micro-USB port, a reset button, an ICSP header, and a built-in WiFi module.
