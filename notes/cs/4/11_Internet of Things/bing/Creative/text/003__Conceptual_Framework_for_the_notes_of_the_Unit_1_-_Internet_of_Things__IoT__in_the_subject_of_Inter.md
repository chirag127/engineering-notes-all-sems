### Conceptual Framework for the notes of the Unit 1 - Internet of Things (IoT) in the subject of Internet of Things

- A conceptual framework is a set of ideas, assumptions, and principles that guide the design, implementation, and evaluation of a system or a phenomenon .
- A conceptual framework for IoT is a way of describing the components, relationships, and functions of the IoT system, as well as the challenges, opportunities, and impacts of IoT on various domains and applications    .
- A conceptual framework for IoT can help to:
  - Provide a common vocabulary and understanding of IoT concepts and terminology
  - Identify the key elements and features of IoT and how they interact and interoperate
  - Clarify the goals, objectives, and benefits of IoT and how they can be measured and evaluated
  - Address the technical, organizational, social, ethical, and legal issues and challenges of IoT and how they can be resolved or mitigated
  - Explore the potential and limitations of IoT and how they can be enhanced or overcome
  - Facilitate the design, development, deployment, and management of IoT solutions and services
  - Support the innovation, adoption, and diffusion of IoT across various sectors and domains
  - Foster the collaboration, communication, and coordination among different stakeholders and actors involved in IoT
- A conceptual framework for IoT can be based on different theoretical perspectives, such as:
  - Resource-based view: IoT resources (such as devices, data, platforms, networks, etc.) enable and empower internal capabilities (such as sensing, processing, communication, etc.) to address external challenges (such as complexity, uncertainty, variability, etc.) and enhance competitiveness (such as efficiency, effectiveness, quality, etc.) 
  - Service-dominant logic: IoT is a service system that co-creates value with customers and other actors through the integration and exchange of resources (such as devices, data, platforms, networks, etc.) and the application of competencies (such as sensing, processing, communication, etc.) 
  - Context-awareness: IoT is a context service network that provides dynamic and personalized e-services based on the context information (such as location, time, activity, preference, etc.) derived from the raw data collected by the IoT devices (such as sensors, actuators, etc.) 
  - OpenFlow: IoT is a virtualized and standardized infrastructure that leverages the OpenFlow technology to enable the flexible and scalable management and control of heterogeneous devices and protocols through a centralized controller 
- A conceptual framework for IoT can have different levels of abstraction and granularity, such as:
  - High-level: IoT is a network of networks that connects physical and virtual things to each other and to the Internet, enabling the collection, processing, and exchange of data and information, and the provision of value-added services and applications 
  - Mid-level: IoT is composed of four layers: the device layer, the network layer, the service layer, and the application layer. The device layer consists of the physical and virtual things that have sensing, actuating, and/or computing capabilities. The network layer consists of the communication and connectivity technologies and protocols that enable the data transmission and routing among the devices. The service layer consists of the platforms and middleware that provide the data storage, processing, and analysis functions and the service orchestration and management functions. The application layer consists of the domain-specific and cross-domain applications and services that utilize the data and information from the service layer to provide value to the users and customers  
  - Low-level: IoT is based on a set of enabling technologies, such as: sensors, actuators, RFID, NFC, Bluetooth, ZigBee, Wi-Fi, 5G, LoRa, MQTT, CoAP, IPv6, 6LoWPAN, cloud computing, edge computing, fog computing, big data, artificial intelligence, machine learning, blockchain, etc.
