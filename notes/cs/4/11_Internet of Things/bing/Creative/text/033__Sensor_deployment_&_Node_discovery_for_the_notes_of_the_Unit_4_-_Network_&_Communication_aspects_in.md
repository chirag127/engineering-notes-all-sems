### Sensor deployment & Node discovery

- Sensor deployment is the process of placing sensor nodes in a specific area or environment to collect data and perform tasks.
- Sensor deployment can be done in different ways, such as deterministic, random, or adaptive, depending on the application and the environment.
- Deterministic deployment is when the sensor nodes are placed in predefined locations, such as on a grid or along a boundary. This method ensures full coverage and connectivity, but may not be feasible or cost-effective in some scenarios.
- Random deployment is when the sensor nodes are scattered randomly in the area of interest, such as by dropping them from an aircraft or a vehicle. This method is easy and scalable, but may result in uneven coverage and connectivity, and may require more nodes to achieve the desired performance.
- Adaptive deployment is when the sensor nodes can adjust their positions or parameters based on the feedback from the environment or the network, such as by using mobile robots or actuators. This method can improve the coverage and connectivity dynamically, but may incur more complexity and overhead.
- Node discovery is the process of identifying and locating the sensor nodes in the network, and establishing communication links among them.
- Node discovery can be done in different ways, such as proactive, reactive, or hybrid, depending on the network size and topology, and the energy and bandwidth constraints.
- Proactive node discovery is when the sensor nodes periodically broadcast or exchange messages to announce their presence and location, and to discover their neighbors. This method ensures fast and accurate node discovery, but may consume more energy and bandwidth, and may cause more collisions and interference.
- Reactive node discovery is when the sensor nodes only send or receive messages when they need to discover or be discovered by other nodes, such as by using a query or a reply mechanism. This method reduces the energy and bandwidth consumption, but may incur more delay and uncertainty in node discovery, and may depend on the availability and reliability of the nodes.
- Hybrid node discovery is when the sensor nodes use a combination of proactive and reactive methods, such as by using a hierarchical or a cluster-based structure, or by adapting the frequency or the duration of the messages based on the network conditions. This method can balance the trade-offs between the proactive and reactive methods, but may require more coordination and synchronization among the nodes.
