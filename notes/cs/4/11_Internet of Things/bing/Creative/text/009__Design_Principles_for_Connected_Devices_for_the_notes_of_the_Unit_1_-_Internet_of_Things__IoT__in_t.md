### Design Principles for Connected Devices

- Connected devices are physical objects that can communicate with other devices or systems through the internet or other networks.
- Connected devices can also be called smart devices, IoT devices, or embedded devices.
- Connected devices can have various functions, such as sensing, processing, actuating, displaying, or interacting with users or the environment.
- Connected devices can enable new applications and services that improve efficiency, convenience, safety, or quality of life.
- Connected devices can also pose challenges and risks, such as security, privacy, interoperability, or reliability.
- To design connected devices, some principles that can be followed are:

  - **User-centric**: The device should meet the needs and expectations of the users, and provide a clear value proposition and user experience.
  - **Context-aware**: The device should adapt to the context of use, such as location, time, activity, or user preferences, and provide relevant and timely information or feedback.
  - **Networked**: The device should be able to connect and communicate with other devices or systems, and leverage the resources and capabilities of the network.
  - **Secure**: The device should protect the data and functionality from unauthorized access or manipulation, and comply with the relevant standards and regulations.
  - **Scalable**: The device should be able to handle the increasing number of users, data, or requests, and maintain the performance and quality of service.
  - **Sustainable**: The device should minimize the environmental impact and resource consumption, and maximize the lifespan and usability of the device.
