### Survey routing protocols for the notes of the Unit 4 - Network & Communication aspects in IoT in the subject of Internet of Things

- Routing protocols are algorithms that determine the best paths for data packets to travel from a source to a destination in a network.
- Routing protocols for Internet of Things (IoT) are designed to meet the specific requirements and challenges of low-power and lossy networks (LLNs) that consist of resource-constrained devices.
- Some of the requirements and challenges of IoT routing protocols are:
  - Scalability: The protocol should be able to handle a large number of devices and data flows in the network.
  - Energy efficiency: The protocol should minimize the energy consumption of the devices and prolong the network lifetime.
  - Reliability: The protocol should ensure the delivery of data packets with minimal loss and delay.
  - Security: The protocol should protect the data and devices from malicious attacks and unauthorized access.
  - Adaptability: The protocol should be able to adapt to the dynamic changes in the network topology, traffic patterns, and environmental conditions.
- Some of the existing IoT routing protocols are:
  - RPL: The Routing Protocol for Low-power and Lossy Networks (RPL) is a standardized protocol by the IETF ROLL working group that supports IPv6 addressing and routing in LLNs. RPL is based on the concept of Destination Oriented Directed Acyclic Graphs (DODAGs), which are tree-like structures that define the routing paths from the devices to a common destination (such as a gateway or a sink node). RPL supports multipoint-to-point (MP2P), point-to-multipoint (P2MP), and point-to-point (P2P) data flows, and uses a set of metrics and constraints to select the optimal paths. RPL also provides mechanisms for loop detection and avoidance, local repair, and security   .
  - CBRP: The Context Based Routing Protocol (CBRP) is a hierarchical protocol that uses the contextual information of the devices (such as location, mobility, battery level, etc.) to form clusters and select cluster heads. CBRP uses a hybrid approach of proactive and reactive routing, where the cluster heads maintain the routing tables for intra-cluster communication, and use on-demand route discovery for inter-cluster communication. CBRP aims to reduce the routing overhead, balance the energy consumption, and improve the network performance .
  - Swarm intelligence based protocols: These are protocols that use the principles of swarm intelligence, which is a collective behavior of decentralized and self-organized systems, such as ants, bees, or birds. Swarm intelligence based protocols use simple local rules and interactions among the devices to achieve global optimization and adaptation. Some examples of these protocols are:
    - Ant Colony Optimization (ACO) based protocols: These are protocols that mimic the behavior of ants in finding the shortest paths to food sources by using pheromone trails. ACO based protocols use artificial pheromones to mark the paths and update them based on the quality and freshness of the paths. ACO based protocols aim to find the shortest and most reliable paths, and avoid congestion and loops .
    - Bee Colony Optimization (BCO) based protocols: These are protocols that mimic the behavior of bees in finding the optimal locations for nectar sources by using waggle dances. BCO based protocols use artificial dances to communicate the information and quality of the paths among the devices. BCO based protocols aim to find the optimal paths, and balance the exploration and exploitation of the paths .
    - Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO) based protocols: These are protocols that mimic the behavior of birds or fish in finding the optimal positions by using velocity and position vectors. PSO based protocols use artificial vectors to update the positions and velocities of the devices based on the best local and global positions. PSO based protocols aim to find the optimal positions, and balance the diversity and convergence of the positions .
