### Wireless Medium Access Issues for the Notes of the Unit 4 - Network & Communication Aspects in IoT in the Subject of Internet of Things

Wireless medium access issues are the challenges and problems that arise when different devices and nodes communicate using a shared wireless channel in the Internet of Things (IoT) applications. Wireless communication is essential for IoT because it enables the connectivity and interoperability of various smart objects and sensors without the need for physical wires. However, wireless communication also introduces some difficulties and limitations for the medium access control (MAC) layer, which is responsible for coordinating the data transmission and reception among the devices and nodes. Some of the wireless medium access issues in IoT are:

- **Half-duplex operation**: This means that the devices and nodes can either send or receive data at a time, but not both simultaneously. This is because the wireless signals are prone to interference and attenuation, and the sender cannot detect the collisions with other transmissions while sending data. Therefore, the MAC layer has to use some mechanisms to avoid or resolve collisions, such as random backoff, carrier sensing, or reservation schemes. These mechanisms may introduce additional overhead and delay, and reduce the network throughput and efficiency.
- **Time-varying channel**: This means that the wireless channel conditions can change dynamically and unpredictably due to various factors, such as mobility, multipath propagation, fading, shadowing, or noise. These factors can affect the signal strength, quality, and reliability of the wireless transmissions, and cause errors, losses, or delays. Therefore, the MAC layer has to adapt to the changing channel conditions and provide some mechanisms to cope with the errors, losses, or delays, such as retransmission, acknowledgment, or error correction schemes. These mechanisms may also introduce additional overhead and delay, and reduce the network throughput and efficiency.
- **Burst channel errors**: This means that the wireless channel can experience periods of high error rates, where a large number of consecutive bits or symbols are corrupted or lost. This can happen due to sudden changes in the channel conditions, such as interference, fading, or noise. These errors can affect the data integrity and reliability of the wireless transmissions, and cause corruption, losses, or delays. Therefore, the MAC layer has to detect and correct the errors, or recover from the losses or delays, using some mechanisms, such as error detection, error correction, or retransmission schemes. These mechanisms may also introduce additional overhead and delay, and reduce the network throughput and efficiency.

These wireless medium access issues pose significant challenges for the design and implementation of the MAC protocols for IoT applications, which require high network performance, low energy consumption, and low latency. Therefore, the MAC protocols for IoT have to consider the characteristics and requirements of the IoT applications, such as the number and type of devices and nodes, the traffic patterns and load, the quality of service and reliability, the security and privacy, and the energy efficiency and lifetime. Some of the existing or proposed MAC protocols for IoT are:

- **IEEE 802.15.4**: This is a standard for low-rate wireless personal area networks (LR-WPANs), which is widely used for IoT applications. It defines the physical and MAC layers for wireless communication among devices and nodes that have low power consumption and low data rates. It uses a slotted or unslotted carrier sense multiple access with collision avoidance (CSMA/CA) scheme for medium access, which avoids collisions by sensing the channel before transmitting and backing off randomly if the channel is busy. It also supports optional features, such as beacon frames, guaranteed time slots, and acknowledgment frames, for synchronization, reservation, and reliability purposes.
- **LoRaWAN**: This is a protocol for low-power wide-area networks (LPWANs), which is designed for IoT applications that require long-range and low-power wireless communication. It defines the MAC layer for wireless communication among devices and nodes that use the LoRa physical layer, which is a modulation technique that enables long-range and low-data-rate transmission using spread spectrum and chirp signals. It uses a pure or hybrid aloha scheme for medium access, which allows devices and nodes to transmit randomly without sensing the channel or coordinating with other devices and nodes. It also supports optional features, such as adaptive data rate, duty cycle, and acknowledgment, for efficiency, fairness, and reliability purposes.
