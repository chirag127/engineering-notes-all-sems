## Unit 3 - Embedded Platforms for IoT

- Embedded platforms are hardware and software systems that provide specific functionality for IoT devices .
- Embedded platforms typically consist of a microcontroller or a microprocessor, memory, input/output interfaces, sensors, actuators, and communication modules.
- Embedded platforms can run various operating systems, such as Linux, Windows, Android, or real-time operating systems (RTOS) that are optimized for low-power, low-memory, and high-performance applications.
- Embedded platforms can also use development frameworks, such as Mbed OS or Amazon FreeRTOS, that provide libraries and tools for simplifying software development and integration of IoT features.
- Embedded platforms can enable edge computing, which is the processing of data at the source or near the source of the data, rather than sending it to the cloud. This can reduce latency, bandwidth, and security risks for IoT applications.
- Embedded platforms can also enable IoT gateways, which are devices that connect and manage multiple IoT devices and sensors, and provide data aggregation, filtering, and analysis.
- Embedded platforms can support various connectivity options for IoT devices, such as Ethernet, Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, Zigbee, LoRa, cellular, or satellite .
- Embedded platforms can offer various security features for IoT devices, such as encryption, authentication, secure boot, secure update, and secure storage .
- Embedded platforms can vary in size, power, performance, and cost, depending on the requirements and constraints of the IoT application.
