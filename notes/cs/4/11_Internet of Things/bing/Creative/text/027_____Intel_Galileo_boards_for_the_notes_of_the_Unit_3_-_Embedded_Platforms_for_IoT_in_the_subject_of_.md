### Intel Galileo boards

- Intel Galileo boards are development boards based on Intel x86 architecture and are designed for the maker and education communities.
- Intel Galileo boards are compatible with Arduino shields designed for the Uno R3, which means they can use the same hardware and software components as Arduino boards.
- Intel Galileo boards are powered by the Intel Quark SoC X1000, a 32-bit Intel Pentium-class system on a chip that runs at 400 MHz and has 16 KB of cache .
- Intel Galileo boards have 256 MB of DDR3 RAM, 8 MB of NOR flash memory, 8 KB of EEPROM, a microSD slot, a mini-PCI Express slot, a 10/100 Ethernet port, a USB host port, a USB client port, and a JTAG header .
- Intel Galileo boards have 14 digital input/output pins, 6 analog input pins, a PWM output pin, an ICSP header, and 4 UARTs .
- Intel Galileo boards can run Linux, Arduino IDE, or Intel XDK IoT Edition as the operating system and development environment .
- Intel Galileo boards can communicate with other devices and sensors using protocols such as I2C, SPI, UART, GPIO, and Ethernet .
- Intel Galileo boards can be used for various Internet of Things applications, such as smart home, wearable, robotics, environmental monitoring, and industrial automation .
- Intel released two versions of Galileo boards, referred to as Gen 1 and Gen 2, which have some minor differences in features and performance .
- Intel discontinued the production of Galileo boards on June 19, 2017, but they are still available from some retailers and online platforms .
