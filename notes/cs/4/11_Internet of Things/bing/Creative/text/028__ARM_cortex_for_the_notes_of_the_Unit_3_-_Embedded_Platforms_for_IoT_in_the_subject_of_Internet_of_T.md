### ARM Cortex for the notes of the Unit 3 - Embedded Platforms for IoT in the subject of Internet of Things

- ARM Cortex is a family of processors designed by ARM for various applications, including IoT and embedded systems.
- ARM Cortex processors are categorized into three series: Cortex-A, Cortex-R, and Cortex-M, based on their performance, power, and features.
- Cortex-A processors are application processors that run complex operating systems like Linux and Android. They are suitable for high-end IoT devices that require high performance, such as smart wearables, gateways, and smart cameras.
- Cortex-R processors are real-time processors that run deterministic and safety-critical applications, such as automotive, industrial, and medical systems. They offer high reliability, low latency, and fault tolerance.
- Cortex-M processors are microcontroller processors that run lightweight operating systems or bare-metal applications. They are ideal for low-power and cost-sensitive IoT devices, such as sensors, actuators, and smart meters.
- ARM Cortex processors support various features and technologies that enable IoT and embedded development, such as:
  - ARM Corstone, a pre-integrated, pre-verified IP subsystem that simplifies the design of SoCs for IoT and embedded devices. It includes essential components, such as memory, interconnect, security, and debug, that are compatible with ARM processors and software.
  - ARM Ethos, a range of micro neural processing units (microNPUs) that accelerate machine learning (ML) inference on IoT and embedded devices. They are designed to work with ARM Cortex-M processors and offer low power consumption, high efficiency, and scalability.
  - ARM Virtual Hardware, a platform that enables software development without hardware, using accurate models of ARM-based SoCs. It allows developers to test and debug their code on virtual devices, including third-party hardware and real-world devices, such as Raspberry Pi.
  - ARM Total Solutions for IoT, a portfolio of solutions that combine specialized processing capabilities with standardized, secure software, and innovative approaches to tooling and development. They target specific IoT use cases and workloads, such as voice recognition, keyword detection, and cloud-native edge devices.
