# Technology behind IoT

The Internet of Things (IoT) is the concept of connecting any device that has an on/off switch to the Internet and to other connected devices. The IoT is a giant network of connected things and people that collect and share data about the way they are used and about the environment around them.

Some of the technologies that are used in IoT systems are:

- **Sensors and actuators**: These are devices that can measure or control physical parameters, such as temperature, humidity, motion, light, sound, pressure, etc. Sensors and actuators are the interface between the physical and digital worlds, and they enable IoT devices to interact with their surroundings.
- **Microcontrollers and processors**: These are the brains of IoT devices, and they are responsible for executing the software code that defines the device's functionality. Microcontrollers and processors can vary in their computing power, memory, and energy consumption, depending on the requirements of the IoT application. Some of the common types of microcontrollers and processors used in IoT devices are CPUs, MCUs, GPUs, security chips, and FPGA.
- **Connectivity**: This is the technology that enables IoT devices to communicate with each other and with the Internet. Connectivity can be wired or wireless, and it can use different protocols and standards, such as GSM cellular networks, Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, Zigbee, LoRaWAN, NB-IoT, eSIM, mesh networks, 5G, and Wi-Fi 6 . Connectivity is essential for IoT devices to send and receive data, and to be remotely controlled or monitored.
- **Cloud computing**: This is the technology that provides scalable and on-demand computing resources and services over the Internet, such as storage, processing, analytics, and machine learning. Cloud computing is often used in IoT systems to store and process the large amounts of data generated by IoT devices, and to provide advanced capabilities, such as artificial intelligence, data visualization, and device management .
- **Edge computing**: This is the technology that increases the computing power at the edges of an IoT network, reducing communication latency and improving response time. Edge computing refers to the use of smart devices, gateways, or servers that can perform some of the data processing and analytics locally, without relying on the cloud. Edge computing can enhance the performance, security, and reliability of IoT systems, especially for applications that require real-time or near-real-time decision making.
