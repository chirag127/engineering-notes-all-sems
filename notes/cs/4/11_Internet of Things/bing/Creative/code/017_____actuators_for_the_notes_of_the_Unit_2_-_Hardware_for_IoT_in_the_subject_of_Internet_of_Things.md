# Actuators for IoT

- An actuator is a device that converts energy into motion. It does this by taking an electrical signal and combining it with an energy source.
- In an IoT system, the actuator can act on data collected by sensors to create an outcome as determined by the chosen settings of the user.
- Actuators enable a physical action based on data that originates with one or more sensors.
- The conversion of sensor data to activity follows this sequence:
  - Sensors detect an event in the physical environment.
  - The sensor data is transmitted to a gateway or cloud platform for processing and analysis.
  - The gateway or cloud platform sends a command to the actuator based on the sensor data and the user's preferences or rules.
  - The actuator executes the command by converting energy into movement and affecting the physical environment.
- Some examples of actuators in IoT are :
  - Servo motors: These are rotary or linear actuators that allow for precise control of angular or linear position, velocity and acceleration. They are commonly used in robotics, automation, and CNC machines.
  - Stepper motors: These are DC motors that move in discrete steps. They are used for applications that require high accuracy, low speed, and high torque, such as 3D printers, scanners, and cameras.
  - DC motors: These are continuous rotation motors that run on direct current. They are used for applications that require high speed, low torque, and variable speed control, such as fans, pumps, and toys.
  - Linear actuators: These are devices that produce linear motion by using a screw, belt, or hydraulic mechanism. They are used for applications that require lifting, pushing, or pulling, such as doors, windows, and valves.
  - Soft actuators: These are devices that produce deformation or bending by using materials that change shape or volume in response to stimuli, such as heat, light, or electricity. They are used for applications that require flexibility, adaptability, and biocompatibility, such as artificial muscles, soft robots, and wearable devices.
