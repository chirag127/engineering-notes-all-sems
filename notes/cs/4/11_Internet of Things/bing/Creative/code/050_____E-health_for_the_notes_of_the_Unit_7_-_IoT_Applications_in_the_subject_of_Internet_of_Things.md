### E-health

E-health is a term that refers to the use of electronic processes and communication technologies to support health and health-related fields. It covers a wide range of applications, such as:

- Telehealth: the delivery of health care services and information remotely via digital devices, such as computers, tablets, smartphones, etc. Telehealth can include telemedicine, teleconsultation, telemonitoring, teleeducation, telecare, etc. Telehealth can improve access, quality, efficiency, and cost-effectiveness of health care, especially for rural and underserved populations.
- Electronic health records (EHRs): the digital storage and exchange of health information among health care providers, patients, and other authorized entities. EHRs can facilitate coordination, continuity, and quality of care, as well as support clinical decision making, research, and public health.
- Mobile health (mHealth): the use of mobile devices and wireless technologies to deliver health care services and information, such as health education, reminders, alerts, diagnostics, treatment, etc. mHealth can empower patients, caregivers, and health workers, as well as improve health outcomes, especially for chronic diseases and low-resource settings.
- Health information systems (HIS): the systems that collect, store, process, analyze, and disseminate health data and information for various purposes, such as health management, planning, evaluation, research, surveillance, etc. HIS can support evidence-based decision making, policy formulation, and health system strengthening.
- E-learning: the use of electronic media and devices to deliver education and training in health and health-related fields, such as medical education, continuing professional development, patient education, etc. E-learning can enhance access, quality, flexibility, and cost-effectiveness of education and training, as well as support lifelong learning and knowledge sharing.

E-health is an emerging and evolving field that has the potential to transform health care delivery and improve health outcomes. However, it also faces many challenges and barriers, such as technical, legal, ethical, social, and cultural issues, as well as the need for adequate infrastructure, standards, policies, and regulations. Therefore, e-health requires a multidisciplinary and collaborative approach that involves various stakeholders, such as health care providers, patients, governments, organizations, researchers, developers, etc.
