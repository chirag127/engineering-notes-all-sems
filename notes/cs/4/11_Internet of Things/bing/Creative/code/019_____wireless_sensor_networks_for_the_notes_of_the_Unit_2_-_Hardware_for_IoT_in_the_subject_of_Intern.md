# Wireless Sensor Networks for IoT

Wireless sensor networks (WSNs) are a subtype of broader IoT networks that consist of wireless interconnected networks of small low-power sensor devices that sense some environmental parameters at regular intervals of time and send them over to some central storage or database. The central storage or database can also be fog or cloud across the Internet. WSNs are used for deploying a large number of wireless sensors that monitor the system, physical and environmental conditions.

Some of the characteristics of WSNs for IoT are:

- They are infrastructure-less, meaning they do not require any pre-existing network or fixed base stations.
- They are self-organizing, meaning they can dynamically adjust their topology and routing according to the changes in the network or the environment.
- They are energy-efficient, meaning they can operate for long periods of time with limited battery power or harvest energy from the environment.
- They are scalable, meaning they can support a large number of sensor nodes with different capabilities and functionalities.
- They are heterogeneous, meaning they can integrate different types of sensors, actuators, and communication protocols.

Some of the applications of WSNs for IoT are:

- Environmental monitoring, such as weather, air quality, soil moisture, wildlife, etc.
- Smart agriculture, such as irrigation, pest control, crop yield, etc.
- Smart cities, such as traffic management, parking, waste management, etc.
- Smart homes, such as security, lighting, heating, etc.
- Smart health, such as vital signs, patient tracking, remote diagnosis, etc.
- Industrial IoT, such as process control, asset management, predictive maintenance, etc. 

Some of the wireless protocols that enable connectivity between sensors are:

- Wi-Fi, which is a widespread and versatile local area network technology that sends information over two primary frequencies, 2.4GHz and 5GHz. Wi-Fi networks can transfer large data packets at relatively fast speeds over medium ranges.
- Bluetooth, which is a short-range wireless technology that operates in the 2.4GHz band. Bluetooth networks can support low-power and low-data-rate applications, such as wearable devices, keyboards, mice, etc.
- Zigbee, which is a low-power and low-data-rate wireless technology that operates in the 2.4GHz band. Zigbee networks can support mesh networking, which allows the nodes to relay data to each other and extend the network coverage.
- LoRa, which is a low-power wide-area network technology that operates in the sub-GHz band. LoRa networks can support long-range and low-data-rate applications, such as smart metering, smart agriculture, etc.
- NB-IoT, which is a narrowband cellular technology that operates in the licensed spectrum. NB-IoT networks can support low-power and low-data-rate applications, such as smart parking, smart lighting, etc.
