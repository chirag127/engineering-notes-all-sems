### Verification Methods

Verification methods are the techniques that are used to check that a software product meets its specifications and requirements. Verification ensures that the software is built correctly, according to the design and standards. Verification is also known as static testing, as it does not involve executing the software.

Some common verification methods in software engineering are:

- **Peer reviews**: This is an informal and easy way of reviewing the documents or the source code of the software with a group of peers, looking for errors, inconsistencies, and improvements. Peer reviews can be done by anyone who has some knowledge of the software, such as developers, testers, managers, or customers. Peer reviews can be done at any stage of the software development life cycle, and can help to detect defects early and improve the quality of the software.
- **Walk-throughs**: This is a formal and systematic way of reviewing the documents or the source code of the software with a group of experts, who follow a predefined agenda and checklist. The purpose of walk-throughs is to find and eliminate errors, ambiguities, and omissions in the software. Walk-throughs can also be used to verify the compliance with standards, regulations, and customer expectations. Walk-throughs are usually led by the author of the software, who explains the logic and functionality of the software to the reviewers, who ask questions and provide feedback.
- **Inspections**: This is a more rigorous and structured way of reviewing the documents or the source code of the software with a group of trained and qualified inspectors, who follow a formal procedure and use specific tools and techniques. The goal of inspections is to identify and remove defects, errors, and deviations from the specifications and requirements of the software. Inspections are usually conducted by an independent team, who report the findings and recommendations to the author of the software, who is responsible for fixing the defects and verifying the corrections.
- **Analysis**: This is a method of verifying the software by using mathematical or logical techniques, such as modeling, simulation, testing, or proof. Analysis can be used to verify the correctness, completeness, consistency, and feasibility of the software. Analysis can also be used to verify the performance, reliability, security, and usability of the software. Analysis can be done manually or with the help of automated tools and software. Analysis can be done at different levels of abstraction, such as requirements, design, or code.
