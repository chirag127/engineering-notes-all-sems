### Configuration Audits

- Configuration audits are a process of verifying that the software product conforms to the specified requirements and design documents .
- Configuration audits are performed on configuration items (CIs), which are the software components, documents, and artifacts that are identified and controlled by configuration management .
- Configuration audits can be classified into two types: Functional Configuration Audit (FCA) and Physical Configuration Audit (PCA) .
  - FCA is a verification of the functional and performance characteristics of the software product against the functional and performance requirements .
  - PCA is a verification of the physical characteristics of the software product against the design and interface specifications .
- Configuration audits are conducted by an independent team of auditors who are not involved in the development or testing of the software product .
- Configuration audits are performed for all releases of the software product, but the level of formality and rigor may vary depending on the project's needs and standards .
- Configuration audits provide benefits such as:
  - Ensuring the quality and reliability of the software product.
  - Detecting and correcting errors and inconsistencies in the software product.
  - Supporting traceability and accountability of the software product.
  - Facilitating change management and configuration control .
  - Providing evidence of compliance with the relevant standards and regulations.
