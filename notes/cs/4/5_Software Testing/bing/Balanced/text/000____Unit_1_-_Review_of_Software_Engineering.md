## Unit 1 - Review of Software Engineering

- Software engineering is the application of engineering principles and practices to the development, operation, and maintenance of software systems.
- Software engineering covers a wide range of activities, such as:
  - Requirements analysis: eliciting, specifying, and validating the needs and constraints of the stakeholders for a software system.
  - Design: creating a high-level structure and architecture of the software system that meets the requirements and follows the design principles and patterns.
  - Implementation: translating the design into executable code using programming languages, tools, and frameworks.
  - Testing: verifying and validating the functionality, quality, and performance of the software system using various types of tests, such as unit, integration, system, and acceptance tests.
  - Deployment: delivering the software system to the intended users or customers, and ensuring its proper installation and configuration.
  - Maintenance: providing support and updates for the software system to fix errors, improve features, and adapt to changing requirements and environments.
  - Evolution: managing the changes and modifications to the software system over its lifecycle, and ensuring its compatibility and consistency with the requirements and design.
- Software engineering also involves other aspects, such as:
  - Project management: planning, organizing, coordinating, and controlling the resources, tasks, risks, and quality of the software project.
  - Process models: defining and following a set of activities, methods, tools, and standards for the software project, such as waterfall, agile, or spiral models.
  - Software quality: ensuring that the software system meets the expectations and needs of the stakeholders, and conforms to the quality attributes, such as functionality, reliability, usability, efficiency, maintainability, and portability.
  - Software metrics: measuring and evaluating the characteristics and outcomes of the software system, such as size, complexity, defects, productivity, and customer satisfaction.
  - Software standards: complying with the norms and guidelines for the software engineering practice, such as IEEE, ISO, or CMMI standards.
  - Software ethics: adhering to the moral and legal principles and responsibilities of the software engineering profession, such as honesty, integrity, accountability, and respect.
