### Exploratory Testing

- Exploratory testing is an approach to software testing that is concisely described as simultaneous learning, test design and test execution.
- Exploratory testing focuses on discovery and relies on the guidance of the individual tester to uncover defects that are not easily covered in the scope of other tests.
- Exploratory testing seeks to find out how the software actually works, and to ask questions about how it will handle difficult and easy cases. The quality of the testing is dependent on the tester's skill of inventing test cases and finding defects.
- Exploratory testing is widely used in Agile models and is all about discovery, investigation and learning. It emphasizes personal freedom and responsibility of the individual tester.
- Exploratory testing is not random testing, but a structured and disciplined activity that uses heuristics and oracles to guide the testing process. Heuristics are rules of thumb that help testers generate test ideas, while oracles are principles or mechanisms that help testers recognize problems.
- Exploratory testing can be performed at any stage of the software development life cycle, but it is especially useful when there is limited or vague specification, when there is a need for quick feedback, when there is a high level of uncertainty or complexity, or when there is a lack of time or resources for more formal testing.
- Exploratory testing can be combined with other testing techniques, such as scripted testing, automated testing, or scenario-based testing, to achieve a balanced and effective testing strategy.
- Exploratory testing can be documented and reported using various methods, such as session-based testing, test charter, mind map, note-taking, screen recording, bug report, or test report.
