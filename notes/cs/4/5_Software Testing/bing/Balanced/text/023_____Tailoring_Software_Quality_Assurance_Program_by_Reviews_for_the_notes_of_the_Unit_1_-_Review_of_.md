### Tailoring Software Quality Assurance Program by Reviews

- Software quality assurance (SQA) is the process of ensuring that a software program meets the quality goals and standards defined by the stakeholders, such as functionality, reliability, usability, security, etc.
- SQA involves various activities and techniques, such as planning, testing, inspection, review, audit, measurement, analysis, improvement, etc.
- Reviews are one of the most important and effective techniques for SQA, as they allow the identification and correction of defects and issues in the software artifacts (such as requirements, design, code, test cases, etc.) before they become costly and risky to fix in later stages of development or operation.
- Reviews can be classified into different types, such as informal reviews, walkthroughs, technical reviews, inspections, etc., depending on the purpose, scope, format, participants, and outcome of the review process.
- Tailoring is the process of adapting and modifying the SQA activities and techniques to suit the specific needs and characteristics of a software project, such as size, complexity, domain, methodology, standards, regulations, etc.
- Tailoring SQA program by reviews means selecting and applying the most appropriate and effective types of reviews for each software artifact and phase of the project, based on the project's quality goals, risks, constraints, and resources.
- Tailoring SQA program by reviews can help to achieve the following benefits:
  - Improve the quality of the software products and processes by detecting and preventing defects and issues early and efficiently.
  - Reduce the cost and time of development and maintenance by avoiding rework, errors, failures, and delays caused by poor quality software.
  - Increase the customer satisfaction and confidence by delivering software that meets or exceeds their expectations and requirements.
  - Comply with the relevant standards and regulations that apply to the software project and its domain, such as ISO, IEEE, CMMI, FDA, etc.
  - Enhance the communication and collaboration among the project stakeholders, such as developers, testers, managers, customers, users, etc., by involving them in the review process and sharing feedback and suggestions.
  - Support the continuous improvement and learning of the software project and organization by collecting and analyzing data and metrics from the review process and identifying areas for improvement and best practices.
