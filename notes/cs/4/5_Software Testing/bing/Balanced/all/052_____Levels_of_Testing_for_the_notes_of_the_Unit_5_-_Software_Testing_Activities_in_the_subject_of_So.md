# Levels of Testing

Levels of testing are the different stages of the software testing process that aim to verify and validate the quality of the software product. The main purpose of levels of testing is to identify and fix defects at different levels of the software development life cycle, before the software is delivered to the end-users.

There are mainly four levels of testing in software testing :

- **Unit testing**: checks if software components are fulfilling functionalities or not. It is the lowest level of testing that involves testing individual methods and functions of the classes, components, or modules used by the software . Unit testing is usually performed by the developers using tools like JUnit, NUnit, etc.
- **Integration testing**: checks the data flow from one module to other modules. It is the level of testing that involves testing the interactions and interfaces between different units or modules of the software . Integration testing is usually performed by the testers using tools like TestNG, Selenium, etc.
- **System testing**: evaluates both functional and non-functional needs for the testing. It is the level of testing that involves testing the software as a whole system, to ensure that it meets the specified requirements and quality standards . System testing is usually performed by the testers using tools like LoadRunner, JMeter, etc.
- **Acceptance testing**: checks the requirements of a specification or contract are met as per its delivery. It is the highest level of testing that involves testing the software by the end-users or customers, to ensure that it satisfies their needs and expectations . Acceptance testing is usually performed by the end-users or customers using tools like UAT, Beta testing, etc.

The diagram also shows that the cost and time of testing increases as the level of testing increases, but the number and severity of defects decreases as the level of testing increases.

The levels of testing are important for ensuring the quality of the software product, as they help to detect and eliminate errors at different stages of the software development life cycle, and to ensure that the software meets the expectations of the end-users or customers.
