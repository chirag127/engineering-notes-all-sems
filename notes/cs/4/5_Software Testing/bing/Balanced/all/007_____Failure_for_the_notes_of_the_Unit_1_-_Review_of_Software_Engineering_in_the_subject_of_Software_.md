# Failure

- Failure is the inability of a software system or component to perform its required functions within specified performance requirements.
- Failure occurs when the software fails to perform in the real environment due to the presence of defects.
- Failure can have various consequences, such as loss of data, functionality, performance, security, usability, or customer satisfaction.
- Failure can also cause damage to hardware, property, or even human lives.
- Failure can be classified into different types, such as:
  - **Functional failure**: The software does not meet the functional requirements or specifications.
  - **Non-functional failure**: The software does not meet the non-functional requirements or quality attributes, such as reliability, efficiency, maintainability, etc.
  - **Process failure**: The software development process is flawed or inefficient, leading to delays, cost overruns, or poor quality.
  - **Human failure**: The software developers, testers, managers, or users make mistakes or errors that affect the software quality or performance.
- Failure can be prevented or reduced by applying various software engineering principles and practices, such as:
  - **Requirements engineering**: The process of eliciting, analyzing, specifying, validating, and managing the software requirements.
  - **Design engineering**: The process of creating a high-level structure and architecture of the software system or component.
  - **Coding engineering**: The process of implementing the software design using a programming language and following coding standards and conventions.
  - **Testing engineering**: The process of verifying and validating the software functionality, quality, and performance using various testing techniques and tools.
  - **Maintenance engineering**: The process of modifying and improving the software system or component after its deployment to fix defects, enhance features, or adapt to changes.
