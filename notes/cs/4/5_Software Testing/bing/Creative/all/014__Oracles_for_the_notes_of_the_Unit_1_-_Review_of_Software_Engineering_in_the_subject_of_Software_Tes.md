### Oracles for the notes of the Unit 1 - Review of Software Engineering in the subject of Software Testing

- An oracle is a mechanism for determining whether a test has passed or failed. 
- It involves comparing the output of the system under test, for a given test-case input, to the output that the oracle determines that the product should have. 
- The oracle problem is the challenge of finding a reliable and feasible way to determine the correct output for a given input. 
- There are different types of oracles, such as specified, derived, implicit, and human.  
- Specified oracles are based on formal specifications, models, or contracts that define the expected behavior of the system.  
- Derived oracles are based on information derived from other artifacts of the system, such as documentation, previous versions, or pseudo-oracles.  
- Implicit oracles are based on general expectations or assumptions about the system, such as consistency, usability, or absence of errors.  
- Human oracles are based on the judgment or intuition of human testers, users, or experts.  
- Oracles can be complete, partial, or probabilistic, depending on how much they can cover the possible outputs and how certain they are about their verdicts.  
- Oracles can also be static or dynamic, depending on whether they are fixed or can change over time. 
- Oracles are essential for software testing, but they are not always available, accurate, or efficient.  
- Some of the challenges and limitations of oracles are:  
  - Abstraction: oracles may not capture all the details or nuances of the system behavior.
  - Complexity: oracles may be too complex or costly to implement or execute.
  - Subjectivity: oracles may depend on human interpretation or opinion, which may vary or be biased.
  - Incompleteness: oracles may not cover all the possible inputs, outputs, or states of the system.
  - Inconsistency: oracles may contradict each other or themselves, or may not match the actual user needs or expectations.
  - Evolution: oracles may become outdated or obsolete as the system or its environment changes.

- A possible mnemonic to remember the types of oracles is: **S**pecify, **D**erive, **I**mply, or **H**umanize. 
- A possible mnemonic to remember the challenges and limitations of oracles is: **A**bstract, **C**omplex, **S**ubjective, **I**ncomplete, **I**nconsistent, or **E**volve.
