### Failure for the notes of the Unit 1 - Review of Software Engineering in the subject of Software Testing

- Failure is the situation when the software does not perform as expected or required, and produces incorrect or undesired results .
- Failure is caused by the execution of defects or faults in the software, which are the errors or mistakes in the code or logic .
- Not all defects or faults result in failures, some may stay inactive in the code and never get triggered. For example, defects in dead code or unreachable branches will never cause failures.
- Failure is detected by the end-users or testers when they observe a difference in the behavior or output of the software from the expected or specified requirements .
- Failure can also be caused by other factors, such as environmental conditions, human errors, or malicious attacks, that affect the hardware, firmware, or software of the system.
- Failure can have serious consequences, such as loss of data, system crash, security breach, or customer dissatisfaction.
- Failure can be prevented or reduced by finding and fixing the defects or faults in the software before they are executed, using various techniques such as reviews, inspections, testing, debugging, or verification and validation .
- Failure can be classified into different types, such as functional, non-functional, catastrophic, minor, intermittent, or permanent, depending on the nature, severity, frequency, or duration of the failure.
- A mnemonic to remember the difference between error, defect, fault, and failure is: **EDFF**

  - **E**rror: The mistake made by the developer or programmer in the code or logic
  - **D**efect: The deviation or discrepancy between the actual and expected output or behavior of the software
  - **F**ault: The state or condition that causes the software to fail or malfunction
  - **F**ailure: The incorrect or undesired result or outcome of the software execution
