### Levels of Testing for the notes of the Unit 5 - Software Testing Activities in the subject of Software Testing

Levels of testing are the different stages of the software testing process, where each stage has a specific purpose and scope. Levels of testing help to ensure the quality and functionality of the software product, as well as to verify that it meets the user's expectations and requirements.

There are mainly four levels of testing in software testing :

- **Unit testing**: This level of testing checks if the individual components or modules of the software are working properly and fulfilling their functionalities. Unit testing is usually done by the developers, who write test cases for each method or function of the code. Unit testing is very low-level and close to the source of the application, and it can be automated and run quickly by a continuous integration server.
- **Integration testing**: This level of testing checks if the different components or modules of the software work well together and the data flow between them is correct. Integration testing is usually done by the testers, who use various techniques and tools to combine and test the software units as a group. Integration testing is more complex and time-consuming than unit testing, and it can reveal errors or defects that were not detected at the unit level.
- **System testing**: This level of testing checks if the software system as a whole meets the functional and non-functional requirements and specifications. System testing is usually done by the testers, who use various types of testing methods, such as functional testing, performance testing, security testing, usability testing, etc., to evaluate the system's behavior, reliability, and compatibility. System testing is often the final test to verify that the system is ready for delivery and deployment .
- **Acceptance testing**: This level of testing checks if the software system satisfies the user's or customer's needs and expectations, as well as the contractual or regulatory standards. Acceptance testing is usually done by the user or customer, or by a third-party representative, who use real-world scenarios and data to test the system's functionality and usability. Acceptance testing is the last stage of testing before the system is accepted and released to the market .

A possible mnemonic to remember the four levels of testing is **UIAS** (Unit, Integration, System, Acceptance), which sounds like "use". Alternatively, one can use the acronym **SUIT** (System, Unit, Integration, Testing), which is a word related to software quality.

Some possible learning tricks to understand the levels of testing are:

- Think of the levels of testing as a pyramid, where the base is the unit testing, the middle is the integration testing, the top is the system testing, and the peak is the acceptance testing. The base is the widest and most frequent level of testing, while the peak is the narrowest and least frequent level of testing. The higher the level of testing, the more complex and costly it is, but also the more valuable and reliable it is.
- Think of the levels of testing as a hierarchy, where the unit testing is the lowest and most basic level of testing, the integration testing is the intermediate and more advanced level of testing, the system testing is the highest and most comprehensive level of testing, and the acceptance testing is the optional and additional level of testing. The lower the level of testing, the more focused and isolated it is, while the higher the level of testing, the more holistic and integrated it is. The higher the level of testing, the more dependent it is on the lower levels of testing.
- Think of the levels of testing as a sequence, where the unit testing is the first and most essential level of testing, the integration testing is the second and more important level of testing, the system testing is the third and most crucial level of testing, and the acceptance testing is the fourth and final level of testing. The first level of testing ensures the quality of the individual components, the second level of testing ensures the quality of the interactions between the components, the third level of testing ensures the quality of the entire system, and the fourth level of testing ensures the quality of the user's satisfaction.
