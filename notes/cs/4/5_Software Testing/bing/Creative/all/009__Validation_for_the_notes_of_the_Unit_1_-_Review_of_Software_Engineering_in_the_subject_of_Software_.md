### Validation for the notes of the Unit 1 - Review of Software Engineering in the subject of Software Testing

- Validation is the process of checking whether the software product is up to the mark or in other words, whether the product meets high-level requirements.
- Validation is also known as software quality control.
- Validation is the dynamic testing of the software product, which means it involves executing the software or its components.
- Validation answers the question: Are we building the right product?
- Validation ensures that the software product fulfills its intended purpose and meets the expectations of the stakeholders.
- Validation activities include:
  - Black box testing: Testing the software without knowing its internal structure or logic.
  - White box testing: Testing the software with knowledge of its internal structure or logic.
  - Unit testing: Testing the individual modules or units of the software.
  - Integration testing: Testing the combination of modules or units of the software.
- Validation techniques include:
  - Identification of unstable components or functionalities.
  - Validation focused on error-handling: Checking how the software handles unexpected or invalid inputs or situations.
  - Compliance with software and system requirements: Checking whether the software conforms to the specifications and standards.
- Validation can be performed by the developers, testers, or users of the software, depending on the level of testing and the type of feedback required.
- Validation can be done at different stages of the software development lifecycle, such as during design, implementation, or deployment.
- Validation can help to improve the quality, reliability, usability, and performance of the software product.
- Validation can also help to reduce the cost, time, and effort of software maintenance and rework.

Some mnemonics and learning tricks for validation are:

- V for Validation, V for Verify the Value: Remember that validation is about verifying the value of the software product, whether it meets the high-level requirements and expectations of the stakeholders.
- V for Validation, V for Very Dynamic: Remember that validation is a dynamic testing process, which means it involves executing the software or its components.
- V for Validation, V for Various Techniques: Remember that validation can use various techniques, such as black box testing, white box testing, unit testing, integration testing, etc.
