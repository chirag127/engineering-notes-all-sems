### Walkthrough for the notes of the Unit 1 - Review of Software Engineering in the subject of Software Testing

- Software engineering is the branch of computer science that deals with the design, development, testing, and maintenance of software applications  .
- Software engineers apply engineering principles and knowledge of programming languages to build software solutions for end users.
- Software engineering involves the following activities:
  - Requirements analysis: identifying and defining the needs and expectations of the stakeholders and the users of the software system.
  - Design: creating a blueprint or a model of the software system that specifies its structure, behavior, interfaces, and quality attributes.
  - Implementation: translating the design into executable code using programming languages, tools, and frameworks.
  - Testing: verifying and validating the software system against the requirements and the design, and finding and fixing defects.
  - Deployment: delivering the software system to the end users and making it available for use.
  - Maintenance: providing support and updates for the software system to ensure its reliability, security, and performance.
- Software engineering follows different models or methodologies to manage the software development process, such as:
  - Waterfall model: a sequential and linear approach that divides the software development process into distinct phases, such as requirements, design, implementation, testing, and deployment. Each phase must be completed before moving to the next one, and changes are difficult to accommodate once the phase is done.
  - Agile model: an iterative and incremental approach that delivers software in small and frequent releases, and adapts to changing requirements and feedback. Each release consists of several iterations or sprints, where a cross-functional team works on a subset of features or user stories, and performs all the software engineering activities within a fixed time frame.
  - Spiral model: a risk-driven and evolutionary approach that combines the features of the waterfall and the agile models, and adds a risk analysis phase at each iteration. The software development process starts with a small and simple prototype, and gradually adds more functionality and complexity, while evaluating and mitigating the risks involved.
- Software engineering requires various skills and competencies, such as:
  - Analytical thinking: the ability to understand complex problems and break them down into smaller and simpler components, and to apply logic and reasoning to find optimal solutions.
  - Programming: the ability to write, debug, and optimize code using different programming languages, paradigms, and styles, and to follow coding standards and best practices.
  - Communication: the ability to express ideas and information clearly and effectively, both verbally and in writing, and to collaborate and coordinate with other engineers, stakeholders, and users.
  - Creativity: the ability to generate original and innovative ideas and solutions, and to use imagination and intuition to overcome challenges and limitations.
  - Learning: the ability to acquire new knowledge and skills, and to keep up with the latest trends and technologies in the software engineering field.

- A possible mnemonic to remember the software engineering activities is **RIDE TM** (Requirements, Implementation, Design, Testing, Maintenance, Deployment).
- A possible mnemonic to remember the software engineering models is **WAS** (Waterfall, Agile, Spiral).
- A possible mnemonic to remember the software engineering skills is **APCL** (Analytical, Programming, Communication, Learning).
