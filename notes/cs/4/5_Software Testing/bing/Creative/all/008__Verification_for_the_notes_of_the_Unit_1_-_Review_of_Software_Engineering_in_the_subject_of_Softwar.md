### Verification for the notes of the Unit 1 - Review of Software Engineering in the subject of Software Testing

- Verification is the process of checking that a software system meets specifications and requirements so that it fulfills its intended purpose .
- Verification answers the question: "Are we building the product right?" .
- Verification is done at each stage of the software development lifecycle to ensure that the software product is consistent, complete, and correct .
- Verification methods include code reviews, walkthroughs, inspections, design and specification analysis, static testing, and formal methods  .
- Verification helps to reduce the number of bugs and defects that may show up in later stages, to keep the software aligned with customer and business requirements, and to estimate the potential issues that might arise.
- Verification can be classified into two types: artifact or specification verification and software verification.
  - Artifact or specification verification checks that the documents and specifications are correctly implemented by the system.
  - Software verification checks that the system design and architecture are accurately engineered and free of errors.
- A mnemonic to remember the difference between verification and validation is: "Verification is like reviewing, validation is like testing" .
- A learning trick to understand the concept of verification is to use the analogy of a recipe and a cake.
  - Verification is like checking the recipe before baking the cake, to make sure that it has all the ingredients, measurements, and instructions that are needed to make a good cake.
  - Validation is like tasting the cake after baking it, to see if it meets the expectations of the customer and the baker.
