### Validation for the notes of the Unit 1 - Review of Software Engineering in the subject of Software Testing

- Validation is the process of checking whether the software product meets the user requirements and expectations.
- Validation is also known as verification or acceptance testing, as it verifies the correctness and completeness of the software product.
- Validation is performed after the software product is developed and before it is delivered to the customer or end-user.
- Validation is usually done by the customer or an independent testing team, who use test cases and scenarios based on the user requirements and specifications.
- Validation aims to ensure that the software product satisfies the intended use and purpose, and that it meets the quality standards and criteria agreed upon by the stakeholders.
- Validation can be done at different levels of software development, such as unit testing, integration testing, system testing, and acceptance testing.
- Validation can also be done using different methods and techniques, such as black-box testing, white-box testing, gray-box testing, functional testing, non-functional testing, etc.
- Validation can help to identify and correct defects, errors, and bugs in the software product, and to improve its quality, reliability, usability, and performance.
- Validation can also help to increase the customer satisfaction and confidence in the software product, and to reduce the risks and costs of software failures and maintenance.
