### Exploratory Testing

- Exploratory testing is a type of software testing that involves simultaneous learning, test design, and test execution.
- Exploratory testing is based on the tester's experience, intuition, and creativity, and does not follow a predefined test plan or script.
- Exploratory testing is often performed when there is limited time, documentation, or specification available, or when the tester wants to discover new risks or scenarios that are not covered by formal testing methods.
- Exploratory testing can be seen as a complementary approach to formal testing, as it can uncover defects or issues that are missed by scripted tests, or provide feedback and insights for improving the test design and coverage.
- Exploratory testing can also be used to evaluate the usability, functionality, performance, security, or reliability of a software product or system, by exploring different aspects and features of the software under test.
- Exploratory testing can be performed at any stage of the software development life cycle, but it is especially useful during the early stages, when the software is still evolving and changing rapidly, or during the later stages, when the software is stable and ready for release.
- Exploratory testing can be conducted by individual testers or by teams, using various tools and techniques, such as heuristics, checklists, mind maps, test sessions, test charters, debriefings, or bug reports.
- Exploratory testing can be enhanced by applying some principles and practices, such as:

  - Test with a clear and specific mission or goal, and document the scope, objectives, and results of the testing activity.
  - Test with a curious and critical mindset, and challenge the assumptions and expectations about the software under test.
  - Test with a variety of inputs, actions, scenarios, and perspectives, and explore the boundaries and limits of the software under test.
  - Test with a feedback loop, and use the information and observations from the testing activity to guide the next steps and decisions.
  - Test with a collaborative and communicative approach, and share the findings and learnings with other stakeholders, such as developers, customers, or users.
