### Oracles

- An oracle is a mechanism for determining whether a test has passed or failed.
- An oracle compares the output of the system under test, for a given test-case input, to the expected output.
- An oracle can be a tool, a person, a document, or a specification that defines the correct behavior of the system.
- Oracles are heuristic, meaning they are not perfect and may have errors or limitations.
- Oracles can be classified into different types, such as:
  - Specified oracles: based on formal or informal specifications of the system, such as requirements, design documents, or models.
  - Derived oracles: based on some properties or characteristics of the system, such as invariants, assertions, or contracts.
  - Inferred oracles: based on the behavior of similar or related systems, such as reference implementations, previous versions, or competitors.
  - Consensual oracles: based on the agreement or consensus of experts, users, or stakeholders, such as surveys, reviews, or feedback.
- Oracles are essential for software testing, but they also pose challenges, such as:
  - Oracle problem: the difficulty or impossibility of finding or creating a reliable oracle for a given system or test case.
  - Oracle cost: the time and resources required to obtain, apply, or maintain an oracle for a given system or test case.
  - Oracle uncertainty: the possibility of errors, inconsistencies, or ambiguities in the oracle or its application for a given system or test case.
