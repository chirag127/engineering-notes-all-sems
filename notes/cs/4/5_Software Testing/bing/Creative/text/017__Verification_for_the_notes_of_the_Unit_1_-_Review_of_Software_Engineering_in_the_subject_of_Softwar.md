### Verification for the notes of the Unit 1 - Review of Software Engineering in the subject of Software Testing

- Verification is the process of checking whether the software meets the specified requirements and design specifications.
- Verification is also known as static testing, as it does not involve executing the software.
- Verification can be done by various methods, such as reviews, inspections, walkthroughs, audits, and analysis.
- Verification helps to detect errors and defects in the early stages of software development, and thus reduces the cost and time of fixing them later.
- Verification also ensures that the software conforms to the standards and guidelines of the organization and the customer.
- Verification can be applied to any artifact of software development, such as requirements, design, code, test cases, documentation, etc.
- Verification can be performed by different roles, such as developers, testers, analysts, managers, customers, etc.
- Verification can be measured by various metrics, such as defect density, defect removal efficiency, review coverage, etc.
- Verification can be classified into two types: formal and informal. Formal verification uses mathematical techniques and tools to prove the correctness of the software, while informal verification uses human judgment and experience to check the quality of the software.
