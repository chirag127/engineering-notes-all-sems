### Exploratory Testing

- Exploratory testing is an approach to software testing that is concisely described as simultaneous learning, test design and test execution.
- Exploratory testing focuses on discovery and relies on the guidance of the individual tester to uncover defects that are not easily covered in the scope of other tests.
- Exploratory testing seeks to find out how the software actually works, and to ask questions about how it will handle difficult and easy cases. The quality of the testing is dependent on the tester's skill of inventing test cases and finding defects.
- Exploratory testing is widely used in Agile models and is all about discovery, investigation and learning. It emphasizes personal freedom and responsibility of the individual tester.
- Exploratory testing is not a technique, but rather a mindset or a way of testing that can be applied to any testing technique or method.
- Exploratory testing can be performed at any stage of the software development life cycle, but it is especially useful when there is limited or no documentation, when the requirements are vague or changing, or when there is not enough time for formal testing.
- Exploratory testing can be done by anyone who has a stake in the quality of the software, such as developers, testers, business analysts, customers, or users.
- Exploratory testing can be enhanced by using tools such as test management software, bug tracking software, screen capture software, note-taking software, or mind mapping software.
- Exploratory testing can be documented by using test charters, test notes, test logs, test reports, or test metrics.
- Exploratory testing can be evaluated by using heuristics, oracles, coverage criteria, or feedback mechanisms.
