### Verification for the notes of the Unit 1 - Review of Software Engineering in the subject of Software Testing

- Verification is the process of checking that a software product meets its specifications and requirements without any bugs or gaps    .
- Verification is also known as **static testing** because it does not involve executing the software, but rather reviewing the documents, code, plans, and other artifacts  .
- Verification aims to answer the question: **Are we building the product right?**    .
- Verification activities include inspections, reviews, walkthroughs, and desk-checking   .
- Verification can be performed at any stage of the software development lifecycle, but it is usually done before validation .
- Verification can be done using formal methods, which provide a mathematical guarantee that the software meets its specifications, or using informal methods, which rely on human judgment and experience .
- Verification can help detect errors, inconsistencies, ambiguities, and incompleteness in the software product and its artifacts, and improve its quality, reliability, and maintainability  .
