### Inspection for the notes of the Unit 1 - Review of Software Engineering in the subject of Software Testing

- Inspection is a **peer review** technique that aims to find and correct defects in software work products, such as requirements, design, code, and test plans.
- Inspection is also known as **Fagan inspection** after Michael Fagan, who developed the process in the mid-1970s.
- Inspection has the following benefits for software quality:
  - It can detect defects early in the software development life cycle, reducing the cost and effort of fixing them later.
  - It can improve the consistency and completeness of the work products, ensuring that they meet the standards and specifications.
  - It can enhance the communication and collaboration among the software team members, as well as the stakeholders and customers.
  - It can provide feedback and learning opportunities for the software engineers, helping them improve their skills and practices.
- Inspection involves the following roles:
  - **Author**: The person who created the work product being inspected.
  - **Moderator**: The person who leads and coordinates the inspection process.
  - **Reader**: The person who reads the work product aloud during the inspection meeting, while the other inspectors point out the defects.
  - **Recorder**: The person who documents the defects and their severity during the inspection meeting.
  - **Inspector**: The person who examines the work product and identifies the defects before and during the inspection meeting.
- Inspection follows the following steps:
  - **Planning**: The moderator selects the work product to be inspected, assigns the roles, schedules the meetings, and defines the entry and exit criteria.
  - **Overview meeting**: The author presents the background and context of the work product to the inspectors, and clarifies any questions or doubts.
  - **Preparation**: The inspectors review the work product individually and note down the defects they find, using a checklist or a guideline.
  - **Inspection meeting**: The reader reads the work product section by section, and the inspectors report the defects they find or have found during the preparation. The recorder records the defects and their severity, and the moderator facilitates the discussion and ensures that the meeting follows the rules and agenda.
  - **Rework**: The author revises the work product based on the defects reported and the action plans agreed upon during the inspection meeting.
  - **Follow-up**: The moderator verifies that the rework has been done correctly and that the work product meets the exit criteria. The moderator also collects and analyzes the inspection data, such as the number and types of defects, the effort and time spent, and the defect detection rate.
