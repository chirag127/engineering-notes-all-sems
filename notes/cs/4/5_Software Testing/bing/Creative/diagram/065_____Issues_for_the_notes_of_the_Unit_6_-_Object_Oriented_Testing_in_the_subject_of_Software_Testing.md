### Issues for the notes of the Unit 6 - Object Oriented Testing in the subject of Software Testing

- Object oriented testing (OOT) is a testing approach that is based on the principles of object oriented programming, such as encapsulation, inheritance, and polymorphism.
- OOT faces some challenges and issues that are different from those of traditional testing methods, such as procedural or functional testing.
- Some of the issues in OOT are:

  - **Testing base classes and derived classes**: OOT must ensure that the base classes and the derived classes are tested separately and together, taking into account the effects of inheritance and dynamic binding. Inheritance can introduce new faults or propagate existing faults from the base class to the derived class. Dynamic binding can make it difficult to determine which method will be executed at run time, depending on the type of the object .
  - **Testing abstract classes and interfaces**: OOT must also deal with the testing of abstract classes and interfaces, which are not directly instantiated but provide common behavior or specifications for other classes. Abstract classes and interfaces can have faults in their definitions or implementations, which can affect the classes that inherit or implement them.
  - **Testing encapsulation and information hiding**: OOT must respect the principle of encapsulation and information hiding, which means that the internal details of a class are not exposed to the outside world. This can make it hard to access the internal state or behavior of a class for testing purposes, and may require the use of special techniques, such as reflection, stubs, or drivers .
  - **Testing polymorphism and overloading**: OOT must also cope with the testing of polymorphism and overloading, which are features that allow a class to have multiple methods with the same name but different parameters or behaviors. Polymorphism and overloading can increase the complexity and diversity of the test cases, and may require the use of equivalence partitioning, boundary value analysis, or combinatorial testing.
  - **Testing object interactions and collaborations**: OOT must also consider the testing of object interactions and collaborations, which are the ways that objects communicate and cooperate with each other to achieve a common goal. Object interactions and collaborations can involve messages, events, callbacks, or patterns, and can introduce faults in the communication protocols, the synchronization mechanisms, or the coordination strategies .
  - **Testing object states and behaviors**: OOT must also account for the testing of object states and behaviors, which are the attributes and methods that define the identity and functionality of an object. Object states and behaviors can change over time, depending on the inputs, outputs, and events that the object receives or generates. OOT must ensure that the object states and behaviors are consistent, correct, and complete, and may require the use of state-based testing, scenario-based testing, or behavior-driven testing.
  - **Testing object-oriented design and models**: OOT must also align with the object-oriented design and models, which are the artifacts that describe the structure and behavior of the system using object-oriented concepts and notations, such as the Unified Modeling Language (UML). OOT must ensure that the design and models are consistent, complete, and correct, and that they reflect the requirements and specifications of the system. OOT must also ensure that the test cases are derived from the design and models, and that they cover all the relevant aspects and scenarios of the system .
