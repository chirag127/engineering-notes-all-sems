### Oracles

An oracle is a mechanism for determining whether a software system has passed or failed a test. It is a source of expected outcome for a test case. Oracles can be derived from various sources, including:

1. **Specification-based oracles:** These oracles are derived from the system's specifications or requirements. They compare the system's behavior with its expected behavior as described in the specifications.

2. **Program-based oracles:** These oracles are derived from the system's code. They use the system's internal data structures and algorithms to determine the expected outcome of a test.

3. **Heuristic-based oracles:** These oracles use heuristics or rules of thumb to determine the expected outcome of a test. They are often used when the expected outcome is not precisely defined.

4. **History-based oracles:** These oracles use the system's past behavior to determine the expected outcome of a test. They are often used in regression testing to ensure that the system's behavior has not changed.

5. **User-based oracles:** These oracles rely on the user's judgment to determine the expected outcome of a test. They are often used in usability testing to ensure that the system is easy to use.

Oracles are an essential part of software testing, as they provide a means of determining whether a test has passed or failed. However, they are not always perfect, and there may be situations where the oracle is unable to determine the expected outcome of a test. In such cases, additional testing or analysis may be required to determine the system's behavior.
