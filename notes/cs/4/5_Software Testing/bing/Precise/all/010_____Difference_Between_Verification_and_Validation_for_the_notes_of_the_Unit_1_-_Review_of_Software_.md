### Difference Between Verification and Validation

Verification and validation are two important concepts in software engineering. They are often used together to ensure that a software product meets the requirements and specifications of the user. Here are the key differences between the two:

1. **Definition**: Verification is the process of checking that a product, system, or component meets specified requirements. Validation, on the other hand, is the process of evaluating a system or component during or at the end of the development process to determine whether it satisfies the specified requirements.

2. **Objective**: The objective of verification is to ensure that the product is being built according to the requirements and design specifications. The objective of validation is to ensure that the final product meets the user's needs and expectations.

3. **Timing**: Verification is carried out during the development process, while validation is carried out after the product has been completed.

4. **Methods**: Verification methods include reviews, inspections, and walkthroughs. Validation methods include testing, demonstration, and simulation.

5. **Focus**: Verification focuses on the process of building the product, while validation focuses on the product itself.

In summary, verification and validation are two complementary processes that help ensure that a software product meets the requirements and expectations of the user. Verification ensures that the product is being built correctly, while validation ensures that the right product is being built. Both are important for delivering a high-quality software product.
