# Configuration Audits

Configuration audits are a type of review that is performed to ensure that the configuration of a system, product, or component is in accordance with specified requirements. These audits are typically performed as part of the configuration management process and are an important aspect of ensuring the quality and reliability of a software system.

There are two main types of configuration audits:

1. **Functional Configuration Audit (FCA):** This type of audit focuses on verifying that the functional requirements of a system have been met. This includes ensuring that all required features and capabilities are present and that the system performs as expected.

2. **Physical Configuration Audit (PCA):** This type of audit focuses on verifying that the physical configuration of a system is in accordance with specified requirements. This includes ensuring that all required hardware and software components are present and correctly installed and configured.

Configuration audits are typically performed by a team of auditors who are independent of the development team. The auditors will review the system's documentation, including design specifications, test plans, and user manuals, to ensure that the system meets the specified requirements. They may also perform tests on the system to verify its functionality and performance.

In conclusion, configuration audits are an important part of the software development process, helping to ensure that the final product meets the specified requirements and is of high quality. They are typically performed as part of the configuration management process and are conducted by an independent team of auditors. There are two main types of configuration audits, functional and physical, which focus on verifying the functional and physical aspects of a system, respectively.
