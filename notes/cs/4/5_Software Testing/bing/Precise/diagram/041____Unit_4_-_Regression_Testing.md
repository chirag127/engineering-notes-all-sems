## Unit 4 - Regression Testing

Regression testing is a type of software testing that ensures that previously developed and tested software still performs correctly after it has been changed or interfaced with other software. Changes may include software enhancements, patches, configuration changes, or updates.

The main objectives of regression testing are:

1. To ensure that the changes made to the software do not have unintended consequences on its functionality.
2. To verify that the software still meets its requirements after changes have been made.
3. To catch any new defects introduced by the changes.

Regression testing can be performed at various levels, including unit, integration, system, and acceptance testing. It can be done manually or automated, depending on the complexity and scope of the changes.

Some common techniques used in regression testing include:

- Retest all: This involves retesting the entire application, regardless of the scope of the changes. This approach is time-consuming and may not be practical for large or complex systems.
- Selective retest: This involves retesting only the parts of the application that have been changed or are likely to be affected by the changes. This approach is more efficient but requires a good understanding of the software and its dependencies.
- Test case prioritization: This involves prioritizing test cases based on their importance, likelihood of detecting defects, or other criteria. This approach can help to focus testing efforts on the most critical areas of the software.

In summary, regression testing is an important part of the software development process that helps to ensure the quality and reliability of the software after changes have been made. It can be performed using various techniques and at different levels of testing, depending on the needs of the project.
