### Difference Between Verification and Validation

Verification and validation are two important concepts in software engineering. They are often used together to ensure that a software product meets the requirements and specifications of the user. However, they are two distinct processes with different objectives and methods.

1. **Verification** is the process of checking that a product, system, or component meets specified requirements. It is a static process that involves reviewing documents, conducting inspections, and performing analyses to ensure that the software is being developed according to the specified requirements.

2. **Validation**, on the other hand, is the process of evaluating a system or component during or at the end of the development process to determine whether it satisfies the specified requirements. It is a dynamic process that involves testing the software to ensure that it meets the user's needs and expectations.

3. In summary, verification is concerned with ensuring that the software is being built correctly, while validation is concerned with ensuring that the right software is being built.

4. Verification is typically performed by the development team, while validation is usually performed by the testing team or the end user.

5. Verification is an ongoing process that takes place throughout the development cycle, while validation is typically performed at the end of the development cycle.

6. Verification helps to prevent errors from being introduced into the software, while validation helps to identify and correct errors that may have been introduced during development.

7. Both verification and validation are important for ensuring the quality of a software product. They help to ensure that the software meets the user's needs and expectations, and that it is free from defects and errors.
