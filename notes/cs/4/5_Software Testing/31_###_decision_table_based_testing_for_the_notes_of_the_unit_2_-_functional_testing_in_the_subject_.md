### Decision Table Based Testing for the notes of the Unit 2 - Functional Testing in the subject of Software Testing

Decision Table Based Testing is a functional testing technique used to test the functional requirements of a software system. It is used to represent complex business rules and relationships between inputs and outputs.

The decision table consists of a table with columns for inputs, conditions, and outputs. The inputs are the variables that affect the system's behavior. The conditions are the rules that determine the output based on the inputs. The outputs are the results of the system's behavior.

To create a decision table, the tester first identifies the inputs and outputs of the system. Then, the conditions are created based on the inputs and outputs. The tester then creates a table with columns for the inputs, conditions, and outputs.

The tester then creates test cases by filling in the inputs and conditions in the table. The expected outputs are then calculated based on the conditions. The actual outputs are then compared to the expected outputs to determine if the system is functioning correctly.

Advantages of Decision Table Based Testing include:
- It is a clear and concise way to represent complex business rules.
- It is easy to understand and maintain.
- It is a systematic and organized way to test the functional requirements of a system.

Disadvantages of Decision Table Based Testing include:
- It can be time-consuming to create the decision table.
- It can be difficult to test all possible combinations of inputs and conditions.

In conclusion, Decision Table Based Testing is a useful technique for functional testing of software systems. It is particularly useful for testing complex business rules and relationships between inputs and outputs.
