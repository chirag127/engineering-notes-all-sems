## Unit 10 - Replication

Replication is the process of copying and propagating DNA to produce identical copies of the genetic material. It is a fundamental process necessary for growth, division, and survival of organisms.

- DNA replication occurs during S phase of cell cycle. It enables the transmitted of genetic material from parent cell to daughter cells.
- The process is semi-conservative - each strand of DNA serves as a template for the synthesis of a new complementary strand. This results in two identical double-stranded DNA molecules.
- The key steps involved are:

1. Initiation - Origin recognition complex binds to origin of replication and recruits helicase and other proteins to form pre-replication complex.

2. Unwinding - Helicase unwinds the double-stranded DNA by breaking hydrogen bonds between nitrogenous bases. Single-stranded binding proteins stabilize the single-stranded DNA.

3. Primer synthesis - RNA primase synthesizes short RNA primers necessary for DNA polymerase to initiate synthesis.

4. Elongation - DNA polymerase extends the primers by adding nucleotides one by one complementary to the template strand. Leading strand is synthesized continuously while lagging strand is synthesized in fragments (Okazaki fragments) that are later joined.

5. Termination - Upon collision of replication forks, termination sequences signal the completion of replication.

- Important enzymes include DNA helicase, DNA polymerase, RNA primase, DNA ligase, single-stranded binding proteins, topoisomerases, etc.
- Replication is highly accurate but errors can occur leading to mutations. Proofreading mechanisms and repair pathways minimize errors.
- Replication allows for transmission of genetic information and is fundamental for growth and division. Disruption can lead to genomic instability and diseases like cancer.
