### Introduction for the notes of the Unit 1 - Characterization of Distributed Systems in the subject of DISTRIBUTED SYSTEM

- Distributed systems are systems in which components located on networked computers communicate and coordinate their actions by passing messages.
- The components interact with one another in order to achieve a common goal. Three significant characteristics of distributed systems are:

1. Concurrency: The component processes that form the system execute concurrently and can communicate with one another via message-passing. This introduces complexity arising from coordinating the concurrent execution.
2. Lack of a global clock: There is no assumed synchronization between the local clocks of the different processes. This complicates determining the ordering and time intervals of events.
3. Independent failure: The system continues to operate even when some of its components have failed or are temporarily unavailable. This requires the system to be fault-tolerant and resilient.

- Some advantages of distributed systems are fault tolerance, massive scalability, heterogeneity, and reduced latency.
- Some disadvantages are increased complexity, lack of determinism, reduced performance, and high cost.
- Examples of distributed systems are the Internet, the web, peer-to-peer systems, mobile networks, cloud computing systems, etc.
- Applications of distributed systems include scientific computing, air traffic control, bank transactions, online shopping, etc.
