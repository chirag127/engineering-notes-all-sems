### Issues in Fault Tolerance for the notes of the Unit 7 - Fault Tolerance in the subject of DISTRIBUTED SYSTEM

The following are some of the major issues in achieving fault tolerance in distributed systems:

1. **Cost**: Providing fault tolerance significantly increases the cost of the system. Extra resources have to be allocated to replicate data and services which increases the overall cost.
2. **Complexity**: The algorithms and mechanisms required to achieve fault tolerance add complexity to the system. This makes the system difficult to design, implement, and maintain.
3. **Performance**: The techniques used for fault tolerance have an impact on the performance of the system. Replicating data and services can reduce performance. Mechanisms like checkpointing and logging also have some performance overhead.
4. **Scalability**: As the system scales to larger sizes, it becomes increasingly difficult to provide fault tolerance in a cost-effective and efficient manner. Managing failures and ensuring consistency becomes challenging at scale.

Some tips to remember the issues:

- Think of faults as enemies attacking your system fortress. Providing fault tolerance is like building strong defenses which requires extra resources (cost).
- The more complex the fault tolerance mechanisms (algorithms, procedures), the more difficult it is to implement and manage (complexity).
- Fault tolerance techniques introduce some drag on the performance of the system (performance).
- As the system grows larger, the fortress grows bigger making it harder to defend all parts effectively (scalability).

- Cost: Cash for a good defense
- Complexity: Complicated fortress design and build
- Performance: Soldiers move slower with heavy armor
- Scalability: Harder to defend a larger fortress with the same resources
