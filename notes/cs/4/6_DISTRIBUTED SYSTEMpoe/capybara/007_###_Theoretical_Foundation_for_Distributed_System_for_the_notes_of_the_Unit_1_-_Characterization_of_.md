### Theoretical Foundation for Distributed System for the notes of the Unit 1 - Characterization of Distributed Systems in the subject of DISTRIBUTED SYSTEM

Distributed system refers to a collection of autonomous computers that are connected via a computer network, which enables them to work together as a single system. The theoretical foundation for distributed system deals with the principles that govern the design and implementation of such systems. The following are some of the key concepts that are covered under this topic:

1. **Concurrency**: Concurrency is an essential aspect of distributed systems, which refers to the ability of multiple processes to execute simultaneously. The concurrency control mechanisms are used to ensure that the processes do not interfere with each other, and the system remains consistent.

2. **Communication**: Communication is the cornerstone of distributed systems, which involves the exchange of messages between different processes. In distributed systems, the communication can be achieved through various mechanisms such as sockets, message queues, and remote procedure calls.

3. **Consistency**: Consistency is a crucial aspect of distributed systems, which refers to the need to ensure that all the replicas of a particular data item are updated simultaneously. The consistency models such as strong consistency and eventual consistency are used to ensure that the system remains consistent even in the presence of failures.

4. **Fault-tolerance**: Fault-tolerance is an essential aspect of distributed systems, which refers to the ability of the system to continue functioning even in the presence of failures. The fault-tolerance mechanisms such as redundancy, replication, and checkpointing are used to ensure that the system remains available even in the event of failures.

5. **Scalability**: Scalability is an important aspect of distributed systems, which refers to the ability of the system to handle increasing workloads. The scalability mechanisms such as load balancing and partitioning are used to ensure that the system can handle increasing workloads without any degradation in performance.

Mnemonics and learning tricks: 
1. C3FS2 - Concurrency, Communication, Consistency, Fault-tolerance, Scalability are the five essential features of distributed systems. 
2. Think of distributed systems as a colony of ants, where each ant represents a process in the system, and the colony works as a single system.
