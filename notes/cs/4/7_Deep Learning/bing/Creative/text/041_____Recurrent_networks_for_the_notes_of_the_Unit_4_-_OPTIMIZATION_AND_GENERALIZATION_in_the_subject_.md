### Recurrent networks

- Recurrent networks are a type of artificial neural networks that can process sequential data or time series data, such as natural language, speech, or video .
- Recurrent networks have a "memory" that allows them to store information from previous inputs and use it to influence the current input and output .
- Recurrent networks can be unfolded in time to form a deep network with multiple layers, each corresponding to a time step .
- Recurrent networks can be trained using backpropagation through time (BPTT), which is a variant of the standard backpropagation algorithm that takes into account the temporal dependencies between inputs and outputs .
- Recurrent networks can suffer from the vanishing or exploding gradient problem, which means that the gradients can become very small or very large as they propagate through time, making learning difficult or unstable .
- Recurrent networks can be improved by using different architectures or variants, such as long short-term memory (LSTM), gated recurrent unit (GRU), bidirectional recurrent neural network (BRNN), or echo state network (ESN) .
- Recurrent networks can achieve state-of-the-art performance on a range of challenging problems that involve sequential data, such as language translation, natural language processing, speech recognition, and image captioning  .
