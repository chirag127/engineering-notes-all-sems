### Computational & Artificial Neuroscience

- Computational neuroscience is a field of study that seeks to understand how the brain works by using mathematical models, simulations, and computer simulations .
- Artificial neuroscience is a field of study that seeks to understand how artificial neural networks, which are inspired by the structure and function of the brain, can perform cognitive tasks .
- Computational and artificial neuroscience are closely related and mutually beneficial fields, as they can both inform and test each other's hypotheses and methods .
- Some of the topics and applications of computational and artificial neuroscience are:
  - How the brain performs perceptual, cognitive, and motor tasks, such as vision, language, memory, decision making, and motor control .
  - How the brain learns from data and adapts to changing environments, such as reinforcement learning, unsupervised learning, and deep learning .
  - How the brain encodes and processes information, such as neural coding, neural dynamics, neural plasticity, and neural computation .
  - How the brain can be interfaced with artificial devices, such as brain-computer interfaces, neural prosthetics, and neurofeedback .
  - How the brain can inspire new algorithms and architectures for artificial intelligence, such as biologically plausible neural networks, spiking neural networks, and neuromorphic computing .
  - How the brain can be modeled and simulated at different levels of abstraction and complexity, such as single neurons, neural circuits, neural systems, and cognitive architectures .
- Some of the challenges and open questions of computational and artificial neuroscience are:
  - How to integrate data and models from different levels of analysis, such as molecular, cellular, circuit, system, and cognitive .
  - How to validate and test computational models against empirical data and behavioral outcomes .
  - How to deal with the complexity, variability, and uncertainty of neural data and neural systems .
  - How to bridge the gap between artificial neural networks and biological neural networks, such as understanding the differences and similarities in their learning mechanisms, representations, and functions .
  - How to understand the neural basis of higher cognitive functions, such as consciousness, creativity, and intelligence  .
