### Linear (PCA, LDA) and manifolds

- Linear dimensionality reduction methods aim to find a low-dimensional subspace that preserves some properties of the original high-dimensional data.
- Principal Component Analysis (PCA) and Linear Discriminant Analysis (LDA) are two popular linear dimensionality reduction methods.
- PCA tries to find the subspace that maximizes the variance of the projected data, while LDA tries to find the subspace that maximizes the class separability of the projected data.
- PCA and LDA can be formulated as eigenvalue problems, where the eigenvectors of some matrix correspond to the directions of the subspace, and the eigenvalues correspond to the importance of each direction.
- PCA and LDA are useful for data visualization, noise reduction, feature extraction, and classification.
- However, PCA and LDA have some limitations, such as:
  - They are sensitive to outliers and scaling of the data.
  - They assume that the data is linearly distributed, which may not be true for complex data such as images, text, or speech.
  - They may not capture the intrinsic structure or manifold of the data, which is the lower-dimensional surface that the data lies on or near.
- A manifold is a mathematical concept that generalizes the idea of a curve or a surface to higher dimensions. For example, a sphere is a two-dimensional manifold embedded in three-dimensional space.
- Manifold learning methods aim to find a low-dimensional representation of the data that preserves the manifold structure, such as the local distances, angles, or neighborhoods of the data points.
- Manifold learning methods can be divided into two categories: linear and nonlinear.
- Linear manifold learning methods assume that the data lies on or near a linear subspace, and they try to find the best linear approximation of the manifold. Examples of linear manifold learning methods are PCA, LDA, and Multidimensional Scaling (MDS).
- Nonlinear manifold learning methods do not assume that the data lies on or near a linear subspace, and they try to find a nonlinear mapping that preserves the manifold structure. Examples of nonlinear manifold learning methods are Isomap, Locally Linear Embedding (LLE), Laplacian Eigenmaps, and t-distributed Stochastic Neighbor Embedding (t-SNE).
- Manifold learning methods are useful for data visualization, dimensionality reduction, clustering, and classification.
- However, manifold learning methods also have some challenges, such as:
  - They may be computationally expensive, especially for large-scale data.
  - They may require tuning of some parameters, such as the number of neighbors, the kernel function, or the perplexity.
  - They may not have a unique or stable solution, and they may depend on the initialization or the random seed.
  - They may not preserve the global structure or the labels of the data, and they may introduce distortions or artifacts in the low-dimensional representation.
