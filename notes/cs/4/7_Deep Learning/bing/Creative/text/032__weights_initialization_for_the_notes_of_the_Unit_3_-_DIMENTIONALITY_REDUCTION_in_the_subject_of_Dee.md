### Weights Initialization

- Weights initialization is the process of assigning initial values to the parameters of a neural network before training.
- It is important to choose appropriate initial values for the weights, as they can affect the speed of convergence, the quality of the local minima, and the generalization performance of the network.
- There are different strategies for weights initialization, such as random, zero, constant, Xavier, He, and orthogonal initialization.
- Random initialization assigns random values to the weights, usually from a uniform or normal distribution. This can help to break the symmetry of the network and avoid redundant features. However, the scale of the random values can have a significant impact on the network's behavior. If the values are too large, they can cause gradient explosion or saturation of the activation functions. If the values are too small, they can cause gradient vanishing or slow learning.
- Zero initialization assigns zero values to all the weights. This can simplify the computation, but it also leads to a symmetric network where all the neurons in a layer perform the same computation and have the same gradient. This can prevent the network from learning meaningful features and reduce its expressive power.
- Constant initialization assigns a fixed value to all the weights, usually a small positive or negative number. This can also break the symmetry of the network, but it can also introduce a bias in the network's output. Moreover, the constant value may not be optimal for the network's architecture or the data distribution.
- Xavier initialization assigns values to the weights according to a formula that depends on the number of input and output units of each layer. The formula aims to preserve the variance of the input and output signals across the layers, and to avoid gradient explosion or vanishing. Xavier initialization works well for networks with linear or sigmoid activation functions, but it can cause problems for networks with rectified linear units (ReLU) or other non-linear activation functions.
- He initialization is a modification of Xavier initialization that adapts to the use of ReLU activation functions. It assigns values to the weights according to a formula that only depends on the number of input units of each layer. The formula aims to preserve the variance of the input signals across the layers, and to avoid gradient explosion or vanishing. He initialization works well for networks with ReLU activation functions, but it can cause problems for networks with other activation functions.
- Orthogonal initialization assigns values to the weights such that the weight matrix of each layer is orthogonal. This means that the columns or rows of the matrix are mutually perpendicular and have unit norm. Orthogonal initialization can help to preserve the orthogonality of the input and output signals across the layers, and to avoid gradient explosion or vanishing. Orthogonal initialization works well for networks with linear or tanh activation functions, but it can cause problems for networks with other activation functions.
