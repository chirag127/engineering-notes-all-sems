### Deep Vs Shallow Networks

- Deep networks are neural networks that have multiple hidden layers between the input and output layers, while shallow networks have only one hidden layer or none at all.
- Both deep and shallow networks are capable of approximating any function, but deep networks can do so with much less computation and parameters than shallow networks for the same level of accuracy .
- Deep networks are able to create deep representations, meaning that at every layer, the network learns a new, more abstract representation of the input that captures its essential features and discards irrelevant details .
- Deep networks can exploit different notions of sparsity of a function class, such as the number of active neurons, the number of nonzero weights, or the number of significant Fourier coefficients, that can reduce the complexity required for approximation and learning.
- Deep networks can also benefit from hierarchical architectures that mimic the structure of natural data, such as images, speech, or text, and leverage prior knowledge and invariances .
- Shallow networks, on the other hand, may suffer from the curse of dimensionality, meaning that they need exponentially more parameters and samples to approximate high-dimensional functions.
- Shallow networks may also have difficulty in learning complex and nonlinear patterns that require multiple levels of abstraction and composition.
- Shallow networks may be more suitable for simple and smooth functions that do not have much structure or variability.
