### Convolutional Networks

- A convolutional network (CNN) is a type of artificial neural network (ANN) that is most commonly applied to analyze visual imagery. 
- CNNs are also known as shift invariant or space invariant artificial neural networks (SIANN), based on the shared-weight architecture of the convolution kernels or filters that slide along input features and provide translation-equivariant responses known as feature maps. 
- CNNs are regularized versions of multilayer perceptrons (MLPs), which are fully connected networks that are prone to overfitting data. 
- CNNs can also be used for other AI tasks, such as natural language processing, recommender systems, medical image analysis, and financial time series.  
- CNNs are very good at picking up on patterns in the input data, such as lines, gradients, circles, or even eyes and faces. They can operate directly on raw data and do not need any preprocessing. 
- CNNs are composed of multiple layers, each one capable of recognizing more sophisticated features. The layers are typically convolutional, activation, and pooling layers, followed by a fully connected layer at the end. 
- The convolutional layer applies a set of filters to the input data, producing a set of feature maps that highlight the presence of certain features. The activation layer applies a nonlinear function to the feature maps, such as ReLU or tanh, to introduce nonlinearity and sparsity. The pooling layer reduces the size and complexity of the feature maps, such as by taking the maximum or average value in a region. The fully connected layer connects all the neurons from the previous layer to the output layer, such as for classification or regression. 
- An example of a simple CNN is the LeNet-5, published by Yann LeCun in 1998, which can recognize handwritten digits. It has five layers: two convolutional, two pooling, and one fully connected. 
- CNNs are inspired by the structure of the human visual cortex, where a series of layers process an incoming image and identify progressively more complex features. 
- CNNs are trained using backpropagation and gradient descent, similar to other ANNs. However, CNNs have fewer parameters than MLPs, due to the shared weights and the reduced connectivity. This makes them more efficient and less prone to overfitting.
