### Backpropagation and regularization for the notes of the Unit 2 - DEEP NETWORKS in the subject of Deep Learning

- Backpropagation is a technique for computing the gradients of the loss function with respect to the weights and biases of a deep neural network. It is based on the chain rule of calculus, which allows us to propagate the errors from the output layer to the hidden layers and the input layer.
- Backpropagation consists of two phases: forward propagation and backward propagation. In the forward propagation phase, the network computes the output for a given input and compares it with the desired output (the target or label). The difference between the output and the target is the loss or error. In the backward propagation phase, the network computes the partial derivatives of the loss with respect to each weight and bias, starting from the output layer and moving backwards to the input layer. These partial derivatives are also called the gradients or the sensitivities.
- The gradients are used to update the weights and biases of the network using a learning rule, such as gradient descent or stochastic gradient descent. The learning rule determines how much the weights and biases are changed in the opposite direction of the gradients, to minimize the loss. The amount of change is controlled by a parameter called the learning rate, which is usually a small positive number. The learning rate determines how fast or slow the network learns from the data.
- Regularization is a technique for preventing overfitting, which is a common problem in deep learning. Overfitting occurs when the network learns the noise or the specific details of the training data, rather than the general patterns or features. This reduces the network's ability to generalize to new or unseen data, and leads to poor performance on the test or validation data.
- Regularization aims to reduce the complexity or the capacity of the network, by adding some constraints or penalties to the loss function or the weights and biases. This prevents the network from memorizing the training data, and encourages it to learn simpler or more robust features. Some common regularization techniques are:
  - L1 and L2 regularization: These techniques add a term to the loss function that is proportional to the sum of the absolute values (L1) or the squares (L2) of the weights and biases. This penalizes large weights and biases, and encourages the network to use fewer or smaller parameters. L1 regularization can also induce sparsity, which means some of the weights and biases become zero, effectively removing some connections or neurons from the network. L2 regularization can also prevent exploding gradients, which are very large gradients that can cause numerical instability or divergence.
  - Dropout: This technique randomly drops out or deactivates some of the neurons or connections in the network during the training phase, with a certain probability. This creates a different network for each training example, and reduces the co-dependence or co-adaptation of the neurons. This also effectively reduces the size or the capacity of the network, and prevents overfitting. Dropout can also be seen as a form of ensemble learning, where multiple networks are trained and averaged to produce the final output.
  - Batch normalization: This technique normalizes the inputs or the outputs of each layer in the network, by subtracting the mean and dividing by the standard deviation. This reduces the internal covariate shift, which is the change in the distribution of the inputs or the outputs of each layer due to the updates of the previous layers. This also improves the stability and the speed of the training process, and allows the network to use higher learning rates or deeper architectures. Batch normalization can also act as a regularizer, by adding some noise or randomness to the inputs or the outputs of each layer.
