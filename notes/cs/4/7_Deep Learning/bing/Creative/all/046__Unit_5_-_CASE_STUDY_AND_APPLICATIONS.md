## Unit 5 - CASE STUDY AND APPLICATIONS

- A case study is a research method that involves an in-depth, detailed examination of a specific case or phenomenon within its real-world context.
- A case study can be used to explore, describe, explain, or evaluate a topic of interest, such as a person, organization, event, policy, or problem.
- A case study can be based on various sources of evidence, such as documents, interviews, observations, artifacts, or surveys.
- A case study can be conducted using different designs, such as single-case or multiple-case, holistic or embedded, descriptive or explanatory, or intrinsic or instrumental.
- A case study can be applied to various fields of study, such as social sciences, education, business, health, law, or engineering.
- A case study can have various benefits and limitations, depending on the research question, purpose, and context.

Some benefits of case studies are:

  - They can provide rich and detailed information about a complex and unique case that cannot be captured by other methods.
  - They can allow for a holistic and comprehensive understanding of a case and its interactions with its environment.
  - They can generate new insights, hypotheses, or theories that can be tested or refined by further research.
  - They can illustrate or challenge existing concepts, frameworks, or models that can be applied or modified for other cases.
  - They can enhance the validity and credibility of the findings by using multiple sources of evidence and triangulation.

Some limitations of case studies are:

  - They can be time-consuming, costly, and labor-intensive to conduct and analyze, especially for multiple-case designs.
  - They can be influenced by the researcher's biases, perspectives, or interpretations, which can affect the objectivity and generalizability of the findings.
  - They can be difficult to replicate or compare with other cases, due to the uniqueness and specificity of each case and its context.
  - They can be challenged by the lack of clear definitions, guidelines, or standards for designing, conducting, and reporting case studies.
  - They can face ethical issues, such as confidentiality, anonymity, or consent, when dealing with sensitive or personal data.

Some examples of case studies and their applications are:

  - A case study of Amazon.com, using the competitive forces and value chain models, to analyze its business strategy, strengths, weaknesses, opportunities, and threats.
  - A case study of Athlete's Warehouse, using the SWOT analysis, to evaluate its potential for expansion, market share, customer satisfaction, and profitability.
  - A case study of a health and social care organization, using the PESTLE analysis, to assess its external factors, such as political, economic, social, technological, legal, and environmental influences.
  - A case study of a patient with bleeding issues, using the ICD-10-CM codebook, to assign codes to his diagnosis, symptoms, and treatment.
  - A case study of a solar core, using the Wikipedia article, to describe its structure, temperature, density, composition, and function.
