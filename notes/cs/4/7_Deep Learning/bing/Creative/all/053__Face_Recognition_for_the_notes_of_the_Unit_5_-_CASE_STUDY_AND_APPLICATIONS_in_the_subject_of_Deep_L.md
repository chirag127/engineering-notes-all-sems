### Face Recognition for the notes of the Unit 5 - CASE STUDY AND APPLICATIONS in the subject of Deep Learning

- Face recognition is the problem of identifying or verifying people in a photograph or video by their face.
- Face recognition is a broad problem that can be applied to various scenarios, such as face authentication, face verification, face identification, face tagging, face clustering, etc.
- Face recognition is a challenging problem due to the variations in pose, illumination, expression, occlusion, aging, and other factors that affect the appearance of faces.
- Face recognition is a process comprised of four main steps: face detection, face alignment, feature extraction, and recognition task.
  - Face detection is the task of locating and cropping the faces in an image or video.
  - Face alignment is the task of transforming the faces to a canonical pose and scale.
  - Feature extraction is the task of extracting a compact and discriminative representation of the faces.
  - Recognition task is the task of comparing the features of different faces and making a decision based on a predefined criterion.
- Deep learning, in particular the deep convolutional neural networks (CNN), has received increasing interest in face recognition, and several deep learning methods have been proposed.
- Deep learning technology has reshaped the research landscape of face recognition since 2014, launched by the breakthroughs of DeepFace and DeepID methods.
- Deep learning models can leverage very large datasets of faces and learn rich and compact representations of faces, allowing modern models to first perform as-well and later to outperform the face recognition capabilities of humans.
- Deep learning models for face recognition can be categorized into different network architectures and loss functions.
  - Network architectures are the design choices of the CNN layers, such as the number, type, size, and connection of the layers.
  - Loss functions are the objective functions that measure the error between the predicted and the true labels of the faces.
  - Some examples of network architectures are VGGFace, ResNet, Inception, MobileNet, etc.
  - Some examples of loss functions are softmax, contrastive, triplet, center, sphereface, etc.
- Deep learning models for face recognition can also be improved by using related face processing methods, such as "one-to-many augmentation" and "many-to-one normalization".
  - One-to-many augmentation is the process of generating more training data from the existing data by applying various transformations, such as rotation, scaling, flipping, cropping, blurring, etc.
  - Many-to-one normalization is the process of reducing the intra-class variations and enhancing the inter-class differences by applying various techniques, such as face frontalization, illumination normalization, expression normalization, etc.
- Deep learning models for face recognition can be applied to various scenes, such as cross-factor, heterogenous, multiple-media and industrial scenes.
  - Cross-factor scene is the scenario where the face recognition model needs to deal with the variations in pose, illumination, expression, occlusion, aging, etc.
  - Heterogenous scene is the scenario where the face recognition model needs to deal with the differences in modality, such as visible, infrared, sketch, etc.
  - Multiple-media scene is the scenario where the face recognition model needs to deal with the variations in quality, resolution, compression, etc.
  - Industrial scene is the scenario where the face recognition model needs to meet the requirements of real-world applications, such as speed, accuracy, robustness, scalability, etc.
