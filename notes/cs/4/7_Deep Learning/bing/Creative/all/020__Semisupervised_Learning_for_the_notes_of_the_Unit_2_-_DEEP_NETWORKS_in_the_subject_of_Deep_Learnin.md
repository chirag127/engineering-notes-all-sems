### Semi-supervised Learning for the notes of the Unit 2 - DEEP NETWORKS in the subject of Deep Learning

- Semi-supervised learning is a learning paradigm that combines labeled and unlabeled data to improve the performance of supervised learning tasks, such as image classification, natural language processing, etc.
- Semi-supervised learning can be useful when the amount of labeled data is scarce or expensive to obtain, but the amount of unlabeled data is abundant and cheap.
- Semi-supervised learning can be seen as a way of leveraging the information from the unlabeled data to regularize, augment, or complement the information from the labeled data.
- There are different approaches to semi-supervised learning, such as self-training, co-training, graph-based methods, generative models, etc. In this note, we will focus on the methods that apply semi-supervised learning to deep neural networks, which are powerful models that can learn complex and high-level features from data.
- Deep neural networks can benefit from semi-supervised learning in several ways, such as:

  - Reducing the risk of overfitting by using unlabeled data as a source of regularization or noise injection.
  - Improving the generalization and robustness of the model by learning from diverse and realistic data distributions.
  - Enhancing the representation and feature extraction capabilities of the model by exploiting the structure and patterns of the unlabeled data.
  - Increasing the scalability and efficiency of the model by reducing the dependency on labeled data and human annotation.

- Some of the dominant semi-supervised learning methods for deep neural networks are:

  - **Ladder networks**: A model that combines supervised and unsupervised learning in a single architecture, consisting of an encoder, a decoder, and a classifier. The encoder maps the input data to a latent representation, the decoder reconstructs the input data from the latent representation, and the classifier predicts the label from the latent representation. The model is trained to minimize the sum of the supervised loss (the cross-entropy between the predicted and true labels) and the unsupervised loss (the mean squared error between the input and reconstructed data). The unsupervised loss acts as a regularizer that forces the model to learn a smooth and invariant latent representation that preserves the information from the input data.
  - **Generative adversarial networks (GANs)**: A model that consists of two competing networks, a generator and a discriminator. The generator tries to produce realistic data samples from a random noise vector, and the discriminator tries to distinguish between the real data samples and the fake ones generated by the generator. The model is trained by alternating the optimization of the generator and the discriminator, such that the generator tries to fool the discriminator and the discriminator tries to catch the generator. GANs can be used for semi-supervised learning by adding a classifier to the discriminator, such that the discriminator not only predicts the real/fake label, but also the class label of the real data samples. The model is trained to minimize the sum of the adversarial loss (the cross-entropy between the predicted and true real/fake labels) and the supervised loss (the cross-entropy between the predicted and true class labels). The adversarial loss acts as a regularizer that forces the model to learn a realistic and diverse data distribution that matches the real data distribution.
  - **Variational autoencoders (VAEs)**: A model that consists of an encoder, a decoder, and a latent variable. The encoder maps the input data to a distribution over the latent variable, the decoder reconstructs the input data from a sample of the latent variable, and the latent variable captures the salient features of the input data. The model is trained to minimize the sum of the reconstruction loss (the negative log-likelihood of the input data given the reconstructed data) and the regularization loss (the Kullback-Leibler divergence between the latent variable distribution and a prior distribution, usually a standard normal distribution). The regularization loss acts as a regularizer that forces the model to learn a smooth and compact latent space that follows the prior distribution. VAEs can be used for semi-supervised learning by adding a classifier to the encoder, such that the encoder not only predicts the latent variable distribution, but also the class label of the input data. The model is trained to minimize the sum of the reconstruction loss, the regularization loss, and the supervised loss (the cross-entropy between the predicted and true class labels). The reconstruction loss and the regularization loss act as regularizers that force the model to learn a meaningful and informative latent representation that preserves the information from the input data.
