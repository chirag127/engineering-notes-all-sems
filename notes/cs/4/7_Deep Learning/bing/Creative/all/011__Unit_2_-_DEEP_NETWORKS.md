## Unit 2 - DEEP NETWORKS

- Deep networks are artificial neural networks (ANNs) with multiple layers of neurons that can learn complex patterns from data.
- Deep networks can be supervised, semi-supervised or unsupervised, depending on the availability and nature of the labels for the training data.
- Deep networks can be applied to various domains such as computer vision, natural language processing, speech recognition, machine translation, bioinformatics, drug design, medical image analysis, climate science, material inspection and board game programs.
- Deep networks can have different architectures such as deep neural networks (DNNs), deep belief networks (DBNs), deep reinforcement learning (DRL), recurrent neural networks (RNNs), convolutional neural networks (CNNs) and transformers.
- Deep networks can have different activation functions, such as sigmoid, tanh, ReLU, softmax, etc., that determine the output of each neuron given its inputs.
- Deep networks can have different optimization methods, such as gradient descent, stochastic gradient descent, momentum, Adam, etc., that update the weights of the network to minimize a loss function.
- Deep networks can have different regularization techniques, such as L2 norm, dropout, batch normalization, etc., that prevent overfitting and improve generalization.
- Deep networks can have different learning rates, which control how fast the weights are updated, and different learning rate schedules, which adjust the learning rate over time.
- Deep networks can have different initialization methods, such as random, Xavier, He, etc., that determine the initial values of the weights before training.
- Deep networks can have different evaluation metrics, such as accuracy, precision, recall, F1-score, etc., that measure the performance of the network on the test data.

Some mnemonics and learning tricks for deep networks are:

- To remember the difference between supervised, semi-supervised and unsupervised learning, think of the labels as teachers: supervised learning has a teacher for every example, semi-supervised learning has a teacher for some examples, and unsupervised learning has no teacher at all.
- To remember the difference between DNNs, DBNs, DRL, RNNs, CNNs and transformers, think of the following analogies: DNNs are like a stack of pancakes, DBNs are like a stack of coins, DRL is like a game of chess, RNNs are like a chain of beads, CNNs are like a grid of pixels, and transformers are like a bunch of magnets.
- To remember the difference between sigmoid, tanh, ReLU and softmax activation functions, think of the following shapes: sigmoid is like an S-curve, tanh is like a squashed S-curve, ReLU is like a ramp, and softmax is like a pie chart.
- To remember the difference between gradient descent, stochastic gradient descent, momentum and Adam optimization methods, think of the following scenarios: gradient descent is like walking down a hill, stochastic gradient descent is like jumping down a hill, momentum is like rolling down a hill, and Adam is like driving down a hill with a GPS.
- To remember the difference between L2 norm, dropout, batch normalization and other regularization techniques, think of the following effects: L2 norm shrinks the weights, dropout turns off some neurons, batch normalization normalizes the inputs, and other techniques add noise or constraints to the network.
- To remember the difference between learning rate and learning rate schedule, think of the following analogy: learning rate is like the speed of a car, and learning rate schedule is like the accelerator or brake pedal.
- To remember the difference between random, Xavier, He and other initialization methods, think of the following distributions: random is like a uniform distribution, Xavier is like a normal distribution with zero mean and small variance, He is like a normal distribution with zero mean and large variance, and other methods are like other distributions or formulas.
- To remember the difference between accuracy, precision, recall, F1-score and other evaluation metrics, think of the following definitions: accuracy is the fraction of correct predictions, precision is the fraction of positive predictions that are true, recall is the fraction of true positives that are predicted, F1-score is the harmonic mean of precision and recall, and other metrics are derived from these or other formulas.
