### History of Deep Learning for the notes of the Unit 2 - DEEP NETWORKS in the subject of Deep Learning

- Deep learning is a branch of machine learning that uses layers of algorithms to process data and imitate the thinking process or develop abstractions.
- Deep learning often uses convolutional neural networks (CNNs) and recurrent neural networks (RNNs) to visually recognize objects and understand human speech.
- Deep learning also uses feature extraction, which is an algorithm that automatically constructs meaningful features of the data for purposes of training, learning, and understanding.
- The history of deep learning can be divided into four main periods:

  - The early period (1943-1969): The first computer model based on the neural networks of the human brain was created by Walter Pitts and Warren McCulloch in 1943. They used a combination of algorithms and mathematics they called threshold logic to mimic the thought process. The first deep learning algorithms were developed by Alexey Ivakhnenko and Valentin Lapa in 1965. They used models with polynomial activation functions that were then analyzed statistically. The first convolutional neural networks were used by Kunihiko Fukushima in 1979. He designed neural networks with multiple pooling and convolutional layers.

  - The first AI winter (1974-1980): The first AI winter was a period of reduced funding and interest in AI and deep learning research, due to the limitations and failures of the existing methods. The impact of this lack of funding limited both DL and AI research. Fortunately, there were individuals who carried on the research without funding.

  - The revival period (1981-1997): The revival period was marked by the development of backpropagation, which is a method of training neural networks by propagating the errors backward through the layers. Backpropagation was first proposed by Paul Werbos in 1974, but it was not widely used until 1985, when David Rumelhart, Geoffrey Hinton, and Ronald Williams published a paper on it. Backpropagation enabled the training of deeper and more complex neural networks, and led to the emergence of new architectures, such as the Hopfield network, the Boltzmann machine, and the long short-term memory (LSTM) network.

  - The second AI winter (1987-1993): The second AI winter was another period of reduced funding and interest in AI and deep learning research, due to the high computational costs and the lack of practical applications of the existing methods. The second AI winter also coincided with the rise of other machine learning methods, such as support vector machines and decision trees, which were more efficient and interpretable than neural networks.

  - The modern period (1998-present): The modern period of deep learning began with the breakthroughs in image recognition and natural language processing, using large-scale datasets and powerful hardware. Some of the milestones of this period include:

    - The LeNet-5 network, developed by Yann LeCun, Leon Bottou, Yoshua Bengio, and Patrick Haffner in 1998, which achieved high accuracy on handwritten digit recognition using CNNs.
    - The AlexNet network, developed by Alex Krizhevsky, Ilya Sutskever, and Geoffrey Hinton in 2012, which won the ImageNet Large Scale Visual Recognition Challenge (ILSVRC) using CNNs with eight layers and 60 million parameters.
    - The Google Brain project, led by Andrew Ng, Jeff Dean, and Greg Corrado in 2012, which used a deep neural network with 16,000 processors to learn features from YouTube videos.
    - The AlphaGo program, developed by DeepMind in 2016, which defeated the world champion of the board game Go using a combination of deep neural networks and reinforcement learning.
    - The Transformer model, developed by Ashish Vaswani, Noam Shazeer, Niki Parmar, Jakob Uszkoreit, Llion Jones, Aidan N. Gomez, Lukasz Kaiser, and Illia Polosukhin in 2017, which introduced the concept of attention mechanisms and self-attention to natural language processing, and enabled the development of powerful language models, such as BERT, GPT, and T5.
