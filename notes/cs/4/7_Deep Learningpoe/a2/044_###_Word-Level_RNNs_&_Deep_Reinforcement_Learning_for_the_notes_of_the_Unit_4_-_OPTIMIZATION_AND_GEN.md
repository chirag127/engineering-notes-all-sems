### Word-Level RNNs & Deep Reinforcement Learning for the notes of the Unit 4 - OPTIMIZATION AND GENERALIZATION in the subject of Deep Learning

- Word-level RNNs: Recurrent Neural Networks can be trained on sequences of words to learn contextualized word representations. They can be used for tasks like next word prediction. Some examples are Char-RNN, Byte-RNN, etc.
- Advantages: Can capture contextual information and long-range dependencies. Help in generating text, machine translation, question answering, etc.
- Disadvantages: Can be slow to train due to sequential nature, can have issues with gradient vanishing/exploding.
- Mnemonics: Think of RNN as a "loop" that passes information forward, so it can use previous words/context to predict next words.

- Deep Reinforcement Learning: Uses reinforcement signals to determine the optimal way to map situations to actions so as to maximize reward. Some examples are Deep Q-Network (DQN), Policy Gradients, etc.
- Advantages: Can solve complex problems, has achieved human-level control in games like DOTA 2 and Starcraft.
- Disadvantages: Sample inefficiency, reliability and interpretation issues.
- Learning tricks: Understand the core Reinforcement Learning loop of observe-act-reward-repeat. Think of the neural network as function approximator for Q/policy. Relate to psychology - "learning from interaction".

- Applications: Used together for dialog systems, machine translation, question answering, etc. RNN can generate sequences of words, DRL can determine optimal strategies/responses. Both are active areas of research in Deep Learning.
