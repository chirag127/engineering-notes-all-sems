## Bioinformatics

- Bioinformatics is the application of computational tools to analyze biological data and make new predictions or discoveries.
- It integrates concepts and methods from biology, computer science, and information technology.
- Some of the core tasks of bioinformatics include:
	- Storing and retrieving biological data.
	- Aligning DNA, RNA, and protein sequences to identify regions of similarity.
	- Predicting the structure and function of biological molecules like proteins.
	- Clustering or classifying samples or genes based on attributes or expression profiles.
	- Identifying candidate drug targets or disease biomarkers.

**Mnemonics:**
- **B**iology + **I**nformatics = **Bioinformatics**
- **C**omputer **S**cience + **B**iology = **C**omputational **B**iology

**Learning Tricks:**
- Relate bioinformatics concepts to daily examples to understand them better, e.g. DNA sequencing is like reading a book, protein folding is like origami, etc.
- Try out some basic bioinformatics tools and analyze your own data to get a hands-on understanding.
- Keep practicing by solving bioinformatics problems and engaging in project-based learning.

**Applications:**
- Genomics - Sequencing and analyzing DNA to understand genetic basis of life and disease.
- Proteomics - Identifying and studying the structure and functions of proteins.
- Drug Discovery - Identifying candidate drug targets and leads.
- Personalized Medicine - Tailoring diagnosis and treatment to individual patients based on their genomic data.
- Agricultural Biology - Sequencing crop genomes and identifying genes related to desirable traits.
- Forensics - Analyzing DNA evidence from crime scenes.

**Advantages:**
- Automation of biological experiments and analyses.
- Handling huge volumes of data efficiently.
- Faster discovery of knowledge through computational modeling and simulations.
- Cross-pollination of ideas from computer science and biology.

**Disadvantages:**
- Require expertise in both biology and computation.
- algorithms and tools can be complex.
- Require powerful computing resources and infrastructure.
- There are gaps between computational predictions and wet-lab validations.
