### Backpropagation

Backpropagation is a method used in training artificial neural networks for propagating the error from the output layers back to the hidden layers. It is commonly used in training deep feedforward neural networks and calculating the gradient of the loss function with respect to the weights.

Some key points about backpropagation are:

- It is efficient as it uses the chain rule to calculate the gradients.
- It requires the activation functions to be differentiable. Commonly used activation functions like sigmoid, tanh, ReLU are differentiable.
- It can get stuck in local minima while training the network.
- It enables deep neural networks to be trained efficiently. Without backpropagation or an equivalent algorithm, training deep networks would be very difficult.

Mnemonics:

- Backprop like a boss - Backpropagation is the boss of training neural networks efficiently.
- Chain rule champion - Backpropagation uses the chain rule to calculate gradients, making it a champion of efficient computation.

Advantages:

- Efficient gradient calculation using chain rule.
- Enables training of deep networks.

Disadvantages:

- Can get stuck in local minima.
- Requires differentiable activation functions.

Applications:

- Training deep neural networks and feedforward networks.
- Used in training models for various applications like computer vision, NLP, speech recognition, etc.
