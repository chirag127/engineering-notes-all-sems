### Convolutional Networks

- Convolutional Networks or ConvNets are a type of Deep Neural Networks that are designed to process data that has a known, grid-like topology. Examples of such data include images, videos, and speech signals.
- They have three main architectural components:
	1. Convolutional layers: Apply filters to the input to extract features and patterns.
	2. Pooling layers: Downsample the features to reduce dimensionality and extract dominant features.
	3. Fully connected layers: Classify the features and make predictions.
- Key characteristics:
	- Local connectivity: Each neuron is only connected to a small region of the input.
	- Shared weights: The same filter is applied across the whole input, leading to translation invariance.
	- Pooling: Allows for robustness to small translations/deformations and reduces dimensionality.

**Mnemonics:**

- "ConvNets have Locally connected layers with Shared weights that Pool features to go Deep"

**Advantages:**

- Hierarchy of increasing abstraction.
- High performance on image/speech tasks.
- Models complex, multivariate dependencies in data.
- Useful for end-to-end learning.

**Applications:**

- Image classification.
- Object detection.
- Semantic segmentation.
- Speech recognition.
- Machine translation.
- Robotics.
- Medical diagnosis.
