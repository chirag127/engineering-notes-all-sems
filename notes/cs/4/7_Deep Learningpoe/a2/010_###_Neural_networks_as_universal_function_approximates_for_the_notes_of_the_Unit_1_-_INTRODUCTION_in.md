### Neural networks as universal function approximates

- Neural networks with at least one hidden layer can approximate any continuous function to arbitrary precision given sufficient parameters and training data. This is known as the universal approximation theorem.
- This means that neural networks are capable of learning complex patterns in large data sets and can be used to solve a wide variety of tasks like classification, regression, etc.
- The key reasons for this capability are:
    - Neural networks can learn complex patterns in data due to their layered structure and weight parameters.
    - They can automatically learn optimal features and abstractions needed for a task from raw input data due to transformation at each layer.
    - The flexibility provided by varying number of layers and nodes allows them to solve a wide range of problems.
- However, in practice, large networks and big data sets are required to achieve good performance which can lead to longer training times and risk of overfitting. Hence, it is important to optimize neural network architectures and hyperparameters for specific tasks.
- Some useful mnemonics/tips for remembering:
    - Think of neural networks as a series of functions (transforms) that convert input to output. More layers/parameters allow approximation of more complex functions.
    - The layered structure and weight parameters act as 'automatic feature learners' - they can learn optimal features needed for a task directly from data.
    - 'With great power comes great responsibility' - the flexibility of neural networks needs to be constrained via optimization to avoid issues like overfitting.
