The underlying principles of parallel and distributed computing are based on the concepts of concurrency, communication, coordination, and consistency. Concurrency refers to the ability of multiple processors or computing devices to execute tasks simultaneously, either on the same or different data. Communication refers to the exchange of information among the processors or devices, either through shared memory or message passing. Coordination refers to the synchronization and scheduling of the tasks and resources among the processors or devices, either through centralized or decentralized mechanisms. Consistency refers to the correctness and reliability of the computation, either through replication, fault tolerance, or error detection and correction.
