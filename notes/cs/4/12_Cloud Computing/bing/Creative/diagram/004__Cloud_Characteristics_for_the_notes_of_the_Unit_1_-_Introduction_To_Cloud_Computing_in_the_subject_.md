According to various sources    , cloud computing has several essential characteristics, such as:

- On-demand self-service: Users can provision and release cloud resources as needed, without requiring human intervention from the cloud provider.
- Broad network access: Cloud resources are accessible over the internet through standard mechanisms and devices, such as laptops, smartphones, tablets, etc.
- Resource pooling: Cloud resources are pooled and shared among multiple users, using a multi-tenant model. The cloud provider dynamically assigns and reassigns resources according to user demand, without regard to the physical location of the resources.
- Rapid elasticity and scalability: Cloud resources can be quickly and easily scaled up or down, depending on the user's needs. Users can access as much or as little resources as they require, and pay only for what they use.
- Measured service: Cloud resources are monitored and measured by the cloud provider, and users are charged accordingly. Users can access reports and dashboards that show the usage and performance of the cloud resources.
- Resiliency and availability: Cloud resources are designed to be resilient and available, even in the event of failures or disasters. The cloud provider ensures that the cloud resources are backed up, replicated, and restored as needed, and that the service level agreements (SLAs) are met.
- Security: Cloud resources are protected by the cloud provider's security measures, such as encryption, authentication, authorization, firewalls, etc. Users can also implement their own security policies and controls, depending on the cloud service model and the cloud provider's offerings.
