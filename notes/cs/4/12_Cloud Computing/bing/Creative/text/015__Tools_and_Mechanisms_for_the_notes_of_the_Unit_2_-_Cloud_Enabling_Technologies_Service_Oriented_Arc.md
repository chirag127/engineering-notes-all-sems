### Tools and Mechanisms for Service Oriented Architecture

Service Oriented Architecture (SOA) is an architectural style for building software applications that use services available in a network such as the web. A service is an implementation of a well-defined business functionality, such as customer lookup, credit card validation, or sensor placement. Services can be composed and reconfigured to support new tasks or missions, and they can be distributed and reused across organizations. The main goals of SOA are to eliminate redundancy, leverage legacy systems, adapt to changing needs, and enable interoperability.

To achieve these goals, SOA requires some tools and mechanisms that facilitate the design, development, deployment, discovery, and invocation of services. Some of the common tools and mechanisms for SOA are:

- **Web Services**: Web services are the preferred standards-based way to realize SOA. Web services are services delivered over the web using technologies such as XML, Web Services Description Language (WSDL), Simple Object Access Protocol (SOAP), and Universal Description, Discovery, and Integration (UDDI). Web services provide a common way of describing, publishing, finding, and invoking services regardless of the platform or language.
- **Enterprise Service Bus (ESB)**: An ESB is a middleware infrastructure that connects service consumers to services. It usually implements a loosely coupled, message-based communication model, but it can also support other mechanisms such as remote procedure calls (RPCs) or web services. An ESB provides features such as routing, transformation, mediation, orchestration, security, and monitoring of service interactions. An ESB can also enable service composition, which is the process of creating new services by combining existing ones.
- **Service Registry and Repository**: A service registry is a database that stores information about the available services, such as their names, locations, interfaces, and policies. A service repository is a database that stores additional metadata about the services, such as their schemas, contracts, documentation, and dependencies. A service registry and repository enable service discovery, which is the process of finding and selecting the appropriate services for a given task or mission.
- **Service Governance**: Service governance is the set of policies, processes, and tools that ensure the quality, consistency, and compliance of services throughout their lifecycle. Service governance covers aspects such as service design, development, testing, deployment, versioning, monitoring, and management. Service governance also involves defining and enforcing service level agreements (SLAs), which are contracts that specify the expected performance, availability, and reliability of services.
- **Service Modeling and Design**: Service modeling and design are the activities that define the requirements, specifications, and architectures of services and service-oriented systems. Service modeling and design involve identifying the business processes and functions that can be implemented as services, and defining their interfaces, behaviors, and interactions. Service modeling and design also involve applying principles and patterns that promote service reusability, modularity, granularity, and interoperability.

These are some of the tools and mechanisms that support SOA, but they are not exhaustive. Depending on the context and the needs of the organization, other tools and mechanisms may be used or developed to facilitate SOA. The main idea is to use the tools and mechanisms that enable the creation and consumption of services that are aligned with the business goals and the technical constraints of the system.
