### Public, Private and Hybrid Clouds

- Cloud computing is the delivery of computing services over the internet, such as servers, storage, databases, networking, software, analytics, and intelligence.
- Cloud computing can be categorized into three general types: public cloud, private cloud, and hybrid cloud.
- Public cloud is cloud computing that’s delivered via the internet and shared across organizations. Examples of public cloud providers include Alibaba Cloud, Amazon Web Services (AWS), Google Cloud, IBM Cloud, and Microsoft Azure.
- Private cloud is cloud computing that is dedicated solely to your organization. It can be hosted on-premises or off-premises by a third-party provider. Private cloud offers more control, security, and customization than public cloud, but also requires more investment and maintenance.
- Hybrid cloud is any environment that uses both public and private clouds. It allows data and applications to move between the two environments, depending on the needs and preferences of the organization. Hybrid cloud offers more flexibility, scalability, and cost-effectiveness than either public or private cloud alone, but also poses more challenges in terms of integration, management, and security.
- Cloud computing services can also be classified into three main types: Infrastructure-as-a-Service (IaaS), Platform-as-a-Service (PaaS), and Software-as-a-Service (SaaS).
- IaaS is the provision of basic computing resources, such as servers, storage, and networking, over the cloud. The customer can rent and use these resources as needed, without having to buy or manage them. Examples of IaaS providers include AWS, Google Cloud, and Microsoft Azure.
- PaaS is the provision of a cloud-based platform that enables the customer to develop, run, and manage applications without having to deal with the underlying infrastructure. The platform provides tools, frameworks, and services for application development, testing, deployment, and management. Examples of PaaS providers include AWS Elastic Beanstalk, Google App Engine, and Microsoft Azure App Service.
- SaaS is the provision of cloud-based software applications that are accessible over the internet. The customer can use these applications without having to install or maintain them. The provider is responsible for the software delivery, updates, and security. Examples of SaaS providers include Google Workspace, Microsoft Office 365, and Salesforce.
