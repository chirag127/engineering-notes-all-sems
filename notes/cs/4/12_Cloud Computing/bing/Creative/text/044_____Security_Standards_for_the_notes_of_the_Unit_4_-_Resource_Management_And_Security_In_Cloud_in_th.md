### Security Standards for Cloud Computing

Security standards are guidelines or best practices that aim to ensure the confidentiality, integrity, and availability of data and resources in cloud computing. Security standards can help cloud service providers and consumers to reduce the risk of security incidents, comply with regulations, and establish trust and confidence in cloud services. Some of the security standards that are relevant for cloud computing are:

- **ISO/IEC 27001/27002**: These are international standards for information security management systems (ISMS) and information security controls. ISO/IEC 27001 specifies the requirements for establishing, implementing, maintaining, and improving an ISMS, while ISO/IEC 27002 provides a code of practice for applying the ISMS. These standards can help cloud service providers and consumers to protect their data and assets from unauthorized access, use, disclosure, modification, or destruction .
- **ISO/IEC 27017**: This is an international standard that provides guidance for cloud security, based on the ISO/IEC 27002 controls. It defines cloud-specific security controls and responsibilities for both cloud service providers and consumers, such as data protection, encryption, access management, incident response, and auditing .
- **ISO/IEC 27018**: This is an international standard that provides guidance for cloud privacy, based on the ISO/IEC 27002 controls. It defines cloud-specific privacy controls and responsibilities for both cloud service providers and consumers, such as data minimization, consent, transparency, accountability, and notification of breaches .
- **General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR)**: This is a European Union regulation that sets the rules for the protection of personal data of individuals in the EU and the European Economic Area (EEA). It applies to any organization that processes personal data of EU/EEA residents, regardless of where the organization or the data is located. It grants data subjects the rights to access, rectify, erase, restrict, port, and object to the processing of their personal data, and imposes obligations on data controllers and processors to ensure data protection by design and by default, conduct data protection impact assessments, appoint data protection officers, and report data breaches.
- **System and Organization Controls (SOC) Reporting**: This is a framework for auditing and reporting on the internal controls of service organizations, such as cloud service providers. It consists of three types of reports: SOC 1, SOC 2, and SOC 3. SOC 1 reports on the controls that affect the financial reporting of the service organization's clients. SOC 2 reports on the controls that affect the security, availability, processing integrity, confidentiality, and privacy of the service organization's services. SOC 3 reports on the same controls as SOC 2, but in a more general and less detailed manner. These reports can help cloud service providers and consumers to verify the effectiveness of the cloud security controls and to meet the compliance requirements of various regulations and standards.
- **Payment Card Industry Data Security Standard (PCI DSS)**: This is a global standard for the security of payment card data, such as credit card and debit card information. It applies to any organization that stores, processes, or transmits payment card data, or that provides services to such organizations. It defines 12 requirements for securing payment card data, such as installing and maintaining firewalls, encrypting data in transit and at rest, restricting access to data, monitoring and testing networks, and reporting and responding to incidents.
- **Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act (HIPAA)**: This is a US federal law that regulates the privacy and security of health information, such as medical records and billing information. It applies to any organization that creates, receives, maintains, or transmits health information, or that provides services to such organizations. It defines the standards and safeguards for protecting health information from unauthorized access, use, disclosure, modification, or destruction, and grants individuals the rights to access, amend, and request an accounting of their health information.
- **CIS AWS Foundations v1.2**: This is a benchmark for securing Amazon Web Services (AWS) cloud environments, based on the best practices and recommendations from the Center for Internet Security (CIS). It defines 49 controls for securing AWS accounts, identity and access management, logging and monitoring, networking, and encryption. It also provides tools and scripts for assessing and implementing the controls.
