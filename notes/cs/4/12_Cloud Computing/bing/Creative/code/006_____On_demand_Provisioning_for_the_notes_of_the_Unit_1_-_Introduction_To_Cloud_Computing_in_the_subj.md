### On-demand Provisioning

- On-demand provisioning is a feature of cloud computing that allows users to access and use cloud resources whenever they need them, without requiring prior planning or reservation.
- On-demand provisioning enables users to scale up or down their cloud resources according to their changing needs and pay only for what they use.
- On-demand provisioning also provides users with flexibility, agility, and efficiency in using cloud services, as they can avoid over-provisioning or under-provisioning of resources, and optimize their performance and cost.
- On-demand provisioning is supported by various cloud service models, such as Infrastructure as a Service (IaaS), Platform as a Service (PaaS), and Software as a Service (SaaS), as well as by different cloud deployment models, such as public, private, hybrid, and community clouds.
- On-demand provisioning relies on the following key components and technologies:

  - Virtualization: the process of creating virtual instances of physical resources, such as servers, storage, and networks, that can be dynamically allocated and released by users.
  - Automation: the process of automating the provisioning and deprovisioning of cloud resources, as well as the configuration and management of cloud services, using predefined policies and rules.
  - Self-service: the process of enabling users to request and access cloud resources and services through a web-based portal or an application programming interface (API), without requiring human intervention or approval.
  - Metering: the process of measuring and recording the usage and consumption of cloud resources and services by users, for billing and reporting purposes.
  - Elasticity: the ability of cloud resources and services to automatically adjust to the changing demand and workload of users, by scaling up or down as needed.
