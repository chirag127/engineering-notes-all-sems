### Cloud Characteristics

Cloud computing is a model for enabling convenient, on-demand network access to a shared pool of configurable computing resources (e.g., networks, servers, storage, applications, and services) that can be rapidly provisioned and released with minimal management effort or service provider interaction.

According to the National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST), cloud computing has five essential characteristics:

- **On-demand self-service**: Users can provision cloud resources such as computing power, storage, network, and software without requiring human intervention from the service provider.
- **Broad network access**: Cloud resources are accessible over the internet or other networks using standard protocols and devices such as laptops, smartphones, tablets, etc.
- **Resource pooling**: Cloud service providers use a multi-tenant model to serve multiple users with the same physical and virtual resources, which are dynamically assigned and reassigned according to user demand. The users generally do not have control or knowledge over the exact location of the resources, but may be able to specify a higher-level location such as a region or a country.
- **Rapid elasticity and scalability**: Cloud resources can be quickly and easily scaled up or down, depending on the user's needs. The users can access as much or as little resources as they want, and pay only for what they use. The cloud can also handle peak loads and sudden spikes in demand by automatically allocating more resources.
- **Measured service**: Cloud service providers monitor and measure the usage of cloud resources by the users, and charge them accordingly. The users can also access reports and dashboards to track their consumption and costs. The cloud services are transparent and accountable, and can be optimized based on the user's feedback and preferences.

Some other characteristics of cloud computing that are often mentioned are  :

- **Automation**: Cloud computing enables the automation of tasks such as installation, configuration, maintenance, backup, recovery, and security of cloud resources and services. This reduces the manual effort and human errors, and improves the efficiency and reliability of the cloud.
- **Availability and reliability**: Cloud computing provides high availability and reliability of cloud resources and services, by using techniques such as redundancy, fault tolerance, backup, disaster recovery, and load balancing. The cloud service providers guarantee a certain level of service level agreement (SLA) to the users, and compensate them in case of any failure or downtime.
- **Security and privacy**: Cloud computing ensures the security and privacy of cloud resources and services, by using methods such as encryption, authentication, authorization, access control, auditing, and compliance. The cloud service providers follow the best practices and standards to protect the data and applications of the users from unauthorized access, modification, or disclosure. The users can also choose the level of security and privacy they want, based on their requirements and regulations.
- **Cost-effectiveness**: Cloud computing reduces the capital and operational expenses of the users, by eliminating the need to purchase, install, maintain, and upgrade hardware and software. The users only pay for the cloud resources and services they use, and can benefit from the economies of scale and the shared infrastructure of the cloud. The cloud also offers flexible and customized pricing models, such as pay-as-you-go, subscription, or freemium.
