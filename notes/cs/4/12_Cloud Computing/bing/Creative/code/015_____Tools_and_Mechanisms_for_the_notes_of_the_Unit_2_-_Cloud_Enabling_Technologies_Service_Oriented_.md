# Tools and Mechanisms for Service Oriented Architecture

Service Oriented Architecture (SOA) is an architectural style that enables the development and integration of software services that are loosely coupled, self-contained, and interoperable  . SOA supports the reuse of existing services to create new applications and workflows, as well as the adaptation and evolution of services to meet changing requirements  . SOA also facilitates the communication and collaboration among different service providers and consumers, as well as the governance and management of service quality and performance  .

Some of the tools and mechanisms that are commonly used in SOA are:

- **Service interface**: A service interface defines the contract or specification of a service, including its inputs, outputs, operations, and policies. A service interface is usually expressed in a standard language, such as Web Services Description Language (WSDL) or OpenAPI, to enable interoperability and discovery of services  .
- **Service registry**: A service registry is a repository that stores and publishes the metadata and descriptions of available services. A service registry enables service discovery and lookup, as well as service governance and monitoring. A service registry can be implemented using standards, such as Universal Description, Discovery and Integration (UDDI) or Service Registry (ebXML Reg/Rep)  .
- **Service bus**: A service bus is a middleware component that provides the communication and integration infrastructure for SOA. A service bus enables the routing, transformation, mediation, and orchestration of messages and events among different services, as well as the enforcement of policies and security. A service bus can be implemented using standards, such as Enterprise Service Bus (ESB) or Service Component Architecture (SCA)  .
- **Service composition**: A service composition is a process or workflow that coordinates and executes multiple services to achieve a complex functionality or goal. A service composition can be implemented using standards, such as Business Process Execution Language (BPEL) or Web Services Choreography Description Language (WS-CDL)  .
- **Service adaptation**: A service adaptation is a mechanism that enables the modification or evolution of a service to meet changing requirements or contexts. A service adaptation can be implemented using techniques, such as service versioning, service substitution, service wrapping, service refactoring, or service reconfiguration.
