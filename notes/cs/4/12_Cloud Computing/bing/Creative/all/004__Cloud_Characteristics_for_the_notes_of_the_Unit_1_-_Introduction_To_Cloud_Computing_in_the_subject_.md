### Cloud Characteristics for the notes of the Unit 1 - Introduction To Cloud Computing in the subject of Cloud Computing

Cloud computing is the on-demand availability of computing resources as services over the internet. It eliminates the need for enterprises to procure, configure, or manage resources themselves, and they only pay for what they use.

There are three types of cloud computing service models:

- Infrastructure as a Service (IaaS): offers compute and storage services, such as virtual machines, databases, and file systems.
- Platform as a Service (PaaS): offers a develop-and-deploy environment to build cloud apps, such as web servers, app engines, and middleware.
- Software as a Service (SaaS): delivers apps as services, such as email, CRM, and office suites.

There are also three different cloud computing deployment models:

- Public cloud: run by third-party cloud service providers, such as AWS, Google Cloud, and Microsoft Azure, who own and manage the cloud infrastructure and offer services to anyone over the internet.
- Private cloud: run by a single organization, who owns and manages the cloud infrastructure and offers services to its own users over a private network.
- Hybrid cloud: a combination of public and private clouds, where some services are run on the public cloud and some on the private cloud, and data and applications can move between them.

Cloud computing has eight key characteristics that differentiate it from traditional, on-premises IT architectures:

- On-demand self-service: users can request and access cloud resources as needed, without human intervention, through a self-service portal or an API call.
- Resource pooling: cloud providers use multi-tenant architectures to serve multiple customers on the same physical or virtual resources, which are dynamically allocated and released according to demand.
- Broad network access: cloud resources are accessible over the internet or a private network, from various devices, such as laptops, smartphones, and tablets.
- Rapid elasticity and scalability: cloud resources can be quickly and easily scaled up or down, depending on the workload, to meet changing demand and performance requirements.
- Pay-per-use pricing: users only pay for the cloud resources they use, based on the amount, duration, and quality of service, without upfront or fixed costs.
- Measured service: cloud providers monitor and measure the usage and performance of cloud resources, and provide reports and billing to the users, based on a predefined metric or SLA.
- Resiliency and availability: cloud providers ensure that cloud resources are reliable and available, by using backup, redundancy, and fault-tolerance techniques, and by offering SLAs with guarantees and compensation for downtime.
- Security: cloud providers protect cloud resources and data from unauthorized access, modification, or disclosure, by using encryption, authentication, authorization, and auditing mechanisms, and by complying with various standards and regulations.
