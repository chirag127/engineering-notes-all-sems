### Resource Provisioning for the notes of the Unit 4 - Resource Management And Security In Cloud in the subject of Cloud Computing

- Resource provisioning in cloud computing is the allocation of a cloud provider's resources and services to a customer.
- Resource provisioning is a key feature of the cloud computing model, as it enables customers to procure cloud services and resources from a cloud provider in a flexible, scalable, and self-service manner.
- Resource provisioning can be conducted using one of three delivery models: advanced provisioning, dynamic provisioning, and user self-provisioning.
  - Advanced provisioning: The customer signs a formal contract of service with the cloud provider. The provider then prepares the agreed-upon resources or services for the customer and delivers them. The customer is charged a flat fee or is billed on a monthly basis.
  - Dynamic provisioning: Cloud resources are deployed flexibly to match a customer's fluctuating demands. Cloud deployments typically scale up to accommodate spikes in usage and scale down when demands decrease. The customer is billed on a pay-per-use basis. When dynamic provisioning is used to create a hybrid cloud environment, it is sometimes referred to as cloud bursting.
  - User self-provisioning: The customer buys resources from the cloud provider through a web interface or portal. This usually involves creating a user account and paying for resources with a credit card. Those resources are then quickly spun up and made available for use -- within hours, if not minutes. Examples of this type of cloud provisioning include an employee purchasing cloud-based productivity applications via the Microsoft 365 suite or G Suite.
- Resource provisioning offers organizations numerous benefits that aren't available with traditional provisioning approaches. Some of the benefits are:
  - Scalability: In the traditional IT provisioning model, an organization makes large investments in its on-premises infrastructure. This requires extensive preparation and forecasting of infrastructure needs, as the on-premises infrastructure is often set up to last for several years. In contrast, cloud provisioning allows organizations to scale their infrastructure up or down as needed, without wasting resources or paying for unused capacity.
  - Speed: Cloud provisioning enables organizations to access cloud resources and services in a matter of minutes, rather than weeks or months. This reduces the time to market for new products and services, and improves the responsiveness and agility of the organization.
  - Cost: Cloud provisioning reduces the capital expenditure (CAPEX) and operational expenditure (OPEX) of the organization, as it eliminates the need to purchase, maintain, and upgrade on-premises hardware and software. Cloud provisioning also enables the organization to pay only for the resources and services they use, rather than fixed contracts or licenses.
  - Innovation: Cloud provisioning empowers the organization's builders to experiment with new technologies and solutions, without having to worry about meeting company standards or incurring extra billing costs. Cloud provisioning also provides access to a wide range of cloud services and third-party software products from AWS Marketplace that can enhance the organization's capabilities and offerings.
- Resource provisioning also poses some challenges and risks that need to be addressed by the organization. Some of the challenges and risks are:
  - Security: Cloud provisioning increases the attack surface and the complexity of the organization's security posture, as it involves sharing sensitive data and resources with a third-party cloud provider. The organization needs to ensure that the cloud provider meets the organization's security requirements and complies with the relevant regulations and standards. The organization also needs to implement appropriate security measures and controls to protect its cloud resources and services from unauthorized access, modification, or deletion.
  - Governance: Cloud provisioning requires the organization to establish and enforce policies and rules for the allocation, usage, and monitoring of cloud resources and services. The organization needs to ensure that the cloud provisioning process is consistent, repeatable, and compliant with the organization's goals and objectives. The organization also needs to track and audit the cloud provisioning activities and outcomes, and measure the performance and value of the cloud resources and services.
  - Management: Cloud provisioning introduces new challenges and complexities for the management of the organization's IT infrastructure and operations. The organization needs to coordinate and integrate its cloud resources and services with its existing on-premises or hybrid infrastructure, and ensure the compatibility and interoperability of the different systems and platforms. The organization also needs to optimize and automate the cloud provisioning process, and leverage tools and services that can help with the provisioning and orchestration of cloud resources and services .
