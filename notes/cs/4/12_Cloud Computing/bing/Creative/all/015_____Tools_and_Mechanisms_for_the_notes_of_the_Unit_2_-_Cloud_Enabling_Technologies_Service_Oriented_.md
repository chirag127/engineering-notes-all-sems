# Tools and Mechanisms for Service Oriented Architecture

Service Oriented Architecture (SOA) is an architectural style that enables the development and integration of software services that are loosely coupled, self-contained, and interoperable  . SOA supports the reuse of existing services to create new applications and workflows, as well as the adaptation and evolution of services to meet changing requirements  . SOA also facilitates the communication and collaboration among different service providers and consumers.

Some of the tools and mechanisms that are commonly used in SOA are:

- **Service interface**: A service interface defines the contract or specification of a service, including its inputs, outputs, operations, and policies. A service interface is typically expressed using a standard language such as Web Services Description Language (WSDL) or OpenAPI  .
- **Service registry**: A service registry is a repository that stores and publishes the metadata of available services, such as their names, locations, interfaces, and descriptions. A service registry enables service discovery and lookup by service consumers and other service providers. A service registry can use a standard protocol such as Universal Description, Discovery and Integration (UDDI) or Service Discovery Protocol (SDP)  .
- **Service bus**: A service bus is a middleware component that facilitates the communication and integration of services across different platforms, protocols, and formats. A service bus provides features such as message routing, transformation, mediation, security, and reliability. A service bus can use a standard protocol such as Simple Object Access Protocol (SOAP) or Representational State Transfer (REST)  .
- **Service orchestration**: Service orchestration is the process of composing and coordinating multiple services to achieve a complex business goal or workflow. Service orchestration involves defining the logic, sequence, and rules of service invocation and interaction. Service orchestration can use a standard language such as Business Process Execution Language (BPEL) or Workflow Description Language (WDL)  .
- **Service adaptation**: Service adaptation is the process of modifying or evolving a service to meet new or changing requirements, such as functionality, quality, or performance. Service adaptation can be done at design time or runtime, and can involve changing the service interface, implementation, or configuration. Service adaptation can use tools and frameworks such as Aspect-Oriented Programming (AOP), Model-Driven Development (MDD), or Service Component Architecture (SCA).
