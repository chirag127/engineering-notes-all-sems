### Security Standards for the notes of the Unit 4 - Resource Management And Security In Cloud in the subject of Cloud Computing

- Security standards are critical to ensure cost-effective and easy migration, to ensure that mission-critical requirements can be met, and to reduce the risk that sizable investments may become prematurely technologically obsolete.
- Security standards are key elements required to ensure a level playing field in the global marketplace.
- Security standards provide a roadmap for businesses transitioning from a traditional approach to a cloud-based approach by providing the right tools, configurations, and policies required for security in cloud usage.
- Security standards help to devise an effective security strategy for the organization.
- Security standards reduce the risk of a security incident in the cloud.
- Some of the security standards relevant to cloud computing are:

  - ISO-27001 / ISO-27002: These are international standards for information security management systems (ISMS) that provide a framework of policies and procedures for securing information assets. ISO-27001 specifies the requirements for establishing, implementing, maintaining and continually improving an ISMS, while ISO-27002 provides guidelines and best practices for implementing the ISMS.
  - ISO-27017: This is a security standard established for cloud service providers and consumers with the goal of reducing the risk of a security incident in the cloud . It provides guidelines for cloud security that can help organizations approach cloud security more systematically and dependably. It also defines the roles and responsibilities of both cloud service providers and consumers in terms of security controls.
  - ISO-27018: This is a security standard that focuses on the protection of personal data in the cloud. It provides guidelines for cloud service providers on how to handle personal data in accordance with the principles of the EU General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR). It also specifies the contractual obligations and responsibilities of cloud service providers and consumers regarding personal data protection.
  - General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR): This is a regulation that aims to harmonize data protection laws across the European Union (EU) and to protect the rights and freedoms of individuals in relation to their personal data. It applies to any organization that processes personal data of individuals in the EU, regardless of where the organization is located or where the data is processed. It imposes strict obligations and penalties on data controllers and processors, such as obtaining consent, ensuring data quality, implementing security measures, notifying data breaches, and respecting data subjects' rights.
  - System and Organisation Controls (SOC) Reporting: This is a framework for auditing and reporting on the internal controls of service organizations, such as cloud service providers, that affect the security, availability, processing integrity, confidentiality, and privacy of the information they process or store on behalf of their customers. There are three types of SOC reports: SOC 1, SOC 2, and SOC 3. SOC 1 reports on the controls relevant to financial reporting, SOC 2 reports on the controls relevant to the trust services criteria (security, availability, processing integrity, confidentiality, and privacy), and SOC 3 reports on the same criteria as SOC 2 but in a more general and less detailed manner.
  - Payment Card Industry Data Security Standard (PCI DSS): This is a security standard that applies to any organization that stores, processes, or transmits cardholder data, such as credit card or debit card information. It aims to protect cardholder data from unauthorized access, use, or disclosure. It specifies 12 requirements for security controls, such as maintaining a secure network, protecting cardholder data, implementing strong access control measures, regularly monitoring and testing networks, and maintaining an information security policy.
  - Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act (HIPAA): This is a US law that regulates the privacy and security of protected health information (PHI), which is any information that relates to the past, present, or future physical or mental health or condition of an individual, or the provision or payment of health care to an individual. It applies to covered entities, such as health care providers, health plans, and health care clearinghouses, and their business associates, such as cloud service providers, that create, receive, maintain, or transmit PHI on behalf of the covered entities.
