### Maxwell Equations in Integral and Differential Form for the Notes of the Unit 2 - Electromagnetic Field Theory in the Subject of ENGINEERING PHYSICS

Maxwell's equations are a set of four fundamental equations that describe the behavior of electric and magnetic fields. These equations are essential for the study of electromagnetic waves and the behavior of electric and magnetic fields in various situations.

Here are Maxwell's equations in both integral and differential forms:

#### Gauss's Law for Electric Fields

Integral Form:

$$\oint_S \vec{E} \cdot d\vec{A} = \frac{1}{\epsilon_0}Q_{enc}$$

Differential Form:

$$\nabla \cdot \vec{E} = \frac{\rho}{\epsilon_0}$$

#### Gauss's Law for Magnetic Fields

Integral Form:

$$\oint_S \vec{B} \cdot d\vec{A} = 0$$

Differential Form:

$$\nabla \cdot \vec{B} = 0$$

#### Faraday's Law

Integral Form:

$$\oint_C \vec{E} \cdot d\vec{l} = -\frac{d}{dt}\int_S \vec{B} \cdot d\vec{A}$$

Differential Form:

$$\nabla \times \vec{E} = -\frac{\partial \vec{B}}{\partial t}$$

#### Ampere's Law

Integral Form:

$$\oint_C \vec{B} \cdot d\vec{l} = \mu_0(I_{enc} + \epsilon_0\frac{d}{dt}\int_S \vec{E} \cdot d\vec{A})$$

Differential Form:

$$\nabla \times \vec{B} = \mu_0\vec{J} + \mu_0\epsilon_0\frac{\partial \vec{E}}{\partial t}$$

In these equations, $\vec{E}$ represents the electric field, $\vec{B}$ represents the magnetic field, $\rho$ represents the charge density, $\vec{J}$ represents the current density, $Q_{enc}$ represents the total charge enclosed by a surface, $I_{enc}$ represents the total current enclosed by a surface, $\epsilon_0$ represents the permittivity of free space, and $\mu_0$ represents the permeability of free space.

Understanding Maxwell's equations is essential for understanding the behavior of electromagnetic waves and the behavior of electric and magnetic fields in various situations. These equations are fundamental to the study of electromagnetics and are used extensively in engineering and physics applications.
