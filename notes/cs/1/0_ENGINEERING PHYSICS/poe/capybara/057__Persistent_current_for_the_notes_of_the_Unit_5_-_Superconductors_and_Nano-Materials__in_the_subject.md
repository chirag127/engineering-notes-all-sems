### Persistent current for the notes of the Unit 5 - Superconductors and Nano-Materials: in the subject of ENGINEERING PHYSICS

Persistent current is an important concept in superconductivity. Here are some key points to remember:

- Persistent current is the current that continues to flow indefinitely in a closed superconducting loop, even after the applied magnetic field is removed.
- This phenomenon is due to the fact that superconductors have zero electrical resistance, which allows for a perfect flow of electrons.
- The magnitude of the persistent current is related to the magnetic flux threading the superconducting loop, and is quantized in units of the flux quantum.
- The flux quantum is a fundamental constant of nature, denoted by the symbol phi_0, which has a value of approximately 2.07 x 10^-15 tesla-meters squared.
- The quantization of the persistent current is a consequence of the wave-like nature of electrons in a superconductor, and is a manifestation of the phenomenon known as superconducting coherence.
- The presence of persistent currents in superconducting loops has important practical applications, such as in the design of superconducting magnets for use in magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) machines and particle accelerators.
- However, the generation of persistent currents requires very low temperatures and high magnetic fields, which can be challenging to achieve in practice.
- In addition to persistent currents, superconductors also exhibit other fascinating phenomena, such as the Meissner effect, where they expel magnetic fields from their interior, and flux pinning, where they can trap magnetic flux in their interior.

Understanding the concept of persistent current is essential for anyone studying superconductivity and its applications.
