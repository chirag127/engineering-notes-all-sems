### Rayleigh's Criterion of Resolution

In the field of wave optics, Rayleigh's criterion of resolution is used to determine the minimum distance between two point sources that can be resolved by an optical system. Here are some key points to understand the concept better:

- Rayleigh's criterion states that two point sources can be resolved if the central maximum of one source falls on the first minimum of the other source.
- The minimum distance between two point sources that can be resolved is given by the formula: 

    `d = 1.22 λ / NA`

    where d is the minimum distance, λ is the wavelength of light, and NA is the numerical aperture of the optical system.
- The numerical aperture is a measure of the light-gathering ability of the lens and is defined as the sine of the half angle of the cone of light that enters the lens.
- The smaller the wavelength of light or the larger the numerical aperture, the smaller the minimum distance that can be resolved.
- Rayleigh's criterion is applicable for both coherent and incoherent sources of light.
- In practice, the resolution of an optical system is limited by various factors such as aberrations, diffraction, and noise.

It is important to understand Rayleigh's criterion of resolution as it plays a crucial role in designing and optimizing optical systems for various applications in engineering and technology.
