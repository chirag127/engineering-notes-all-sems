### Principles of Laser Action

Laser, which stands for Light Amplification by Stimulated Emission of Radiation, is a device that produces a narrow and intense beam of light. Laser action is based on the principle of stimulated emission, which is a quantum mechanical process. Below are the principles of laser action:

- Stimulated Emission: Stimulated emission is the process in which an atom in an excited state is stimulated by an incoming photon to emit a second photon of the same frequency, phase, and direction. The emitted photon is in phase with the incoming photon and is coherent with it. This process leads to the amplification of the light energy.

- Population Inversion: Population inversion is a condition in which more atoms are in the excited state than in the ground state. This condition is achieved by pumping energy into the system, which can be done through electrical discharge, flash lamps, or other sources. Population inversion is essential for laser action to occur.

- Optical Feedback: Optical feedback is the process in which a portion of the output beam is reflected back into the laser cavity. This feedback causes the atoms to emit more photons, leading to further amplification of the light energy.

- Resonant Cavity: The laser cavity is a resonant cavity that consists of two mirrors, one of which is partially reflecting. This cavity is designed to allow the light to bounce back and forth between the mirrors, building up the intensity of the light beam.

- Laser Beam Characteristics: The laser beam is monochromatic, coherent, and highly directional. Monochromatic means that the laser emits light of a single wavelength. Coherent means that the light waves are in phase with each other. And highly directional means that the laser beam is very narrow and focused.

In conclusion, laser action is based on the principles of stimulated emission, population inversion, optical feedback, and resonant cavity. These principles are essential for the laser to produce a highly intense and coherent beam of light. Understanding the principles of laser action is crucial for the study of fiber optics and laser in the subject of engineering physics.
