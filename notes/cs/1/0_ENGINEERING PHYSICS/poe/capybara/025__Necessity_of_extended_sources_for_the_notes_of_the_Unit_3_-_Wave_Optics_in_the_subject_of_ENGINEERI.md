### Necessity of extended sources for the notes of the Unit 3 - Wave Optics in the subject of ENGINEERING PHYSICS

- In the field of Wave Optics, extended sources play a crucial role in understanding the behavior of light waves.
- Extended sources refer to sources of light that have dimensions larger than that of a point source.
- The use of extended sources allows for the study of interference and diffraction patterns, which are not possible with point sources.
- The study of interference and diffraction patterns is necessary for the design and analysis of optical systems such as lenses, mirrors, and gratings.
- The behavior of light waves in extended sources can be described using the Huygens-Fresnel principle, which states that every point on an extended source can be considered as a source of secondary waves.
- The interference and diffraction patterns formed by these secondary waves can then be analyzed to determine properties such as the intensity and phase of the resulting wave.
- Examples of extended sources include the sun, fluorescent lamps, and LED lights.
- The use of extended sources is also important in practical applications such as microscopy, spectroscopy, and telecommunications.
- In microscopy, the use of extended sources allows for the study of the structure and behavior of biological cells and tissues.
- In spectroscopy, extended sources are used to study the absorption and emission of light by various materials, which can provide information about their chemical composition and properties.
- In telecommunications, the use of extended sources such as lasers allows for the transmission of information over long distances through optical fibers.
- In conclusion, the study of Wave Optics in the subject of Engineering Physics requires an understanding of the behavior of light waves in extended sources, which is essential for the design and analysis of optical systems and practical applications in various fields.
