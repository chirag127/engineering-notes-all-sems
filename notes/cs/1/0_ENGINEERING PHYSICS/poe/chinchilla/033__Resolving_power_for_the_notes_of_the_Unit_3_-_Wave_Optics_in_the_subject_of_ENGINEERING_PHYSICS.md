### Resolving Power

Resolving power is the ability of an optical instrument to distinguish two closely spaced objects as separate entities. It is a measure of the instrument's ability to resolve or separate small details in an object.

The resolving power of an optical instrument depends on several factors such as wavelength of light, aperture size of the instrument, and the quality of the optics.

The following are the important points related to resolving power:

- Resolving power is inversely proportional to the size of the aperture of the instrument.
- The larger the aperture size, the better the resolving power of the instrument.
- Resolving power is directly proportional to the wavelength of light used in the instrument.
- The shorter the wavelength of light, the better the resolving power of the instrument.
- Resolving power also depends on the quality of the optics used in the instrument.
- The better the quality of the optics, the better the resolving power of the instrument.
- The resolving power of an optical instrument can be expressed mathematically using the Rayleigh criterion.

The Rayleigh criterion states that two objects can be resolved if the first minimum of the diffraction pattern of one object falls on the maximum of the diffraction pattern of the other object. Mathematically, the Rayleigh criterion is given by:

$sin\theta = 1.22\frac{\lambda}{D}$

Where, $\theta$ is the angular separation between the two objects, $\lambda$ is the wavelength of light, and D is the diameter of the aperture.

In conclusion, resolving power is an important parameter for optical instruments, and it depends on several factors such as aperture size, wavelength of light, and the quality of optics. The Rayleigh criterion provides a mathematical expression for resolving power, which can be used to calculate the maximum resolution of an instrument.
