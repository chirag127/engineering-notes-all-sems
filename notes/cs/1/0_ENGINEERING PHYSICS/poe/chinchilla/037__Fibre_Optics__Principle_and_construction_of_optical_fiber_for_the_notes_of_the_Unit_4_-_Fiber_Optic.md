### Fibre Optics: Principle and construction of optical fiber

Fiber optics is a technology that utilizes thin strands of glass or plastic to transmit information through light signals. This technology is widely used in telecommunications, medical equipment, and other industries. In this article, we will discuss the principles and construction of optical fiber.

#### Principle of Optical Fiber

The principle of optical fiber is based on the total internal reflection of light. When light enters a fiber optic cable, it travels through the core of the cable by reflecting off the walls of the core. The core is made of glass or plastic, which has a higher refractive index than the surrounding cladding. This means that when light enters the core, it is reflected back into the core, rather than being refracted out of the cable.

#### Construction of Optical Fiber

An optical fiber is made up of three basic components: the core, the cladding, and the coating. The core is the central part of the fiber, through which light travels. It is made of either glass or plastic, and its diameter ranges from 8 to 62.5 microns.

The cladding is a layer surrounding the core, which is made of a material with a lower refractive index than the core. This causes the light to reflect off the walls of the core and prevents it from escaping the fiber.

The coating is a layer of plastic that surrounds the cladding. Its main function is to protect the fiber from damage and to provide mechanical strength.

#### Types of Optical Fiber

There are two main types of optical fiber: single-mode fiber and multi-mode fiber. Single-mode fiber has a small core diameter and can transmit signals over long distances with high bandwidth. Multi-mode fiber has a larger core diameter and can transmit signals over shorter distances with lower bandwidth.

#### Advantages of Optical Fiber

Optical fiber has several advantages over traditional copper cables. First, it can transmit signals over longer distances without signal degradation. Second, it has a higher bandwidth capacity, which means that it can transmit more data at a faster rate. Third, it is immune to electromagnetic interference, which can disrupt signals in copper cables.
