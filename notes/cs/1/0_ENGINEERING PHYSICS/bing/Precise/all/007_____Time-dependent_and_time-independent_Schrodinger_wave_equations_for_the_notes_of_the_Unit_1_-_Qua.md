# Time-dependent and time-independent Schrodinger wave equations

The Schrodinger wave equation is a fundamental equation in quantum mechanics that describes how a quantum system changes over time. It is named after Erwin Schrodinger, who derived the equation in 1926.

There are two forms of the Schrodinger wave equation: the time-dependent and the time-independent Schrodinger wave equations.

## Time-dependent Schrodinger wave equation

The time-dependent Schrodinger wave equation describes the evolution of the wave function of a quantum system over time. It is given by the equation:

iħ ∂ψ/∂t = Hψ

where i is the imaginary unit, ħ is the reduced Planck constant, ψ is the wave function of the system, t is time, and H is the Hamiltonian operator, which represents the total energy of the system.

## Time-independent Schrodinger wave equation

The time-independent Schrodinger wave equation describes the stationary states of a quantum system. It is obtained by assuming that the wave function can be separated into a time-dependent part and a time-independent part, and then applying the time-dependent Schrodinger wave equation. The resulting equation is:

Hψ = Eψ

where E is the total energy of the system.

The time-independent Schrodinger wave equation is often used to solve for the stationary states of a quantum system, which are the states with definite energy. These states are important because they form a complete set of states that can be used to describe any state of the system.

In summary, the Schrodinger wave equation is a fundamental equation in quantum mechanics that describes how a quantum system changes over time. There are two forms of the Schrodinger wave equation: the time-dependent and the time-independent Schrodinger wave equations. The time-dependent Schrodinger wave equation describes the evolution of the wave function of a quantum system over time, while the time-independent Schrodinger wave equation describes the stationary states of a quantum system. These equations are important tools for understanding and predicting the behavior of quantum systems.
