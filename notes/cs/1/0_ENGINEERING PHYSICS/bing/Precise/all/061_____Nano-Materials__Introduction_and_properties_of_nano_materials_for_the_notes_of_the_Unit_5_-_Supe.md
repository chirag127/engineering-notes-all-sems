# Unit 5 - Superconductors and Nano-Materials

## Nano-Materials: Introduction and properties of nano materials

Nano-materials are materials that have at least one dimension in the nanometer scale, typically between 1 and 100 nanometers. At this scale, materials exhibit unique properties that are not observed in their bulk form. These properties are due to the increased surface area to volume ratio and quantum confinement effects.

Some of the unique properties of nano-materials include:

1. Increased reactivity: Due to the increased surface area, nano-materials are more reactive than their bulk counterparts. This property is useful in applications such as catalysis and drug delivery.

2. Enhanced mechanical properties: Nano-materials can have enhanced mechanical properties such as increased strength and toughness. This is due to the presence of a large number of grain boundaries and the ability to control the microstructure at the nanoscale.

3. Optical properties: Nano-materials can exhibit unique optical properties such as fluorescence and surface plasmon resonance. These properties are useful in applications such as sensing and imaging.

4. Electrical properties: Nano-materials can exhibit unique electrical properties such as increased conductivity and quantum tunneling. These properties are useful in applications such as electronics and energy storage.

5. Magnetic properties: Nano-materials can exhibit unique magnetic properties such as superparamagnetism and giant magnetoresistance. These properties are useful in applications such as data storage and spintronics.

In summary, nano-materials are materials with at least one dimension in the nanometer scale and exhibit unique properties due to their increased surface area to volume ratio and quantum confinement effects. These properties include increased reactivity, enhanced mechanical properties, unique optical, electrical, and magnetic properties. These properties make nano-materials useful in a wide range of applications.
