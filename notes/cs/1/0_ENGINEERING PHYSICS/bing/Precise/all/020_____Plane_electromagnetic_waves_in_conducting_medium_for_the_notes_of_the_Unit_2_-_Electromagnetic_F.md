### Plane Electromagnetic Waves in Conducting Medium

Unit 2 - Electromagnetic Field Theory in the subject of ENGINEERING PHYSICS

1. A conducting medium is a material that allows the flow of electric charge.
2. When an electromagnetic wave travels through a conducting medium, the electric field of the wave causes the free electrons in the medium to oscillate.
3. This oscillation of electrons produces an electric current, which in turn generates a magnetic field.
4. The magnetic field then interacts with the electric field of the wave, causing the wave to be attenuated or weakened as it travels through the medium.
5. The attenuation of the wave is determined by the conductivity of the medium, with higher conductivity leading to greater attenuation.
6. The speed of the wave is also affected by the conductivity of the medium, with the wave traveling slower in a more conductive medium.
7. The interaction between the electromagnetic wave and the conducting medium can be described by the wave equation, which takes into account the conductivity, permittivity, and permeability of the medium.
8. Solutions to the wave equation can be used to determine the behavior of electromagnetic waves in conducting media, including reflection, refraction, and transmission.
