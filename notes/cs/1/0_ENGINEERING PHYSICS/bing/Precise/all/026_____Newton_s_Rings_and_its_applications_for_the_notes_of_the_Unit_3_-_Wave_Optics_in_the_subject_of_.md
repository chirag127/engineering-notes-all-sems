# Newton’s Rings and its applications

Newton's rings is a phenomenon in which an interference pattern is created by the reflection of light between two surfaces - a spherical surface and an adjacent flat surface. It is named after Sir Isaac Newton, who first studied the effect in the 17th century.

The phenomenon is observed due to the interference of light waves reflected from the top and bottom surfaces of a thin film of air between the two surfaces. When viewed with monochromatic light, the pattern appears as a series of concentric, alternating bright and dark rings centered at the point of contact between the two surfaces.

Applications of Newton's Rings include:
1. Determining the wavelength of light: The diameter of the rings in the Newton's Rings pattern is directly proportional to the square root of the wavelength of the light used. By measuring the diameter of the rings, the wavelength of the light can be determined.
2. Measuring the refractive index of a transparent material: The refractive index of a transparent material can be determined by placing a thin film of the material between the two surfaces and observing the change in the diameter of the rings.
3. Testing the quality of optical surfaces: Newton's Rings can be used to test the quality of optical surfaces, such as lenses and mirrors. Any deviation from a perfect surface will cause a distortion in the pattern of the rings.
