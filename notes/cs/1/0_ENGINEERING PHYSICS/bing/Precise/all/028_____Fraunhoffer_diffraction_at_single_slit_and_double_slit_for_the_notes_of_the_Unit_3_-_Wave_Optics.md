# Fraunhoffer Diffraction at Single Slit and Double Slit

Fraunhoffer diffraction is a type of diffraction that occurs when a wave passes through an aperture or slit and the distance between the aperture and the screen is much larger than the size of the aperture. This type of diffraction is also known as far-field diffraction.

## Single Slit Diffraction

When a monochromatic light wave passes through a single slit, the wave spreads out and forms a diffraction pattern on a screen placed at a distance from the slit. The diffraction pattern consists of a central bright fringe, known as the central maximum, and a series of alternating bright and dark fringes on either side of the central maximum.

The width of the central maximum is inversely proportional to the width of the slit. As the width of the slit decreases, the width of the central maximum increases. The intensity of the central maximum is also affected by the width of the slit. As the width of the slit decreases, the intensity of the central maximum decreases.

The position of the bright fringes in the diffraction pattern can be calculated using the formula:

`d sin(θ) = mλ`

where `d` is the width of the slit, `θ` is the angle between the central maximum and the bright fringe, `m` is the order of the fringe, and `λ` is the wavelength of the light.

## Double Slit Diffraction

When a monochromatic light wave passes through two slits, the waves from each slit interfere with each other to form an interference pattern on a screen placed at a distance from the slits. The interference pattern consists of a series of bright and dark fringes.

The position of the bright fringes in the interference pattern can be calculated using the formula:

`d sin(θ) = mλ`

where `d` is the distance between the two slits, `θ` is the angle between the central maximum and the bright fringe, `m` is the order of the fringe, and `λ` is the wavelength of the light.

The width of the bright fringes in the interference pattern is inversely proportional to the distance between the two slits. As the distance between the two slits decreases, the width of the bright fringes increases.

The intensity of the bright fringes in the interference pattern is also affected by the width of the slits. As the width of the slits decreases, the intensity of the bright fringes decreases.

In summary, Fraunhoffer diffraction at a single slit and double slit is a phenomenon that occurs when a wave passes through an aperture or slits and forms a diffraction or interference pattern on a screen placed at a distance from the aperture or slits. The position, width, and intensity of the fringes in the pattern are affected by the width of the slit or the distance between the two slits, and the wavelength of the light.
