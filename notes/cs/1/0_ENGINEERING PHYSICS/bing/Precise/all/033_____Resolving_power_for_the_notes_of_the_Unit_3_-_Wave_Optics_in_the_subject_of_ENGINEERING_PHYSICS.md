### Resolving Power

Resolving power is a measure of the ability of an optical instrument to distinguish between two closely spaced objects. It is defined as the minimum distance between two points that can be distinguished as separate entities by the instrument.

In the context of wave optics, the resolving power of an instrument is limited by the phenomenon of diffraction. When light passes through an aperture or around an obstacle, it spreads out and interferes with itself, creating a diffraction pattern. This can cause closely spaced objects to appear blurred and indistinguishable.

The Rayleigh criterion is commonly used to determine the resolving power of an optical instrument. According to this criterion, two points are considered to be just resolved when the central maximum of the diffraction pattern of one point coincides with the first minimum of the diffraction pattern of the other point.

The resolving power of an instrument can be improved by increasing the size of the aperture or by using light of a shorter wavelength. This is because the angle of diffraction is inversely proportional to the size of the aperture and the wavelength of the light.

In summary, the resolving power of an optical instrument is its ability to distinguish between closely spaced objects, and is limited by the phenomenon of diffraction. The Rayleigh criterion is commonly used to determine the resolving power, and it can be improved by increasing the size of the aperture or using light of a shorter wavelength.
