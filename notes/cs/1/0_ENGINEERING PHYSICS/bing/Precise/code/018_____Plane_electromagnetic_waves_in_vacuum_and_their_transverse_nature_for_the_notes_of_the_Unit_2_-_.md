### Plane Electromagnetic Waves in Vacuum and Their Transverse Nature

#### Unit 2 - Electromagnetic Field Theory in the Subject of Engineering Physics

1. Electromagnetic waves are transverse waves that consist of oscillating electric and magnetic fields that propagate through space.
2. In a vacuum, these waves travel at the speed of light, which is approximately 299,792,458 meters per second.
3. The electric and magnetic fields of an electromagnetic wave are perpendicular to each other and to the direction of propagation of the wave.
4. This means that the electric and magnetic fields oscillate in planes that are perpendicular to the direction in which the wave is traveling.
5. The transverse nature of electromagnetic waves can be demonstrated using polarization filters, which only allow light waves with a specific orientation of the electric field to pass through.
6. When two polarization filters are placed at right angles to each other, no light can pass through, demonstrating that the electric field of the light wave is oscillating in a plane perpendicular to the direction of propagation.
7. Electromagnetic waves can have different frequencies and wavelengths, and the relationship between these two properties is given by the equation c = fλ, where c is the speed of light, f is the frequency of the wave, and λ is its wavelength.
8. The electromagnetic spectrum includes a wide range of frequencies and wavelengths, from radio waves with long wavelengths and low frequencies to gamma rays with short wavelengths and high frequencies.
9. Electromagnetic waves can be generated by accelerating electric charges, and they can interact with matter in various ways, including absorption, reflection, and refraction.
10. The study of electromagnetic waves and their interactions with matter is an important part of the field of electromagnetism and has many practical applications in areas such as communication, medicine, and energy production.
