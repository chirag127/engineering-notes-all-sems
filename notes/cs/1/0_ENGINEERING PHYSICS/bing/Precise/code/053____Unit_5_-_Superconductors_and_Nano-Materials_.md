## Unit 5 - Superconductors and Nano-Materials:

Superconductors are materials that exhibit zero electrical resistance and the expulsion of magnetic fields below a certain temperature, known as the critical temperature. This phenomenon is known as superconductivity.

- Superconductivity was first discovered in 1911 by Heike Kamerlingh Onnes, who observed that the resistance of mercury dropped to zero at a temperature of 4.2 K.
- Superconductors have a wide range of applications, including in MRI machines, particle accelerators, and power transmission lines.
- The critical temperature of a superconductor varies depending on the material, with some materials exhibiting superconductivity at temperatures as high as 138 K.
- The mechanism behind superconductivity is not yet fully understood, but it is believed to involve the formation of Cooper pairs, in which two electrons form a bound state and move through the material without resistance.

Nano-materials are materials with at least one dimension on the nanoscale, typically between 1 and 100 nanometers.

- Nano-materials have unique properties due to their small size and high surface area to volume ratio.
- They have a wide range of applications, including in electronics, medicine, and energy production.
- Some common nano-materials include carbon nanotubes, graphene, and quantum dots.
- The properties of nano-materials can be tuned by changing their size, shape, and composition.
- The study of nano-materials is a rapidly growing field, with new discoveries and applications being made regularly.
