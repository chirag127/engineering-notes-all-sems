### Introduction to Diffraction

Diffraction is the phenomenon of bending of light around the corners of an obstacle or aperture into the region of geometrical shadow of the obstacle. It is a characteristic property of waves, including light waves, sound waves, and water waves. Diffraction is observed when the size of the obstacle or aperture is comparable to the wavelength of the wave.

Some key points to remember about diffraction are:
- Diffraction is a wave property and is observed for all types of waves.
- The amount of diffraction depends on the size of the obstacle or aperture relative to the wavelength of the wave.
- The smaller the obstacle or aperture, the more the wave spreads out.
- The longer the wavelength of the wave, the more it spreads out.

In the context of wave optics, diffraction is an important phenomenon as it provides evidence for the wave nature of light. It is also used in various practical applications such as diffraction gratings and X-ray diffraction.
