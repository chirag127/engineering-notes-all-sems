### Newton’s Rings and its applications

Newton's Rings is a phenomenon in which an interference pattern is created by the reflection of light between two surfaces - a spherical surface and an adjacent flat surface. It is named after Sir Isaac Newton, who first studied the effect in the 17th century.

The phenomenon is observed when a plano-convex lens is placed on a flat glass surface, and monochromatic light is shone onto the lens from above. The light reflects off the bottom surface of the lens and the top surface of the glass, creating an interference pattern of bright and dark rings.

The radius of the rings is given by the formula: Rn = sqrt[(R^2) - (nλR/2)], where R is the radius of curvature of the lens, n is the order of the ring, and λ is the wavelength of the light.

Applications of Newton's Rings include:
- Determining the wavelength of light: By measuring the radii of the rings and using the above formula, the wavelength of the light can be calculated.
- Testing the quality of optical surfaces: The presence of any defects or irregularities on the surfaces will cause distortions in the interference pattern, allowing for the detection of such defects.
- Measuring the refractive index of a liquid: By placing a liquid between the lens and the glass surface, the interference pattern will change, allowing for the calculation of the refractive index of the liquid.
