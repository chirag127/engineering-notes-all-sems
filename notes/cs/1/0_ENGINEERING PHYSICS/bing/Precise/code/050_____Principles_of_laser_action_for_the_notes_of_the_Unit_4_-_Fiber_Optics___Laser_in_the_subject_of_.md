### Principles of laser action for the notes of the Unit 4 - Fiber Optics & Laser in the subject of ENGINEERING PHYSICS

1. **Population Inversion:** For laser action to occur, the number of atoms in the higher energy state must be greater than the number of atoms in the lower energy state. This condition is known as population inversion.

2. **Optical Feedback:** Laser action requires a feedback mechanism to sustain the stimulated emission of radiation. This is achieved by placing the lasing medium between two mirrors, forming an optical cavity. The mirrors reflect the emitted radiation back into the lasing medium, stimulating further emission.

3. **Pumping:** To achieve population inversion, the atoms in the lasing medium must be excited to the higher energy state. This is achieved by pumping energy into the lasing medium, typically using an electrical discharge or an intense light source.

4. **Gain:** The gain of a laser is the amplification of the emitted radiation as it passes through the lasing medium. The gain must be greater than the losses in the optical cavity for laser action to occur.

5. **Threshold:** The threshold of a laser is the minimum amount of pumping energy required to achieve population inversion and initiate laser action.

6. **Coherence:** The emitted radiation from a laser is highly coherent, meaning that the waves are in phase and have a well-defined direction. This is a result of the stimulated emission process, where the emitted radiation is in phase with the stimulating radiation.

7. **Monochromaticity:** The emitted radiation from a laser is highly monochromatic, meaning that it has a very narrow range of wavelengths. This is a result of the narrow energy levels in the lasing medium and the selective feedback provided by the optical cavity.

8. **Directionality:** The emitted radiation from a laser is highly directional, meaning that it is emitted in a well-defined direction. This is a result of the optical cavity, which only allows radiation traveling in a specific direction to be amplified and emitted.

9. **Intensity:** The emitted radiation from a laser is highly intense, meaning that it has a high power per unit area. This is a result of the amplification of the emitted radiation as it passes through the lasing medium multiple times.
