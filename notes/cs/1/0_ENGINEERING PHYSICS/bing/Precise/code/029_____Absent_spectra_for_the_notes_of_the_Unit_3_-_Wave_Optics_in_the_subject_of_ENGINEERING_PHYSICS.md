### Absent Spectra

- Absent spectra, also known as absorption spectra, are produced when light passes through a cool gas.
- The gas absorbs certain wavelengths of light, leaving dark lines in the spectrum.
- These dark lines correspond to the wavelengths of light that have been absorbed by the gas.
- The pattern of dark lines is unique to each element, allowing scientists to identify the elements present in a gas by analyzing its absorption spectrum.
- Absent spectra are used in a variety of fields, including astronomy, chemistry, and environmental science.
- In astronomy, for example, scientists can use the absorption spectra of stars to determine their chemical composition.
- In chemistry, absorption spectra can be used to identify the presence of certain elements in a sample.
- In environmental science, absorption spectra can be used to measure the concentration of pollutants in the air or water.
- Absent spectra are an important tool for scientists in many different fields, allowing them to analyze and understand the world around us.
