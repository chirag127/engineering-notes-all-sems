### Laser: Absorption of radiation

1. Absorption of radiation is the process by which an atom, molecule or ion absorbs electromagnetic radiation and undergoes a transition from a lower energy state to a higher energy state.
2. The energy of the absorbed photon is equal to the difference in energy between the two states.
3. The absorption of radiation is a fundamental process in the operation of a laser.
4. In a laser, the active medium is pumped with energy, usually in the form of light or electrical current, to create a population inversion.
5. This means that there are more atoms or molecules in an excited state than in the ground state.
6. When a photon of the right energy passes through the active medium, it can stimulate an excited atom or molecule to drop to a lower energy state and emit a photon of the same energy.
7. This process is called stimulated emission and is the basis for laser amplification.
8. The emitted photon is in phase with the stimulating photon, resulting in coherent light.
9. The absorption of radiation is also important in the attenuation of laser light as it passes through a medium.
10. The absorption coefficient of a material determines how much laser light is absorbed as it passes through the material.
11. The absorption of laser light can also be used for spectroscopic analysis, as different materials absorb light at different wavelengths.
12. This can be used to identify the composition of a material or to measure the concentration of a particular substance.
