### Resolving Power

Resolving power is the ability of an optical instrument to distinguish between two closely spaced objects. It is a measure of the clarity of an image produced by the instrument. The resolving power of an optical instrument is determined by the wavelength of the light being used and the numerical aperture of the instrument.

Here are some key points to remember about resolving power:

1. The resolving power of an optical instrument is inversely proportional to the wavelength of the light being used. This means that shorter wavelengths of light, such as blue or violet, will produce images with higher resolving power than longer wavelengths, such as red or orange.

2. The numerical aperture of an optical instrument is a measure of its ability to gather light. The higher the numerical aperture, the greater the resolving power of the instrument.

3. The resolving power of an optical instrument can be improved by using light of shorter wavelengths or by increasing the numerical aperture of the instrument.

4. The Rayleigh criterion is a commonly used measure of the resolving power of an optical instrument. According to this criterion, two objects are considered to be resolved if the central maximum of the diffraction pattern of one object falls on the first minimum of the diffraction pattern of the other object.

5. The resolving power of the human eye is limited by the spacing of the photoreceptor cells in the retina. The average resolving power of the human eye is about 0.1 arc minutes.
