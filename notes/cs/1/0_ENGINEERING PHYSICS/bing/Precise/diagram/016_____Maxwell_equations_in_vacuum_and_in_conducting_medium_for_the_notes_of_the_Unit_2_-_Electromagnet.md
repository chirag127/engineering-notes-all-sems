### Unit 2 - Electromagnetic Field Theory
#### Maxwell's Equations in Vacuum and in Conducting Medium

Maxwell's equations are a set of four partial differential equations that describe the behavior of the electric and magnetic fields. These equations were first presented by James Clerk Maxwell in the 19th century. The equations are as follows:

1. Gauss's Law: This law states that the electric flux through any closed surface is equal to the charge enclosed within the surface divided by the permittivity of free space. Mathematically, it is represented as: ∇ • **E** = ρ/ε0
2. Gauss's Law for Magnetism: This law states that the magnetic flux through any closed surface is zero. Mathematically, it is represented as: ∇ • **B** = 0
3. Faraday's Law of Induction: This law states that the electromotive force (EMF) induced in a closed loop is equal to the negative of the rate of change of the magnetic flux through the loop. Mathematically, it is represented as: ∇ x **E** = - ∂**B**/∂t
4. Ampere's Law with Maxwell's Correction: This law states that the magnetic field around a closed loop is equal to the sum of the current flowing through the loop and the displacement current, which is the rate of change of the electric flux through the loop. Mathematically, it is represented as: ∇ x **B** = μ0(**J** + ε0 ∂**E**/∂t)

In a conducting medium, the electric field **E** is related to the current density **J** by the conductivity σ of the medium, as **J** = σ**E**. This relationship can be used to modify Maxwell's equations in a conducting medium.
