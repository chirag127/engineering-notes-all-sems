### Persistent Current

- A persistent current is a steady electric current that flows indefinitely in a superconducting loop.
- This phenomenon occurs because superconductors have zero electrical resistance, allowing the current to flow without any energy loss.
- The current will continue to flow until an external force, such as a magnetic field or heat, disrupts the superconducting state.
- Persistent currents are used in superconducting magnets, which are commonly used in medical imaging and particle accelerators.
- The strength of the persistent current is determined by the critical current of the superconductor, which is the maximum current that can flow before the superconductor transitions back to its normal state.
- The critical current is dependent on factors such as the temperature, magnetic field strength, and the type of superconductor used.
- Persistent currents have been observed to flow for years without any measurable decay, demonstrating the potential for long-term energy storage in superconducting systems.
