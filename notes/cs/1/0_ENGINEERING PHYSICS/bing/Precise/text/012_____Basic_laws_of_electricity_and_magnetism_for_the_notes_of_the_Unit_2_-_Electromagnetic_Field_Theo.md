### Basic laws of electricity and magnetism for the notes of the Unit 2 - Electromagnetic Field Theory in the subject of ENGINEERING PHYSICS

1. **Coulomb's Law**: This law states that the force between two point charges is directly proportional to the product of the magnitudes of the charges and inversely proportional to the square of the distance between them. The force acts along the line joining the two charges.

2. **Gauss's Law**: This law states that the electric flux through any closed surface is equal to the total charge enclosed within the surface divided by the permittivity of free space.

3. **Ohm's Law**: This law states that the current flowing through a conductor between two points is directly proportional to the voltage across the two points, and inversely proportional to the resistance between them.

4. **Ampere's Law**: This law states that the magnetic field in space around an electric current is proportional to the electric current which serves as its source.

5. **Faraday's Law of Electromagnetic Induction**: This law states that the electromotive force (EMF) induced in a closed circuit is equal to the rate of change of the magnetic flux through the circuit.

6. **Lenz's Law**: This law states that the direction of the induced EMF and hence the induced current in a closed circuit is always such as to oppose the change in magnetic flux producing it.

7. **Biot-Savart Law**: This law states that the magnetic field at any point due to a steady current flowing in a conductor is directly proportional to the current and inversely proportional to the square of the distance from the point to the conductor.

These are some of the basic laws of electricity and magnetism that are covered in Unit 2 - Electromagnetic Field Theory in the subject of ENGINEERING PHYSICS. These laws form the foundation for the study of electromagnetism and are essential for understanding the behavior of electric and magnetic fields. It is important to have a thorough understanding of these laws in order to excel in the subject of ENGINEERING PHYSICS.
