### Numerical Aperture

Numerical aperture (NA) is a measure of the light-gathering ability of an optical system. It is defined based on geometrical considerations and is a theoretical parameter calculated from the optical design. In fiber optics, NA describes the range of angles within which light that is incident on the fiber will be transmitted along it .

- In laser physics, numerical aperture is defined slightly differently. Laser beams spread out as they propagate, but slowly. Far away from the narrowest part of the beam, the spread is roughly linear with distance—the laser beam forms a cone of light in the "far field" .

- Numerical aperture is considered as the light-gathering capacity of an optical fiber. It is defined as the sine of half of the angle of the fiber’s light acceptance cone (i.e. NA = sin(θa), where θa is called the acceptance cone angle) .

- The spot size of the beam at a distance d (the distance between the fiber end and the detector) is the radius of the spot (r) .
