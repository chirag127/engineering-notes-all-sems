### Einstein’s Coefficients for the notes of the Unit 4 - Fiber Optics & Laser in the subject of ENGINEERING PHYSICS

- Einstein's coefficients are a set of mathematical equations that describe the interaction of light with matter.
- These coefficients were introduced by Albert Einstein in 1917 to explain the phenomenon of stimulated emission, which is the basis of laser operation.
- There are three coefficients: the absorption coefficient, the spontaneous emission coefficient, and the stimulated emission coefficient.
- The absorption coefficient describes the probability of an atom absorbing a photon and transitioning from a lower energy state to a higher energy state.
- The spontaneous emission coefficient describes the probability of an atom in an excited state spontaneously emitting a photon and transitioning to a lower energy state.
- The stimulated emission coefficient describes the probability of an atom in an excited state being stimulated by an incoming photon to emit a photon and transition to a lower energy state.
- These coefficients are important in understanding the behavior of lasers and other optical devices that rely on the interaction of light with matter.
