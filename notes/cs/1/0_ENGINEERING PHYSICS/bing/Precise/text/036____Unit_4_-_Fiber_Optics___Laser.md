## Unit 4 - Fiber Optics & Laser

Fiber optics and lasers are two technologies that have revolutionized the field of telecommunications and medicine. Here are some key points to consider when studying these topics:

1. **Fiber Optics** is a technology that uses thin, flexible fibers of glass or plastic to transmit light signals over long distances. These signals can carry large amounts of data, making fiber optics ideal for high-speed internet and telecommunications.

2. **Lasers** are devices that produce intense beams of light. They work by stimulating the emission of light from atoms or molecules, resulting in a highly focused and coherent beam. Lasers have many applications, including telecommunications, medicine, and manufacturing.

3. **Optical fibers** are the key component of fiber optic technology. They are made of glass or plastic and have a diameter slightly thicker than a human hair. The core of the fiber is surrounded by a cladding layer, which helps to guide the light along the fiber.

4. **Total internal reflection** is the principle that allows light to be transmitted along an optical fiber. When light hits the boundary between the core and the cladding at a shallow angle, it is reflected back into the core, allowing the light to travel along the fiber without escaping.

5. **Laser diodes** are commonly used as light sources in fiber optic communication systems. They are small, efficient, and can be modulated at high speeds, making them ideal for transmitting data over long distances.

6. **Fiber optic communication** systems use lasers to transmit data over optical fibers. The data is encoded onto the laser beam using a process called modulation, and then decoded at the receiving end using a detector.

7. **Fiber optic sensors** are used in a variety of applications, including temperature, pressure, and strain sensing. They work by detecting changes in the properties of the light traveling through the fiber, such as its intensity, phase, or polarization.

8. **Medical lasers** have many uses, including surgery, vision correction, and cancer treatment. They work by delivering intense, focused light to a specific area, allowing doctors to perform precise and minimally invasive procedures.
