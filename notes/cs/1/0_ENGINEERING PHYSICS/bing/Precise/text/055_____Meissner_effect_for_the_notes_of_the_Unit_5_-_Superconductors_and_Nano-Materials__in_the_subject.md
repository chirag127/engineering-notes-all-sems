### Meissner effect

- The Meissner effect is a property of all superconductors, discovered by the German physicists W. Meissner and R. Ochsenfeld in 1933.
- As a superconductor in a magnetic field is cooled to the temperature at which it abruptly loses electrical resistance, all or part of the magnetic field within the material is expelled.
- This effect of superconductivity is used in magnetic levitation, which is the base of modern high-speed bullet trains.
- In the superconducting state, due to the expulsion of the external magnetic field, the sample of superconducting material levitates above a magnet or vice versa.
- One of the most well-known demonstrations of the Meissner effect is its ability to make a magnet levitate above a superconductor.
