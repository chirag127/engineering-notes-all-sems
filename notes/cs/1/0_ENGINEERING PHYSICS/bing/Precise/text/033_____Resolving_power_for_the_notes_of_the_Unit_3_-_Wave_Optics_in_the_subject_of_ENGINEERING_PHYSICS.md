### Resolving Power

Resolving power is the ability of an optical instrument to distinguish between two closely spaced objects. It is a measure of the clarity of an image produced by the instrument. The resolving power of an optical instrument depends on the wavelength of the light being used and the numerical aperture of the instrument.

1. **Rayleigh's Criterion**: According to Rayleigh's criterion, two point sources are said to be just resolved when the central maximum of the diffraction pattern of one source coincides with the first minimum of the diffraction pattern of the other source. The minimum angular separation between the two sources that can be resolved is given by θ = 1.22λ/D, where λ is the wavelength of the light and D is the diameter of the aperture of the instrument.

2. **Resolving Power of a Microscope**: The resolving power of a microscope is given by R = λ/2NA, where NA is the numerical aperture of the objective lens. The numerical aperture is defined as NA = n sinα, where n is the refractive index of the medium between the object and the objective lens, and α is the half-angle of the cone of light entering the objective lens.

3. **Resolving Power of a Telescope**: The resolving power of a telescope is given by R = λ/D, where D is the diameter of the objective lens or mirror. The larger the diameter of the objective, the greater the resolving power of the telescope.

4. **Resolving Power of a Spectrometer**: The resolving power of a spectrometer is given by R = λ/Δλ, where Δλ is the smallest difference in wavelength that can be resolved by the instrument. The resolving power of a spectrometer depends on the quality of the diffraction grating used and the width of the entrance and exit slits.

In summary, the resolving power of an optical instrument is its ability to distinguish between two closely spaced objects. It depends on the wavelength of the light being used and the numerical aperture of the instrument. Different types of optical instruments have different formulas for calculating their resolving power.
