### Introduction to Diffraction

Diffraction is a phenomenon that occurs when a wave encounters an obstacle or a slit. It is described as the bending of waves around the corners of an obstacle or through an aperture into the region of geometrical shadow of the obstacle/aperture. The diffracting object or aperture effectively becomes a secondary source of the propagating wave. 

In the context of wave optics, diffraction is the interference of waves according to the Huygens-Fresnel principle. The principle states that every point on a wavefront is itself the source of spherical wavelets. The sum of these spherical wavelets forms the wavefront.

Diffraction can be observed in various phenomena such as the diffraction of sound waves around corners, the diffraction of light waves through a small aperture, and the diffraction of electrons through a crystal lattice.

Some key points to remember about diffraction are:
- Diffraction is a result of the wave nature of light.
- The amount of diffraction depends on the size of the obstacle or aperture relative to the wavelength of the incident wave.
- The smaller the obstacle or aperture, the greater the diffraction.
- Diffraction can be observed with all types of waves, including sound, light, and water waves.
