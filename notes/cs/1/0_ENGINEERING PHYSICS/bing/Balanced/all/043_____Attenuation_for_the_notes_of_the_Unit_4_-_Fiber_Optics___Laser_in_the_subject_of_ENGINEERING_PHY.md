# Attenuation in Fiber Optics

- Attenuation is the reduction in intensity of the light beam (or signal) with respect to distance travelled through a transmission medium .
- Attenuation is measured in decibels (dB) and is calculated by the formula: Attenuation (dB) = 10 x Log10 (P in/P out) = 20xLog10 (V in/V out) where P is power and V is voltage.
- Attenuation limits the distance in which the signal can travel through optical fiber and affects the performance and reliability of the fiber optic network.
- Attenuation can be caused by intrinsic and extrinsic factors .
  - Intrinsic factors are inherent to the fiber material and include:
    - Absorption: the conversion of optical power into heat due to the interaction of light with the molecular structure of the fiber . Absorption depends on the wavelength of the light and the impurities in the fiber.
    - Scattering: the deflection of light from its original direction due to the microscopic variations in the refractive index of the fiber . Scattering depends on the wavelength of the light and the fiber diameter.
    - Dispersion: the spreading of light pulses over time due to the different velocities of different wavelengths or modes in the fiber . Dispersion causes distortion and interference of the signal and limits the bandwidth of the fiber.
  - Extrinsic factors are external to the fiber material and include:
    - Bending: the deviation of the fiber from its original shape due to mechanical stress or installation . Bending causes leakage of light from the core to the cladding or the air and increases the attenuation.
    - Splicing: the joining of two fiber segments by fusion or mechanical means . Splicing causes loss of light due to imperfect alignment, mismatch of fiber characteristics, or air gaps between the fibers.
    - Connectors: the devices that link the fiber to the source, receiver, or other fibers . Connectors cause loss of light due to reflection, misalignment, dirt, or damage on the connector surfaces.
- Attenuation can be reduced by using appropriate fiber types, wavelengths, sources, receivers, splices, connectors, and installation techniques .
