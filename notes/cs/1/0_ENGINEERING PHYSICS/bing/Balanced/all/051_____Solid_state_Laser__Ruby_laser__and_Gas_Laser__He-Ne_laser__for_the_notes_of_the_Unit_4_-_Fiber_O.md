# Solid State Laser (Ruby Laser) and Gas Laser (He-Ne Laser)

## Solid State Laser

- A solid state laser is a laser that uses a solid material as the gain medium, where the light amplification takes place.
- The most common solid state laser is the ruby laser, which was the first laser ever invented.
- Other examples of solid state lasers are neodymium-doped yttrium aluminum garnet (Nd:YAG) laser, titanium-sapphire (Ti:sapphire) laser, and erbium-doped fiber laser (EDFL).

## Ruby Laser

- A ruby laser is a solid state laser that uses a synthetic ruby crystal as the gain medium.
- The ruby crystal consists of a cylindrical rod of aluminum oxide (Al2O3) doped with a small amount of chromium (Cr) ions, which provide the lasing action .
- The ruby rod is placed between two mirrors, one of which is fully reflective and the other is partially reflective, forming an optical resonator .
- The ruby rod is pumped by a flash lamp, which emits a broad spectrum of light that excites the chromium ions from the ground state to a higher energy level .
- The chromium ions then decay to a metastable state, where they can stay for a long time without emitting photons .
- When a photon of the right wavelength (694.3 nm) passes through the ruby rod, it stimulates the emission of another photon of the same wavelength and phase from a chromium ion in the metastable state, creating a chain reaction of stimulated emission .
- The photons bounce back and forth between the mirrors, forming a coherent beam of red light that exits through the partially reflective mirror .
- The ruby laser is a three-level laser, meaning that it has three energy levels involved in the lasing process: the ground state, the metastable state, and the higher energy state .
- The ruby laser produces pulses of light, rather than a continuous beam, because the population inversion (the condition where more atoms are in the excited state than in the ground state) is quickly depleted by the stimulated emission .
- The ruby laser has a very narrow linewidth of 0.53 nm, which means that it emits light of a very pure color and high spectral brightness.

## Gas Laser

- A gas laser is a laser that uses a gas as the gain medium, where the light amplification takes place.
- The most common gas laser is the helium-neon (He-Ne) laser, which was the first continuous-wave laser ever invented.
- Other examples of gas lasers are carbon dioxide (CO2) laser, argon-ion (Ar+) laser, and nitrogen (N2) laser.

## He-Ne Laser

- A He-Ne laser is a gas laser that uses a mixture of helium and neon gases as the gain medium.
- The He-Ne laser consists of a glass tube filled with the gas mixture at a low pressure, with electrodes at both ends, and mirrors at the ends of the tube, forming an optical resonator.
- The gas mixture is excited by an electric discharge, which ionizes the helium atoms and transfers some of their energy to the neon atoms.
- The neon atoms then emit photons of various wavelengths, depending on the energy levels involved in the transitions.
- The most common wavelength emitted by the He-Ne laser is 632.8 nm, which is a bright red color.
- The photons of this wavelength are amplified by the stimulated emission process, as they travel back and forth between the mirrors, forming a coherent beam of red light that exits through one of the mirrors.
- The He-Ne laser is a four-level laser, meaning that it has four energy levels involved in the lasing process: two for the helium atoms and two for the neon atoms.
