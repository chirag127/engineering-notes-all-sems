# Temperature dependence of critical field for superconductors

- The critical field of a superconductor is the maximum magnetic field that the material can withstand without losing its superconductivity.
- The critical field depends on the temperature of the superconductor, and generally increases as the temperature decreases.
- For type I superconductors, which are pure metals or alloys, the critical field can be approximated by the empirical formula:

$$H_c(T) = H_c(0) \left[ 1 - \left( \frac{T}{T_c} \right)^2 \right]$$

where $H_c(0)$ is the critical field at absolute zero, and $T_c$ is the critical temperature of the superconductor.

- For type II superconductors, which are complex materials with mixed or layered structures, the critical field is divided into two regions: the lower critical field $H_{c1}$ and the upper critical field $H_{c2}$.
- The lower critical field $H_{c1}$ is the field at which the superconductor starts to allow some magnetic flux to penetrate its interior, forming vortices of supercurrents around the flux lines. The superconductor is still in the Meissner state, where it expels most of the magnetic field.
- The upper critical field $H_{c2}$ is the field at which the superconductor becomes fully normal, losing its superconductivity. The superconductor is in the normal state, where it behaves like a normal conductor with resistance.
- The lower and upper critical fields of type II superconductors depend on the temperature and the material properties, such as the coherence length, the penetration depth, and the anisotropy of the superconducting layers.
- The lower critical field can be estimated by the formula:

$$H_{c1}(T) = \frac{\phi_0}{4\pi \lambda^2(T)} \ln \left( \frac{\lambda(T)}{\xi(T)} \right)$$

where $\phi_0$ is the magnetic flux quantum, $\lambda(T)$ is the penetration depth, and $\xi(T)$ is the coherence length of the superconductor.

- The upper critical field can be calculated by the formula:

$$H_{c2}(T) = \frac{\phi_0}{2\pi \xi^2(T)}$$

where $\xi(T)$ is the coherence length of the superconductor.

- The coherence length and the penetration depth are temperature-dependent quantities that characterize the spatial extent of the superconducting order parameter and the magnetic field penetration, respectively.
- The coherence length decreases as the temperature increases, approaching zero at the critical temperature. The penetration depth increases as the temperature increases, diverging at the critical temperature.
- Therefore, the lower and upper critical fields of type II superconductors also decrease as the temperature increases, approaching zero at the critical temperature.
