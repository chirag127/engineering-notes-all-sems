# Dispersive Power

- Dispersive power is a measure of how much a transparent medium can separate different colors of light by refraction.
- Dispersive power depends on the refractive indices of the medium for different wavelengths of light.
- Dispersive power is denoted by the symbol ω and has no units.
- The formula for dispersive power is given by:

$$
\omega = \frac{\theta}{\delta} = \frac{\mu_v - \mu_r}{\mu_y - 1}
$$

where:

  - θ is the angular dispersion, which is the difference between the angles of deviation for violet and red light.
  - δ is the mean deviation, which is the angle of deviation for yellow light.
  - μv, μr, and μy are the refractive indices of the medium for violet, red, and yellow light, respectively.
  - A is the angle of the prism.

- A higher value of ω means that the medium can produce more dispersion of light, and vice versa.
- Dispersive power can be used to compare the dispersion of different media or different prisms.
