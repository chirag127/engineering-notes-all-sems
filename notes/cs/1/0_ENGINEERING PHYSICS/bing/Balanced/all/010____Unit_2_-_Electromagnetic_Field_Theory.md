## Unit 2 - Electromagnetic Field Theory

- Electromagnetic field theory is the study of the physical phenomena associated with electric and magnetic fields, and their interactions with matter and radiation.
- Electric fields are produced by electric charges, and magnetic fields are produced by electric currents and changing electric fields.
- Electric and magnetic fields can exert forces on charged particles, and can also induce electric currents and voltages in conductors and dielectrics.
- Electric and magnetic fields can also propagate through space as electromagnetic waves, which carry energy and momentum, and can interact with matter and other electromagnetic waves.
- The fundamental equations that describe the behavior of electric and magnetic fields are known as Maxwell's equations, which are derived from the laws of conservation of charge, energy, and momentum, and the experimental laws of Coulomb, Ampere, Faraday, and Gauss.
- Maxwell's equations can be written in different forms, such as differential, integral, or vector forms, depending on the choice of coordinate system and notation.
- Maxwell's equations can be solved for specific problems using various methods, such as boundary conditions, superposition principle, separation of variables, Green's functions, or numerical methods.
- Some important concepts and applications of electromagnetic field theory are:

  - Electrostatics: the study of electric fields and potentials in the absence of time-varying phenomena.
  - Magnetostatics: the study of magnetic fields and potentials in the absence of time-varying phenomena.
  - Electrodynamics: the study of electric and magnetic fields and potentials in the presence of time-varying phenomena, such as electric currents, changing electric fields, and electromagnetic waves.
  - Electromagnetic induction: the phenomenon of generating electric currents and voltages in conductors and dielectrics due to changing magnetic fields or moving conductors in magnetic fields.
  - Electromagnetic radiation: the phenomenon of emitting and absorbing electromagnetic waves by accelerating charges or changing currents.
  - Electromagnetic wave propagation: the study of how electromagnetic waves travel through different media, such as vacuum, air, or materials, and how they are affected by boundaries, interfaces, and obstacles.
  - Electromagnetic wave polarization: the property of electromagnetic waves that describes the orientation and shape of their electric and magnetic field vectors.
  - Electromagnetic wave interference: the phenomenon of superposing two or more electromagnetic waves to produce a resultant wave with a different amplitude and phase.
  - Electromagnetic wave diffraction: the phenomenon of bending and spreading of electromagnetic waves around edges and apertures.
  - Electromagnetic wave scattering: the phenomenon of deflecting and redistributing electromagnetic waves by obstacles or irregularities in the medium.
  - Electromagnetic wave reflection and refraction: the phenomena of changing the direction and speed of electromagnetic waves when they encounter a boundary between two different media.
  - Electromagnetic wave transmission and absorption: the phenomena of passing through and losing energy to a medium by electromagnetic waves.
  - Electromagnetic wave dispersion and attenuation: the phenomena of varying the speed and amplitude of electromagnetic waves with frequency and distance in a medium.
  - Electromagnetic wave modulation and demodulation: the processes of encoding and decoding information onto and from electromagnetic waves by varying their amplitude, frequency, phase, or polarization.
  - Electromagnetic wave antennas and radiation patterns: the devices and methods of generating and receiving electromagnetic waves, and the spatial distribution of their intensity and direction.
