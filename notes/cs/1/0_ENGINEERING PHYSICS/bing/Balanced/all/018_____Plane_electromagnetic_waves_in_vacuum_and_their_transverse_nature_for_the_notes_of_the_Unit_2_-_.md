# Plane electromagnetic waves in vacuum and their transverse nature

- A plane electromagnetic wave is a wave that has a constant amplitude and a constant frequency in a plane perpendicular to the direction of propagation.
- A plane electromagnetic wave consists of an electric field and a magnetic field that oscillate in phase and are perpendicular to each other and to the direction of propagation.
- A plane electromagnetic wave can be represented by the following equations:

  - E = E_0 sin(kz - wt + f) i
  - B = B_0 sin(kz - wt + f) j

  where E_0 and B_0 are the amplitudes of the electric and magnetic fields, k is the wave number, w is the angular frequency, f is the phase constant, i and j are the unit vectors along the x and y axes, and z is the direction of propagation.
- A plane electromagnetic wave travels in a vacuum with a constant speed c, which is equal to the speed of light, c = 3 x 10^8 m/s.
- A plane electromagnetic wave satisfies the following relations:

  - c = w/k
  - E_0/B_0 = c
  - k = 2p/l
  - w = 2pf

  where l is the wavelength and f is the frequency of the wave.
- A plane electromagnetic wave is transverse in nature, meaning that the electric and magnetic fields vibrate in a plane perpendicular to the direction of propagation.
- The transverse nature of electromagnetic waves can be derived from Gauss's law for electric and magnetic fields, which state that the net electric and magnetic flux through any closed surface is zero.
- This implies that the electric and magnetic fields have no component along the direction of propagation, and therefore they must be perpendicular to it.
- The transverse nature of electromagnetic waves can also be verified by using the right-hand rule, which states that if the thumb of the right hand points in the direction of propagation, the fingers curl in the direction of the electric field, and the palm faces the direction of the magnetic field.
