# Absent spectra

- Absent spectra are the missing orders of principal maxima in the diffraction pattern of a plane grating.
- They occur when the minima due to diffraction coincide with the maxima due to interference for a certain order of spectrum.
- The condition for absent spectra is given by the equation: n = N sin θ, where n is the order of spectrum, N is the number of lines per unit length of the grating, and θ is the angle of diffraction.
- The order of the first absent spectrum is equal to the number of lines per unit length of the grating.
- The order of the second absent spectrum is equal to twice the number of lines per unit length of the grating, and so on.
- Absent spectra reduce the resolving power of the grating, as they create gaps in the spectrum.
- Absent spectra can be avoided by using a blazed grating, which has a sawtooth-shaped profile that reflects more light in a particular direction.
