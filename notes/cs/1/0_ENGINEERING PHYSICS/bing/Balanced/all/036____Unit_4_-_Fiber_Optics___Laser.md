## Unit 4 - Fiber Optics & Laser

- Fiber optics are thin strands of glass or plastic that can transmit light signals over long distances.
- Fiber optics have many advantages over conventional wires, such as higher bandwidth, lower loss, immunity to electromagnetic interference, and security.
- Fiber optics are widely used in telecommunications, data transmission, medical imaging, sensing, and illumination.

- Laser is an acronym for light amplification by stimulated emission of radiation. It is a device that produces a coherent and intense beam of light of a single wavelength and color.
- Laser light is different from ordinary light in three ways: it is monochromatic, coherent, and collimated.
- Monochromatic means that the laser light has only one wavelength and color. Coherent means that the laser light waves are in phase with each other and have the same direction. Collimated means that the laser light beam is very narrow and does not diverge much over distance.
- Laser light can be generated by different types of media, such as gases, liquids, solids, or fibers. The media are called the gain medium, and they are excited by an external energy source, such as electricity, light, or chemical reaction. The excitation causes the atoms or molecules in the gain medium to emit photons of a specific wavelength and energy.
- The photons are then amplified by a process called stimulated emission, in which an incoming photon causes an excited atom or molecule to emit another photon of the same wavelength, energy, and phase. The photons are then reflected back and forth by two mirrors, one of which is partially transparent, forming a resonant cavity. The partially transparent mirror allows some of the photons to escape as the laser beam.

- Fiber lasers are a sub-category of diode pumped solid state lasers which utilize a doped optical fiber core as the amplification medium. They contain a spool of fiber optic cable which has a core that has been doped with a variety of rare earth elements from the lanthanide family of the periodic table.
- The rare earth elements, such as ytterbium or erbium, are responsible for generating the desired laser light. The fiber core is surrounded by a cladding layer that has a lower refractive index than the core, which ensures that the light is confined within the core by total internal reflection.
- The fiber laser is powered by a diode laser, which pumps light into the fiber core through a coupler. The pump light excites the rare earth ions in the core, creating a population inversion. The population inversion is the condition in which there are more atoms or molecules in an excited state than in a lower energy state, which is necessary for laser action.
- The pump light also serves as the seed light for the laser, which is amplified by the stimulated emission of the rare earth ions. The laser light travels along the fiber core, and is reflected back and forth by two fiber Bragg gratings, which act as the mirrors of the resonant cavity. The fiber Bragg gratings are periodic variations of the refractive index of the fiber core, which reflect light of a specific wavelength and transmit light of other wavelengths. The fiber Bragg gratings determine the wavelength and the bandwidth of the laser output.
- Fiber lasers have many advantages over other types of lasers, such as high efficiency, compact size, low maintenance, high beam quality, and flexibility. Fiber lasers can also be easily integrated with other fiber optic components, such as modulators, switches, filters, and sensors. Fiber lasers have a wide range of applications, such as material processing, medical surgery, telecommunications, spectroscopy, and defense.
