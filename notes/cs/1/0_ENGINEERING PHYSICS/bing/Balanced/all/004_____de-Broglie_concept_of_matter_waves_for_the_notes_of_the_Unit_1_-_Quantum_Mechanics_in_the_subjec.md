# De-Broglie Concept of Matter Waves

- De-Broglie concept of matter waves is based on the idea that matter has a dual nature, i.e., it can exhibit both particle-like and wave-like properties depending on the situation .
- De-Broglie proposed this concept in 1924 in his PhD thesis, inspired by the photon theory of light by Einstein .
- According to De-Broglie, when matter is in motion, it shows wave-like properties such as interference and diffraction, and when it is at rest, it shows particle-like properties such as mass and momentum .
- De-Broglie derived a relation between the wavelength (λ) of the matter wave and the momentum (p) of the particle as follows :

  - λ = h/p
  - where h is the Planck's constant.

- De-Broglie's concept of matter waves was experimentally verified by Davisson and Germer in 1927, who observed the diffraction of electrons by a nickel crystal .
- De-Broglie's concept of matter waves has important applications in quantum mechanics, such as explaining the quantization of angular momentum in the hydrogen atom, the uncertainty principle, the wave-particle duality, and the Schrödinger equation .
