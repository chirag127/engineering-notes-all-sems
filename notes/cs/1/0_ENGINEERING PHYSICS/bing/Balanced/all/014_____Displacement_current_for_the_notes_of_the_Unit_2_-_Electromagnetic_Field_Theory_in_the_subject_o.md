# Displacement current

- Displacement current is a phenomenon in electromagnetism that is analogous to an ordinary electric current, but occurs when the electric field changes with time .
- Displacement current density is the quantity ∂D/∂t, where D is the electric displacement field, and t is time.
- Displacement current density has the same units as electric current density (A/m^2^), and it is a source of the magnetic field just as actual current is.
- Displacement current was introduced by James Clerk Maxwell in his equations to explain the magnetic fields produced by changing electric fields, and to make the equations consistent with the law of conservation of charge .
- Displacement current plays a central role in the propagation of electromagnetic waves, such as light and radio waves, through empty space or dielectric materials .
- A traveling, varying magnetic field is everywhere associated with a periodically changing electric field that may be conceived in terms of a displacement current.
- Displacement current allows the electric and magnetic fields to be coupled when the electric flux is time-varying.
