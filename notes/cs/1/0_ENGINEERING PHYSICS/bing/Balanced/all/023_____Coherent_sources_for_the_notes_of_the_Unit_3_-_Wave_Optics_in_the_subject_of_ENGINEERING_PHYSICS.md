# Coherent sources for the notes of the Unit 3 - Wave Optics in the subject of ENGINEERING PHYSICS

- Wave optics is the branch of physics that studies the nature and behavior of light as a wave phenomenon.
- Interference is the phenomenon of superposition of two or more waves of the same frequency and same amplitude, resulting in the formation of bright and dark fringes.
- Coherent sources are the sources of light that produce waves that have a constant phase difference, the same frequency and the same amplitude .
- Coherent sources are necessary for the observation of interference patterns, as they ensure that the positions of maxima and minima are fixed and stable .
- Examples of coherent sources are lasers, which produce monochromatic, parallel and unbroken wave chains, and two slits illuminated by a single source of light, which act as secondary coherent sources.
- Incoherent sources are the sources of light that produce waves that have a random or changing phase difference, different frequencies or different amplitudes.
- Incoherent sources do not produce stable interference patterns, as the positions of maxima and minima vary with time and space.
- Examples of incoherent sources are ordinary light bulbs, which produce polychromatic, divergent and broken wave chains, and two independent sources of light, which act as primary incoherent sources.
- Coherence can be classified into two types: temporal coherence and spatial coherence.
- Temporal coherence is the measure of the correlation between the phases of a wave at different points in time.
- Temporal coherence depends on the bandwidth or spectrum of the source, and is inversely proportional to it.
- Temporal coherence determines the visibility of interference fringes produced by a single source of light.
- Spatial coherence is the measure of the correlation between the phases of a wave at different points in space.
- Spatial coherence depends on the size and shape of the source, and is inversely proportional to it.
- Spatial coherence determines the visibility of interference fringes produced by two sources of light.
- Coherence has many applications in physics, such as holography, interferometry, spectroscopy, lasers, optical communication, etc .
