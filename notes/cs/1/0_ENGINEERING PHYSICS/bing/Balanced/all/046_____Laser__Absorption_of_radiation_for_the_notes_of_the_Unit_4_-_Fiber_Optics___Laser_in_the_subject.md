# Laser: Absorption of radiation

- Absorption of radiation is the process by which electrons in the ground state absorb energy from photons to jump into the higher energy level.
- The amount of energy absorbed by an electron is equal to the difference between the energy levels of the initial and final states.
- The absorption of radiation depends on the wavelength of the photon and the energy gap of the material.
- The absorption coefficient is a measure of how much radiation is absorbed by a unit length of the material. It is inversely proportional to the penetration depth of the radiation.
- When laser radiation is applied to the surface of a material, part of it is absorbed by the conduction electrons in a thin surface layer and rapidly converted into heat through collisions between the electrons and the lattice ions .
- The absorption of laser radiation can cause various effects on the material, such as melting, vaporization, ablation, or phase transformation.
- The absorption of laser radiation can also be used for various applications, such as laser welding, cutting, drilling, marking, or engraving.
