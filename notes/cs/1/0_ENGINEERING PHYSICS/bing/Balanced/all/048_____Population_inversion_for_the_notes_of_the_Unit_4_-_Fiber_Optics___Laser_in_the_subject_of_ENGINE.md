# Population inversion

- Population inversion is a condition in which the number of atoms or molecules in an excited state is greater than the number in the ground state.
- Population inversion is necessary for the operation of a laser, as it allows stimulated emission to dominate over absorption.
- Population inversion can be achieved by various methods, such as optical pumping, electric discharge, chemical reaction, or nuclear reaction.
- Population inversion can be described by the Boltzmann distribution, which gives the relative population of atoms or molecules in different energy levels as a function of temperature.
- Population inversion can also be quantified by the inversion factor, which is the ratio of the population in the upper laser level to the population in the lower laser level.
- Population inversion can be maintained by a continuous or pulsed supply of energy to the system, which keeps the atoms or molecules in the excited state.
