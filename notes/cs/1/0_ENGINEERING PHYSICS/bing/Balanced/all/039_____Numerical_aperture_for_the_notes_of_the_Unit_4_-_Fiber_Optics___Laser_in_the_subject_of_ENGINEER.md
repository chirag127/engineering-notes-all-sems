# Numerical Aperture for the Notes of the Unit 4 - Fiber Optics & Laser in the Subject of Engineering Physics

- Numerical aperture (NA) is a measure of the ability of an optical fiber to collect or confine the incident light ray inside it .
- It is among the most basic property of optical fiber and shows the efficiency with which light is collected inside the fiber in order to get propagated.
- Numerical aperture is commonly used in microscopy to describe the acceptance cone of an objective (and hence its light-gathering ability and resolution), and in fiber optics, in which it describes the range of angles within which light that is incident on the fiber will be transmitted along it.
- Numerical aperture is defined as the sine of half of the angle of the fiber's light acceptance cone. That is, NA = sin θa, where θa is called the acceptance angle .
- The acceptance angle is the maximum angle at which the light can enter the fiber and still be guided along the core .
- The numerical aperture depends on the refractive indices of the core and the cladding of the fiber, as well as the wavelength of the light  .
- The numerical aperture can be calculated by the formula NA = √(n1^2 - n2^2), where n1 is the refractive index of the core and n2 is the refractive index of the cladding  .
- The numerical aperture can also be calculated by the formula NA = n0 sin θ0, where n0 is the refractive index of the medium outside the fiber and θ0 is the half-angle of the cone of light that can enter the fiber from the medium .
- The numerical aperture determines the maximum angle of incidence for which total internal reflection occurs at the core-cladding interface, and hence the maximum amount of light that can be coupled into the fiber  .
- The numerical aperture also affects the bandwidth, attenuation, and dispersion of the fiber, as well as the number of modes that can propagate in the fiber  .
- The numerical aperture is usually specified for a particular wavelength of light, as it varies slightly with the wavelength due to the dispersion of the refractive indices .
- The numerical aperture is a dimensionless quantity and typically ranges from 0.1 to 0.3 for most optical fibers  .
