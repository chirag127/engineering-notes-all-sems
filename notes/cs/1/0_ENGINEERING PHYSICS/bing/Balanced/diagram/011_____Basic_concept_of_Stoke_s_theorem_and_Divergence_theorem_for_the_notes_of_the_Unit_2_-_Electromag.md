### Stoke's theorem
- Stoke's theorem relates a surface integral of the curl of a vector field to a line integral of the vector field along the boundary of the surface.
- The surface must be orientable, meaning it has two distinct sides, and the boundary must be a simple closed curve.
- The theorem can be expressed as:

$$\iint_S \nabla \times \mathbf{F} \cdot d\mathbf{S} = \oint_{\partial S} \mathbf{F} \cdot d\mathbf{r}$$

- Where $\mathbf{F}$ is a vector field, $S$ is a surface, $\partial S$ is the boundary of $S$, and $d\mathbf{S}$ and $d\mathbf{r}$ are the differential surface and line elements, respectively.
- The theorem can be seen as a generalization of the fundamental theorem of calculus for two dimensions, which relates the integral of the derivative of a function to the value of the function at the endpoints of an interval.
- The theorem can be used to simplify the calculation of surface integrals, especially when the curl of the vector field is easier to evaluate than the vector field itself.
- The theorem can also be used to prove some important results in electromagnetism, such as the Maxwell-Faraday equation and the Ampere-Maxwell law.

### Divergence theorem
- Divergence theorem relates a volume integral of the divergence of a vector field to a surface integral of the vector field over the boundary of the volume.
- The volume must be a closed and bounded region in three dimensions, and the surface must be smooth and oriented outward.
- The theorem can be expressed as:

$$\iiint_V \nabla \cdot \mathbf{F} \, dV = \iint_{\partial V} \mathbf{F} \cdot d\mathbf{S}$$
