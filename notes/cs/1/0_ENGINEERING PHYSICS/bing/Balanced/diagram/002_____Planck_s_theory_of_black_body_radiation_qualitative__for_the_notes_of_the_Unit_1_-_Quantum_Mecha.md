### Planck's theory of black body radiation (qualitative)

- A black body is a hypothetical body that completely absorbs all radiant energy falling upon it, reaches some equilibrium temperature, and then reemits that energy as quickly as it absorbs it.
- The spectrum of the radiation emitted by a black body depends only on its temperature, not on its shape, size, or material.
- The classical theory of electromagnetism failed to explain the observed spectrum of black body radiation, especially at high frequencies (short wavelengths), where it predicted an infinite amount of energy, known as the ultraviolet catastrophe .
- In 1900, German physicist Max Planck heuristically derived a formula for the observed spectrum by assuming that a hypothetical electrically charged oscillator in a cavity that contained black-body radiation could only change its energy in a minimal increment, E, that was proportional to the frequency of its associated electromagnetic wave .
- This increment, E, is called a quantum of energy, and Planck's constant, h, is the proportionality factor .
- Planck's law states that the spectral density of electromagnetic radiation emitted by a black body in thermal equilibrium at a given temperature T is given by :

where B(ν,T) is the spectral radiance, ν is the frequency, h is Planck's constant, c is the speed of light, k is Boltzmann's constant, and e is the base of the natural logarithm .

- Planck's law can also be expressed in terms of wavelength, λ, instead of frequency, by using the relation ν = c/λ :

![Planck's law formula in terms of wavelength](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/24e7b4e696c6a4b5f4f36f0f721d9f8a9f8a86d9)

where B(λ,T) is the spectral radiance per unit wavelength .

- Planck's law implies that the energy of a black body radiation is quantized, meaning that it can only take discrete values, and that the energy is proportional to the frequency of the radiation .

where λmax is the wavelength of maximum intensity, T is the temperature, and b is Wien's displacement constant .

- Planck's theory of black body radiation was the first successful application of quantum theory, and paved the way for the development of quantum mechanics in the early 20th century .
