### Acceptance cone

- The acceptance cone is the cone-shaped region of space around the optical fiber that defines the range of angles within which light can enter the fiber and be propagated through it .
- The acceptance cone is derived by rotating the acceptance angle, which is the maximum angle with the axis of the optical fiber at which light can enter the fiber, around the fiber axis .
- The acceptance cone depends on the numerical aperture (NA) of the fiber, which is a measure of the light-gathering ability and resolution of the fiber. The NA is given by the formula:

    NA = n0 sin θa

    where n0 is the refractive index of the surrounding medium, and θa is the acceptance angle.

- The larger the acceptance cone, the larger the NA, which means that the fiber can accept and propagate more light. However, a larger NA also means a larger modal dispersion, which limits the bandwidth and transmission distance of the fiber.
- The acceptance cone can be illustrated by the following diagram:

    ![Acceptance cone diagram](https://semesters.in/wp-content/uploads/2020/06/Acceptance-Cone-of-Optical-Fiber.png)

    In the diagram, n1 is the refractive index of the core, n2 is the refractive index of the cladding, and n0 is the refractive index of the surrounding medium. The acceptance angle θa is the angle between the fiber axis and the ray that grazes the core-cladding interface. The critical angle θc is the angle of incidence at the core-cladding interface for which the angle of refraction is 90°. The relation between θa and θc is given by Snell's law:

    n0 sin θa = n1 sin θc

    The NA can also be expressed in terms of the refractive indices of the core and the cladding as:

    NA = √(n1^2 - n2^2)

    where n1 > n2.
