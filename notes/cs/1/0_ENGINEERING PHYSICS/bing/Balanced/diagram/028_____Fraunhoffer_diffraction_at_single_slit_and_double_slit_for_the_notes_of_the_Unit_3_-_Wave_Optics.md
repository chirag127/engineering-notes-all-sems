### Fraunhofer diffraction at single slit and double slit

- Fraunhofer diffraction is a type of diffraction that occurs when plane waves are incident on a diffracting object, and the diffraction pattern is observed at a sufficiently long distance from the object (in the far-field region), or at the focal plane of a converging lens.
- Fraunhofer diffraction can be used to analyze the shape and size of the diffracting object, such as a slit, a grating, or an aperture.
- Fraunhofer diffraction at a single slit is the simplest case of diffraction, where a parallel beam of monochromatic light of wavelength `λ` is incident on a slit of width `d`.
- According to Huygens' principle, every point of the wavefront in the plane of the slit acts as a secondary source and sends secondary waves in all directions.
- The secondary waves interfere constructively or destructively at different points on a screen placed at a large distance from the slit, forming a diffraction pattern of bright and dark fringes.
- The intensity of the diffraction pattern at an angle `θ` from the center of the slit is given by the formula :

```
I(θ) = I0 (sin(πd sin(θ)/λ) / (πd sin(θ)/λ))^2
```

where `I0` is the intensity at the center of the slit.

- The minima of the diffraction pattern occur when the argument of the sine function is equal to an integer multiple of `π`, i.e., :

```
d sin(θ) = nλ, n = ±1, ±2, ±3, ...
```

- The maxima of the diffraction pattern occur when the argument of the sine function is equal to an odd multiple of `π/2`, i.e., :

```
d sin(θ) = (2n + 1)λ/2, n = 0, ±1, ±2, ...
```

- The width of the central maximum is twice the width of the other maxima, and is inversely proportional to the width of the slit .
- Fraunhofer diffraction at a double slit is a more complex case of diffraction, where a parallel beam of monochromatic light of wavelength `λ` is incident on two slits of width `a` separated by a distance `d`  .
- The diffraction pattern of a double slit is the result of the superposition of the diffraction patterns of each slit, and the interference of the secondary waves from each slit  .
- The intensity of the diffraction pattern at an angle `θ` from the center of the slits is given by the formula  :

```
I(θ) = I0 (sin(πa sin(θ)/λ) / (πa sin(θ)/λ))^2 (cos(πd sin(θ)/λ))^2
```

where `I0` is the intensity at the center of the slits.

- The first factor in the formula represents the single slit diffraction pattern, and the second factor represents the interference pattern of two point sources  .
- The minima of the diffraction pattern occur when either factor is equal to zero, i.e.,  :

```
a sin(θ) = nλ, n = ±1, ±2, ±3, ... (single slit minima)
d sin(θ) = (n + 1/2)λ, n = 0, ±1, ±2, ... (double slit minima)
```
