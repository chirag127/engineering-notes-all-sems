### Principles of laser action

- Laser is an acronym for **Light Amplification by Stimulated Emission of Radiation**.
- Laser action is the process of producing a coherent, monochromatic, and intense beam of light by stimulating the atoms or molecules of a medium to emit photons in a synchronized manner .
- The basic principles of laser action are as follows  :

  - **Spontaneous emission**: This is the natural process of an atom or molecule in an excited state (higher energy level) to decay to a lower energy level by emitting a photon of a random frequency, phase, and direction.
  - **Stimulated emission**: This is the process of an atom or molecule in an excited state to decay to a lower energy level by emitting a photon of the same frequency, phase, and direction as an incident photon. This results in the amplification of the incident photon and the creation of a coherent beam of light.
  - **Stimulated absorption**: This is the process of an atom or molecule in a lower energy level to absorb an incident photon and jump to a higher energy level. This results in the attenuation of the incident photon and the creation of a population of excited atoms or molecules.
  - **Non-radiative decay**: This is the process of an atom or molecule in an excited state to lose energy by other means than emitting a photon, such as collisions, vibrations, or heat. This results in the reduction of the population of excited atoms or molecules and the efficiency of the laser action.
  - **Population inversion**: This is the condition in which the number of atoms or molecules in an excited state is greater than the number of atoms or molecules in a lower energy state. This is necessary for the laser action to occur, as it ensures that the stimulated emission is more probable than the stimulated absorption.
  - **Pumping**: This is the process of supplying energy to the medium to create a population inversion. This can be done by various methods, such as electrical discharge, optical radiation, chemical reaction, or nuclear reaction.
  - **Optical cavity**: This is the structure that contains the medium and reflects the light back and forth to enhance the stimulated emission and the amplification of the light. The cavity usually consists of two mirrors, one of which is partially transparent to allow the output of the laser beam. The cavity also determines the wavelength and the mode of the laser beam.
