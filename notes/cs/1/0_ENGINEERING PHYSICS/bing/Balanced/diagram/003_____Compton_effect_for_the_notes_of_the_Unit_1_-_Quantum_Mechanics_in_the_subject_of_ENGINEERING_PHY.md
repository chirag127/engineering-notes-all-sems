### Compton effect

- The Compton effect is the phenomenon of the increase in wavelength of X-rays and other energetic electromagnetic radiations that have been elastically scattered by electrons.
- The Compton effect was discovered by Arthur Compton in 1923, who won the Nobel Prize in Physics for his work.
- The Compton effect demonstrates the particle nature of electromagnetic radiation, as it shows that photons can collide with electrons and transfer some of their energy and momentum to them .
- The Compton effect can be explained by the conservation of energy and momentum for the collision between a photon and an electron.

- The Compton effect has applications in gamma spectroscopy, X-ray astronomy, and medical imaging .
