### Rayleigh’s criterion of resolution

- Rayleigh’s criterion of resolution is a measure of the ability of an optical system to distinguish two adjacent point sources of light, such as stars or distant objects.
- According to Rayleigh’s criterion, two point sources are just resolvable when the central maximum of the diffraction pattern of one source coincides with the first minimum of the diffraction pattern of the other source. See Figure 1.
- Rayleigh’s criterion can be applied to circular apertures, such as lenses or telescopes, where the diffraction pattern is an Airy disk. In this case, two point sources are just resolvable when the center of one Airy disk falls on the first minimum of the other Airy disk. See Figure 2.
- Rayleigh’s criterion can be expressed mathematically as:

  - θ = 1.22 λ / D

  - where θ is the minimum angular separation between the two point sources, λ is the wavelength of light, and D is the diameter of the aperture.
- Rayleigh’s criterion is based on the assumption that the human eye can detect the contrast between the central maximum and the first minimum of the diffraction pattern. However, in practice, the contrast may be too low or the sources may be too faint to be resolved by the eye. Therefore, Rayleigh’s criterion is only a theoretical limit of resolution and not a guarantee of resolution.
