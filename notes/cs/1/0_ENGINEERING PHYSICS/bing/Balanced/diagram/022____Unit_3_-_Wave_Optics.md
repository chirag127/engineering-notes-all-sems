## Unit 3 - Wave Optics

Wave optics is the branch of physics that studies the behavior of light as a wave. It explains phenomena such as interference, diffraction, polarization, and scattering of light.

Some of the main topics covered in this unit are:

- Huygens' principle: It states that every point on a wavefront acts as a source of secondary spherical wavelets, and the new wavefront is the envelope of these wavelets.
- Interference of light: It is the phenomenon of superposition of two or more coherent light waves, resulting in a pattern of bright and dark fringes.
- Young's double slit experiment: It is an experiment that demonstrates the interference of light by passing it through two narrow slits separated by a small distance.
- Diffraction of light: It is the phenomenon of bending of light around the edges of an obstacle or an aperture.
- Single slit diffraction: It is the diffraction pattern produced by a single narrow slit illuminated by a monochromatic light source.
- Polarization of light: It is the phenomenon of restricting the vibrations of light to a single plane.
- Brewster's law: It states that the angle of incidence for which the reflected light is completely polarized is equal to the polarizing angle, and the sum of the polarizing angle and the angle of refraction is 90 degrees.
- Scattering of light: It is the phenomenon of deflection of light by the particles of the medium through which it passes.
- Rayleigh scattering: It is the scattering of light by particles that are much smaller than the wavelength of light.
- Tyndall effect: It is the phenomenon of scattering of light by colloidal particles, resulting in a blue color of the sky and a red color of the sun at sunrise and sunset.
