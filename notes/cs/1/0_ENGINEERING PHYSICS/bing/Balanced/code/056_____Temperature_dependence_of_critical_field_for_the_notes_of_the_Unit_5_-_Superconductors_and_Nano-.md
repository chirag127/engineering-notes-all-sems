### Temperature dependence of critical field for superconductors

- The critical field of a superconductor is the maximum magnetic field that the material can withstand without losing its superconductivity.
- The critical field depends on the temperature of the superconductor, and generally decreases as the temperature increases.
- The temperature dependence of the critical field can be described approximately by the empirical formula:

```math
B_c(T) = B_c(0) [1 - (T/T_c)^2]
```

where $B_c(0)$ is the critical field at absolute zero temperature, and $T_c$ is the critical temperature of the superconductor.

- The formula is valid for low-temperature superconductors (LTS), which have $T_c$ below 30 K, and for magnetic fields much smaller than the upper critical field $H_{c2}$, which is the maximum field that can penetrate the superconductor in the mixed state.
- For high-temperature superconductors (HTS), which have $T_c$ above 77 K, the temperature dependence of the critical field is more complicated and depends on the material properties and the orientation of the magnetic field.
- Some HTS show an anomalous temperature dependence of the orbital part of the upper critical field $H_{c2}$, which has a negative curvature at any temperature . This can be explained by the influence of localization effects in two-dimensional or quasi-two-dimensional systems.
- The lower critical field $H_{c1}$, which is the minimum field that can penetrate the superconductor in the mixed state, also depends on the temperature and the material properties of the superconductor.
- For some unconventional superconductors, such as the heavy-fermion compound CeCoIn$_5$, the temperature dependence of the lower critical field shows a non-monotonic behavior, which can be attributed to the presence of multiple superconducting bands or a spin-triplet component in the superconducting wavefunction.
