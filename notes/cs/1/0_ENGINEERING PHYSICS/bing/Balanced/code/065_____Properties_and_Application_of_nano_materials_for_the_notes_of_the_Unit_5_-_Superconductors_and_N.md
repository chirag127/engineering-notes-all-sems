### Properties and Applications of Nanomaterials

Nanomaterials are materials that have at least one dimension in the range of 1 to 100 nanometers (nm). They exhibit novel physical and chemical properties that are different from those of bulk materials due to their high surface area to volume ratio, quantum confinement effects, and enhanced reactivity .

Some of the properties of nanomaterials are:

- Optical properties: Nanomaterials can absorb, emit, and scatter light in different ways depending on their size, shape, and composition. They can also exhibit plasmonic effects, which are the collective oscillations of electrons on the surface of metal nanoparticles. These properties can be used to create optical detectors, sensors, lasers, displays, solar cells, and biomedical devices .
- Electrical properties: Nanomaterials can have high electrical conductivity, low resistance, and tunable band gaps depending on their structure and doping. They can also exhibit quantum effects, such as Coulomb blockade and single-electron tunneling, which are the discrete transfer of electrons through nanoscale structures. These properties can be used to create transistors, diodes, memory devices, nanowires, and quantum dots .
- Magnetic properties: Nanomaterials can have high magnetic moments, low coercivity, and superparamagnetism depending on their size, shape, and composition. They can also exhibit giant magnetoresistance, which is the change in electrical resistance due to an applied magnetic field. These properties can be used to create magnetic storage devices, sensors, actuators, and biomedical applications .
- Mechanical properties: Nanomaterials can have high strength, stiffness, hardness, and elasticity depending on their structure and defects. They can also exhibit enhanced fracture toughness, wear resistance, and self-healing capabilities due to their nanoscale features. These properties can be used to create coatings, lubricants, adhesives, and nanodevices .
- Thermal properties: Nanomaterials can have high thermal conductivity, low thermal expansion, and high melting point depending on their structure and defects. They can also exhibit phonon confinement, which is the restriction of lattice vibrations due to the nanoscale dimensions. These properties can be used to create heat sinks, thermoelectric devices, and nanofluids .
- Catalytic properties: Nanomaterials can have high catalytic activity, selectivity, and stability due to their high surface area, active sites, and reactivity. They can also exhibit size-dependent and shape-dependent effects, which are the influence of the nanoparticle size and shape on the catalytic performance. These properties can be used to create catalysts, fuel cells, and environmental remediation  .

Some of the applications of nanomaterials are:

- Electronics: Nanomaterials can be used to create nanoscale devices and circuits that have improved performance, functionality, and efficiency. For example, carbon nanotubes and graphene can be used as nanowires, transistors, and electrodes due to their high electrical conductivity and mechanical strength. Quantum dots and nanocrystals can be used as light-emitting diodes, lasers, and solar cells due to their tunable optical properties. Nanoparticles and nanocomposites can be used as sensors, actuators, and memory devices due to their magnetic, thermal, and piezoelectric properties .
- Medicine: Nanomaterials can be used to create nanomedicines and nanodevices that have enhanced diagnostic, therapeutic, and preventive capabilities. For example, gold nanoparticles and magnetic nanoparticles can be used as contrast agents, drug carriers, and hyperthermia agents due to their optical, magnetic, and thermal properties. Nanoparticles and nanofibers can be used as scaffolds, coatings, and wound dressings due to their biocompatibility, biodegradability, and antibacterial properties. Nanosensors and nanorobots can be used as biosensors, biochips, and nanosurgery tools due to their sensitivity, specificity, and mobility  .
