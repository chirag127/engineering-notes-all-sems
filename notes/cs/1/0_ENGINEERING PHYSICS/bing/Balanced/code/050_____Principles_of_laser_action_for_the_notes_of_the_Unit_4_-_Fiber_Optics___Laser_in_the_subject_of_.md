# Principles of laser action

- Laser is an acronym for **Light Amplification by Stimulated Emission of Radiation**.
- Laser action is the process of producing a coherent, monochromatic, and intense beam of light by stimulating atoms or molecules to emit photons of the same frequency, phase, and direction.
- The basic principles of laser action are :
  - **Spontaneous emission**: An atom or molecule in an excited state can decay to a lower energy state by emitting a photon of energy equal to the energy difference between the two states. This process is random and isotropic, meaning that the photon can be emitted in any direction and with any phase.
  - **Stimulated emission**: An atom or molecule in an excited state can also decay to a lower energy state by emitting a photon of energy equal to the energy difference between the two states, but only if it is stimulated by another photon of the same frequency, phase, and direction. This process is coherent and directional, meaning that the emitted photon has the same frequency, phase, and direction as the stimulating photon. The two photons then travel together as a single wave.
  - **Stimulated absorption**: An atom or molecule in a lower energy state can absorb a photon of energy equal to the energy difference between the two states and jump to a higher energy state. This process is the reverse of stimulated emission and reduces the intensity of the light beam.
  - **Non-radiative decay**: An atom or molecule in an excited state can also decay to a lower energy state by transferring its excess energy to other atoms or molecules through collisions or vibrations. This process is non-radiative and does not involve the emission or absorption of photons. It causes the loss of energy and coherence of the light beam.
  - **Population inversion**: For laser action to occur, there must be more atoms or molecules in the excited state than in the lower energy state, so that the rate of stimulated emission is higher than the rate of stimulated absorption. This condition is called population inversion and can be achieved by various methods of pumping or excitation, such as electrical discharge, optical pumping, chemical reaction, or nuclear reaction.
  - **Amplification of light**: When a light beam passes through a medium with population inversion, it undergoes amplification by stimulated emission, as more photons are added to the beam by the excited atoms or molecules. The amplification factor depends on the length of the medium, the cross-section of the beam, and the degree of population inversion. To increase the amplification, the medium is usually placed between two mirrors that reflect the light back and forth, forming a resonant cavity or an optical feedback system. One of the mirrors is partially transparent, allowing some of the amplified light to escape as the laser output.
