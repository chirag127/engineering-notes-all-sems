### Absent spectra

- Absent spectra are the missing orders of principal maxima in the diffraction pattern of a plane grating.
- They occur when the minima due to diffraction coincide with the maxima due to interference for a certain order of diffraction.
- The condition for absent spectra is given by the equation: `nN = mλ`, where `n` is the order of diffraction, `N` is the number of lines per unit length of the grating, `m` is an integer, and `λ` is the wavelength of light.
- The order of diffraction that is absent is given by `n = mλN`.
- For example, if `N = 5000` lines per cm and `λ = 5000` Å, then `n = 1` is absent, meaning the first order spectrum is missing.
- Absent spectra can be used to determine the grating constant `N` by measuring the wavelength of light that causes a certain order to disappear.
- Absent spectra can also be used to identify the elements present in a light source by comparing the missing wavelengths with the known emission spectra of the elements.
