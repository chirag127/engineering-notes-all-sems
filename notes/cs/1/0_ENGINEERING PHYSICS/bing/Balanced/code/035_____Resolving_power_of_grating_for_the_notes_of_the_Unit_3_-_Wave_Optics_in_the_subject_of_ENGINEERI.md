### Resolving power of grating

- Resolving power of grating is the ability of a grating to separate two spectral lines of very close wavelengths into distinct maxima .
- Resolving power of grating is measured by the ratio of the mean wavelength to the smallest difference in wavelengths that can be resolved, i.e. R.P. = λ/dλ .
- Resolving power of grating depends on the order of diffraction, the number of lines per unit length of the grating, and the width of the grating  .
- Resolving power of grating can be derived by considering the condition for two wavelengths λ and λ + dλ to be just resolved in the same order of diffraction n  .
- The condition is that the maximum of one wavelength coincides with the minimum of the other wavelength, i.e. the path difference between the two rays from the extreme ends of the grating is equal to half the wavelength difference  .
- Mathematically, this can be expressed as:

    `d(sinθ + sinα) = n(λ + dλ) - nλ`

    `d(sinθ + sinα) = ndλ`

    where d is the grating element, θ is the angle of diffraction, and α is the angle of incidence  .

- Dividing both sides by λ and rearranging, we get:

    `R.P. = λ/dλ = nd(sinθ + sinα)/λ`

    This is the expression for the resolving power of grating  .

- The resolving power of grating is maximum when sinθ + sinα = 1, i.e. when the angle of diffraction is 90°  .
- The resolving power of grating is also proportional to the number of lines illuminated by the incident light, i.e. N = b/d, where b is the width of the grating  .
- Therefore, the resolving power of grating can also be written as:

    `R.P. = λ/dλ = nN`

    This shows that the resolving power of grating increases with the order of diffraction and the number of lines  .
