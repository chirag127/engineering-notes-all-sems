### Nano-Materials: Introduction and properties of nano materials

- Nanomaterials are materials composed of a set of substances where at least one dimension is less than approximately 100 nm.
- The size of nanomaterials is measured in nanometers, which are one billionth of a meter or 10^-9^ m.
- Nanomaterials have unique physical and chemical properties that differ from those of bulk materials due to the quantum effects that occur at the nanoscale .
- Some of the properties of nanomaterials that are influenced by their size and shape include optical, electrical, magnetic, thermal, mechanical, catalytic, and biological properties .
- Nanomaterials can be classified based on various approaches, such as their dimensionality, morphology, composition, origin, or functionality .
- Some examples of nanomaterials are nanofilaments, nanotubes, nanowires, nanocables, nanothin films, dendrimers, quantum dots, nanocrystals, nanocomposites, and nanoceramics .
- Nanomaterials have many applications in various fields, such as electronics, energy, medicine, biotechnology, cosmetics, agriculture, environment, and defense   .
- Nanomaterials also have some advantages and disadvantages, depending on their properties, synthesis methods, and potential impacts on human health and the environment .
- Some of the advantages of nanomaterials are: enhanced performance, functionality, and efficiency; reduced material consumption and waste; improved durability and reliability; and new opportunities for innovation and discovery .
- Some of the disadvantages of nanomaterials are: high cost and complexity of production; lack of standardization and regulation; ethical and social issues; and possible toxicity and environmental risks .
