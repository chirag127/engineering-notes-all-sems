### Fiber optic communication principle

- Fiber optic communication is a method of transmitting information from one place to another by sending pulses of infrared light through an optical fiber.
- The light is a form of carrier wave that is modulated to carry information.
- Optical fiber is a thin, transparent fiber made of glass or plastic that can guide light along its length .
- The principle of fiber optic communication is based on total internal reflection, which occurs when light travels from a denser medium to a less dense medium and is reflected back at the interface .
- The optical fiber consists of a core, a cladding and a protective coating.
- The core is the central region of the fiber where the light is confined and propagated.
- The cladding is the outer layer of the fiber that surrounds the core and has a lower refractive index than the core, which ensures that the light is reflected back into the core.
- The coating is a material that protects the fiber from physical damage and environmental factors.
- The optical fiber can be classified into two types based on the number of modes or paths that the light can take within the fiber: single-mode fiber and multimode fiber .
- Single-mode fiber has a very thin core that allows only one mode of light to propagate, which reduces the distortion and attenuation of the signal and enables higher bandwidth and longer distance transmission .
- Multimode fiber has a thicker core that allows multiple modes of light to propagate, which increases the dispersion and attenuation of the signal and limits the bandwidth and distance of transmission .
- The advantages of fiber optic communication include: high data rate, low attenuation, immunity to electromagnetic interference, security, flexibility and reliability  .
- The disadvantages of fiber optic communication include: high cost, fragility, difficulty of splicing and connecting, and need for specialized equipment and skills  .
