# Einstein’s Coefficients for Fiber Optics & Laser

- Einstein’s coefficients are the probabilities of absorption and emission of electromagnetic radiation by an atom or a molecule.
- They are denoted by A 21 , B 21 and B 12 , where the subscripts 1 and 2 represent the lower and upper energy levels of the system, respectively.
- A 21 is the coefficient of spontaneous emission, which is the process of an atom or a molecule in the upper energy level emitting a photon and decaying to the lower energy level without any external influence.
- B 21 is the coefficient of stimulated emission, which is the process of an atom or a molecule in the upper energy level emitting a photon and decaying to the lower energy level when stimulated by another photon of the same frequency and phase.
- B 12 is the coefficient of absorption, which is the process of an atom or a molecule in the lower energy level absorbing a photon and jumping to the upper energy level.
- The Einstein coefficients are determined by the atomic or molecular system and do not depend on the state of the gas or the radiation field.
- The Einstein coefficients are related by the following equations:

  - A 21 /B 21 = 8πhν3/c3, where h is the Planck constant, ν is the frequency of the radiation, and c is the speed of light.
  - B 12 = B 21 , which implies that the probability of absorption is equal to the probability of stimulated emission for the same transition.

- The Einstein coefficients are important for understanding the principles of laser, which is a device that produces coherent and monochromatic light by stimulated emission of radiation.
- The laser operation requires a population inversion, which is a condition where the number of atoms or molecules in the upper energy level is greater than the number of atoms or molecules in the lower energy level.
- The population inversion can be achieved by various methods, such as optical pumping, electrical discharge, chemical reaction, etc.
- The laser also requires a resonant cavity, which is a structure that reflects the emitted photons back and forth, enhancing the stimulated emission process and creating a positive feedback loop.
- The laser output can be continuous or pulsed, depending on the mode of operation and the type of laser medium.
- Some examples of lasers are ruby laser, helium-neon laser, carbon dioxide laser, semiconductor laser, etc.
- Lasers have many applications in various fields, such as communication, medicine, industry, research, etc.
