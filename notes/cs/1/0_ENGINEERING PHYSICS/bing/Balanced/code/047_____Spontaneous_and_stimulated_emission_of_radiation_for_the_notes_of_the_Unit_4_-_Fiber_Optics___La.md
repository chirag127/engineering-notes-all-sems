### Spontaneous and stimulated emission of radiation

- Spontaneous emission is the process in which an atom or molecule in an excited state decays to a lower energy state and emits a photon of energy equal to the energy difference between the two states .
- Spontaneous emission occurs without any external influence and is random in direction, phase, and polarization .
- Spontaneous emission is the source of natural light, such as the glow of fireflies, stars, and fluorescent materials.
- Stimulated emission is the process in which an atom or molecule in an excited state is induced to emit a photon of the same energy, phase, polarization, and direction as an incident photon that matches the energy difference between the two states .
- Stimulated emission occurs when the incident photon interacts with the excited atom or molecule and causes it to drop to a lower energy state, releasing another photon in the process .
- Stimulated emission is the basis of laser operation, as it amplifies the incident light and produces coherent and monochromatic light .
