### Phase velocity and group velocity

- Phase velocity is the rate at which the phase of a wave propagates in any medium. It is given by the ratio of the angular frequency to the wave number: `v_p = ω/k`.
- Group velocity is the velocity with which the overall envelope shape of the wave's amplitudes propagates through space. It is given by the derivative of the angular frequency with respect to the wave number: `v_g = dω/dk`.
- Phase velocity and group velocity are related by Rayleigh's formula: `v_g = v_p + kv_p'` where `v_p' = dv_p/dk` is the derivative of the phase velocity with respect to the wave number.
- If the phase velocity is independent of the wave number, then the group velocity equals the phase velocity and there is no dispersion. This means that the wave shape does not change as it travels.
- In quantum mechanics, a wave packet is a superposition of plane waves with different wave numbers and frequencies. The wave packet represents the state of a particle in a continuous case.
- The phase velocity of a wave packet is related to the energy of the particle by the de Broglie relation: `v_p = E/ℏk` where `ℏ` is the reduced Planck constant.
- The group velocity of a wave packet is related to the momentum of the particle by the de Broglie relation: `v_g = ℏk/m` where `m` is the mass of the particle.
- For a free particle, the energy is given by `E = ℏ^2k^2/2m` and the phase velocity is `v_p = ℏk/2m`. The group velocity is twice the phase velocity: `v_g = 2v_p`.
- For a particle in a potential, the energy and the wave number are not constant and depend on the position. The phase velocity and the group velocity vary along the wave packet and are not equal.
