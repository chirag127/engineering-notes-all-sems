# Unit 3 - Wave Optics

- Wave optics, also called physical optics, is a branch of optics that studies the behavior of light when it exhibits wave characteristics, such as interference, diffraction, polarization, and other phenomena that cannot be explained by ray approximation of geometric optics  .
- Some of the important concepts and formulas in wave optics are:

  - Huygens' principle: Every point on a wavefront acts as a source of secondary spherical wavelets that spread out in all directions with the same speed as the wave. The new wavefront is the envelope of these wavelets .
  - Superposition principle: When two or more waves overlap in a medium, the resultant displacement at any point is the algebraic sum of the displacements due to each wave  .
  - Interference: The phenomenon of superposition of two or more coherent waves to produce a resultant wave of varying intensity  .
  - Young's double slit experiment: An experiment that demonstrates the interference of light waves from two coherent sources (slits) separated by a small distance. The interference pattern consists of alternate bright and dark fringes on a screen  .
  - Fringe width: The distance between two consecutive bright or dark fringes in the interference pattern. It is given by the formula:

    $$b = \frac{\lambda D}{d}$$

    where $\lambda$ is the wavelength of light, $D$ is the distance between the slits and the screen, and $d$ is the separation between the slits  .
  - Diffraction: The phenomenon of bending of light waves around the edges of an obstacle or an aperture  .
  - Single slit diffraction: The diffraction pattern produced by a single slit of width $a$. The pattern consists of a central bright maximum and several secondary maxima and minima on either side. The angular position of the first minimum is given by the formula:

    $$\sin \theta = \frac{\lambda}{a}$$

    where $\theta$ is the angle measured from the center of the slit, and $\lambda$ is the wavelength of light  .
  - Polarization: The phenomenon of restricting the vibration of light waves to a single plane  .
  - Malus' law: The law that states that the intensity of polarized light transmitted through a polarizer is proportional to the square of the cosine of the angle between the plane of polarization and the transmission axis of the polarizer. The formula is:

    $$I = I_0 \cos^2 \theta$$

    where $I$ is the transmitted intensity, $I_0$ is the incident intensity, and $\theta$ is the angle between the plane of polarization and the transmission axis  .
  - Brewster's law: The law that states that the angle of incidence for which the reflected light is completely polarized is equal to the polarizing angle. The formula is:

    $$\tan \theta_p = \frac{n_2}{n_1}$$

    where $\theta_p$ is the polarizing angle, and $n_1$ and $n_2$ are the refractive indices of the two media  .

- These are some of the main topics and formulas in wave optics. For more details and examples, please refer to the web search results   .
