### Phase velocity and group velocity

- Phase velocity is the velocity at which the phase of a wave propagates in a medium. It is given by the ratio of the angular frequency to the wave number: v_p = ω/k .
- Group velocity is the velocity at which the envelope or shape of a wave packet propagates in a medium. It is given by the derivative of the angular frequency with respect to the wave number: v_g = dω/dk .
- Phase velocity and group velocity are related by Rayleigh's formula: v_g = v_p + kv_p' , where v_p' is the derivative of the phase velocity with respect to the wave number.
- In quantum mechanics, phase velocity and group velocity have different interpretations for the associated particle. The phase velocity is related to the total energy of the particle by the de Broglie relation: v_p = E/ℏk . The group velocity is related to the kinetic energy of the particle by the relation: v_g = 2E/ℏk .
- For a free particle, the phase velocity and the group velocity are constant and equal to v_p = ℏk/2m and v_g = ℏk/m, respectively. Therefore, the group velocity is twice the phase velocity for a free particle .
- For a particle in a potential, the phase velocity and the group velocity depend on the shape of the potential and the wave number. The group velocity can be interpreted as the classical velocity of the particle, while the phase velocity has no physical meaning .
