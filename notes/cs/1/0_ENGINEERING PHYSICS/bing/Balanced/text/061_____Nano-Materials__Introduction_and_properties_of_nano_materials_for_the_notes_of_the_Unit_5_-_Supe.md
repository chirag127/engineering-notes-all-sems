### Nano-Materials: Introduction and properties of nano materials

- Nano-materials are materials composed of a set of substances where at least one dimension is less than approximately 100 nm.
- The size of nano-materials is measured in nanometers, which are one billionth of a meter or 10^-9^ m.
- Nano-materials have unique physical and chemical properties that differ from those of bulk materials due to the quantum effects that occur at the nanoscale  .
- Some of the properties of nano-materials that are influenced by their size and shape include optical, electrical, magnetic, thermal, mechanical, catalytic, and biological properties .
- Examples of nano-materials include nanofilaments, nanotubes, nanowires, nanocables, nanothin film, dendrimers, quantum dots, nanocrystals, nanocomposites, and nanoceramics .
- Nano-materials can be classified based on various approaches, such as their dimensionality, morphology, composition, origin, and functionality .
- Nano-materials have many advantages and disadvantages depending on their applications and potential impacts on human health and the environment .
- Some of the advantages of nano-materials are: enhanced performance, reduced weight, improved durability, lower cost, and novel functionalities .
- Some of the disadvantages of nano-materials are: toxicity, environmental pollution, ethical issues, social implications, and regulatory challenges .
- Nano-materials have a wide range of applications in various fields, such as electronics, energy, medicine, biotechnology, cosmetics, textiles, aerospace, and defense  .
