## Unit 3 - Wave Optics

- Wave optics, also known as physical optics, is the branch of optics that studies the interference, diffraction, polarization, and other phenomena that depend on the wave nature of light.
- The basic principle of wave optics is the superposition of waves, which states that when two or more waves overlap in space, the resultant wave is the sum of the individual waves at each point.
- Interference is the phenomenon of the formation of bright and dark regions when two or more coherent waves overlap in space. Coherent waves are waves that have a constant phase difference and the same frequency and wavelength.
- The conditions for constructive interference (bright regions) and destructive interference (dark regions) depend on the path difference and the phase difference between the interfering waves.
- The interference pattern can be observed on a screen or a detector placed at some distance from the sources of the waves.
- Some examples of interference phenomena are Young's double slit experiment, thin film interference, and Michelson interferometer.
- Diffraction is the phenomenon of the bending of light around the edges of an obstacle or an aperture. The amount of diffraction depends on the size of the obstacle or the aperture and the wavelength of light.
- The diffraction pattern can be observed on a screen or a detector placed at some distance from the obstacle or the aperture.
- Some examples of diffraction phenomena are single slit diffraction, diffraction grating, and Fresnel and Fraunhofer diffraction.
- Polarization is the phenomenon of the restriction of the vibration of light to a particular plane. Unpolarized light consists of waves that vibrate in all possible planes perpendicular to the direction of propagation. Polarized light consists of waves that vibrate in only one plane.
- The polarization of light can be achieved by various methods, such as reflection, refraction, scattering, and transmission through polarizing materials.
- Some examples of polarization phenomena are Brewster's law, Malus's law, and optical activity.
- Other phenomena that depend on the wave nature of light are the Doppler effect, the Zeeman effect, and the photoelectric effect.
