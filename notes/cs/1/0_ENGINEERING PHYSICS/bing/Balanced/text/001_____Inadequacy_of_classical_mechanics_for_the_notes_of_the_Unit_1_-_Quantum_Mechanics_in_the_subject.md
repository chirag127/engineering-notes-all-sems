### Inadequacy of classical mechanics

- Classical mechanics is the branch of physics that deals with the motion of macroscopic objects under the influence of forces.
- Classical mechanics is based on Newton's laws of motion, which describe how forces affect the motion of objects.
- Classical mechanics can explain correctly the motion of celestial bodies, such as planets and stars, and microscopic terrestrial bodies, such as bullets and cars, that move with non-relativistic speeds (much less than the speed of light).
- However, classical mechanics fails to explain certain phenomena observed in nature, especially at the atomic and subatomic scales, where the objects move with relativistic speeds (close to the speed of light) or interact with electromagnetic radiation.
- Some of the phenomena that classical mechanics cannot explain are:

  - The stability of atoms: According to classical mechanics, an electron orbiting a nucleus should emit electromagnetic radiation and lose energy, eventually falling into the nucleus. However, atoms are stable and do not collapse.
  - The discrete spectra of atoms: According to classical mechanics, an electron orbiting a nucleus should emit electromagnetic radiation of any frequency, depending on its energy. However, atoms emit and absorb electromagnetic radiation of only certain discrete frequencies, corresponding to the transitions between discrete energy levels of the electrons.
  - The blackbody radiation: According to classical mechanics, a blackbody (an ideal object that absorbs and emits all frequencies of electromagnetic radiation) should emit electromagnetic radiation of any frequency, depending on its temperature. However, the observed spectrum of blackbody radiation does not match the classical prediction, especially at high frequencies, where the classical theory predicts an infinite energy output (the ultraviolet catastrophe).
  - The photoelectric effect: According to classical mechanics, a metal surface exposed to electromagnetic radiation should emit electrons with any energy, depending on the intensity of the radiation. However, the observed phenomenon shows that the energy of the emitted electrons depends only on the frequency of the radiation, and there is a minimum frequency (the threshold frequency) below which no electrons are emitted, regardless of the intensity of the radiation.
  - The Compton effect: According to classical mechanics, a photon (a particle of light) colliding with an electron should transfer some of its energy and momentum to the electron, without changing its frequency. However, the observed phenomenon shows that the frequency of the photon decreases after the collision, and the amount of decrease depends on the angle of scattering.

- These phenomena indicate that classical mechanics is inadequate to describe the nature of matter and radiation at the microscopic level, and that a new theory is needed to account for the observed phenomena. This new theory is quantum mechanics, which introduces the concepts of wave-particle duality, uncertainty principle, quantization of energy and momentum, and superposition of states. Quantum mechanics is able to explain all the phenomena that classical mechanics cannot, and also reduces to classical mechanics in the limit of large objects and low speeds.
