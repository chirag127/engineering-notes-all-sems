### Solid state Laser (Ruby laser) and Gas Laser (He-Ne laser)

- A solid state laser is a laser that uses a solid material as its gain medium, rather than a liquid or a gas. 
- A ruby laser is a type of solid state laser that uses a synthetic ruby crystal as its gain medium. 
- A ruby laser produces pulses of coherent visible light at a wavelength of 694.3 nm, which is a deep red color. 
- A ruby laser is a three level laser, meaning that it has three energy levels: the ground state, the metastable state, and the upper state. 
- A ruby laser is pumped by a flash lamp, which excites the electrons in the ruby crystal from the ground state to the upper state. 
- The electrons in the upper state quickly decay to the metastable state, where they can stay for a long time. This creates a population inversion between the metastable state and the ground state. 
- When a photon of the right wavelength passes through the ruby crystal, it can stimulate an electron in the metastable state to drop to the ground state, emitting another photon of the same wavelength and phase. This is the process of stimulated emission. 
- The photons are reflected back and forth by the mirrors at the ends of the ruby rod, forming a resonant cavity. Some of the photons escape through the partially transparent mirror, forming the laser beam. 
- A gas laser is a laser that uses a gas as its gain medium, rather than a solid or a liquid. 
- A helium-neon laser (He-Ne laser) is a type of gas laser that uses a mixture of helium and neon gases as its gain medium. 
- A He-Ne laser produces continuous wave (CW) coherent light at a wavelength of 632.8 nm, which is a bright red color. 
- A He-Ne laser is a four level laser, meaning that it has four energy levels: the ground state, the lower state, the upper state, and the metastable state. 
- A He-Ne laser is pumped by an electric discharge, which excites the electrons in the helium atoms from the ground state to the metastable state. 
- The excited helium atoms collide with the neon atoms, transferring some of their energy to them. This raises the neon atoms from the lower state to the upper state. This creates a population inversion between the upper state and the lower state. 
- When a photon of the right wavelength passes through the gas mixture, it can stimulate a neon atom in the upper state to drop to the lower state, emitting another photon of the same wavelength and phase. This is the process of stimulated emission. 
- The photons are reflected back and forth by the mirrors at the ends of the gas tube, forming a resonant cavity. Some of the photons escape through the partially transparent mirror, forming the laser beam.
