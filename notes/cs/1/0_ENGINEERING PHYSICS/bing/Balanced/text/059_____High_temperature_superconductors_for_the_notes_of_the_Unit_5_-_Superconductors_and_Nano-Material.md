### High temperature superconductors

- High temperature superconductors (HTS) are materials that behave as superconductors at temperatures above 77 K (−196.2 °C; −321.1 °F), the boiling point of liquid nitrogen.
- Superconductors are materials that have zero electrical resistance and can expel magnetic fields when cooled below a critical temperature (Tc).
- HTS have higher Tc than conventional superconductors, which are mostly metallic alloys that require liquid helium to reach superconductivity at temperatures below 30 K (−243.2 °C; −405.7 °F).
- HTS have potential applications in power transmission, magnetic levitation, quantum computing, and other fields that require high efficiency and low loss of energy.
- The main class of HTS is copper oxides combined with other metals, especially the rare-earth barium copper oxides (REBCOs) such as yttrium barium copper oxide (YBCO).
- The second class of HTS is the iron-based compounds, which have a layered structure similar to the copper oxides but with iron and arsenic or other elements.
- The mechanism of HTS is still not fully understood, but it involves the formation of Cooper pairs, which are pairs of electrons that overcome their mutual repulsion and bind together at low temperatures .
- Cooper pairs can move through the lattice of atoms without resistance, as they are in a quantum state that is unaffected by the thermal vibrations of the atoms.
- The Cooper pairs are also affected by the magnetic fields, which can break them apart and destroy superconductivity. This is known as the Meissner effect, and it limits the maximum magnetic field (Hc) that a superconductor can withstand.
- HTS have higher Hc than conventional superconductors, which means they can operate in stronger magnetic fields and generate higher currents.
- HTS also have a higher critical current density (Jc), which is the maximum current that can flow through a superconductor without resistance. Jc depends on the temperature, the magnetic field, and the microstructure of the material.
- HTS are usually fabricated as thin films, wires, or tapes, which have to be aligned and textured to achieve high Jc and avoid defects that can degrade the performance of the superconductor.
- HTS are still challenging to produce, test, and use, as they require special techniques and equipment to handle the high temperatures, pressures, and magnetic fields involved.
- HTS are also subject to controversy, as some claims of superconductivity at very high temperatures (above 200 K) have not been replicated or verified by other researchers.
- HTS are an active area of research and development, as scientists and engineers seek to discover new materials, improve the existing ones, and explore the possible applications of HTS in various domains.
