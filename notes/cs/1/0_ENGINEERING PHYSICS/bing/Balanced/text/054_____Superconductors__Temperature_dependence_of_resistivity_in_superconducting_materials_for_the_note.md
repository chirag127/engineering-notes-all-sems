### Superconductors: Temperature dependence of resistivity in superconducting materials

- Superconductors are materials that have zero electrical resistance below a certain critical temperature (Tc)  .
- The resistivity of materials depends on the temperature. The equation ρt = ρ0 [1 + α (T – T0) shows the relation between the temperature and the resistivity for any material  .
- In the equation, ρ0 is the resistivity at a standard temperature, ρt is the resistivity at t 0 C, T0 is the reference temperature and α is the temperature coefficient of resistivity  .
- For superconductors, the resistivity drops abruptly to zero when the temperature is lowered below Tc  .
- The value of Tc depends on the type and composition of the superconducting material. For most metals, Tc is very low, below 12 K. For some ceramics, Tc can be as high as 135 K  .
- The phenomenon of superconductivity is explained by two theories: the BCS theory and the Ginzburg-Landau theory  .
- The BCS theory proposes that below Tc, the electrons in the material form pairs called Cooper pairs, which behave as bosons and condense into a quantum state with zero resistance  .
- The Ginzburg-Landau theory describes the superconducting state as a complex order parameter that varies in space and time. The theory predicts the existence of vortices, which are regions of normal conductivity within the superconducting material  .
- The superconducting materials are classified into two types: type I and type II  .
- Type I superconductors are pure metals or alloys that have a single critical magnetic field (Hc) above which the superconductivity is destroyed. They have a complete Meissner effect, which means they expel all the magnetic flux from their interior  .
- Type II superconductors are mostly ceramics or compounds that have two critical magnetic fields (Hc1 and Hc2) between which the superconductivity is partial. They have a mixed state, which means they allow some magnetic flux to penetrate their interior in the form of vortices  .
