### Plane electromagnetic waves in vacuum and their transverse nature

- A plane electromagnetic wave is a wave that has a constant amplitude and a constant frequency in a plane perpendicular to the direction of propagation.
- A plane electromagnetic wave consists of an electric field (**E**) and a magnetic field (**B**) that oscillate in phase and are perpendicular to each other and to the direction of propagation .
- The direction of propagation of a plane electromagnetic wave is given by the vector product of **E** and **B**, i.e., **k** = **E** x **B**.
- In vacuum, a plane electromagnetic wave travels at a constant speed of **c** = 3 x 10^8^ m/s, which is the speed of light.
- The transverse nature of electromagnetic waves means that the electric and magnetic fields fluctuate in a plane perpendicular to the direction of propagation  .
- The transverse nature of electromagnetic waves can be derived from Gauss's law for electric and magnetic fields, which state that the net electric and magnetic flux through any closed surface is zero .
- This implies that the electric and magnetic fields have no component along the direction of propagation, and hence they are transverse .
- The transverse nature of electromagnetic waves can also be verified by experiments, such as the polarization of light, which shows that the electric field of light can be oriented in different directions in the plane perpendicular to the propagation .
- The transverse nature of electromagnetic waves is a distinctive feature that distinguishes them from longitudinal waves, such as sound waves, which have a component along the direction of propagation .
