### Temperature dependence of critical field for superconductors

- The critical field (Hc) of a superconductor is the maximum external magnetic field that can be applied without destroying the superconducting state.
- The critical field depends on the temperature (T) of the superconductor, and it decreases as the temperature increases, reaching zero at the critical temperature (Tc).
- The temperature dependence of the critical field can be expressed by the empirical formula:

$$H_c(T) = H_c(0) \left[ 1 - \left( \frac{T}{T_c} \right)^2 \right]$$

where $H_c(0)$ is the critical field at absolute zero.

- This formula is valid for ordinary (type I) superconductors, which have a single critical field and a single critical temperature.
- For high-temperature (type II) superconductors, which have two critical fields ($H_{c1}$ and $H_{c2}$) and a mixed state between them, the temperature dependence of the critical fields is more complicated and depends on the material properties and the dimensionality of the superconductor.
- Some general features of the temperature dependence of the critical fields for type II superconductors are:

  - $H_{c1}(T)$ is the lower critical field, below which the superconductor expels the magnetic field completely (Meissner effect). It decreases with increasing temperature, reaching zero at $T_c$.
  - $H_{c2}(T)$ is the upper critical field, above which the superconductor becomes normal. It increases with decreasing temperature, reaching a maximum value at $T = 0$.
  - The mixed state occurs between $H_{c1}(T)$ and $H_{c2}(T)$, where the superconductor allows the magnetic field to penetrate in the form of quantized vortices, which carry a normal core and a superconducting current around them.
  - The temperature dependence of $H_{c2}(T)$ can be affected by localization effects, which reduce the coherence length of the superconducting wavefunction and increase the orbital depairing of the Cooper pairs. This can result in an anomalous negative curvature of $H_{c2}(T)$ at low temperatures, as observed in some high-temperature superconductors.
