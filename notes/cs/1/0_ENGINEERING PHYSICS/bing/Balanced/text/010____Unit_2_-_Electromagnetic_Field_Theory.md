## Unit 2 - Electromagnetic Field Theory

- Electromagnetic field theory is the study of the interaction between electric and magnetic fields, which are produced by electric charges and currents.
- The basic principles of electromagnetic field theory are derived from the experimental observations of Coulomb, Ampere, Faraday, and Maxwell, and are summarized by Maxwell's equations, which relate the electric and magnetic fields to their sources and to each other.
- The electric field is a vector field that exerts a force on a unit positive charge at any point in space. The electric field can be calculated from the electric potential, which is a scalar function that represents the work done to move a unit charge from a reference point to a given point. The electric potential can be obtained by solving Poisson's equation or Laplace's equation, depending on the presence or absence of electric charges in the region of interest.
- The magnetic field is also a vector field that exerts a force on a moving charge or a current at any point in space. The magnetic field can be calculated from the vector potential, which is a vector function that satisfies the condition that the curl of the vector potential is equal to the magnetic field. The vector potential can be obtained by solving a modified version of Poisson's equation, where the source term is the electric current density.
- The electric and magnetic fields are not independent of each other, but are related by the phenomena of electromagnetic induction and electromagnetic radiation. Electromagnetic induction is the process by which a changing magnetic field induces an electric field, and vice versa. Electromagnetic radiation is the process by which an accelerating charge or a changing current emits electromagnetic waves, which are self-propagating oscillations of the electric and magnetic fields that travel at the speed of light in vacuum.
- The electric and magnetic properties of matter affect the behavior of the electromagnetic fields in different ways. The electric properties of matter are characterized by the electric polarization, which is the electric dipole moment per unit volume induced by an external electric field. The magnetic properties of matter are characterized by the magnetization, which is the magnetic dipole moment per unit volume induced by an external magnetic field. The polarization and magnetization are related to the electric and magnetic susceptibilities, which measure the degree of response of the material to the applied fields. The electric and magnetic fields in matter are modified by the presence of bound charges and currents, which are the result of the polarization and magnetization of the material.
- Electromagnetic field theory has many applications in physics, engineering, and technology, such as electric circuits, antennas, transmission lines, waveguides, optical fibers, lasers, radar, wireless communication, and medical imaging.
