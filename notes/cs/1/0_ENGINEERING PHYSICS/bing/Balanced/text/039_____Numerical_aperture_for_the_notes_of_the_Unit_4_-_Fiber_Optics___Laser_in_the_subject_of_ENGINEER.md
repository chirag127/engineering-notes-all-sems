### Numerical aperture

- Numerical aperture (NA) is a measure of the ability of an optical fiber to collect or confine the incident light ray inside it .
- It is among the most basic property of optical fiber and shows the efficiency with which light is collected inside the fiber in order to get propagated.
- Numerical aperture is defined as the sine of half of the angle of the fiber's light acceptance cone , i.e., NA = sin θa, where θa is called the acceptance angle.
- The acceptance angle is the maximum angle at which the light can enter the fiber and still be transmitted along it .
- The numerical aperture depends on the refractive indices of the core and the cladding of the fiber, as well as the wavelength of the light .
- The numerical aperture can be calculated by the formula NA = √(n1^2 - n2^2), where n1 is the refractive index of the core and n2 is the refractive index of the cladding.
- The numerical aperture is a dimensionless quantity and has no units.
- The numerical aperture determines the resolution and light-gathering ability of an optical fiber .
- A higher numerical aperture means a larger acceptance angle and a higher ability to collect light, but also a lower resolution and a higher attenuation due to modal dispersion .
- A lower numerical aperture means a smaller acceptance angle and a lower ability to collect light, but also a higher resolution and a lower attenuation due to modal dispersion .
- Numerical aperture is commonly used in microscopy and fiber optics applications.
