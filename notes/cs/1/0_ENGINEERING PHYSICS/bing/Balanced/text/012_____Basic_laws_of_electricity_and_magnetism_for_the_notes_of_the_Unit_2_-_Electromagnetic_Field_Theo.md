### Basic laws of electricity and magnetism

Electricity and magnetism are two aspects of the same phenomenon: electromagnetism. Electric charges and magnetic fields interact with each other in various ways, and these interactions are governed by some basic laws that can be expressed in mathematical equations. Here are some of the important laws of electricity and magnetism:

- **Coulomb's law**: This law states that the force of attraction or repulsion between two point charges is directly proportional to the product of their charges and inversely proportional to the square of the distance between them. The force is along the line joining the charges and has the same magnitude for both charges. The mathematical expression of Coulomb's law is:

  $$F = k \frac{q_1 q_2}{r^2}$$

  where $F$ is the force, $k$ is a constant, $q_1$ and $q_2$ are the charges, and $r$ is the distance between them.

- **Gauss's law**: This law states that the net electric flux through any closed surface is equal to the total charge enclosed by the surface divided by the permittivity of free space. The electric flux is a measure of the number of electric field lines passing through the surface. The mathematical expression of Gauss's law is:

  $$\Phi_E = \oint_S \vec{E} \cdot d\vec{A} = \frac{Q}{\epsilon_0}$$

  where $\Phi_E$ is the electric flux, $\vec{E}$ is the electric field, $d\vec{A}$ is a differential area element of the surface, $Q$ is the total charge enclosed, and $\epsilon_0$ is the permittivity of free space.

- **Faraday's law of electromagnetic induction**: This law states that the induced electromotive force (emf) in a loop of wire is equal to the rate of change of magnetic flux through the loop. The magnetic flux is a measure of the number of magnetic field lines passing through the loop. The mathematical expression of Faraday's law is:

  $$\mathcal{E} = -\frac{d\Phi_B}{dt}$$

  where $\mathcal{E}$ is the emf, $\Phi_B$ is the magnetic flux, and $t$ is the time.

- **Ampere's law**: This law states that the line integral of the magnetic field around any closed loop is equal to the product of the permeability of free space and the net current passing through the loop. The mathematical expression of Ampere's law is:

  $$\oint_C \vec{B} \cdot d\vec{l} = \mu_0 I$$

  where $\vec{B}$ is the magnetic field, $d\vec{l}$ is a differential length element of the loop, $\mu_0$ is the permeability of free space, and $I$ is the net current.

- **Lorentz force law**: This law states that the force on a charged particle moving in an electric and magnetic field is equal to the charge times the sum of the electric field and the cross product of the velocity and the magnetic field. The mathematical expression of Lorentz force law is:

  $$\vec{F} = q(\vec{E} + \vec{v} \times \vec{B})$$

  where $\vec{F}$ is the force, $q$ is the charge, $\vec{E}$ is the electric field, $\vec{v}$ is the velocity, and $\vec{B}$ is the magnetic field.

- **Maxwell's equations**: These are four equations that summarize the basic laws of electricity and magnetism and show how they are related. They are:

  $$\nabla \cdot \vec{E} = \frac{\rho}{\epsilon_0}$$

  $$\nabla \cdot \vec{B} = 0$$

  $$\nabla \times \vec{E} = -\frac{\partial \vec{B}}{\partial t}$$

  $$\nabla \times \vec{B} = \mu_0 \vec{J} + \mu_0 \epsilon_0 \frac{\partial \vec{E}}{\partial t}$$

  where $\nabla$ is the del operator, $\rho$ is the charge density, $\vec{J}$ is the current density, and the other symbols have been defined before.
