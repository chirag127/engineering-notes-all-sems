### Rayleigh’s criterion of resolution

- Rayleigh’s criterion of resolution is a measure of the ability of an optical instrument to distinguish two closely spaced point sources of light, such as stars or distant objects.
- According to Rayleigh’s criterion, two point sources are just resolvable when the central maximum of the diffraction pattern of one source coincides with the first minimum of the diffraction pattern of the other source. See Figure 1.
- Rayleigh’s criterion can be applied to different types of apertures, such as circular, rectangular, or slit. For a circular aperture, such as a telescope lens, the criterion for resolution is when the center of one Airy disk pattern falls on the first minimum of the Airy disk pattern of the second source. See Figure 2.
- The minimum angular separation θ between two point sources that can be resolved by a circular aperture of diameter D is given by the formula:

θ = 1.22 λ / D

where λ is the wavelength of the light.

- The smaller the value of θ, the better the resolution. The resolution can be improved by increasing the diameter of the aperture or decreasing the wavelength of the light.
- Rayleigh’s criterion is based on the assumption that the point sources are of equal intensity and monochromatic. It also does not account for the effects of aberrations, atmospheric turbulence, or noise in the optical system.
