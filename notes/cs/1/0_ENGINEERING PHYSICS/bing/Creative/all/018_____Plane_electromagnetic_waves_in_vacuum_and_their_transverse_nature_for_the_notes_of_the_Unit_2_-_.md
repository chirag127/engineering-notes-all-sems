# Plane electromagnetic waves in vacuum and their transverse nature

- A plane electromagnetic wave is a wave that has a constant amplitude and a constant frequency in a plane perpendicular to the direction of propagation.
- A plane electromagnetic wave consists of oscillating electric and magnetic fields that are perpendicular to each other and to the direction of propagation  .
- The electric field vector E and the magnetic field vector B are in phase and have the same wavelength and frequency .
- The ratio of the magnitudes of E and B is equal to the speed of light c in vacuum, which is 3 x 10^8^ m/s .
- The speed of a plane electromagnetic wave in vacuum is independent of its frequency and wavelength.
- The plane electromagnetic wave satisfies the wave equation and the Maxwell's equations in vacuum .
- The plane electromagnetic wave carries energy and momentum in the direction of propagation .
- The plane electromagnetic wave exerts radiation pressure on a surface that absorbs or reflects it .
- The plane electromagnetic wave can be polarized, which means that the direction of the electric field vector can be fixed or varied in a specific way .
- The plane electromagnetic wave can be superposed, reflected, refracted, diffracted, and interfered with other plane electromagnetic waves .
