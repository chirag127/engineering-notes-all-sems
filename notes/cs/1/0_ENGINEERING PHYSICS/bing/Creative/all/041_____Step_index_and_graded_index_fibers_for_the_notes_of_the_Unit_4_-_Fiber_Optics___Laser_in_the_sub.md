# Step index and graded index fibers

## Introduction

Optical fibers are thin strands of glass or plastic that can transmit light signals over long distances. Optical fibers are used in various applications such as telecommunications, data transmission, sensing, and imaging. Optical fibers can be classified into different types based on their structure, mode of propagation, and refractive index profile.

## Step index fiber

- A step index fiber is a type of optical fiber that has a core with a constant refractive index and a cladding with a lower refractive index. The core and the cladding are separated by a sharp boundary, hence the name step index.
- A step index fiber can be either single mode or multimode, depending on the core diameter and the wavelength of the light. A single mode fiber has a core diameter that is comparable to the wavelength of the light, and it can support only one mode of propagation. A multimode fiber has a larger core diameter and can support multiple modes of propagation.
- A step index fiber has a high modal dispersion, which is the spreading of the light pulses due to the different path lengths and velocities of the modes. This limits the bandwidth and the distance of the step index fiber. The modal dispersion can be reduced by using a graded index fiber.

## Graded index fiber

- A graded index fiber is a type of optical fiber that has a core with a varying refractive index and a cladding with a lower refractive index. The refractive index of the core decreases gradually from the center to the edge, forming a parabolic profile. This causes the light rays to bend towards the center of the core, hence the name graded index.
- A graded index fiber is always multimode, as it can support multiple modes of propagation. However, the modes have similar path lengths and velocities, as the light rays are constantly refracted towards the center of the core. This reduces the modal dispersion and increases the bandwidth and the distance of the graded index fiber.
- A graded index fiber offers superior transmission quality, higher bandwidth, and longer distance than a step index fiber. In modern communication applications, graded index fiber is preferred over step index fiber.

## Conclusion

- Step index fiber and graded index fiber are two types of multimode fibers
- Graded index fiber offers superior transmission quality, higher bandwidth, and longer distance than step index fiber
- Higher modal dispersion limits the bandwidth and the distance of the step index fiber
- In modern communication applications, graded index fiber is preferred over step index fiber
- Step index fiber is cheaper but it cannot offer higher bandwidth nor longer distances
