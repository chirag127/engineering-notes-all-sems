# Unit 3 - Wave Optics

- Wave optics, also called physical optics, is a branch of optics that studies the behavior of light when it exhibits wave characteristics, such as interference, diffraction, polarization, and other phenomena that cannot be explained by ray approximation of geometric optics  .
- Wave optics is based on the principle of superposition, which states that when two or more waves overlap in space, the resultant wave is the sum of the individual waves at each point.
- Some of the important topics and concepts in wave optics are:
  - Huygens' principle, which states that every point on a wavefront acts as a source of secondary spherical wavelets, and the new wavefront is the envelope of these wavelets .
  - Interference, which is the phenomenon of constructive and destructive superposition of two or more coherent waves, resulting in a pattern of bright and dark fringes  .
  - Young's double-slit experiment, which demonstrates the interference of light waves from two narrow slits separated by a small distance, and provides evidence for the wave nature of light  .
  - Diffraction, which is the bending of light waves around obstacles or apertures, resulting in a deviation from the straight-line propagation of light  .
  - Single-slit diffraction, which is the diffraction of light waves from a single narrow slit, resulting in a central bright fringe and a series of secondary fringes on either side  .
  - Diffraction grating, which is an array of many narrow slits separated by equal distances, and produces a spectrum of colors due to the interference of diffracted light waves from different slits  .
  - Polarization, which is the phenomenon of restricting the vibration of light waves to a single plane, resulting in a change in the intensity and direction of light  .
  - Malus' law, which states that the intensity of polarized light transmitted through a polarizer is proportional to the square of the cosine of the angle between the transmission axis of the polarizer and the polarization direction of the incident light  .
  - Brewster's angle, which is the angle of incidence at which the reflected light is completely polarized perpendicular to the plane of incidence, and is given by the relation tan θ = n, where θ is the Brewster's angle and n is the refractive index of the medium  .
