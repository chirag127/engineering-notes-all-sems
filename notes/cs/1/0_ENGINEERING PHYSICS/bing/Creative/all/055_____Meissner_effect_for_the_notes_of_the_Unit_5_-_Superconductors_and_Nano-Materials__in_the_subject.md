# Meissner effect

- The Meissner effect is the expulsion of a magnetic field from the interior of a material that is in the process of becoming a superconductor .
- A superconductor is a material that loses its resistance to the flow of electrical currents when cooled below a certain temperature, called the critical temperature .
- The Meissner effect was discovered by the German physicists W. Meissner and R. Ochsenfeld in 1933 .
- The Meissner effect is a property of all superconductors, and it is one of the defining characteristics of the superconducting state .
- The Meissner effect can be demonstrated by making a magnet levitate above a superconductor, as the superconductor repels the magnetic field of the magnet .
- The Meissner effect can be explained by the formation of superconducting currents on the surface of the material, which cancel out the applied magnetic field inside the material .
- The Meissner effect is a quantum phenomenon that involves the breaking of the symmetry of the electromagnetic field by the superconducting state.
- The Meissner effect is different from the diamagnetic effect, which is the tendency of a material to weakly oppose an applied magnetic field. The Meissner effect is a perfect diamagnetism, which means that the magnetic field is completely expelled from the superconductor.
