### Particle in a one-dimensional box

- A particle in a one-dimensional box is a fundamental quantum mechanical approximation describing the translational motion of a single particle confined inside an infinitely deep well from which it cannot escape  .
- The walls of a one-dimensional box may be seen as regions of space with an infinitely large potential energy.
- The particle can only exist inside the box, where the potential energy is zero, and its wavefunction must vanish at the boundaries of the box .
- The Schrödinger equation for the particle in a one-dimensional box is given by:

$$-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\frac{d^2\psi}{dx^2}=E\psi$$

where $\hbar$ is the reduced Planck constant, $m$ is the mass of the particle, $x$ is the position coordinate, $E$ is the energy of the particle, and $\psi$ is the wavefunction of the particle .

- The general solution of the Schrödinger equation is:

$$\psi(x)=A\sin(kx)+B\cos(kx)$$

where $A$ and $B$ are constants, and $k$ is the wave number given by:

$$k=\frac{\sqrt{2mE}}{\hbar}$$

- Applying the boundary conditions that $\psi(0)=0$ and $\psi(L)=0$, where $L$ is the length of the box, we obtain:

$$B=0$$

and

$$kL=n\pi$$

where $n$ is a positive integer .

- Therefore, the wavefunction of the particle in a one-dimensional box is:

$$\psi_n(x)=\sqrt{\frac{2}{L}}\sin\left(\frac{n\pi x}{L}\right)$$

where $n=1,2,3,...$ is the quantum number .

- The energy of the particle in a one-dimensional box is:

$$E_n=\frac{n^2\pi^2\hbar^2}{2mL^2}$$

where $n=1,2,3,...$ is the quantum number .

- The energy levels of the particle in a one-dimensional box are discrete and quantized, and depend on the quantum number $n$ and the size of the box $L$ .
- The particle in a one-dimensional box model can be used to explain some phenomena such as the emission spectra of hydrogen atoms, the electronic structure of conjugated molecules, and the band structure of solids .
