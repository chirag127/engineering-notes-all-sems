### Solid state Laser (Ruby laser) and Gas Laser (He-Ne laser)

- A solid state laser is a laser that uses a solid material as its gain medium, where the atoms are excited by an external source of energy to emit light.
- A ruby laser is a type of solid state laser that uses a synthetic ruby crystal as its gain medium . It was the first working laser invented by Theodore H. Maiman in 1960.
- A ruby laser produces pulses of coherent visible light at a wavelength of 694.3 nm, which is a deep red color, with a very narrow linewidth of 0.53 nm. It is a three level laser, meaning that it has three energy levels for the electrons in the ruby atoms: ground, metastable, and excited.
- The ruby crystal is rod-shaped and has a polished surface with a reflective coating on both ends, forming a resonant cavity. One end is partially transparent, allowing some light to escape as the laser output.
- The ruby crystal is surrounded by a flash lamp, which provides the external energy source to pump the ruby atoms from the ground state to the excited state. When the flash lamp is triggered, it emits a bright flash of white light that lasts for a few milliseconds.
- The ruby atoms absorb some of the light from the flash lamp and jump to the excited state, where they stay for a very short time before dropping to the metastable state, where they stay for a longer time. This creates a population inversion, meaning that there are more atoms in the higher energy state than in the lower energy state.
- When an atom in the metastable state is stimulated by a photon of the same wavelength as the ruby laser, it emits another photon of the same wavelength and phase, creating a chain reaction of stimulated emission. This amplifies the light in the resonant cavity, producing a pulse of coherent red light.
- The pulse of light travels back and forth in the resonant cavity, reflecting off the mirrors at the ends of the ruby rod, until it reaches a sufficient intensity to escape through the partially transparent mirror as the laser output.
- A gas laser is a laser that uses a gas as its gain medium, where the atoms or molecules are excited by an electric current or a chemical reaction to emit light.
- A helium-neon laser (He-Ne laser) is a type of gas laser that uses a mixture of helium and neon gas as its gain medium. It was the first continuous wave laser invented by Ali Javan, William Bennett, and Donald Herriott in 1961.
- A He-Ne laser produces a continuous beam of coherent light at a wavelength of 632.8 nm, which is a bright red color, with a very narrow linewidth of 0.002 nm. It is a four level laser, meaning that it has four energy levels for the electrons in the neon atoms: ground, lower, upper, and higher.
- The helium-neon gas mixture is contained in a glass tube with electrodes at both ends, forming a discharge tube. The tube is connected to a high voltage power supply, which provides the electric current to pump the gas atoms from the ground state to the higher state.
- The helium atoms are more easily excited by the electric current than the neon atoms, so they absorb most of the energy and jump to the higher state, where they stay for a long time. The neon atoms remain mostly in the ground state.
- The helium atoms in the higher state collide with the neon atoms in the ground state, transferring some of their energy to the neon atoms and causing them to jump to the upper state, where they stay for a short time before dropping to the lower state, where they stay for a longer time. This creates a population inversion, meaning that there are more neon atoms in the higher energy state than in the lower energy state.
- When a neon atom in the upper state is stimulated by a photon of the same wavelength as the He-Ne laser, it emits another photon of the same wavelength and phase, creating a chain reaction of stimulated emission. This amplifies the light in the discharge tube, producing a continuous beam of coherent red light.
