### Compton effect

- The Compton effect is the phenomenon of **elastic scattering** of photons by free or loosely bound electrons, resulting in a **change in wavelength** of the photons .
- The Compton effect was discovered by **Arthur Compton** in 1923, who measured the scattering of X-rays by graphite and observed that the scattered X-rays had a longer wavelength than the incident X-rays .
- The Compton effect provided experimental evidence for the **particle nature** of electromagnetic radiation, as it could not be explained by the classical wave theory of light .
- The Compton effect can be explained by treating the photons as **particles** with energy and momentum, and applying the **conservation laws** of energy and momentum to the collision between the photon and the electron.
- The change in wavelength of the photon after the collision is given by the **Compton formula** :

- The Compton effect is important for understanding the **interaction** of high-energy photons with matter, such as in X-ray spectroscopy, gamma ray astronomy, and nuclear physics . It also has applications in **medical imaging** and **radiation therapy**.
