### Plane electromagnetic waves in vacuum and their transverse nature

- A plane electromagnetic wave is a wave that has a constant amplitude and a constant frequency in a plane perpendicular to the direction of propagation.
- A plane electromagnetic wave consists of an electric field (**E**) and a magnetic field (**B**) that oscillate in phase and are perpendicular to each other and to the direction of propagation  .
- The direction of propagation of a plane electromagnetic wave is given by the vector product of **E** and **B**, i.e., **k** = **E** x **B**.
- In vacuum, a plane electromagnetic wave travels at a constant speed of **c** = 3 x 10^8^ m/s, which is the speed of light.
- The transverse nature of electromagnetic waves means that the electric and magnetic fields vibrate in planes that are perpendicular to the direction of propagation  .
- The transverse nature of electromagnetic waves can be demonstrated by using polarizers, which are devices that allow only certain orientations of electric or magnetic fields to pass through .
- The transverse nature of electromagnetic waves can also be explained by using Maxwell's equations, which show that the divergence of the electric and magnetic fields in a plane electromagnetic wave is zero, i.e., ∇ · **E** = 0 and ∇ · **B** = 0 .
- The transverse nature of electromagnetic waves is a consequence of the fact that electromagnetic waves are generated by accelerating charges, which produce electric and magnetic fields that are perpendicular to the direction of acceleration .
