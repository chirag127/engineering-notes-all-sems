### Nano-Materials: Introduction and properties of nano materials

- Nanomaterials are materials that have at least one dimension in the range of 1 to 100 nanometers (nm)   .
- Nanomaterials can have unique physical and chemical properties that differ from their bulk counterparts, such as higher surface area, quantum effects, enhanced reactivity, and novel optical, electrical, and magnetic properties   .
- Nanomaterials can be classified based on various approaches, such as dimensionality, morphology, composition, origin, and functionality  .
- Some examples of nanomaterials are nanofilaments, nanotubes, nanowires, nanocables, nanothin films, dendrimers, quantum dots, nanocrystals, nanocomposites, and nanoceramics  .
- Nanomaterials have many applications in various fields, such as electronics, energy, medicine, biotechnology, cosmetics, catalysis, and environmental remediation    .
- Nanomaterials also have some advantages and disadvantages, such as improved performance, functionality, and efficiency, but also potential toxicity, environmental impact, and ethical issues  .
