# Quantum wires and quantum well

## Quantum well

- A quantum well is a two-dimensional (2D) structure or a thin layer of a lower bandgap semiconductor material sandwiched between two layers of a higher bandgap material.
- A quantum well can confine the motion of charge carriers (electrons and holes) in one direction, while allowing them to move freely in the other two directions.
- A quantum well can modify the electronic, optical and transport properties of the semiconductor material, such as creating discrete energy levels, enhancing radiative transitions, increasing carrier mobility and reducing scattering.
- A quantum well can be fabricated by various techniques, such as molecular beam epitaxy (MBE), metal-organic chemical vapor deposition (MOCVD), atomic layer deposition (ALD) and ion implantation.
- A quantum well can be used for various applications, such as lasers, light-emitting diodes (LEDs), photodetectors, solar cells, modulators and transistors.

## Quantum wire

- A quantum wire is a one-dimensional (1D) structure or a thin rod of a lower bandgap semiconductor material embedded in a higher bandgap matrix material.
- A quantum wire can confine the motion of charge carriers in two directions, while allowing them to move freely in one direction.
- A quantum wire can further modify the electronic, optical and transport properties of the semiconductor material, such as creating subbands, increasing density of states, enhancing quantum interference, reducing phonon scattering and increasing ballistic transport.
- A quantum wire can be fabricated by various techniques, such as lithography, etching, growth on patterned substrates, self-assembly and nanowire synthesis.
- A quantum wire can be used for various applications, such as nanoelectronics, optoelectronics, information technology and biotechnology.
