### Newton’s Rings and its applications

Newton's rings are a phenomenon in which an interference pattern is created by the reflection of light between two surfaces, typically a spherical surface and an adjacent touching flat surface. It is named after Isaac Newton, who investigated the effect in 1666.

Some of the applications of Newton's rings are:

- For testing the uniformity of a polished surface by studying the interference pattern the surface makes when placed in contact with a perfectly flat glass surface. Any deviation from uniformity will cause distortion in the rings.
- To control the thickness of paint that is used on posters. Too much paint would exceed total weight requirements, and too little paint would not cover the poster adequately. By observing the interference pattern of the paint film, the optimal thickness can be determined.
- To measure the thickness of thin films, such as oil, soap, or air, by using a monochromatic light source and a lens of known radius of curvature. The diameter of the rings can be related to the thickness of the film by a formula derived from the geometry and the principle of interference.
- To measure the refractive index of transparent materials, such as liquids, gases, or solids, by placing them between the spherical and flat surfaces and observing the change in the interference pattern. The refractive index can be calculated from the change in the diameter of the rings using Snell's law and the interference condition.
- To measure the shape of lenses and other curved surfaces, such as mirrors, by comparing the interference pattern they produce with a reference flat surface. The deviation from flatness can be quantified by the deviation from circularity of the rings.
