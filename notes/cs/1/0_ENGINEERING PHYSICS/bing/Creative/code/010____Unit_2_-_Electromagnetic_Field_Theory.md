## Unit 2 - Electromagnetic Field Theory

- Electromagnetic field theory is the study of the interaction between electric and magnetic fields, and the sources that produce them, such as electric charges and currents .
- Electromagnetic fields are described by Maxwell's equations, which are four differential equations that relate the electric and magnetic fields to their sources and to each other  .
- Electromagnetic fields can be classified into two types: static and dynamic. Static fields are produced by stationary or constant sources, such as electric charges or magnets. Dynamic fields are produced by changing or moving sources, such as alternating currents or electromagnetic waves .
- Electromagnetic fields can be represented in different ways, such as scalar and vector potentials, electric and magnetic flux densities, electric and magnetic field intensities, and electric and magnetic polarizations .
- Electromagnetic fields can interact with matter in various ways, such as inducing electric currents, generating forces and torques, polarizing and magnetizing materials, and transmitting energy and information  .
- Electromagnetic fields can propagate in free space or in different media, such as conductors, dielectrics, and magnetic materials. The propagation characteristics depend on the frequency, wavelength, and polarization of the fields, and the properties of the medium, such as permittivity, permeability, and conductivity  .
- Electromagnetic fields can radiate from sources, such as antennas, or scatter from obstacles, such as objects or boundaries. The radiation and scattering patterns depend on the shape, size, and orientation of the sources and obstacles, and the frequency and polarization of the fields  .
