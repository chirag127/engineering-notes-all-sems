### Poynting vector and Poynting theorem

- The Poynting vector, denoted by **S**, is a vector quantity that represents the directional energy flux (the energy transfer per unit area per unit time) of an electromagnetic field .
- The Poynting vector is defined as the cross product of the electric field vector **E** and the magnetic field vector **H** at any point in space, that is, **S = E x H**   .
- The Poynting vector has the units of watts per square meter (W/m^2^) and the direction of **S** is perpendicular to both **E** and **H** in the right-hand rule sense .
- The Poynting vector is used in conjunction with Poynting's theorem, which is the continuity equation expressing the conservation of electromagnetic energy in a given region of space   .
- Poynting's theorem states that the net electromagnetic power flowing into a region of space may be either dissipated as heat, or used to change the energy stored in electric and magnetic fields within that region   .
