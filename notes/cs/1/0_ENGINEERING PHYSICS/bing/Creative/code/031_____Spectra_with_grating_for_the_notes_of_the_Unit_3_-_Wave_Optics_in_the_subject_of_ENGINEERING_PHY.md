### Spectra with grating

- A **diffraction grating** is an optical device that consists of a large number of equally spaced parallel slits or grooves on a transparent or reflective surface. The slits or grooves act as sources of coherent light when illuminated by a monochromatic or polychromatic light source. The light waves from the slits or grooves interfere with each other to produce a **spectrum** of different wavelengths or colors in different directions.
- The spectrum produced by a diffraction grating can be classified into two types: **transmission spectrum** and **reflection spectrum**. A transmission spectrum is obtained when the light passes through the grating, while a reflection spectrum is obtained when the light reflects from the grating. The transmission spectrum and the reflection spectrum are identical in terms of the wavelengths and angles of the diffracted light, but they are inverted with respect to each other.
- The spectrum produced by a diffraction grating can also be classified into two types: **dispersion spectrum** and **interference spectrum**. A dispersion spectrum is obtained when the grating is illuminated by a polychromatic light source, such as white light. The grating disperses the white light into its constituent colors or wavelengths, which are arranged in a rainbow-like pattern. The dispersion spectrum is also called a **continuous spectrum**, as it contains all the wavelengths of the visible spectrum. An interference spectrum is obtained when the grating is illuminated by a monochromatic light source, such as a laser. The grating produces multiple images of the light source, which interfere with each other to form a series of bright and dark fringes. The interference spectrum is also called a **line spectrum**, as it contains only discrete wavelengths of the light source.
- The spectrum produced by a diffraction grating can be analyzed using the **grating equation**, which relates the wavelength of the diffracted light, the angle of diffraction, the grating spacing, and the order of diffraction. The grating equation is given by:

    $$d(\sin \theta_m - \sin \theta_i) = m \lambda$$

    where $d$ is the grating spacing, $\theta_i$ is the angle of incidence, $\theta_m$ is the angle of diffraction, $m$ is the order of diffraction, and $\lambda$ is the wavelength of the diffracted light. The order of diffraction is an integer that indicates the number of wavelengths by which the diffracted light is shifted from the incident light. The order of diffraction can be positive or negative, depending on the direction of the diffracted light. The grating equation can be used to calculate the wavelength of the diffracted light for a given angle of diffraction, or vice versa.

The light source emits a polychromatic or monochromatic light, which is made parallel by the collimator. The parallel light falls on the grating, which diffracts it into different directions according to the grating equation.
