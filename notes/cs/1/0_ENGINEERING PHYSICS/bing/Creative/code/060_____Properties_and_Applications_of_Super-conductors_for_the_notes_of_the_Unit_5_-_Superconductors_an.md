# Properties and Applications of Superconductors

## Properties of Superconductors

- A superconductor is a material that has **zero electrical resistance** when cooled below a certain critical temperature.
- A superconductor also exhibits the **Meissner effect**, which means that it expels any external magnetic field from its interior and creates a counteracting magnetic field.
- A superconductor has a **critical magnetic field**, which is the maximum applied magnetic field that will allow it to remain superconducting at a given temperature.
- A superconductor has a **London moment**, which is a magnetic moment induced by a rotating superconductor in a magnetic field.
- A superconductor has a **phase transition**, which is a sudden change in its physical properties when it becomes superconducting or normal.

## Applications of Superconductors

- Superconductors are used in **particle accelerators**, such as the Large Hadron Collider, to create powerful electromagnets that can accelerate and steer charged particles.
- Superconductors are used in **generators**, such as the one in the Itaipu Dam, to produce electricity with high efficiency and low losses.
- Superconductors are used in **transportation**, such as the maglev trains in Japan and China, to levitate the trains above the tracks and reduce friction and noise.
- Superconductors are used in **computing**, such as the quantum computers developed by IBM and Google, to perform complex calculations with high speed and low power consumption.
- Superconductors are used in **electric motors**, such as the ones in the Boeing 787 Dreamliner, to reduce weight and increase torque.
- Superconductors are used in **medical**, such as the MRI scanners in hospitals, to create high-resolution images of the human body.
- Superconductors are used in **power transmission**, such as the one in the Tres Amigas project, to connect different power grids and improve reliability and stability.
- Superconductors are used in **sensors**, such as the SQUIDs (superconducting quantum interference devices), to measure extremely small magnetic fields and currents.
