### Numerical aperture for the notes of the Unit 4 - Fiber Optics & Laser in the subject of ENGINEERING PHYSICS

- Numerical aperture (NA) is a measure of the ability of an optical fiber to collect or confine the incident light ray inside it .
- It is among the most basic property of optical fiber and shows the efficiency with which light is collected inside the fiber in order to get propagated.
- Numerical aperture is defined as the sine of half of the angle of the fiber's light acceptance cone .
- The acceptance cone is the cone of light that can enter the fiber and be transmitted along it .
- The acceptance angle is the maximum angle of incidence for which the light ray can enter the fiber and be guided by total internal reflection .
- The numerical aperture depends on the refractive indices of the core and the cladding of the fiber, as well as the wavelength of the light .
- The numerical aperture can be calculated by the following formula  :

```
NA = n0 sin θa = √(n1^2 - n2^2)
```

where

- n0 is the refractive index of the medium outside the fiber (usually air)
- n1 is the refractive index of the core of the fiber
- n2 is the refractive index of the cladding of the fiber
- θa is the acceptance angle

- The numerical aperture determines the maximum number of modes that can propagate in a multimode fiber, as well as the resolution and light-gathering ability of an optical microscope .
- A higher numerical aperture means a larger acceptance angle and a higher light collection efficiency, but also a higher modal dispersion and a lower bandwidth .
- A lower numerical aperture means a smaller acceptance angle and a lower light collection efficiency, but also a lower modal dispersion and a higher bandwidth .
- For single mode fibers, the numerical aperture is not a good estimate of the acceptance angle, as the ray model of light does not apply to them.
