## Unit 4 - Fiber Optics & Laser

- Fiber optics is the technology of transmitting light signals through thin, flexible fibers of glass or plastic.
- Laser is an acronym for light amplification by stimulated emission of radiation. It is a device that produces a coherent, monochromatic, and intense beam of light.
- The main components of a fiber optic system are:
  - Transmitter: It converts electrical signals into light signals and couples them into the fiber.
  - Fiber: It carries the light signals from the transmitter to the receiver.
  - Receiver: It converts the light signals back into electrical signals and processes them.
- The main advantages of fiber optics are:
  - Higher bandwidth: Fiber optics can carry more data than copper wires or radio waves.
  - Lower attenuation: Fiber optics suffer less signal loss than other media.
  - Immunity to electromagnetic interference: Fiber optics are not affected by external noise or crosstalk.
  - Security: Fiber optics are difficult to tap or hack.
  - Safety: Fiber optics do not pose fire or shock hazards.
- The main disadvantages of fiber optics are:
  - Higher cost: Fiber optics are more expensive than copper wires or radio waves.
  - Fragility: Fiber optics are more prone to damage or breakage than other media.
  - Difficulty of splicing: Fiber optics require special tools and skills to join or repair.
  - Incompatibility: Fiber optics are not compatible with existing electrical or electronic devices and need converters or adapters.
- The main types of fiber optics are:
  - Single-mode fiber: It has a very thin core and allows only one mode of light to propagate. It has low attenuation and high bandwidth, but it is more expensive and difficult to couple.
  - Multi-mode fiber: It has a thicker core and allows multiple modes of light to propagate. It has higher attenuation and lower bandwidth, but it is cheaper and easier to couple.
  - Plastic optical fiber: It has a plastic core and cladding and is more flexible and durable than glass fiber. It has very high attenuation and low bandwidth, but it is very cheap and easy to install.
- The main types of laser are:
  - Gas laser: It uses a gas mixture as the active medium and produces a continuous or pulsed beam of light. Examples are helium-neon laser, carbon dioxide laser, and argon-ion laser.
  - Solid-state laser: It uses a solid crystal or glass as the active medium and produces a pulsed or continuous beam of light. Examples are ruby laser, neodymium-doped yttrium aluminum garnet (Nd:YAG) laser, and titanium-sapphire laser.
  - Semiconductor laser: It uses a semiconductor material as the active medium and produces a continuous or pulsed beam of light. Examples are gallium arsenide (GaAs) laser, indium phosphide (InP) laser, and vertical-cavity surface-emitting laser (VCSEL).
  - Dye laser: It uses a liquid dye as the active medium and produces a tunable beam of light. Examples are rhodamine 6G laser, coumarin laser, and pyrromethene laser.
- The main applications of fiber optics and laser are:
  - Communication: Fiber optics and laser are used to transmit voice, data, and video signals over long distances and at high speeds.
  - Medicine: Fiber optics and laser are used to perform surgery, diagnosis, therapy, and imaging of various diseases and conditions.
  - Industry: Fiber optics and laser are used to cut, weld, drill, measure, and inspect various materials and products.
  - Military: Fiber optics and laser are used to guide, detect, jam, and destroy various targets and threats.
  - Entertainment: Fiber optics and laser are used to create spectacular light shows, displays, and holograms.
