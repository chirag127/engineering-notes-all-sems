### Persistent current in superconductors

- A persistent current is a perpetual electric current that does not require an external power source.
- A persistent current is possible in superconductors because they have zero resistance and no energy dissipation  .
- A persistent current can be induced in a closed loop of superconducting material by applying and removing a magnetic field above and below the critical temperature of the superconductor .
- A persistent current can also be maintained by connecting two superconductors with different critical temperatures through a weak link or a Josephson junction .
- A persistent current can generate a magnetic field that opposes the applied field, according to Lenz's law .
- A persistent current can be used to create superconducting magnets, which have applications in medical imaging, particle accelerators, and magnetic levitation .
