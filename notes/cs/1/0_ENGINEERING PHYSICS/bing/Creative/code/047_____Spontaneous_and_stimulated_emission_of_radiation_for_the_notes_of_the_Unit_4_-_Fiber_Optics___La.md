### Spontaneous and stimulated emission of radiation

- Spontaneous emission is the process in which an atom or molecule in an excited state decays to a lower energy state and emits a photon of energy equal to the energy difference between the two states .
- Spontaneous emission does not require any external agency or interaction with other photons . It is a random and spontaneous process that occurs after a certain lifetime of the excited state.
- The photons emitted by spontaneous emission are incoherent, meaning they have random phases, directions, polarizations, and frequencies  .
- Spontaneous emission is the source of natural light, such as sunlight, starlight, and fluorescence .

- Stimulated emission is the process in which an atom or molecule in an excited state is induced to emit a photon of energy equal to the energy difference between the two states by an incident photon of the same frequency  .
- Stimulated emission requires the presence of an external photon that matches the energy gap of the atom or molecule  . It is a non-random and induced process that occurs when the incident photon interacts with the excited state .
- The photons emitted by stimulated emission are coherent, meaning they have the same phase, direction, polarization, and frequency as the incident photon   .
- Stimulated emission is the basis of laser operation, as it amplifies the incident light and produces a monochromatic, collimated, and intense beam of light .
