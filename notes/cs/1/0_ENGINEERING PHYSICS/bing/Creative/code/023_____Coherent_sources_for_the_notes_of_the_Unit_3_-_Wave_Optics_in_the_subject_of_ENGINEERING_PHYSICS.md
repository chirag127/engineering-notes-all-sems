### Coherent sources for the notes of the Unit 3 - Wave Optics in the subject of ENGINEERING PHYSICS

- Wave optics is the branch of physics that studies the nature and behavior of light as a wave phenomenon.
- Interference is the phenomenon of superposition of two or more waves of the same frequency and wavelength, resulting in a new wave pattern.
- Coherent sources are the sources of light that emit waves with the same frequency, wavelength and phase or having a constant phase difference.
- Coherent sources are necessary for producing stable and sustained interference patterns, where the positions of maxima and minima are fixed.
- Examples of coherent sources are lasers, which produce monochromatic, parallel and unbroken wave chains, and two slits illuminated by a single source of light, which act as secondary coherent sources.
- Incoherent sources are the sources of light that emit waves with different frequencies, wavelengths and phases or having a random phase difference.
- Incoherent sources produce irregular and transient interference patterns, where the positions of maxima and minima are constantly changing.
- Examples of incoherent sources are ordinary light bulbs, which produce polychromatic, divergent and broken wave chains, and two independent sources of light, which act as primary incoherent sources.
- Coherence can be classified into two types: temporal coherence and spatial coherence.
- Temporal coherence is the measure of the correlation between the phases of two waves at different times. It depends on the duration of the wave train and the bandwidth of the source.
- Spatial coherence is the measure of the correlation between the phases of two waves at different points in space. It depends on the size and shape of the source and the distance from the source.
- The degree of coherence is the quantitative expression of the coherence of a source. It ranges from 0 (completely incoherent) to 1 (completely coherent).
- The degree of coherence can be calculated by the formula:

$$\gamma = \frac{I_{max} - I_{min}}{I_{max} + I_{min}}$$

where $I_{max}$ and $I_{min}$ are the maximum and minimum intensities of the interference pattern.
