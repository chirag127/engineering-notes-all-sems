### Principles of laser action

- Laser is an acronym for **Light Amplification by Stimulated Emission of Radiation**.
- Laser action is the process of producing a coherent, monochromatic, and intense beam of light by stimulating the atoms or molecules of a medium to emit photons in a synchronized manner .
- Laser action involves the following steps :
  - **Population inversion**: The condition in which the number of atoms or molecules in an excited state is greater than the number in the ground state. This can be achieved by pumping energy into the medium using various methods, such as optical, electrical, or chemical sources.
  - **Stimulated emission**: The process in which an incoming photon of the same frequency as the transition between two energy levels of an atom or molecule induces the emission of another photon of the same frequency, phase, and direction. This results in the amplification of light .
  - **Optical feedback**: The mechanism that ensures that the stimulated emission occurs repeatedly within the medium, creating a chain reaction of photon multiplication. This can be achieved by using a resonant cavity, such as a pair of mirrors, that reflects the photons back and forth through the medium .
  - **Output coupling**: The process of extracting a fraction of the amplified light from the resonant cavity as the laser beam. This can be achieved by using a partially transparent mirror or a hole in one of the mirrors .
