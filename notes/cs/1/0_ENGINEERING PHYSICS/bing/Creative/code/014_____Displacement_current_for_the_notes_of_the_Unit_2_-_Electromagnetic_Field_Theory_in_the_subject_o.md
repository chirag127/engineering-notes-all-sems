### Displacement current

- Displacement current is a phenomenon in electromagnetism that is analogous to an ordinary electric current, but does not involve the flow of electric charges .
- Displacement current is defined as the rate of change of the electric displacement field D, which is related to the electric field E by the permittivity of the medium ε.
- Displacement current density Jd is given by the equation Jd = ∂D/∂t, where t is time.
- Displacement current was introduced by James Clerk Maxwell in his equations to explain the magnetic field produced by a changing electric field, such as in a capacitor .
- Displacement current plays a central role in the propagation of electromagnetic waves, such as light and radio waves, through empty space or dielectric materials .
- Displacement current allows the electric and magnetic fields to be coupled when the electric flux is time-varying, and thus enables the existence of electromagnetic waves.
