### Resolving power of grating

- Resolving power of grating is the ability of a grating to separate two spectral lines of very close wavelengths into distinct maxima.
- Resolving power of grating is measured by the ratio of the mean wavelength to the smallest difference in wavelengths that can be resolved, i.e. R.P. = λ/dλ .
- Resolving power of grating depends on the order of diffraction, the number of lines per unit length of the grating, and the width of the grating.
- Resolving power of grating can be derived from the condition of minimum angular separation between two spectral lines of wavelengths λ and λ + dλ in a given order n .
- The condition is given by d(sinθ + sinθ') = n(λ + dλ) - nλ, where d is the grating element, θ is the angle of diffraction for λ, and θ' is the angle of diffraction for λ + dλ .
- Simplifying the condition, we get d(cosθ)dθ = ndλ, where dθ is the angular separation between the two lines .
- The resolving power of grating is then given by R.P. = λ/dλ = d(cosθ)/ndθ .
- The resolving power of grating is maximum when θ = 0, i.e. when the spectral lines are in the normal direction .
- The resolving power of grating is also proportional to the total number of lines N on the grating, i.e. R.P. = Nn .
- The resolving power of grating can be increased by increasing the order of diffraction, the number of lines per unit length, or the width of the grating .
- The resolving power of grating is used to measure the spectral resolution of a grating or a spectroscopic instrument .
- A standard benchmark for the resolving power of grating is the resolution of the sodium doublet, which are two sodium lines at 589.00 nm and 589.59 nm.
- Another standard example is the resolution of the hydrogen and deuterium lines, which are at 656.28 nm and 656.10 nm respectively.
