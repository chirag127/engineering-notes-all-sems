### Displacement current

- Displacement current is a quantity appearing in Maxwell's equations that is defined in terms of the rate of change of the electric displacement field (D) .
- Displacement current density has the same units as electric current density, and it is a source of the magnetic field just as actual current is .
- Displacement current can be explained by the phenomenon observed in a capacitor. When a capacitor starts charging, there is no conduction of charge between the plates, but there is a changing electric field that induces a magnetic field .
- Displacement current is given by the formula:

`I_d = ε_0 dφ_E/dt`

where `I_d` is the displacement current, `ε_0` is the permittivity of free space, `φ_E` is the electric flux, and `t` is the time .

- Displacement current is related to the Ampere-Maxwell law, which states that the magnetic field around a closed loop is equal to the sum of the conduction current and the displacement current through the loop .
- The Ampere-Maxwell law can be written as:

`∮ B ⋅ dl = μ_0 (I_c + I_d)`

where `B` is the magnetic field, `dl` is the differential length element, `μ_0` is the permeability of free space, and `I_c` is the conduction current .

- Displacement current is important for the consistency of Maxwell's equations and the existence of electromagnetic waves. Without displacement current, there would be no magnetic field associated with a changing electric field, and vice versa .
