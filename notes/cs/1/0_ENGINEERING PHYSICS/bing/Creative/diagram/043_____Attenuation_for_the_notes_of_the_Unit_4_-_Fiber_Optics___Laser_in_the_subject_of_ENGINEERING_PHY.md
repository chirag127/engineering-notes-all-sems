### Attenuation

- Attenuation is the reduction in intensity of the light beam (or signal) with respect to distance travelled through a transmission medium .
- Attenuation limits the distance in which the signal can travel through optical fiber and is measured in decibels (dB).
- Attenuation (dB) = 10 x Log 10 (P in/P out) = 20xLog 10 (V in/V out) , where P is power and V is voltage.
- Attenuation can be caused by intrinsic and extrinsic factors .

#### Intrinsic factors

- Intrinsic factors are inherent to the fiber optic material and cannot be eliminated.
- They include absorption, scattering, and dispersion .
- Absorption is the conversion of light energy into heat by the fiber material or impurities in the fiber .
- Scattering is the deflection of light rays from their original direction by microscopic fluctuations in the fiber density or refractive index .
- Dispersion is the spreading of light pulses over time due to the different velocities of different wavelengths or modes in the fiber .

#### Extrinsic factors

- Extrinsic factors are external to the fiber optic material and can be minimized or avoided by proper installation and maintenance.
- They include bending, splicing, connectors, and environmental effects .
- Bending is the loss of light due to the curvature of the fiber, which can cause some light rays to escape from the core or reflect at a higher angle .
- Splicing is the joining of two fiber segments, which can cause light loss due to misalignment, air gaps, or impurities at the splice point .
- Connectors are the devices that connect fiber segments or fiber to other equipment, which can cause light loss due to reflection, misalignment, or dirt on the connector surface .
- Environmental effects are the changes in temperature, humidity, or pressure that can affect the fiber characteristics or cause physical damage to the fiber .
