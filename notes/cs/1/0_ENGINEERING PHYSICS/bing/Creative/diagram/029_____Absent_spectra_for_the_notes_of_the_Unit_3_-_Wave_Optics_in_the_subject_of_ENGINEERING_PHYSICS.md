### Absent spectra

- Absent spectra are the missing orders of principal maxima in the diffraction pattern of a plane grating.
- They occur when the minima due to diffraction coincide with the maxima due to interference for a certain order of diffraction.
- The condition for absent spectra is given by:

$$
nN = m
$$

where n is the order of diffraction, N is the number of lines per unit length of the grating, and m is an integer.

- The order of diffraction that is absent is given by:

$$
n = \frac{m}{N}
$$

- For example, if N = 5 lines per mm, then the orders of diffraction that are absent are n = 1/5, 2/5, 3/5, 4/5, etc.
- Absent spectra reduce the resolving power of the grating, as they reduce the number of lines that contribute to the diffraction pattern.
- Absent spectra can be avoided by using a grating with a large number of lines per unit length, or by using a blazed grating, which has a triangular profile that enhances the intensity of a certain order of diffraction.
