### Laser applications

- A laser is a device that emits light through a process of optical amplification based on the stimulated emission of electromagnetic radiation.
- The word "laser" is an acronym for "light amplification by stimulated emission of radiation".
- Lasers have many applications in various fields, such as communication, industry, medicine, science, military, and entertainment.
- Most laser applications fall into one of a few broad categories: (1) transmission and processing of information, (2) precise delivery of energy, and (3) alignment, measurement, and imaging.

#### Transmission and processing of information

- Lasers are used to transmit and process information in various ways, such as optical fiber communication, optical data storage, barcode scanners, laser printers, holography, and optical computing .
- Optical fiber communication is the transmission of data through thin strands of glass or plastic that carry light signals. Lasers are used as light sources to generate and modulate the signals.
- Optical data storage is the recording and retrieval of information using optical media, such as compact discs (CDs), digital versatile discs (DVDs), and Blu-ray discs. Lasers are used to read and write the data on the discs by creating and detecting pits on the surface.
- Barcode scanners are devices that read and decode the information encoded in barcodes, which are patterns of black and white stripes or dots. Lasers are used to scan the barcodes and generate an electrical signal that corresponds to the data.
- Laser printers are devices that produce high-quality text and graphics on paper by using a laser beam to scan a charged drum and transfer toner to the paper.
- Holography is a technique that creates three-dimensional images of objects by using a laser beam to record and reconstruct the interference pattern of light waves reflected or transmitted by the object.
- Optical computing is a field of research that aims to use light instead of electricity to perform computations and data processing. Lasers are potential components of optical computers, as they can generate, manipulate, and switch light signals.

#### Precise delivery of energy

- Lasers are used to deliver energy precisely and selectively to a target, such as a material, a tissue, or an atom .
- In industry, lasers are used for various processes, such as cutting, welding, drilling, marking, engraving, and surface treatment of materials. Lasers can produce high-intensity and focused beams that can melt, vaporize, or ablate the material with high accuracy and speed.
- In medicine, lasers are used for various treatments, such as surgery, vision correction, dentistry, dermatology, and photodynamic therapy . Lasers can cut, coagulate, or vaporize the tissue with minimal damage to the surrounding area, reduce bleeding and infection, and improve healing . Lasers can also correct refractive errors in the eye, such as myopia, hyperopia, and astigmatism, by reshaping the cornea using a technique called LASIK. Lasers can also treat various skin conditions, such as acne, scars, wrinkles, tattoos, and hair, by selectively destroying the target cells or pigment. Lasers can also activate photosensitive drugs that can kill cancer cells or bacteria by a process called photodynamic therapy.
- In science, lasers are used for various experiments, such as spectroscopy, microscopy, laser cooling, and laser trapping. Lasers can excite, ionize, or manipulate atoms or molecules and measure their properties, such as energy levels, spectra, and interactions. Lasers can also reduce the temperature and increase the order of a gas of atoms or molecules by using the radiation pressure of light, a technique called laser cooling. Lasers can also trap and manipulate individual atoms or molecules using focused laser beams, a technique called laser trapping or optical tweezers.
