### Principles of laser action

- Laser is an acronym for **Light Amplification by Stimulated Emission of Radiation**.
- Laser action is the process of producing a coherent, monochromatic, and intense beam of light by stimulating the atoms or molecules of a medium to emit photons in a synchronized manner.
- The basic principles of laser action are as follows :

  - **Spontaneous emission**: This is the natural process of an atom or molecule in an excited state (higher energy level) to decay to a lower energy level and emit a photon of a random frequency, phase, and direction.
  - **Stimulated emission**: This is the process of an atom or molecule in an excited state to be induced by an incoming photon of the same frequency, phase, and direction to decay to a lower energy level and emit another photon of the same frequency, phase, and direction. This results in the amplification of the incoming photon by creating a second identical photon.
  - **Stimulated absorption**: This is the process of an atom or molecule in a lower energy level to be excited by an incoming photon of the same frequency to a higher energy level and absorb the photon. This results in the attenuation of the incoming photon by removing it from the beam.
  - **Non-radiative decay**: This is the process of an atom or molecule in an excited state to lose energy by other means than emitting a photon, such as collisions, vibrations, or heat. This results in the reduction of the population of the excited state and the efficiency of the laser.
  - **Population inversion**: This is the condition of having more atoms or molecules in an excited state than in a lower energy state. This is necessary for achieving laser action, as it ensures that the stimulated emission rate is higher than the stimulated absorption rate, and that the net gain of photons is positive.
  - **Amplification of light**: This is the result of having a population inversion and a positive net gain of photons in a medium. The photons are amplified by stimulated emission as they travel through the medium, creating a coherent, monochromatic, and intense beam of light.

- The basic components of a laser are as follows:

  - **Active medium**: This is the material that provides the atoms or molecules that undergo the transitions between energy levels and emit or absorb photons. The active medium can be a gas, a liquid, a solid, or a plasma, depending on the type of laser.
  - **Pump source**: This is the device that provides the energy to create a population inversion in the active medium. The pump source can be an electric current, a flash lamp, a chemical reaction, or another laser, depending on the type of laser.
  - **Optical cavity**: This is the structure that confines and reflects the photons within the active medium, creating a positive feedback loop that enhances the amplification of light. The optical cavity consists of two mirrors, one of which is partially transparent to allow some of the light to escape as the laser output.
  - **Output coupler**: This is the partially transparent mirror that allows some of the light to exit the optical cavity as the laser output. The output coupler determines the power and the quality of the laser beam.
