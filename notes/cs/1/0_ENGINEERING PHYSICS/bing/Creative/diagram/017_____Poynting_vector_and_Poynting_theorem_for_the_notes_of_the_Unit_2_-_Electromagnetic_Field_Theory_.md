### Poynting vector and Poynting theorem

- The Poynting vector, denoted by **S**, is a vector quantity that represents the directional energy flux (the energy transfer per unit area per unit time) of an electromagnetic field.
- The Poynting vector is defined as the cross product of the electric field vector **E** and the magnetic field vector **H** at any point in space, that is, **S = E x H** .
- The Poynting vector has the units of watts per square meter (W/m^2^) and the direction of **S** is the direction of energy flow.
- The Poynting theorem, also known as the electromagnetic energy conservation law, states that the net electromagnetic power flowing into a region of space may be either dissipated, or used to change the energy stored in electric and magnetic fields within that region.
- The Poynting theorem can be expressed mathematically as:

  **∇ · S = - ∂u/∂t - J · E**

  where **u** is the electromagnetic energy density, **J** is the electric current density, and **∇ · S** is the divergence of the Poynting vector.

- The Poynting theorem can be interpreted as follows:

  - The left-hand side of the equation, **∇ · S**, represents the net power per unit volume flowing out of a region of space through its boundary.
  - The first term on the right-hand side, **- ∂u/∂t**, represents the rate of change of electromagnetic energy per unit volume stored in the region of space.
  - The second term on the right-hand side, **- J · E**, represents the rate of work done per unit volume by the electric field on the electric charges in the region of space.
  - The Poynting theorem implies that the net power flowing out of a region of space is equal to the sum of the rate of decrease of electromagnetic energy and the rate of work done by the electric field in that region.

- The Poynting theorem can be applied to various situations involving electromagnetic fields, such as transmission lines, antennas, waveguides, and radiation.
