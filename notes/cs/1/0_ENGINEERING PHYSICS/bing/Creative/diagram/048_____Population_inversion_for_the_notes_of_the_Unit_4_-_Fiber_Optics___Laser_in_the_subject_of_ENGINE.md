### Population inversion

- Population inversion is a state of a system, for example a laser gain medium, where a higher-lying energy level is more strongly populated than a lower-lying level.
- Population inversion is an important concept for lasers optics, as it is essential for light amplification by stimulated emission .
- Stimulated emission is the process by which an excited atom emits a photon of the same frequency and phase as an incident photon, resulting in the amplification of the incident light.
- Population inversion cannot occur under conditions of thermal equilibrium, where the level populations are described by a Boltzmann distribution, which favors lower energy levels.
- Population inversion can be achieved by various methods, such as optical pumping, electrical pumping, chemical pumping, or collisional pumping.
- Optical pumping is the method of using a light source, such as a flash lamp or a laser, to excite the atoms of the gain medium to higher energy levels.
- Electrical pumping is the method of using an electric current, such as a discharge tube or a diode, to excite the atoms of the gain medium to higher energy levels.
- Chemical pumping is the method of using a chemical reaction, such as combustion or explosion, to produce excited molecules that can transfer their energy to the atoms of the gain medium.
- Collisional pumping is the method of using a gas or plasma, such as a microwave or a radio frequency, to collide with the atoms of the gain medium and transfer their kinetic energy to them.
- Population inversion can be achieved in different types of lasers, such as gas lasers, solid-state lasers, fiber lasers, or semiconductor lasers, depending on the nature and structure of the gain medium.
- Population inversion can also be achieved in different configurations of the gain medium, such as a four-level system, a three-level system, or a two-level system, depending on the number and arrangement of the energy levels involved.
- A four-level system is a configuration where the population inversion is achieved between two intermediate levels, while the ground state and the highest state are rapidly depopulated by spontaneous emission or non-radiative transitions.
- A three-level system is a configuration where the population inversion is achieved between the ground state and an intermediate level, while the highest state is rapidly depopulated by spontaneous emission or non-radiative transitions.
- A two-level system is a configuration where the population inversion is achieved between the ground state and the highest state, which is theoretically impossible to maintain in a steady state, as the stimulated emission rate will eventually equal the absorption rate.
- Population inversion is influenced by various factors, such as the pumping rate, the gain medium length, the cavity losses, the mode structure, or the saturation effects.
- The pumping rate is the rate at which the atoms of the gain medium are excited to higher energy levels by the pumping source.
- The gain medium length is the distance along which the light travels through the gain medium and interacts with the excited atoms.
- The cavity losses are the losses of light due to reflection, scattering, absorption, or diffraction at the boundaries or the components of the laser cavity.
- The mode structure is the set of spatial and temporal patterns of the electromagnetic field that can resonate in the laser cavity.
- The saturation effects are the effects of reducing the population inversion and the gain as the stimulated emission rate increases with the intensity of the light .
- Population inversion is a necessary condition for all lasers, regardless of their power or wavelength, because all lasers operate on the same basic principle of a chain reaction of stimulated emission.
