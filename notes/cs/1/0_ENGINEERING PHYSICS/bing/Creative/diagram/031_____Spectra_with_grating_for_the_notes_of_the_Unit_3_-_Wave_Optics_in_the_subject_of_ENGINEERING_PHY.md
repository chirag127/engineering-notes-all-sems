### Spectra with grating

- A **diffraction grating** is an optical device that consists of a large number of equally spaced parallel slits or grooves that diffract light into a series of bright and dark fringes or spectra .
- The **grating equation** relates the wavelength of the light, the grating spacing, the angle of incidence and the angle of diffraction for a given order of the spectrum .
- The **grating constant** or **grating pitch** is the distance between adjacent slits or grooves in the grating .
- The **order** of the spectrum is an integer that indicates how many wavelengths the light is shifted by the grating. The zeroth order corresponds to the undiffracted light, while the higher orders correspond to the diffracted light .
- The **dispersion** of the grating is the angular separation of two wavelengths in the same order of the spectrum. The dispersion depends on the grating constant, the order and the wavelength of the light .
- The **resolving power** of the grating is the ability to distinguish two closely spaced wavelengths in the spectrum. The resolving power depends on the number of slits or grooves in the grating, the grating constant, the order and the wavelength of the light .
- The **spectrometer** is an instrument that uses a diffraction grating to measure the spectrum of a light source. The spectrometer consists of a collimator, a grating, a telescope and a scale .
- The **emission spectrum** of a light source is the set of wavelengths that the source emits. The emission spectrum can be used to identify the chemical composition of the source.
- The **absorption spectrum** of a light source is the set of wavelengths that the source absorbs. The absorption spectrum can be used to determine the presence of certain elements or molecules in the source.
- The **continuous spectrum** of a light source is the set of wavelengths that the source emits or absorbs without any gaps. The continuous spectrum is produced by a hot solid, liquid or dense gas.
- The **line spectrum** of a light source is the set of discrete wavelengths that the source emits or absorbs. The line spectrum is produced by a hot thin gas or a cold gas in front of a continuous source.
