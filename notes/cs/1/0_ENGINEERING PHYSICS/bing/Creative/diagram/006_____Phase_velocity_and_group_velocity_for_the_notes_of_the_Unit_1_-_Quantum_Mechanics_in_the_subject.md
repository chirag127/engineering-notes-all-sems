### Phase velocity and group velocity

- Phase velocity is the rate at which the phase of a wave propagates in any medium. It is given by the ratio of the angular frequency to the wave number: `v_p = ω/k`.
- Group velocity is the velocity with which the overall envelope shape of the wave's amplitudes propagates through space. It is given by the derivative of the angular frequency with respect to the wave number: `v_g = dω/dk`.
- Phase velocity and group velocity are related by Rayleigh's formula: `v_p v_g = c^2`, where `c` is the speed of light in vacuum.
- If the wave is non-dispersive, meaning that the angular frequency is proportional to the wave number, then the phase velocity and the group velocity are equal and constant.
- If the wave is dispersive, meaning that the angular frequency depends on the wave number in a non-linear way, then the phase velocity and the group velocity are different and vary with the wave number.
- In quantum mechanics, a wave packet is a superposition of plane waves with different wave numbers and frequencies. The wave packet represents the state of a particle with a certain position and momentum uncertainty.
- The phase velocity of a wave packet is the velocity of the individual plane waves that make up the packet. It is related to the energy of the particle by the de Broglie relation: `E = ℏω = ℏv_p k`.
- The group velocity of a wave packet is the velocity of the envelope of the packet. It is related to the momentum of the particle by the de Broglie relation: `p = ℏk = ℏv_g dω/dk`.
- For a free particle, the angular frequency is proportional to the square of the wave number: `ω = ℏk^2/2m`, where `m` is the mass of the particle. Therefore, the phase velocity is `v_p = ℏk/2m` and the group velocity is `v_g = ℏk/m`.
- For a free particle, the group velocity is twice the phase velocity: `v_g = 2v_p`. This means that the envelope of the wave packet moves faster than the individual plane waves. The group velocity is also equal to the classical velocity of the particle: `v_g = p/m`.
