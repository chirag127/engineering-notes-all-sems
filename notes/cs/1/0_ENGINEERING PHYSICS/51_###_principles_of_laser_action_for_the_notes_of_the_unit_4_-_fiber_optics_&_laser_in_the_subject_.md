### Principles of laser action for the notes of the Unit 4 - Fiber Optics & Laser in the subject of ENGINEERING PHYSICS

Principles of laser action refer to the basic physical processes that allow a laser to generate coherent light. The key ingredients for laser action are:

1. Active medium: A material capable of amplifying light through stimulated emission.

2. Pumping source: A device used to excite the active medium and increase the population inversion.

3. Optical resonator: A cavity that defines the spatial and temporal coherence of the laser beam.

4. Stimulated emission: The process by which an excited atom in the active medium emits a photon that is in phase with an incoming photon and reinforces the laser beam.

5. Population inversion: A condition where the number of excited atoms is greater than the number of ground-state atoms in the active medium.

6. Optical feedback: The reflection of light from the end mirrors of the optical resonator that reinforces the laser beam and increases its intensity.

These principles are essential for understanding the behavior of lasers and their applications in fiber optics and other fields.
