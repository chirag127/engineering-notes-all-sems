### Interference in uniform and wedge shaped thin films for the notes of the Unit 3 - Wave Optics in the subject of ENGINEERING PHYSICS

Interference is a phenomenon in which two or more waves combine to produce a resultant wave. This can occur in thin films, such as those found in wedge or uniform shapes. 

In uniform thin films, interference occurs due to the reflection and transmission of light waves at the film's surfaces. The resulting interference pattern is dependent on the thickness of the film and the wavelength of the light. 

In wedge-shaped thin films, the interference pattern is more complex due to the varying thickness of the film. The interference pattern is produced by the combination of light waves that are reflected and transmitted at each of the film's surfaces. The pattern is dependent on both the angle of the wedge and the wavelength of the light. 

In both uniform and wedge-shaped thin films, the interference patterns can be observed by shining light on the films and observing the resulting patterns on a screen. The patterns can also be analyzed to determine the thickness of the films and the nature of the materials they are made of.
