### Attenuation for the notes of the Unit 4 - Fiber Optics & Laser in the subject of ENGINEERING PHYSICS

Attenuation in fiber optics refers to the reduction in amplitude of the optical signal as it travels through the fiber optic cable. This reduction is caused by absorption and scatter losses. Absorption losses are due to the material properties of the glass fiber, while scatter losses are caused by microscopic irregularities in the glass structure. Attenuation can be reduced by using higher quality fiber, increasing the signal power, or using optical amplifiers. Attenuation is measured in decibels per kilometer (dB/km).

Note: Attenuation is a critical factor in the design and performance of fiber optic networks, as it determines the maximum distance that a signal can travel before it becomes too weak to be detected. It is important to choose the appropriate fiber type and design the network to minimize attenuation.
