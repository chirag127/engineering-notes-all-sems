### Dispersion for the notes of the Unit 4 - Fiber Optics & Laser in the subject of ENGINEERING PHYSICS

Dispersion in fiber optics refers to the spreading of light into its component colors (wavelengths) as it travels through the fiber. This occurs because different wavelengths of light travel at different speeds in the fiber, causing them to separate or "disperse." There are two main types of dispersion: modal dispersion and material dispersion. Modal dispersion is caused by differences in the number of times a light pulse bounces back and forth between the cladding and core of the fiber, while material dispersion is caused by differences in the refractive index of the fiber for different wavelengths of light. Dispersion can limit the bandwidth and distance of fiber optic communication systems, so it is important to control and minimize dispersion through the use of dispersion-shifted fiber, dispersion compensating fibers, and wavelength division multiplexing.
