#### Topic: Subject-Verb Agreement

Subject-verb agreement is a crucial rule in English grammar that governs the relationship between the subject and the verb in a sentence. In simple terms, it means that the verb should agree with the subject in number (singular or plural).

Here are some important points to keep in mind while using subject-verb agreement:

- The verb should agree with the subject in number. For example, "He runs" is correct, while "He run" is incorrect.
- When the subject and verb are separated by a phrase or clause, the verb should still agree with the subject. For example, "The boy, along with his friends, is going to the party" is correct, while "The boy, along with his friends, are going to the party" is incorrect.
- When the subject is a collective noun, the verb should agree with the noun's definition (singular or plural). For example, "The team is playing well" is correct, while "The team are playing well" is incorrect.
- When the subject consists of two or more nouns connected by "and," the verb should be plural. For example, "The cat and the dog are playing in the yard" is correct, while "The cat and the dog is playing in the yard" is incorrect.
- When the subject consists of two or more singular nouns connected by "or," the verb should be singular. For example, "Either the cat or the dog is responsible for the mess" is correct, while "Either the cat or the dog are responsible for the mess" is incorrect.
- When the subject consists of a singular noun that refers to a group of people or things, the verb should be plural. For example, "The committee have made their decision" is correct, while "The committee has made its decision" is incorrect.

Overall, subject-verb agreement is an important aspect of English grammar that helps to ensure that sentences are clear, concise, and grammatically correct. By following the rules of subject-verb agreement, you can improve your writing and communication skills.
