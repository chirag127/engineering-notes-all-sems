### Homonyms, New word Formation, Select word power

Homonyms: Words that sound alike but have different spellings and meanings.

- There/their/they're: There (in a place), their (possessive), they're (contraction of they are)
- Two/too/to: Two (number), too (also, very), to (in a direction)
- Blew/blue: Blew (past tense of blow), blue (color)

New word formation: Creating new words through processes like affixation and compounding.

- Affixation: Adding prefixes and suffixes to base words to create new words, e.g. happy -> unhappiness, connect -> connection
- Compounding: Combining two words to form a new word, e.g. snowflake, football, butterfly

Selecting powerful words: Choosing impactful words to strengthen writing and speeches.

- Specific: Replace vague words like thing with specific words like automobile or flower
- Concise: Remove unnecessary words and be precise
- Emotive: Use words that evoke emotion like stirring, captivating, compelling instead of good, nice, interesting
- Varied: Use a range of words for a lively tone instead of repetitive writing

To summarize, being aware of homonyms, utilizing methods to create new meaningful words, and selecting powerful words can enhance verbal and written communication. Consistent practice of these concepts will develop one's vocabulary and language skills.
