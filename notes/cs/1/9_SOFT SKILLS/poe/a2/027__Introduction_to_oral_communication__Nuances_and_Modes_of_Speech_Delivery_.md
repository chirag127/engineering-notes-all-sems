### Introduction to oral communication, Nuances and Modes of Speech Delivery,

1. Oral communication: Oral communication refers to the verbal exchange of thoughts, ideas, and information. It is one-on-one communication either in-person or over the phone. Some key aspects of oral communication include tone of voice, pace of speech, accent, and word choice.

2. Nuances: Nuances refer to subtle differences in tone, expression, or meaning. In oral communication, nuances are conveyed through tone of voice, pace, pauses, volume, and other vocal cues. Being aware of nuances allows us to interpret the emotional subtext of a conversation and understand the speaker's implied meaning.

3. Modes of speech delivery: There are three primary modes of speech delivery:

- Reading: Speech is read word-for-word from a written document. Reading delivers content with accuracy but can sound stiff or boring.
- Memorization: Speech is memorized and delivered from memory. Memorization allows for stronger delivery but risks inaccuracies if parts of the speech are forgotten.
- Impromptu: Speech is delivered spontaneously without reading or memorizing. Impromptu speaking is authentic but risks filler words or disorganized thoughts. The most compelling speeches use a blend of memorization and impromptu elements.
