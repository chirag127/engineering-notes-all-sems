### 3. Personality Development, Harold R. Wallace et. al, Cengage Learning India Pvt. Ltd; New Delhi

1. Personality refers to the long-standing patterns of thoughts, feelings, and behaviors that are characteristic of an individual. It includes all the physical, mental, emotional and social characteristics that make a person unique.
2. Personality develops as a result of the interplay of genetic, biological, environmental and situational factors.
3. The major approaches to understanding personality are:

- Psychoanalytic approach: Focuses on the influence of the unconscious mind and early childhood experiences.
- Humanistic approach: Emphasizes the importance of self-actualization and free will.
- Social-cognitive approach: Views behavior as influenced by expectations and personal factors.
- Trait approach: Identifies broad personal characteristics that summarize individuals' personalities.

4. The 'Big Five' personality traits are:

- Openness to experience: Imagination and insight
- Conscientiousness: Organization and hard work
- Extraversion: Energy, positive emotions, and tendency to seek stimulation in the company of others
- Agreeableness: Tendency to be compassionate and cooperative
- Neuroticism: Tendency to experience negative emotions

5. Personality assessment can be done using objective tests, projective tests and personal interviews. The most commonly used objective personality tests are Minnesota Multiphasic Personality Inventory, 16 Personality Factor Questionnaire, and NEO Personality Inventory.

6. Personality development involves developing positive traits and managing negative traits through conscious efforts and environmental support. Techniques include setting goals, positive self-talk, relaxation techniques, and surrounding oneself with a strong support system.
