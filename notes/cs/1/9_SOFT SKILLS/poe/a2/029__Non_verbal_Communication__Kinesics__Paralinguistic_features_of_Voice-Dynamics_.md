### Non verbal Communication: Kinesics, Paralinguistic features of Voice-Dynamics,

1. Kinesics: Study of bodily movements and gestures including facial expressions. Important aspects are:
- Eye contact: Amount, duration and pattern of eye contact determines the dynamics of communication. Excessive or lack of it can lead to misinterpretation.
- Facial expressions: Smile, frown, raised eyebrows, etc. are used to convey emotions and attitudes.
- Gestures: Movements of hands, head or other body parts to emphasize speech or convey meaning. Cultural differences exist in meaning of gestures.
- Posture: Standing or sitting posture of individuals affects communication dynamics. For example, slouching may indicate disinterest while leaning forward shows interest.

2. Paralinguistic features: Vocal cues that accompany speech and affect the dynamics of communication. Includes:
- Pitch: High or low pitch, variations convey emotional state or intention of the speaker.
- Volume: Loudness or softness of speech conveys intensity of emotions or social context.
- Pace: Rapid or slow speaking pace impacts the interpretation of the message. Fast pace may show excitement while slow pace implies careful consideration of words.
- Pauses: Delays in speech can be used for effect to emphasize points or to deal with cognitive difficulties in finding appropriate words. Length and frequency of pauses is significant.

The above points highlight how non-verbal cues impact the dynamics of face-to-face communication through bodily movements and vocal elements even when verbal language is absent or incongruent. Understanding such cues is important to interpret intent and meaning.
