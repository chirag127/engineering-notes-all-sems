### Homonyms, New word Formation, Select word power

- Homonyms are words that have the same spelling and pronunciation but different meanings. For example, bat can mean a flying mammal or a wooden stick used in sports.
- New word formation is the process of creating new words from existing words or parts of words. There are different ways of forming new words, such as:
  - Affixation: adding a prefix or a suffix to a word. For example, un + happy = unhappy, happy + ness = happiness.
  - Compounding: combining two or more words to form a new word. For example, black + board = blackboard, sun + flower = sunflower.
  - Blending: merging parts of two words to form a new word. For example, smoke + fog = smog, breakfast + lunch = brunch.
  - Clipping: shortening a word by deleting some letters or syllables. For example, advertisement = ad, television = TV.
  - Acronym: forming a word from the initial letters of a phrase or a name. For example, NASA = National Aeronautics and Space Administration, AIDS = Acquired Immune Deficiency Syndrome.
  - Conversion: changing the word class of a word without changing its form. For example, run (verb) = run (noun), green (adjective) = green (noun).
- Select word power is the ability to choose the most appropriate and effective words for a given context or purpose. It involves knowing the meaning, usage, and connotation of words, as well as their synonyms, antonyms, and collocations. For example, to select word power, you should:
  - Use specific and concrete words instead of vague and abstract words. For example, instead of saying "She was nice", say "She was friendly and helpful".
  - Use precise and accurate words instead of general and ambiguous words. For example, instead of saying "He likes animals", say "He likes dogs and cats".
  - Use positive and polite words instead of negative and rude words. For example, instead of saying "He is lazy and stupid", say "He needs to work harder and learn more".
  - Use varied and rich words instead of repetitive and dull words. For example, instead of saying "She is very very happy", say "She is ecstatic and overjoyed".
