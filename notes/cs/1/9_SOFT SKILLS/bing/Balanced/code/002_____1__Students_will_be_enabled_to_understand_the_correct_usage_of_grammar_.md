Grammar is the set of rules and conventions that govern how words and sentences are structured and used in a language. Grammar helps us to communicate clearly and effectively, and to avoid misunderstandings and errors. 

Some of the main aspects of grammar are:

- **Parts of speech**: These are the categories of words that have different functions and meanings in a sentence, such as nouns, verbs, adjectives, adverbs, pronouns, prepositions, conjunctions, and interjections.
- **Sentence structure**: This is the way that words and phrases are arranged and connected to form a complete and meaningful sentence, such as subject, predicate, object, complement, modifier, clause, and phrase.
- **Punctuation**: These are the symbols and marks that help to separate and organize sentences and parts of sentences, such as periods, commas, colons, semicolons, question marks, exclamation marks, quotation marks, apostrophes, hyphens, dashes, parentheses, brackets, and ellipses.
- **Spelling**: This is the way that words are written using the letters of the alphabet, following the standard rules and conventions of the language, such as vowel sounds, consonant sounds, silent letters, prefixes, suffixes, and root words.
- **Capitalization**: This is the use of uppercase and lowercase letters to indicate the beginning of sentences, proper nouns, titles, acronyms, and other special cases, such as names of people, places, organizations, events, and publications.
- **Tenses**: These are the forms of verbs that indicate the time and aspect of an action or state, such as present, past, future, simple, progressive, perfect, and perfect progressive.
- **Moods**: These are the forms of verbs that indicate the attitude or mode of expression of the speaker or writer, such as indicative, imperative, subjunctive, conditional, and interrogative.
- **Voices**: These are the forms of verbs that indicate the relationship between the subject and the object of an action, such as active and passive.
- **Agreement**: This is the correspondence or harmony between different parts of speech in terms of number, person, gender, and case, such as subject-verb agreement and noun-pronoun agreement.
- **Modifiers**: These are the words or phrases that add information or details to other words or phrases, such as adjectives, adverbs, articles, determiners, quantifiers, and clauses.
- **Clauses**: These are the groups of words that contain a subject and a predicate, and that can function as a part of a sentence or as a whole sentence, such as independent clauses, dependent clauses, noun clauses, adjective clauses, and adverb clauses.
- **Phrases**: These are the groups of words that do not contain a subject and a predicate, and that can function as a part of a sentence, such as noun phrases, verb phrases, prepositional phrases, participial phrases, and infinitive phrases.

To understand the correct usage of grammar, students need to:

- Learn the definitions and examples of the different aspects of grammar.
- Practice identifying and using the different aspects of grammar in various contexts and situations.
- Review and revise the rules and conventions of grammar regularly and frequently.
- Check and correct their own and others' grammar errors and mistakes.
- Read and write texts that demonstrate the correct and effective use of grammar.
