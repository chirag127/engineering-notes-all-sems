- Technical communication is the process of conveying information and instructions to a specific audience for a specific purpose.
- Technical communication can be written or oral, formal or informal, digital or print, and can involve various media and formats.
- Technical communication has four main characteristics: accuracy, clarity, conciseness, and appropriateness.
- Technical communication can be classified into three types: instructional, informational, and persuasive.
- Instructional communication provides directions or guidance on how to perform a task or use a product.
- Informational communication provides facts, data, or explanations about a topic or phenomenon.
- Persuasive communication aims to influence the attitudes, beliefs, or actions of the audience.
- Technical communication can serve various purposes, such as informing, instructing, persuading, reporting, proposing, recommending, documenting, or evaluating.
- Technical communication can have various audiences, such as experts, peers, managers, customers, clients, users, or the general public.
- Technical communication can have various contexts, such as academic, professional, industrial, scientific, or social.
