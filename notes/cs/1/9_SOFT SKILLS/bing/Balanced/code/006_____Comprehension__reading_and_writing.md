### Comprehension, reading and writing

Comprehension, reading and writing are interrelated skills that are essential for academic success and lifelong learning. 

- Comprehension is the ability to understand and interpret texts, both written and spoken. It involves extracting meaning from words, sentences, paragraphs and larger units of discourse, as well as making connections, inferences and judgments based on the text and prior knowledge.
- Reading is the process of decoding and comprehending written texts. It involves recognizing words, applying grammar and vocabulary knowledge, and using various strategies to enhance comprehension, such as skimming, scanning, predicting, summarizing and questioning.
- Writing is the process of encoding and communicating ideas in written texts. It involves generating and organizing content, choosing appropriate words, sentences and paragraphs, applying grammar and punctuation rules, and revising and editing for clarity, coherence and accuracy.

Some of the benefits of developing comprehension, reading and writing skills are:

- They enable learners to access and use information from various sources and disciplines, such as literature, science, history and mathematics.
- They foster critical thinking, creativity and problem-solving skills, as learners can analyze, evaluate and synthesize information from texts, and express their own opinions, arguments and solutions.
- They enhance communication and collaboration skills, as learners can share their ideas and perspectives with others, and respond to feedback and questions.
- They promote self-regulation and metacognition, as learners can monitor and reflect on their own learning processes and outcomes, and set goals and strategies for improvement.
