## Adverbs and adverbial phrases

- An adverb is a word that modifies a verb, an adjective, or another adverb. It usually answers the questions how, when, where, why, or to what extent.
- Examples: He ran quickly. (how) She arrived yesterday. (when) They live nearby. (where) He studied hard because he wanted to pass the exam. (why) She is very smart. (to what extent)
- An adverbial phrase is a group of words that functions as an adverb. It usually consists of an adverb and its modifiers, or a preposition and its object.
- Examples: He ran with great speed. (with great speed is an adverbial phrase modifying ran) She arrived after lunch. (after lunch is an adverbial phrase modifying arrived) They live in the city. (in the city is an adverbial phrase modifying live) He studied hard in order to pass the exam. (in order to pass the exam is an adverbial phrase modifying studied) She is smart enough to solve the problem. (enough to solve the problem is an adverbial phrase modifying smart)

## Prepositions and prepositional phrases

- A preposition is a word that shows the relationship between a noun or a pronoun and another word in the sentence. It usually indicates the position, direction, time, or manner of something.
- Examples: The book is on the table. (on shows the position of the book) He went to the park. (to shows the direction of his movement) She works from nine to five. (from and to show the time of her work) He drives with care. (with shows the manner of his driving)
- A prepositional phrase is a group of words that begins with a preposition and ends with a noun or a pronoun, which is called the object of the preposition. It functions as an adjective or an adverb in the sentence.
- Examples: The book on the table is mine. (on the table is a prepositional phrase functioning as an adjective modifying book) He went to the park with his friends. (to the park and with his friends are prepositional phrases functioning as adverbs modifying went) She works from nine to five every day. (from nine to five and every day are prepositional phrases functioning as adverbs modifying works) He drives with care and caution. (with care and caution are prepositional phrases functioning as adverbs modifying drives)

## Conjunctions and clauses

- A conjunction is a word that connects words, phrases, or clauses in a sentence. It usually shows the relationship between the connected elements, such as contrast, addition, cause, or condition.
- Examples: He likes apples but hates oranges. (but shows contrast) She is smart and kind. (and shows addition) He studied hard because he wanted to pass the exam. (because shows cause) He will go to the party if he finishes his homework. (if shows condition)
- A clause is a group of words that has a subject and a predicate. It can be either independent or dependent. An independent clause can stand alone as a complete sentence, while a dependent clause cannot and needs to be attached to an independent clause.
- Examples: He likes apples. (independent clause) He hates oranges. (independent clause) He likes apples but hates oranges. (two independent clauses connected by a conjunction) Because he wanted to pass the exam. (dependent clause) He studied hard. (independent clause) He studied hard because he wanted to pass the exam. (an independent clause and a dependent clause connected by a conjunction)

## Punctuation and capitalization

- Punctuation is the use of symbols, such as commas, periods, question marks, quotation marks, etc., to mark the boundaries and clarify the meaning of sentences and their parts. It helps the reader to understand the writer's intended message and tone.
