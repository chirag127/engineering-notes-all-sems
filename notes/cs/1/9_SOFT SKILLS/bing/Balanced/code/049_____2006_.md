# 2006

2006 was a year that marked many significant events in various fields such as politics, sports, science, entertainment, and culture. Some of the main events that happened in 2006 are:

- President George W. Bush signed the USA Patriot Act into law, extending the government's authority to conduct surveillance and detain suspected terrorists.
- Democrats gained control of both houses of Congress during the midterm elections, ending 12 years of Republican dominance.
- The Dow Jones Industrial Average closed above 12,000 for the first time in its 110-year history, reflecting the strength of the U.S. economy.
- Del Monte announced that it would end pineapple production in Hawaii because it was no longer economically feasible to grow pineapple in the state.
- An earthquake struck Java, Indonesia, killing over 6,600 people and displacing more than 1.5 million others.
- A massive mudslide occurred in Southern Leyte, Philippines, killing an estimated 1,126 people and burying an entire village.
- Explosions occurred at the al-Askari Shrine in Samara, Iraq, one of the holiest sites for Shia Muslims, sparking sectarian violence that killed hundreds of people.
- The 2006 Winter Olympics were held in Turin, Italy, with Germany topping the medal table with 29 medals, followed by the United States with 25 and Canada with 24.
- Prime Minister Ariel Sharon of Israel suffered a second, apparently more serious stroke, leaving him in a coma and ending his political career.
- The International Astronomical Union redefined the criteria for planets, demoting Pluto to a dwarf planet and adding three new ones: Ceres, Eris, and Makemake.
- North Korea conducted its first nuclear test, defying international sanctions and provoking condemnation from the United Nations and other countries.
- Saddam Hussein, the former president of Iraq, was executed by hanging for crimes against humanity committed during his rule.
- Google acquired YouTube, the popular video-sharing website, for $1.65 billion, making it one of the largest acquisitions in the history of the internet.
- The Da Vinci Code, a controversial novel by Dan Brown, was adapted into a blockbuster film starring Tom Hanks and Audrey Tautou, sparking protests and lawsuits from religious groups and historians.
- High School Musical, a Disney Channel original movie, became a cultural phenomenon, launching the careers of Zac Efron, Vanessa Hudgens, and other young stars.
