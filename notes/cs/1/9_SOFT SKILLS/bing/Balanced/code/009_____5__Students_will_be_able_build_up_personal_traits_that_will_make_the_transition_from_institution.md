### 5. Students will be able build up personal traits that will make the transition from institution

- The transition from institution to work or further education can be challenging for many students, especially those who lack the necessary personal traits to cope with the changes and demands of the new environment.
- Some of the personal traits that can help students make a successful transition are:
  - Self-awareness: the ability to recognize one's strengths, weaknesses, interests, values, and goals, and to use this information to guide one's decisions and actions.
  - Self-regulation: the ability to manage one's emotions, impulses, and behaviors, and to adapt to different situations and feedback.
  - Self-efficacy: the belief in one's capabilities to achieve one's goals and to overcome challenges and obstacles.
  - Self-motivation: the drive to pursue one's goals and to persist in the face of difficulties and setbacks.
  - Self-advocacy: the ability to communicate one's needs, preferences, and rights, and to seek and use available resources and support.
  - Self-care: the ability to take care of one's physical, mental, and emotional health and well-being, and to seek help when needed.
- Students can build up these personal traits by engaging in various activities and experiences, such as:
  - Participating in extracurricular activities, clubs, or volunteer work that match one's interests and values, and that provide opportunities to develop one's skills and talents, and to interact with diverse people and perspectives.
  - Seeking feedback from teachers, peers, mentors, or counselors, and using it to improve one's performance and to identify one's areas of improvement and growth.
  - Setting realistic and specific goals for one's academic, career, and personal development, and monitoring one's progress and achievements.
  - Taking responsibility for one's learning and actions, and reflecting on one's successes and failures, and on the factors that contribute to them.
  - Exploring different career options and pathways, and learning about the requirements, expectations, and opportunities of various fields and occupations.
  - Practicing effective communication, negotiation, and problem-solving skills, and learning how to express one's opinions, needs, and rights respectfully and assertively.
  - Maintaining a healthy lifestyle, and engaging in activities that promote one's physical, mental, and emotional well-being, such as exercise, relaxation, hobbies, or socializing.
  - Seeking professional help or counseling when facing personal, academic, or career challenges or difficulties, and using the available resources and support services.
