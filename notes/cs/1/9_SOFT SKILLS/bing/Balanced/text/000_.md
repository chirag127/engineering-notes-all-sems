# Nuclear Fusion

Nuclear fusion is the process of combining two or more atomic nuclei to form a heavier nucleus, releasing energy in the process. Nuclear fusion is the source of energy for the Sun and other stars.

Some points to remember about nuclear fusion are:

- Nuclear fusion requires high temperatures and pressures to overcome the electrostatic repulsion between positively charged nuclei.
- The most common fusion reaction in stars is the proton-proton chain, which converts hydrogen into helium and releases energy and neutrinos.
- Another fusion reaction that occurs in stars is the CNO cycle, which involves carbon, nitrogen and oxygen as catalysts for hydrogen fusion.
- The fusion of lighter elements up to iron releases energy, while the fusion of heavier elements absorbs energy. This is because iron has the highest binding energy per nucleon among all elements.
- Nuclear fusion can also be achieved artificially in devices such as tokamaks, stellarators, inertial confinement fusion and fusion rockets. However, none of these methods have achieved sustained net energy output yet.
- Nuclear fusion has many potential benefits, such as producing clean and abundant energy, reducing greenhouse gas emissions, and enabling interstellar travel. However, it also faces many challenges, such as technical difficulties, safety issues, environmental impacts and political barriers.
