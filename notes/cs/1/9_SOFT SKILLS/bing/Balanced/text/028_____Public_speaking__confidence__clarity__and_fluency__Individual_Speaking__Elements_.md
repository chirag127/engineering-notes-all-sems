### Public speaking: confidence, clarity, and fluency, Individual Speaking: Elements

Public speaking is the act of communicating a message to an audience, usually in a formal or professional setting. Public speaking requires confidence, clarity, and fluency to deliver an effective and engaging speech. 

Some of the elements of individual speaking are:

- **Purpose**: The speaker should have a clear and specific goal for their speech, such as informing, persuading, entertaining, or inspiring the audience. The purpose should guide the content, structure, and delivery of the speech.
- **Audience**: The speaker should know who their audience is, what their needs, interests, and expectations are, and how to adapt their speech accordingly. The speaker should also consider the size, setting, and mood of the audience, and use appropriate language, tone, and style.
- **Content**: The speaker should have a well-organized and coherent speech, with a clear introduction, body, and conclusion. The speaker should use relevant and credible evidence, examples, and stories to support their main points and arguments. The speaker should also use transitions, signposts, and summaries to help the audience follow the speech.
- **Structure**: The speaker should use a logical and effective structure for their speech, such as chronological, topical, problem-solution, cause-effect, or compare-contrast. The speaker should also use an outline to plan and organize their speech, and follow a clear and consistent pattern of organization.
- **Delivery**: The speaker should use vocal and nonverbal techniques to enhance their speech, such as volume, pitch, rate, pauses, eye contact, gestures, facial expressions, and posture. The speaker should also use visual aids, such as slides, charts, graphs, or images, to supplement their speech and make it more interesting and memorable. The speaker should also practice their speech and rehearse their delivery before the actual presentation.
