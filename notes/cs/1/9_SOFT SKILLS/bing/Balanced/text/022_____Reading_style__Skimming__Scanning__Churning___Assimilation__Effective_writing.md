### Reading style: Skimming; Scanning; Churning & Assimilation, Effective writing

- Reading is a skill that involves understanding and processing written information.
- Different reading styles can be used for different purposes and contexts.
- Some of the common reading styles are:

  - Skimming: This is a fast reading technique that involves looking for the main idea or gist of a text. Skimming can be useful for getting an overview of a text, finding out the topic, or identifying the main arguments or conclusions. Skimming can be done by reading the title, headings, subheadings, introduction, conclusion, and first sentences of paragraphs.
  - Scanning: This is another fast reading technique that involves looking for specific information or details in a text. Scanning can be useful for finding answers to questions, locating facts or figures, or identifying keywords or terms. Scanning can be done by using the table of contents, index, glossary, or visual aids, or by moving the eyes quickly over the text to find the relevant information.
  - Churning: This is a slow and careful reading technique that involves analyzing and evaluating a text. Churning can be useful for understanding the meaning, structure, and logic of a text, or for forming an opinion or judgment about it. Churning can be done by reading the text word by word, sentence by sentence, or paragraph by paragraph, and by taking notes, summarizing, or paraphrasing the main points.
  - Assimilation: This is a deep and active reading technique that involves integrating and applying the information from a text to one's own knowledge and experience. Assimilation can be useful for learning new concepts, skills, or perspectives, or for solving problems or making decisions. Assimilation can be done by relating the text to one's own background, interests, and goals, and by using strategies such as questioning, reflecting, comparing, contrasting, or synthesizing the information.

- Effective writing is a skill that involves communicating one's ideas clearly, concisely, and coherently to the intended audience and purpose.
- Some of the elements of effective writing are:

  - Content: This refers to the information, ideas, and arguments that are presented in the writing. Content should be relevant, accurate, and well-organized to support the main message or thesis of the writing.
  - Structure: This refers to the arrangement and sequence of the parts of the writing, such as the introduction, body, and conclusion. Structure should follow a logical and coherent order that guides the reader through the writing and connects the main points and subpoints.
  - Language: This refers to the choice and use of words, sentences, and paragraphs in the writing. Language should be appropriate, clear, and precise to convey the meaning and tone of the writing. Language should also follow the rules and conventions of grammar, spelling, punctuation, and formatting.
