### Speaking style ; content and sequencing

- Speaking style refers to how a speaker communicates their message, such as their tone, pace, volume, clarity, and language choice.
- Content refers to what a speaker says, such as their main points, supporting evidence, examples, and conclusions.
- Sequencing refers to how a speaker organizes their content, such as their introduction, body, and conclusion, and the transitions between them.
- A good speaking style should be appropriate for the purpose, audience, and context of the speech, and should convey the speaker's credibility, confidence, and personality.
- A good content should be relevant, accurate, clear, concise, and interesting for the audience, and should support the speaker's main goal and thesis statement.
- A good sequencing should follow a logical and coherent structure, such as chronological, topical, spatial, problem-solution, cause-effect, or compare-contrast, and should use signposts, summaries, and previews to guide the audience through the speech.
