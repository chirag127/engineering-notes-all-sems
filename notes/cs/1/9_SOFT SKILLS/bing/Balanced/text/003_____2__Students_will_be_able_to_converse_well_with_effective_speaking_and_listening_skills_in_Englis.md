### 2. Students will be able to converse well with effective speaking and listening skills in English.

- To converse well in English, students need to develop both their speaking and listening skills.
- Speaking skills involve the ability to produce clear, coherent, and appropriate speech in different contexts and for different purposes.
- Listening skills involve the ability to comprehend, interpret, and respond to spoken messages in various situations and with different speakers.
- Some of the sub-skills that are essential for effective speaking and listening in English are:

  - Pronunciation: the ability to produce the sounds, stress, intonation, and rhythm of English accurately and naturally.
  - Vocabulary: the ability to use a range of words and expressions that are suitable for the topic, audience, and purpose of the conversation.
  - Grammar: the ability to use the rules and structures of English correctly and appropriately to convey meaning and avoid ambiguity.
  - Fluency: the ability to speak smoothly and without hesitation, using strategies such as pausing, repeating, rephrasing, and self-correcting when needed.
  - Coherence: the ability to organize and connect ideas logically and clearly, using devices such as transitions, markers, and references.
  - Interaction: the ability to initiate, maintain, and end a conversation, using skills such as turn-taking, asking and answering questions, giving and receiving feedback, and using non-verbal cues.
  - Comprehension: the ability to understand the main and supporting ideas, the speaker's intention, and the implied meaning of a spoken message.
  - Interpretation: the ability to analyze, evaluate, and infer information from a spoken message, using skills such as critical thinking, problem-solving, and creativity.
  - Response: the ability to react and reply to a spoken message, using skills such as paraphrasing, summarizing, agreeing, disagreeing, and expressing opinions.

- To develop these skills, students need to practice speaking and listening in English regularly, using authentic and meaningful materials and tasks that reflect real-life situations and purposes.
- Some of the activities that can help students improve their speaking and listening skills in English are:

  - Role-plays: students act out scenarios that require them to use specific language functions, such as making requests, giving advice, or expressing opinions.
  - Discussions: students exchange ideas and opinions on a given topic, using skills such as agreeing, disagreeing, supporting, and challenging.
  - Presentations: students prepare and deliver a speech on a topic of their choice or interest, using skills such as planning, organizing, and delivering.
  - Interviews: students ask and answer questions on a particular subject, using skills such as probing, clarifying, and elaborating.
  - Debates: students argue for or against a motion or a proposition, using skills such as persuading, refuting, and concluding.
  - Podcasts: students listen to or create audio recordings on various topics, using skills such as listening for gist, listening for detail, and summarizing.
  - Stories: students listen to or tell stories, using skills such as narrating, describing, and sequencing.
  - Songs: students listen to or sing songs, using skills such as identifying words, rhymes, and meanings.
  - Games: students play games that involve speaking and listening, such as bingo, charades, or taboo.
