# Public speaking: confidence, clarity, and fluency, Individual Speaking: Elements

Public speaking is the act of communicating a message to an audience, usually in a formal or professional setting. Public speaking requires confidence, clarity, and fluency to deliver an effective and engaging speech. Confidence is the belief in one's own abilities and the willingness to face challenges. Clarity is the quality of being clear, coherent, and understandable. Fluency is the ability to speak smoothly, accurately, and expressively.

Some of the elements of individual speaking are:

- **Purpose**: The goal or objective of the speech, such as to inform, persuade, entertain, or inspire the audience.
- **Audience**: The group of people who will listen to the speech, their characteristics, needs, expectations, and interests.
- **Topic**: The subject or theme of the speech, which should be relevant, appropriate, and interesting for the purpose and the audience.
- **Content**: The information, facts, opinions, stories, examples, and arguments that support the main idea of the speech.
- **Structure**: The organization and arrangement of the content, such as introduction, body, and conclusion, and the use of transitions, signposts, and summaries to guide the audience.
- **Language**: The choice of words, phrases, sentences, and figures of speech that convey the meaning and tone of the speech.
- **Delivery**: The presentation and performance of the speech, such as voice, gestures, eye contact, facial expressions, posture, and movement.
