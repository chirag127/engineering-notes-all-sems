# Chronological etc, Official and Business Letter writing, Agenda, Notices

## Chronological etc

- Chronological etc is a term that refers to the various ways of organizing information in a text according to the order of time or events.
- Some common types of chronological etc are:
  - Chronological order: presenting information in the order that it happened or will happen, such as in a timeline, a biography, a history, or a procedure.
  - Reverse chronological order: presenting information in the reverse order of time, such as in a resume, a list of references, or a bibliography.
  - Flashback: interrupting the present time of a narrative to show an earlier event, such as in a memoir, a novel, or a movie.
  - Flashforward: interrupting the present time of a narrative to show a future event, such as in a prophecy, a prediction, or a vision.
  - Parallel: presenting two or more events or stories that happen at the same time or in the same period, such as in a comparison, a contrast, or a juxtaposition.

## Official and Business Letter writing

- Official and business letter writing is a form of communication that follows certain conventions and formats to convey a formal and professional message to a specific audience or purpose.
- Some common types of official and business letter writing are:
  - Inquiry letter: requesting information or clarification from a person or an organization, such as a customer, a supplier, or a government agency.
  - Complaint letter: expressing dissatisfaction or dissatisfaction with a product, a service, or a situation, and asking for a resolution, a refund, or an apology.
  - Adjustment letter: responding to a complaint letter and offering a solution, a compensation, or an explanation.
  - Order letter: placing an order for a product or a service from a seller or a provider, and specifying the details, such as the quantity, the price, the delivery, and the payment.
  - Acknowledgment letter: confirming the receipt of an order, a payment, a document, or a message, and thanking the sender or the customer.
  - Cover letter: accompanying a resume or a curriculum vitae and introducing oneself to a potential employer or a recruiter, and highlighting one's qualifications, skills, and achievements.
  - Recommendation letter: endorsing or supporting a person or a candidate for a job, a scholarship, a grant, or an award, and providing evidence of their suitability, performance, and character.
  - Resignation letter: notifying an employer or a supervisor of one's decision to leave a job or a position, and expressing gratitude, appreciation, and professionalism.

## Agenda

- An agenda is a list of items or topics that are planned to be discussed or addressed in a meeting, a conference, a workshop, or an event.
- An agenda usually includes the following elements:
  - The title, date, time, and location of the meeting or the event.
  - The name and role of the chairperson, the facilitator, or the organizer.
  - The objectives or the goals of the meeting or the event.
  - The order and the duration of the items or the topics.
  - The name and the affiliation of the speakers, the presenters, or the participants.
  - The expected outcomes or the actions to be taken after the meeting or the event.

## Notices

- A notice is a written announcement or a notification that informs or alerts a specific audience or the public about a certain matter, such as an event, a policy, a rule, or a change.
- A notice usually includes the following elements:
  - The heading, which states the purpose or the subject of the notice, such as "Notice of Meeting", "Notice of Cancellation", or "Notice of Vacancy".
  - The date and the place of issuing the notice, such as "March 15, 2023, New York City".
  - The name and the signature of the authority or the person issuing the notice, such as "John Smith, Director of Marketing".
  - The body, which provides the details or the information of the matter, such as the date, time, location, reason, or condition of the event, the policy, the rule, or the change.
  - The contact information, such as the phone number, the email address, or the website, for further inquiries or clarifications.
