# Speaking style ; content and sequencing

- Speaking style refers to how a speaker communicates their message, such as their tone, pace, volume, clarity, and expression.
- Content refers to what a speaker says, such as their main points, supporting details, examples, and evidence.
- Sequencing refers to how a speaker organizes their content, such as their introduction, body, conclusion, transitions, and signposts.

Some tips for improving speaking style, content, and sequencing are:

- Speaking style
  - Adapt your style to your audience, purpose, and context. For example, use formal language for academic or professional settings, and informal language for casual or personal settings.
  - Vary your tone, pace, and volume to emphasize key points, create interest, and avoid monotony. For example, speak louder and faster when you want to show excitement or urgency, and softer and slower when you want to show calmness or seriousness.
  - Speak clearly and articulately, avoiding mumbling, slurring, or filler words. For example, pronounce each word distinctly, pause between sentences, and use words like "um", "uh", or "like" sparingly.
  - Use facial expressions, gestures, and eye contact to convey your emotions, attitudes, and engagement. For example, smile when you are happy or friendly, nod when you agree or understand, and look at your audience when you speak or listen.

- Content
  - Choose a topic that is relevant, interesting, and appropriate for your audience, purpose, and context. For example, select a topic that your audience can relate to, learn from, or enjoy, and that matches your goals and expectations.
  - Develop a clear and specific thesis statement that summarizes your main idea and argument. For example, state your thesis at the beginning of your speech, and make sure it is concise, focused, and debatable.
  - Support your thesis with credible and sufficient evidence, such as facts, statistics, examples, quotations, or anecdotes. For example, cite your sources, use reliable and current information, and provide enough details and explanations.
  - Use rhetorical devices, such as repetition, contrast, analogy, or metaphor, to enhance your content and persuade your audience. For example, repeat your key points, compare and contrast different perspectives, use analogies to explain complex concepts, or use metaphors to create vivid images.

- Sequencing
  - Follow a logical and coherent structure that guides your audience through your content. For example, use a common pattern, such as chronological, spatial, topical, problem-solution, or cause-effect, to organize your main points.
  - Include an introduction, a body, and a conclusion in your speech. For example, in your introduction, capture your audience's attention, establish your credibility, and preview your main points. In your body, discuss your main points in detail, using evidence and rhetorical devices. In your conclusion, summarize your main points, restate your thesis, and end with a memorable statement or call to action.
