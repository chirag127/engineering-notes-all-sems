# Organization, Delivery

Organization and delivery are two essential aspects of effective communication. Organization refers to the structure and order of the main points and supporting details in a speech or a written text. Delivery refers to the way the speaker or the writer presents the message to the audience, using verbal and nonverbal cues.

Some of the main points to consider for organization and delivery are:

- **Purpose**: The speaker or the writer should have a clear and specific purpose for communicating, and should state it in a thesis statement or a central idea at the beginning of the speech or the text.
- **Audience**: The speaker or the writer should analyze the audience's needs, interests, expectations, and background, and tailor the message accordingly.
- **Topic**: The speaker or the writer should choose a topic that is relevant, interesting, and manageable for the purpose and the audience.
- **Research**: The speaker or the writer should gather and evaluate credible and reliable sources of information to support the main points and the thesis statement or the central idea.
- **Outline**: The speaker or the writer should organize the main points and the supporting details in a logical and coherent manner, using an outline that follows a clear pattern of organization, such as chronological, spatial, topical, causal, problem-solution, etc.
- **Introduction**: The speaker or the writer should capture the audience's attention, establish credibility and rapport, and preview the main points and the thesis statement or the central idea in the introduction.
- **Body**: The speaker or the writer should develop the main points and the supporting details in the body of the speech or the text, using transitions, signposts, and internal summaries to guide the audience through the message.
- **Conclusion**: The speaker or the writer should summarize the main points and the thesis statement or the central idea, reinforce the purpose and the significance of the message, and provide a memorable closing statement in the conclusion.
- **Language**: The speaker or the writer should use clear, concise, and appropriate language that suits the purpose, the audience, and the topic, and that avoids jargon, slang, clichés, and grammatical errors.
- **Style**: The speaker or the writer should use a consistent and suitable tone, voice, and mood that convey the attitude and the emotion of the message, and that enhance the credibility and the rapport with the audience.
- **Visual aids**: The speaker or the writer should use relevant, effective, and ethical visual aids, such as graphs, charts, images, videos, etc., to supplement and illustrate the main points and the supporting details, and to increase the audience's understanding and retention of the message.
- **Delivery**: The speaker or the writer should practice and rehearse the speech or the text, and deliver it with confidence, enthusiasm, and professionalism, using appropriate verbal and nonverbal cues, such as eye contact, gestures, facial expressions, posture, voice, volume, rate, pitch, pauses, etc.
