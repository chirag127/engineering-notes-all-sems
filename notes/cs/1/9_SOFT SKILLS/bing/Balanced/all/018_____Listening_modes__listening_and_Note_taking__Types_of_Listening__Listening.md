# Listening modes, listening and note taking, types of listening, listening

## Listening modes

- Listening modes are the different ways that listeners can approach a listening task, depending on their purpose and goals.
- There are four main listening modes: attentive, responsive, selective, and active.

### Attentive listening

- Attentive listening is when the listener pays close attention to the speaker and tries to understand the message without interrupting or judging.
- Attentive listening requires concentration, patience, and respect for the speaker.
- Attentive listening is useful for learning new information, building rapport, and showing empathy.

### Responsive listening

- Responsive listening is when the listener gives feedback to the speaker, such as nodding, smiling, asking questions, or paraphrasing.
- Responsive listening shows that the listener is interested, engaged, and following the conversation.
- Responsive listening is useful for clarifying misunderstandings, checking comprehension, and expressing agreement or disagreement.

### Selective listening

- Selective listening is when the listener focuses on specific parts of the message that are relevant or important to them, and ignores or filters out the rest.
- Selective listening can be intentional or unintentional, depending on the listener's motivation and attention span.
- Selective listening is useful for finding specific information, solving problems, or making decisions.

### Active listening

- Active listening is when the listener goes beyond the surface meaning of the message and tries to infer the speaker's intentions, feelings, and perspectives.
- Active listening involves listening with empathy, curiosity, and openness, and asking probing or reflective questions to deepen the understanding.
- Active listening is useful for developing critical thinking, resolving conflicts, and building trust.

## Listening and note taking

- Listening and note taking are essential skills for academic success, as they help students to capture, organize, and review the main ideas and details from lectures, presentations, or discussions.
- Listening and note taking require active involvement, strategic planning, and effective techniques.

### Before listening

- Before listening, students should prepare themselves by reviewing the topic, setting a purpose, and predicting what they might hear.
- Students should also choose a note-taking method that suits their learning style, such as outlining, mapping, charting, or summarizing.

### While listening

- While listening, students should focus on the speaker's cues, such as tone, pace, gestures, and transitions, to identify the main points and supporting details.
- Students should also use symbols, abbreviations, and keywords to write down the essential information, and leave gaps for missing or unclear parts.

### After listening

- After listening, students should review their notes as soon as possible, and fill in the gaps, correct the errors, and add more details or examples.
- Students should also organize their notes into a coherent structure, and highlight or underline the key points.
- Students should also review their notes regularly, and use them to study, revise, or write assignments.

## Types of listening

- There are different types of listening, depending on the purpose, context, and relationship of the communication.
- Some common types of listening are: informative, appreciative, critical, empathic, and relational.

### Informative listening

- Informative listening is when the listener's main goal is to learn or acquire new information or knowledge from the speaker.
- Informative listening requires attentive and responsive listening skills, as well as note-taking and summarizing skills.
- Informative listening is common in educational or professional settings, such as lectures, seminars, workshops, or meetings.

### Appreciative listening

- Appreciative listening is when the listener's main goal is to enjoy or appreciate the speaker's message, such as their style, creativity, or humor.
- Appreciative listening requires an open and positive attitude, as well as an awareness of the speaker's purpose and audience.
- Appreciative listening is common in recreational or entertainment settings, such as concerts, movies, podcasts, or speeches.

### Critical listening

- Critical listening is when the listener's main goal is to evaluate or analyze the speaker's message, such as their logic, evidence, or arguments.
- Critical listening requires active and selective listening skills, as well as critical thinking and questioning skills.
- Critical listening is common in academic or professional settings, such as debates, reviews, or reports.

### Empathic listening

- Empathic listening is when the listener's main goal is to understand or share the speaker's feelings, emotions, or experiences.
- Empathic listening requires active and attentive listening skills, as well as empathy, compassion, and respect.
- Empathic listening is common in personal or social settings, such as counseling, coaching, or friendship.
