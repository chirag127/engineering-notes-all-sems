# 5. Students will be able build up personal traits that will make the transition from institution

- The transition from institution to the workplace or further education can be challenging for many students, especially those who lack the necessary personal traits to adapt and succeed in different environments.
- Some of the personal traits that can help students make a smooth and successful transition are:
  - Self-awareness: the ability to recognize one's strengths, weaknesses, interests, values, goals, and motivations, and to use this information to guide one's decisions and actions.
  - Self-regulation: the ability to manage one's emotions, impulses, stress, and time, and to set and follow through with realistic and attainable goals.
  - Communication: the ability to express oneself clearly and effectively, both verbally and non-verbally, and to listen and respond appropriately to others, especially in diverse and multicultural settings.
  - Collaboration: the ability to work cooperatively and respectfully with others, both within and across teams, and to contribute to the achievement of shared goals and outcomes.
  - Critical thinking: the ability to analyze, evaluate, and synthesize information from various sources and perspectives, and to apply logic and creativity to solve problems and make decisions.
  - Lifelong learning: the ability to seek out and acquire new knowledge and skills, both formally and informally, and to adapt to changing demands and opportunities in the workplace and society.
- Students can build up these personal traits by engaging in various activities and experiences during their institution, such as:
  - Participating in extracurricular activities, clubs, societies, or volunteer work that align with their interests and values, and that expose them to different people and cultures.
  - Taking advantage of academic and career counseling, mentoring, and coaching services that can help them identify and pursue their goals and aspirations, and provide them with feedback and guidance.
  - Seeking out and applying for internships, apprenticeships, or other work-based learning opportunities that can help them gain practical skills and experience in their chosen field or industry, and that can connect them with potential employers and mentors.
  - Enrolling in courses or programs that challenge them to think critically and creatively, and that foster their curiosity and passion for learning.
  - Reflecting on their learning and development, and documenting their achievements and progress in a portfolio or resume that showcases their personal traits and competencies.
