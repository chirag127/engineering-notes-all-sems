### 1. Students will be enabled to understand the correct usage of grammar.

- Grammar is the set of rules and conventions that govern how words and sentences are structured and used in a language.
- Grammar helps to communicate clearly and effectively, avoid misunderstandings and errors, and express one's thoughts and ideas with precision and coherence.
- Grammar can be divided into different levels or categories, such as phonology (the sound system of a language), morphology (the structure and formation of words), syntax (the arrangement and order of words and phrases), semantics (the meaning and interpretation of words and sentences), and pragmatics (the use of language in context and for specific purposes).
- Grammar can also be classified into descriptive grammar (the description and analysis of how language is actually used by speakers and writers) and prescriptive grammar (the prescription and enforcement of rules and norms for how language should be used correctly and appropriately).
- To understand the correct usage of grammar, students need to learn and practice the following skills and strategies:

  - Identify and recognize the different parts of speech (such as nouns, verbs, adjectives, adverbs, pronouns, prepositions, conjunctions, and interjections) and their functions and roles in sentences.
  - Analyze and construct different types of sentences (such as simple, compound, complex, and compound-complex) and clauses (such as independent, dependent, relative, and noun) and their relationships and connections.
  - Apply and follow the rules and principles of agreement (such as subject-verb agreement, pronoun-antecedent agreement, and noun-noun agreement) and consistency (such as tense, voice, mood, and number) in sentences.
  - Use and punctuate correctly the different types of punctuation marks (such as periods, commas, semicolons, colons, apostrophes, quotation marks, hyphens, dashes, parentheses, and brackets) and their functions and effects in sentences.
  - Choose and use appropriately the different types of modifiers (such as adjectives, adverbs, phrases, and clauses) and their positions and placements in sentences.
  - Avoid and correct common grammatical errors and mistakes (such as fragments, run-ons, comma splices, misplaced and dangling modifiers, faulty parallelism, and unclear pronoun references) in sentences.
  - Compare and contrast the different registers and styles of language (such as formal, informal, academic, and colloquial) and their grammatical features and variations.
  - Adapt and adjust one's grammar to suit different purposes, audiences, contexts, and situations of communication.
