### Delhi 2013

- Delhi 2013 refers to the Delhi Legislative Assembly election that was held on 4 December 2013, with the result announced on 8 December resulting in formation of the Fifth Legislative Assembly of Delhi.
- The election was contested by various political parties, such as the Bharatiya Janata Party (BJP), the Indian National Congress (INC), the Aam Aadmi Party (AAP), the Bahujan Samaj Party (BSP), and others.
- The BJP won a plurality of 31 seats out of 70, closely followed by AAP, in its first election, with 28 seats. The INC, which was the ruling party since 1998, was reduced to 8 seats. The BSP and others won 3 seats each.
- The election resulted in a hung assembly, as no party could secure a majority of 36 seats. The BJP, being the single largest party, was invited by the Lieutenant Governor of Delhi to form the government, but declined due to lack of support from other parties. The AAP, which had campaigned on an anti-corruption platform, initially refused to form the government with the support of the INC, but later changed its stance after conducting a referendum among its supporters.
- The AAP leader Arvind Kejriwal was sworn in as the Chief Minister of Delhi on 28 December 2013, along with six ministers from his party. The AAP government lasted for 49 days, as Kejriwal resigned on 14 February 2014, after failing to pass the Jan Lokpal Bill, an anti-corruption legislation, in the assembly. The assembly was then dissolved and fresh elections were held in 2015.
- The Delhi 2013 election was significant for several reasons, such as:
  - It marked the emergence of AAP as a new political force in Delhi and India, challenging the dominance of the BJP and the INC.
  - It showed the dissatisfaction of the voters with the incumbent government, which was accused of corruption, price rise, and poor governance.
  - It demonstrated the impact of social media and civil society movements on electoral politics, as AAP used these platforms to mobilize support and funds.
  - It highlighted the issues of women's safety, water and electricity supply, and public transport, among others, as the main concerns of the voters.
  - It reflected the diversity and complexity of the electorate, as different sections of the society, such as the urban poor, the middle class, the minorities, and the migrants, voted for different parties and candidates.
