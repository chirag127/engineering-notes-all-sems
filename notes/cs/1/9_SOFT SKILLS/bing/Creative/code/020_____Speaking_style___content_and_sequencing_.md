### Speaking style ; content and sequencing.

Speaking style is the way a speaker uses language to communicate their ideas and emotions to the audience. It includes aspects such as tone, pitch, volume, speed, accent, pronunciation, vocabulary, grammar, and gestures. Speaking style can affect the speaker's credibility, persuasiveness, and rapport with the listeners.

Content and sequencing are two important elements of preparing and delivering a speech. Content refers to the information, arguments, examples, and evidence that the speaker uses to support their main points and purpose. Sequencing refers to the order and structure of the speech, such as the introduction, body, and conclusion, and the transitions between them.

Some tips for effective speaking style, content, and sequencing are:

- Adapt your speaking style to the occasion, purpose, and audience of your speech. For example, use formal language for a professional presentation, and informal language for a casual conversation.
- Vary your tone, pitch, volume, and speed to emphasize key points, create interest, and express emotions. For example, use a higher pitch and volume to show excitement, and a lower pitch and volume to show seriousness.
- Avoid monotone, mumbling, or speaking too fast or too slow, as these can make you sound bored, nervous, or unclear.
- Use clear and correct pronunciation, and avoid filler words, such as "um", "like", or "you know". If you have an accent, try to speak slowly and clearly, and repeat or rephrase important words if necessary.
- Use appropriate and relevant vocabulary and grammar, and avoid jargon, slang, or offensive language. If you use technical terms, explain them or provide examples for the audience.
- Use gestures, facial expressions, and eye contact to complement your verbal message, and show confidence, enthusiasm, and engagement with the audience. Avoid distracting or excessive gestures, such as fidgeting, pointing, or crossing your arms.
- Choose content that is accurate, credible, and well-researched, and that supports your main points and purpose. Use a variety of sources, such as books, articles, statistics, surveys, interviews, or personal experiences, and cite them properly.
- Organize your content into a clear and logical sequence, with an introduction, a body, and a conclusion. The introduction should capture the audience's attention, state the topic and purpose, and preview the main points. The body should develop the main points with supporting details, and use transitions to connect them. The conclusion should summarize the main points, restate the purpose, and provide a memorable closing remark.
- Use signposts, such as words or phrases that indicate the structure and direction of your speech, such as "first", "next", "in conclusion", or "for example". Signposts can help the audience follow your speech and remember your main points.
