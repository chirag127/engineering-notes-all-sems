### Unit-5: Workplace skills

Workplace skills are the talents, traits and qualifications that can help you perform well at work. They are useful skills for both securing a job and growing your career. Workplace skills can be grouped into two categories: soft skills and hard skills .

- Soft skills are the interpersonal and behavioral abilities that help you work effectively with others, manage your time, cope with stress, and adapt to change. Some examples of soft skills are communication, problem-solving, teamwork, leadership, empathy, and creativity .
- Hard skills are the technical and practical knowledge and abilities that are specific to your job role, industry, or function. Some examples of hard skills are computer programming, accounting, data analysis, foreign language, and project management.

Both soft and hard skills are important for success in the workplace, but different jobs may require different levels and combinations of skills. Therefore, it is essential to identify the skills that are relevant to your desired position and highlight them in your resume, cover letter, and interview.

Some tips to improve your workplace skills are:

- Seek feedback from your supervisor, colleagues, or clients on your strengths and areas for improvement.
- Set SMART (specific, measurable, achievable, relevant, and time-bound) goals for developing your skills and track your progress.
- Enroll in online courses, workshops, webinars, or podcasts that can teach you new skills or update your existing ones.
- Join professional associations, networks, or communities that can offer you mentoring, coaching, or peer support.
- Practice your skills in real or simulated scenarios, such as role-playing, case studies, or mock interviews.
- Seek opportunities to apply your skills in different contexts, such as volunteering, freelancing, or taking on new projects or tasks.
