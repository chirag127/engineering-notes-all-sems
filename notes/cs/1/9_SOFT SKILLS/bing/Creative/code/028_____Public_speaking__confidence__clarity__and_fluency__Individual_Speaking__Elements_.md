# Public speaking: confidence, clarity, and fluency

Public speaking is the process of communicating a message to an audience, either in person or through a mediated channel. Public speaking can have various purposes, such as informing, persuading, entertaining, or inspiring the listeners. Public speaking can also be a skill that can enhance one's personal and professional development.

To be an effective public speaker, one needs to have confidence, clarity, and fluency in delivering the speech. These are some of the factors that can influence the quality and impact of the speech, as well as the speaker's credibility and rapport with the audience.

## Confidence

Confidence is the feeling of self-assurance and trust in one's abilities and knowledge. Confidence can help the speaker to overcome nervousness, anxiety, or fear of public speaking, and to express the message with conviction and enthusiasm. Confidence can also make the speaker appear more confident, competent, and trustworthy to the audience.

Some of the ways to build confidence in public speaking are:

- Prepare the speech well in advance and practice it several times, preferably in front of a mirror, a recorder, or a friend.
- Know the purpose, the audience, and the context of the speech, and tailor the message accordingly.
- Use positive self-talk and affirmations to boost one's self-esteem and motivation.
- Dress appropriately and comfortably for the occasion, and maintain a good posture and eye contact with the audience.
- Use humor, anecdotes, or personal stories to engage the audience and to show one's personality and authenticity.
- Seek feedback and constructive criticism from others, and learn from one's mistakes and successes.

## Clarity

Clarity is the quality of being clear, coherent, and understandable in one's speech. Clarity can help the speaker to convey the message effectively and efficiently, and to avoid confusion, ambiguity, or misinterpretation by the audience. Clarity can also make the speaker appear more organized, logical, and professional to the audience.

Some of the ways to achieve clarity in public speaking are:

- Use simple, precise, and familiar words and phrases, and avoid jargon, slang, or technical terms that the audience may not understand.
- Use short, simple, and declarative sentences, and avoid long, complex, or run-on sentences that may lose the audience's attention or comprehension.
- Use transitions, signposts, and summaries to connect the main points and to guide the audience through the speech.
- Use examples, illustrations, statistics, or quotations to support and explain the main points and to make them more concrete and relatable to the audience.
- Use vocal variety, such as changes in pitch, volume, rate, and tone, to emphasize the key words and phrases and to avoid monotony and boredom.
- Use visual aids, such as slides, charts, graphs, or images, to complement and enhance the verbal message and to appeal to the audience's senses.

## Fluency

Fluency is the quality of being smooth, natural, and effortless in one's speech. Fluency can help the speaker to deliver the message with ease and grace, and to avoid interruptions, hesitations, or errors that may distract or annoy the audience. Fluency can also make the speaker appear more fluent, confident, and competent to the audience.

Some of the ways to improve fluency in public speaking are:

- Practice the speech repeatedly and aloud, and try to memorize the main points and the outline, but not the exact words or sentences.
- Use notes, cue cards, or an outline to remind oneself of the main points and the structure of the speech, but do not read from them word for word.
- Use pauses, silences, or fillers, such as "um", "uh", or "you know", sparingly and strategically, and only when necessary to gather one's thoughts or to create suspense or emphasis.
- Use gestures, facial expressions, or body movements to accompany and reinforce the verbal message and to express one's emotions and attitudes.
- Use eye contact, smiles, or nods to establish and maintain rapport and interaction with the audience, and to show one's interest and attention.

# Individual speaking: elements

Individual speaking is a type of public speaking that involves one speaker delivering a speech to an audience, without the assistance or intervention of other speakers. Individual speaking can have various forms, such as informative, persuasive, impromptu, or extemporaneous speeches, depending on the purpose, the occasion, and the time limit of the speech.
