### Introduction to oral communication, Nuances and Modes of Speech Delivery

Oral communication is the process of expressing ideas, feelings, opinions, or information through spoken words. Oral communication can be formal or informal, depending on the context, purpose, and audience of the message. Oral communication can also be classified into different types, such as interpersonal, group, public, or mass communication.

Some of the benefits of oral communication are:

- It is fast and immediate, allowing for instant feedback and clarification.
- It is personal and expressive, conveying emotions, tone, and body language.
- It is flexible and adaptable, allowing for adjustments and improvisations according to the situation and the listener's response.
- It is persuasive and influential, appealing to the listener's logic, emotions, and values.

Some of the challenges of oral communication are:

- It is ephemeral and transient, leaving no permanent record or evidence of the message.
- It is prone to errors and misunderstandings, due to noise, distractions, or misinterpretations.
- It is limited by time and space, restricting the amount and depth of information that can be conveyed.
- It is affected by personal and cultural biases, stereotypes, and prejudices that may influence the speaker's or the listener's perception and interpretation of the message.

To overcome these challenges and improve oral communication skills, one needs to pay attention to the following aspects:

- The content of the message, which should be clear, concise, accurate, relevant, and organized.
- The delivery of the message, which should be confident, fluent, coherent, appropriate, and engaging.
- The feedback of the message, which should be attentive, respectful, constructive, and responsive.

Nuances and modes of speech delivery are two important factors that affect the delivery of the message. Nuances are the subtle variations in meaning, expression, or tone that convey the speaker's attitude, intention, or emotion. Modes of speech delivery are the different ways of presenting the message, depending on the level of preparation, spontaneity, and interaction involved.

Some of the common nuances of speech delivery are:

- Volume, which is the loudness or softness of the voice, indicating the level of confidence, enthusiasm, or emphasis.
- Pitch, which is the highness or lowness of the voice, indicating the level of excitement, interest, or emotion.
- Rate, which is the speed or slowness of the speech, indicating the level of urgency, nervousness, or clarity.
- Pause, which is the silence or hesitation between words or sentences, indicating the level of thoughtfulness, uncertainty, or emphasis.
- Stress, which is the force or emphasis on certain words or syllables, indicating the level of importance, contrast, or surprise.
- Intonation, which is the rise and fall of the voice, indicating the level of certainty, question, or expression.
- Articulation, which is the clarity or accuracy of the pronunciation, indicating the level of professionalism, education, or culture.
- Modulation, which is the variation or change of the voice, indicating the level of interest, engagement, or emotion.

Some of the common modes of speech delivery are:

- Impromptu, which is the delivery of the speech without any prior preparation or notes, relying on the speaker's knowledge, experience, and creativity.
- Extemporaneous, which is the delivery of the speech with some prior preparation and notes, but without memorizing the exact words, relying on the speaker's understanding, flexibility, and spontaneity.
- Manuscript, which is the delivery of the speech by reading the exact words from a written text, relying on the speaker's accuracy, precision, and formality.
- Memorized, which is the delivery of the speech by reciting the exact words from memory, relying on the speaker's retention, expression, and confidence.
