### Transformation of Sentences: Simple, Compound and Complex

- A sentence is a group of words that expresses a complete thought. A sentence can be classified into different types based on its structure and meaning.
- A simple sentence is a sentence that has only one independent clause. An independent clause is a group of words that can stand alone as a sentence. For example, "She sings well."
- A compound sentence is a sentence that has two or more independent clauses joined by a coordinating conjunction (for, and, nor, but, or, yet, so) or a semicolon (;). For example, "She sings well and plays the guitar."
- A complex sentence is a sentence that has one independent clause and one or more dependent clauses. A dependent clause is a group of words that cannot stand alone as a sentence and depends on the independent clause for its meaning. A dependent clause is usually introduced by a subordinating conjunction (such as because, although, when, etc.) or a relative pronoun (such as who, which, that, etc.). For example, "She sings well because she practices every day."
- A sentence can be transformed from one type to another by adding or removing words, changing the word order, or changing the punctuation. For example, the simple sentence "She sings well." can be transformed into a compound sentence by adding another independent clause: "She sings well and plays the guitar." It can also be transformed into a complex sentence by adding a dependent clause: "She sings well because she practices every day."

### Subject-verb agreement

- Subject-verb agreement is the rule that the verb in a sentence must agree with the subject in number (singular or plural) and person (first, second, or third). For example, "He sings well." (singular subject, singular verb) "They sing well." (plural subject, plural verb)
- Some common rules for subject-verb agreement are:
  - The verb agrees with the subject, not the complement. For example, "The singer is a woman." (singular subject, singular verb) "The singers are women." (plural subject, plural verb)
  - The verb agrees with the subject, not the modifier. For example, "The singer with the guitar sings well." (singular subject, singular verb) "The singers with the guitars sing well." (plural subject, plural verb)
  - The verb agrees with the subject closest to it when the subject is compound or collective. For example, "The singer and the guitarist sing well." (plural subject, plural verb) "The band sings well." (singular subject, singular verb)
  - The verb agrees with the subject in inverted sentences. For example, "There is a singer on the stage." (singular subject, singular verb) "There are singers on the stage." (plural subject, plural verb)
  - The verb agrees with the subject in questions. For example, "Does he sing well?" (singular subject, singular verb) "Do they sing well?" (plural subject, plural verb)

### Prefix and Suffix

- A prefix is a word part that is added to the beginning of a word to change its meaning. For example, the prefix un- means not, so adding un- to the word happy makes unhappy, which means not happy.
- A suffix is a word part that is added to the end of a word to change its meaning. For example, the suffix -ful means full of, so adding -ful to the word joy makes joyful, which means full of joy.
- Some common prefixes and suffixes are:
  - Prefixes: un-, re-, dis-, in-, im-, il-, ir-, non-, pre-, post-, anti-, bi-, tri-, quad-, multi-, etc.
  - Suffixes: -ful, -less, -able, -ible, -er, -or, -ist, -ism, -ness, -ment, -tion, -sion, -ly, -y, -ed, -ing, -en, -s, -es, etc.

### Antonyms, Synonyms, Homophones

- Antonyms are words that have opposite meanings. For example, hot and cold, big and small, happy and sad, etc.
- Synonyms are words that have similar meanings. For example, happy and glad, big and large, fast and quick, etc.
- Homophones are words that sound the same but have different meanings and spellings. For example, see and sea, hear and here, write and right, etc.
