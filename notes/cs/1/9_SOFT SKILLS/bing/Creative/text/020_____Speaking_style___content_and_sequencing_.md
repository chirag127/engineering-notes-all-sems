### Speaking style ; content and sequencing.

- Speaking style refers to how a speaker communicates their message, such as their tone, pace, volume, gestures, eye contact, and word choice.
- Content refers to what a speaker says, such as their main points, supporting details, examples, and evidence.
- Sequencing refers to how a speaker organizes their content, such as their introduction, body, conclusion, transitions, and signposts.

- A good speaking style should be clear, confident, engaging, and appropriate for the audience and the purpose of the speech.
- A good content should be relevant, accurate, credible, and well-researched.
- A good sequencing should be logical, coherent, and easy to follow.

- Some tips for improving speaking style are:
  - Practice your speech aloud and record yourself to identify areas of improvement.
  - Use vocal variety to avoid monotony and emphasize key points.
  - Use pauses, pitch, and intonation to convey meaning and emotion.
  - Use body language and facial expressions to enhance your message and connect with your audience.
  - Use appropriate language and avoid jargon, slang, and filler words.
  - Adapt your style to the context, occasion, and audience expectations.

- Some tips for improving content are:
  - Choose a topic that interests you and your audience.
  - Narrow down your topic and formulate a clear and specific thesis statement.
  - Research your topic from reliable and diverse sources and cite them properly.
  - Develop your main points and support them with relevant and sufficient details, examples, and evidence.
  - Use rhetorical devices such as analogies, anecdotes, statistics, and quotations to persuade or inform your audience.

- Some tips for improving sequencing are:
  - Use a standard speech structure such as the problem-solution, cause-effect, or chronological order.
  - Write an effective introduction that captures the audience's attention, establishes your credibility, and previews your main points.
  - Write a clear and concise conclusion that summarizes your main points, restates your thesis, and provides a call to action or a memorable closing remark.
  - Use transitions and signposts to link your ideas and guide your audience through your speech.
  - Use an outline to organize your speech and ensure a logical flow of information.
