### Reading style: Skimming; Scanning; Churning & Assimilation, Effective writing

- Reading is a skill that involves processing and understanding written texts.
- Different reading styles can be used for different purposes and contexts.
- Some of the common reading styles are:

  - Skimming: This is a fast reading technique that involves looking for the main idea, topic sentences, headings, and keywords in a text. Skimming is useful for getting a general overview of a text, finding specific information, or deciding whether a text is relevant or not.
  - Scanning: This is a reading technique that involves searching for specific details, facts, figures, names, dates, or terms in a text. Scanning is useful for finding answers to questions, filling in gaps, or verifying information.
  - Churning: This is a reading technique that involves reading a text word by word, sentence by sentence, and paragraph by paragraph. Churning is useful for understanding the meaning, structure, and logic of a text, analyzing the author's arguments, or evaluating the quality of a text.
  - Assimilation: This is a reading technique that involves integrating the information from a text with one's prior knowledge, experience, and perspective. Assimilation is useful for forming opinions, making connections, or applying the information to new situations.

- Effective writing is a skill that involves producing clear, coherent, and accurate texts that communicate the intended message to the audience.
- Some of the elements of effective writing are:

  - Purpose: This is the reason or goal for writing a text. The purpose can be to inform, persuade, entertain, instruct, or express oneself. The purpose should guide the choice of topic, tone, style, and format of the text.
  - Audience: This is the group of people who will read or receive the text. The audience can be general or specific, formal or informal, familiar or unfamiliar. The audience should influence the level of language, vocabulary, and examples used in the text.
  - Organization: This is the way the text is structured and arranged. The organization should follow a logical and coherent order that supports the purpose and message of the text. The organization can vary depending on the type and genre of the text, but it usually consists of an introduction, a body, and a conclusion.
  - Content: This is the information, ideas, arguments, or opinions that are presented in the text. The content should be relevant, accurate, and sufficient to achieve the purpose and address the audience of the text. The content should be supported by evidence, examples, or details that are appropriate and credible.
  - Style: This is the way the text is written and expressed. The style should reflect the personality, voice, and tone of the writer. The style should also suit the purpose, audience, and genre of the text. The style can be influenced by the choice of words, sentences, paragraphs, and punctuation.
  - Mechanics: This is the correctness and accuracy of the text in terms of grammar, spelling, and punctuation. The mechanics should follow the rules and conventions of the language and the genre of the text. The mechanics should also ensure the clarity, readability, and professionalism of the text.
