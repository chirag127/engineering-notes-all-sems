### 2. Students will be able to converse well with effective speaking and listening skills in English.

- To converse well in English, students need to develop both their speaking and listening skills.
- Speaking skills involve the ability to produce clear, coherent, and appropriate speech for different purposes, audiences, and contexts.
- Listening skills involve the ability to comprehend, interpret, and respond to spoken messages from various sources and situations.
- Some of the sub-skills that are essential for effective speaking and listening are:

  - Pronunciation: the ability to produce the sounds, stress, intonation, and rhythm of English accurately and naturally.
  - Vocabulary: the ability to use a range of words and expressions that are suitable for the topic, register, and genre of the conversation.
  - Grammar: the ability to use the rules and structures of English correctly and meaningfully.
  - Fluency: the ability to speak smoothly and confidently, without hesitation, repetition, or pauses.
  - Coherence: the ability to organize and connect ideas logically and clearly, using cohesive devices and transitions.
  - Interaction: the ability to initiate, maintain, and end a conversation, using appropriate turn-taking, feedback, and politeness strategies.
  - Comprehension: the ability to understand the main ideas, details, and implications of what is said, using contextual clues and inferencing skills.
  - Interpretation: the ability to analyze, evaluate, and synthesize the information and opinions expressed, using critical thinking and reasoning skills.
  - Response: the ability to give relevant, accurate, and complete answers, comments, or opinions, using appropriate language and tone.

- To improve their speaking and listening skills, students need to practice them regularly and systematically, using authentic and engaging materials and activities.
- Some of the strategies that can help students enhance their speaking and listening skills are:

  - Exposure: listening to and speaking with native or proficient speakers of English, through various media and platforms, such as podcasts, videos, online chats, etc.
  - Imitation: copying and repeating the speech patterns, vocabulary, and expressions of good speakers, using models, recordings, or scripts.
  - Feedback: seeking and receiving constructive comments and suggestions on their speaking and listening performance, from teachers, peers, or self-assessment tools.
  - Correction: identifying and correcting their own or others' errors and mistakes in pronunciation, vocabulary, grammar, fluency, coherence, etc.
  - Expansion: adding more information, details, examples, or arguments to their speech, using paraphrasing, elaboration, or justification techniques.
  - Variation: adapting and modifying their speech according to the purpose, audience, and context of the conversation, using different registers, genres, and styles.
  - Reflection: reviewing and evaluating their own or others' speaking and listening skills, using criteria, rubrics, or checklists, and setting goals for improvement.
