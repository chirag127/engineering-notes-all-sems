### Introduction to oral communication, Nuances and Modes of Speech Delivery

Oral communication refers to the spoken exchange of information, ideas, and thoughts between two or more people. It is one of the most common and effective forms of communication. Some of the key aspects of oral communication include:

1. **Clarity:** The speaker should be clear and concise in their speech, using language that is easily understood by the audience.

2. **Tone:** The tone of voice used by the speaker can convey emotions and attitudes, and can greatly impact the effectiveness of the communication.

3. **Non-verbal cues:** Body language, facial expressions, and gestures can also convey meaning and should be used effectively in oral communication.

4. **Active listening:** Effective oral communication also involves active listening on the part of the audience, to ensure that the message is received and understood.

There are several modes of speech delivery, including:

1. **Impromptu:** This is an unprepared, spontaneous speech, often given in response to an unexpected situation or question.

2. **Extemporaneous:** This is a speech that is prepared in advance, but delivered without notes or a script.

3. **Manuscript:** This is a speech that is written out in full and read from a script.

4. **Memorized:** This is a speech that is written out in full and memorized by the speaker.

Each mode of speech delivery has its own nuances and can be effective in different situations. It is important for the speaker to choose the appropriate mode for the situation and audience.
