### Homonyms, New Word Formation, Select Word Power

#### Homonyms
- Homonyms are words that have the same spelling and pronunciation but different meanings.
- Examples of homonyms include "bat" (a flying mammal or a piece of sports equipment), "bank" (a financial institution or the side of a river), and "bark" (the sound a dog makes or the outer layer of a tree).

#### New Word Formation
- New words can be formed in a variety of ways, including compounding, blending, clipping, and back-formation.
- Compounding involves combining two or more words to create a new word, such as "toothbrush" or "sunflower".
- Blending involves combining parts of two or more words to create a new word, such as "brunch" (from "breakfast" and "lunch") or "smog" (from "smoke" and "fog").
- Clipping involves shortening a word to create a new word, such as "ad" (from "advertisement") or "gym" (from "gymnasium").
- Back-formation involves creating a new word by removing a real or supposed affix from an existing word, such as "edit" (from "editor") or "babysit" (from "babysitter").

#### Select Word Power
- Select word power refers to the ability to choose the most appropriate words to convey a particular meaning or message.
- This skill involves having a strong vocabulary and understanding the nuances of word usage and connotation.
- To improve your select word power, you can read widely, learn new words and their meanings, and practice using words in context. It can also be helpful to pay attention to how words are used by others, particularly in writing.
