Cell Cycle:
- The cell cycle is the series of events that occur in a cell leading to its division and duplication (replication).
- The cell cycle consists of two main stages: interphase (G1, S, G2) and the mitotic phase (M).
- Interphase is the stage in which the cell grows and replicates its DNA in preparation for cell division.
- In G1, the cell grows and carries out normal cellular functions.
- In S, DNA replication occurs.
- In G2, the cell checks for DNA damage and ensures that the replicated DNA is correct before proceeding to cell division.
- The mitotic phase (M) is the stage in which the cell physically divides into two daughter cells.
- Mitosis is the type of cell division that results in the formation of two identical daughter cells, each with the same number of chromosomes as the parent cell.
- The cell cycle is regulated by a series of checkpoints to ensure that the cell divides at the appropriate time and that the genetic material is properly distributed to the daughter cells.
- The cell cycle is an essential process for the growth and reproduction of cells and is tightly regulated to ensure the proper functioning of the organism.
