### Plant Cells

Plant cells are the basic units of life in green plants, photosynthetic eukaryotes of the kingdom Plantae. They have some distinctive features that differentiate them from animal cells and other types of eukaryotic cells. Here are some of the main characteristics of plant cells:

- Plant cells have a **cell wall** surrounding the cell membrane. The cell wall is a rigid structure made of cellulose, hemicelluloses, and pectin. It provides support, protection, and shape to the plant cell. It also allows the exchange of substances between the cell and the environment through pores called plasmodesmata .
- Plant cells have **chloroplasts**, specialized organelles that contain the pigment chlorophyll and are the site of photosynthesis. Photosynthesis is the process by which plants convert light energy into chemical energy, producing organic molecules and oxygen. Chloroplasts have their own DNA and can divide independently of the cell nucleus .
- Plant cells have a large **central vacuole**, a water-filled space enclosed by a membrane called the tonoplast. The central vacuole occupies most of the volume of the plant cell and serves various functions, such as maintaining turgor pressure, storing nutrients and waste products, and regulating pH .
- Plant cells have other organelles that are common to most eukaryotic cells, such as the nucleus, the endoplasmic reticulum, the Golgi apparatus, the mitochondria, the cytoskeleton, and the ribosomes. However, some of these organelles may have different shapes or functions in plant cells compared to animal cells. For example, plant mitochondria have cristae that are tubular rather than flattened, and plant ribosomes are slightly smaller than animal ribosomes.
- Plant cells communicate with each other through chemical signals and electrical impulses. Some of the molecules involved in plant cell signaling are hormones, cytokinins, auxins, ethylene, and jasmonates. These molecules can regulate plant growth, development, defense, and stress responses. Plant cells can also sense and respond to environmental stimuli, such as light, gravity, temperature, and water availability.
