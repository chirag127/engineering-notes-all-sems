### Cell cycle

- The cell cycle is the ordered sequence of events that occur in a cell in preparation for cell division.
- The cell cycle consists of four main stages: G1, S, G2, and M  .
- G1 is the first gap phase, where the cell grows in size and synthesizes proteins and organelles .
- S is the synthesis phase, where the cell replicates its DNA and chromosomes  .
- G2 is the second gap phase, where the cell checks for DNA damage and prepares for mitosis .
- M is the mitosis phase, where the cell divides its nucleus and cytoplasm into two daughter cells .
- The stages G1, S, and G2 are collectively called interphase, which accounts for the span between cell divisions .
- The cell cycle is regulated by checkpoints, which are mechanisms that ensure the accuracy and completeness of each stage .
- The cell cycle is essential for the growth, development, and repair of multicellular organisms, as well as for the reproduction of unicellular organisms  .
