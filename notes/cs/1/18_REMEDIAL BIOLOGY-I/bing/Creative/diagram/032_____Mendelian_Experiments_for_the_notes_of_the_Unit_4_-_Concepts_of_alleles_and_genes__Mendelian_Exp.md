### Mendelian Experiments

- Mendelian experiments are the studies of inheritance of traits in pea plants conducted by Gregor Mendel, a monk and a scientist, in the 19th century  .
- Mendel chose pea plants because they have distinct and easily observable traits, such as flower color, seed shape, and plant height, and they can self-pollinate or cross-pollinate .
- Mendel selected seven pairs of contrasting traits and performed controlled crosses between pure-breeding plants with different forms of the same trait, such as purple and white flowers  .
- Mendel observed the phenotypes and ratios of the offspring in the first generation (F1) and the second generation (F2) and recorded the results  .
- Mendel's results showed that the traits did not blend in the offspring, but rather appeared in discrete units that segregated in a predictable pattern  .
- Mendel also performed dihybrid crosses, in which he studied the inheritance of two traits at the same time, such as seed shape and color  .
- Mendel's dihybrid crosses revealed that the traits were inherited independently of each other, meaning that the seed shape did not affect the seed color and vice versa  .
- Mendel also tested the role of the male and female gametes in determining the traits of the offspring by performing reciprocal crosses, in which he reversed the direction of the pollen and the ovum transfer.
- Mendel's reciprocal crosses showed that the traits were inherited equally from both parents, regardless of which gamete contributed the trait.
- Mendel's experiments led him to propose three laws of inheritance: the law of segregation, the law of independent assortment, and the law of dominance .
- The law of segregation states that each individual has two factors (now called alleles) for each trait, and that these factors separate during gamete formation and recombine at fertilization .
- The law of independent assortment states that the factors for different traits are distributed to gametes independently of each other, meaning that the inheritance of one trait does not influence the inheritance of another trait .
- The law of dominance states that when two different factors (alleles) are present in an individual, one of them (the dominant allele) masks the expression of the other (the recessive allele), and only the dominant allele determines the phenotype of the individual .
- Mendel's experiments and laws are the foundation of modern genetics and provide a simple and elegant explanation of how traits are inherited in a Mendelian fashion    .
