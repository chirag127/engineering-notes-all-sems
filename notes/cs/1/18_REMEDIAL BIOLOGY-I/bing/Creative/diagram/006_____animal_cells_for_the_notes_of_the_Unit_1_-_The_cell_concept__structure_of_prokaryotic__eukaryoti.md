### Animal cells

- Animal cells are the basic unit of life in organisms of the kingdom Animalia .
- They are eukaryotic cells, meaning that they have a true nucleus and specialized structures called organelles that carry out different functions  .
- Animal cells, unlike plant and fungi cells, do not have a cell wall. Instead, multicellular animals have other structures that provide support to their tissues and organs, such as skeleton and cartilage.
- Additionally, animal cells also lack chloroplasts found in plant cells. Chloroplasts are the organelles that perform photosynthesis, the process of converting light energy into chemical energy.
- Some of the common organelles found in animal cells are  :
  - Plasma membrane: the outer layer that encloses the cell and regulates the movement of substances in and out of the cell.
  - Nucleus: the control center of the cell that contains the genetic material (DNA) and directs the activities of the cell.
  - Cytoplasm: the fluid-filled space inside the cell that contains various molecules and organelles.
  - Mitochondria: the powerhouses of the cell that produce energy (ATP) by breaking down glucose and oxygen.
  - Ribosomes: the sites of protein synthesis that are either free in the cytoplasm or attached to the endoplasmic reticulum (ER).
  - Endoplasmic reticulum (ER): a network of membranes that transports and modifies proteins and lipids. There are two types of ER: rough ER (with ribosomes) and smooth ER (without ribosomes).
  - Golgi apparatus: a stack of flattened membranes that sorts and packages proteins and lipids for delivery to other parts of the cell or outside the cell.
  - Lysosomes: the organelles that contain digestive enzymes that break down waste materials, foreign particles, and damaged organelles.
  - Centrosomes: the organelles that organize the microtubules, which are the protein filaments that form the cytoskeleton and the spindle fibers during cell division.
  - Vacuoles: the organelles that store water, salts, sugars, and other substances. Animal cells have smaller and more numerous vacuoles than plant cells.
  - Cytoskeleton: the network of protein filaments that provides shape, support, and movement to the cell. The main components of the cytoskeleton are microtubules, microfilaments, and intermediate filaments.
- Animal cells can communicate with each other through cell signaling, which is the process of sending and receiving signals using chemical messengers, such as hormones, neurotransmitters, and cytokines .
- Animal cells can be observed using a compound microscope, which is a device that uses two or more lenses to magnify an object. The basic parts of a compound microscope are the eyepiece, the objective lens, the stage, the light source, and the focus knob .
- Animal cells are composed of various chemical constituents, such as water, carbohydrates, lipids, proteins, nucleic acids, and minerals. These molecules are essential for the structure and function of the cell.
