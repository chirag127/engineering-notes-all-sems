### Structure and use of compound microscope

- A compound microscope is an instrument that magnifies objects otherwise too small to be seen, producing an image in which the object appears larger.
- A compound microscope has two or more convex lenses, one objective is used at a time, it produces 2-dimensional images, and its typical magnification is between 40x and 1000x.
- A compound microscope is mainly used for studying the structural details of cell, tissue, or sections of organs.
- A compound microscope has two main parts: non-optical parts and optical parts .

#### Non-optical parts

- Base: The base is also known as the foot which is either U or horseshoe-shaped. It supports the whole microscope and contains the light source.
- Pillar: The pillar is a vertical metal rod that connects the base to the arm.
- Arm: The arm is a curved metal part that holds the upper parts of the microscope. It is used to carry the microscope.
- Stage: The stage is a flat platform where the specimen is placed for observation. It has a hole in the center to allow light to pass through. It also has clips or a mechanical stage to hold the specimen in place .
- Coarse adjustment knob: The coarse adjustment knob is a large knob that moves the stage up and down to bring the specimen into focus. It is used for low power objectives .
- Fine adjustment knob: The fine adjustment knob is a smaller knob that moves the stage slightly to sharpen the focus. It is used for high power objectives .
- Condenser: The condenser is a lens system that collects and concentrates the light from the source and directs it to the specimen. It has a knob to adjust its height and a diaphragm to control the amount of light .

#### Optical parts

- Eyepiece or ocular lens: The eyepiece or ocular lens is the lens that is closest to the eye of the observer. It magnifies the image formed by the objective lens. It usually has a magnification of 10x .
- Body tube: The body tube is a hollow metal cylinder that holds the eyepiece and the objective lenses. It maintains the correct distance between them .
- Nosepiece or revolving turret: The nosepiece or revolving turret is a rotating part that holds the objective lenses. It allows the user to change the objective lens by rotating it .
- Objective lenses: The objective lenses are the lenses that are closest to the specimen. They magnify the specimen and form an image inside the microscope. They have different magnifications, such as 4x, 10x, 40x, and 100x .
- Light source or illuminator: The light source or illuminator is a lamp that provides light for the microscope. It can be either a mirror that reflects external light or an electric bulb that emits light .

### How to use a compound microscope

- Plug in the microscope and turn on the light source.
- Place the specimen on a glass slide and cover it with a cover slip.
- Place the slide on the stage and secure it with the clips or the mechanical stage.
- Select the lowest power objective lens (4x) and rotate the nosepiece until it clicks into place.
- Look through the eyepiece and use the coarse adjustment knob to move the stage up until the specimen comes into focus.
- Use the fine adjustment knob to sharpen the focus and the diaphragm to adjust the light intensity.
- Observe the specimen and note its features.
- To increase the magnification, rotate the nosepiece to select a higher power objective lens (10x, 40x, or 100x).
