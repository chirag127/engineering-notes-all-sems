### Stem

- The stem is the part of the plant that supports the leaves, flowers and fruits, and transports water and nutrients between them.
- The stem is composed of different types of tissues, such as epidermis, cortex, vascular tissue and pith.
- The epidermis is the outermost layer of cells that protects the stem from water loss, infection and injury.
- The cortex is the layer below the epidermis that consists of parenchyma cells, which store food and water, and collenchyma cells, which provide mechanical support.
- The vascular tissue is the tissue that conducts water and minerals (xylem) and organic substances (phloem) throughout the plant. The vascular tissue is arranged in bundles, which may be scattered (monocots) or arranged in a ring (dicots).
- The pith is the central part of the stem that consists of parenchyma cells, which store food and water.
- The stem may have different modifications, such as tendrils, thorns, bulbs, corms, rhizomes, stolons, tubers, etc., for various functions, such as climbing, defense, storage, propagation, etc..
