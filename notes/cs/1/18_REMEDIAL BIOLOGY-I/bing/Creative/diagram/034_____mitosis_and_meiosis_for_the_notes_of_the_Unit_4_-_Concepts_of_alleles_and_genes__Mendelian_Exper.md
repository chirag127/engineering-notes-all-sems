### Mitosis and Meiosis

- Mitosis and meiosis are both types of cell division that involve the separation of chromosomes in the nucleus .
- Mitosis is the process by which most cells in the body divide, involves a single round of cell division, and produces two identical, diploid daughter cells .
- Meiosis is the process by which gametes (sex cells) are produced, involves two rounds of cell division, and produces four different, haploid daughter cells .
- The main difference between mitosis and meiosis is that mitosis maintains the same number of chromosomes in the daughter cells as in the parent cell, while meiosis reduces the number of chromosomes by half in the daughter cells .
- Mitosis and meiosis are important for growth, repair, and reproduction of organisms .
- Mitosis and meiosis have different phases that involve the alignment, separation, and movement of chromosomes .
- Mitosis consists of four main phases: prophase, metaphase, anaphase, and telophase.
- Meiosis consists of two main stages: meiosis I and meiosis II, each with four phases: prophase I, metaphase I, anaphase I, and telophase I, and prophase II, metaphase II, anaphase II, and telophase II.
- Mitosis and meiosis are regulated by the cell cycle, which is a series of events that control the timing and frequency of cell division.
- Mitosis and meiosis are influenced by various factors, such as genetic variation, environmental conditions, and errors in chromosome separation   .
