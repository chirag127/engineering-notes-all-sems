### Five Kingdom Classification

- The five kingdom classification was proposed by **Robert.H. Whittaker** in 1969  .
- This classification was based on certain characters like **mode of nutrition, thallus organization, cell structure, phylogenetic relationships and reproduction**  .
- This classification includes five kingdoms: **Monera, Protista, Fungi, Plantae and Animalia**    .
- The main features and advantages of each kingdom are:

| Kingdom | Features | Advantages |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Monera | - Prokaryotic organisms with no true nucleus or membrane-bound organelles    .<br>- Unicellular or colonial in form    .<br>- Autotrophic or heterotrophic mode of nutrition    .<br>- Reproduce by binary fission or budding    .<br>- Examples: bacteria, cyanobacteria, archaebacteria, etc. | - The simplest and most primitive form of life    .<br>- The most diverse and abundant group of organisms on earth    .<br>- The most adaptable and resilient group of organisms that can survive in extreme environments    .<br>- The most important group of organisms for biogeochemical cycles, decomposition, nitrogen fixation, etc    . |
| Protista | - Eukaryotic organisms with a true nucleus and membrane-bound organelles    .<br>- Mostly unicellular or colonial, some multicellular    .<br>- Autotrophic or heterotrophic mode of nutrition    .<br>- Reproduce by asexual or sexual methods    .<br>- Examples: protozoa, algae, slime molds, etc. | - The most diverse and complex group of unicellular organisms    .<br>- The link between prokaryotes and multicellular eukaryotes    .<br>- The most important group of organisms for primary production, food chain, oxygen production, etc    . |
| Fungi | - Eukaryotic organisms with a true nucleus and membrane-bound organelles    .<br>- Multicellular, except yeasts    .<br>- Heterotrophic mode of nutrition by absorption    .<br>- Reproduce by asexual or sexual spores [^2^
