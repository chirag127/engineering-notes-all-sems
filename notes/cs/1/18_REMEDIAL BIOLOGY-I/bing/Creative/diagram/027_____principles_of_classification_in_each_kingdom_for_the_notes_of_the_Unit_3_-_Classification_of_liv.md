### Principles of classification in each kingdom

The five kingdom classification system was proposed by R.H. Whittaker in 1969 based on the following criteria  :

- Cellular structure: prokaryotic or eukaryotic
- Body organization: unicellular or multicellular
- Mode of nutrition: autotrophic or heterotrophic
- Phylogenetic relationships: evolutionary history and molecular evidence
- Reproduction: sexual or asexual

The five kingdoms are:

- **Monera**: This kingdom includes all the prokaryotic organisms, such as bacteria and cyanobacteria. They are unicellular and have a simple cell structure without a nucleus or membrane-bound organelles. They can be autotrophic or heterotrophic, depending on the source of carbon and energy. They reproduce mainly by binary fission, but some can also exchange genetic material by conjugation, transformation, or transduction.
- **Protista**: This kingdom includes all the eukaryotic organisms that are not plants, animals, or fungi. They are mostly unicellular, but some are colonial or multicellular. They have a complex cell structure with a nucleus and membrane-bound organelles. They can be autotrophic or heterotrophic, depending on the type of protist. They reproduce by various methods, such as binary fission, multiple fission, budding, spore formation, or sexual reproduction.
- **Fungi**: This kingdom includes all the eukaryotic organisms that are heterotrophic and absorb nutrients from organic matter. They are mostly multicellular, but some are unicellular. They have a cell wall made of chitin and a body composed of hyphae, which form a network called mycelium. They reproduce by spores, which can be produced asexually or sexually.
- **Plantae**: This kingdom includes all the eukaryotic organisms that are autotrophic and synthesize organic molecules from inorganic substances using light energy. They are multicellular and have a cell wall made of cellulose and a body composed of roots, stems, and leaves. They reproduce by spores or seeds, which can be produced asexually or sexually.
- **Animalia**: This kingdom includes all the eukaryotic organisms that are heterotrophic and ingest food. They are multicellular and have no cell wall. They have a body composed of tissues, organs, and organ systems. They reproduce by gametes, which are produced sexually.

The advantages of the five kingdom classification system are:

- It reflects the diversity and complexity of life forms.
- It is based on both observable and molecular characteristics.
- It shows the evolutionary relationships among different groups of organisms.

The limitations of the five kingdom classification system are:

- It does not account for the intermediate forms or transitional groups of organisms.
- It does not consider the genetic variations or molecular differences among organisms within the same kingdom.
- It does not include the viruses, which are non-living but have some characteristics of living organisms.
