### Systematic and binomial system of nomenclature

- Systematic is the branch of biology that deals with the identification, naming and classification of living organisms based on their similarities and differences.
- Classification is the process of grouping organisms into categories or taxa (singular: taxon) based on their shared characteristics and evolutionary relationships.
- Nomenclature is the system of assigning names to organisms according to a set of rules and conventions.
- Binomial nomenclature is the system of scientifically naming organisms developed by Carl Linnaeus in the 18th century .
- Binomial nomenclature is required in biology to unify the naming system throughout life sciences and, as a result, offer a single unique name identifier for a species across languages.
- Binomial nomenclature is made up of two names: the genus name and the species name.
- The genus name is the first word and is always capitalized. The species name is the second word and is always lowercase. Both names are written in italics or underlined.
- For example, the scientific name of the human species is Homo sapiens, where Homo is the genus name and sapiens is the species name.
- The genus name can be abbreviated with the first letter followed by a period, such as H. sapiens, when the genus is clear from the context.
- Sometimes, a third name called the subspecies name or the variety name can be added after the species name to indicate a distinct population within a species.
- For example, the scientific name of the Bengal tiger is Panthera tigris tigris, where Panthera is the genus name, tigris is the species name and tigris is the subspecies name.
- The rules and conventions of binomial nomenclature are governed by the International Code of Zoological Nomenclature (ICZN) for animals and the International Code of Nomenclature for algae, fungi, and plants (ICNafp) for plants.
- Some of the basic rules of binomial nomenclature are :
  - The scientific name must be unique and not duplicated for any other organism.
  - The scientific name must be based on Latin or Latinized words, although they can be derived from other languages.
  - The scientific name must agree with the grammatical gender of the genus name.
  - The scientific name must be published and accepted by the scientific community.
  - The scientific name must follow the principle of priority, which means that the oldest valid name for an organism has precedence over any newer names.
