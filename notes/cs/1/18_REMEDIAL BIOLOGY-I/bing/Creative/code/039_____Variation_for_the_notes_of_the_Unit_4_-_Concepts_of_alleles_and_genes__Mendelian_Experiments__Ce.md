### Variation

Variation is the difference between individuals of the same species, caused by genetic and environmental factors . Variation is important for biological evolution, as it provides the raw material for natural selection.

#### Types of variation

There are two main types of variation: continuous and discontinuous.

- Continuous variation is when the data come in a range, such as height, weight, or skin color. Continuous variation is influenced by both genetic and environmental factors, and shows a normal distribution curve.
- Discontinuous variation is when the data come in groups, such as blood type, eye color, or sex. Discontinuous variation is determined by one or a few genes, and is not affected by the environment.

#### Causes of variation

There are two main sources of variation in population: genetic and environmental.

- Genetic variation arises due to mutation and recombination during meiosis. Mutation is a change in the DNA base sequence that results in the generation of one or more new alleles. Recombination is the exchange of genetic material between homologous chromosomes during crossing over, which creates new combinations of alleles in the gametes.
- Environmental variation arises due to the effect of external factors on the expression of the genetic potentials. Environmental factors include temperature, light, nutrition, stress, and disease. Environmental variation can be reversible or irreversible, depending on the degree and duration of the environmental influence.
