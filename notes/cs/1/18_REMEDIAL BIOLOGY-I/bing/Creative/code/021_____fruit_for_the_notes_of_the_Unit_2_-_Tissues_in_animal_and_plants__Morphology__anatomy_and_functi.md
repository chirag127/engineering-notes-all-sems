### Fruit

- A fruit is the **soft, pulpy part of a flowering plant that contains seeds**.
- It is formed from the **ovaries of angiosperms** (plants that produce flowers and seeds enclosed in a carpel) and is exclusive only to this group of plants .
- Fruits are a characteristic of flowering plants. As soon as **pollen and fertilization occur**, the ovary of a plant becomes a fruit and the ovules become a seed.
- Fruits are classified into four types namely: **simple, aggregate, multiple, and accessory fruits**.
- Simple fruits are formed from a single ovary of a single flower, such as apples, oranges, and cherries.
- Aggregate fruits are formed from several ovaries of a single flower, such as strawberries, raspberries, and blackberries.
- Multiple fruits are formed from the ovaries of several flowers that fuse together, such as pineapples, figs, and mulberries.
- Accessory fruits are formed from other parts of the flower besides the ovary, such as the receptacle, the calyx, or the bracts, such as pears, rose hips, and watermelons.
- Fruits have various functions in the life cycle of plants, such as **protecting the seeds, dispersing the seeds, attracting pollinators, and providing food for animals and humans**.
- Fruits can be classified into **fleshy or dry** depending on their texture and water content.
- Fleshy fruits have a soft and juicy pulp, such as grapes, bananas, and tomatoes.
- Dry fruits have a hard and dry covering, such as nuts, grains, and pods.
- Dry fruits can be further classified into **dehiscent or indehiscent** depending on whether they split open or remain intact when mature.
- Dehiscent fruits release their seeds when they dry and crack open, such as beans, peas, and poppies.
- Indehiscent fruits retain their seeds inside their covering until they are eaten or decay, such as acorns, almonds, and sunflower seeds.
