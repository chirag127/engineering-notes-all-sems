### Principles of classification in each kingdom

- Classification is the process of grouping living organisms into categories based on their similarities and differences.
- The five kingdom classification system was proposed by R.H. Whittaker in 1969, based on the following criteria  :
  - Cellular structure: prokaryotic or eukaryotic
  - Body organization: unicellular or multicellular
  - Mode of nutrition: autotrophic or heterotrophic
  - Phylogenetic relationships: evolutionary history and common ancestry
  - Reproduction: sexual or asexual
- The five kingdoms are: Monera, Protista, Fungi, Plantae and Animalia.
- Monera: This kingdom includes all the prokaryotic organisms, such as bacteria and cyanobacteria. They are unicellular and have no membrane-bound organelles. They can be autotrophic or heterotrophic, and reproduce by binary fission or conjugation  .
- Protista: This kingdom includes all the eukaryotic organisms that are not plants, animals or fungi. They are mostly unicellular, but some are colonial or multicellular. They have membrane-bound organelles and a nucleus. They can be autotrophic or heterotrophic, and reproduce by binary fission, multiple fission, budding or sexual methods  .
- Fungi: This kingdom includes all the eukaryotic organisms that are saprophytic or parasitic. They are mostly multicellular, but some are unicellular. They have cell walls made of chitin and lack chlorophyll. They are heterotrophic and obtain nutrients by absorption. They reproduce by spores, fragmentation or sexual methods  .
- Plantae: This kingdom includes all the eukaryotic organisms that are photosynthetic. They are multicellular and have cell walls made of cellulose. They have chloroplasts and store food as starch. They are autotrophic and synthesize organic molecules from inorganic sources. They reproduce by spores, seeds or vegetative methods  .
- Animalia: This kingdom includes all the eukaryotic organisms that are ingestive. They are multicellular and have no cell walls. They have mitochondria and store food as glycogen. They are heterotrophic and obtain nutrients by ingestion and digestion. They reproduce by sexual methods  .
