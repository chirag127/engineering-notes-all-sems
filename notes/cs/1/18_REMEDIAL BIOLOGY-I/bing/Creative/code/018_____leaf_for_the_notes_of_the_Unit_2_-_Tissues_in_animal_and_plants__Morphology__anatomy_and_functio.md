### Leaf

- A leaf is a thin, flat organ of the plant that is mainly responsible for photosynthesis and transpiration .
- A leaf develops laterally at the node of the stem and originates from the shoot apical meristem.
- The main function of a leaf is to produce food for the plant by photosynthesis. Chlorophyll, the substance that gives plants their characteristic green colour, absorbs light energy.
- The internal structure of the leaf is protected by the leaf epidermis, which is continuous with the stem epidermis. The leaf epidermis may have stomata, which are pores that allow gas exchange and water loss.
- The leaf mesophyll is the tissue between the upper and lower epidermis that contains the chloroplasts. The mesophyll may be divided into two layers: the palisade layer, which is closer to the upper epidermis and has more chloroplasts, and the spongy layer, which is closer to the lower epidermis and has more air spaces.
- The leaf veins are the vascular bundles that transport water and nutrients to and from the leaf. The veins may form different patterns, such as parallel, netted, or dichotomous.
- The leaf margin is the edge of the leaf blade. The margin may have different shapes, such as entire, serrated, lobed, or crenate.
- The leaf base is the part of the leaf that attaches to the stem. The leaf base may have different structures, such as stipules, which are leaf-like appendages, or sheath, which is a tubular extension that wraps around the stem.
- The leaf apex is the tip of the leaf blade. The apex may have different forms, such as acute, obtuse, acuminate, or mucronate.

### Types of Leaves

- Leaves can be classified into different types based on their shape, size, arrangement, and complexity .
- Based on shape, leaves can be linear, lanceolate, ovate, orbicular, cordate, or palmate.
- Based on size, leaves can be microphylls, which are small and have a single vein, or megaphylls, which are large and have branched veins.
- Based on arrangement, leaves can be alternate, opposite, or whorled. Alternate leaves are arranged singly at each node, opposite leaves are arranged in pairs at each node, and whorled leaves are arranged in groups of three or more at each node.
- Based on complexity, leaves can be simple or compound. Simple leaves have a single blade, while compound leaves have two or more leaflets attached to a common petiole. Compound leaves can be further divided into pinnate, which have leaflets arranged along a central axis, or palmate, which have leaflets radiating from a common point .

### Modifications of Leaves

- Leaves can be modified for different functions, such as storage, protection, support, or reproduction.
- Some examples of modified leaves are:

  - Spines, which are reduced leaves that deter herbivores or reduce water loss, such as in cacti.
  - Tendrils, which are coiled or twining leaves that help the plant to climb or attach to a support, such as in peas.
  - Bulbs, which are fleshy leaves that store food and water, such as in onions.
  - Scales, which are dry and membranous leaves that protect the buds or stems, such as in garlic.
  - Bracts, which are colourful or showy leaves that attract pollinators or mimic flowers, such as in poinsettia.
  - Insectivorous leaves, which are leaves that trap and digest insects or other small animals, such as in Venus flytrap.
