## Tissues in animal and plants

- A tissue is a group of similar cells that work together to perform a specific function.
- There are two main types of tissues in plants and animals: meristematic and permanent in plants, and epithelial, connective, muscle and nervous in animals.

### Plant tissues

- Meristematic tissues are composed of actively dividing cells that are responsible for the growth of the plant. They are found in the tips of roots and stems (apical meristems), the cambium layer between the xylem and phloem (lateral meristems), and the intercalary meristems at the nodes of stems.
- Permanent tissues are derived from the meristematic tissues and have a specific function and structure. They are classified into three types: simple, complex and special.
  - Simple tissues are made of one type of cell and perform a common function. They include parenchyma, collenchyma and sclerenchyma.
    - Parenchyma cells are thin-walled, loosely packed and have large vacuoles. They are involved in photosynthesis, storage and secretion.
    - Collenchyma cells are thick-walled, elongated and have irregular shapes. They provide mechanical support and flexibility to the plant parts.
    - Sclerenchyma cells are hard, lignified and have thick secondary walls. They provide mechanical strength and rigidity to the plant parts. They are of two types: fibres and sclereids.
  - Complex tissues are made of more than one type of cell and perform a common function. They include xylem and phloem.
    - Xylem is the vascular tissue that conducts water and minerals from the roots to the leaves. It is composed of four types of cells: tracheids, vessels, xylem parenchyma and xylem fibres.
    - Phloem is the vascular tissue that conducts organic food from the leaves to the other parts of the plant. It is composed of four types of cells: sieve tubes, companion cells, phloem parenchyma and phloem fibres.
  - Special tissues are modified to perform a specific function. They include secretory tissues and epidermis.
    - Secretory tissues are involved in the production and secretion of various substances, such as nectar, resin, latex, etc. They are of two types: glandular and nonglandular.
    - Epidermis is the outermost layer of cells that covers the plant body. It protects the plant from water loss, infection and injury. It may have specialized structures, such as stomata, trichomes, cuticle, etc.

### Animal tissues

- Epithelial tissues are composed of tightly packed cells that form a continuous layer over the body surfaces, cavities and organs. They are involved in protection, absorption, secretion, excretion and sensation. They are classified based on the shape and arrangement of cells. The shapes include squamous, cuboidal and columnar. The arrangements include simple, stratified and pseudostratified.
- Connective tissues are composed of cells that are embedded in an extracellular matrix of protein fibers and ground substance. They are involved in binding, supporting, protecting and transporting various substances in the body. They are classified based on the type and density of the matrix. The types include loose, dense, cartilage, bone and blood.
  - Loose connective tissues have a sparse matrix of collagen and elastic fibers. They include areolar, adipose and reticular tissues.
    - Areolar tissues fill the spaces between organs and provide elasticity and support.
    - Adipose tissues store fat and provide insulation and cushioning.
    - Reticular tissues form a network of thin fibers that support the lymphoid organs, such as spleen and lymph nodes.
  - Dense connective tissues have a dense matrix of collagen and elastic fibers. They include regular, irregular and elastic tissues.
    - Regular tissues have parallel bundles of collagen fibers that provide strength and flexibility. They are found in tendons and ligaments.
    - Irregular tissues have randomly arranged collagen fibers that provide resistance to stress. They are found in the dermis of the skin and the capsules of organs.
    - Elastic tissues have abundant elastic fibers that provide elasticity and recoil. They are found in the walls of arteries and the vocal cords.
