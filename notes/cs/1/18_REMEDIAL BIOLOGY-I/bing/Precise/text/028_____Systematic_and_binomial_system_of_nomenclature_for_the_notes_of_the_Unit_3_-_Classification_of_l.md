### Systematic and Binomial System of Nomenclature

- Systematic and binomial system of nomenclature is a method of naming organisms using two names, the first being the genus and the second being the species.
- This system was developed by Carl Linnaeus in the 18th century and is still used today.
- The binomial system of nomenclature is used to provide a unique and universally accepted name for each species of organism.
- The first part of the name, the genus, is always capitalized, while the second part, the species, is not.
- The entire name is usually written in italics or underlined.
- This system is used to classify organisms into groups based on their shared characteristics and evolutionary relationships.
- In the five kingdom classification system, organisms are grouped into the kingdoms Monera, Protista, Fungi, Plantae, and Animalia.
- Each kingdom is further divided into smaller groups, such as phyla, classes, orders, families, genera, and species.
- The principles of classification in each kingdom vary, but generally involve the use of shared characteristics to group organisms together.
- The concept of animal and plant classification involves grouping organisms based on their shared characteristics, such as body plan, mode of nutrition, and reproductive methods.
- This system of classification is used to better understand the diversity of life and the evolutionary relationships between different organisms.
