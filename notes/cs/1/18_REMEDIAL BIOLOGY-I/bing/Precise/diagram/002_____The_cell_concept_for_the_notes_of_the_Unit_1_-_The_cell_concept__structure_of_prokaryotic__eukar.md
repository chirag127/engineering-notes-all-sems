### The Cell Concept

1. The cell is the basic unit of life.
2. All living organisms are composed of one or more cells.
3. Cells arise from pre-existing cells through cell division.
4. Cells contain hereditary information, which is passed from cell to cell during cell division.
5. The activities of an organism depend on the total activity of its independent cells.
6. The cell is the smallest unit of life that can perform all life processes.
7. Cells are the building blocks of all living things.

### Structure of Prokaryotic and Eukaryotic Cells

- Prokaryotic cells are simple in structure, with no defined nucleus, and include bacteria and archaea.
- Eukaryotic cells are more complex, with a defined nucleus and membrane-bound organelles, and include plant and animal cells.

### Plant Cells and Animal Cells

- Plant cells have a cell wall, chloroplasts, and a large central vacuole, while animal cells do not.
- Animal cells have centrioles and lysosomes, while plant cells do not.

### Structure and Function of Cell Membrane

- The cell membrane is a thin, flexible barrier that surrounds the cell.
- It is composed of a lipid bilayer, with proteins embedded within it.
- The cell membrane regulates the movement of substances in and out of the cell.

### Cell Organelles and Their Function

- Nucleus: contains the cell's genetic material and controls the cell's activities.
- Mitochondria: produces energy for the cell through cellular respiration.
- Ribosomes: synthesizes proteins.
- Endoplasmic reticulum: transports materials within the cell.
- Golgi apparatus: modifies, sorts, and packages proteins and lipids.
- Lysosomes: breaks down waste materials and cellular debris.
- Vacuoles: stores materials such as water, salts, and carbohydrates.
- Chloroplasts (in plant cells): converts sunlight into chemical energy through photosynthesis.

### Basics of Cell Signaling

- Cells communicate with each other through chemical signals.
- These signals can be transmitted through direct contact, or through the release of signaling molecules.
- The receiving cell responds to the signal through a series of events known as signal transduction.

### Structure and Use of Compound Microscope

- A compound microscope has two or more lenses that magnify the image of a specimen.
- The specimen is placed on a slide and illuminated from below.
- The image is viewed through the eyepiece.

### Basic Chemical Constituents of Living Body

- The basic chemical constituents of living organisms are water, carbohydrates, lipids, proteins, and nucleic acids.
- These molecules are essential for life processes such as metabolism, growth, and reproduction.
