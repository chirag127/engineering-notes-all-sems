### Seed

- A seed is a mature ovule that contains an embryo, along with a food supply, and is covered by a protective coat.
- Seeds are the primary means by which flowering plants reproduce.
- The food supply in the seed is used by the embryo during germination and early growth until the seedling is able to produce its own food through photosynthesis.
- The protective coat, or seed coat, helps to prevent the seed from drying out and protects it from mechanical damage and predation.
- Seeds can remain dormant for extended periods of time before germinating, allowing the plant to survive unfavorable conditions.
- The process of seed germination begins when the seed absorbs water, causing the embryo to swell and break through the seed coat.
- The radicle, or embryonic root, emerges first, followed by the shoot, which will develop into the stem and leaves of the plant.
- The morphology, anatomy, and function of the seed vary among different plant species.
- In some plants, the seed coat is hard and impermeable, requiring physical or chemical scarification to allow water to enter and initiate germination.
- In other plants, the seed coat is permeable, allowing water to enter without any special treatment.
- The food supply in the seed can be in the form of endosperm, a tissue that surrounds the embryo, or it can be stored in the cotyledons, the first leaves of the embryo.
- Some seeds, such as those of legumes, have a structure called a hilum, which is a scar left by the stalk that attached the ovule to the ovary wall.
- The micropyle, a small opening in the seed coat, allows water to enter the seed during germination.
- Seeds can be dispersed by various means, including wind, water, animals, and explosive mechanisms.
- The study of seeds, their structure, development, and germination is known as seed science.
