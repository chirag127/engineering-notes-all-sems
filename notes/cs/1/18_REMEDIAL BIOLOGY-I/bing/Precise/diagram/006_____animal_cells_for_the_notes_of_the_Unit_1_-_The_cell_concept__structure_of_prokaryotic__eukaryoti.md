### Animal Cells

Animal cells are a type of eukaryotic cell, which means they have a defined nucleus and other membrane-bound organelles. They are distinct from plant cells in several ways, including the lack of a cell wall and chloroplasts. Here are some key points about animal cells:

1. Animal cells are surrounded by a plasma membrane, which is a selectively permeable barrier that controls the movement of substances in and out of the cell.
2. The nucleus is the control center of the cell, containing the genetic material (DNA) and directing the cell's activities.
3. The cytoplasm is the gel-like substance that fills the cell and contains the organelles.
4. The endoplasmic reticulum (ER) is a network of membranes involved in protein and lipid synthesis.
5. The Golgi apparatus is responsible for modifying, sorting, and packaging proteins and lipids for transport to their final destinations.
6. Mitochondria are the powerhouses of the cell, generating ATP (energy) through cellular respiration.
7. Lysosomes contain digestive enzymes that break down waste materials and cellular debris.
8. The cytoskeleton is a network of protein fibers that provides structural support and helps with cell movement.
9. Animal cells also contain other organelles such as ribosomes (protein synthesis), centrioles (cell division), and peroxisomes (detoxification).
