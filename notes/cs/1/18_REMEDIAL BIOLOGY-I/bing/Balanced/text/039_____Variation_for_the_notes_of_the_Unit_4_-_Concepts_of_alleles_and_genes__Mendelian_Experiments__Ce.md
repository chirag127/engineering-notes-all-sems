### Variation

- Variation is the difference between individuals of the same species, caused by genetic and environmental factors .
- Genetic variation arises due to mutation and recombination during meiosis. Mutation is a change in the DNA sequence of a gene, which may result in a new allele or trait. Recombination is the exchange of genetic material between homologous chromosomes during meiosis, which may produce new combinations of alleles or traits.
- Environmental variation arises due to the effect of environmental factors on the expression of the genetic potentials. Environmental factors include temperature, light, nutrition, stress, etc. These factors may influence the phenotype or observable characteristics of an individual, such as height, weight, color, behavior, etc.
- Variation is important for biological evolution, as it provides the raw material for natural selection. Natural selection is the process by which individuals with certain traits that are better adapted to their environment survive and reproduce more than others, leading to changes in the frequency of alleles or traits in a population over time.
- Types of natural selection include directional selection, stabilizing selection, and disruptive selection. Directional selection favors one extreme of a trait over the other, leading to a shift in the mean value of the trait in a population. Stabilizing selection favors the intermediate value of a trait over the extremes, leading to a reduction in the variation of the trait in a population. Disruptive selection favors both extremes of a trait over the intermediate, leading to a split in the population into two or more subgroups with different traits.
- Examples of natural selection include the evolution of antibiotic resistance in bacteria, the evolution of industrial melanism in peppered moths, the evolution of beak size and shape in Darwin's finches, etc.
