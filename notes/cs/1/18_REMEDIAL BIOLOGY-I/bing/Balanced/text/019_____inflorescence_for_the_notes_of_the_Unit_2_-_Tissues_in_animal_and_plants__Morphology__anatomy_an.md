### Inflorescence

- An inflorescence is the arrangement of a cluster of flowers on a floral axis or peduncle  .
- The inflorescence is of two main types, based on the mode of development and arrangement of flowers on the axis  :
  - Racemose inflorescence (Indeterminate Inflorescence): The main axis continues to grow and produces flowers laterally in an acropetal succession (from base to apex). The older flowers are at the base and the younger ones are at the apex  .
  - Cymose inflorescence (Determinate Inflorescence): The main axis terminates in a flower and produces lateral branches that also end in flowers. The flowers are produced in a basipetal succession (from apex to base). The older flowers are at the apex and the younger ones are at the base  .
- There are also special types of inflorescence, such as:
  - Hypanthodium: A fleshy receptacle with a basal opening, enclosing numerous small unisexual flowers . Example: Ficus (Fig).
  - Cyathium: A cup-shaped involucre enclosing a single female flower and several male flowers . Example: Euphorbia.
  - Verticillaster: A pair of cymes arranged in opposite directions at the nodes of a square stem . Example: Salvia, Ocimum, Coleus.
  - Spadix: A fleshy spike of flowers enclosed by a large, often brightly colored, bract called spathe . Example: Colocasia, Alocasia, Anthurium.
  - Capitulum or Head: A condensed or shortened raceme or spike of sessile flowers on a flattened receptacle . Example: Helianthus, Tridax, Calendula.
- There are also mixed types of inflorescence, such as:
  - Thyrsus: A mixed inflorescence with a main racemose axis and lateral cymose branches . Example: Jasmine, Lantana.
  - Anthocarpous: A mixed inflorescence with a main cymose axis and lateral racemose branches . Example: Nyctanthes, Clerodendron.
