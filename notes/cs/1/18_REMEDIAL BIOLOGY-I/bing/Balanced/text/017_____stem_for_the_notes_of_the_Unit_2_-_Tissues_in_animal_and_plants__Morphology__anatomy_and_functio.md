### Stem

- The stem is an **axial organ** of the shoot system that supports and elevates the leaves, fruits, and flowers.
- The stem also arranges the leaves in a way that they receive direct sunlight for **photosynthesis**.
- The stem contains **vascular tissues** (xylem and phloem) that conduct water and nutrients across the plant.
- The stem also has **storage** and **synthetic** functions, as it can store water, starch, and other substances, and produce hormones and secondary metabolites.
- The stem has a **radial structure**, meaning that it has a central axis with tissues arranged in concentric layers.
- The stem bears **nodes** and **internodes**. Nodes are the regions where leaves are attached, while internodes are the portions between two nodes.
- The stem has a **dermal tissue** called the **epidermis** that covers and protects the stem. The epidermis may have a waxy **cuticle** that limits water loss, and may also have specialized structures such as hairs, glands, and stomata .
- The stem has a **ground tissue** called the **cortex** that lies between the epidermis and the vascular tissue. The cortex is mainly composed of **parenchyma** cells that have various functions, such as photosynthesis, storage, and secretion.
- The stem has a **vascular tissue** that consists of two types of conducting tissues: **xylem** and **phloem**. Xylem transports water and minerals from the roots to the shoots, while phloem transports organic molecules from the sources (such as leaves) to the sinks (such as fruits).
- The xylem and phloem are arranged in **vascular bundles** that are distributed in a ring-like pattern in the stem. The vascular bundles are surrounded by a layer of cells called the **bundle sheath**.
- The stem has a **meristematic tissue** called the **vascular cambium** that lies between the xylem and the phloem in each vascular bundle. The vascular cambium is a layer of undifferentiated cells that can divide and produce new xylem and phloem cells, resulting in the **secondary growth** of the stem.
- The stem also has a **meristematic tissue** called the **cork cambium** that lies under the epidermis in the cortex. The cork cambium produces **cork cells** that replace the epidermis and form a protective layer called the **periderm**.
- The stem can be classified into different types based on the shape, size, branching, and modification. Some examples of stem types are **herbaceous** (soft and green), **woody** (hard and lignified), **erect** (vertical), **prostrate** (horizontal), **climbing** (supported by other plants), **tuberous** (swollen and modified for storage), **bulbous** (short and fleshy), **thorny** (with sharp outgrowths), and **succulent** (with thick and fleshy tissues).
