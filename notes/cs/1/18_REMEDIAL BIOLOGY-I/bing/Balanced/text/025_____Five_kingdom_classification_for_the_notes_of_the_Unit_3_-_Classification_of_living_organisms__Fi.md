### Five Kingdom Classification

- The five kingdom classification is a system of grouping living organisms based on their characteristics such as cell structure, mode of nutrition, reproduction, etc.
- It was proposed by Robert H. Whittaker in 1969 and is one of the most common ways to classify living beings  .
- The five kingdoms are Monera, Protista, Fungi, Plantae and Animalia.
- The major groups and principles of classification in each kingdom are as follows:

#### Kingdom Monera
- It includes all the prokaryotic organisms, which are unicellular and lack a true nucleus and membrane-bound organelles.
- They can be autotrophic or heterotrophic, aerobic or anaerobic, and may have different shapes such as cocci, bacilli, spirilla, etc.
- They reproduce mainly by binary fission, but some can also exchange genetic material by conjugation, transformation, transduction, etc.
- They are classified into two major groups: Archaebacteria and Eubacteria.
- Archaebacteria are the ancient bacteria that live in extreme environments such as hot springs, salt lakes, marshes, etc. They have unique cell wall and membrane composition and metabolic pathways.
- Eubacteria are the modern bacteria that are found in diverse habitats such as soil, water, air, plants, animals, etc. They have a peptidoglycan cell wall and a variety of metabolic pathways.

#### Kingdom Protista
- It includes all the eukaryotic organisms that are unicellular or colonial, and do not fit into the other kingdoms.
- They have a true nucleus and membrane-bound organelles, and can be autotrophic or heterotrophic, motile or non-motile, free-living or parasitic, etc.
- They reproduce mainly by asexual methods such as binary fission, multiple fission, budding, etc., but some can also undergo sexual reproduction by conjugation, syngamy, etc.
- They are classified into several groups based on their features such as:

  - Chrysophytes: They are the golden algae (desmids) and diatoms that have golden-brown pigments and silica cell walls. They are mostly photosynthetic and aquatic.
  - Dinoflagellates: They are the marine algae that have two flagella and cellulose plates. They can be photosynthetic or heterotrophic, and some can produce bioluminescence and toxins.
  - Euglenoids: They are the freshwater algae that have one or two flagella and a pellicle. They can be photosynthetic or heterotrophic, and some can switch between the two modes.
  - Slime moulds: They are the fungus-like protists that have a plasmodial or cellular body. They feed on decaying organic matter and can form spores for reproduction.
  - Protozoans: They are the animal-like protists that have different modes of locomotion and nutrition. They can be classified into four subgroups: Amoeboids, Flagellates, Ciliates and Sporozoans.

#### Kingdom Fungi
- It includes all the eukaryotic organisms that are multicellular (except yeasts) and have a filamentous body called mycelium, which is composed of hyphae.
- They are heterotrophic and obtain their nutrients by absorption from the substrates they grow on. They secrete enzymes to break down complex organic molecules into simpler ones.
- They reproduce by asexual or sexual methods, and produce spores that are dispersed by wind, water, animals, etc.
- They are classified into four major groups based on their spore-producing structures and life cycles:

  - Zygomycetes: They are the fungi that form zygospores, which are thick-walled resting spores that result from the fusion of two compatible hyphae. They include the bread moulds, such as Rhizopus.
