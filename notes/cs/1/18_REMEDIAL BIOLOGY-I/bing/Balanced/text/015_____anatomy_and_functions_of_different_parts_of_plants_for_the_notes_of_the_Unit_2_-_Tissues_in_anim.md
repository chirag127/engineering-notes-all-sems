### Anatomy and Functions of Different Parts of Plants

- Plants are multicellular organisms that have two distinct organ systems: a shoot system and a root system.
- The shoot system consists of the stem, leaves, flowers, fruits and seeds, while the root system consists of the roots and root hairs.
- The stem is the main axis of the plant that supports the leaves and flowers. It also conducts water and minerals from the roots to the leaves, and food from the leaves to the rest of the plant.
- The stem can grow in length (primary growth) or in thickness (secondary growth). The stem has different types of tissues, such as epidermis, cortex, vascular tissue and pith.
- The leaf is the main organ of photosynthesis, the process of converting light energy into chemical energy. The leaf has a flat and thin structure that maximizes the surface area for light absorption.
- The leaf has an epidermis that protects the inner tissues and regulates gas exchange. The leaf also has a mesophyll that contains chlorophyll, the pigment that gives plants their green color and captures light energy. The leaf also has veins that transport water and food.
- The flower is the reproductive organ of flowering plants. It consists of four main parts: sepals, petals, stamens and carpels.
- The sepals are the outermost part of the flower that protect the bud. The petals are the colorful and attractive part of the flower that attract pollinators. The stamens are the male part of the flower that produce pollen, the male gamete. The carpels are the female part of the flower that contain the ovary, the ovule and the stigma.
- The fruit is the part of the flower that develops after fertilization. It protects the seeds and aids in their dispersal. The fruit can be fleshy or dry, depending on the type of plant.
- The seed is the part of the plant that contains the embryo, the endosperm and the seed coat. The embryo is the young plant that will grow into a new plant. The endosperm is the food reserve for the embryo. The seed coat is the protective layer that prevents water loss and infection.
- The root is the part of the plant that anchors the plant to the soil and absorbs water and minerals from the soil. It also stores food and hormones for the plant.
- The root can grow in length (primary growth) or in thickness (secondary growth). The root has different types of tissues, such as epidermis, cortex, endodermis, pericycle, vascular tissue and pith.
- The root hair is the extension of the root epidermis that increases the surface area for water and mineral absorption.
