### Systematic and binomial system of nomenclature

- Systematic is the branch of science that deals with unique properties of species and groups to recognise, describe, name and arrange the diverse organisms according to an organised plan .
- Binomial nomenclature is a formal system of naming species of living things by giving each a name composed of two parts, both of which use Latin grammatical forms, although they can be based on words from other languages.
- The first part of the name is the genus, which is a group of closely related species. The second part is the specific epithet, which is unique to each species within the genus. The two parts are written in italics, with the genus name capitalised and the specific epithet in lowercase. For example, the scientific name of modern humans is Homo sapiens .
- The binomial system of nomenclature was developed by Carl Linnaeus, a Swedish botanist and zoologist, who published a large work, Systema Naturae (The System of Nature), in which he attempted to identify every known plant and animal.
- The advantages of binomial nomenclature are that it avoids confusion, provides a universal language, reflects evolutionary relationships, and allows for easy identification of organisms.
- The rules of binomial nomenclature are governed by the International Code of Zoological Nomenclature (ICZN) for animals and the International Code of Nomenclature for algae, fungi, and plants (ICNafp) for plants. Some of the basic rules are:
  - The scientific name must be unique for each species.
  - The scientific name must be based on Latin or latinised words.
  - The scientific name must be written in italics or underlined.
  - The genus name must be capitalised and the specific epithet must be in lowercase.
  - The genus name can be abbreviated to its first letter, followed by a period, when writing the name of more than one species in the same genus. For example, H. sapiens and H. erectus are both species in the genus Homo.
  - The author and date of publication of the scientific name can be added in parentheses after the name to indicate its origin and validity. For example, Homo sapiens Linnaeus, 1758.
