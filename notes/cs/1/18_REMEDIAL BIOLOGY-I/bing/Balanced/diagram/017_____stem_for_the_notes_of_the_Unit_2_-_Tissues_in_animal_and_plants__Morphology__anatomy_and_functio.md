### Stem

- The stem is the part of the plant that supports the leaves, flowers, and fruits and connects them to the roots.
- The stem also transports water, minerals, and organic nutrients between the roots and the aerial parts of the plant.
- The stem can also store food, water, and other substances in some plants.
- The stem can also perform photosynthesis in some plants that have green stems.

#### Morphology of stem

- The stem has nodes and internodes. Nodes are the points where leaves or branches arise. Internodes are the regions between two nodes.
- The stem also has buds, which are undeveloped shoots that can grow into leaves, flowers, or branches. Buds can be terminal (at the tip of the stem) or axillary (in the axils of the leaves).
- The stem also has scars, which are marks left by fallen leaves, flowers, or fruits. Scars can help identify the age and growth pattern of the stem.

#### Anatomy of stem

- The stem has three types of tissues: epidermis, vascular tissue, and ground tissue.
- The epidermis is the outermost layer of cells that covers and protects the stem. The epidermis may have hairs, glands, or other structures that modify its function.
- The vascular tissue is the tissue that transports water, minerals, and organic nutrients in the stem. The vascular tissue consists of two types of conducting elements: xylem and phloem. Xylem transports water and minerals from the roots to the leaves. Phloem transports organic nutrients from the leaves to the roots or other parts of the plant.
- The ground tissue is the tissue that fills the space between the epidermis and the vascular tissue. The ground tissue consists of three types of cells: parenchyma, collenchyma, and sclerenchyma. Parenchyma cells are thin-walled and versatile. They can perform photosynthesis, storage, or secretion. Collenchyma cells are thick-walled and provide mechanical support to the stem. Sclerenchyma cells are hard and rigid and provide structural strength to the stem.

#### Functions of stem

- The stem performs several functions for the plant, such as:
  - Support: The stem provides a framework for the leaves, flowers, and fruits and helps them to expose to sunlight, air, and pollinators.
  - Conduction: The stem transports water, minerals, and organic nutrients between the roots and the aerial parts of the plant.
  - Storage: The stem can store food, water, and other substances in some plants, such as cacti, potatoes, and onions.
  - Photosynthesis: The stem can perform photosynthesis in some plants that have green stems, such as asparagus, celery, and cactus.
  - Reproduction: The stem can produce new plants by vegetative propagation in some plants, such as strawberry, mint, and ginger.
