# Tissues in animal and plants

- A tissue is a group of similar cells that work together to perform a specific function.
- There are two main types of tissues: animal tissues and plant tissues.
- Animal tissues are classified into four types: epithelial, connective, muscular, and nervous.
- Plant tissues are classified into two types: meristematic and permanent.

## Animal tissues

- Epithelial tissue: covers the body surface and lines the body cavities. It protects, absorbs, secretes, and senses. Examples are skin, lining of mouth, and glands.
- Connective tissue: binds and supports other tissues. It provides structure, strength, and elasticity. Examples are blood, bone, cartilage, and adipose tissue.
- Muscular tissue: consists of cells that can contract and relax. It enables movement of body parts and organs. Examples are skeletal, cardiac, and smooth muscles.
- Nervous tissue: consists of cells that can generate and transmit electrical impulses. It coordinates and controls the activities of the body. Examples are neurons, brain, and spinal cord.

## Plant tissues

- Meristematic tissue: consists of actively dividing cells that are responsible for growth and development. It is found at the tips of roots and stems, and in the cambium layer of woody plants.
- Permanent tissue: consists of cells that have differentiated and lost their ability to divide. It performs various functions such as photosynthesis, storage, transport, and support. Examples are parenchyma, collenchyma, sclerenchyma, xylem, and phloem.

# Morphology, anatomy and functions of different parts of plants: Root, stem, leaf, inflorescence, flower, fruit and seed.

- Morphology is the study of the external form and structure of plants.
- Anatomy is the study of the internal structure and organization of plants.
- Function is the role or purpose of a plant part in relation to the whole plant.

## Root

- The root is the part of the plant that anchors it to the soil and absorbs water and minerals from the soil.
- The root has three main regions: the root cap, the root tip, and the root zone.
- The root cap is a protective layer of cells that covers the root tip and secretes a slimy substance called mucilage that lubricates the root as it grows through the soil.
- The root tip is the region where the root cells are actively dividing and elongating. It contains the apical meristem, which is the source of new root cells.
- The root zone is the region where the root cells differentiate into various tissues and perform their functions. It consists of three main tissues: the epidermis, the cortex, and the vascular cylinder.
- The epidermis is the outermost layer of cells that covers the root. It has hair-like extensions called root hairs that increase the surface area for absorption of water and minerals.
- The cortex is the layer of cells between the epidermis and the vascular cylinder. It stores food and water in the form of starch and provides support to the root.
- The vascular cylinder is the central core of the root that contains the xylem and the phloem. The xylem transports water and minerals from the root to the stem and the leaves. The phloem transports food from the leaves to the root and other parts of the plant. The vascular cylinder is surrounded by a layer of cells called the endodermis, which regulates the movement of substances into and out of the vascular tissue.
