### Evidences for biological evolution

- Biological evolution is the change in the inherited characteristics of populations of organisms over time.
- There are many lines of evidence that support the theory of biological evolution, such as fossils, comparative anatomy, molecular biology, biogeography, and direct observation.
- **Fossils** are the preserved remains or traces of organisms that lived in the past. Fossils show the diversity of life forms that existed in different geological periods, and how some of them are related to modern species. Fossils also provide clues about the environmental conditions and evolutionary processes that shaped life on Earth  .
- **Comparative anatomy** is the study of the similarities and differences in the structures of different organisms. Comparative anatomy can reveal homologous structures, which are features that are similar in different species because they inherited them from a common ancestor, such as the bones of the forelimbs of mammals. Comparative anatomy can also reveal analogous structures, which are features that are similar in different species because they evolved independently to perform the same function, such as the wings of insects and birds .
- **Molecular biology** is the study of the molecules that make up the cells and genes of living organisms. Molecular biology can show the degree of similarity or difference in the DNA sequences, proteins, and metabolic pathways of different species. Molecular biology can also reveal the genetic code, which is the universal language that translates DNA into proteins, and that is shared by all living organisms. Molecular biology can provide evidence for the common ancestry and evolutionary relationships of life forms  .
- **Biogeography** is the study of the distribution and diversity of organisms across different regions and continents. Biogeography can show how the geographical isolation and migration of populations can lead to speciation, which is the formation of new species from existing ones. Biogeography can also show how the climate and geological changes can affect the evolution and extinction of species. Biogeography can provide evidence for the adaptation and diversification of life forms to different environments  .
- **Direct observation** is the witnessing of evolutionary changes in living organisms over short periods of time. Direct observation can show how natural selection, which is the differential survival and reproduction of individuals with certain traits, can lead to changes in the frequency of those traits in a population. Direct observation can also show how artificial selection, which is the intentional breeding of individuals with certain traits, can lead to changes in the characteristics of domesticated species. Direct observation can provide evidence for the variation and inheritance of traits in living organisms  .
