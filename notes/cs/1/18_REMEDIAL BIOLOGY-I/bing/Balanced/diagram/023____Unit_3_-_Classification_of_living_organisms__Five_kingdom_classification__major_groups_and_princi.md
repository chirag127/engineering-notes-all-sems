## Unit 3 - Classification of living organisms

- Classification is the process of grouping organisms based on their similarities and differences.
- Classification helps to organize the diversity of life and to understand the evolutionary relationships among organisms.
- There are different levels of classification, such as domain, kingdom, phylum, class, order, family, genus and species.
- The most widely used system of classification is the five kingdom system, proposed by R.H. Whittaker in 1969.
- The five kingdoms are Monera, Protista, Fungi, Plantae and Animalia.

### Monera

- Monera is the kingdom of prokaryotic organisms, which lack a true nucleus and membrane-bound organelles.
- Monera includes bacteria, cyanobacteria (blue-green algae) and archaebacteria (extremophiles).
- Monera can be classified based on their shape, cell wall, mode of nutrition, oxygen requirement and genetic material.
- Examples of monera are Escherichia coli, Streptococcus, Nostoc and Methanobacterium.

### Protista

- Protista is the kingdom of eukaryotic organisms, which have a true nucleus and membrane-bound organelles, but are not multicellular or specialized.
- Protista includes protozoa, algae, slime molds and water molds.
- Protista can be classified based on their mode of locomotion, mode of nutrition, life cycle and habitat.
- Examples of protista are Amoeba, Paramecium, Euglena, Chlamydomonas and Phytophthora.

### Fungi

- Fungi is the kingdom of eukaryotic organisms, which are heterotrophic, multicellular (except yeasts) and have cell walls made of chitin.
- Fungi include molds, yeasts, mushrooms, lichens and mycorrhizae.
- Fungi can be classified based on their structure, reproduction, spore type and symbiotic relationships.
- Examples of fungi are Rhizopus, Saccharomyces, Agaricus, Penicillium and Lichen.

### Plantae

- Plantae is the kingdom of eukaryotic organisms, which are autotrophic, multicellular and have cell walls made of cellulose.
- Plantae includes bryophytes, pteridophytes, gymnosperms, angiosperms and algae (some consider algae as protists).
- Plantae can be classified based on their vascular tissue, seed type, flower structure, fruit type and life cycle.
- Examples of plantae are Moss, Fern, Pine, Rose and Algae.

### Animalia

- Animalia is the kingdom of eukaryotic organisms, which are heterotrophic, multicellular and lack cell walls.
- Animalia includes sponges, cnidarians, worms, mollusks, arthropods, echinoderms, chordates and vertebrates.
- Animalia can be classified based on their symmetry, body cavity, segmentation, appendages, skeleton and nervous system.
- Examples of animalia are Sponge, Jellyfish, Earthworm, Snail, Butterfly, Starfish, Shark and Human.

## Systematic and binomial system of nomenclature

- Systematic is the branch of biology that deals with the identification, naming and classification of organisms.
- Nomenclature is the system of assigning names to organisms.
- Binomial system of nomenclature is the universal and standardized system of naming organisms, proposed by Carolus Linnaeus in 1758.
- Binomial system of nomenclature uses two names for each organism: the genus name and the species name.
- The genus name is the first part of the name, which indicates the group of closely related species. The genus name is always capitalized and italicized or underlined.
- The species name is the second part of the name, which indicates the specific identity of the organism. The species name is always lowercase and italicized or underlined.
- The binomial name is also followed by the name of the author who first described the organism, usually abbreviated.
- Examples of binomial names are Homo sapiens (L.), Panthera tigris (L.) and Zea mays (L.).
