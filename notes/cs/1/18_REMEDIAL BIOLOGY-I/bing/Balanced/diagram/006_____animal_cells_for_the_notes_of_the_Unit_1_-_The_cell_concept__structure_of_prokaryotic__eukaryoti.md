### Animal cells

- Animal cells are the basic structural and functional units of animal tissues and organs  .
- They are eukaryotic cells, which means they have membrane-bound organelles suspended in the cytoplasm enveloped by a plasma membrane .
- The plasma membrane is a selectively permeable barrier that allows nutrients to enter and waste products to leave the cell.
- The cytoplasm is a gel-like substance that contains water, salts, and various molecules and organelles.
- The organelles are specialized structures that perform different functions for the cell, such as energy production, protein synthesis, digestion, and movement .
- The most common types of animal cells are skin cells, muscle cells, blood cells, nerve cells, and fat cells. Each type has a specific shape and function that suits its role in the body.
- Some of the major organelles in animal cells are:

  - Nucleus: The control center of the cell that contains the genetic material (DNA) and regulates the expression of genes .
  - Mitochondria: The powerhouses of the cell that produce energy (ATP) by breaking down glucose and oxygen in a process called cellular respiration .
  - Ribosomes: The sites of protein synthesis that use the information from the DNA to make proteins that are needed for the cell's functions .
  - Endoplasmic reticulum (ER): A network of membranes that transport and modify proteins and lipids within the cell . There are two types of ER: rough ER, which has ribosomes attached to it, and smooth ER, which does not have ribosomes .
  - Golgi apparatus: A stack of flattened membranes that sort and package proteins and lipids from the ER and send them to other destinations in or outside the cell .
  - Lysosomes: Spherical organelles that contain digestive enzymes that break down unwanted or damaged materials in the cell .
  - Centrosomes: Structures that organize the microtubules, which are protein filaments that form the cytoskeleton and help the cell maintain its shape and move . Centrosomes also play a role in cell division by forming the spindle fibers that separate the chromosomes .
  - Cytoskeleton: A network of protein filaments that provide support and structure to the cell and enable its movement and transport of materials . The cytoskeleton consists of three types of filaments: microtubules, microfilaments, and intermediate filaments .
  - Cilia and flagella: Hair-like or whip-like extensions of the plasma membrane that help the cell move or move fluids over the cell surface . Cilia are short and numerous, while flagella are long and few .
