### Principles of classification in each kingdom

- Classification is the process of grouping living organisms into categories based on their similarities and differences.
- The five kingdom classification system was proposed by R.H. Whittaker in 1969 and is based on the following criteria   :
  - Cellular structure: whether the organism is prokaryotic (lacking a nucleus and membrane-bound organelles) or eukaryotic (having a nucleus and membrane-bound organelles).
  - Body organization: whether the organism is unicellular (consisting of a single cell) or multicellular (consisting of many cells).
  - Mode of nutrition: whether the organism is autotrophic (making its own food) or heterotrophic (obtaining food from other sources).
  - Phylogenetic relationships: the evolutionary history and genetic similarities of the organism.
  - Reproduction: the mode and type of reproduction of the organism.

- The five kingdoms are Monera, Protista, Fungi, Plantae and Animalia     . The main characteristics and examples of each kingdom are:

  - Monera: prokaryotic, unicellular, autotrophic or heterotrophic, asexual reproduction, examples are bacteria and cyanobacteria.
  - Protista: eukaryotic, mostly unicellular, autotrophic or heterotrophic, asexual or sexual reproduction, examples are protozoa, algae and slime molds.
  - Fungi: eukaryotic, mostly multicellular, heterotrophic, asexual or sexual reproduction, examples are mushrooms, molds and yeasts.
  - Plantae: eukaryotic, multicellular, autotrophic, sexual reproduction, examples are mosses, ferns, conifers and flowering plants.
  - Animalia: eukaryotic, multicellular, heterotrophic, sexual reproduction, examples are sponges, worms, insects, fish, amphibians, reptiles, birds and mammals.

- Each kingdom is further divided into smaller groups called phyla (or divisions in plants), classes, orders, families, genera and species. The scientific name of an organism consists of its genus and species, and is called the binomial nomenclature . For example, the scientific name of human is Homo sapiens.
