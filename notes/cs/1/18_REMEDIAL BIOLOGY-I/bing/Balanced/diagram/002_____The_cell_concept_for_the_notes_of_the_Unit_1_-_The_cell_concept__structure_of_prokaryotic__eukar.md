### The cell concept
- The cell concept is the idea that the cell is the fundamental unit of life. All living organisms are composed of one or more cells, and all cells arise from pre-existing cells .
- The cell concept has three main implications:
  - Cells are the smallest entities that can perform all the functions of life, such as metabolism, growth, reproduction, and response to stimuli .
  - Cells have a common origin and share a common set of molecules, such as DNA, RNA, proteins, lipids, and carbohydrates .
  - Cells are diverse in structure and function, depending on their specialization and adaptation to different environments and roles .

### Structure of prokaryotic, eukaryotic cells, plant cells and animal cells
- Prokaryotic cells are cells that lack a nucleus and other membrane-bound organelles. They are usually unicellular and have a simple structure. They include bacteria and archaea .
- Eukaryotic cells are cells that have a nucleus and other membrane-bound organelles. They are usually multicellular and have a complex structure. They include protists, fungi, plants, and animals .
- Plant cells are eukaryotic cells that have a cell wall, a large central vacuole, and plastids, such as chloroplasts, which are involved in photosynthesis. They also have a cytoskeleton, a plasma membrane, a nucleus, and other organelles .
- Animal cells are eukaryotic cells that lack a cell wall, a large central vacuole, and plastids. They have a cytoskeleton, a plasma membrane, a nucleus, and other organelles, such as mitochondria, which are involved in cellular respiration. They also have lysosomes, which are involved in digestion and recycling of cellular materials .

### Structure and function of cell membrane
- The cell membrane is a thin, flexible layer that surrounds the cell and separates it from the external environment. It is composed of a phospholipid bilayer, with hydrophilic heads facing the aqueous solutions inside and outside the cell, and hydrophobic tails facing each other in the middle .
- The cell membrane has several functions, such as :
  - Regulating the passage of substances into and out of the cell, by means of selective permeability, diffusion, osmosis, facilitated diffusion, active transport, endocytosis, and exocytosis.
  - Maintaining the shape and integrity of the cell, by means of the cytoskeleton, which is attached to the membrane proteins.
  - Communicating with other cells, by means of cell surface receptors, which bind to specific molecules or signals from other cells or the environment.
  - Participating in metabolic reactions, by means of membrane enzymes, which catalyze various biochemical processes.
