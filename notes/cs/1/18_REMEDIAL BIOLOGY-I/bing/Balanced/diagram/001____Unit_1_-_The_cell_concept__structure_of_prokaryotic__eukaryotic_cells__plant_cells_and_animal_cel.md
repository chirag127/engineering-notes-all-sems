## Unit 1 - The cell concept, structure of prokaryotic, eukaryotic cells, plant cells and animal cells, Structure and function of cell membrane, cell organelles and their function, Basics of cell signaling, Structure and use of compound microscope, Basic chemical constituents of living body

- The cell concept is the idea that all living organisms are composed of one or more cells, and that cells are the basic units of structure and function in life.
- Prokaryotic cells are cells that lack a nucleus and other membrane-bound organelles. They are usually unicellular and have a simple structure. Examples of prokaryotes are bacteria and archaea.
- Eukaryotic cells are cells that have a nucleus and other membrane-bound organelles. They are usually multicellular and have a complex structure. Examples of eukaryotes are animals, plants, fungi and protists.
- Plant cells and animal cells are both types of eukaryotic cells, but they have some differences. Plant cells have a cell wall, chloroplasts, and a large central vacuole, while animal cells do not. Animal cells have centrioles, lysosomes, and a small or absent vacuole, while plant cells do not.
- The cell membrane is a thin layer of phospholipids and proteins that surrounds the cell and regulates the movement of substances in and out of the cell. It also maintains the cell shape and communicates with other cells.
- Cell organelles are specialized structures within the cell that perform specific functions. Some examples of cell organelles are the nucleus, mitochondria, ribosomes, endoplasmic reticulum, Golgi apparatus, lysosomes, peroxisomes, cytoskeleton, cilia and flagella.
- Cell signaling is the process by which cells communicate with each other and respond to external stimuli. It involves the use of molecules called signals, receptors, and effectors. Signals can be hormones, neurotransmitters, cytokines, or other molecules. Receptors are proteins on the cell membrane or inside the cell that bind to signals. Effectors are molecules or processes that carry out the response to the signal.
- A compound microscope is a device that uses two or more lenses to magnify an object. It consists of an eyepiece, an objective lens, a stage, a light source, and a condenser. The eyepiece and the objective lens form an image of the object, which is then magnified by the eyepiece. The stage holds the object, the light source provides illumination, and the condenser focuses the light on the object.
- The basic chemical constituents of living body are water, organic molecules, and inorganic ions. Water is the most abundant and essential molecule in living organisms, as it provides the medium for chemical reactions, transports substances, and regulates temperature. Organic molecules are molecules that contain carbon and hydrogen, and are the building blocks of life. They include carbohydrates, lipids, proteins, and nucleic acids. Inorganic ions are atoms or molecules that have an electric charge, and are involved in various physiological processes. They include sodium, potassium, calcium, magnesium, chloride, phosphate, bicarbonate, and others.
