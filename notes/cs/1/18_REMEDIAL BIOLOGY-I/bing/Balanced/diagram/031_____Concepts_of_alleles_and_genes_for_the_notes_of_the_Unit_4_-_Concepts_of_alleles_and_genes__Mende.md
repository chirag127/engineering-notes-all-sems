### Concepts of alleles and genes

- Genes are chunks of DNA that contribute to particular traits or functions by coding for proteins that influence physiology .
- Alleles are different versions of a gene, which vary according to the nucleotide base present at a particular genome location .
- An individual's combination of alleles is known as their genotype .
- Bacteria, because they have a single ring of DNA, have one allele per gene per organism. In sexually reproducing organisms, each parent gives an allele for each gene, giving the offspring two alleles per gene.
- Alleles can be dominant or recessive, meaning that they can mask or be masked by the effects of another allele.
- The expression of alleles in the phenotype (the observable characteristics of an organism) depends on the mode of inheritance, which can be complete dominance, incomplete dominance, codominance, multiple alleles, or sex-linked.
- Mendel's experiments with pea plants showed how alleles segregate and assort independently during gamete formation and fertilization, following the principles of segregation and independent assortment.
- Cell cycle is the series of events that lead to cell division and duplication of DNA, resulting in two daughter cells.
- Mitosis is the process of nuclear division in which the chromosomes are separated into two identical sets and distributed to two daughter nuclei.
- Meiosis is the process of nuclear division in which the number of chromosomes is halved and the chromosomes undergo crossing over and independent assortment, resulting in four genetically different daughter nuclei.
- Techniques to study mitosis and meiosis include microscopy, staining, karyotyping, and molecular methods.
- Origin of life is the scientific question of how life emerged from non-living matter on Earth, which involves hypotheses such as abiogenesis, panspermia, and creationism.
- Evidences for biological evolution include fossil records, comparative anatomy, molecular biology, biogeography, and embryology.
- Mechanism of evolution is the explanation of how evolution occurs through natural processes, such as variation, mutation, recombination, natural selection, genetic drift, gene flow, and speciation.
- Variation is the difference in traits among individuals of a population, which can be caused by mutation (changes in DNA sequence) or recombination (shuffling of alleles during meiosis).
- Natural selection is the process by which individuals with certain traits have higher survival and reproduction rates than others, leading to changes in allele frequencies over generations.
- Types of natural selection include directional selection (favoring one extreme trait), stabilizing selection (favoring the intermediate trait), and disruptive selection (favoring both extreme traits).
