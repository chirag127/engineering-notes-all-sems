### Cell cycle

- The cell cycle is an ordered series of events involving cell growth and cell division that produces two new daughter cells  .
- Cells on the path to cell division proceed through a series of precisely timed and carefully regulated stages of growth, DNA replication, and division that produce two genetically identical cells  .
- The cell cycle can be thought of as the life cycle of a cell.
- The cell cycle is sometimes referred to as the “cell division cycle” for that reason.
- New cells are born through the division of their “parent” cell, producing two “daughter” cells from one single “parent” cell.

#### Stages of the cell cycle

- The cell cycle consists of four main stages: G1, S, G2, and M    .
- G1 is the first gap phase, where the cell grows and prepares for DNA replication    .
- S is the synthesis phase, where the cell replicates its DNA and produces two identical copies of each chromosome    .
- G2 is the second gap phase, where the cell checks for DNA damage and repairs any errors before entering mitosis    .
- M is the mitotic phase, where the cell divides its nucleus and cytoplasm into two daughter cells    .
- G1, S, and G2 are collectively called interphase, which is the longest phase of the cell cycle    .
- M is divided into four subphases: prophase, metaphase, anaphase, and telophase    .
- Prophase is the first subphase of mitosis, where the chromosomes condense and the nuclear envelope breaks down    .
- Metaphase is the second subphase of mitosis, where the chromosomes align at the middle of the cell    .
- Anaphase is the third subphase of mitosis, where the sister chromatids separate and move to opposite poles of the cell    .
- Telophase is the fourth and final subphase of mitosis, where the chromosomes decondense and the nuclear envelope reforms    .
- Cytokinesis is the process of dividing the cytoplasm and the cell membrane into two daughter cells    .
- Cytokinesis usually occurs simultaneously with telophase, but it is not considered a part of mitosis    .

#### Regulation of the cell cycle

- The cell cycle is controlled by a complex network of molecular signals and checkpoints that ensure the accuracy and coordination of each stage    .
- The main regulators of the cell cycle are proteins called cyclins and cyclin-dependent kinases (CDKs)    .
