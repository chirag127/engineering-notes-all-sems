## Unit 3 - Classification of living organisms

- Classification is the process of grouping living organisms into categories based on their similarities and differences.
- Classification helps in understanding the diversity of life, the evolutionary relationships among organisms and the ecological roles of different groups of organisms.
- The most widely accepted system of classification is the five kingdom classification, proposed by R.H. Whittaker in 1969.
- The five kingdoms are Monera, Protista, Fungi, Plantae and Animalia.
- Each kingdom is further divided into smaller groups called phyla (or divisions in plants), classes, orders, families, genera and species.
- The smallest and most basic unit of classification is the species, which consists of a group of organisms that can interbreed and produce fertile offspring.
- The scientific name of a species is given by the binomial system of nomenclature, which uses two words: the genus name and the specific epithet.
- The genus name is capitalized and the specific epithet is lowercase. Both words are italicized or underlined. For example, the scientific name of human is Homo sapiens.
- The binomial system of nomenclature was developed by Carolus Linnaeus, a Swedish botanist, in the 18th century.
- Linnaeus also devised a hierarchical system of classification, in which each group of organisms is nested within a larger group, forming a series of ranks.
- The main ranks of classification are kingdom, phylum, class, order, family, genus and species. There are also intermediate ranks, such as subphylum, subclass, suborder, etc.
- The principles of classification in each kingdom are based on the morphological, anatomical, physiological, biochemical, genetic and molecular characteristics of the organisms.
- The concept of animal and plant classification is the division of the kingdoms Animalia and Plantae into smaller groups based on their common features and evolutionary history.
- The kingdom Animalia includes all multicellular, heterotrophic, eukaryotic organisms that lack cell walls and chlorophyll. They are divided into about 35 phyla, such as Porifera, Cnidaria, Platyhelminthes, Nematoda, Annelida, Mollusca, Arthropoda, Echinodermata, Chordata, etc.
- The kingdom Plantae includes all multicellular, autotrophic, eukaryotic organisms that have cell walls and chlorophyll. They are divided into about 12 divisions, such as Bryophyta, Pteridophyta, Gymnospermae, Angiospermae, etc.
