### Morphology

Morphology is the study of the physical form and external structure of plants. It is useful for the identification and classification of plants based on their similarities and differences. Morphology covers both the vegetative and reproductive parts of plants, such as roots, stems, leaves, flowers, fruits and seeds. Morphology also examines the development, variation and evolution of plant forms and structures.

Some of the main topics of plant morphology are:

- **Root**: The root is the part of the plant that grows underground and anchors the plant to the soil. It also absorbs water and minerals from the soil and transports them to the stem. Roots can be classified into two types: taproot and fibrous root. Taproot is a single, thick and main root that grows vertically downwards. Fibrous root is a network of thin and branched roots that spread horizontally. Examples of plants with taproot are carrot, radish and beet. Examples of plants with fibrous root are grass, wheat and rice.

- **Stem**: The stem is the part of the plant that grows above the ground and supports the leaves, flowers and fruits. It also conducts water and nutrients from the root to the leaves and food from the leaves to the rest of the plant. Stems can be classified into two types: herbaceous and woody. Herbaceous stems are soft, green and flexible. They usually die after one or two seasons. Woody stems are hard, brown and rigid. They usually live for many years and form the trunk and branches of trees and shrubs.

- **Leaf**: The leaf is the part of the plant that is mainly responsible for photosynthesis, the process of making food from light, water and carbon dioxide. Leaves also help in transpiration, the process of losing water vapor from the plant surface. Leaves can be classified into two types: simple and compound. Simple leaves have a single blade attached to a stalk called petiole. Compound leaves have more than one blade attached to a common petiole. Examples of plants with simple leaves are mango, guava and rose. Examples of plants with compound leaves are neem, tamarind and mimosa.

- **Inflorescence**: The inflorescence is the part of the plant that bears the flowers. It is a modified branch or stem that has a specific arrangement of flowers. Inflorescences can be classified into two types: racemose and cymose. Racemose inflorescences have flowers that open from the base to the apex of the axis. Cymose inflorescences have flowers that open from the apex to the base of the axis. Examples of plants with racemose inflorescences are mustard, radish and lily. Examples of plants with cymose inflorescences are jasmine, cotton and ixora.

- **Flower**: The flower is the part of the plant that is involved in sexual reproduction. It is a modified shoot that has four whorls of organs: sepals, petals, stamens and carpels. Sepals are the outermost whorl that protect the flower bud. Petals are the next whorl that attract pollinators. Stamens are the male reproductive organs that produce pollen grains. Carpels are the female reproductive organs that contain ovules. Flowers can be classified into two types: complete and incomplete. Complete flowers have all the four whorls of organs. Incomplete flowers lack one or more whorls of organs. Examples of plants with complete flowers are rose, hibiscus and sunflower. Examples of plants with incomplete flowers are maize, grass and papaya.

- **Fruit**: The fruit is the part of the plant that develops from the fertilized ovary of the flower. It contains the seeds and protects them from predators and harsh environments. Fruits can be classified into two types: simple and complex. Simple fruits are formed from a single ovary of a single flower. Complex fruits are formed from more than one ovary of a single flower or from the ovaries of more than one flower. Examples of plants with simple fruits are mango, apple and tomato. Examples of plants with complex fruits are pineapple, fig and mulberry.
