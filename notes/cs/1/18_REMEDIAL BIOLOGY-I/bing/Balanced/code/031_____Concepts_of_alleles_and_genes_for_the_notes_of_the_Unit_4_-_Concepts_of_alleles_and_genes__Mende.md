### Concepts of alleles and genes

- Genes are segments of DNA that code for proteins and determine the traits or functions of an organism .
- Alleles are different versions of a gene that have different nucleotide sequences and may result in different phenotypes  .
- An organism's genotype is the combination of alleles it inherits from its parents .
- An organism's phenotype is the observable expression of its genotype, influenced by both genetic and environmental factors.
- In sexually reproducing organisms, each parent contributes one allele for each gene to the offspring.
- In diploid organisms, each gene has two alleles, one from each parent.
- In haploid organisms, such as bacteria, each gene has one allele per organism.
- Alleles can be dominant or recessive, meaning that they can mask or be masked by another allele in determining the phenotype.
- Alleles can also be co-dominant or incomplete dominant, meaning that they can both contribute to the phenotype in different ways.
- The frequency and distribution of alleles in a population can change over time due to various evolutionary forces, such as mutation, recombination, natural selection, genetic drift, and gene flow.
