### Variation

- Variation is the difference between individuals of the same species, caused by genetic and environmental factors .
- Variation can be classified into two types: continuous and discontinuous.
  - Continuous variation is when the data come in a range, such as height, weight, or skin color. There are no distinct categories or groups for these traits.
  - Discontinuous variation is when the data come in groups, such as blood type, eye color, or tongue rolling. There are distinct categories or groups for these traits.
- Variation is important for the survival and evolution of species, as it provides the raw material for natural selection .
- Variation can arise from two sources: genetic and environmental.
  - Genetic variation is the result of different alleles or forms of genes that individuals inherit from their parents. Genetic variation can be increased by mutation and recombination during meiosis.
  - Environmental variation is the result of different factors that affect the expression of genes, such as temperature, nutrition, or exposure to chemicals. Environmental variation can be modified by acclimation or adaptation.
