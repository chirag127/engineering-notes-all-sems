# Stem

- The stem is an **axial organ** of the shoot system.
- The main functions of the stem are to **support and elevate** the leaves, fruits, and flowers; to **transport** water and nutrients; to **photosynthesize** and **store** food .
- The stem has a **radial structure**, with no root hairs and continuous growth.
- The stem bears **nodes** and **internodes**. Nodes are the regions where leaves are born, while internodes are the portions between two nodes.
- The stem has three types of tissues: **dermal**, **vascular**, and **ground**  .
  - Dermal tissue covers and protects the stem. It consists of one layer of cells called the **epidermis**, which has a waxy **cuticle** to limit water loss .
  - Vascular tissue conducts water and nutrients throughout the plant. It consists of two types of tissues: **xylem** and **phloem**. Xylem transports water and minerals from the roots to the leaves, while phloem transports sugars and organic molecules from the leaves to the roots .
  - Ground tissue fills the space between the dermal and vascular tissues. It consists of three types of cells: **parenchyma**, **collenchyma**, and **sclerenchyma**. Parenchyma cells are the most common and versatile cells, involved in photosynthesis, storage, and secretion. Collenchyma cells provide flexible support to the stem, especially in young and growing parts. Sclerenchyma cells provide rigid support and strength to the stem, especially in mature and woody parts  .
- The stem can be classified into two types based on the presence or absence of secondary growth: **herbaceous** and **woody**.
  - Herbaceous stems have only primary growth, which means they increase in length but not in thickness. They have a simple structure, with a single layer of epidermis, a ring of vascular bundles, and a central pith of ground tissue.
  - Woody stems have both primary and secondary growth, which means they increase in both length and thickness. They have a complex structure, with multiple layers of tissues, such as cork, cork cambium, phloem, vascular cambium, xylem, and pith.
