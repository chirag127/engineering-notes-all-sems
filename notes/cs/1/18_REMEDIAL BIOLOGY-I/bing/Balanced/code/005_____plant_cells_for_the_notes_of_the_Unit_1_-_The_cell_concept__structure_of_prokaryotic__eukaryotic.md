### Plant Cells

Plant cells are the basic units of life in plants. They are eukaryotic cells, which means they have a true nucleus and membrane-bound organelles. Plant cells have some distinctive features that differentiate them from animal cells, such as:

- A cell wall that surrounds the cell membrane and provides shape and support. The cell wall is mainly composed of cellulose, a polysaccharide that forms long, rigid fibers  .
- A large central vacuole that occupies most of the cell volume and stores water, salts, sugars, and other substances. The central vacuole also helps maintain the cell's turgor pressure and rigidity   .
- Chloroplasts that are the sites of photosynthesis, the process of converting light energy into chemical energy. Chloroplasts contain the green pigment chlorophyll, which absorbs light and drives the synthesis of organic molecules from carbon dioxide and water   .
- Plasmodesmata that are small channels that connect adjacent plant cells and allow the exchange of molecules and signals. Plasmodesmata help coordinate the activities of plant cells and tissues  .

Plant cells also have other common organelles that are found in most eukaryotic cells, such as:

- A nucleus that contains the genetic material (DNA) and controls the cell's activities. The nucleus is surrounded by a double membrane called the nuclear envelope, which has pores that allow the passage of molecules in and out  .
- A cytoplasm that is the fluid-filled space inside the cell, where various chemical reactions take place. The cytoplasm contains the cytoskeleton, a network of protein filaments that provide structure and movement to the cell  .
- A cell membrane that is a thin layer of phospholipids and proteins that separates the cell from its environment. The cell membrane regulates the transport of substances in and out of the cell and communicates with other cells  .
- Mitochondria that are the organelles that produce energy for the cell. Mitochondria use oxygen and glucose to generate ATP, the universal energy currency of the cell. Mitochondria have their own DNA and can divide independently of the nucleus   .
- Ribosomes that are the organelles that synthesize proteins. Ribosomes are composed of RNA and proteins and can be found either free in the cytoplasm or attached to the endoplasmic reticulum (ER), a network of membranes that transports and modifies proteins   .
- A Golgi apparatus that is a stack of flattened membranes that sorts and packages proteins and other molecules for secretion or delivery to other organelles. The Golgi apparatus also produces some polysaccharides that are used to make the cell wall   .
- Lysosomes that are the organelles that digest and recycle waste materials and foreign invaders. Lysosomes contain enzymes that break down macromolecules into smaller units that can be reused or expelled by the cell   .
- Peroxisomes that are the organelles that detoxify harmful substances and produce hydrogen peroxide, which is then converted into water and oxygen by the enzyme catalase. Peroxisomes also participate in some metabolic pathways, such as fatty acid oxidation and photorespiration  .

Plant cells can be classified into different types based on their functions and locations, such as:

- Parenchyma cells that are the most common and versatile type of plant cells. They have thin cell walls and large vacuoles and can perform various functions, such as photosynthesis, storage, secretion, and wound healing  .
