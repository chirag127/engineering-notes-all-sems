### Cell cycle
- The cell cycle is an ordered series of events involving cell growth and cell division that produces two new daughter cells  .
- The cell cycle can be divided into two major phases: interphase and mitotic phase  .
- Interphase is the longest phase of the cell cycle, where the cell prepares for division by growing, replicating its DNA, and checking for errors  .
- Interphase consists of three subphases: G1, S, and G2  .
  - G1 (gap 1) phase: the cell grows in size and synthesizes proteins and organelles  .
  - S (synthesis) phase: the cell replicates its DNA and produces two identical copies of each chromosome  .
  - G2 (gap 2) phase: the cell continues to grow and prepares for mitosis by producing microtubules and other components  .
- Mitotic phase is the shortest phase of the cell cycle, where the cell divides its nucleus and cytoplasm into two daughter cells  .
- Mitotic phase consists of two subphases: mitosis and cytokinesis  .
  - Mitosis is the process of nuclear division, where the chromosomes are separated and distributed to the daughter nuclei  .
  - Mitosis can be further divided into four stages: prophase, metaphase, anaphase, and telophase  .
    - Prophase: the chromosomes condense and become visible, the nuclear envelope breaks down, and the spindle apparatus forms  .
    - Metaphase: the chromosomes align at the equator of the cell, attached to the spindle fibers  .
    - Anaphase: the sister chromatids separate and move to the opposite poles of the cell  .
    - Telophase: the chromosomes decondense and become invisible, the nuclear envelope reforms, and the spindle apparatus disassembles  .
  - Cytokinesis is the process of cytoplasmic division, where the cell membrane pinches in and separates the two daughter cells  .
- The cell cycle is regulated by a complex network of proteins and signals that ensure the accuracy and timing of each event  .
- The cell cycle checkpoints are the main mechanisms of regulation, where the cell monitors its internal and external conditions before proceeding to the next phase  .
- The cell cycle checkpoints are located at the end of G1, the end of G2, and the middle of mitosis (M checkpoint)  .
  - G1 checkpoint: the cell checks for sufficient nutrients, growth factors, DNA damage, and cell size  .
  - G2 checkpoint: the cell checks for DNA replication completion, DNA damage, and cell size  .
  - M checkpoint: the cell checks for proper chromosome alignment and attachment to the spindle fibers  .
- The cell cycle regulators are the proteins that control the cell cycle checkpoints and the transitions between phases  .
