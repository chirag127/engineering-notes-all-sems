## Unit 2 - Tissues in animal and plants, Morphology, anatomy and functions of different parts of plants: Root, stem, leaf, inflorescence, flower, fruit and seed.

Unit 2: Tissues in Animal and Plant, Morphology, Anatomy and Functions of Different Parts of Plants

Tissues are groups of similar cells that perform a specific function in an organism. There are two main types of tissues in animals and plants: Meristematic and Permanent. Meristematic tissues are responsible for growth and development, while permanent tissues perform specific functions such as support, transportation, and storage.

In plants, the different parts of the plant have specific morphological and anatomical features that serve specific functions. The following are the different parts of a plant and their functions:

1. Root: The root anchors the plant in the soil and absorbs water and nutrients from the soil.

2. Stem: The stem provides support for the plant and transports water, nutrients, and sugars between the roots and the leaves.

3. Leaf: The leaf is the main site of photosynthesis in the plant and is responsible for producing energy through the process of photosynthesis.

4. Inflorescence: The inflorescence is a cluster of flowers on a stem.

5. Flower: The flower is the reproductive structure of the plant and is responsible for producing seeds.

6. Fruit: The fruit is the mature ovary of the flower and contains seeds.

7. Seed: The seed is the reproductive structure of the plant and contains the embryo plant.

In conclusion, the different parts of a plant have specific morphological and anatomical features that serve specific functions. Understanding the anatomy and functions of these parts is important for understanding the biology of plants.
