Basics of Cell Signaling:
- Cell signaling is the process by which cells communicate with each other and respond to changes in their environment.
- Signaling occurs through the transfer of signals from one cell to another, often through the release of signaling molecules such as hormones, growth factors, and neurotransmitters.
- These signaling molecules bind to specific receptors on the target cell, triggering a series of events that lead to a cellular response.
- Cell signaling can occur between cells in close proximity (paracrine signaling) or between cells that are far apart (endocrine signaling).
- Signaling pathways can be simple or complex, involving multiple signaling molecules, receptors, and intermediate signaling proteins.
- The specificity of cell signaling is maintained through the use of specific receptors and signaling molecules, as well as the regulation of signaling pathways by negative feedback mechanisms.
- Cell signaling plays a crucial role in many biological processes, including development, growth, and the regulation of cellular functions such as metabolism, division, and death.
