Structure and function of cell membrane:
- The cell membrane, also known as the plasma membrane, is a thin, semi-permeable layer that surrounds the cell and separates the interior of the cell from its external environment.
- The cell membrane is composed of a lipid bilayer, which consists of two layers of phospholipid molecules.
- Phospholipids have a hydrophobic (water-fearing) tail and a hydrophilic (water-loving) head, which allows them to form a stable barrier that separates the interior of the cell from the outside.
- The lipid bilayer is fluid and allows for the movement of lipids and small molecules, but acts as a barrier to larger molecules and ions.
- The cell membrane also contains proteins, which serve various functions such as transport of molecules across the membrane, cell-cell communication, and recognition.
- The fluid mosaic model describes the structure of the cell membrane as a fluid mixture of lipids and proteins that move and change shape.
- The cell membrane is selectively permeable, meaning that it allows some substances to pass through while others are blocked. This is important for maintaining the proper balance of ions and other substances inside the cell.
- The cell membrane also plays a role in cell signaling, as changes in the membrane can initiate signals that lead to changes in the cell's behavior.
