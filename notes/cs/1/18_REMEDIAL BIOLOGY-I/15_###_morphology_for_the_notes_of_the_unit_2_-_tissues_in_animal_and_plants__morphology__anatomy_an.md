### Morphology for the notes of the Unit 2 - Tissues in animal and plants, Morphology, anatomy and functions of different parts of plants: Root, stem, leaf, inflorescence, flower, fruit and seed. in the subject of REMEDIAL BIOLOGY-I KCS

Morphology is the study of the form and structure of living organisms and their parts. In the context of plants, morphology refers to the study of the form and structure of different parts of plants, such as roots, stems, leaves, inflorescences, flowers, fruits, and seeds.

1. Root: The root is the part of the plant that is responsible for anchoring the plant in the soil and absorbing water and nutrients. Roots also store food and energy for the plant.

2. Stem: The stem is the part of the plant that supports the leaves, flowers, and fruits. The stem also transports water, nutrients, and sugars between the roots and the rest of the plant.

3. Leaf: The leaf is the part of the plant that is responsible for photosynthesis, the process by which plants make food using energy from sunlight. Leaves also help regulate water loss and temperature in the plant.

4. Inflorescence: An inflorescence is a cluster of flowers on a plant. Inflorescences are important for attracting pollinators and reproducing the plant.

5. Flower: The flower is the reproductive structure of the plant. Flowers contain the male and female reproductive organs and are important for sexual reproduction in plants.

6. Fruit: The fruit is the structure that develops from the ovary of a flower and contains the seeds of the plant. Fruits are important for protecting and dispersing the seeds of the plant.

7. Seed: The seed is the reproductive structure that contains the embryo plant and the food supply for the developing plant. Seeds are important for the reproduction and dispersal of plants.

In conclusion, the study of the morphology of plants is important for understanding the form and structure of different parts of plants and how they function to support the survival and reproduction of the plant.
