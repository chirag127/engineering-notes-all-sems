Five Kingdom Classification:
- The Five Kingdom Classification system is a system of classification used to categorize living organisms into five broad categories: Monera, Protista, Fungi, Plantae, and Animalia.
- Monera includes single-celled organisms such as bacteria, which lack a nucleus and other membrane-bound organelles.
- Protista includes single-celled organisms with a nucleus, such as algae and protozoa.
- Fungi are multicellular organisms that lack chlorophyll and obtain their nutrients by absorbing organic matter.
- Plantae includes multicellular, photosynthetic organisms that have cell walls and a variety of specialized structures such as roots, stems, and leaves.
- Animalia includes multicellular, heterotrophic organisms that lack cell walls and obtain their nutrients by consuming other organisms.
- This system of classification is based on morphological, physiological, and evolutionary relationships between organisms.
- The Five Kingdom Classification system is useful for organizing and understanding the diversity of life on Earth, and provides a framework for further study and classification of individual species.
