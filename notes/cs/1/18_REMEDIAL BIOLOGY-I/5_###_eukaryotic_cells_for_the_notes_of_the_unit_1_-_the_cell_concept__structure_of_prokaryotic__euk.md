Eukaryotic cells are cells that contain a nucleus and other membrane-bound organelles. They are found in all multicellular organisms, including animals, plants, fungi, and protists.

1. Nucleus: The nucleus is a membrane-bound organelle that contains the cell's genetic material, DNA. It is responsible for controlling the cell's activities and ensuring that the cell functions properly.

2. Membrane-bound Organelles: Eukaryotic cells contain many other membrane-bound organelles, including mitochondria, which produce energy for the cell, and the endoplasmic reticulum and Golgi apparatus, which are involved in protein synthesis and modification.

3. Cytoskeleton: The cytoskeleton is a network of protein fibers that provides structure and support to the cell. It helps the cell maintain its shape and also plays a role in cell division and movement.

4. Vacuoles: Vacuoles are membrane-bound organelles that store water, nutrients, and waste products. They are particularly important in plant cells, where they help to maintain the cell's shape and support the plant.

5. Chloroplasts: Chloroplasts are membrane-bound organelles that are unique to plant cells. They contain chlorophyll and are responsible for photosynthesis, the process by which plants produce energy from sunlight.

6. Size: Eukaryotic cells are generally larger than prokaryotic cells, with some reaching up to 100 micrometers in diameter.

7. Complexity: Eukaryotic cells are also more complex than prokaryotic cells, with more specialized structures and functions. This complexity allows eukaryotic cells to carry out a wider range of functions, including growth, reproduction, and response to stimuli.

8. Evolution: Eukaryotic cells are thought to have evolved from a symbiotic relationship between primitive prokaryotic cells. This relationship allowed the cells to specialize and become more complex, leading to the evolution of eukaryotic cells.
