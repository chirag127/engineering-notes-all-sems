Major Groups in Five Kingdom Classification:
- The five kingdom classification system is a system for categorizing living organisms into five broad groups based on their characteristics.
- The five kingdoms are Monera (prokaryotes), Protista (single-celled eukaryotes), Fungi, Plantae (plants), and Animalia (animals).
- Each kingdom is further divided into smaller groups based on additional characteristics.
- Monera includes bacteria, which are single-celled organisms without a nucleus.
- Protista includes single-celled eukaryotes, such as algae and protozoa.
- Fungi are multicellular organisms that are decomposers and feed on dead organic matter.
- Plantae includes all plants, from mosses and ferns to flowering plants.
- Animalia includes all animals, from invertebrates such as sponges and insects to vertebrates such as fish, birds, and mammals.
- The five kingdom classification system is a useful tool for organizing and understanding the diversity of life on Earth.
