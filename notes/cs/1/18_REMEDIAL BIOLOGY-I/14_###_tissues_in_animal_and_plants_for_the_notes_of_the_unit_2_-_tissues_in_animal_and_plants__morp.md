Unit 2: Tissues in Animal and Plants, Morphology, Anatomy and Functions of Different Parts of Plants: Root, Stem, Leaf, Inflorescence, Flower, Fruit, and Seed in the subject of Remedial Biology-I KCS

Tissues are groups of similar cells that perform a specific function in an organism. There are two main types of tissues in animal and plants: Meristematic tissues and Permanent tissues.

Meristematic tissues are actively dividing cells that are responsible for growth and development. They are found in the root tips, shoot tips, and buds of plants.

Permanent tissues are mature cells that have ceased dividing and perform specific functions such as transport, support, and protection. There are three main types of permanent tissues in plants: Epidermal tissue, Ground tissue, and Vascular tissue.

The root, stem, leaf, inflorescence, flower, fruit, and seed are different parts of a plant that have specific morphological, anatomical, and functional characteristics.

The root is the part of the plant that absorbs water and nutrients from the soil. The stem provides support and transport of water and nutrients from the roots to the leaves. The leaves are the main site of photosynthesis in the plant. The inflorescence is a group of flowers that are arranged in a specific pattern. The flower is the reproductive structure of a plant that contains the male and female reproductive organs. The fruit is the mature ovary of a flower that contains the seeds. The seed is the reproductive structure of a plant that contains the embryo and the food supply.

In conclusion, tissues play an important role in the anatomy and function of animals and plants. Understanding the different types of tissues and the morphological, anatomical, and functional characteristics of different parts of plants is an important aspect of the subject of Remedial Biology-I KCS.
