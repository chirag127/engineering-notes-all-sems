
### Structure of Prokaryotic Cells 

- Prokaryotic cells are the simplest form of life and are characterized by the absence of a nucleus. 
- They are typically 10 to 100 times smaller than eukaryotic cells and have a simpler internal structure. 
- Prokaryotic cells have a cell wall, which provides them with structural support, and a cell membrane, which separates the internal components from the external environment. 
- The cell membrane is composed of a phospholipid bilayer and is responsible for regulating the passage of substances into and out of the cell. 
- Prokaryotic cells also contain ribosomes, which are responsible for protein synthesis, and a single circular chromosome, which contains the genetic material. 
- In addition, prokaryotic cells may contain plasmids, which are small circular pieces of DNA that can replicate independently of the main chromosome. 
- Prokaryotic cells are typically unicellular but can also form multicellular structures. 
- They reproduce asexually through a process known as binary fission.
