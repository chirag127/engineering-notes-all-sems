
### Structure and Use of Compound Microscope

A compound microscope is a powerful tool used in biology to observe and study cells and other small structures. It uses a combination of lenses to magnify the image of a specimen.

1. The microscope has two lenses: an objective lens and an eyepiece lens. The objective lens is the lens closest to the specimen, while the eyepiece lens is the lens closest to the viewer.

2. The magnification of the microscope is determined by the combination of the objective and eyepiece lenses. The magnification of the microscope is equal to the magnification of the objective lens multiplied by the magnification of the eyepiece lens.

3. To focus the microscope, the objective lens is adjusted to bring the image into focus. The eyepiece lens is then adjusted to sharpen the image.

4. The microscope can be used to observe the structure of prokaryotic, eukaryotic, plant and animal cells. It can also be used to observe the structure and function of cell membranes, cell organelles and their functions, and the basics of cell signaling.

5. The microscope can also be used to observe the basic chemical constituents of living bodies, such as proteins, carbohydrates, lipids, and nucleic acids.
