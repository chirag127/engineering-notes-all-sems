

### Mendelian Experiments 

* Mendelian experiments are a set of experiments conducted by Gregor Mendel in the mid-19th century to study the inheritance of traits in pea plants. 
* Mendel used selective breeding techniques to crossbreed pea plants with different traits, such as flower color, seed shape, and pod color, and observed the patterns of inheritance of these traits. 
* He found that traits were inherited in a predictable manner and that they could be explained by the presence of two factors, which he called alleles. 
* He also showed that the alleles of a trait could be dominant or recessive and that the inheritance of a trait was determined by the combination of alleles present in the organism. 
* His experiments laid the foundation for the study of genetics and the understanding of how traits are inherited. 

### Concepts of Alleles and Genes 

* Alleles are alternative forms of a gene. They are responsible for the different phenotypes (the physical characteristics) of an organism. 
* Genes are the units of heredity, which are passed from parent to offspring. They are composed of DNA and are located on chromosomes. 
* Genes are responsible for the expression of traits, such as eye color, hair color, and height, and for the production of proteins and enzymes, which are necessary for the functioning of the organism. 

### Cell Cycle (Elementary Idea)

* The cell cycle is the sequence of events that occur in a cell from the time it is formed until it divides. 
* It consists of four main stages: interphase, mitosis, cytokinesis, and gap phases. 
* During interphase, the cell grows and prepares for division. During mitosis, the cell divides into two daughter cells. During cytokinesis, the cytoplasm and organelles are divided between the two daughter cells. Finally, during the gap phases, the cells prepare for the next round of cell division. 

### Mitosis and Meiosis 

* Mitosis is the process of cell division in which a single cell divides into two identical daughter cells. 
* Meiosis is a specialized form of cell division that produces gametes (sex cells). 
* During meiosis, the number of chromosomes is reduced by half, and the resulting cells have only one copy of each chromosome. 

### Techniques to Study Mitosis and Meiosis 

* To study mitosis and meiosis, scientists use a variety of techniques, such as microscopy, cell staining, and genetic sequencing. 
* Microscopy allows scientists to observe the stages of cell division under a microscope. 
* Cell staining techniques, such as fluorescent in situ hybridization (FISH) and immunofluorescence, allow scientists to visualize specific molecules or structures in the cells. 
* Genetic sequencing techniques, such as PCR, allow scientists to analyze the genetic makeup of cells. 

### Origin of Life 

* The origin of life is a scientific mystery. Scientists believe that life on Earth began more than 3.5 billion years ago, but the exact circumstances of its origin are still unknown. 
* Some theories suggest that life may have originated from organic molecules that were present on the early Earth, while other theories propose that life may have been brought to Earth from outer space. 

### Evidences for Biological Evolution 

* Biological evolution is the process by which organisms change over time. 
* There is a wealth of evidence to support the theory of biological evolution, including the fossil record, comparative anatomy, comparative embryology, and molecular biology. 
* The fossil record shows the gradual change in organisms over time, while comparative anatomy and embryology demonstrate the similarities between organisms. Molecular biology shows that all organisms share a common ancestor. 

### Mechanism of Evolution −Variation (Mutation and Recombination) 

* Variation is the basis of evolution. Variation is produced by mutation and recombination. 
* Mutations are random changes in the DNA of an organism that can result in new traits. 
* Recombination is the process by which genetic material is shuffled between two parents during reproduction. 
* These two processes create new combinations of alleles, which can lead to the emergence of new traits. 

### Natural Selection with Examples 

* Natural selection is the process by which organisms with advantageous traits become more common in a population over time. 
* Natural selection acts on the variation that is present in a population, favoring those individuals that are better adapted to their environment. 
* For example, in a population of gazelles, those individuals that can run faster will have an advantage in escaping predators and will be more likely to survive and reproduce.
