
## Unit 1 - The Cell Concept

* Cells are the fundamental units of life and are found in all living organisms. 
* Prokaryotic cells are the simplest form of cells and are found in bacteria and archaea. They are smaller than eukaryotic cells and lack a nucleus and membrane-bound organelles. 
* Eukaryotic cells are more complex and are found in plants, animals, fungi, and protists. They have a membrane-bound nucleus and organelles. 
* Plant cells and animal cells are both eukaryotic cells but have distinct differences. Plant cells have a cell wall, chloroplasts, and a large central vacuole, whereas animal cells lack these structures. 
* The cell membrane is a semi-permeable barrier that separates the cell from its environment. It is composed of a phospholipid bilayer and contains proteins that facilitate transport of molecules in and out of the cell. 
* Cell organelles are specialized structures within the cell that perform specific functions. Examples include the nucleus, mitochondria, ribosomes, and endoplasmic reticulum. 
* Cell signaling is the process by which cells communicate with each other and their environment. It is essential for the coordination of cell activities and development of multicellular organisms. 
* A compound microscope is an instrument used to view small objects. It uses lenses to magnify the image and can be used to observe cells and other small structures. 
* Living organisms are composed of four major classes of molecules: carbohydrates, lipids, proteins, and nucleic acids. These molecules are essential for the functioning of the cell.
