### Techniques to study mitosis and meiosis

1. Microscopy
- Light microscopy: Cells are viewed using visible light. Prepared slides of dividing cells are observed to study mitosis and meiosis. Chromosomes and cellular changes can be observed. Limitation: Resolution is limited to 0.2 micrometers. Cannot see fine details.
- Electron microscopy: Uses beams of electrons rather than light to view cells. Provides higher magnification and resolution to study cellular details. Chromosomes, spindle fibres and other organelles can be seen in detail. Limitation: Sample preparation is complex. Not suitable for live cells.

2. Staining techniques
- Chromosome staining: Cells are treated with staining dyes such as Giemsa stain, carmine stain to colour the chromosomes for clear observation.
- Fluorescence microscopy: Fluorescent dyes and probes are used to stain specific cell components. Fluorescent dye helps to view the stained components under fluorescent microscope clearly. Useful to view dynamic changes in living cells. Limitation: Expensive and trained personnel required.

3. Cell synchronization
- The cell cycle of a population of cells is synchronized so that majority of cells are in the same stage of cell cycle. This helps to study specific stages of mitosis and meiosis.
- Common methods: Thymidine block, colchicine treatment, lactose depletion. Limitation: The process of synchronization may affect the normal behavior of cells.

4. Biochemical assays
- Biochemical techniques are used to study enzymes and proteins involved in cell cycle processes. For example, assays to study the activity of cyclin-CDK complexes and isolation of microtubule proteins involved in spindle formation.
- Helps understand the molecular mechanisms underlying mitosis and meiosis. Limitation: Complex techniques requiring technical expertise and specialized laboratory equipment.
