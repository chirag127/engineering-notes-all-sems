### Basics of cell signaling

- Cell signaling refers to the process by which cells communicate with each other and respond to the surroundings. It is crucial for the proper functioning and coordination of cells.
- Cell signaling involves the transmission of molecular signals from one cell to another via direct contact or through secreted molecules. These signals are received and converted into cellular responses via signal transduction pathways.
- The basic steps in cell signaling are:
	1. Signal reception: The signaling molecules bind to receptor proteins on the cell surface or within the cell.
	2. Signal transduction: The signal is transduced into the cell through a signaling pathway. This often involves a cascade of biochemical reactions inside the cell.
	3. Cellular response: The signal is amplified and triggers a cellular response, such as changes in metabolism, gene expression, or cell division.
- There are several types of signals involved in cell signaling:
	- Molecular signals: Includes neurotransmitters, hormones, cytokines, growth factors, etc.
	- Adhesion molecules: Facilitate cell-cell contact. For example, integrins.
	- Extracellular matrix: Provides mechanical and biochemical support to cells. Various molecules in the matrix can signal to cells.
