### Mitosis and Meiosis

- Mitosis: Cell division resulting in two identical daughter cells each having the same number of chromosomes as the parent cell. Purpose is growth, asexual reproduction, and cell repair.
- Meiosis: Cell division resulting in four cells each with half the number of chromosomes as the parent cell. Purpose is production of gametes (eggs and sperm) for sexual reproduction.
- Differences between mitosis and meiosis:
  - Number of divisions: Mitosis has one round of division, meiosis has two rounds resulting in four cells.
  - Number of chromosomes: Daughter cells from mitosis have the same number of chromosomes as parent cell. Daughter cells from meiosis have half the number of chromosomes as parent cell.
  - Pairing of chromosomes: In meiosis, homologous chromosomes pair up and undergo crossover to produce genetic variation. No pairing in mitosis.
- Techniques to study mitosis and meiosis: Microscopy (light, electron), staining (e.g. aceto-orcein, Giemsa, hematoxylin), cell culture, biochemical assays.
- Origin of life and evolution: The origin of first life from inorganic matter and its subsequent evolution over time resulting in biodiversity is explained by scientific theories such as chemical evolution, panspermia, abiogenesis, and Darwinian evolution. Evidence for evolution includes fossil records, comparative anatomy, molecular biology, biogeography, etc.
- Mechanism of evolution: Mutation and recombination lead to variations in a population. Natural selection acts on this variation resulting in adaptation and speciation. Types of natural selection include directional, stabilizing, and disruptive selection.
