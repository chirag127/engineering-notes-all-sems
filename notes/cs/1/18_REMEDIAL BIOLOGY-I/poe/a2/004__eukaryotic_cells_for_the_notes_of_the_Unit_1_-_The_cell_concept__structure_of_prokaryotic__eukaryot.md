### Eukaryotic Cells

- Eukaryotic cells are cells that contain a nucleus enclosed within membranes.
- The nucleus houses the cell's DNA and is the control center of the cell.
- Eukaryotic cells are typically larger than prokaryotic cells and have additional intracellular components called organelles that carry out specific functions.
- The major organelles in eukaryotic cells include:
    - Nucleus: Contains DNA and controls cellular activities
    - Mitochondria: Produce cellular energy through respiration
    - Endoplasmic reticulum: Network of folded membranes involved in protein transport and synthesis
    - Ribosomes: Organelles that assemble proteins based on mRNA sequences
    - Golgi apparatus: Packages and transports proteins for use inside and outside the cell
    - Vacuoles: Storage organelles that hold waste products and intracellular fluids
- Examples of eukaryotic cells include plant cells, animal cells, and fungi. Plant and animal cells share some organelles but differ in others, such as the presence of a cell wall and chloroplasts in plant cells.
- The organization and compartmentalization of eukaryotic cells allows for greater complexity and efficiency in carrying out cellular processes compared to prokaryotic cells. The separation of activities into organelles prevents unwanted chemical interactions and allows for tighter regulation of cell functions.
