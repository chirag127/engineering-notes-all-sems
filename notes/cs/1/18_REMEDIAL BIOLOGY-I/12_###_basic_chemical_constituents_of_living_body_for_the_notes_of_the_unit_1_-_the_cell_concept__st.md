Basic Chemical Constituents of Living Body in Remedial Biology I KCS

Living organisms are made up of various chemical compounds that are essential for their survival and functioning. The basic chemical constituents of living bodies include water, carbohydrates, lipids, proteins, nucleic acids, and minerals.

1. Water: Water is the most abundant compound in living bodies and is essential for many biological processes such as transport of nutrients, regulation of temperature, and maintenance of fluid balance.

2. Carbohydrates: Carbohydrates are a major source of energy for living organisms and are found in foods such as sugars, starches, and fibers.

3. Lipids: Lipids are a group of compounds that include fats, oils, and waxes. They are an important energy source and also play a role in insulation, cushioning, and protection of organs.

4. Proteins: Proteins are the building blocks of living bodies and are involved in a wide range of functions such as structural support, movement, and regulation of metabolic processes.

5. Nucleic acids: Nucleic acids are the building blocks of DNA and RNA and are involved in the storage and transfer of genetic information.

6. Minerals: Minerals are essential for the proper functioning of living bodies and play a role in the formation of bones, teeth, and other structures.

In conclusion, the basic chemical constituents of living bodies play a crucial role in the functioning and survival of living organisms. Understanding the properties and functions of these compounds is an important aspect of the subject of Remedial Biology I KCS.
