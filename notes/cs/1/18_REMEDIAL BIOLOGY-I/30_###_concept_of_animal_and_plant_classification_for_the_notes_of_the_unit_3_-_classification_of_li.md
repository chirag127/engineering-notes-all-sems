### Concept of animal and plant classification for the notes of the Unit 3 - Classification of living organisms (Five kingdom classification, major groups and principles of classification in each kingdom), Systematic and binomial system of nomenclature, Concept of animal and plant classification in the subject of REMEDIAL BIOLOGY-I KCS

Classification is the process of grouping living organisms based on their similarities and differences. This information is used to understand the relationships between different species and to develop a systematic understanding of the diversity of life.

1. Five Kingdom Classification: The five kingdom classification system separates living organisms into five broad categories: Monera, Protista, Fungi, Plantae, and Animalia.

2. Major Groups: Within each kingdom, species are further grouped into major groups based on shared characteristics. For example, within the Kingdom Animalia, species are grouped into subkingdoms such as vertebrates, invertebrates, and chordates.

3. Principles of Classification: The principles of classification are based on the idea that species that are more closely related should be classified together. This is determined by comparing the physical and genetic characteristics of different species.

4. Systematic and Binomial System of Nomenclature: The systematic and binomial system of nomenclature is a standardized system for naming species. It consists of a two-part name, with the first part indicating the genus and the second part indicating the species.

5. Animal Classification: Animal classification is based on a number of factors, including anatomy, physiology, and genetics. Species are grouped into classes, orders, families, genera, and species based on shared characteristics.

6. Plant Classification: Plant classification is based on similar principles to animal classification. Species are grouped into divisions, classes, orders, families, genera, and species based on shared characteristics such as morphology, anatomy, and genetics.

7. Importance of Classification: Classification is important for understanding the relationships between different species and for organizing information about the diversity of life. It also helps to facilitate communication between scientists and provides a framework for further research and discovery.
