### 6. To study Operational Amplifier as Adder and Subtractor

6. Study of Operational Amplifier as Adder and Subtractor:

Operational Amplifiers (Op-Amps) are a type of integrated circuit that are widely used in many electronic circuits, including analog circuits. One of the key applications of operational amplifiers is as an adder and subtractor.

1. Adder circuit: An operational amplifier can be used as an adder by connecting two or more input signals to the non-inverting and inverting inputs of the operational amplifier. The output of the operational amplifier is proportional to the sum of the inputs, and the gain of the circuit can be adjusted by changing the value of a feedback resistor.

2. Subtractor circuit: An operational amplifier can also be used as a subtractor by connecting two input signals to the non-inverting and inverting inputs of the operational amplifier. The output of the operational amplifier is proportional to the difference between the inputs, and the gain of the circuit can be adjusted by changing the value of a feedback resistor.

3. Applications: Operational amplifiers as adders and subtractors are widely used in many applications, including signal processing, data acquisition, and control systems. They are particularly useful for adding or subtracting signals that have different amplitudes or different frequencies.

4. Limitations: Operational amplifiers have some limitations when used as adders and subtractors. One of the main limitations is the input offset voltage, which is the difference between the voltages at the non-inverting and inverting inputs of the operational amplifier. This offset voltage can cause errors in the output of the circuit.

In conclusion, operational amplifiers are widely used as adders and subtractors in many electronic circuits. They are useful for adding or subtracting signals that have different amplitudes or different frequencies, and have many applications in signal processing, data acquisition, and control systems. However, operational amplifiers have some limitations, including the input offset voltage, which can cause errors in the output of the circuit.
