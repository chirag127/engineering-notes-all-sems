### Characteristics of Zener Diode:

1. V-I Characteristics: The voltage-current (V-I) characteristics of a Zener diode is similar to a normal PN junction diode. However, when the reverse voltage reaches a particular voltage (called Zener voltage), the current increases sharply. This sharp increase in current at the Zener voltage is due to avalanche breakdown and is shown in the V-I characteristics. The Zener voltage can be varied based on the dopant concentration and can range from 2V to 200V.

2. Graphical Measurement of Resistance: The resistance of a Zener diode can be graphically measured from the V-I characteristics. The slope of the curve in the forward biased region gives the forward resistance. The slope of the curve in the reverse biased region (before breakdown occurs) gives the reverse resistance. The resistance is low in the forward biased region and high in the reverse biased region. The resistance drops sharply in the reverse biased region once the breakdown occurs at the Zener voltage.
