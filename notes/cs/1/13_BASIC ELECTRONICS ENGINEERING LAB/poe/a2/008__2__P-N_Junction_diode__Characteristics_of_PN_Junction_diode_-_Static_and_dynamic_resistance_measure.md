### 2. P-N Junction diode: Characteristics of PN Junction diode - Static and dynamic resistance measurement from graph.

1. When P-N junction is reverse biased, the depletion layer widens and acts as an insulator. The reverse saturation current flows through the diode which is very small. The reverse resistance is very high.
2. When P-N junction is forward biased, the depletion layer narrows down and electrons flow from N to P and holes flow from P to N. The forward resistance is very low.
3. The voltage-current characteristics of PN junction diode can be plotted between voltage across the diode (V) and current through the diode (I).
4. The point where V=0 and I!=0, the curve shows the forward breakover voltage (V<sub>bd</sub>) and saturation current (I<sub>o</sub>).
5. The point where V=0 and I=0, gives the cut-in voltage (V<sub>t</sub>) or threshold voltage of PN junction diode.
6. For small values of forward voltage V< V<sub>t</sub>, the resistance is infinite and for V> V<sub>t</sub>, the resistance decreases rapidly. This shows the static resistance characteristics of the diode.
7. The dynamic resistance is obtained from the slope of the Voltage-Current characteristics curve. The slope gives the incremental resistance which decreases rapidly near the cut-in voltage and becomes almost constant for higher currents showing almost constant dynamic resistance.
