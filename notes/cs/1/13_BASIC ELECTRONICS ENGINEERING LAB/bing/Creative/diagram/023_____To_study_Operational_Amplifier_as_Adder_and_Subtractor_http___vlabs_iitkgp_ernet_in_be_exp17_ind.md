### Operational Amplifier as Adder and Subtractor

- An operational amplifier (op-amp) is a DC-coupled high-gain electronic voltage amplifier with a differential input and, usually, a single-ended output.
- An op-amp can be used as part of a positive or negative feedback amplifier or as an adder or subtractor type circuit using just pure resistances in both the input and the feedback loop.
- An adder circuit is a circuit that can add two or more input voltages and produce a single output voltage that is proportional to the sum of the inputs.
- A subtractor circuit is a circuit that can subtract one input voltage from another and produce a single output voltage that is proportional to the difference of the inputs.
- A differential amplifier is a combination of the inverting and noninverting voltage amplifiers that can amplify the difference between two input voltages.
- A differential amplifier can be used as a subtractor circuit by connecting one input to a reference voltage and the other input to the signal voltage.

#### Op-amp Adder

![Op-amp adder circuit diagram](https://www.gopracticals.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/08/Op-Amp-Adder-Circuit-Diagram.jpg)

The output voltage of the op-amp adder is given by:

$$V_{out} = -R_f \left( \frac{V_1}{R_1} + \frac{V_2}{R_2} + \frac{V_3}{R_3} + ... \right)$$

#### Op-amp Subtractor

![Op-amp subtractor circuit diagram](https://www.eeeguide.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/07/Subtractor-using-Op-Amp-or-Difference-Amplifier-Circuit.jpg)

The output voltage of the op-amp subtractor is given by:

$$V_{out} = \frac{R_2}{R_1} V_2 - \frac{R_4}{R_3} V_1$$

#### Op-amp Differential Amplifier

![Op-amp differential amplifier circuit diagram](https://www.electronics-tutorials.ws/wp-content/uploads/2018/05/opamp-opamp51.gif)

The output voltage of the op-amp differential amplifier is given by:

$$V_{out} = \frac{R_2}{R_1} \left( V_2 - V_1 \right)$$
