### Applications of PN Junction diode: Half & Full wave rectifier- Measurement of Vrms,Vdc, and ripple factor.

- A PN junction diode is a semiconductor device that is formed by joining a p-type semiconductor (which has more holes than electrons) and an n-type semiconductor (which has more electrons than holes)  .
- A PN junction diode has the property of allowing the electric current to flow in one direction (from p to n) and blocking it in the opposite direction (from n to p)  .
- A PN junction diode is one of the basic building blocks of electronic devices such as transistors, solar cells, LEDs, and integrated circuits  .
- A PN junction diode can be used as a rectifier, which is a device that converts alternating current (AC) to direct current (DC) .
- A rectifier can be either half-wave or full-wave, depending on how many cycles of the AC input are converted to DC output .
- A half-wave rectifier uses only one diode and converts only the positive half-cycles of the AC input to DC output, while the negative half-cycles are blocked .
- A full-wave rectifier uses either two or four diodes and converts both the positive and negative half-cycles of the AC input to DC output, by reversing the polarity of the negative half-cycles .
- The output of a rectifier is not a smooth DC voltage, but a pulsating DC voltage that has some variations or ripples .
- The quality of a rectifier can be measured by three parameters: the root mean square (RMS) voltage, the average or DC voltage, and the ripple factor .
- The RMS voltage is the effective value of the AC input voltage that produces the same heating effect as the DC output voltage .
- The average or DC voltage is the arithmetic mean of the DC output voltage over one cycle of the AC input voltage .
- The ripple factor is the ratio of the RMS value of the AC component (or ripple) to the DC component of the output voltage .
- The formulas for calculating these parameters are different for half-wave and full-wave rectifiers .
- For a half-wave rectifier, the RMS voltage is equal to the peak value of the AC input voltage divided by the square root of 2, the average or DC voltage is equal to the peak value of the AC input voltage divided by pi, and the ripple factor is equal to the square root of 2 minus 1 .
- For a full-wave rectifier, the RMS voltage is equal to the peak value of the AC input voltage divided by the square root of 2, the average or DC voltage is equal to twice the peak value of the AC input voltage divided by pi, and the ripple factor is equal to the square root of 2 minus 2 divided by the square root of 2 .
- The ripple factor can be reduced by using a capacitor or an inductor in parallel with the load resistor, which acts as a filter to smooth out the output voltage .
