### 3. PCB Lab: a. Artwork & printing of a simple PCB. b. Etching & drilling of PCB

A printed circuit board (PCB) is a board that electrically connects electronic components using conductive traces, pads, and other features. PCBs are used in various electronic devices and applications. To make a PCB, one needs to follow these steps:

a. Artwork & printing of a simple PCB.

- Artwork is the process of designing the layout of the PCB using a software tool such as Eagle, KiCad, or Altium. The layout includes the placement and routing of the components, the size and shape of the board, and the location of the holes and vias.
- Printing is the process of transferring the artwork to a copper-clad board using a printer or a photocopier. The board is coated with a photosensitive film that reacts to light and forms a mask on the copper layer. The mask protects the copper traces that will remain on the board, and exposes the unwanted copper that will be etched away.

b. Etching & drilling of PCB.

- Etching is the process of removing the unwanted copper from the board using a chemical solution such as ferric chloride or sodium persulfate. The board is immersed in the solution and agitated until the exposed copper is dissolved. The board is then rinsed and dried.
- Drilling is the process of making holes in the board for the components and vias using a drill press or a CNC machine. The holes are aligned with the pads and traces on the board. The board is then cleaned and inspected for any defects.
