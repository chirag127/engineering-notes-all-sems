### PN Junction Diode

A PN junction diode is a semiconductor device that consists of a p-type and an n-type region, separated by a depletion region. The p-type region has an excess of holes, while the n-type region has an excess of electrons. The depletion region is formed when some of the electrons from the n-type region diffuse into the p-type region and recombine with the holes, creating a region with no free charge carriers. The depletion region also creates a potential barrier, or a built-in voltage, that opposes the further diffusion of charge carriers.

A PN junction diode has two terminals: anode and cathode. The anode is connected to the p-type region, while the cathode is connected to the n-type region. The diode allows current to flow only when it is forward biased, that is, when the anode is at a higher potential than the cathode. When the diode is reverse biased, that is, when the anode is at a lower potential than the cathode, the diode blocks the current flow.
