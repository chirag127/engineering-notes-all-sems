# 5. Soldering shop: Soldering and disordering of Resistor in PCB. Soldering and disordering of IC in PCB. Soldering and disordering of Capacitor in PCB

Soldering is the process of joining two or more metal parts by melting and flowing a filler metal (solder) into the joint. Soldering is used to create electrical connections, mechanical joints, or both. Soldering can be done with a soldering iron, a soldering gun, a soldering station, or a reflow oven.

Disordering is the process of removing soldered parts from a circuit board or a joint. Disordering can be done with a desoldering pump, a desoldering braid, a hot air gun, or a rework station.

## Soldering and disordering of Resistor in PCB

A resistor is a passive electronic component that limits or regulates the flow of electric current in a circuit. Resistors are usually soldered to printed circuit boards (PCBs) using through-hole or surface-mount technology.

### Soldering of Resistor in PCB

- To solder a through-hole resistor, first insert the leads of the resistor into the corresponding holes on the PCB. Bend the leads slightly to hold the resistor in place. Then, heat up the soldering iron and apply a small amount of solder to the tip. Touch the tip of the soldering iron to the joint where the lead and the pad meet, and feed more solder until the joint is covered. Remove the soldering iron and let the joint cool. Cut off the excess lead with a wire cutter.
- To solder a surface-mount resistor, first apply a small amount of solder paste to the pads on the PCB where the resistor will be placed. Then, use tweezers to pick up the resistor and align it with the pads. Heat up the soldering iron and touch the tip to one end of the resistor, melting the solder paste and creating a joint. Repeat the same for the other end of the resistor. Alternatively, you can use a reflow oven to heat up the whole PCB and melt the solder paste.

### Disordering of Resistor in PCB

- To desolder a through-hole resistor, first heat up the soldering iron and apply a small amount of fresh solder to the tip. Then, touch the tip of the soldering iron to the joint where the lead and the pad meet, and melt the solder. Use a desoldering pump or a desoldering braid to suck up the molten solder. Repeat the same for the other joint. Then, use pliers or tweezers to pull out the resistor from the PCB.
- To desolder a surface-mount resistor, first heat up the soldering iron and apply a small amount of fresh solder to the tip. Then, touch the tip of the soldering iron to one end of the resistor, and lift it up slightly with tweezers. Repeat the same for the other end of the resistor. Alternatively, you can use a hot air gun or a rework station to heat up the whole resistor and remove it with tweezers.

## Soldering and disordering of IC in PCB

An integrated circuit (IC) is a set of electronic components that are fabricated on a single chip of semiconductor material. ICs are usually soldered to PCBs using through-hole or surface-mount technology.

### Soldering of IC in PCB

- To solder a through-hole IC, first insert the pins of the IC into the corresponding holes on the PCB. Make sure the orientation of the IC matches the markings on the PCB. Then, heat up the soldering iron and apply a small amount of solder to the tip. Touch the tip of the soldering iron to the joint where the pin and the pad meet, and feed more solder until the joint is covered. Remove the soldering iron and let the joint cool. Repeat the same for all the pins of the IC.
- To solder a surface-mount IC, first apply a small amount of solder paste to the pads on the PCB where the IC will be placed. Then, use tweezers to pick up the IC and align it with the pads. Heat up the soldering iron and touch the tip to one corner of the IC, melting the solder paste and creating a joint. Repeat the same for the opposite corner of the IC. Then, use the soldering iron to drag the tip along the edges of the IC, melting the solder paste and creating joints for all the pins. Alternatively, you can use a reflow oven to heat up the whole PCB and melt the solder paste.
