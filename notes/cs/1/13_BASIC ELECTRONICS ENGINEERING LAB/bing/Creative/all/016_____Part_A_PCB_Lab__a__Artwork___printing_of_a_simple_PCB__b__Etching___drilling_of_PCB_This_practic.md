# Part A PCB Lab: a. Artwork & printing of a simple PCB. b. Etching & drilling of PCB

## Introduction

- A printed circuit board (PCB) is a thin board made of fiberglass, composite epoxy, or other laminate material, on which electronic components are mounted and connected by copper traces.
- PCBs are used to create compact, reliable, and low-cost electronic circuits for various applications, such as computers, smartphones, radios, etc.
- The process of making a PCB involves several steps, such as designing, artwork, printing, etching, drilling, soldering, and testing.

## Artwork and printing of a simple PCB

- The first step in making a PCB is to design the circuit layout using a software tool, such as Eagle, KiCad, or Proteus.
- The circuit layout consists of the placement and routing of the components, pads, vias, and traces on the board.
- The circuit layout is then converted into a black and white image, called the artwork, which shows the positive or negative pattern of the copper traces on the board.
- The artwork can be printed on a transparent film, such as a photographic paper or a laser printer film, using a printer or a photocopier.
- The printed artwork is then aligned and placed on a copper-clad board, which is a thin sheet of copper laminated on a non-conductive substrate, such as fiberglass or epoxy.
- The copper-clad board and the artwork are then exposed to a source of ultraviolet (UV) light, such as a UV lamp or a sun lamp, for a certain amount of time, depending on the type of board and film used.
- The UV light transfers the image of the artwork onto the copper layer of the board, creating a photoresist layer that protects the copper traces from being etched away.

## Etching and drilling of PCB

- The next step in making a PCB is to etch away the unwanted copper from the board, leaving only the desired traces and pads.
- The etching process involves immersing the board in a chemical solution, such as ferric chloride or ammonium persulfate, which dissolves the exposed copper.
- The etching time depends on the concentration and temperature of the solution, the thickness and quality of the copper, and the size and complexity of the traces.
- The etched board is then rinsed with water and dried with a cloth or a paper towel.
- The photoresist layer is then removed from the board using a solvent, such as acetone or alcohol, or by peeling it off with a knife or a scraper.
- The board is then inspected for any defects, such as shorts, opens, or scratches, and corrected if needed.
- The final step in making a PCB is to drill holes for the components, such as resistors, capacitors, LEDs, etc., that need to be inserted through the board and soldered on the pads.
- The drilling process involves using a drill machine or a hand drill with a suitable drill bit, such as 0.8 mm or 1 mm, depending on the size of the component leads.
- The drill holes should be aligned with the pads and traces on the board, and should not damage the copper layer or the substrate.
- The drilled board is then cleaned with a brush or a cloth to remove any dust or debris.
