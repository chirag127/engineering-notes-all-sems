# 3. PCB Lab: a. Artwork & printing of a simple PCB. b. Etching & drilling of PCB

## a. Artwork & printing of a simple PCB

- A PCB (printed circuit board) is a thin board that holds electronic components and connects them with copper traces.
- To make a PCB, one needs to design the circuit layout and create an artwork that shows the positions and sizes of the components and traces.
- The artwork can be created using a software tool such as Eagle, KiCad, or Fritzing, or by drawing it manually on a paper or a transparent film.
- The artwork should have the following features:
  - It should be clear and accurate, with no errors or overlaps.
  - It should have a 1:1 scale, meaning that the dimensions of the artwork match the dimensions of the actual PCB.
  - It should have a mirror image of the circuit layout, as the artwork will be transferred to the copper side of the PCB.
  - It should have alignment marks or holes to help align the artwork with the PCB.
  - It should have a legend or a label that indicates the name, date, and version of the artwork.
- To print the artwork, one can use a laser printer or a photocopier to print it on a special paper or film that can transfer the toner to the PCB.
- Alternatively, one can use a UV printer or a plotter to print the artwork directly on the PCB.

## b. Etching & drilling of PCB

- Etching is the process of removing the unwanted copper from the PCB, leaving only the traces and pads that form the circuit.
- To etch a PCB, one needs to prepare the following materials and equipment:
  - A PCB with a copper layer on one or both sides.
  - An etchant solution, such as ferric chloride or sodium persulfate, that can dissolve copper.
  - A plastic tray or a glass container that can hold the etchant solution and the PCB.
  - A pair of gloves, goggles, and apron to protect oneself from the etchant solution.
  - A sponge or a brush to apply the etchant solution to the PCB.
  - A water source and a sink to rinse the PCB after etching.
- The steps for etching a PCB are as follows:
  - Transfer the artwork to the PCB using one of the methods mentioned above, such as ironing, UV exposure, or direct printing.
  - Cut the PCB to the desired size and shape, leaving some margin around the artwork.
  - Drill the alignment holes on the PCB using a drill bit that matches the size of the holes on the artwork.
  - Align the artwork with the PCB using the alignment holes and tape the edges to secure the artwork to the PCB.
  - Place the PCB in the tray or container and pour enough etchant solution to cover the PCB.
  - Gently agitate the tray or container to ensure that the etchant solution reaches all the copper areas on the PCB.
  - Monitor the etching process and check the PCB periodically to see if the unwanted copper is removed.
  - When the etching is complete, remove the PCB from the tray or container and rinse it with water to stop the etching reaction and remove the etchant residue.
  - Peel off the artwork from the PCB and dry the PCB with a cloth or a paper towel.
- Drilling is the process of making holes on the PCB for mounting the components and connecting them with wires or headers.
- To drill a PCB, one needs to prepare the following materials and equipment:
  - A PCB with the etched circuit layout.
  - A drill or a drill press with a set of drill bits that match the sizes of the holes on the artwork.
  - A clamp or a vise to hold the PCB securely while drilling.
  - A piece of wood or a scrap PCB to place under the PCB while drilling to avoid damaging the drill bit or the work surface.
  - A pair of pliers or a tweezers to remove the burrs or the copper flakes from the drilled holes.
- The steps for drilling a PCB are as follows:
  - Mark the center of each hole on the PCB using a marker or a punch.
  - Select the appropriate drill bit for each hole and insert it into the drill or the drill press.
  - Clamp or vise the PCB to the wood or the scrap PCB and align the drill bit with the marked hole.
  - Drill the hole slowly and steadily, applying moderate pressure and avoiding excessive heat or vibration.
  - Repeat the process for each hole on the PCB, changing the drill bit as needed.
  - Remove the clamp or the vise and inspect the PCB for any defects or errors.
