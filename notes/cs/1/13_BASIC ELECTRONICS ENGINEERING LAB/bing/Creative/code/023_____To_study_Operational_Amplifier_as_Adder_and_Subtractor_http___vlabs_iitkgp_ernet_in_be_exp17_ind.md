### Operational Amplifier as Adder and Subtractor

- An operational amplifier (op-amp) is a DC-coupled high-gain electronic voltage amplifier with a differential input and, usually, a single-ended output.
- An op-amp can perform basic arithmetic operations such as addition, subtraction, integration and differentiation by using different configurations of resistors and capacitors.
- An adder is an electronic circuit that produces an output, which is equal to the sum of the applied inputs. A subtractor is an electronic circuit that produces an output, which is equal to the difference of the applied inputs.
- An op-amp based adder circuit can be constructed by using the inverting configuration of the op-amp, where the input voltages are applied to the inverting terminal through resistors and the output voltage is taken from the inverting terminal through a feedback resistor.
- An op-amp based subtractor circuit can be constructed by using the differential amplifier configuration of the op-amp, where the input voltages are applied to both the inverting and non-inverting terminals through resistors and the output voltage is taken from the difference of the two input voltages.
- The advantages of using op-amp as adder and subtractor are that they are simple, cheap, accurate, and can handle multiple inputs and outputs.
- The disadvantages of using op-amp as adder and subtractor are that they are limited by the power supply voltage, bandwidth, slew rate, and input and output impedances of the op-amp.
