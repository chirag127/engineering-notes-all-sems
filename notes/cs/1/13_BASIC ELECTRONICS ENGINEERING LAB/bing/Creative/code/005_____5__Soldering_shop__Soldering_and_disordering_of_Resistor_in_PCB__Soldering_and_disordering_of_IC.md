### 5. Soldering shop: Soldering and disordering of Resistor in PCB. Soldering and disordering of IC in PCB. Soldering and disordering of Capacitor in PCB

Soldering is a process of joining two or more metal parts by melting and flowing a filler metal (solder) into the joint. Soldering is used to create electrical connections between components on a printed circuit board (PCB).

Disordering is a process of removing soldered components from a PCB by heating and melting the solder joint and pulling out the component. Disorder is used to repair or replace faulty or damaged components on a PCB.

#### Soldering and disordering of Resistor in PCB

A resistor is a passive electronic component that limits or regulates the flow of electric current in a circuit. Resistors are usually soldered on the PCB using through-hole or surface-mount techniques.

To solder a resistor on a PCB, follow these steps:

- Clean the PCB and the resistor leads with a flux or a solvent to remove any dirt or oxidation.
- Insert the resistor leads into the holes on the PCB or align them with the pads on the surface of the PCB.
- Heat up the soldering iron and apply a small amount of solder to the tip.
- Touch the tip of the soldering iron to the joint where the resistor lead and the PCB meet and feed more solder until the joint is covered with a shiny and smooth layer of solder.
- Remove the soldering iron and let the joint cool down.
- Trim the excess resistor leads with a wire cutter.

To disorder a resistor from a PCB, follow these steps:

- Heat up the soldering iron and apply a small amount of solder to the tip.
- Touch the tip of the soldering iron to the joint where the resistor lead and the PCB meet and melt the solder.
- Use a solder sucker or a desoldering braid to remove the molten solder from the joint.
- Pull out the resistor from the PCB with a pair of tweezers or pliers.
- Clean the PCB and the resistor leads with a flux or a solvent to remove any residue or oxidation.

#### Soldering and disordering of IC in PCB

An IC (integrated circuit) is a complex electronic component that contains many transistors, resistors, capacitors, and other elements on a single chip. ICs are usually soldered on the PCB using surface-mount techniques.

To solder an IC on a PCB, follow these steps:

- Clean the PCB and the IC pins with a flux or a solvent to remove any dirt or oxidation.
- Align the IC with the pads on the surface of the PCB and make sure the orientation is correct.
- Heat up the soldering iron and apply a small amount of solder to the tip.
- Touch the tip of the soldering iron to one of the corner pins of the IC and feed more solder until the pin is soldered to the pad.
- Repeat the same process for the opposite corner pin of the IC.
- Use a soldering iron or a hot air gun to reflow the solder on the rest of the pins of the IC and make sure there are no bridges or gaps between the pins and the pads.
- Remove the soldering iron or the hot air gun and let the joint cool down.

To disorder an IC from a PCB, follow these steps:

- Heat up the soldering iron and apply a small amount of solder to the tip.
- Touch the tip of the soldering iron to one of the corner pins of the IC and melt the solder.
- Use a solder sucker or a desoldering braid to remove the molten solder from the pin.
- Repeat the same process for the opposite corner pin of the IC.
- Use a soldering iron or a hot air gun to heat up the rest of the pins of the IC and melt the solder.
- Use a pair of tweezers or pliers to lift the IC from the PCB.
- Clean the PCB and the IC pins with a flux or a solvent to remove any residue or oxidation.
