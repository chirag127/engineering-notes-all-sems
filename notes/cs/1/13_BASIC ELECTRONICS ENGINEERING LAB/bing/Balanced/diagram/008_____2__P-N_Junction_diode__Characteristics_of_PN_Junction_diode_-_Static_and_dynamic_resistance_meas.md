### 2. P-N Junction diode: Characteristics of PN Junction diode - Static and dynamic resistance measurement from graph.

- A P-N junction diode is a semiconductor device that consists of a p-type and an n-type region separated by a depletion region where charge is stored.
- A P-N junction diode has the ability to rectify electric current, that is, to allow current to flow in one direction only .
- A P-N junction diode has a nonlinear current-voltage (I-V) characteristic, which depends on the applied voltage and the biasing mode (forward or reverse) of the diode .
- A P-N junction diode can also transform light energy into electrical energy (photodiode) or emit light when current flows through it (light-emitting diode) .

#### Static and dynamic resistance measurement from graph

- The static resistance of a P-N junction diode is the ratio of the applied voltage to the current at a given point on the I-V characteristic.
- The static resistance can be calculated by drawing a straight line from the origin to the point of interest on the I-V graph and finding its slope.
- The static resistance is different for forward and reverse biasing modes, and it varies with the applied voltage.
- The dynamic resistance of a P-N junction diode is the ratio of the small change in voltage to the small change in current around a given operating point on the I-V characteristic.
- The dynamic resistance can be calculated by drawing a tangent line at the operating point on the I-V graph and finding its slope.
- The dynamic resistance is also different for forward and reverse biasing modes, and it is smaller than the static resistance at the same point.
- The dynamic resistance is useful for analyzing the behavior of the diode in alternating current (AC) circuits.

- The forward biasing mode is when the p-type region is connected to the positive terminal of the voltage source and the n-type region is connected to the negative terminal.
- The reverse biasing mode is when the p-type region is connected to the negative terminal of the voltage source and the n-type region is connected to the positive terminal.
- The forward biasing mode allows current to flow through the diode, while the reverse biasing mode blocks current flow.
- The forward voltage (VF) is the minimum voltage required to overcome the potential barrier and make the diode conduct in the forward biasing mode.
- The reverse breakdown voltage (VR) is the maximum voltage that the diode can withstand in the reverse biasing mode before it breaks down and allows a large current to flow.
