### Applications of PN Junction diode: Half & Full wave rectifier- Measurement of Vrms,Vdc, and ripple factor.

- A PN junction diode is a semiconductor device that allows current to flow in one direction only. It has two terminals, anode and cathode, and a junction between them.
- A rectifier is a circuit that converts alternating current (AC) to direct current (DC). Rectifiers are used in power supplies, radios, and other electronic devices that require DC voltage.
- A half wave rectifier is a rectifier that uses only one diode and passes only one half cycle of the AC input voltage to the output. The other half cycle is blocked by the diode. The output voltage is pulsating DC, which has gaps and fluctuations.
- A full wave rectifier is a rectifier that uses two diodes and passes both half cycles of the AC input voltage to the output. The diodes are connected in opposite directions, so that one diode conducts when the input is positive and the other diode conducts when the input is negative. The output voltage is also pulsating DC, but it has no gaps and less fluctuations.

- Vrms is the root mean square value of the AC input voltage. It is the effective value of the AC voltage that produces the same heating effect as a DC voltage of the same value. Vrms can be calculated by dividing the peak value of the AC voltage by the square root of 2. For example, if the peak value of the AC voltage is 10 V, then Vrms = 10 / √2 = 7.07 V.
- Vdc is the average value of the DC output voltage. It is the value of the DC voltage that is equivalent to the area under the output waveform. Vdc can be calculated by multiplying the peak value of the output voltage by a factor that depends on the type of rectifier. For a half wave rectifier, the factor is 0.318, and for a full wave rectifier, the factor is 0.636. For example, if the peak value of the output voltage is 10 V, then Vdc = 10 x 0.318 = 3.18 V for a half wave rectifier, and Vdc = 10 x 0.636 = 6.36 V for a full wave rectifier.
- Ripple factor is a measure of the amount of AC component in the DC output voltage. It is the ratio of the root mean square value of the AC component to the average value of the DC component. Ripple factor can be calculated by dividing the standard deviation of the output voltage by the average value of the output voltage. For a half wave rectifier, the ripple factor is 1.21, and for a full wave rectifier, the ripple factor is 0.482. A lower ripple factor means a smoother and more stable output voltage.

- The applications of half wave and full wave rectifiers are as follows:

  - Half wave rectifiers are used in low power applications, such as signal detection, modulation, and demodulation. They are also used in battery chargers, voltage doublers, and peak detectors.
  - Full wave rectifiers are used in high power applications, such as power supplies, inverters, and converters. They are also used in bridge circuits, filters, and regulators.
