### 5. Characteristics of BJT: BJT in CE configuration

- BJT stands for bipolar junction transistor, which is a type of semiconductor device that can amplify or switch electrical signals.
- BJT has three terminals: base (B), collector (C) and emitter (E). The base is the control terminal that regulates the current flow between the collector and the emitter. The collector is the output terminal that collects the charge carriers from the emitter. The emitter is the input terminal that emits the charge carriers into the base region.
- BJT can be connected in three different configurations by keeping one terminal common and using the other two terminals for the input and output. These configurations are: common base (CB), common emitter (CE) and common collector (CC).
- In CE configuration, the emitter is the common terminal for both input and output. The input signal is applied between the base and the emitter, and the output signal is taken between the collector and the emitter. This configuration is widely used for amplification and switching applications because of its high power gain and medium voltage gain.
- The characteristics of BJT in CE configuration can be divided into two types: input characteristics and output characteristics. Input characteristics show the relation between the input current (I B) and the input voltage (V BE) for a fixed output voltage (V CE). Output characteristics show the relation between the output current (I C) and the output voltage (V CE) for a fixed input current (I B).
- The input characteristics of BJT in CE configuration are nonlinear and exponential, which means that a small change in V BE can cause a large change in I B. The input resistance of BJT in CE configuration is low, which means that it draws more current from the input source.
- The output characteristics of BJT in CE configuration are divided into three regions: cut-off region, active region and saturation region. In the cut-off region, both the base and the collector currents are zero, and the transistor is off. In the active region, the base current is nonzero and the collector current is proportional to the base current, and the transistor is on and acts as an amplifier. In the saturation region, both the base and the collector currents are maximum, and the transistor is on and acts as a switch.
- The output characteristics of BJT in CE configuration are also affected by the Early effect, which is the variation of the effective base width due to the reverse bias of the collector-base junction. The Early effect causes the output characteristics to bend upward at higher values of V CE, which increases the output resistance and reduces the current gain of the transistor. The Early voltage (V A) is a parameter that measures the strength of the Early effect.
- The output characteristics of BJT in CE configuration are also limited by the breakdown voltage, which is the maximum voltage that can be applied across the collector and the emitter without damaging the transistor. The breakdown voltage depends on the doping level and the geometry of the transistor. The common-emitter breakdown voltage (BV CE0) is lower than the common-base breakdown voltage (BV CBO) because of the impact ionization of the charge carriers in the collector junction.
