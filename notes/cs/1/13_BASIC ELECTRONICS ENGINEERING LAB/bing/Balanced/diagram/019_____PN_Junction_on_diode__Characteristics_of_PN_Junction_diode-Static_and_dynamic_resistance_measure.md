### PN Junction Diode: Characteristics of PN Junction Diode-Static and Dynamic Resistance Measurement from Graph

A PN junction diode is a semiconductor device that allows current to flow in one direction only. It is formed by joining a p-type semiconductor and an n-type semiconductor, creating a depletion region at the junction. The depletion region acts as a potential barrier that prevents the flow of electrons from the n-region to the p-region, and holes from the p-region to the n-region, unless an external voltage is applied.

The characteristics of a PN junction diode can be studied by plotting a graph of the current (I) versus the voltage (V) across the diode, called the I-V curve. The I-V curve shows how the current varies with the applied voltage for different modes of operation of the diode.

The modes of operation of a PN junction diode are:

- Forward bias: When the positive terminal of the external voltage is connected to the p-region and the negative terminal to the n-region, the diode is said to be forward biased. In this mode, the external voltage reduces the potential barrier at the junction, allowing the electrons and holes to cross the depletion region and create a current. The current increases exponentially with the voltage, as shown by the I-V curve in the figure below.

- Reverse bias: When the positive terminal of the external voltage is connected to the n-region and the negative terminal to the p-region, the diode is said to be reverse biased. In this mode, the external voltage increases the potential barrier at the junction, preventing the flow of charge carriers across the depletion region. The current is very small and almost constant, due to the thermal generation of minority carriers in the semiconductor. This current is called the reverse saturation current, and is shown by the I-V curve in the figure below.

- Breakdown: When the reverse bias voltage is increased beyond a certain value, called the breakdown voltage, the diode undergoes a sudden increase in current, due to the avalanche or Zener effect. The diode is said to be in breakdown mode, and the I-V curve shows a sharp rise in current, as shown by the figure below.

The resistance of a PN junction diode is defined as the ratio of the voltage across the diode to the current through the diode. The resistance of a diode depends on the mode of operation and the applied voltage. There are two types of resistance that can be measured from the I-V curve of a diode:

- Static resistance: This is the resistance of the diode at a particular point on the I-V curve, given by the slope of the tangent drawn at that point. The static resistance is also called the DC resistance, as it represents the resistance of the diode under a constant voltage. The static resistance of a diode in forward bias is low, and in reverse bias is high, as shown by the figure below.

![Static resistance of a PN junction diode](https://www.electronics-tutorials.ws/wp-content/uploads/2018/05/diode-dio4.gif)

- Dynamic resistance: This is the resistance of the diode in a small range of voltage around a particular point on the I-V curve, given by the slope of the secant drawn between the two points. The dynamic resistance is also called the AC resistance, as it represents the resistance of the diode under a varying voltage. The dynamic resistance of a diode in forward bias is lower than the static resistance, and in reverse bias is higher than the static resistance, as shown by the figure below.

![Dynamic resistance of a PN junction diode](https://www.electronics-tutorials.ws/wp-content/uploads/2018/05/diode-dio5.gif)

The static and dynamic resistance of a PN junction diode can be measured from the I-V curve by using the following formulas:

- Static resistance: Rs = V/I
- Dynamic resistance: Rd = (V2 - V1) / (I2 - I1)

Where V and I are the voltage and current at a particular point, and V1, V2, I1, and I2 are the voltage and current at two points in a small range around that point.
