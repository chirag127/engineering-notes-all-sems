### 1. Study of various types of Active & Passive Components based on their ratings.

- Active components are those that can generate or amplify electrical signals, such as transistors, diodes, and integrated circuits. Passive components are those that can only store or dissipate electrical energy, such as resistors, capacitors, and inductors.
- The ratings of active and passive components are the specifications that indicate their performance, characteristics, and limitations. Some common ratings are:
  - Power rating: the maximum amount of power that a component can handle without being damaged or overheated. It is usually expressed in watts (W) or milliwatts (mW).
  - Voltage rating: the maximum amount of voltage that a component can withstand across its terminals without breaking down or arcing. It is usually expressed in volts (V) or millivolts (mV).
  - Current rating: the maximum amount of current that a component can carry without exceeding its power rating or causing excessive heat. It is usually expressed in amperes (A) or milliamperes (mA).
  - Resistance rating: the amount of opposition that a component offers to the flow of current. It is usually expressed in ohms (Ω) or kilohms (kΩ).
  - Capacitance rating: the amount of electric charge that a component can store at a given voltage. It is usually expressed in farads (F) or microfarads (µF).
  - Inductance rating: the amount of magnetic flux that a component can generate when current flows through it. It is usually expressed in henries (H) or millihenries (mH).
  - Frequency rating: the range of frequencies that a component can operate at without losing its functionality or efficiency. It is usually expressed in hertz (Hz) or kilohertz (kHz).
- Some examples of active and passive components and their ratings are:
  - Transistor: an active component that can act as a switch or an amplifier for electrical signals. It has three terminals: base, collector, and emitter. Some ratings are:
    - Power dissipation: the maximum power that a transistor can dissipate without being damaged. For example, a 2N2222 transistor has a power dissipation of 625 mW.
    - Collector-emitter voltage: the maximum voltage that can be applied between the collector and the emitter terminals. For example, a 2N2222 transistor has a collector-emitter voltage of 40 V.
    - Collector current: the maximum current that can flow through the collector terminal. For example, a 2N2222 transistor has a collector current of 800 mA.
    - Gain: the ratio of the output current to the input current of a transistor. For example, a 2N2222 transistor has a gain of 100.
  - Resistor: a passive component that reduces the current flow and drops the voltage in a circuit. It has two terminals. Some ratings are:
    - Resistance: the amount of opposition that a resistor offers to the current flow. For example, a 10 kΩ resistor has a resistance of 10,000 Ω.
    - Power rating: the maximum power that a resistor can handle without being damaged or overheated. For example, a 10 kΩ resistor with a 1/4 W power rating can handle up to 0.25 W of power.
    - Tolerance: the percentage of deviation from the nominal resistance value. For example, a 10 kΩ resistor with a 5% tolerance can have an actual resistance value between 9.5 kΩ and 10.5 kΩ.
  - Capacitor: a passive component that stores electric charge and blocks direct current while allowing alternating current to pass. It has two terminals. Some ratings are:
    - Capacitance: the amount of electric charge that a capacitor can store at a given voltage. For example, a 100 µF capacitor can store 100 µC of charge at 1 V.
    - Voltage rating: the maximum voltage that a capacitor can withstand across its terminals without breaking down or leaking. For example, a 100 µF capacitor with a 16 V voltage rating can handle up to 16 V of voltage.
