### 4. Characteristics of Zener diode: V-I characteristics of zener diode, Graphical measurement of forward and reverse resistance.

- A Zener diode is a special type of diode designed to reliably allow current to flow in both forward and reverse directions when a certain set reverse voltage, known as the Zener voltage, is reached .
- Zener diodes are widely used as voltage references and as shunt regulators to regulate the voltage across small circuits .
- Zener diodes operate similarly to conventional diodes when in the forward-bias mode. They have a bias turn-on voltage of between 0.3 and 0.7V.
- Zener diodes are heavily doped than ordinary diodes. They have extra thin depletion region. When we apply a voltage more than the Zener breakdown voltage (can range from 1.2 volts to 200 volts), the depletion region vanishes, and large current starts to flow through the junction.
- The V-I characteristics of a Zener diode are shown in the following figure:

![V-I characteristics of Zener diode](https://www.electronics-tutorials.ws/wp-content/uploads/2018/05/diode-dio7.gif)

- The forward region of the curve is similar to that of a normal diode. The reverse region shows a sharp rise in the current after the breakdown voltage is reached. The breakdown voltage is also called the Zener voltage and is denoted by VZ.
- The forward and reverse resistances of a Zener diode can be measured graphically by drawing tangents to the V-I curve at the operating points. The slope of the tangent gives the inverse of the resistance. The following figure shows an example of measuring the forward and reverse resistances:

![Graphical measurement of forward and reverse resistances](https://www.electrical4u.com/wp-content/uploads/2013/07/forward-and-reverse-resistance-of-zener-diode.png)

- The forward resistance (RF) is the inverse of the slope of the tangent at point A. The reverse resistance (RZ) is the inverse of the slope of the tangent at point B.
