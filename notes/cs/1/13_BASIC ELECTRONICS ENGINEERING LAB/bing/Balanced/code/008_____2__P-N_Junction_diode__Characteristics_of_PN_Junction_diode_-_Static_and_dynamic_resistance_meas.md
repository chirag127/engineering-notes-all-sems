### 2. P-N Junction diode: Characteristics of PN Junction diode - Static and dynamic resistance measurement from graph.

A P-N junction diode is a semiconductor device that consists of a P-type and an N-type region joined together by a junction. The P-type region has a high concentration of holes (positive charge carriers) and the N-type region has a high concentration of electrons (negative charge carriers). The junction forms a depletion region where there are no free charge carriers due to the diffusion and recombination of holes and electrons across the junction. The depletion region creates an electric field that opposes further diffusion of charge carriers.

The characteristics of a P-N junction diode are the relationship between the voltage applied across the diode and the current flowing through it. The characteristics can be divided into two regions: forward bias and reverse bias.

- Forward bias: When the P-type region is connected to the positive terminal of a battery and the N-type region is connected to the negative terminal, the diode is said to be forward biased. In this condition, the applied voltage reduces the width of the depletion region and allows more charge carriers to cross the junction. The current increases exponentially with the applied voltage. In this condition, the applied voltage increases the width of the depletion region and prevents any charge carriers from crossing the junction. The current is very small and almost constant, regardless of the applied voltage. The reverse bias characteristics can be plotted on a graph of current (I) versus voltage (V) as shown below.

The resistance of a P-N junction diode is the ratio of the applied voltage to the resulting current. The resistance can be calculated from the slope of the characteristics curve at any point. The resistance can be classified into two types: static resistance and dynamic resistance.

- Static resistance: The static resistance is the resistance of the diode at a fixed voltage. It can be obtained by dividing the voltage by the current at any point on the characteristics curve. The static resistance is also called the DC resistance or the average resistance. The static resistance of a diode varies with the applied voltage and the polarity of the bias. The static resistance of a diode in forward bias is low and decreases with increasing voltage. The static resistance of a diode in reverse bias is high and increases with increasing voltage.

- Dynamic resistance: The dynamic resistance is the resistance of the diode for a small change in voltage. It can be obtained by dividing the change in voltage by the change in current at any point on the characteristics curve. The dynamic resistance is also called the AC resistance or the differential resistance. The dynamic resistance of a diode is more useful than the static resistance for analyzing the behavior of the diode in alternating current (AC) circuits. The dynamic resistance of a diode in forward bias is low and decreases with increasing current. The dynamic resistance of a diode in reverse bias is high and increases with decreasing current.

The static and dynamic resistance of a P-N junction diode can be measured from the graph of the characteristics curve by using the following formulas:

- Static resistance = V/I
- Dynamic resistance = ΔV/ΔI

Where V is the voltage, I is the current, ΔV is the change in voltage, and ΔI is the change in current.

For example, in the graph below, the static resistance of the diode in forward bias at point A is 0.6 V / 0.01 A = 60 Ω. The dynamic resistance of the diode in forward bias at point A is (0.7 V - 0.6 V) / (0.02 A - 0.01 A) = 10 Ω.
