### Part B Study of Lab Equipment’s and Components: CRO, Multi meter, Function Generator, Power supplyActive, Passive Components and Bread Board

The objective of this part is to study various electronic components and design of various electronic circuits using lab equipment and components. The following points will cover the main aspects of this part:

- CRO: A cathode ray oscilloscope (CRO) is a device that displays the waveform of an electrical signal on a screen. It can measure voltage, frequency, phase, and other parameters of a signal. A CRO consists of a cathode ray tube (CRT), a vertical and a horizontal amplifier, a time base generator, and a trigger circuit. A CRO can be used to observe and analyze the behavior of various electronic circuits and signals.
- Multimeter: A multimeter is a device that can measure multiple electrical quantities, such as voltage, current, resistance, capacitance, and continuity. A multimeter can be either analog or digital, depending on the type of display and the accuracy of the measurement. A multimeter can be used to test and troubleshoot various electronic components and circuits.
- Function Generator: A function generator is a device that can produce various types of periodic signals, such as sine, square, triangle, and sawtooth waves. A function generator can vary the frequency, amplitude, and shape of the output signal. A function generator can be used to provide input signals to various electronic circuits and systems for testing and analysis.
- Power Supply: A power supply is a device that converts the alternating current (AC) from the mains to a direct current (DC) of a desired voltage and current. A power supply can be either fixed or variable, depending on the ability to adjust the output voltage and current. A power supply can be used to provide power to various electronic circuits and components.
- Active Components: Active components are those electronic components that can amplify or control the flow of electric current or voltage. Active components require an external source of power to operate. Some examples of active components are transistors, diodes, integrated circuits, and optoelectronic devices.
- Passive Components: Passive components are those electronic components that can store or dissipate electric energy, but cannot amplify or control the flow of electric current or voltage. Passive components do not require an external source of power to operate. Some examples of passive components are resistors, capacitors, inductors, and transformers.
- Bread Board: A bread board is a device that allows the user to create temporary electronic circuits without soldering. A bread board consists of a plastic board with holes and metal strips that connect the holes electrically. A bread board can be used to prototype and test various electronic circuits and components before making a permanent circuit.
