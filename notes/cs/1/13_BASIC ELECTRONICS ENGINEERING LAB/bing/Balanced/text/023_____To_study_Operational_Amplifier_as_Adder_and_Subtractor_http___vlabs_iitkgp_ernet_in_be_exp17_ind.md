### To study Operational Amplifier as Adder and Subtractor

- An operational amplifier (op-amp) is a DC-coupled high-gain electronic voltage amplifier with a differential input and, usually, a single-ended output.
- An op-amp can be used as part of a positive or negative feedback amplifier or as an adder or subtractor type circuit using just pure resistances in both the input and the feedback loop.
- An adder circuit is a type of op-amp circuit that can perform the addition of two or more input voltages and produce an output voltage that is proportional to the sum of the inputs.
- A subtractor circuit is a type of op-amp circuit that can perform the subtraction of two input voltages and produce an output voltage that is proportional to the difference of the inputs.

- The sign of the output voltage depends on the polarity of the input voltages and the position of the input resistors. If the input resistors are connected to the inverting input of the op-amp, the output voltage will be negative and the circuit will act as a subtractor. If the input resistors are connected to the non-inverting input of the op-amp, the output voltage will be positive and the circuit will act as an adder.
- The gain of the op-amp adder or subtractor circuit is determined by the ratio of the feedback resistor to the input resistors. The higher the ratio, the higher the gain. The gain can be adjusted by changing the values of the resistors.
- The op-amp adder or subtractor circuit can be used for various applications, such as signal processing, audio mixing, voltage level shifting, etc.
