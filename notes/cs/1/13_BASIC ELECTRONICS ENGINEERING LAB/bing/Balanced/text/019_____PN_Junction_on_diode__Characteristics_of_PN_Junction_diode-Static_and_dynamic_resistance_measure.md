### PN Junction Diode: Characteristics of PN Junction Diode-Static and Dynamic Resistance Measurement from Graph

- A PN junction diode is a semiconductor device that allows current to flow in one direction only. It is formed by joining a p-type and an n-type semiconductor material, creating a potential barrier voltage across the junction. 
- The VI characteristics of a PN junction diode show the relationship between the voltage applied across the diode and the current flowing through it. The characteristics are nonlinear and depend on the polarity and magnitude of the applied voltage. 
- The static resistance of a PN junction diode is the ratio of the applied voltage to the current at a given point on the VI characteristics curve. It is also called the DC resistance or the ohmic resistance. The static resistance varies with the applied voltage and is different for forward and reverse bias. 
- The dynamic resistance of a PN junction diode is the ratio of the small change in voltage to the corresponding small change in current at a given operating point on the VI characteristics curve. It is also called the AC resistance or the differential resistance. The dynamic resistance is a measure of how the diode responds to small variations in the applied voltage and is useful for analyzing the diode behavior in AC circuits. 
- The static and dynamic resistance of a PN junction diode can be measured from the graph of the VI characteristics by using the following formulas:

  - Static resistance = V/I, where V is the voltage and I is the current at a given point on the curve.
  - Dynamic resistance = ΔV/ΔI, where ΔV is the small change in voltage and ΔI is the small change in current around a given point on the curve.

- In the diagram, the point A represents the operating point of the diode under forward bias. The static resistance at this point is given by RA = VA/IA, where VA and IA are the voltage and current at point A. The dynamic resistance at this point is given by rA = ΔVA/ΔIA, where ΔVA and ΔIA are the small changes in voltage and current around point A. The slope of the tangent line at point A is equal to the inverse of the dynamic resistance, i.e., 1/rA. 
- Similarly, the point B represents the operating point of the diode under reverse bias. The static resistance at this point is given by RB = VB/IB, where VB and IB are the voltage and current at point B. The dynamic resistance at this point is given by rB = ΔVB/ΔIB, where ΔVB and ΔIB are the small changes in voltage and current around point B. The slope of the tangent line at point B is equal to the inverse of the dynamic resistance, i.e., 1/rB.
