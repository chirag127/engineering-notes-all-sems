### Part B Study of Lab Equipment’s and Components: CRO, Multi meter, Function Generator, Power supplyActive, Passive Components and Bread Board.

- The aim of this part is to study the basic features, functions, and applications of some common lab equipment and components used in electronics engineering.
- The equipment and components are:
  - CRO: Cathode Ray Oscilloscope, a device that displays the waveform of an electrical signal on a screen. It can measure voltage, frequency, phase, and other parameters of the signal. 
  - Multimeter: A device that can measure multiple electrical quantities, such as voltage, current, resistance, capacitance, etc. It can be analog or digital. 
  - Function Generator: A device that can generate different types of electrical signals, such as sine, square, triangular, etc. It can vary the frequency, amplitude, and shape of the signal. 
  - Power supply: A device that provides a constant and regulated voltage or current to a circuit. It can be AC or DC, fixed or variable. 
  - Active components: Components that can amplify or control the flow of electrical signals, such as transistors, diodes, LEDs, etc. They require an external power source to operate. 
  - Passive components: Components that can store or dissipate electrical energy, such as resistors, capacitors, inductors, etc. They do not require an external power source to operate. 
  - Breadboard: A board that allows easy and temporary connection of electronic components and wires without soldering. It has rows and columns of holes that are internally connected by metal strips. 
- The procedure to study each equipment or component is as follows:
  - CRO: Connect the power cord of the CRO to a mains socket and switch it on. Adjust the intensity, focus, and position knobs to get a clear and stable dot on the screen. Connect the probe of the CRO to the output of a function generator or any other signal source. Adjust the time base and voltage knobs to get a suitable scale and display of the waveform. Observe the shape, amplitude, frequency, and phase of the signal. Use the trigger and sweep modes to stabilize and synchronize the waveform. 
  - Multimeter: Connect the power cord of the multimeter to a mains socket and switch it on. Select the desired quantity and range to measure using the selector knob. Connect the probes of the multimeter to the terminals of the component or circuit to be measured. Read the value displayed on the screen or the pointer. 
  - Function Generator: Connect the power cord of the function generator to a mains socket and switch it on. Select the desired type and frequency of the signal using the selector knob and the frequency dial. Adjust the amplitude and offset of the signal using the amplitude and offset knobs. Connect the output of the function generator to the input of a CRO or any other device to observe the signal. 
  - Power supply: Connect the power cord of the power supply to a mains socket and switch it on. Select the desired voltage or current range using the selector knob. Adjust the voltage or current using the variable knob. Connect the output terminals of the power supply to the circuit or component to be powered. 
  - Active components: Identify the type and specifications of the active component using the datasheet or the markings on the component. Connect the component to a suitable power supply and a load circuit using wires and a breadboard. Observe the behavior and characteristics of the component using a multimeter or a CRO. 
  - Passive components: Identify the type and value of the passive component using the datasheet or the color code on the component. Connect the component to a suitable power supply and a load circuit using wires and a breadboard. Observe the behavior and characteristics of the component using a multimeter or a CRO. 
  - Breadboard: Identify the rows and columns of holes and the internal connections on the breadboard. Insert the leads of the components and wires into the holes to make the desired circuit. Ensure that the connections are tight and secure. Use a multimeter or a CRO to verify the circuit.
