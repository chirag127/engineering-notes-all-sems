### 3. PCB Lab: a. Artwork & printing of a simple PCB. b. Etching & drilling of PCB

A printed circuit board (PCB) is a board that electrically connects and mechanically supports electronic components using conductive tracks, pads, and other features. PCBs are widely used in various electronic devices and circuits.

The process of making a PCB involves the following steps:

- Artwork and printing: This is the design and layout of the PCB, which can be done using software tools or manually. The artwork shows the position and size of the components, the traces, the pads, the holes, and the labels. The artwork is then printed on a special paper or film, called the mask, which is used to transfer the pattern onto the copper-clad board.
- Etching: This is the removal of the unwanted copper from the board, leaving only the desired traces and pads. The etching can be done using chemical solutions, such as ferric chloride or ammonium persulfate, or using physical methods, such as laser or plasma. The mask is placed over the board and the etching solution is applied, dissolving the exposed copper. The mask is then removed and the board is rinsed and dried.
- Drilling: This is the creation of holes in the board, which are used to mount the components and connect the traces. The drilling can be done using a drill press, a CNC machine, or a hand drill. The size and location of the holes are determined by the artwork. The board is then cleaned and inspected for any defects or errors.
