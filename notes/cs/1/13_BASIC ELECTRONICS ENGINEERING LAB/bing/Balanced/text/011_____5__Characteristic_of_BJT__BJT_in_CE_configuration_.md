### 5. Characteristic of BJT: BJT in CE configuration.

- BJT stands for bipolar junction transistor, which is a type of semiconductor device that can amplify or switch electrical signals.
- A BJT has three terminals: the base (B), the collector (C), and the emitter (E).
- A BJT can be classified into two types: NPN and PNP, depending on the arrangement of the N-type and P-type semiconductor materials in the device.
- In the CE configuration, the base terminal is the input, the collector terminal is the output, and the emitter terminal is the common terminal for both input and output.
- The CE configuration is the most widely used configuration for BJT amplifiers, because it provides high voltage gain, high current gain, high input impedance, and low output impedance.
- The characteristic curves of a BJT in CE configuration show the relationship between the input current (IB), the output current (IC), and the output voltage (VCE).
- The characteristic curves can be divided into three regions: the cutoff region, the active region, and the saturation region.
- In the cutoff region, the base current is zero or very small, and the output current is also zero or very small. The output voltage is equal to the supply voltage (VCC).
- In the active region, the base current is positive for NPN and negative for PNP, and the output current is proportional to the base current. The output voltage is less than the supply voltage, and depends on the load resistance (RL).
- In the saturation region, the base current is large enough to make the output current reach its maximum value, which is limited by the supply voltage and the load resistance. The output voltage is very small, and close to zero.
