### 4. Characteristics of Zener diode: V-I characteristics of zener diode, Graphical measurement of forward and reverse resistance.

- A Zener diode is a special type of diode that is designed to operate in the reverse direction when the voltage across its terminals reaches a certain value, called the Zener voltage  .
- A Zener diode is heavily doped, which means it has a very thin depletion region and a high concentration of impurities in the p-n junction.
- A Zener diode has two modes of operation: forward biased and reverse biased   .
  - In forward biased mode, the Zener diode behaves like a normal diode, allowing current to flow from the anode to the cathode when the applied voltage is greater than the forward voltage (about 0.7 V for silicon diodes)   .
  - In reverse biased mode, the Zener diode blocks the current flow until the applied voltage reaches the Zener voltage, at which point the diode breaks down and allows a large current to flow in the opposite direction   .

![V-I characteristics of a Zener diode](https://www.electronics-tutorials.ws/wp-content/uploads/2013/07/diode_7_1.gif)

- The graph shows that the Zener diode has a linear relationship between the voltage and the current in the forward biased region, and a nonlinear relationship in the reverse biased region    .
- The Zener voltage is the point where the curve bends sharply and the reverse current increases rapidly    .
- The forward resistance of a Zener diode is the slope of the curve in the forward biased region, and it is usually very low (a few ohms)    .
- The reverse resistance of a Zener diode is the slope of the curve in the reverse biased region, and it is usually very high (several kilo-ohms or mega-ohms) until the Zener voltage is reached, after which it drops to a very low value (a few ohms)    .
- The forward and reverse resistance of a Zener diode can be measured graphically by drawing a tangent line to the curve at the desired point and calculating the inverse of the slope    .
- Zener diodes are widely used as voltage references and as shunt regulators to regulate the voltage across small circuits . They can also be used for protection against overvoltage and for clipping and clamping circuits  .
