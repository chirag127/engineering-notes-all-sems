### Characteristics of Zener diode: V-I characteristics of Zener diode, Graphical measurement of forward and reverse resistance.

- A Zener diode is a special type of diode that allows current to flow in both forward and reverse directions when a certain voltage, called the Zener voltage, is reached.
- The Zener diode is heavily doped and has a thin depletion region. It is designed to operate in the reverse breakdown region, where the current is large and relatively constant.
- The V-I characteristics of a Zener diode are shown in the figure below. The forward characteristics are similar to a normal diode, with a turn-on voltage of about 0.7 V. The reverse characteristics show a sharp increase in current when the reverse voltage reaches the Zener voltage (VZ). The slope of the reverse characteristics is the reverse resistance (RZ) of the diode.

![V-I characteristics of Zener diode](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/1c/Zener_diode_IV_curve.svg/1200px-Zener_diode_IV_curve.svg.png)

- The Zener voltage can be measured graphically by plotting the reverse voltage and current on a log-log scale. The Zener voltage is the point where the curve becomes nearly vertical. The forward and reverse resistances can be measured by finding the inverse of the slopes of the forward and reverse characteristics, respectively.
- Zener diodes are widely used as voltage references and as shunt regulators to regulate the voltage across small circuits. They are connected in parallel with the load and the voltage source, as shown in the figure below. The Zener diode maintains a constant voltage across the load, regardless of the variations in the input voltage or the load current .
