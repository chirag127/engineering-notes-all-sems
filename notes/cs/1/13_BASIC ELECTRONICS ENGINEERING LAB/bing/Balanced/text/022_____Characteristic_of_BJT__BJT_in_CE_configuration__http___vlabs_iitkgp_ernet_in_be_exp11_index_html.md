### Characteristics of BJT in CE configuration

- BJT stands for bipolar junction transistor, which is a three-terminal device that can amplify electrical signals.
- CE stands for common emitter, which is one of the three possible configurations of BJT, where the emitter terminal is common to both input and output circuits.
- The input signal is applied between the base and the emitter terminals, and the output signal is taken from the collector and the emitter terminals.
- The CE configuration is an inverting amplifier, which means that the output signal is 180 degrees out of phase with the input signal.
- The CE configuration has the following characteristics:

  - Input characteristics: These are the curves that show the relation between the base current (I_B) and the base-emitter voltage (V_BE) for different values of collector-emitter voltage (V_CE). The input characteristics are similar to the forward characteristics of a diode, as the base-emitter junction is forward biased. The input resistance (r_in) is the ratio of the change in V_BE to the change in I_B at a constant V_CE. The input resistance is typically low, in the range of hundreds of ohms.
  - Output characteristics: These are the curves that show the relation between the collector current (I_C) and the collector-emitter voltage (V_CE) for different values of base current (I_B). The output characteristics are divided into three regions: cut-off, active, and saturation. In the cut-off region, both the base-emitter and the collector-base junctions are reverse biased, and the collector current is negligible. In the active region, the base-emitter junction is forward biased and the collector-base junction is reverse biased, and the collector current is proportional to the base current. In the saturation region, both the junctions are forward biased, and the collector current reaches a maximum value that depends on the supply voltage and the load resistance. The output resistance (r_out) is the ratio of the change in V_CE to the change in I_C at a constant I_B. The output resistance is typically high, in the range of tens of kilo-ohms.
  - Current gain: This is the ratio of the output current to the input current, which is also called the beta (β) or the h_FE of the transistor. The current gain is a measure of how much the transistor can amplify the input signal. The current gain is usually high, in the range of tens to hundreds. The current gain is not a constant, but varies with the operating point and the temperature of the transistor.
  - Voltage gain: This is the ratio of the output voltage to the input voltage, which is also called the amplification factor (A_v) of the transistor. The voltage gain is a measure of how much the transistor can increase the voltage of the input signal. The voltage gain is usually medium, in the range of tens. The voltage gain is given by the product of the current gain and the ratio of the load resistance to the output resistance (A_v = β * R_L / r_out).
  - Power gain: This is the ratio of the output power to the input power, which is also called the power amplification factor (A_p) of the transistor. The power gain is a measure of how much the transistor can increase the power of the input signal. The power gain is usually high, in the range of thousands. The power gain is given by the product of the current gain and the voltage gain (A_p = β * A_v).

- The CE configuration is widely used for voltage and power amplification, as it has high power gain, medium voltage gain, and high output resistance. It is also suitable for impedance matching, as it can transform a low impedance input to a high impedance output.
