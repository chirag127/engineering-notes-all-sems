### 1. Study of various types of Active & Passive Components based on their ratings.

- Active components are those that can amplify or control the flow of electric current or voltage. They usually require an external source of energy to operate. Examples of active components are transistors, diodes, integrated circuits, etc.
- Passive components are those that do not amplify or control the electric current or voltage. They only store or dissipate energy. Examples of passive components are resistors, capacitors, inductors, etc.
- The ratings of components are the values of electrical parameters that specify their performance and limitations. They are usually given by the manufacturers or standards. Some common ratings of components are:
  - Power rating: the maximum amount of power that a component can handle without being damaged or overheated. It is measured in watts (W) or milliwatts (mW).
  - Voltage rating: the maximum amount of voltage that a component can withstand across its terminals without breaking down or arcing. It is measured in volts (V) or millivolts (mV).
  - Current rating: the maximum amount of current that a component can carry without exceeding its power rating or causing excessive heat. It is measured in amperes (A) or milliamperes (mA).
  - Resistance rating: the amount of opposition that a component offers to the flow of electric current. It is measured in ohms (Ω) or kiloohms (kΩ).
  - Capacitance rating: the amount of electric charge that a component can store per unit of voltage. It is measured in farads (F) or microfarads (µF).
  - Inductance rating: the amount of magnetic flux that a component can generate per unit of current. It is measured in henrys (H) or millihenrys (mH).
  - Frequency rating: the range of frequencies that a component can operate at without losing its functionality or efficiency. It is measured in hertz (Hz) or kilohertz (kHz).
