# 5. Soldering shop: Soldering and disordering of Resistor in PCB. Soldering and disordering of IC in PCB. Soldering and disordering of Capacitor in PCB

- Soldering is the process of joining two or more metal parts by melting and flowing a filler metal (solder) into the joint.
- Disorder (or desolder) is the process of removing solder and components from a circuit board for repair or reuse.
- PCB (printed circuit board) is a thin board made of fiberglass, composite epoxy, or other laminate material, on which electronic components are mounted and connected by copper traces.
- Resistor is a passive electronic component that resists the flow of electric current and reduces the voltage in a circuit.
- IC (integrated circuit) is a set of electronic circuits on a small chip of semiconductor material, such as silicon, that can perform various functions, such as amplification, logic, memory, etc.
- Capacitor is a passive electronic component that stores electric charge and energy in an electric field.

## Soldering and disordering of resistor in PCB

- To solder a resistor in a PCB, follow these steps:
  - Clean the PCB and the resistor leads with a brush or a cloth to remove any dirt or oxidation.
  - Insert the resistor leads through the holes in the PCB and bend them slightly to hold the resistor in place.
  - Heat up the soldering iron and apply a small amount of solder to the tip.
  - Touch the tip of the soldering iron to the joint where the resistor lead and the copper trace meet, and feed more solder until the joint is covered with a shiny and smooth layer of solder.
  - Remove the soldering iron and let the joint cool down. Do not move the resistor until the solder is solidified.
  - Cut off the excess resistor leads with a wire cutter or a plier.
  - Repeat the same steps for the other resistor lead and any other resistors you want to solder in the PCB.
- To disorder a resistor from a PCB, follow these steps:
  - Heat up the soldering iron and apply a small amount of solder to the tip.
  - Touch the tip of the soldering iron to the joint where the resistor lead and the copper trace meet, and melt the solder.
  - Use a solder sucker or a desoldering braid to remove the molten solder from the joint. Be careful not to damage the PCB or the resistor.
  - Pull out the resistor lead from the hole in the PCB with a tweezer or a plier. Do not apply too much force or you may break the resistor or the PCB.
  - Repeat the same steps for the other resistor lead and any other resistors you want to disorder from the PCB.

## Soldering and disordering of IC in PCB

- To solder an IC in a PCB, follow these steps:
  - Clean the PCB and the IC pins with a brush or a cloth to remove any dirt or oxidation.
  - Align the IC with the corresponding footprint on the PCB and make sure the orientation is correct. You can use a notch or a dot on the IC and the PCB to match the pin 1 position.
  - Gently press the IC into the PCB and make sure all the pins are inserted through the holes in the PCB.
  - Heat up the soldering iron and apply a small amount of solder to the tip.
  - Touch the tip of the soldering iron to the joint where the IC pin and the copper trace meet, and feed more solder until the joint is covered with a thin and smooth layer of solder. Do not apply too much solder or you may create a short circuit between adjacent pins.
  - Remove the soldering iron and let the joint cool down. Do not move the IC until the solder is solidified.
  - Repeat the same steps for the other IC pins and any other ICs you want to solder in the PCB.
- To disorder an IC from a PCB, follow these steps:
  - Heat up the soldering iron and apply a small amount of solder to the tip.
  - Touch the tip of the soldering iron to the joint where the IC pin and the copper trace meet, and melt the solder.
  - Use a solder sucker or a desoldering braid to remove the molten solder from the joint. Be careful not to damage the PCB or the IC.
  - Use a chip puller or a tweezer to gently lift the IC from the PCB. Do not apply too much force or you may bend or break the IC pins or the PCB.
  - Repeat the same steps for the other IC pins and any other ICs you want to disorder from the PCB.
