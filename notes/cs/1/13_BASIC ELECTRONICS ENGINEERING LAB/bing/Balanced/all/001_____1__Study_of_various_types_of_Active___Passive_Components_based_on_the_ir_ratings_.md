### 1. Study of various types of Active & Passive Components based on their ratings.

- Active components are those that can amplify or control the flow of electric current in a circuit. They require an external source of energy to operate. Examples of active components are transistors, diodes, integrated circuits, etc.
- Passive components are those that do not require any external source of energy to operate. They can only store or dissipate energy in a circuit. Examples of passive components are resistors, capacitors, inductors, etc.
- The ratings of components are the values of their electrical parameters that specify their performance and limitations. They are usually given by the manufacturers or standards organizations. Some common ratings of components are:
  - Resistance: The opposition to the flow of current in a resistor. It is measured in ohms (Ω).
  - Capacitance: The ability to store electric charge in a capacitor. It is measured in farads (F).
  - Inductance: The ability to store magnetic energy in an inductor. It is measured in henries (H).
  - Voltage: The potential difference across a component. It is measured in volts (V).
  - Current: The rate of flow of electric charge in a component. It is measured in amperes (A).
  - Power: The rate of energy conversion or dissipation in a component. It is measured in watts (W).
  - Frequency: The number of cycles of alternating current or voltage in a component per second. It is measured in hertz (Hz).
  - Impedance: The total opposition to the flow of alternating current in a component. It is a complex quantity that consists of resistance and reactance. It is measured in ohms (Ω).
  - Reactance: The opposition to the flow of alternating current in a component due to capacitance or inductance. It is a complex quantity that depends on the frequency. It is measured in ohms (Ω).
  - Gain: The ratio of output to input voltage, current, or power in an active component. It is a dimensionless quantity that indicates the amplification or attenuation of a signal.
  - Bandwidth: The range of frequencies that an active component can amplify or process without significant loss or distortion. It is measured in hertz (Hz).
  - Efficiency: The ratio of output power to input power in an active component. It is a dimensionless quantity that indicates the percentage of input power that is converted to useful output power.
