### 6. To study Operational Amplifier as Adder and Subtractor

An operational amplifier (op-amp) is a high-gain electronic voltage amplifier with a differential input and, usually, a single-ended output. It is used to perform a wide variety of mathematical operations, including addition and subtraction.

#### Adder
An adder circuit is used to add two or more input voltages and provide a single output voltage. The circuit can be designed using an op-amp with multiple inputs. The output voltage is equal to the sum of the input voltages multiplied by the gain of the op-amp.

#### Subtractor
A subtractor circuit is used to subtract one input voltage from another and provide a single output voltage. The circuit can be designed using an op-amp with two inputs. The output voltage is equal to the difference between the two input voltages multiplied by the gain of the op-amp.

#### Applications
Op-amp adder and subtractor circuits are commonly used in analog computing, instrumentation, and control systems. They can be used to perform mathematical operations on analog signals, such as scaling, summing, and differencing.

#### Advantages
- High gain
- High input impedance
- Low output impedance
- Wide bandwidth
- Low noise

#### Disadvantages
- Limited output voltage range
- Limited output current
- Limited bandwidth
- Non-ideal frequency response
- Non-ideal input characteristics

#### Conclusion
Operational amplifiers are versatile electronic components that can be used to perform a wide variety of mathematical operations, including addition and subtraction. They are commonly used in analog computing, instrumentation, and control systems due to their high gain, wide bandwidth, and low noise. However, they do have some limitations, including a limited output voltage range and non-ideal frequency response. It is important to consider these limitations when designing circuits using op-amps.
