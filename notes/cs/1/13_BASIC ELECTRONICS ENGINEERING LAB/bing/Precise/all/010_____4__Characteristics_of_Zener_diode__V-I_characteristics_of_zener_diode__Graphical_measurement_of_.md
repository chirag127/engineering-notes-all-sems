# 4. Characteristics of Zener diode

A Zener diode is a type of diode that is designed to operate in the reverse breakdown region. The following are the characteristics of a Zener diode:

## V-I characteristics of Zener diode
- The V-I characteristics of a Zener diode show the relationship between the voltage across the diode and the current flowing through it.
- In the forward bias region, the Zener diode behaves like a normal diode and conducts current when the voltage across it exceeds the forward voltage (Vf).
- In the reverse bias region, the Zener diode conducts a small leakage current until the reverse voltage (Vr) reaches the Zener voltage (Vz). At this point, the diode enters the reverse breakdown region and the current through it increases rapidly.
- The Zener voltage is the voltage at which the diode enters the reverse breakdown region. It is a characteristic of the diode and is determined by the doping level of the semiconductor material.

## Graphical measurement of forward and reverse resistance
- The forward and reverse resistance of a Zener diode can be determined graphically from its V-I characteristics.
- The forward resistance (Rf) is the slope of the V-I curve in the forward bias region. It can be calculated by dividing the change in voltage by the change in current.
- The reverse resistance (Rr) is the slope of the V-I curve in the reverse bias region before the diode enters the reverse breakdown region. It can be calculated in the same way as the forward resistance.
- The forward and reverse resistance of a Zener diode are typically very different. The forward resistance is usually low, while the reverse resistance is high.
