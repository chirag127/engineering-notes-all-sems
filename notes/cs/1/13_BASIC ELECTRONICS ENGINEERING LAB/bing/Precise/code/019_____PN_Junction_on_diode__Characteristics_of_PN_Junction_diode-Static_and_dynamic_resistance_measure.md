### PN Junction on diode: Characteristics of PN Junction diode-Static and dynamic resistance measurement from graph.

A PN junction diode is a two-terminal electronic device formed by the junction of a p-type semiconductor and an n-type semiconductor. The p-type semiconductor has an excess of positive charge carriers (holes), while the n-type semiconductor has an excess of negative charge carriers (electrons).

The characteristics of a PN junction diode can be studied by measuring its static and dynamic resistance from a graph. Static resistance is the ratio of the DC voltage applied across the diode to the DC current flowing through it. Dynamic resistance, on the other hand, is the ratio of the small change in voltage to the corresponding small change in current.

To measure the static and dynamic resistance of a PN junction diode from a graph, the following steps can be followed:

1. Plot the forward and reverse characteristics of the diode on a graph, with the voltage on the x-axis and the current on the y-axis.
2. To measure the static resistance, select a point on the forward characteristic curve and draw a line parallel to the x-axis from that point to the y-axis. The value of the current at the point where the line intersects the y-axis is the static resistance of the diode at that voltage.
3. To measure the dynamic resistance, select two points on the forward characteristic curve that are close to each other. Draw a line connecting these two points. The slope of this line is the dynamic resistance of the diode at that voltage.

It is important to note that the static and dynamic resistance of a PN junction diode are not constant and vary with the applied voltage. The static resistance decreases with an increase in the applied voltage, while the dynamic resistance increases with an increase in the applied voltage. This is due to the non-linear nature of the diode's characteristic curve.
