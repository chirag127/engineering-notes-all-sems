### 3. Applications of PN Junction diode: Half & Full wave rectifier Measurement of Vrms, Vdc, and ripple factor.

A PN junction diode is a two-terminal electronic device that allows the flow of current in only one direction. It is formed by joining a p-type semiconductor material with an n-type semiconductor material. The PN junction diode has several applications, including its use as a rectifier in power supplies.

1. **Half-wave rectifier**: A half-wave rectifier is a circuit that converts an alternating current (AC) input into a direct current (DC) output. It does this by only allowing the positive half-cycles of the AC input to pass through the diode, while blocking the negative half-cycles. The output of a half-wave rectifier is a pulsating DC voltage.

2. **Full-wave rectifier**: A full-wave rectifier is a circuit that converts an AC input into a DC output by allowing both the positive and negative half-cycles of the AC input to pass through the diode. The output of a full-wave rectifier is a smoother DC voltage than that of a half-wave rectifier.

3. **Measurement of Vrms, Vdc, and ripple factor**: The root mean square (RMS) value of an AC voltage, denoted as Vrms, is a measure of the effective value of the voltage. The DC voltage, denoted as Vdc, is the average value of the voltage over a period of time. The ripple factor is a measure of the smoothness of the DC output of a rectifier. It is defined as the ratio of the RMS value of the AC component of the output voltage to the DC component of the output voltage.
