### 3. Applications of PN Junction diode: Half & Full wave rectifier, Measurement of Vrms, Vdc, and ripple factor.

A PN junction diode is a two-terminal electronic device that allows the flow of current in only one direction. It is formed by the junction of p-type and n-type semiconductors. One of the main applications of a PN junction diode is in rectification, which is the conversion of alternating current (AC) to direct current (DC).

There are two types of rectifiers: half-wave rectifiers and full-wave rectifiers.

- **Half-wave rectifier:** In a half-wave rectifier, only one half of the AC wave is allowed to pass through the diode, while the other half is blocked. This results in a pulsating DC output, with a frequency equal to that of the AC input.

- **Full-wave rectifier:** In a full-wave rectifier, both halves of the AC wave are allowed to pass through the diode, but in opposite directions. This results in a smoother DC output, with a frequency double that of the AC input.

The performance of a rectifier can be measured by its Vrms, Vdc, and ripple factor.

- **Vrms:** The root mean square (RMS) value of the AC input voltage.

- **Vdc:** The average value of the DC output voltage.

- **Ripple factor:** A measure of the smoothness of the DC output. It is defined as the ratio of the RMS value of the AC component of the output voltage to the DC component of the output voltage.

In summary, a PN junction diode can be used in rectification to convert AC to DC. The performance of a rectifier can be measured by its Vrms, Vdc, and ripple factor. A full-wave rectifier provides a smoother DC output than a half-wave rectifier.
