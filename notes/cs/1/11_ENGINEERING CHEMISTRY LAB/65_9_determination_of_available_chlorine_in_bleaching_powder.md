9. Determination of available chlorine in bleaching powder.

The available chlorine in bleaching powder can be determined by a number of methods, including:

1. Volumetric method: This method involves titrating a solution of bleaching powder with a sodium thiosulfate solution of known concentration, using a visual indicator such as starch. The amount of sodium thiosulfate required to react with the available chlorine in the bleaching powder gives a measure of the available chlorine content.

2. Amperometric method: This method involves passing a current through a solution of bleaching powder and measuring the amount of chlorine produced. The available chlorine content is then calculated based on the current and the known reaction rate.

3. Spectrophotometric method: This method involves measuring the absorbance of light by a solution of bleaching powder at a specific wavelength. The available chlorine content is then calculated based on the absorbance and the known extinction coefficient of chlorine.

The method chosen will depend on the accuracy and precision required, as well as the equipment and resources available. Regardless of the method used, it is important to follow proper laboratory procedures, including using accurate and calibrated equipment, and following established protocols for sample preparation and analysis.
