### 8. Determination of the strength of Potassium dichromate using internal indicator.

Potassium dichromate (K2Cr2O7) is a strong oxidizing agent commonly used in chemical titrations to determine the strength of reducing agents. The strength of K2Cr2O7 can be determined using an internal indicator, which changes color in response to the amount of oxidizing agent present in the solution.

One common internal indicator used is ferrous ammonium sulfate (FAS), which changes color from green to yellow as the oxidizing agent oxidizes the iron(II) ions to iron(III) ions. The endpoint of the titration is indicated by the appearance of a permanent yellow color, which indicates that all the iron(II) ions have been oxidized to iron(III) ions.

To determine the strength of K2Cr2O7 using an internal indicator, a solution of FAS is added to a known volume of the K2Cr2O7 solution. The solution is then titrated with a reducing agent of known strength, such as ascorbic acid, until the endpoint is reached. The volume of the reducing agent required to reach the endpoint is used to calculate the strength of the K2Cr2O7 solution.

It is important to carefully control the conditions of the titration, including the temperature and the stirring of the solution, to ensure accurate results. Additionally, it is important to use a fresh, accurately prepared solution of the internal indicator to ensure that the endpoint is clearly defined.
