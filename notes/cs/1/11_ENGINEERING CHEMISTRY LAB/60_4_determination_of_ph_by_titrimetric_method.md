4. Determination of pH by titrimetric method.

The determination of pH by titrimetric method involves measuring the pH of a solution by titrating it with a known volume of a standard solution of a strong acid or base. The pH of the solution is determined by measuring the volume of the titrant required to reach the endpoint of the reaction, which is the point at which the solution changes color or exhibits some other change that indicates the reaction is complete. The pH of the solution can then be calculated using the equation for the reaction, which takes into account the concentration of the titrant, the volume of the titrant used, and the stoichiometry of the reaction. This method is widely used in a variety of applications, including the determination of the pH of water, soil, and other materials. The accuracy and precision of the method depend on the quality of the titrant and the instrumentation used, as well as the skill of the operator. Overall, the determination of pH by titrimetric method is a reliable and widely used method for measuring the pH of a solution.
