Bloom's Taxonomy is a framework for organizing and categorizing learning outcomes. It was developed by Benjamin Bloom in the 1950s and has been widely used in education to design and evaluate learning goals and objectives.

The taxonomy consists of six levels of increasing complexity and specificity, ranging from the most basic knowledge and understanding to the highest level of analysis and evaluation. The six levels are:

1. Remembering: recall or recognition of previously learned material
2. Understanding: comprehension of the meaning of the material
3. Applying: use of the material in a new situation or context
4. Analyzing: breaking down the material into its component parts and understanding their relationships
5. Evaluating: making judgments about the value of the material based on criteria and standards
6. Creating: putting the material together in a new or original way to form a unique product or solution.

The levels of Bloom's Taxonomy can be used to guide the development of learning outcomes, assessments, and instructional materials. By aligning learning goals and objectives with the levels of the taxonomy, educators can ensure that students are challenged to engage with the material at increasing levels of complexity and that their learning is aligned with the intended outcomes.
