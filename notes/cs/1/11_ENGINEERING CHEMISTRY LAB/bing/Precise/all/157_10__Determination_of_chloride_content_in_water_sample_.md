# Determination of Chloride Content in Water Sample

1. Chloride ions are one of the major inorganic anions in water and wastewater.
2. The chloride test measures the level of chloride ions in water.
3. The presence of chloride ions can be an indicator of water pollution from sources such as industrial waste, sewage, and road salt.
4. The most common method for determining the chloride content in water is the argentometric method, also known as the Mohr method.
5. In this method, a measured volume of water sample is titrated with a standard solution of silver nitrate using potassium chromate as an indicator.
6. The end point of the titration is indicated by the formation of a red-brown precipitate of silver chromate.
7. The amount of silver nitrate used in the titration is used to calculate the concentration of chloride ions in the water sample.
8. Other methods for determining the chloride content in water include ion chromatography, ion-selective electrodes, and spectrophotometry.
9. The acceptable level of chloride ions in drinking water varies by location and is regulated by local authorities.
10. High levels of chloride ions in water can have negative effects on the taste and odor of the water, as well as on the environment and aquatic life.
