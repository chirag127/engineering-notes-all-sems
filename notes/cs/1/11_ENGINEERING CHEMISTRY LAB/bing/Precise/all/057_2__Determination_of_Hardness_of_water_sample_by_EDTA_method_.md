# Determination of Hardness of Water Sample by EDTA Method

1. **Introduction:** Hardness of water is a measure of the concentration of calcium and magnesium ions in water. These ions form insoluble compounds with soap, causing the formation of scum and reducing the effectiveness of the soap. The hardness of water can be determined by titration with a chelating agent, such as ethylenediaminetetraacetic acid (EDTA).
2. **Principle:** The EDTA method for determining the hardness of water is based on the complexometric titration of calcium and magnesium ions with EDTA. In this method, a solution of EDTA is added to a water sample until all the calcium and magnesium ions have formed complexes with the EDTA. The end point of the titration is indicated by a color change of a metal ion indicator, such as Eriochrome Black T.
3. **Procedure:** To determine the hardness of a water sample by the EDTA method, the following steps are followed:
    1. A known volume of the water sample is pipetted into a conical flask.
    2. A few drops of buffer solution are added to the flask to adjust the pH of the solution.
    3. A few drops of Eriochrome Black T indicator are added to the flask. The solution turns wine red in color.
    4. The solution is titrated with a standard solution of EDTA until the color changes from wine red to blue.
    5. The volume of EDTA solution used is recorded.
4. **Calculations:** The hardness of the water sample can be calculated from the volume of EDTA solution used in the titration. The concentration of calcium and magnesium ions in the water sample can be calculated from the volume and molarity of the EDTA solution used. The hardness of the water sample is expressed in terms of milligrams of calcium carbonate per liter of water (mg CaCO3/L).
5. **Conclusion:** The EDTA method is a simple and accurate method for determining the hardness of water. It is widely used in water treatment plants and in the analysis of water samples.
