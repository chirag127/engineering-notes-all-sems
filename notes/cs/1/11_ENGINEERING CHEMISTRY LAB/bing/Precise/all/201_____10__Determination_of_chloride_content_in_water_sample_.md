# Determination of Chloride Content in Water Sample

1. Chloride ions are one of the major inorganic anions in water and wastewater.
2. The chloride test measures the level of chloride ions in water.
3. Chloride ions can enter water sources through various means, including natural processes such as the weathering of rocks and human activities such as the use of road salt and the discharge of industrial effluents.
4. High levels of chloride ions in water can have negative impacts on aquatic life and can also affect the taste of drinking water.
5. The most common method for determining the chloride content in water is the argentometric method, also known as the Mohr method.
6. In this method, a measured volume of water sample is titrated with a standard solution of silver nitrate using potassium chromate as an indicator.
7. The end point of the titration is indicated by the formation of a red-brown precipitate of silver chromate.
8. The amount of silver nitrate used in the titration is used to calculate the concentration of chloride ions in the water sample.
9. Other methods for determining the chloride content in water include ion chromatography, ion-selective electrodes, and colorimetric methods.
10. Regular monitoring of the chloride content in water sources is important for maintaining the health of aquatic ecosystems and ensuring the quality of drinking water.
