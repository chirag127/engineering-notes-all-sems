# 12. Preparation of Urea formaldehyde (UF) resin

Urea formaldehyde (UF) resin is a type of thermosetting polymer that is commonly used in the production of adhesives, finishes, and molded objects. Here are the steps involved in the preparation of UF resin:

1. **Reacting Urea and Formaldehyde:** The first step in the preparation of UF resin is the reaction of urea and formaldehyde. This reaction takes place in an aqueous solution, with the molar ratio of urea to formaldehyde typically ranging from 1:1.5 to 1:2.5. The reaction is carried out at a pH of 7-8 and a temperature of 70-90°C.

2. **Condensation:** The next step is the condensation of the reaction product. This is achieved by heating the reaction mixture to a temperature of 90-100°C and adjusting the pH to 4.5-5.5. This causes the formation of methylene and methylene ether linkages between the urea molecules, resulting in the formation of a viscous resin.

3. **Concentration:** The resin is then concentrated by removing excess water through evaporation. This is typically done under vacuum at a temperature of 60-80°C.

4. **Curing:** The final step in the preparation of UF resin is curing. This involves the addition of a curing agent, such as ammonium chloride or ammonium sulfate, to the resin. The curing agent causes the resin to crosslink, forming a hard, insoluble polymer. The curing process typically takes place at a temperature of 100-130°C.

These are the basic steps involved in the preparation of UF resin. The exact conditions and proportions may vary depending on the specific application and desired properties of the final product.
