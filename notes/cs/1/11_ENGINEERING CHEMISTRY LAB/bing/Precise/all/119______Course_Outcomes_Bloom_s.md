# Course Outcomes Bloom’s

Bloom's Taxonomy is a framework for categorizing educational goals and objectives into different levels of complexity and specificity. It is commonly used to design curriculum and assess student learning. The taxonomy is divided into six levels, with each level building upon the previous one. The six levels are:

1. **Remembering**: This level involves recalling information from memory. It is the most basic level of learning and includes tasks such as defining, listing, and recognizing.
2. **Understanding**: This level involves comprehending the meaning of information. It includes tasks such as explaining, summarizing, and interpreting.
3. **Applying**: This level involves using information in a new situation. It includes tasks such as solving problems, demonstrating, and implementing.
4. **Analyzing**: This level involves breaking down information into parts and understanding their relationships. It includes tasks such as comparing, contrasting, and categorizing.
5. **Evaluating**: This level involves making judgments about the value of information. It includes tasks such as critiquing, justifying, and assessing.
6. **Creating**: This level involves combining information to form a new whole. It includes tasks such as designing, constructing, and planning.

Course outcomes are statements that describe what students should know or be able to do by the end of a course. These outcomes are often aligned with Bloom's Taxonomy to ensure that students are achieving higher levels of learning. For example, a course outcome might state that by the end of the course, students will be able to analyze and evaluate arguments in a particular field of study. This outcome aligns with the analyzing and evaluating levels of Bloom's Taxonomy. By designing course outcomes that align with Bloom's Taxonomy, educators can ensure that their courses are promoting higher levels of learning and critical thinking.
