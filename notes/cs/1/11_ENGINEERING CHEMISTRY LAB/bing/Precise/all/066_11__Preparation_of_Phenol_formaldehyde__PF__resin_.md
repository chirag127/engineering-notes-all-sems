# Preparation of Phenol formaldehyde (PF) resin

Phenol formaldehyde (PF) resins are synthetic polymers obtained by the reaction of phenol or substituted phenol with formaldehyde. They were the first commercial synthetic resins (plastics) and are used as the basis for Bakelite.

- **Preparation**: A step-growth polymerization reaction that can be either acid- or base-catalyzed is used for the preparation of Phenol-formaldehyde resins. Since formaldehyde exists as a dynamic equilibrium of methylene glycol oligomers for the most part in the solution, it is a reactive derivative of methane.
- **Control PF resin**: Control PF resin with a formaldehyde to phenol (F/P) molar ratio of 2.0 can be synthesized with phenol charged once and formaldehyde charged twice. NaOH (40% solution) can be divided into two portions and charged with formaldehyde respectively.
- **Heating**: Phenol, formaldehyde, water, and catalyst are mixed in the desired amount, depending on the resin to be formed, and are then heated. The first part of the reaction, at around 70 °C, forms a thick reddish-brown tacky material, which is rich in hydroxymethyl and benzylic ether groups.
