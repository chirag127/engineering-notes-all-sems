### 9. Determination of available chlorine in bleaching powder

Bleaching powder, also known as calcium hypochlorite, is a chemical compound with the formula Ca(ClO)2. It is widely used as a disinfectant and bleaching agent. The available chlorine in bleaching powder refers to the amount of chlorine that can be released as a disinfectant when the powder is dissolved in water.

Here are the steps to determine the available chlorine in bleaching powder:

1. Weigh accurately about 1g of the sample of bleaching powder and transfer it to a conical flask.
2. Add about 20 ml of distilled water to the flask and shake well to dissolve the bleaching powder.
3. Add 1-2 ml of potassium iodide solution (10%) and 2-3 ml of acetic acid (20%) to the flask.
4. Titrate the liberated iodine with sodium thiosulphate solution (0.1 N) using starch as an indicator.
5. The end point is reached when the blue color of the starch-iodine complex disappears.
6. Repeat the titration with a blank solution (without the bleaching powder) to determine the amount of sodium thiosulphate required for the blank.
7. Subtract the volume of sodium thiosulphate required for the blank from the volume required for the sample to obtain the volume of sodium thiosulphate required for the available chlorine in the sample.
8. Calculate the percentage of available chlorine in the sample using the formula: % available chlorine = (Volume of sodium thiosulphate required for sample x Normality of sodium thiosulphate x 35.5) / Weight of sample.

This is the standard procedure for determining the available chlorine in bleaching powder. It is important to follow the steps carefully and accurately to obtain reliable results.
