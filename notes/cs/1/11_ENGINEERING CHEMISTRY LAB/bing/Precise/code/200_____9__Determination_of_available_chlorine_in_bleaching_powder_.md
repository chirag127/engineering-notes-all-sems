### 9. Determination of available chlorine in bleaching powder

Bleaching powder, also known as calcium hypochlorite, is a chemical compound with the formula Ca(ClO)2. It is widely used as a disinfectant and bleaching agent. The available chlorine in bleaching powder refers to the amount of chlorine that can be released as a disinfectant when the powder is dissolved in water.

The available chlorine in bleaching powder can be determined by the following steps:

1. Weigh a sample of bleaching powder and dissolve it in water.
2. Add excess potassium iodide (KI) to the solution. The iodide ions will react with the available chlorine to produce iodine.
3. Titrate the iodine produced with a standard solution of sodium thiosulfate (Na2S2O3) using starch as an indicator. The reaction between iodine and thiosulfate is as follows: 2S2O3^2- + I2 -> S4O6^2- + 2I^-
4. From the volume of thiosulfate solution used, the amount of iodine produced can be calculated. Since the reaction between iodine and thiosulfate is 1:1, the amount of available chlorine in the bleaching powder can be determined.

This method is known as iodometric titration and is commonly used to determine the available chlorine in bleaching powder. It is a simple, accurate, and reliable method for this purpose.
