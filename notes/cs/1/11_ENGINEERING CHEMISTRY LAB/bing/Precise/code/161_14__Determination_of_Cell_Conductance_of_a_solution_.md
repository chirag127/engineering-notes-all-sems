# Determination of Cell Conductance of a Solution

1. Conductance is the measure of the ability of a solution to conduct an electric current.
2. The conductance of a solution is determined by the concentration and mobility of the ions present in the solution.
3. The cell conductance of a solution is the conductance of the solution measured between two electrodes of a conductivity cell.
4. The cell constant is the ratio of the distance between the electrodes to the area of the electrodes.
5. The cell conductance is calculated by multiplying the measured conductance by the cell constant.
6. The cell conductance can be determined using a conductivity meter, which measures the conductance of the solution and automatically applies the cell constant to calculate the cell conductance.
7. The cell conductance can also be determined using a Wheatstone bridge circuit, where the unknown conductance of the solution is compared to a known conductance to determine the cell conductance.
8. The cell conductance is affected by temperature, and temperature compensation is necessary to obtain accurate measurements.
9. The cell conductance can be used to determine the concentration of ions in a solution, by comparing the measured cell conductance to a calibration curve obtained using solutions of known concentrations.
