### 7. Determination of the strength of Ferrous ammonium sulfate using external indicator

Ferrous ammonium sulfate, also known as Mohr's salt, is a double salt of iron sulfate and ammonium sulfate. It is commonly used in the determination of the strength of oxidizing agents using an external indicator. Here are the steps involved in the process:

1. A solution of ferrous ammonium sulfate is prepared by dissolving a known amount of the salt in water.
2. The solution is then titrated against the oxidizing agent whose strength is to be determined.
3. An external indicator, such as potassium permanganate or diphenylamine, is used to determine the end point of the titration.
4. The end point is reached when the color of the indicator changes, indicating that all the ferrous ions have been oxidized to ferric ions.
5. The strength of the oxidizing agent can then be calculated using the volume of the solution used and the concentration of the ferrous ammonium sulfate solution.

This method is commonly used in analytical chemistry to determine the strength of oxidizing agents such as potassium permanganate, hydrogen peroxide, and potassium dichromate. It is a simple and accurate method that can be easily performed in a laboratory setting.
