### 3. Determination of Alkalinity of water sample

Alkalinity is a measure of the capacity of water to neutralize acids. It is determined by measuring the amount of acid needed to lower the pH of the water sample to a specific value. The alkalinity of water is primarily due to the presence of bicarbonate, carbonate, and hydroxide ions.

The procedure for determining the alkalinity of a water sample is as follows:

1. Collect a water sample in a clean container.
2. Measure the pH of the water sample using a pH meter.
3. Add a known amount of acid to the water sample while stirring.
4. Continue adding acid until the pH of the water sample reaches a specific value, usually 4.5.
5. Record the amount of acid added to the water sample.
6. Calculate the alkalinity of the water sample using the recorded data.

The alkalinity of the water sample is expressed in milligrams per liter (mg/L) of calcium carbonate (CaCO3). A water sample with high alkalinity can neutralize more acid than a water sample with low alkalinity. Alkalinity is an important parameter in water treatment and environmental monitoring. It is used to assess the buffering capacity of water and its ability to resist changes in pH.
