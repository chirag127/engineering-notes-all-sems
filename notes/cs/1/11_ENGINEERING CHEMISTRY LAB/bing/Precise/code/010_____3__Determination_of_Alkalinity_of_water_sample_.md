### 3. Determination of Alkalinity of water sample

Alkalinity is a measure of the ability of water to neutralize acids. It is an important parameter in water quality analysis, as it provides information about the buffering capacity of water.

1. Alkalinity is determined by titrating a water sample with a standard acid solution, usually sulfuric acid or hydrochloric acid, to a specific pH endpoint.
2. The endpoint is usually determined by a color change of an indicator, such as phenolphthalein or methyl orange.
3. The amount of acid required to reach the endpoint is used to calculate the alkalinity of the water sample.
4. Alkalinity is typically reported in milligrams per liter (mg/L) as calcium carbonate (CaCO3).
5. Alkalinity can also be determined by using an automatic titrator or by measuring the pH of the water sample before and after the addition of a known amount of acid.
