### 3. Determination of Alkalinity of water sample

Alkalinity is a measure of the ability of water to neutralize acids. It is an important parameter in water quality analysis, as it provides information about the buffering capacity of water. The following points explain the determination of alkalinity in a water sample:

1. The water sample is collected in a clean container and its pH is measured.
2. A known volume of the water sample is taken and titrated against a standard acid solution, usually sulfuric acid or hydrochloric acid.
3. The titration is carried out using a suitable indicator, such as phenolphthalein or methyl orange, which changes color at the endpoint of the titration.
4. The volume of the acid solution required to reach the endpoint is noted and used to calculate the alkalinity of the water sample.
5. The alkalinity is usually expressed in terms of milligrams of calcium carbonate (CaCO3) per liter of water.

This is a brief overview of the determination of alkalinity in a water sample. It is important to follow standard procedures and use accurate equipment to obtain reliable results.
