### 4. Determination of pH by titrimetric method

Titrimetric method is a technique used to determine the pH of a solution. This method involves the use of a titrant, which is a solution of known concentration, to react with the solution being tested. The following are the steps involved in the determination of pH by titrimetric method:

1. **Preparation of the titrant**: The titrant is prepared by dissolving a known amount of a strong acid or base in a known volume of water. The concentration of the titrant is then calculated.

2. **Selection of an appropriate indicator**: An indicator is a substance that changes color at a specific pH range. The indicator is chosen based on the pH range of the solution being tested.

3. **Titration**: The solution being tested is placed in a flask, and the indicator is added. The titrant is then added dropwise from a burette while stirring the solution. The addition of the titrant is continued until the indicator changes color, indicating that the endpoint of the titration has been reached.

4. **Calculation of pH**: The volume of the titrant used is recorded, and the number of moles of the titrant is calculated. The number of moles of the acid or base in the solution being tested is then calculated using the stoichiometry of the reaction. The pH of the solution is then calculated using the concentration of the acid or base.

This method is accurate and precise, and it is commonly used in laboratories to determine the pH of solutions. However, it requires the use of specialized equipment and chemicals, and it may not be suitable for all applications.
