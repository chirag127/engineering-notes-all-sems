### 10. Determination of chloride content in water sample

1. Chloride content in water can be determined using a titration method known as the Mohr method.
2. The Mohr method involves titrating the water sample with a silver nitrate solution.
3. A chromate indicator is used to signal the endpoint of the titration.
4. The silver nitrate reacts with the chloride ions in the water sample to form a precipitate of silver chloride.
5. The chromate ions react with the excess silver ions to form a red precipitate of silver chromate, indicating the endpoint of the titration.
6. The volume of silver nitrate solution used in the titration can be used to calculate the concentration of chloride ions in the water sample.
7. The concentration of chloride ions can be converted to the mass of chloride in the water sample by multiplying by the molar mass of chloride.
8. The mass of chloride can be expressed as a percentage of the total mass of the water sample to give the percentage chloride content.
9. The Mohr method is a simple and accurate method for determining the chloride content in water samples.
10. Other methods for determining chloride content in water samples include ion chromatography and ion-selective electrode analysis.
