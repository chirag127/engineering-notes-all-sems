# 5. Determination of surface tension of given liquid

- Surface tension is a property of liquids that arises from the intermolecular forces between the molecules at the surface layer.
- Surface tension causes the liquid surface to behave like a stretched elastic membrane, and to minimize its area for a given volume.
- Surface tension can be measured by various methods, such as the capillary rise method, the drop weight method, the ring method, and the stalagmometer method.
- In this experiment, we will use the stalagmometer method to determine the surface tension of a given liquid.
- A stalagmometer is a device that consists of a glass tube with a bulb at one end and a fine nozzle at the other end.
- The tube is filled with the liquid to be tested, and then inverted over a graduated cylinder.
- The liquid drops out of the nozzle due to gravity, and the number of drops and the volume of liquid collected in the cylinder are recorded.
- The surface tension of the liquid is given by the formula:

$$\gamma = \frac{4\pi r^3 \rho g}{3V}$$

where $\gamma$ is the surface tension, $r$ is the radius of the nozzle, $\rho$ is the density of the liquid, $g$ is the acceleration due to gravity, and $V$ is the average volume of one drop.

- To perform the experiment, we need the following apparatus:

  - A stalagmometer
  - A graduated cylinder
  - A thermometer
  - A stopwatch
  - A balance
  - A liquid sample (such as water, alcohol, or glycerin)

- The procedure of the experiment is as follows:

  1. Clean and dry the stalagmometer and the graduated cylinder.
  2. Fill the stalagmometer with the liquid sample up to the mark on the bulb, and wipe off any excess liquid from the nozzle.
  3. Invert the stalagmometer over the graduated cylinder, and hold it vertically.
  4. Start the stopwatch and count the number of drops falling from the nozzle until the liquid level reaches the lower mark on the bulb.
  5. Stop the stopwatch and record the time taken, the number of drops, and the volume of liquid collected in the cylinder.
  6. Repeat the steps 2 to 5 for at least three trials, and calculate the average values of the time, the number of drops, and the volume of liquid.
  7. Measure the mass of the empty stalagmometer and the mass of the stalagmometer filled with the liquid sample, and calculate the mass of the liquid.
  8. Measure the temperature of the liquid sample, and look up the density of the liquid at that temperature from a reference table.
  9. Calculate the average volume of one drop by dividing the volume of liquid by the number of drops.
  10. Calculate the radius of the nozzle by using the formula:

  $$r = \sqrt{\frac{3V}{4\pi \rho g}}$$

  where $V$ is the average volume of one drop, $\rho$ is the density of the liquid, and $g$ is the acceleration due to gravity.
  11. Calculate the surface tension of the liquid by using the formula:

  $$\gamma = \frac{4\pi r^3 \rho g}{3V}$$

  where $\gamma$ is the surface tension, $r$ is the radius of the nozzle, $\rho$ is the density of the liquid, $g$ is the acceleration due to gravity, and $V$ is the average volume of one drop.
