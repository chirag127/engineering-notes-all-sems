### 1. Calibration of Analytical Equipment and apparatus

- Calibration is the process of evaluating and adjusting the precision and accuracy of measurement equipment.
- Calibration ensures that the equipment and apparatus produce valid and reliable results that are consistent with the standards and specifications.
- Calibration is required for all analytical equipment and apparatus that are used for quantitative measurements, such as balances, pipettes, thermometers, pH meters, spectrophotometers, etc.
- Calibration should be performed at regular intervals, depending on the frequency of use, the stability of the equipment, the manufacturer's recommendations, and the in-house standard operating procedures.
- Calibration should be done by qualified and trained personnel, using appropriate reference standards and methods.
- Calibration should be documented and recorded, including the date, the equipment identification, the reference standards, the calibration method, the results, and the acceptance criteria.
- Calibration should be verified by using check standards or quality control samples before and after the calibration.
- Calibration should be repeated if the equipment is repaired, modified, relocated, or suspected of malfunctioning.
