### Viscosity, Conductance of Solution, Chloride and Iron Content in the Water

- Viscosity is a measure of the resistance of a fluid to flow. It depends on the intermolecular forces, temperature, and composition of the fluid. Viscosity decreases with increasing temperature and decreases with increasing solute concentration. Viscosity affects the transport of mass, heat, and momentum in fluids.
- Conductance of solution is a measure of the ability of a solution to conduct electric current. It depends on the concentration, type, and mobility of the ions in the solution, as well as the temperature and the geometry of the electrodes. Conductance increases with increasing ion concentration and mobility, and decreases with increasing viscosity.
- Chloride and iron content in the water are indicators of the quality and purity of the water. Chloride ions are present in seawater, brackish water, and some groundwater sources. They can affect the taste, corrosion, and conductivity of the water. Iron ions are present in some groundwater sources, especially in areas with iron-rich rocks. They can cause staining, discoloration, and taste problems in the water. Chloride and iron content can be measured by various methods, such as titration, colorimetry, spectrophotometry, or ion chromatography .
