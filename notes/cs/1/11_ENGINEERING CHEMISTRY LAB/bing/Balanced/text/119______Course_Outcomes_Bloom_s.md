### Course Outcomes Bloom’s

- Course outcomes are statements that describe what students should be able to do or demonstrate after completing a course.
- Bloom’s taxonomy is a framework for classifying educational objectives into six levels of cognitive complexity: remember, understand, apply, analyze, evaluate, and create.
- Course outcomes should be aligned with Bloom’s taxonomy to ensure that students are challenged to develop higher-order thinking skills and not just memorize facts.
- Course outcomes should also be specific, measurable, achievable, relevant, and time-bound (SMART) to facilitate assessment and feedback.
- Course outcomes should be written using action verbs that indicate the level of cognitive skill required, such as define, explain, compare, contrast, solve, design, etc.
- Course outcomes should be communicated to students at the beginning of the course and throughout the course to guide their learning and expectations.
- Course outcomes should be evaluated periodically to measure student achievement and identify areas for improvement.
