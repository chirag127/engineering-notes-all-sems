# Determination of Alkalinity of Water Sample

- Alkalinity is a measure of the capacity of water to neutralize acids. It is mainly caused by the presence of bicarbonate, carbonate, and hydroxide ions in water.
- Alkalinity is important for aquatic life, water quality, and water treatment. It affects the pH, buffering capacity, and corrosion potential of water.
- Alkalinity can be determined by titrating a water sample with a standard acid solution, such as sulfuric acid, and measuring the volume of acid required to reach a specific pH endpoint.
- The most common endpoints for alkalinity titration are pH 4.5, pH 8.3, and pH 10.0. These correspond to the equivalence points of carbonic acid, bicarbonate, and carbonate, respectively.
- The total alkalinity of water is the sum of the alkalinity due to bicarbonate, carbonate, and hydroxide. It can be calculated from the volume of acid used to reach pH 4.5.
- The bicarbonate alkalinity of water is the alkalinity due to bicarbonate only. It can be calculated from the volume of acid used to reach pH 8.3.
- The carbonate alkalinity of water is the alkalinity due to carbonate and hydroxide. It can be calculated from the volume of acid used to reach pH 10.0.
- The hydroxide alkalinity of water is the alkalinity due to hydroxide only. It can be calculated from the difference between the carbonate alkalinity and the volume of acid used to reach pH 10.0.
- The phenolphthalein alkalinity of water is the alkalinity due to carbonate and hydroxide that can be detected by the color change of phenolphthalein indicator. It is equal to the volume of acid used to reach pH 8.3 minus the volume of acid used to reach pH 10.0.
- The methyl orange alkalinity of water is the alkalinity due to bicarbonate and carbonate that can be detected by the color change of methyl orange indicator. It is equal to the volume of acid used to reach pH 4.5 minus the volume of acid used to reach pH 8.3.
