### 7. Determination of the strength of Ferrous ammonium sulfate using external indicator.

- Ferrous ammonium sulfate, also known as Mohr's salt, is a double salt of iron(II) sulfate and ammonium sulfate, with the formula (NH4)2Fe(SO4)2·6H2O.
- It is often used as a standard solution for titrating oxidizing agents, such as potassium permanganate or potassium dichromate, in redox reactions.
- The strength of a ferrous ammonium sulfate solution can be determined by titrating it with a standard solution of potassium permanganate, using an external indicator such as starch or potassium ferricyanide.
- The reaction between ferrous ammonium sulfate and potassium permanganate is:

(NH4)2Fe(SO4)2 + KMnO4 + H2SO4 -> Fe2(SO4)3 + K2SO4 + (NH4)2SO4 + MnSO4 + H2O

- The reaction between ferrous ammonium sulfate and potassium dichromate is:

(NH4)2Fe(SO4)2 + K2Cr2O7 + H2SO4 -> Fe2(SO4)3 + K2SO4 + (NH4)2SO4 + Cr2(SO4)3 + H2O

- The end point of the titration is detected by the change of color of the external indicator. Starch turns blue in the presence of iodine, which is formed by the reaction of excess potassium permanganate and potassium iodide. Potassium ferricyanide turns brown in the presence of iron(III) ions, which are formed by the oxidation of ferrous ammonium sulfate.
- The procedure for the titration is as follows:

  - Prepare a standard solution of potassium permanganate or potassium dichromate of known concentration and a solution of potassium iodide or potassium ferricyanide as the external indicator.
  - Pipette a known volume of ferrous ammonium sulfate solution into a conical flask and add some dilute sulfuric acid.
  - Fill a burette with the standard solution of potassium permanganate or potassium dichromate and note the initial reading.
  - Add the external indicator solution to the conical flask and swirl the mixture.
  - Titrate the ferrous ammonium sulfate solution with the standard solution until the color of the external indicator changes.
  - Note the final reading of the burette and calculate the volume of the standard solution used.
  - Repeat the titration until concordant results are obtained.
  - Calculate the strength of the ferrous ammonium sulfate solution using the following formula:

Strength of ferrous ammonium sulfate = (Volume of standard solution x Normality of standard solution x Equivalent weight of ferrous ammonium sulfate) / (Volume of ferrous ammonium sulfate solution x 1000)
