# CO2 Molecular Properties and Surface Tension

- Carbon dioxide (CO2) is a linear molecule with a bond length of 116.3 pm between the carbon and the oxygen atoms and a bond angle of 180 degrees.
- CO2 is a non-polar molecule with a dipole moment of zero. It has four vibrational modes: symmetric stretching, asymmetric stretching, and two degenerate bending modes.
- CO2 is a gas at standard temperature and pressure, with a density of 1.977 kg/m3 and a molar mass of 44.0095 g/mol .
- CO2 can be dissolved in water and other solvents, forming weak carbonic acid (H2CO3) and bicarbonate (HCO3-) and carbonate (CO3 2-) ions. The solubility of CO2 in water depends on the temperature, pressure, and pH of the solution.
- Surface tension is the energy or work required to increase the surface area of a liquid due to intermolecular forces. It is measured in units of N/m or J/m2.
- The surface tension of pure water at 20 °C is 72.8 mN/m. The surface tension of water decreases as the temperature increases or as solutes are added to the water.
- The surface tension of CO2-loaded aqueous solutions depends on the concentration of CO2, the type and concentration of the solvent (e.g. monoethanolamine, MEA), and the temperature of the solution .
- According to some experimental studies, the surface tension of CO2-loaded aqueous MEA solutions decreases as the CO2 loading increases, as the MEA concentration decreases, or as the temperature increases .
- The surface tension of CO2-loaded aqueous MEA solutions can be correlated by empirical equations or models based on the Eötvös or Szyszkowski equations, which relate the surface tension to the temperature, density, and molecular weight of the solution .
