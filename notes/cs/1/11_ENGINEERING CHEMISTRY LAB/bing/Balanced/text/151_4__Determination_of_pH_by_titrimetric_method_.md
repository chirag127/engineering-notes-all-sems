# Determination of pH by titrimetric method

- Titrimetric method is a technique of quantitative analysis that involves measuring the volume of a solution of known concentration (titrant) that is required to react completely with a solution of unknown concentration (analyte).
- pH is a measure of the acidity or alkalinity of a solution, defined as the negative logarithm of the hydrogen ion concentration: pH = -log[H+].
- Determination of pH by titrimetric method involves using a pH meter to monitor the change in pH of the analyte solution as the titrant is added gradually.
- A pH meter consists of a glass electrode that is sensitive to hydrogen ions and a reference electrode that provides a constant potential. The potential difference between the two electrodes is proportional to the pH of the solution.
- A plot of the pH of the analyte solution versus the volume of the titrant added is called a pH curve or a titration curve. The shape of the curve depends on the nature and strength of the acid and base involved in the titration.
- The point where the titration curve changes slope sharply is called the endpoint or the equivalence point. It indicates that the analyte and the titrant have reacted stoichiometrically and the solution is neutralized.
- The volume of the titrant required to reach the endpoint can be used to calculate the concentration of the analyte using the following formula: C_aV_a = C_bV_b, where C_a and C_b are the concentrations of the analyte and the titrant, and V_a and V_b are the volumes of the analyte and the titrant.
- The accuracy and precision of the titrimetric method depend on the choice of the titrant, the calibration of the pH meter, the detection of the endpoint, and the quality of the glassware and pipettes.
