### CO-3 Measure the hardness and alkalinity of the water. K3

- Hardness of water is the measure of the concentration of calcium and magnesium ions in water. It is expressed in terms of equivalent calcium carbonate (CaCO3).
- Alkalinity of water is the measure of the capacity of water to neutralize acids. It is mainly due to the presence of bicarbonates, carbonates and hydroxides of calcium, magnesium and other metals. It is expressed in terms of equivalent calcium carbonate (CaCO3) as well.
- To measure the hardness of water, the following steps are followed:
  - A standard solution of ethylenediaminetetraacetic acid (EDTA) is prepared and its concentration is determined by titrating it against a standard solution of calcium carbonate.
  - A known volume of water sample is taken in a conical flask and a few drops of eriochrome black T indicator are added. The indicator turns the solution wine red.
  - The EDTA solution is added from a burette until the color changes from wine red to blue, indicating the end point of the titration.
  - The volume of EDTA used is noted and the hardness of water is calculated using the formula:

    Hardness (mg/L as CaCO3) = (Volume of EDTA x Normality of EDTA x 1000) / Volume of water sample

- To measure the alkalinity of water, the following steps are followed:
  - A standard solution of sulfuric acid (H2SO4) is prepared and its concentration is determined by titrating it against a standard solution of sodium carbonate (Na2CO3).
  - A known volume of water sample is taken in a conical flask and a few drops of phenolphthalein indicator are added. The indicator turns the solution pink if the pH is above 8.3.
  - The H2SO4 solution is added from a burette until the color changes from pink to colorless, indicating the end point of the first titration. The volume of H2SO4 used is noted and the phenolphthalein alkalinity (P-alkalinity) is calculated using the formula:

    P-alkalinity (mg/L as CaCO3) = (Volume of H2SO4 x Normality of H2SO4 x 1000 x 50) / Volume of water sample

  - A few drops of methyl orange indicator are added to the same flask. The indicator turns the solution orange if the pH is between 3.1 and 4.4.
  - The H2SO4 solution is added from the same burette until the color changes from orange to red, indicating the end point of the second titration. The total volume of H2SO4 used is noted and the methyl orange alkalinity (M-alkalinity) is calculated using the formula:

    M-alkalinity (mg/L as CaCO3) = (Total volume of H2SO4 x Normality of H2SO4 x 1000 x 50) / Volume of water sample

- The total alkalinity of water is the sum of P-alkalinity and M-alkalinity. It can also be calculated using the formula:

    Total alkalinity (mg/L as CaCO3) = (Volume of H2SO4 x Normality of H2SO4 x 1000 x 100) / Volume of water sample

- The types of alkalinity of water are:
  - Phenolphthalein alkalinity: It is due to the presence of hydroxides and half of the carbonates in water. It is also called hydroxide-carbonate alkalinity.
  - Methyl orange alkalinity: It is due to the presence of carbonates and bicarbonates in water. It is also called carbonate-bicarbonate alkalinity.
  - Total alkalinity: It is due to the presence of hydroxides, carbonates and bicarbonates in water. It is also called hydroxide-carbonate-bicarbonate alkalinity.
