# Determination of Hardness of Water by EDTA Method

- Hardness of water is the measure of the concentration of calcium and magnesium ions in water.
- EDTA (ethylenediaminetetraacetic acid) is a chelating agent that can form stable complexes with metal ions, such as Ca2+ and Mg2+.
- The EDTA method is a complexometric titration method that can be used to determine the total hardness or the calcium hardness of water samples.
- The principle of the EDTA method is that when a small amount of a dye such as eriochrome black T (EBT) is added to a water sample at a pH of 10 ± 0.1, the Ca2+ and Mg2+ ions form wine-red colored complexes with EBT. However, when EDTA is added as a titrant, it has a stronger affinity towards Ca2+ and Mg2+ ions and displaces EBT from the complexes, forming colorless EDTA-metal complexes. The end point of the titration is marked by the color change of the solution from wine-red to blue.

- The procedure for the EDTA method is as follows:

  - Take a sample volume of 20 mL (V mL) and dilute it to 40 mL by adding 20 mL of distilled water in an Erlenmeyer flask.
  - Add 1 mL of ammonia buffer to bring the pH to 10 ± 0.1.
  - Add 1 or 2 drops of EBT indicator solution.
  - Titrate the sample with EDTA solution of known concentration (C mg/mL) with vigorous shaking until the wine-red color just turns blue.
  - Record the volume of EDTA solution used (T mL).

- The calculation of the hardness of water by EDTA method is based on the following formula:

  - Hardness (mg/L as CaCO3) = (C × T × 1000) / V

  - Where C is the concentration of EDTA solution in mg/mL, T is the volume of EDTA solution used in mL, and V is the volume of water sample taken in mL.

  - The hardness can be reported as total hardness or calcium hardness, depending on whether the water sample contains only calcium ions or both calcium and magnesium ions.
