# CO-4 Know the fundamental concepts of the preparation of phenol

- Phenol is an aromatic compound with a hydroxyl group (-OH) attached to a benzene ring.
- Phenol is also known as carbolic acid or phenic acid.
- Phenol has a pungent smell and is corrosive to the skin and eyes.
- Phenol is used as an antiseptic, disinfectant, solvent, and precursor for many industrial chemicals and polymers.
- Phenol can be prepared from various sources, such as coal tar, benzene, cumene, and chlorobenzene.

## Preparation of phenol from coal tar

- Coal tar is a by-product of the destructive distillation of coal.
- Coal tar contains about 1% of phenol along with other aromatic compounds, such as naphthalene, anthracene, and pyridine.
- Phenol can be extracted from coal tar by fractional distillation, followed by washing with sodium hydroxide solution to remove acidic impurities.
- The purified phenol can be crystallized from water or distilled under reduced pressure.

## Preparation of phenol from benzene

- Benzene is a six-carbon aromatic hydrocarbon with a cyclic structure and alternating double bonds.
- Benzene can be converted to phenol by two methods: direct hydroxylation and indirect hydroxylation.

### Direct hydroxylation

- Direct hydroxylation is the reaction of benzene with concentrated sulfuric acid and concentrated nitric acid to form nitrobenzene, which is then reduced to phenylamine (aniline) by tin and hydrochloric acid.
- Phenylamine is then diazotized by sodium nitrite and hydrochloric acid to form phenyldiazonium chloride, which is then hydrolyzed by water to form phenol and nitrogen gas.

### Indirect hydroxylation

- Indirect hydroxylation is the reaction of benzene with chlorine or bromine in the presence of an iron catalyst to form chlorobenzene or bromobenzene, which is then hydrolyzed by sodium hydroxide solution under high temperature and pressure to form phenol and sodium chloride or sodium bromide.

## Preparation of phenol from cumene

- Cumene is a common name for isopropylbenzene, which is an alkylbenzene with an isopropyl group (-CH(CH3)2) attached to a benzene ring.
- Cumene can be oxidized by air in the presence of a cobalt catalyst to form cumene hydroperoxide, which is then decomposed by sulfuric acid to form phenol and acetone.

## Preparation of phenol from chlorobenzene

- Chlorobenzene is an aromatic compound with a chlorine atom attached to a benzene ring.
- Chlorobenzene can be hydrolyzed by sodium hydroxide solution under high temperature and pressure to form phenol and sodium chloride.
- This method is similar to the indirect hydroxylation of benzene, but it requires more energy and produces more waste.
