# Calibration of Analytical Equipment and Apparatus

- Calibration is the process of comparing the output of an analytical instrument or device with a known standard of accuracy and adjusting it accordingly.
- Calibration ensures that the instrument or device is working properly and giving reliable and accurate results.
- Calibration is essential for maintaining the quality and validity of analytical data and results.
- Calibration should be performed periodically, before and after each use, or whenever there is a change in the operating conditions or environment of the instrument or device.
- Calibration should be documented and recorded for traceability and verification purposes.
- Calibration can be classified into two types: external and internal calibration.
  - External calibration involves using a reference material or standard solution of known concentration or purity to calibrate the instrument or device. The reference material or standard solution should be similar to the sample being analyzed and have a certified value and uncertainty.
  - Internal calibration involves using an internal standard or a calibration curve to calibrate the instrument or device. The internal standard is a substance that is added to the sample and has a known response or signal. The calibration curve is a plot of the response or signal of the instrument or device versus the concentration or amount of the analyte.
- Some examples of analytical equipment and apparatus that require calibration are:
  - Balances and weighing devices: used to measure the mass or weight of samples and reagents. They should be calibrated using standard weights of known mass and accuracy.
  - Volumetric devices: used to measure or deliver a fixed volume of liquid. They include pipettes, burettes, flasks, cylinders, and syringes. They should be calibrated using distilled water at a specific temperature and pressure.
  - Spectrophotometers: used to measure the absorbance or transmittance of light by a sample. They should be calibrated using blank solutions, reference solutions, or standard curves.
  - pH meters: used to measure the acidity or alkalinity of a solution. They should be calibrated using buffer solutions of known pH and temperature.
  - Thermometers: used to measure the temperature of samples and reagents. They should be calibrated using ice-water baths, boiling water baths, or fixed-point devices.
