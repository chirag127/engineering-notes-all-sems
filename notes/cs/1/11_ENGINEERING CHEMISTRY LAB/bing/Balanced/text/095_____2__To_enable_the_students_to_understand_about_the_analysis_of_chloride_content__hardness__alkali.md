### 2. To enable the students to understand about the analysis of chloride content, hardness, alkalinity of water.

- Chloride content is the amount of chloride ions (Cl-) present in water. Chloride ions are derived from various sources, such as natural salts, industrial effluents, sewage, fertilizers, etc. Chloride content is an important parameter for assessing the quality of water, as high levels of chloride can cause corrosion, taste and odor problems, and health issues.
- Hardness is the measure of the ability of water to precipitate soap. Hard water contains high concentrations of calcium (Ca2+) and magnesium (Mg2+) ions, which react with soap to form insoluble scum. Hardness can also cause scaling, clogging, and reduced efficiency of boilers, pipes, and appliances. Hardness can be classified into temporary hardness and permanent hardness, depending on the type of salts present in water.
- Alkalinity is the measure of the capacity of water to neutralize acids. Alkalinity is mainly due to the presence of bicarbonate (HCO3-), carbonate (CO32-), and hydroxide (OH-) ions in water. Alkalinity is an important parameter for maintaining the pH and buffering capacity of water, as well as preventing corrosion and metal dissolution. Alkalinity can be expressed in terms of equivalents of calcium carbonate (CaCO3).

- The analysis of chloride content, hardness, and alkalinity of water involves various methods and procedures, such as titration, colorimetry, spectrophotometry, etc. The students should be able to understand the principles, techniques, and calculations involved in these methods, as well as the sources of errors and uncertainties. The students should also be able to interpret the results and compare them with the standard values and guidelines for drinking water quality.
