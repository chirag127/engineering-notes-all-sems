### 9. Determination of available chlorine in bleaching powder.

- Bleaching powder is a mixture of calcium hypochlorite, calcium chloride, and calcium hydroxide. It is used as a disinfectant and a bleaching agent.
- Available chlorine is the amount of chlorine that can be liberated from bleaching powder by the action of an acid. It is expressed as a percentage of the mass of bleaching powder.
- The determination of available chlorine in bleaching powder involves the following steps:

  - A known mass of bleaching powder is dissolved in water and filtered to remove any insoluble impurities.
  - A measured volume of the filtrate is titrated with a standard solution of sodium thiosulfate using starch as an indicator. The reaction is as follows:

    $$\text{Ca(OCl)}_2 + 2\text{Na}_2\text{S}_2\text{O}_3 \rightarrow \text{CaCl}_2 + 2\text{Na}_2\text{SO}_4 + \text{S}_2\text{O}_3 + \text{Cl}_2$$

  - The end point of the titration is marked by the disappearance of the blue color of the starch-iodine complex, which is formed by the reaction of iodide ions with chlorine:

    $$\text{I}^- + \text{Cl}_2 \rightarrow \text{I}_2 + 2\text{Cl}^-$$
    $$\text{I}_2 + \text{starch} \rightarrow \text{blue complex}$$

  - The volume of sodium thiosulfate used is noted and the percentage of available chlorine is calculated using the following formula:

    $$\text{% of available chlorine} = \frac{\text{volume of Na}_2\text{S}_2\text{O}_3 \times \text{normality of Na}_2\text{S}_2\text{O}_3 \times 35.5 \times 100}{\text{mass of bleaching powder} \times 10}$$

    where 35.5 is the equivalent mass of chlorine and 10 is the conversion factor from milliliters to liters.
