# 5. Determination of surface tension of given liquid

- Surface tension is the property of a liquid that makes its surface behave like a stretched elastic membrane.
- Surface tension is caused by the cohesive forces between the molecules of the liquid, which tend to minimize the surface area of the liquid.
- Surface tension can be measured by various methods, such as the capillary rise method, the drop weight method, the ring method, and the stalagmometer method.
- In this experiment, we will use the stalagmometer method to determine the surface tension of a given liquid.
- A stalagmometer is a device that consists of a glass tube with a bulb at one end and a narrow opening at the other end.
- The stalagmometer is filled with the liquid to be tested and then inverted over a graduated cylinder.
- The liquid drops from the narrow opening of the stalagmometer and the number of drops and the volume of liquid collected in the graduated cylinder are noted.
- The surface tension of the liquid is given by the formula:

$$\gamma = \frac{4\pi r^3 \rho g}{3V}$$

where $\gamma$ is the surface tension, $r$ is the radius of the opening of the stalagmometer, $\rho$ is the density of the liquid, $g$ is the acceleration due to gravity, and $V$ is the volume of liquid corresponding to one drop.

- To perform the experiment, we need the following apparatus:

  - A stalagmometer
  - A graduated cylinder
  - A thermometer
  - A stopwatch
  - A balance
  - A liquid to be tested (such as water, alcohol, glycerin, etc.)
  - A liquid of known surface tension (such as distilled water)

- The procedure of the experiment is as follows:

  - Clean and dry the stalagmometer and the graduated cylinder.
  - Fill the stalagmometer with the liquid of known surface tension (such as distilled water) up to the bulb.
  - Invert the stalagmometer over the graduated cylinder and allow the liquid to drop from the narrow opening.
  - Start the stopwatch and count the number of drops until a certain volume of liquid (such as 10 mL) is collected in the graduated cylinder.
  - Stop the stopwatch and note the time taken and the volume of liquid collected.
  - Repeat the above steps for at least three trials and calculate the average volume of liquid corresponding to one drop ($V_1$).
  - Repeat the above steps with the liquid to be tested and calculate the average volume of liquid corresponding to one drop ($V_2$).
  - Measure the temperature of both liquids and note it down.
  - Weigh the stalagmometer when empty and when filled with the liquid to be tested and calculate the mass of the liquid ($m$).
  - Calculate the density of the liquid ($\rho$) by dividing the mass by the volume of the stalagmometer ($V_s$).
  - Measure the radius of the opening of the stalagmometer ($r$) by using a magnifying glass and a scale.
  - Calculate the surface tension of the liquid ($\gamma_2$) by using the formula:

  $$\gamma_2 = \frac{V_1}{V_2} \gamma_1$$

  where $\gamma_1$ is the surface tension of the liquid of known surface tension (such as distilled water).

  - Record the observations and calculations in a tabular form and report the result with proper units and significant figures.
