### CO-5 Estimate the rate constant of reaction. K3

- The rate constant of a reaction, k, is a measure of how fast the reaction proceeds at a given temperature.
- The rate constant can be determined experimentally by measuring the concentration of reactants or products as a function of time and applying the rate law of the reaction.
- The rate law of a reaction relates the rate of the reaction to the concentrations of the reactants raised to some powers, called the order of the reaction with respect to each reactant.
- The overall order of the reaction is the sum of the orders of the reactants.
- For example, for the reaction A + B -> C, the rate law can be written as:

  - rate = k[A]^m[B]^n

  - where k is the rate constant, [A] and [B] are the concentrations of A and B, and m and n are the orders of the reaction with respect to A and B, respectively.

- The rate constant can be estimated by using the method of initial rates, which involves measuring the initial rate of the reaction at different initial concentrations of the reactants and finding the values of k, m, and n that best fit the data.
- Alternatively, the rate constant can be estimated by using the method of integrated rate laws, which involves finding the mathematical expression that relates the concentration of a reactant or product to time and fitting the experimental data to the equation.
- The integrated rate law depends on the order of the reaction. For example, for a first-order reaction, the integrated rate law is:

  - ln[A] = -kt + ln[A]0

  - where [A] is the concentration of the reactant at time t, [A]0 is the initial concentration of the reactant, and k is the rate constant.

- The rate constant can be estimated by plotting ln[A] versus t and finding the slope of the line, which is equal to -k.
- The rate constant can also be affected by factors such as temperature, catalysts, and pressure. The Arrhenius equation relates the rate constant to the temperature and the activation energy of the reaction:

  - k = Ae^(-Ea/RT)

  - where A is the frequency factor, Ea is the activation energy, R is the gas constant, and T is the temperature.

- The rate constant can be estimated by plotting ln k versus 1/T and finding the slope of the line, which is equal to -Ea/R.
