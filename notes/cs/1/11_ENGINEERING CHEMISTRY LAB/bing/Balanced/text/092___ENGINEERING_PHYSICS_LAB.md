# Engineering Physics Lab

Engineering physics lab is a practical course that complements the theoretical knowledge of physics and its applications in engineering and technology. It involves experimental techniques and modern equipment to perform experiments and analyze data. Engineering physics lab can cover various topics depending on the syllabus and electives of the B.Tech engineering physics program. Some of the possible topics are:

- Mechanics and thermodynamics: This topic involves experiments related to the principles and applications of classical mechanics, such as kinematics, dynamics, conservation laws, rotational motion, harmonic motion, etc. It also involves experiments related to the concepts and applications of thermodynamics, such as heat, temperature, entropy, heat engines, etc. Some examples of experiments are:

  - Measurement of acceleration due to gravity using a simple pendulum
  - Verification of Hooke's law and determination of Young's modulus of a material
  - Determination of the coefficient of viscosity of a liquid by Poiseuille's method
  - Measurement of the specific heat capacity of a solid by the method of mixtures
  - Study of the Carnot cycle and efficiency of a heat engine

- Electricity and magnetism: This topic involves experiments related to the principles and applications of electricity and magnetism, such as electric fields, electric potential, electric circuits, capacitance, resistance, Kirchhoff's laws, magnetic fields, magnetic forces, electromagnetic induction, etc. Some examples of experiments are:

  - Measurement of the resistance of a wire by the Wheatstone bridge method
  - Determination of the capacitance of a capacitor by the charging and discharging method
  - Verification of Ohm's law and study of the series and parallel combination of resistors
  - Measurement of the magnetic field of a bar magnet by the deflection magnetometer method
  - Study of the Faraday's law of electromagnetic induction and Lenz's law

- Optics and waves: This topic involves experiments related to the principles and applications of optics and waves, such as reflection, refraction, interference, diffraction, polarization, lenses, mirrors, optical instruments, sound waves, Doppler effect, etc. Some examples of experiments are:

  - Determination of the focal length of a convex lens by the u-v method
  - Verification of the laws of reflection and refraction by using a plane mirror and a glass slab
  - Study of the interference pattern of light by the Young's double slit experiment
  - Determination of the wavelength of light by the diffraction grating method
  - Measurement of the speed of sound in air by the resonance tube method

- Modern physics and nanoscience: This topic involves experiments related to the principles and applications of modern physics and nanoscience, such as quantum physics, atomic physics, nuclear physics, solid state physics, nanomaterials, nanodevices, etc. Some examples of experiments are:

  - Measurement of the Planck's constant by the photoelectric effect method
  - Determination of the Rydberg constant by the hydrogen spectrum method
  - Study of the radioactive decay and half-life of a radioactive source
  - Measurement of the band gap of a semiconductor by the four-probe method
  - Synthesis and characterization of nanomaterials by the chemical reduction method

Engineering physics lab can help the students to develop their skills in experimental design, data collection, data analysis, error estimation, report writing, and presentation. It can also help them to understand the physical phenomena and their engineering applications in a better way. Engineering physics lab can also expose the students to the latest developments and innovations in the field of physics and technology.
