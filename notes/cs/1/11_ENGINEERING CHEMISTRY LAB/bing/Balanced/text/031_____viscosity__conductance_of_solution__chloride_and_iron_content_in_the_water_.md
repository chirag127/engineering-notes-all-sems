### Viscosity, Conductance of Solution, Chloride and Iron Content in the Water

- Viscosity is a measure of the resistance of a fluid to deformation under shear stress. It is related to the internal friction of the fluid molecules. Viscosity depends on the temperature, pressure, and composition of the fluid. 
- Conductance of solution is a measure of the ability of a solution to conduct electric current. It is proportional to the concentration and mobility of the ions in the solution. Conductance of solution depends on the temperature, pressure, and type of solvent and solute. 
- Chloride and iron content in the water are indicators of the water quality and potential health effects. Chloride is a major component of salt and can affect the taste, odor, and corrosion of water. Iron is a common element in rocks and soils and can cause staining, clogging, and bacterial growth in water. 
- Some important points to remember are:

  - Viscosity of water decreases with increasing temperature and pressure, and increases with increasing salinity and dissolved solids.
  - Conductance of solution increases with increasing temperature and pressure, and increases with increasing concentration and mobility of ions. Chloride ions are one of the main contributors to the conductance of seawater.
  - Chloride and iron content in the water can vary depending on the source, treatment, and distribution of the water. Chloride levels in drinking water are usually below 250 mg/L, while iron levels are usually below 0.3 mg/L.
  - Viscosity, conductance of solution, chloride and iron content in the water can be measured using various methods and instruments, such as viscometers, conductivity meters, titration, spectrophotometry, etc.
