### 3. Determination of Alkalinity of water sample.

- Alkalinity is the measure of the capacity of water to neutralize acids. It is mainly caused by the presence of bicarbonates, carbonates, and hydroxides of calcium, magnesium, and other metals in water.
- Alkalinity is important for water quality because it affects the pH, corrosion, and disinfection processes of water. High alkalinity can cause scaling and reduce the effectiveness of chlorine. Low alkalinity can cause corrosion and pH fluctuations.
- Alkalinity can be determined by titrating a water sample with a standard acid solution, such as sulfuric acid or hydrochloric acid, using a suitable indicator, such as phenolphthalein or methyl orange, to detect the endpoint of the titration.
- The procedure for determining alkalinity of water sample is as follows:

  - Take a 100 mL aliquot of the water sample in a conical flask and add 2-3 drops of phenolphthalein indicator. If the sample turns pink, it indicates the presence of phenolphthalein alkalinity (P-alkalinity), which is due to hydroxides and half of the carbonates. Record the initial burette reading of the standard acid solution.
  - Titrate the sample with the standard acid solution until the pink color disappears. Record the final burette reading and calculate the volume of acid used. This volume corresponds to the P-alkalinity of the sample.
  - Add 2-3 drops of methyl orange indicator to the same sample and continue the titration with the standard acid solution until the color changes from yellow to orange. Record the final burette reading and calculate the volume of acid used. This volume corresponds to the total alkalinity (T-alkalinity) of the sample, which is due to hydroxides, carbonates, and bicarbonates.
  - The P-alkalinity and T-alkalinity can be expressed in terms of milligrams per liter (mg/L) of calcium carbonate (CaCO3) equivalent by multiplying the volume of acid used by the normality of the acid and by a factor of 50.
  - The types and amounts of alkalinity present in the sample can be determined by using the following relationships:

    - If P-alkalinity = 0, then T-alkalinity = bicarbonate alkalinity (B-alkalinity).
    - If P-alkalinity = T-alkalinity, then P-alkalinity = hydroxide alkalinity (OH-alkalinity) and B-alkalinity = 0.
    - If P-alkalinity < T-alkalinity, then P-alkalinity = 2 x carbonate alkalinity (C-alkalinity) and B-alkalinity = T-alkalinity - P-alkalinity.
    - If P-alkalinity > T-alkalinity, then the sample contains interfering substances and the results are invalid.
