### 12. Preparation of Urea formaldehyde (UF) resin

- Urea formaldehyde (UF) resin is a thermosetting polymer that is widely used in adhesives, plywood, particle board, medium-density fibreboard (MDF), and molded objects.
- UF resin is synthesized by the condensation reaction of urea and formaldehyde in the presence of a base or an acid catalyst .
- The reaction can be divided into three steps: methylolation, condensation, and curing .
- Methylolation: In this step, formaldehyde reacts with urea to form monomethylolurea and dimethylolurea, which are the monomers of UF resin . The reaction is carried out in an aqueous solution containing more than 50% formaldehyde and urea in a molar ratio of 2-3:1 at pH 6-11 . The reaction is exothermic and requires heating to at least 80°C .
- Condensation: In this step, the monomers undergo further polymerization to form low molecular weight prepolymers with a high degree of branching . The reaction is catalyzed by an acid, such as a mineral or organic acid, which is added to bring the pH to 0.5-3.5 . The reaction is also exothermic and requires heating to 90-100°C .
- Curing: In this step, the prepolymers crosslink to form a three-dimensional network of UF resin with high strength and rigidity . The reaction is catalyzed by heat, pressure, or a hardener, such as ammonia, which is added to the resin . The reaction is endothermic and requires heating to 120-150°C .
- The properties and performance of UF resin depend on the reaction conditions, such as the molar ratio of formaldehyde to urea, the pH, the temperature, the catalyst, and the hardener .
- UF resin has some advantages, such as low cost, fast curing, high water resistance, and good adhesion to wood .
- UF resin also has some disadvantages, such as low thermal stability, poor weather resistance, and high formaldehyde emission, which can cause health and environmental problems .
