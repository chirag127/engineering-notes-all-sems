### 8. Determination of the strength of Potassium dichromate using internal indicator.

- Potassium dichromate is a primary standard substance that can be used to determine the strength of a solution of an oxidizing or reducing agent.
- The internal indicator is a substance that changes color when the equivalence point of the titration is reached. It is usually added to the solution of the analyte (the substance whose strength is to be determined).
- The most common internal indicator for potassium dichromate titrations is diphenylamine sulfonic acid (DPSA), which turns from colorless to blue when oxidized by potassium dichromate.
- The procedure for the determination of the strength of potassium dichromate using internal indicator is as follows:

  - Weigh accurately about 0.2 g of potassium dichromate and dissolve it in 100 mL of distilled water in a volumetric flask. This is the standard solution of potassium dichromate.
  - Pipette out 10 mL of the standard solution into a conical flask and add 10 mL of dilute sulfuric acid and a few drops of DPSA indicator.
  - Titrate the solution with the unknown solution of the oxidizing or reducing agent (such as ferrous ammonium sulfate or sodium thiosulfate) from a burette until the color changes from orange to blue. Note the burette reading at the end point. This is the first titration.
  - Repeat the titration two more times and take the average of the three readings. This is the mean titration.
  - Calculate the strength of the unknown solution using the formula:

    Strength of unknown solution = (Strength of potassium dichromate x Mean titration) / Volume of standard solution

  - Report the result with the appropriate units and significant figures.
