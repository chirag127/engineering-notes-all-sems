## Topic: Nuclear Fusion

- Nuclear fusion is the process of combining two or more atomic nuclei into a larger one, releasing energy in the process.
- Nuclear fusion is the source of energy for the Sun and other stars, where hydrogen nuclei fuse into helium nuclei under high temperature and pressure.
- Nuclear fusion has the potential to provide clean and abundant energy for human civilization, but it is very difficult to achieve and sustain in a controlled manner on Earth.
- The main challenges of nuclear fusion are:
  - Heating the fuel to very high temperatures (over 100 million degrees Celsius) to overcome the electrostatic repulsion between the positively charged nuclei.
  - Confining the hot plasma (ionized gas) in a stable and efficient way, preventing it from escaping or cooling down.
  - Achieving a high enough density and confinement time of the plasma to reach the ignition point, where the fusion reactions produce more energy than the energy input.
- The most common and promising method of achieving nuclear fusion on Earth is using a tokamak, a doughnut-shaped device that uses magnetic fields to confine the plasma.
- Some of the current and planned tokamak experiments are:
  - ITER (International Thermonuclear Experimental Reactor), a multinational project under construction in France, aiming to demonstrate the feasibility of fusion as a large-scale and carbon-free source of energy.
  - KSTAR (Korea Superconducting Tokamak Advanced Research), a South Korean experiment that achieved a world record of sustaining a 100 million degrees Celsius plasma for 30 seconds in 2022.
  - SPARC (Society for Plasma Research and Applications), a US-based project that plans to build a smaller and cheaper tokamak that can achieve net energy gain by 2025.
