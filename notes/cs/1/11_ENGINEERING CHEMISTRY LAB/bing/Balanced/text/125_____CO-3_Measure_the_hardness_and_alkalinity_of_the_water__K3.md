### CO-3 Measure the hardness and alkalinity of the water. K3

- Hardness of water is the measure of the concentration of calcium and magnesium ions in water. It is expressed in terms of equivalent calcium carbonate (CaCO3).
- Alkalinity of water is the measure of the capacity of water to neutralize acids. It is mainly due to the presence of bicarbonates, carbonates and hydroxides of calcium, magnesium and other metals. It is also expressed in terms of equivalent calcium carbonate (CaCO3).
- To measure the hardness of water, a standard method is the EDTA titration method. EDTA (ethylenediaminetetraacetic acid) is a chelating agent that forms a stable complex with calcium and magnesium ions. The end point of the titration is detected by using a metal indicator such as Eriochrome Black T (EBT) or Calcon, which changes color from red to blue when all the hardness ions are complexed by EDTA.
- To measure the alkalinity of water, a standard method is the phenolphthalein and methyl orange titration method. Phenolphthalein is an indicator that changes color from colorless to pink in alkaline solutions. Methyl orange is an indicator that changes color from yellow to red in acidic solutions. The end point of the titration is detected by using a standard acid solution such as sulfuric acid or hydrochloric acid.
- The procedure for measuring the hardness and alkalinity of water is as follows:

  - Collect a water sample in a clean container and measure its pH using a pH meter or a pH paper.
  - To measure the total hardness, take 50 mL of the water sample in a conical flask and add a few drops of EBT or Calcon indicator. The solution will turn red due to the presence of hardness ions. Titrate the solution with a standard EDTA solution of known concentration until the color changes from red to blue. Note down the volume of EDTA used. The total hardness can be calculated by using the formula:

    Total hardness (mg/L as CaCO3) = (Volume of EDTA used x Normality of EDTA x 1000) / Volume of water sample

  - To measure the total alkalinity, take another 50 mL of the water sample in a conical flask and add a few drops of phenolphthalein indicator. If the solution turns pink, it means that the water sample has phenolphthalein alkalinity due to the presence of hydroxides and carbonates. Titrate the solution with a standard acid solution of known concentration until the color changes from pink to colorless. Note down the volume of acid used. The phenolphthalein alkalinity can be calculated by using the formula:

    Phenolphthalein alkalinity (mg/L as CaCO3) = (Volume of acid used x Normality of acid x 1000) / Volume of water sample

  - To measure the total alkalinity, continue the titration of the same solution with the standard acid solution until the color changes from colorless to red due to the addition of methyl orange indicator. Note down the total volume of acid used. The total alkalinity can be calculated by using the formula:

    Total alkalinity (mg/L as CaCO3) = (Total volume of acid used x Normality of acid x 1000) / Volume of water sample

  - The bicarbonate alkalinity can be obtained by subtracting the phenolphthalein alkalinity from the total alkalinity. The carbonate alkalinity can be obtained by multiplying the phenolphthalein alkalinity by 2. The hydroxide alkalinity can be obtained by subtracting the carbonate alkalinity from the phenolphthalein alkalinity.
