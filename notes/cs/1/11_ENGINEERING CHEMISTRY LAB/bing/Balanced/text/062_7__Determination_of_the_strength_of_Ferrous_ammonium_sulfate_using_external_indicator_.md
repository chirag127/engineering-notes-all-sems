# Determination of the strength of Ferrous ammonium sulfate using external indicator

- Ferrous ammonium sulfate, also known as Mohr's salt, is a light green crystalline salt with the formula (NH4)2Fe(SO4)2.6H2O. It is used as a reducing agent in redox titrations.
- An external indicator is a substance that changes color when added to the titration flask after the end point has been reached. It is used to confirm the end point of the titration and to avoid over-titration.
- One example of an external indicator is potassium ferricyanide, which forms a blue precipitate of Prussian blue with excess ferrous ions after the end point of the titration with potassium dichromate.
- The procedure for determining the strength of ferrous ammonium sulfate using external indicator is as follows:

  - Prepare a standard solution of potassium dichromate (K2Cr2O7) of known concentration (e.g. 0.025 M) by dissolving a weighed amount of the salt in distilled water and making up the volume in a volumetric flask.
  - Pipette out a known volume (e.g. 10 mL) of the ferrous ammonium sulfate solution of unknown concentration into a conical flask and add some dilute sulfuric acid (H2SO4) to prevent the hydrolysis of ferrous ions.
  - Titrate the ferrous ammonium sulfate solution with the standard potassium dichromate solution using a burette until a faint green color is observed in the flask. This indicates that all the ferrous ions have been oxidized to ferric ions by the dichromate ions. Record the volume of the potassium dichromate solution used as V1.
  - Add a few drops of the external indicator (potassium ferricyanide) to the flask and swirl. If a blue color appears, it means that some ferrous ions are still present and the titration is not complete. Add more potassium dichromate solution dropwise until the blue color disappears. Record the final volume of the potassium dichromate solution used as V2.
  - Repeat the titration with another aliquot of the ferrous ammonium sulfate solution and take the average of V2 as the final volume of the potassium dichromate solution required for the titration.
  - Calculate the strength of the ferrous ammonium sulfate solution using the following formula:

    - Strength of ferrous ammonium sulfate solution = (V2 x N2 x M2) / (V1 x M1)

    - Where V2 is the final volume of the potassium dichromate solution in mL, N2 is the normality of the potassium dichromate solution, M2 is the molar mass of ferrous ammonium sulfate (392.14 g/mol), V1 is the volume of the ferrous ammonium sulfate solution in mL, and M1 is the molarity of the ferrous ammonium sulfate solution.
