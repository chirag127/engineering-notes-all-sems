# Determination of the strength of Ferrous ammonium sulfate using external indicator

- Ferrous ammonium sulfate, also known as Mohr's salt, is a double salt of iron(II) sulfate and ammonium sulfate, with the formula (NH4)2Fe(SO4)2·6H2O.
- It is commonly used as a standard solution for titrating oxidizing agents, such as potassium permanganate or potassium dichromate, in redox titrations.
- The strength of a ferrous ammonium sulfate solution can be determined by titrating it with a standard solution of potassium permanganate, using an external indicator such as starch or potassium ferricyanide.
- The reaction between ferrous ammonium sulfate and potassium permanganate is as follows:

    5(NH4)2Fe(SO4)2·6H2O + KMnO4 + 8H2SO4 -> 5Fe2(SO4)3 + 2MnSO4 + 10(NH4)2SO4 + K2SO4 + 48H2O

- The reaction is a self-indicating one, as the purple color of potassium permanganate disappears when all the ferrous ions are oxidized to ferric ions.
- However, near the end point, the color change is not very sharp, and an external indicator is needed to detect the exact end point.
- Starch is a common external indicator, as it forms a blue-black complex with iodine, which is produced by the reaction of excess potassium permanganate and potassium iodide:

    2KMnO4 + 10KI + 8H2SO4 -> 6K2SO4 + 2MnSO4 + 8H2O + 5I2

- The blue-black color of starch-iodine complex indicates the presence of excess potassium permanganate, and the end point is reached when the color disappears.
- Alternatively, potassium ferricyanide can be used as an external indicator, as it forms a blue precipitate of Turnbull's blue with ferrous ions, which are present before the end point:

    3Fe2+ + 2K3[Fe(CN)6] -> Fe3[Fe(CN)6]2 + 6K+

- The blue color of Turnbull's blue indicates the presence of ferrous ions, and the end point is reached when the color disappears.
- The procedure for determining the strength of ferrous ammonium sulfate using external indicator is as follows:

    - Prepare a standard solution of potassium permanganate by dissolving a known mass of KMnO4 in distilled water and standardizing it against a primary standard, such as sodium oxalate or oxalic acid.
    - Pipette a known volume of ferrous ammonium sulfate solution into a conical flask and add some dilute sulfuric acid to provide the acidic medium for the reaction.
    - Add a few drops of starch or potassium ferricyanide solution as the external indicator to the flask.
    - Titrate the ferrous ammonium sulfate solution with the standard potassium permanganate solution from a burette, swirling the flask constantly, until the end point is reached, as indicated by the disappearance of the blue-black or blue color.
    - Record the final burette reading and calculate the volume of potassium permanganate used.
    - Repeat the titration at least two more times and take the average of the concordant results.
    - Use the balanced equation and the mole ratio to calculate the moles of ferrous ammonium sulfate reacted and the moles of potassium permanganate used.
    - Use the molar mass of ferrous ammonium sulfate to calculate the mass of ferrous ammonium sulfate reacted and the mass of ferrous ammonium sulfate in the pipetted volume.
    - Use the volume of ferrous ammonium sulfate solution to calculate the concentration or strength of ferrous ammonium sulfate solution in g/L or mol/L.
