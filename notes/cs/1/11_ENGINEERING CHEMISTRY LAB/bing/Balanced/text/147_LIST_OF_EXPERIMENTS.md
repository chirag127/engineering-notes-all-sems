# List of Experiments

- An experiment is a procedure that tests a hypothesis or a question by collecting and analyzing data under controlled conditions.
- Experiments are used to test the effects of one or more factors (called independent variables) on one or more outcomes (called dependent variables).
- Experiments can be classified into different types based on their design, purpose, and level of control.
- Some common types of experiments are:

  - **Randomized controlled trial (RCT)**: An experiment in which participants are randomly assigned to either a treatment group or a control group, and the outcomes are compared between the groups. RCTs are considered the gold standard for testing the effectiveness of interventions in medicine, education, and social sciences.
  - **Factorial design**: An experiment in which two or more independent variables are manipulated simultaneously, and their effects on the dependent variable are measured. Factorial designs can test for interactions between the independent variables, as well as their main effects.
  - **Quasi-experiment**: An experiment in which the participants are not randomly assigned to the groups, but the groups are formed based on some existing characteristic or condition. Quasi-experiments are often used when randomization is not possible or ethical, but they have lower internal validity than RCTs.
  - **Natural experiment**: An experiment in which the independent variable is manipulated by a natural event or phenomenon, rather than by the researcher. Natural experiments can provide causal evidence in situations where controlled experiments are not feasible or ethical, but they have lower external validity than RCTs.
  - **Observational study**: A study in which the researcher does not manipulate any variable, but only observes and measures the existing relationships between variables. Observational studies can describe patterns and associations, but they cannot establish causality.
