### 12. Preparation of Urea formaldehyde (UF) resin

- Urea formaldehyde (UF) resin is a thermosetting polymer that is widely used as an adhesive and a coating material for wood products, such as plywood, particleboard, and fiberboard.
- UF resin is synthesized by the condensation reaction of urea and formaldehyde in an aqueous solution, under acidic or alkaline conditions, with a molar ratio of formaldehyde to urea ranging from 1.1:1 to 2.5:1.
- The reaction involves three main steps: methylolation, condensation, and curing.

#### Methylolation

- In this step, formaldehyde reacts with urea to form monomethylolurea (MMU) and dimethylolurea (DMU), which are the primary monomers of UF resin.
- The reaction is catalyzed by a base, such as ammonia or pyridine, and is carried out at a temperature of 50-90°C and a pH of 7-9.
- The reaction can be represented by the following equations:

Urea + Formaldehyde → Monomethylolurea + Water

Urea + 2 Formaldehyde → Dimethylolurea + 2 Water

- The degree of methylolation depends on the formaldehyde to urea ratio, the pH, the temperature, and the reaction time. A higher degree of methylolation leads to a higher reactivity and a lower viscosity of the resin.

#### Condensation

- In this step, the methylolureas undergo further condensation reactions to form oligomers and polymers of UF resin, with the elimination of water molecules.
- The reaction is catalyzed by an acid, such as sulfuric acid or oxalic acid, and is carried out at a temperature of 80-100°C and a pH of 0.5-3.5.
- The reaction can be represented by the following equations:

Monomethylolurea + Formaldehyde → Methylenediurea + Water

Dimethylolurea + Formaldehyde → 5,5-Dimethylhydantoin + Water

Methylenediurea + Dimethylolurea → Trimethylenetriurea + Water

- The degree of condensation depends on the formaldehyde to urea ratio, the pH, the temperature, and the reaction time. A higher degree of condensation leads to a higher molecular weight and a higher viscosity of the resin.

#### Curing

- In this step, the UF resin is applied to the wood substrate and heated to a temperature of 120-180°C, under pressure, to form a cross-linked network and a hard and insoluble product.
- The curing process involves further condensation reactions between the methylol and methylene groups of the resin, as well as the reaction of the resin with the hydroxyl groups of the wood.
- The curing time depends on the resin formulation, the wood species, the moisture content, the temperature, and the pressure. A shorter curing time leads to a higher bonding strength and a lower formaldehyde emission of the product.
