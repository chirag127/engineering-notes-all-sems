# Element detection and identification of functional groups in organic compounds

- Organic compounds are composed of carbon and hydrogen, and may also contain other elements such as oxygen, nitrogen, sulfur, phosphorus, halogens, etc.
- Element detection is the process of determining the presence or absence of a particular element in an organic compound.
- Identification of functional groups is the process of determining the type and nature of the specific groups of atoms that are responsible for the characteristic chemical reactions of an organic compound.
- Some of the common methods for element detection and identification of functional groups in organic compounds are:

  - **Lassaigne's test**: This is a general test for the detection of nitrogen, sulfur and halogens in an organic compound. It involves fusing the organic compound with sodium metal in a porcelain crucible, and then dissolving the fused mass in distilled water. The resulting solution is called sodium extract, and it is used for various tests to detect the elements.
  - **Test for nitrogen**: The sodium extract is boiled with iron(II) sulfate and concentrated sulfuric acid. If nitrogen is present, a brown ring is formed at the junction of the two layers, due to the formation of iron(III) nitrosyl complex.
  - **Test for sulfur**: The sodium extract is acidified with acetic acid and lead acetate is added. If sulfur is present, a black precipitate of lead sulfide is formed.
  - **Test for halogens**: The sodium extract is acidified with nitric acid and silver nitrate is added. If halogens are present, a precipitate of silver halide is formed, which is white for chloride, pale yellow for bromide and yellow for iodide. The precipitates can be further distinguished by their solubility in ammonia solution.
  - **Test for oxygen**: There is no specific test for oxygen, but its presence can be inferred from the presence of other elements and functional groups that contain oxygen, such as alcohols, aldehydes, ketones, carboxylic acids, esters, ethers, etc.
  - **Test for phosphorus**: The organic compound is heated with an oxidizing agent, such as nitric acid or sodium peroxide, and the resulting solution is treated with ammonium molybdate. If phosphorus is present, a yellow precipitate of ammonium phosphomolybdate is formed.
  - **Test for functional groups**: There are various tests for different functional groups, based on their characteristic reactions with specific reagents. Some of the common tests are:

    - **Test for alcohols**: Alcohols are organic compounds that contain a hydroxyl group (-OH) attached to a carbon atom. Some of the tests for alcohols are:

      - **Lucas test**: This test is used to distinguish between primary, secondary and tertiary alcohols. The alcohol is treated with Lucas reagent, which is a mixture of zinc chloride and concentrated hydrochloric acid. If a tertiary alcohol is present, a turbidity or a cloudy layer is formed immediately, due to the formation of a water-insoluble alkyl chloride. If a secondary alcohol is present, a turbidity is formed after 5 to 10 minutes. If a primary alcohol is present, no turbidity is formed even after a long time.
      - **Victor Meyer's test**: This test is used to distinguish between aliphatic and aromatic alcohols. The alcohol is heated with iodine and sodium hydroxide, and the resulting vapors are passed through a condenser and collected in a test tube containing nitric acid and silver nitrate. If an aliphatic alcohol is present, a red or brown color is formed, due to the formation of iodoform. If an aromatic alcohol is present, no color change is observed.
      - **Esterification test**: This test is used to confirm the presence of an alcohol. The alcohol is heated with an acid, such as acetic acid or sulfuric acid, and the resulting mixture is cooled and poured into water. If an alcohol is present, a fruity smell is observed, due to the formation of an ester.
