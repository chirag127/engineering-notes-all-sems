# Nuclear Fusion

Nuclear fusion is the process of combining two or more atomic nuclei into a single, heavier nucleus, releasing a large amount of energy in the process. Nuclear fusion is the source of energy for the Sun and other stars.

Some of the main points to know about nuclear fusion are:

- Nuclear fusion requires high temperatures and pressures to overcome the electrostatic repulsion between positively charged nuclei. The temperature needed for fusion to occur is around 100 million degrees Celsius.
- The most common fusion reaction in stars is the proton-proton chain, which fuses four hydrogen nuclei into one helium nucleus, releasing two positrons, two neutrinos, and gamma rays. This reaction converts about 0.7% of the mass of the hydrogen into energy, according to Einstein's mass-energy equivalence formula E = mc^2.
- Another fusion reaction that can occur in stars is the carbon-nitrogen-oxygen (CNO) cycle, which uses carbon, nitrogen, and oxygen as catalysts to fuse hydrogen into helium. This reaction is more efficient than the proton-proton chain at higher temperatures and densities, and dominates in stars more massive than the Sun.
- The fusion reaction that is most feasible for human-made reactors is the deuterium-tritium (D-T) reaction, which fuses a deuterium nucleus (an isotope of hydrogen with one proton and one neutron) and a tritium nucleus (an isotope of hydrogen with one proton and two neutrons) into a helium nucleus and a neutron, releasing a lot of energy. This reaction has a low energy threshold and a high cross-section, meaning that it is easier to initiate and sustain than other fusion reactions.
- The main challenge of achieving nuclear fusion on Earth is to create and maintain the conditions for fusion to occur, such as high temperature, high pressure, and plasma confinement. Plasma is a state of matter where the atoms are ionized, meaning that they have lost or gained electrons and become electrically charged. Plasma is affected by magnetic and electric fields, which can be used to confine and control it.
- The main methods of achieving nuclear fusion on Earth are magnetic confinement and inertial confinement. Magnetic confinement uses strong magnetic fields to confine and compress the plasma, while inertial confinement uses powerful lasers or ion beams to heat and compress a small pellet of fusion fuel. Both methods have their advantages and disadvantages, and neither has achieved a net energy gain, meaning that more energy is produced by fusion than is consumed by the reactor.
- The main benefits of nuclear fusion are that it can provide a virtually unlimited source of clean energy, with no greenhouse gas emissions, no radioactive waste, and no risk of nuclear meltdown or proliferation. The main drawbacks of nuclear fusion are that it is very difficult and expensive to achieve, and that it may produce some harmful byproducts, such as neutrons, tritium, and radiation.
