# Synthesis of Adipic Acid

- Adipic acid is an organic compound with the formula (CH2)4(COOH)2. It is a white crystalline solid that is used as a precursor for the production of nylon and other polymers.
- Adipic acid can be synthesized from various starting materials, such as cyclohexane, cyclohexene, cyclohexanol, or benzene. Some of the common methods are:

  - Oxidation of cyclohexane with nitric acid or air in the presence of a catalyst. This is an industrial process that produces large amounts of adipic acid and nitrous oxide, a greenhouse gas.
  - Oxidation of cyclohexene with hydrogen peroxide and a tungsten catalyst. This is a green and selective method that avoids the formation of nitrous oxide and other byproducts.
  - Oxidation of cyclohexanol with nitric acid or potassium permanganate. This is a laboratory method that requires high temperatures and pressures and yields a mixture of adipic acid and glutaric acid.
  - Oxidation of benzene with nitric acid and a vanadium catalyst. This is a historical method that was used before the discovery of the cyclohexane route. It involves the formation of nitrobenzene, phenol, and maleic anhydride as intermediates.

- Adipic acid can also be synthesized from renewable sources, such as glucose, fatty acids, or lignin. Some of the emerging methods are:

  - Biocatalytic conversion of glucose to adipic acid using engineered microorganisms or enzymes. This is a sustainable and environmentally friendly method that mimics the natural metabolic pathways of living cells.
  - Hydrogenation of muconic acid, a derivative of glucose, using a ruthenium catalyst. This is a simple and efficient method that can be performed under mild conditions and produces high yields of adipic acid.
  - Oxidative cleavage of fatty acids or lignin using ozone or oxygen in the presence of a metal catalyst. This is a versatile and selective method that can utilize various biomass feedstocks and generate adipic acid and other valuable chemicals.
