### CO-1 Get an understanding of the use of different analytical instruments. K3

- Analytical instruments are devices that are used to measure, analyze, or monitor physical, chemical, or biological properties of samples.
- Different analytical instruments have different applications, principles, and techniques, depending on the type of sample and the property of interest.
- Some examples of analytical instruments are:

  - Spectrophotometers: measure the amount of light absorbed or emitted by a sample at different wavelengths.
  - Chromatographs: separate the components of a mixture based on their affinity to a stationary phase or a mobile phase.
  - Mass spectrometers: identify the molecular mass and structure of a sample by ionizing it and detecting the resulting fragments.
  - Electrochemical analyzers: measure the electrical potential or current of a sample in relation to a reference electrode or a redox reaction.
  - Microscopes: magnify the image of a sample using light, electrons, or other forms of radiation.
  - Biosensors: detect the presence or concentration of a specific biomolecule or analyte in a sample using a biological recognition element and a transducer.

- To get an understanding of the use of different analytical instruments, one should:

  - Know the basic principles and techniques of each instrument and how they relate to the sample and the property of interest.
  - Know the advantages and limitations of each instrument and how to select the most appropriate one for a given analytical problem.
  - Know how to operate, calibrate, and maintain each instrument and how to interpret and report the results obtained.
  - Know how to apply quality control and assurance methods to ensure the reliability and accuracy of the measurements.
  - Know how to troubleshoot and solve common problems or errors that may arise during the use of each instrument.
