### 4. Determination of pH by titrimetric method

- Titrimetric method is a technique of quantitative analysis that involves measuring the volume of a solution of known concentration (titrant) that is required to react completely with a solution of unknown concentration (titrand).
- pH is a measure of the acidity or alkalinity of a solution, defined as the negative logarithm of the hydrogen ion concentration: pH = -log[H+].
- Determination of pH by titrimetric method involves using an acid-base titration, in which an acid and a base react to form a salt and water, and the equivalence point is reached when the moles of acid and base are equal.
- To perform an acid-base titration, the following steps are required:
  - Prepare a standard solution of the titrant, such as a strong acid or a strong base, and fill a buret with it.
  - Measure a known volume of the titrand, such as a weak acid or a weak base, and transfer it to a flask or a beaker.
  - Add a few drops of an appropriate indicator to the titrand, which changes color at or near the equivalence point.
  - Record the initial volume of the titrant in the buret, and slowly add the titrant to the titrand while stirring, until the indicator changes color, indicating the end point of the titration.
  - Record the final volume of the titrant in the buret, and calculate the volume of the titrant used (Vt) by subtracting the initial volume from the final volume.
  - Use the stoichiometry of the acid-base reaction and the known concentration of the titrant (Ct) to calculate the concentration of the titrand (Cr) using the formula: Ct * Vt = Cr * Vr, where Vr is the volume of the titrand.
  - Use the concentration of the titrand and the dissociation constant of the weak acid or the weak base to calculate the pH of the titrand using the appropriate formula, such as the Henderson-Hasselbalch equation for a buffer solution.
- To plot a pH curve, the pH of the titrand is measured at various points during the titration, using a pH meter or a pH indicator, and the pH is plotted against the volume of the titrant added. The shape of the pH curve depends on the strength and the ratio of the acid and the base involved in the titration. The equivalence point is the point where the pH curve shows a sharp change in slope, corresponding to the point where the moles of acid and base are equal. The end point is the point where the indicator changes color, which ideally should coincide with the equivalence point.
