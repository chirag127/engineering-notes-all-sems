### 8. Determination of the strength of Potassium dichromate using internal indicator.

- Potassium dichromate is a primary standard substance that can be used to determine the strength of a reducing agent such as ferrous ammonium sulphate.
- The reaction between potassium dichromate and ferrous ammonium sulphate is given by:

  `K2Cr2O7 + 6FeSO4 + 7H2SO4 -> Cr2(SO4)3 + 3K2SO4 + 6Fe2(SO4)3 + 7H2O`

- The end point of this titration can be detected by using an internal indicator such as diphenylamine sulphonic acid or barium diphenylamine sulphonate.
- These indicators are colourless in acidic medium and turn blue in the presence of excess ferrous ions.
- The procedure for the determination of the strength of potassium dichromate is as follows:

  - Weigh accurately about 0.5 g of potassium dichromate and dissolve it in 100 mL of distilled water in a volumetric flask.
  - Pipette out 20 mL of this solution into a conical flask and add 10 mL of dilute sulphuric acid and a few drops of the internal indicator.
  - Titrate this solution with ferrous ammonium sulphate solution taken in a burette until a faint blue colour appears at the end point.
  - Repeat the titration for concordant readings and calculate the average volume of ferrous ammonium sulphate solution used.
  - Calculate the strength of potassium dichromate solution using the formula:

    `Strength of K2Cr2O7 = (6 x strength of FeSO4 x volume of FeSO4) / (volume of K2Cr2O7)`

    where strength of FeSO4 is the normality of ferrous ammonium sulphate solution.
