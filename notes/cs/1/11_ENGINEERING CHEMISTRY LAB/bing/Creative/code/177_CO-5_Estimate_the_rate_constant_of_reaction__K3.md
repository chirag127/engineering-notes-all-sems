# CO-5 Estimate the rate constant of reaction. K3

- The rate constant of a reaction, denoted by k, is a measure of how fast the reaction proceeds at a given temperature and concentration of reactants and products.
- The rate constant can be calculated from the rate law of the reaction, which relates the rate of the reaction to the concentrations of the reactants raised to some powers.
- For a termolecular reaction, where three molecules collide and react, the rate law is usually of the form: r = k3[A][B][C], where r is the rate of the reaction, k3 is the termolecular rate constant, and [A], [B], and [C] are the concentrations of the reactants.
- To estimate the termolecular rate constant, k3, one needs to measure the rate of the reaction and the concentrations of the reactants under the same conditions, and then use the rate law equation to solve for k3.
- Alternatively, if the termolecular reaction is the sum of two or more elementary reactions, one can use the relationship between the equilibrium constants of the individual reactions and the overall reaction to estimate k3 .
- For example, if the termolecular reaction is: CO(g) + 3H2(g) ⇌ CH4(g) + H2O(g), and it is the sum of two elementary reactions: CO(g) + H2(g) ⇌ COH(g) and COH(g) + 2H2(g) ⇌ CH4(g) + H2O(g), then the equilibrium constant for the overall reaction, K3, is equal to the product of the equilibrium constants for the individual reactions, K1 and K2 .
- K3 = K1K2 = (k1/k-1)(k2/k-2) = (k1k2)/(k-1k-2), where k1, k2, k-1, and k-2 are the rate constants for the forward and reverse reactions of the elementary steps.
- If the rate constants for the elementary reactions are known, then k3 can be estimated from the equation: k3 = K3(k-1k-2)/(k1k2).
