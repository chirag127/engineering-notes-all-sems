### 14. Determination of Cell Conductance of a Solution

- Cell conductance of a solution is the reciprocal of its resistance, which is the opposition to the flow of electric current through the solution.
- The resistance of a solution depends on the concentration, charge, and mobility of the ions it contains, as well as the geometry of the cell used to measure it.
- A cell is a device that consists of two electrodes immersed in a solution and connected to a source of electric potential.
- A conductivity cell is a special type of cell that is used to measure the conductance of electrolytic solutions, which are solutions that can conduct electricity due to the presence of ions .
- A conductivity cell has two electrodes of equal area and fixed distance apart, so that the resistance of the solution between them is proportional to the length of the solution column and inversely proportional to the cross-sectional area of the electrodes.
- The cell constant, C, is the ratio of the length to the area of the electrodes, and it has units of m^-1^. It is a characteristic of the cell and it can be determined by measuring the resistance of a solution of known conductivity, such as 0.0200 M KCl.
- The conductivity, K, of a solution is the conductance of the solution in a cell with a cell constant of 1 m^-1^. It has units of S m^-1^ or Ω^-1^ m^-1^, where S is the symbol for siemens, the unit of conductance.
- The conductivity of a solution can be calculated from the experimental resistance, R, and the cell constant, C, by using the formula: K = C/R.
- The conductivity of a solution is related to the concentration, c, and the equivalent conductance, Λ, of the electrolyte by the formula: K = cΛ.
- The equivalent conductance, Λ, is the conductance of a solution containing one equivalent of the electrolyte per unit volume. It depends on the degree of dissociation, α, and the molar conductivities, λ, of the ions in the solution by the formula: Λ = α(λ+ + λ-).
- The molar conductivity, λ, of an ion is the conductance of a solution containing one mole of the ion per unit volume. It depends on the charge, z, and the mobility, u, of the ion by the formula: λ = zFu, where F is the Faraday constant.
- The mobility, u, of an ion is the velocity of the ion per unit electric field. It is influenced by the size, shape, and hydration of the ion, as well as the viscosity and temperature of the solution.
