# LIST OF EXPERIMENTS

- An experiment is a scientific procedure that aims to test a hypothesis, answer a question, or demonstrate a known fact.
- Experiments usually involve manipulating one or more variables, measuring their effects, and comparing them with a control group.
- Experiments can be classified into different types based on their purpose, design, and execution.

## Types of experiments

- **Natural experiments**: These are experiments that exploit natural variations in the environment or historical events as sources of data. For example, studying the effects of a natural disaster, a policy change, or a genetic mutation on a population.
- **Quasi-experiments**: These are experiments that lack random assignment of subjects to treatment and control groups, but still attempt to control for confounding factors. For example, studying the effects of a school program, a drug, or a social intervention on a group of people who are not randomly selected.
- **Randomized controlled trials (RCTs)**: These are experiments that randomly assign subjects to treatment and control groups, and measure the outcomes of interest. RCTs are considered the gold standard for causal inference, as they can eliminate confounding factors and selection bias. For example, testing the efficacy and safety of a new vaccine, a medical device, or a behavioral intervention on a sample of volunteers.
- **Laboratory experiments**: These are experiments that are conducted in a controlled and artificial setting, where the experimenter can manipulate the variables of interest and isolate their effects. Laboratory experiments are often used to test theories, hypotheses, and mechanisms in various fields of science. For example, studying the behavior of atoms, molecules, cells, animals, or humans under different conditions.
- **Field experiments**: These are experiments that are conducted in a natural and realistic setting, where the experimenter can still manipulate the variables of interest, but has less control over the environment and other factors. Field experiments are often used to test the external validity and generalizability of theories, hypotheses, and interventions in various fields of science. For example, studying the impact of a marketing campaign, a political message, or a social norm on a population.
