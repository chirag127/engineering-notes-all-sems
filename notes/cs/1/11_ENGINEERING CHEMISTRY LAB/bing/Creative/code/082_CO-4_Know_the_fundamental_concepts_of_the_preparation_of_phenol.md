# CO-4 Know the fundamental concepts of the preparation of phenol

- Phenol is an aromatic compound with the formula C6H5OH. It has a hydroxyl group (-OH) attached to a benzene ring.
- Phenol is a weak acid that can lose a proton to form phenoxide ion (C6H5O-). It can also undergo electrophilic aromatic substitution reactions with various electrophiles.
- Phenol can be prepared from various sources, such as coal tar, benzene, cumene, and chlorobenzene. Some of the common methods of preparation of phenol are:

  - From coal tar: Coal tar is a by-product of the destructive distillation of coal. It contains about 1% of phenol along with other aromatic compounds. Phenol can be extracted from coal tar by fractional distillation, followed by washing with sodium hydroxide solution to remove acidic impurities. The phenol can then be purified by crystallization or distillation.

  - From benzene: Benzene can be converted to phenol by treating it with concentrated sulfuric acid and nitric acid to form nitrobenzene. Nitrobenzene can then be reduced to phenylamine (aniline) by using tin and hydrochloric acid or iron and hydrochloric acid. Phenylamine can then be diazotized by treating it with sodium nitrite and hydrochloric acid to form phenyldiazonium chloride. Phenyldiazonium chloride can then be hydrolyzed by heating it with water to form phenol and nitrogen gas.

  - From cumene: Cumene is an alkylbenzene with the formula C6H5CH(CH3)2. It can be prepared by alkylation of benzene with propene in the presence of a catalyst. Cumene can then be oxidized by air to form cumene hydroperoxide, which can be cleaved by treating it with dilute sulfuric acid to form phenol and acetone.

  - From chlorobenzene: Chlorobenzene is an aryl halide with the formula C6H5Cl. It can be prepared by chlorination of benzene in the presence of a catalyst. Chlorobenzene can then be hydrolyzed by treating it with sodium hydroxide solution at high temperature and pressure to form phenol and sodium chloride. This method is also known as the Dow process.
