### K3

- K3 is a type of surface in mathematics that is named after three mathematicians: Kummer, Kähler, and Kodaira.
- A K3 surface is a complex algebraic surface that has no singularities, no curves, and a trivial canonical bundle. It also has a finite group of automorphisms and a Hodge diamond with only one non-zero entry in each row and column.
- K3 surfaces are important examples of Calabi-Yau manifolds, which are spaces that admit a Ricci-flat metric and have applications in string theory and mirror symmetry.
- K3 surfaces can be constructed in various ways, such as by taking a quartic surface in projective space, a double cover of the projective plane branched along a sextic curve, or a Kummer surface associated to an abelian surface.
