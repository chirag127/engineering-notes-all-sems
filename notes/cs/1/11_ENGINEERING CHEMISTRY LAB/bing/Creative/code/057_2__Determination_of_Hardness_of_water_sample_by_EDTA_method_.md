# 2. Determination of Hardness of water sample by EDTA method.

- Hardness of water is the measure of the concentration of calcium and magnesium ions in water.
- Hard water causes problems such as scaling of pipes, boilers, and appliances, soap scum formation, and reduced effectiveness of detergents and cleaners.
- EDTA (ethylenediaminetetraacetic acid) is a chelating agent that can form stable complexes with metal ions such as calcium and magnesium.
- EDTA method is a titrimetric method that involves titrating a water sample with a standard solution of EDTA in the presence of a suitable indicator that changes color at the end point of the titration.
- The indicator used for EDTA method is Eriochrome Black T (EBT), which is a metallochromic indicator that forms a wine-red complex with free calcium and magnesium ions in water, and turns blue when all the metal ions are complexed with EDTA.
- The procedure of EDTA method is as follows:

  - Take a known volume of water sample in a conical flask and add a few drops of buffer solution (pH 10) to maintain a constant pH during the titration.
  - Add 2-3 drops of EBT indicator and swirl the flask. The solution should turn wine-red due to the presence of free metal ions.
  - Titrate the solution with a standard EDTA solution from a burette until the color changes from wine-red to blue, indicating the end point of the titration.
  - Record the volume of EDTA solution used and repeat the titration for accuracy.
  - Calculate the hardness of water sample using the formula:

    - Hardness (mg/L) = (Volume of EDTA solution used x Normality of EDTA solution x 1000) / Volume of water sample
