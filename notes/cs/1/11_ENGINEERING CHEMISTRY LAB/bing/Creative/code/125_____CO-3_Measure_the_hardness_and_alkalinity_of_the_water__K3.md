### CO-3 Measure the hardness and alkalinity of the water. K3

- Hardness and alkalinity are two parameters that indicate the water quality and the suitability of water for various purposes.
- Hardness measures the total concentration of divalent salts, mainly calcium and magnesium, that can form insoluble deposits or scale in pipes, boilers, and appliances.
- Alkalinity measures the total amount of bases, mainly bicarbonate and carbonate, that can neutralize acids and buffer the pH of water .
- Hardness and alkalinity are related through the common ions formed in aquatic systems. The counter-ions associated with the bicarbonate and carbonate fraction of alkalinity are the principal cations responsible for hardness.
- To measure the hardness and alkalinity of water, the following steps are required:

  - Collect a representative water sample in a clean container and label it.
  - Determine the total hardness of the water sample by titrating it with a standard solution of ethylenediaminetetraacetic acid (EDTA) using a suitable indicator such as eriochrome black T or calmagite.
  - Record the volume of EDTA used and calculate the total hardness in mg/L as calcium carbonate (CaCO3) using the formula:

    `Total hardness (mg/L as CaCO3) = (Volume of EDTA used in mL) x (Concentration of EDTA in mg/L as CaCO3) / (Volume of water sample in mL)`

  - Determine the total alkalinity of the water sample by titrating it with a standard solution of sulfuric acid (H2SO4) using a suitable indicator such as phenolphthalein or bromocresol green-methyl red.
  - Record the volume of H2SO4 used and calculate the total alkalinity in mg/L as CaCO3 using the formula:

    `Total alkalinity (mg/L as CaCO3) = (Volume of H2SO4 used in mL) x (Concentration of H2SO4 in mg/L as CaCO3) / (Volume of water sample in mL)`

  - Compare the results with the guidelines for water hardness and alkalinity for different purposes . For example, water with hardness less than 60 mg/L as CaCO3 is classified as soft, while water with alkalinity less than 20 mg/L as CaCO3 is classified as low.
