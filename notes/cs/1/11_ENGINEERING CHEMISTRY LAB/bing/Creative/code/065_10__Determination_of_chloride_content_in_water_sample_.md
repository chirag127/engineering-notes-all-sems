# Determination of chloride content in water sample

- Chloride ions are one of the major inorganic anions in water and wastewater.
- Chloride ions can be determined by various methods, such as argentometric, potentiometric, colorimetric, and gravimetric methods.
- Argentometric method is based on the precipitation of chloride ions with silver nitrate solution in the presence of an indicator.
- Potentiometric method is based on the measurement of the potential difference between a chloride-specific electrode and a reference electrode in a solution containing chloride ions.
- Colorimetric method is based on the formation of a colored complex between chloride ions and a reagent, such as mercuric thiocyanate or ferric thiocyanate, and measuring the absorbance of the solution at a specific wavelength.
- Gravimetric method is based on the precipitation of chloride ions as silver chloride and weighing the dried precipitate.

## Argentometric method (Mohr's method)

- This method is suitable for water samples with pH between 6.5 and 10 and chloride concentration between 10 and 1000 mg/L.
- The principle of this method is that water containing chloride ions is titrated with a standard silver nitrate solution until a reddish-brown precipitate of silver chromate is formed at the end point.
- The indicator used is potassium chromate, which supplies chromate ions that react with excess silver ions to form the precipitate.
- The reaction is quantitative and follows a 1:1 stoichiometry between silver nitrate and chloride ions.
- The procedure for this method is as follows:

  - Take 50 mL of sample (V mL) and dilute to 100 mL with distilled water in a conical flask.
  - If the sample is highly colored, add 3 mL of aluminum hydroxide and shake well, allow to settle, filter, wash and collect the filtrate.
  - Bring the sample pH to 7-8 by adding acid or alkali if necessary.
  - Add 1 mL of indicator (potassium chromate) and mix well.
  - Titrate the solution against standard silver nitrate solution (N/50 or 0.02 M) from a burette until a reddish-brown precipitate is obtained.
  - Note the volume of silver nitrate solution used (V1 mL).
  - Repeat the titration with a blank (distilled water) and note the volume of silver nitrate solution used (V2 mL).
  - Calculate the chloride concentration in the sample as follows:

    - Chloride concentration (mg/L) = (V1 - V2) x N x 35.45 x 1000 / V
    - Where N is the normality of silver nitrate solution and 35.45 is the equivalent weight of chloride ion.

## Potentiometric method

- This method is suitable for water samples with chloride concentration between 1 and 1000 mg/L.
- The principle of this method is that the potential difference between a chloride-specific electrode and a reference electrode is proportional to the logarithm of the chloride ion activity in the solution.
- The chloride-specific electrode is a solid-state electrode that consists of a silver wire coated with silver chloride and a membrane of silver sulfide or silver bromide.
- The reference electrode is usually a saturated calomel electrode (SCE) or a silver-silver chloride electrode.
- The procedure for this method is as follows:

  - Prepare a series of standard solutions of known chloride concentrations by diluting a stock solution of sodium chloride (1000 mg/L) with distilled water.
  - Immerse the chloride-specific electrode and the reference electrode in each standard solution and measure the potential difference with a potentiometer or a pH meter.
  - Plot the potential difference versus the logarithm of the chloride concentration and obtain a calibration curve.
  - Immerse the electrodes in the sample solution and measure the potential difference.
  - Read the chloride concentration from the calibration curve.
