### 15. Determination of Rate constant of hydrolysis of esters

- Esters are organic compounds that have the general formula RCOOR', where R and R' are alkyl or aryl groups.
- Esters can undergo hydrolysis, which is a chemical reaction with water, to produce carboxylic acids and alcohols.
- The hydrolysis of esters can be catalyzed by acids or bases, or can occur spontaneously under certain conditions.
- The rate of hydrolysis of esters depends on several factors, such as the nature of the ester, the temperature, the pH, and the concentration of the reactants and products.
- The rate of hydrolysis of esters can be expressed by the following rate law:

`rate = k[ester][water]`

where k is the rate constant, [ester] and [water] are the molar concentrations of the ester and water, respectively.

- The rate constant k can be determined experimentally by measuring the rate of hydrolysis of a known concentration of ester at a constant temperature and pH.
- One method to measure the rate of hydrolysis of esters is to use a conductometric titration, which is a technique that measures the electrical conductivity of a solution as a function of the volume of a titrant added.
- The conductometric titration of the hydrolysis of esters involves the following steps:

  - Prepare a known concentration of ester in a solvent, such as ethanol, and add a known volume of water.
  - Add a few drops of an acid or a base as a catalyst, depending on the type of ester.
  - Place the solution in a beaker and insert a conductivity cell, which consists of two electrodes connected to a conductivity meter.
  - Start the titration by adding a standard solution of a strong base or a strong acid, depending on the catalyst used, from a burette to the beaker, while stirring the solution and recording the conductivity and the volume of the titrant added.
  - Plot the conductivity versus the volume of the titrant added, and identify the equivalence point, which is the point where the conductivity reaches a maximum or a minimum, depending on the type of ester and the catalyst used.
  - Calculate the amount of ester hydrolyzed at the equivalence point, using the stoichiometry of the reaction and the volume and concentration of the titrant.
  - Calculate the rate of hydrolysis of the ester, using the amount of ester hydrolyzed, the initial concentration of the ester, and the time taken to reach the equivalence point.
  - Repeat the titration at different temperatures and pH values, and calculate the corresponding rate constants.
  - Plot the natural logarithm of the rate constant versus the reciprocal of the absolute temperature, and obtain the activation energy and the frequency factor of the hydrolysis reaction, using the Arrhenius equation:

  `ln k = ln A - Ea/RT`

  where A is the frequency factor, Ea is the activation energy, R is the gas constant, and T is the absolute temperature.

- The activation energy and the frequency factor are constants that reflect the energy barrier and the collision frequency of the reactants, respectively. They can be used to compare the rates of hydrolysis of different esters under different conditions.
