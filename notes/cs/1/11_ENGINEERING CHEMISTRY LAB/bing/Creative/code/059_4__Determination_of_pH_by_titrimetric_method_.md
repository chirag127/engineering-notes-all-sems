# Determination of pH by titrimetric method

Titrimetric method is a technique of quantitative analysis that involves measuring the volume of a solution of known concentration (titrant) that is required to react completely with a solution of unknown concentration (analyte) . The point at which the reaction is complete is called the endpoint or equivalence point. The volume of titrant added at the endpoint is called the equivalence volume.

pH is a measure of the acidity or alkalinity of a solution, defined as the negative logarithm of the hydrogen ion concentration: pH = -log[H3O+] . pH can be determined by various methods, such as using indicators, pH meters, or potentiometers. Potentiometry is a method of measuring the potential difference between two electrodes in an electrochemical cell . One of the electrodes is a reference electrode, which has a constant and known potential, and the other is an indicator electrode, which responds to the analyte. The potential difference between the electrodes depends on the concentration of the analyte, and can be related to the pH by the Nernst equation.

A potentiometric titration is a type of titration in which the pH of the solution is measured by a potentiometer throughout the titration . A plot of the pH of the solution versus the volume of titrant added is called a pH curve or a titration curve. The shape of the curve depends on the nature and strength of the acid and base involved in the titration. The endpoint of the titration can be determined by finding the point of inflection of the curve, where the slope changes abruptly. The equivalence point of the titration can be calculated by using the stoichiometry of the reaction and the equivalence volume.

The following are the steps for determining the pH by titrimetric method :

- Prepare a standard solution of the titrant and a sample solution of the analyte.
- Calibrate the pH meter or the potentiometer using buffer solutions of known pH.
- Rinse the electrodes with distilled water and dry them gently with a tissue paper.
- Fill a burette with the titrant and record the initial volume.
- Transfer a measured volume of the analyte to a beaker or a flask and add a few drops of an indicator (optional).
- Immerse the electrodes in the analyte solution and record the initial pH.
- Add the titrant gradually from the burette to the analyte solution, stirring constantly, and record the pH and the volume of titrant after each addition.
- Continue the titration until the endpoint is reached, indicated by a color change of the indicator (if used) or a sharp change in the pH.
- Record the final volume of the titrant and the final pH of the solution.
- Plot the pH curve by using the pH and the volume of titrant data.
- Find the point of inflection of the curve and mark the equivalence point and the equivalence volume.
- Calculate the concentration of the analyte by using the stoichiometry of the reaction and the equivalence volume.
- Calculate the pH of the solution at the equivalence point by using the Nernst equation or an appropriate formula.
