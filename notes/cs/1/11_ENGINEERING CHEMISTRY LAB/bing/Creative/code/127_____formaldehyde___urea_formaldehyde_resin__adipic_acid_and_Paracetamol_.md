### Formaldehyde
- Formaldehyde is a naturally occurring organic compound with the formula CH2O and structure H−CHO.
- Formaldehyde is a colorless, highly toxic, and flammable gas at room temperature.
- Formaldehyde is used in the production of fertilizer, paper, plywood, and some resins. It is also used as a food preservative and in household products, such as antiseptics, medicines, and cosmetics.
- Formaldehyde is a by-product of combustion and certain natural processes, such as forest fires, automobile exhaust, and tobacco smoke. It can also be formed in the atmosphere by the action of sunlight and oxygen on atmospheric methane and other hydrocarbons .
- Formaldehyde exposure can cause irritation of the eyes, nose, and throat, as well as respiratory and skin problems. It can also increase the risk of some cancers, such as leukemia and nasopharyngeal cancer .

### Urea formaldehyde resin
- Urea formaldehyde resin is a synthetic polymer made by the condensation of urea and formaldehyde.
- Urea formaldehyde resin is a thermosetting resin, which means it can be cured by heat and pressure to form a hard and durable material.
- Urea formaldehyde resin is widely used as an adhesive for wood products, such as particleboard, plywood, and fiberboard. It is also used as a coating for paper, textiles, and electrical appliances.
- Urea formaldehyde resin can release formaldehyde gas during its production, processing, and use, which can pose health hazards to workers and consumers.
- Urea formaldehyde resin can be modified by adding other chemicals, such as melamine, phenol, or resorcinol, to improve its properties and reduce its formaldehyde emission.

### Adipic acid
- Adipic acid is an organic compound with the formula C6H10O4 and structure HOOC−(CH2)4−COOH.
- Adipic acid is a white, crystalline solid that is slightly soluble in water and alcohol.
- Adipic acid is mainly used as a raw material for the production of nylon 6,6, a synthetic fiber and plastic that is used in clothing, carpets, and engineering applications.
- Adipic acid is also used as a food additive, such as a flavoring agent, a gelling agent, and a leavening agent. It can impart a sour or tart taste to foods, such as soft drinks, candies, and baked goods.
- Adipic acid is usually produced by the oxidation of cyclohexane or cyclohexanol with nitric acid, which generates nitrous oxide, a greenhouse gas, as a by-product. Alternative methods of production, such as biotechnology or electrochemistry, are being developed to reduce the environmental impact of adipic acid manufacturing.

### Paracetamol
- Paracetamol is an organic compound with the formula C8H9NO2 and structure HOC6H4NHCOCH3.
- Paracetamol is a white, crystalline powder that is slightly soluble in water and alcohol.
- Paracetamol is a common over-the-counter medicine that is used to relieve pain and fever. It is also known as acetaminophen in the United States and Canada.
- Paracetamol works by inhibiting the synthesis of prostaglandins, which are chemical messengers that mediate inflammation and pain in the body.
- Paracetamol is generally safe and effective when taken as directed, but it can cause liver damage or failure if taken in large doses or with alcohol. Paracetamol overdose is a serious medical emergency that requires immediate treatment.
