# Course Objectives

- Course objectives are statements that describe what students should be able to do or know by the end of a course.
- Course objectives are usually derived from the course goals, which are broader and more general statements of the overall purpose and direction of the course.
- Course objectives are important for several reasons:
  - They provide a clear and measurable way to assess the learning outcomes of the course.
  - They guide the selection and organization of the course content, activities, and assessments.
  - They communicate the expectations and standards of the course to the students and other stakeholders.
- Course objectives should be SMART, which stands for Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, and Time-bound.
  - Specific: The objective should state exactly what the student should be able to do or know, using action verbs and clear criteria.
  - Measurable: The objective should be observable and quantifiable, using appropriate tools and methods to evaluate the student's performance.
  - Achievable: The objective should be realistic and attainable, considering the level and background of the students, the resources and time available, and the scope and difficulty of the course.
  - Relevant: The objective should be aligned with the course goals, the program outcomes, and the discipline standards.
  - Time-bound: The objective should indicate when and how often the student should demonstrate the desired skill or knowledge, using deadlines and benchmarks.
- Course objectives should be written in the following format: By the end of this course, the student will be able to (action verb) (criteria) (condition).
  - For example: By the end of this course, the student will be able to write a persuasive essay using at least three sources and following the APA style guidelines.
