# Preparation of Phenol Formaldehyde (PF) Resin

- Phenol formaldehyde resin is a type of thermosetting polymer that is obtained by the condensation reaction of phenol and formaldehyde .
- Phenol formaldehyde resin can be prepared by either acid- or base-catalyzed method . The choice of the catalyst affects the properties and applications of the resin.
- In the acid-catalyzed method, phenol and formaldehyde are mixed in a molar ratio of 1:1 or less, and a strong acid such as hydrochloric acid or sulfuric acid is added as the catalyst . The reaction proceeds at a low temperature (below 100°C) and produces a linear resin with a high molecular weight and a high degree of cross-linking . This resin is called novolac and it is hard, brittle, and insoluble in most solvents . Novolac resin requires a curing agent such as hexamethylenetetramine to form a three-dimensional network and become a thermoset .
- In the base-catalyzed method, phenol and formaldehyde are mixed in a molar ratio of more than 1:1, and a weak base such as ammonia or sodium hydroxide is added as the catalyst . The reaction proceeds at a high temperature (above 100°C) and produces a branched resin with a low molecular weight and a low degree of cross-linking . This resin is called resol and it is soft, flexible, and soluble in some solvents . Resol resin can self-cure without a curing agent and form a thermoset .
- Phenol formaldehyde resin can also be modified by adding other monomers such as urea, melamine, or furfural. These co-condensed resins have different properties and applications than the pure phenol formaldehyde resin. For example, phenol-urea-formaldehyde resin has improved water resistance and reduced formaldehyde emission than urea-formaldehyde resin.
