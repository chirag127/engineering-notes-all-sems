### 9. Determination of available chlorine in bleaching powder

Bleaching powder is a compound of calcium hypochlorite [Ca(OCl)<sub>2</sub>] and calcium chloride [CaCl<sub>2</sub>]. It is used as a disinfectant and a bleaching agent. The available chlorine in bleaching powder is the amount of chlorine that can be liberated when the powder is treated with dilute acid. The available chlorine is expressed as a percentage of the mass of the bleaching powder.

The procedure for determining the available chlorine in bleaching powder is as follows:

- Weigh a sample of bleaching powder and dissolve it in water.
- Transfer a measured volume of the solution to an Erlenmeyer flask and add excess of potassium iodide [KI] and acetic acid [CH<sub>3</sub>COOH].
- The chlorine in the bleaching powder reacts with the iodide to form iodine [I<sub>2</sub>].
- Titrate the liberated iodine with a standard solution of sodium thiosulfate [Na<sub>2</sub>S<sub>2</sub>O<sub>3</sub>] using starch as an indicator.
- The end point is the disappearance of the blue color of the starch-iodine complex.
- The volume of sodium thiosulfate used is proportional to the amount of chlorine in the bleaching powder.
- Calculate the percentage of available chlorine using the following formula:

`% available chlorine = (V x N x 35.5 x 100) / (W x 1000)`

where V is the volume of sodium thiosulfate in mL, N is the normality of sodium thiosulfate, W is the mass of bleaching powder in g, and 35.5 is the equivalent mass of chlorine.
