## LIST OF EXPERIMENTS

An experiment is a scientific procedure that is carried out to test a hypothesis, observe a phenomenon, or measure an effect. Experiments usually involve manipulating one or more variables and measuring their effects on other variables. Experiments can be classified into different types based on their purpose, design, and method.

Some of the common types of experiments are:

- **Controlled experiments**: These are experiments where the researcher controls all the variables except the one that is being tested. The variable that is being tested is called the independent variable, and the variable that is being measured is called the dependent variable. The researcher also creates a control group that is identical to the experimental group except for the independent variable. The control group serves as a baseline for comparison. Controlled experiments are often used to establish causal relationships between variables.

- **Natural experiments**: These are experiments where the researcher does not manipulate any variables, but observes the effects of a natural event or phenomenon on the variables of interest. The natural event or phenomenon acts as the independent variable, and the researcher measures the dependent variable. Natural experiments are often used to study the effects of rare or unpredictable events that cannot be replicated in a laboratory.

- **Quasi-experiments**: These are experiments where the researcher manipulates the independent variable, but does not create a control group or randomly assign the participants to the experimental or control groups. The researcher may use existing groups or select the participants based on some criteria. Quasi-experiments are often used when controlled experiments are not feasible or ethical.

- **Field experiments**: These are experiments where the researcher conducts the experiment in a natural setting, such as a school, a workplace, or a community. The researcher may manipulate the independent variable, create a control group, or use existing groups. Field experiments are often used to study the effects of interventions or policies in real-world contexts.

- **Laboratory experiments**: These are experiments where the researcher conducts the experiment in a controlled and artificial setting, such as a laboratory. The researcher can manipulate the independent variable, create a control group, and randomly assign the participants to the experimental or control groups. Laboratory experiments are often used to study the effects of variables that can be precisely measured and controlled.
