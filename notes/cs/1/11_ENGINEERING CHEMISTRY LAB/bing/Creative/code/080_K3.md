# K3 surface

A K3 surface is a type of algebraic surface that has some special properties. Here are some points to know about K3 surfaces:

- A K3 surface is a smooth, proper, geometrically connected algebraic surface over any field that has trivial canonical bundle and no non-zero global holomorphic 1-forms .
- A K3 surface is a minimal surface of Kodaira dimension zero, meaning that it has no curves of negative self-intersection and no non-trivial holomorphic vector fields .
- A K3 surface is simply connected, meaning that it has no non-trivial loops that can be continuously shrunk to a point .
- A K3 surface has 24 singular points in its Picard group, which is the group of divisors modulo linear equivalence .
- A K3 surface has a Hodge diamond of the form

```
1
0 20 0
1 0 1
0 20 0
1
```

which means that its cohomology groups have certain dimensions and types .
- A K3 surface has Euler characteristic 24, meaning that the alternating sum of its Betti numbers is 24 .
- A K3 surface is Calabi-Yau, meaning that it admits a Ricci-flat Kähler metric .
- A K3 surface is hyperkähler, meaning that it admits three complex structures that are compatible with the same Kähler form .
- A K3 surface is a complex manifold of dimension 2, meaning that it can be locally described by two complex coordinates .
- A K3 surface is a smooth 4-manifold, meaning that it can be locally described by four real coordinates .
- A K3 surface is a symplectic manifold, meaning that it has a closed non-degenerate 2-form that defines a Poisson bracket on its smooth functions .

Some examples of K3 surfaces are:

- The Fermat quartic surface, which is defined by the equation

```
x^4 + y^4 + z^4 + w^4 = 0
```

in the projective space P^3^ .
- The Kummer surface, which is obtained by taking the quotient of an abelian surface by the involution that negates all the coordinates .
- The Dwork family of quintic surfaces, which are defined by the equation

```
x^5 + y^5 + z^5 + w^5 + a x y z w = 0
```

in the projective space P^3^, where a is a parameter.
- The Schoen surface, which is obtained by gluing two copies of the Fermat quartic surface along a smooth curve of genus 19.

K3 surfaces are important objects in algebraic geometry, complex geometry, differential geometry, and mathematical physics. They have many applications and connections to topics such as moduli spaces, mirror symmetry, string theory, and 4-manifold theory  .
