### 9. Determination of available chlorine in bleaching powder

Bleaching powder is a compound of calcium hypochlorite [Ca(OCl)<sub>2</sub>] and calcium chloride [CaCl<sub>2</sub>]. It is used as a disinfectant and a bleaching agent. The available chlorine in bleaching powder is the amount of chlorine that can be liberated when the compound is treated with dilute acids. The available chlorine is expressed as a percentage of the mass of bleaching powder.

The principle of the determination is based on the following reactions:

Ca(OCl)Cl + H<sub>2</sub>SO<sub>4</sub> → CaSO<sub>4</sub> + H<sub>2</sub>O + Cl<sub>2</sub>

Cl<sub>2</sub> + 2KI → 2KCl + I<sub>2</sub>

I<sub>2</sub> + 2Na<sub>2</sub>S<sub>2</sub>O<sub>3</sub> → Na<sub>2</sub>S<sub>4</sub>O<sub>6</sub> + 2NaI

The bleaching powder is dissolved in water and treated with excess of potassium iodide (KI) and acetic acid (CH<sub>3</sub>COOH). The chlorine gas liberated reacts with KI to form iodine (I<sub>2</sub>), which is then titrated with sodium thiosulfate (Na<sub>2</sub>S<sub>2</sub>O<sub>3</sub>) solution using starch as an indicator. The end point is the disappearance of the blue color of the starch-iodine complex. The amount of Na<sub>2</sub>S<sub>2</sub>O<sub>3</sub> used is proportional to the amount of Cl<sub>2</sub> liberated, and hence to the available chlorine in the bleaching powder.

The procedure for the determination is as follows:

- Weigh accurately about 0.5 g of bleaching powder and transfer it to a 250 mL conical flask.
- Add about 100 mL of distilled water and shake well to dissolve the bleaching powder.
- Add 10 mL of 10% KI solution and 10 mL of 20% CH<sub>3</sub>COOH solution and mix well.
- Titrate the liberated iodine with 0.1 N Na<sub>2</sub>S<sub>2</sub>O<sub>3</sub> solution using a burette until the yellow color of iodine is almost gone.
- Add a few drops of starch solution and continue the titration until the blue color disappears.
- Record the volume of Na<sub>2</sub>S<sub>2</sub>O<sub>3</sub> used as V<sub>1</sub> mL.
- Perform a blank titration by repeating the same procedure without adding the bleaching powder. Record the volume of Na<sub>2</sub>S<sub>2</sub>O<sub>3</sub> used as V<sub>2</sub> mL.
- Calculate the percentage of available chlorine in the bleaching powder using the formula:

% available chlorine = (V<sub>2</sub> - V<sub>1</sub>) x N x 35.5 x 100 / W

where N is the normality of Na<sub>2</sub>S<sub>2</sub>O<sub>3</sub> solution, W is the weight of bleaching powder in g, and 35.5 is the equivalent weight of chlorine.
