## Topic: Nuclear Fusion

- Nuclear fusion is the process of combining two or more lighter nuclei into a heavier nucleus, releasing a large amount of energy in the process.
- Nuclear fusion is the source of energy for the Sun and other stars, where hydrogen nuclei fuse into helium nuclei under high temperature and pressure.
- Nuclear fusion has the potential to provide clean, abundant and sustainable energy for human civilization, but it is very difficult to achieve and control on Earth.
- The main challenges of nuclear fusion are:
  - Achieving the high temperature and pressure required to overcome the electrostatic repulsion between positively charged nuclei.
  - Sustaining the fusion reaction for a long enough time to produce more energy than is consumed by the heating and confinement systems.
  - Dealing with the high-energy neutrons and radiation that are produced by the fusion reaction, which can damage the materials and components of the fusion reactor.
- The main approaches to achieve nuclear fusion on Earth are:
  - Magnetic confinement fusion, which uses strong magnetic fields to confine and compress a plasma of hydrogen isotopes (deuterium and tritium) in a doughnut-shaped device called a tokamak or a stellarator.
  - Inertial confinement fusion, which uses powerful lasers or ion beams to heat and compress a tiny pellet of hydrogen isotopes to the point of ignition.
  - Alternative concepts, such as muon-catalyzed fusion, which uses muons (heavy electrons) to replace the electrons in hydrogen atoms and reduce the electrostatic repulsion, or fusion-fission hybrids, which use a fusion reactor to trigger a fission reaction in a subcritical mass of uranium or plutonium.
- The main advantages of nuclear fusion are:
  - It produces no greenhouse gases or long-lived radioactive waste, unlike nuclear fission or fossil fuels.
  - It uses abundant and widely available fuels, such as seawater and lithium, unlike oil, coal or uranium.
  - It has a high energy density, meaning that a small amount of fuel can produce a large amount of energy, unlike solar or wind power.
  - It has a low risk of accidents, proliferation or terrorism, unlike nuclear fission or fossil fuels.
- The main disadvantages of nuclear fusion are:
  - It is very expensive and complex to build and operate a fusion reactor, and the technology is still in the experimental stage, unlike nuclear fission or fossil fuels.
  - It requires a large amount of electricity to start and maintain the fusion reaction, and the net energy gain is still uncertain, unlike solar or wind power.
  - It produces high-energy neutrons and radiation that can damage the reactor and pose a health and environmental hazard, unlike solar or wind power.
