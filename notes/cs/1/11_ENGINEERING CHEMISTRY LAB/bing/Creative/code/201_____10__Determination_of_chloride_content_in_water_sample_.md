# Determination of chloride content in water sample

## Introduction

Chloride is one of the major anions found in natural waters. It originates from various sources, such as seawater intrusion, industrial effluents, agricultural runoff, road salt, and domestic sewage. Chloride can affect the taste, corrosion, and biological activity of water. Therefore, it is important to monitor and control the chloride content in water samples.

## Principle

The most common method for determining the chloride content in water sample is the Mohr method. This method is based on the precipitation of chloride ions as silver chloride in the presence of chromate indicator. The end point of the titration is marked by the formation of a red-brown precipitate of silver chromate, which indicates that all the chloride ions have been precipitated.

## Procedure

- Take a 50 mL aliquot of the water sample in a conical flask and add a few drops of potassium chromate indicator.
- Titrate the sample with 0.1 N silver nitrate solution from a burette until a permanent red-brown color appears.
- Record the volume of silver nitrate solution used as V1.
- Repeat the titration with a blank sample of distilled water and record the volume of silver nitrate solution used as V2.
- Calculate the chloride content in the water sample using the following formula:

Chloride content (mg/L) = (V1 - V2) x N x 35.45 x 1000 / 50

where N is the normality of silver nitrate solution and 35.45 is the equivalent weight of chloride.

## Precautions

- The water sample should be free from turbidity and color, as they may interfere with the end point detection.
- The potassium chromate indicator should be added in a small amount, as excess chromate may cause co-precipitation of silver chromate with silver chloride.
- The silver nitrate solution should be standardized against a known solution of sodium chloride before use.
- The titration should be performed in a well-lit room, as the color change may be difficult to observe in dim light.
- The conical flask should be swirled constantly during the titration to ensure complete mixing of the reactants.
