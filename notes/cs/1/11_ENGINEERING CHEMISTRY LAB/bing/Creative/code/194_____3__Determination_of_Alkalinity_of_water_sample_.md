### 3. Determination of Alkalinity of water sample.

- Alkalinity is the measure of the capacity of water to neutralize acids. It is mainly caused by the presence of bicarbonates, carbonates, and hydroxides of calcium, magnesium, sodium, and potassium in water.
- Alkalinity is important for water quality because it affects the pH, corrosion, and disinfection processes. High alkalinity can cause scaling and reduce the effectiveness of chlorine. Low alkalinity can cause corrosion and pH fluctuations.
- Alkalinity can be determined by titrating a water sample with a standard acid solution, such as sulfuric acid or hydrochloric acid, using a suitable indicator, such as phenolphthalein or methyl orange, to detect the endpoint of the titration.
- The procedure for determining the alkalinity of water sample is as follows:

  - Take 100 mL of water sample in a conical flask and add 2-3 drops of phenolphthalein indicator. The indicator will turn pink if the water sample has any phenolphthalein alkalinity (due to the presence of hydroxides or carbonates).
  - Titrate the water sample with 0.02 N sulfuric acid or hydrochloric acid from a burette until the pink color disappears. Note down the volume of acid used as V1. This is the phenolphthalein alkalinity of the water sample.
  - Add 2-3 drops of methyl orange indicator to the same water sample. The indicator will turn orange or yellow if the water sample has any methyl orange alkalinity (due to the presence of bicarbonates).
  - Titrate the water sample with 0.02 N sulfuric acid or hydrochloric acid from the same burette until the color changes to pink. Note down the volume of acid used as V2. This is the total alkalinity of the water sample.
  - The phenolphthalein alkalinity (P) and the methyl orange alkalinity (M) can be calculated as follows:

    - P = V1 x N x 50
    - M = V2 x N x 50

    where V1 and V2 are the volumes of acid used in mL, N is the normality of the acid, and 50 is the conversion factor for 100 mL of water sample.
  - The types and concentrations of alkalinity present in the water sample can be determined by comparing the values of P and M as follows:

    - If P = 0 and M > 0, then only bicarbonates are present. The concentration of bicarbonates (HCO3-) can be calculated as M mg/L.
    - If P = M > 0, then only carbonates are present. The concentration of carbonates (CO3 2-) can be calculated as M/2 mg/L.
    - If P < M > 0, then both carbonates and bicarbonates are present. The concentration of carbonates (CO3 2-) can be calculated as P/2 mg/L, and the concentration of bicarbonates (HCO3-) can be calculated as (M-P) mg/L.
    - If P > M > 0, then hydroxides, carbonates, and bicarbonates are present. The concentration of hydroxides (OH-) can be calculated as (P-M) mg/L, the concentration of carbonates (CO3 2-) can be calculated as M/2 mg/L, and the concentration of bicarbonates (HCO3-) can be calculated as 0 mg/L.
