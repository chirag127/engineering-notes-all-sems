# 6. Determination of Viscosity of a given liquid by viscometer

## Introduction

- Viscosity is a measure of the resistance of a fluid to flow or deform under an applied shear stress.
- Viscosity is important for many applications, such as lubrication, fluid dynamics, transport phenomena, and rheology.
- Viscosity can be measured by various methods, such as capillary viscometer, rotational viscometer, falling ball viscometer, etc.
- In this experiment, we will use a capillary viscometer, also known as an Ostwald viscometer, to determine the viscosity of a given liquid.

## Theory

- A capillary viscometer consists of a U-shaped glass tube with two bulbs and a narrow capillary section between them.
- The liquid to be tested is filled in the lower bulb and the upper bulb is connected to a constant pressure source, such as a water reservoir.
- When the pressure is applied, the liquid flows through the capillary section and the time taken for a fixed volume of liquid to pass through two marks on the tube is measured.
- The flow rate of the liquid is inversely proportional to its viscosity, according to the Hagen-Poiseuille equation:

  Q = (pi * r^4 * delta P) / (8 * eta * L)

  where Q is the flow rate, r is the radius of the capillary, delta P is the pressure difference, eta is the viscosity, and L is the length of the capillary.

- The viscosity of the liquid can be calculated by comparing the flow rate of the liquid with the flow rate of a reference fluid, such as water, whose viscosity is known at a given temperature.
- The relative viscosity of the liquid is defined as the ratio of the flow time of the liquid to the flow time of the reference fluid:

  eta_r = t / t_0

  where eta_r is the relative viscosity, t is the flow time of the liquid, and t_0 is the flow time of the reference fluid.

- The viscosity of the liquid can be obtained by multiplying the relative viscosity by the viscosity of the reference fluid:

  eta = eta_r * eta_0

  where eta is the viscosity of the liquid, and eta_0 is the viscosity of the reference fluid.

## Procedure

- Clean the capillary viscometer and dry it with a soft cloth.
- Fill the lower bulb with distilled water and connect the upper bulb to the water reservoir.
- Adjust the height of the reservoir so that the water level in the lower bulb is slightly above the upper mark on the tube.
- Open the valve and let the water flow through the capillary section until the water level reaches the lower mark on the tube.
- Start the stopwatch and record the time taken for the water level to rise from the lower mark to the upper mark on the tube. This is the flow time of the reference fluid, t_0.
- Repeat the measurement at least three times and take the average value of t_0.
- Drain the water from the viscometer and rinse it with the liquid to be tested.
- Fill the lower bulb with the liquid and repeat the steps 3 to 6 to obtain the flow time of the liquid, t.
- Measure the temperature of the liquid and the reference fluid and note it down.
- Calculate the relative viscosity, eta_r, and the viscosity, eta, of the liquid using the formulas given in the theory section.
- Compare the viscosity of the liquid with the values given in the literature or the standard tables and comment on the accuracy and precision of the experiment.

## Precautions

- The capillary viscometer should be clean and free of air bubbles before use.
- The liquid and the reference fluid should be at the same temperature during the experiment.
- The pressure difference should be constant and sufficient to maintain a steady flow of the liquid.
- The stopwatch should be started and stopped accurately at the marks on the tube.
- The measurements should be repeated several times and the average values should be used for calculations.
- The errors and uncertainties in the measurements should be estimated and reported.
