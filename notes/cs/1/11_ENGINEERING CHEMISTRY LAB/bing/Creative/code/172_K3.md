# K3

- K3 is a type of surface in mathematics that is named after three mathematicians: Kummer, Kähler, and Kodaira.
- A K3 surface is a complex algebraic surface that has trivial canonical bundle and no curves of negative self-intersection.
- A K3 surface is also a Calabi-Yau manifold, which means that it has a Ricci-flat metric and a holomorphic volume form.
- K3 surfaces are important in various fields of mathematics and physics, such as algebraic geometry, string theory, and mirror symmetry.
- Some examples of K3 surfaces are:

  - The quartic surface in projective 3-space defined by a homogeneous polynomial of degree 4.
  - The double cover of the projective plane branched along a smooth sextic curve.
  - The Kummer surface, which is obtained by taking the quotient of a 2-torus by an involution and resolving the 16 singular points.
  - The Fermat quartic surface, which is defined by the equation x^4 + y^4 + z^4 + w^4 = 0 in projective 3-space.
