### 4. Determination of pH by titrimetric method

- Titrimetric method is a technique of quantitative analysis that involves measuring the volume of a solution of known concentration (titrant) that is required to react completely with a solution of unknown concentration (analyte)  .
- pH is a measure of the acidity or alkalinity of a solution, defined as the negative logarithm of the hydrogen ion concentration: pH = -log[H3O+]  .
- Determination of pH by titrimetric method involves using a pH meter to monitor the change in pH of the analyte solution as the titrant is added   .
- A pH meter consists of a glass electrode that responds to the hydrogen ion activity in the solution, and a reference electrode that provides a constant potential. The difference in potential between the two electrodes is proportional to the pH of the solution  .
- A plot of the pH of the analyte solution versus the volume of the titrant added is called a pH curve or a titration curve  . The shape of the curve depends on the nature and strength of the acid and base involved in the titration .
- The point at which the titration is complete, where the moles of titrant are equal to the moles of analyte, is called the equivalence point or the stoichiometric point  . The pH at the equivalence point depends on the type of acid-base reaction .
- The point at which the indicator changes color, signaling the end of the titration, is called the endpoint. The endpoint should be as close as possible to the equivalence point for an accurate titration  .
- The choice of the indicator depends on the pH range of the color change and the pH of the equivalence point. The indicator should have a sharp color change over a narrow pH range that includes the equivalence point  .
- Some examples of titrimetric methods for pH determination are:
  - Acid-base titration: A titration between an acid and a base, where the pH changes rapidly near the equivalence point  .
  - Redox titration: A titration between a reducing agent and an oxidizing agent, where the pH changes due to the formation or consumption of hydrogen ions in the redox reaction .
  - Complexometric titration: A titration between a metal ion and a complexing agent, where the pH changes due to the formation or dissociation of the complex .
  - Precipitation titration: A titration between two ions that form a sparingly soluble precipitate, where the pH changes due to the common ion effect or the hydrolysis of the precipitate .
