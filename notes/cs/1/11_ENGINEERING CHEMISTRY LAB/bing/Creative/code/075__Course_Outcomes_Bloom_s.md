# Course Outcomes and Bloom's Taxonomy

- Course outcomes are statements that describe what students are expected to know and be able to do by the end of a course.
- Bloom's taxonomy is a framework for classifying educational objectives into six levels of cognitive complexity: remember, understand, apply, analyze, evaluate, and create.
- Course outcomes should be aligned with Bloom's taxonomy to ensure that students achieve different levels of learning and demonstrate higher-order thinking skills.
- Course outcomes should be specific, measurable, achievable, relevant, and time-bound (SMART) to provide clear and realistic expectations for students and instructors.
- Course outcomes should be written using action verbs that indicate the level of cognitive skill required by the students, such as define, explain, compare, contrast, solve, design, etc.
- Course outcomes should be assessed using appropriate methods and tools that match the level of cognitive skill and the type of learning outcome, such as quizzes, exams, assignments, projects, portfolios, etc.
- Course outcomes should be communicated to students at the beginning of the course and throughout the course to help them monitor their progress and self-regulate their learning.
- Course outcomes should be reviewed and revised periodically to ensure that they reflect the current needs and expectations of the students, the instructors, the discipline, and the society.
