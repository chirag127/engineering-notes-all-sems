### 1. To enable the students to understand about the fundamental concepts of analytical instruments

- Analytical instruments are devices that are used to measure, analyze, or monitor physical, chemical, or biological properties of samples.
- Analytical instruments can be classified into different categories based on their principles, applications, or techniques, such as spectroscopy, chromatography, mass spectrometry, electrochemistry, microscopy, etc.
- Analytical instruments can be used for various purposes, such as quality control, research and development, environmental monitoring, forensic analysis, clinical diagnosis, etc.
- Analytical instruments consist of three main components: a sample introduction system, a detector, and a data processing system.
- A sample introduction system is responsible for preparing, transporting, and introducing the sample into the detector. It can include devices such as syringes, pumps, valves, columns, nebulizers, etc.
- A detector is the part of the instrument that converts the physical, chemical, or biological signal from the sample into an electrical signal that can be measured and recorded. It can include devices such as photodiodes, electrodes, ion sources, etc.
- A data processing system is the part of the instrument that analyzes, displays, and stores the data obtained from the detector. It can include devices such as computers, software, printers, etc.
- Analytical instruments can have different characteristics, such as sensitivity, selectivity, accuracy, precision, resolution, range, etc., that affect their performance and suitability for different applications.
- Analytical instruments can also have different advantages and limitations, such as cost, speed, reliability, complexity, maintenance, etc., that need to be considered when choosing or designing an instrument for a specific purpose.
