### CO-3 Measure the hardness and alkalinity of the water. K3

- Hardness and alkalinity are two parameters that indicate the water quality and the suitability of water for various purposes.
- Hardness measures the total amount of divalent salts, mainly calcium and magnesium, that can form insoluble deposits or scale in pipes, boilers, and appliances.
- Alkalinity measures the total amount of bases, mainly bicarbonate and carbonate, that can neutralize acids and buffer the pH of water.
- Hardness and alkalinity are related through common ions formed in aquatic systems. The counter-ions associated with the bicarbonate and carbonate fraction of alkalinity are the principal cations responsible for hardness.
- Hardness and alkalinity are influenced by the geology of the area where the water is located and the dissolution of carbon dioxide from the atmosphere. The ions responsible for hardness and alkalinity originate from the dissolution of geological minerals into rain and groundwater.
- Hardness and alkalinity can be measured by titration methods using standard solutions of acids and indicators.
- To measure hardness, a water sample is titrated with a standard solution of ethylenediaminetetraacetic acid (EDTA) using a metal indicator such as Eriochrome Black T or Calmagite. The endpoint of the titration is marked by a color change from red to blue.
- To measure alkalinity, a water sample is titrated with a standard solution of sulfuric acid using phenolphthalein and methyl orange as indicators. The endpoint of the titration is marked by a color change from pink to colorless for phenolphthalein alkalinity and from yellow to orange for methyl orange alkalinity.
- The results of the titration are expressed in milligrams per liter (mg/L) or parts per million (ppm) as calcium carbonate (CaCO3) equivalents.
- The hardness and alkalinity of water can affect the taste, odor, corrosion, scaling, and biological activity of water. Therefore, it is important to measure and control the hardness and alkalinity of water for domestic, industrial, and agricultural uses.
