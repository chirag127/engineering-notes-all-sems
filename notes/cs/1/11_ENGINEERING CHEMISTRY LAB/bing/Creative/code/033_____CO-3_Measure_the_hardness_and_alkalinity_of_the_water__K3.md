### CO-3 Measure the hardness and alkalinity of the water. K3

- Hardness and alkalinity are two important parameters of water quality that indicate the concentration of dissolved minerals and bases in water respectively.
- Hardness is caused by compounds of calcium and magnesium, and by a variety of other metals, such as iron, manganese, and zinc. Hard water can cause scaling, corrosion, and reduced efficiency of water appliances and pipes.
- Alkalinity measures the total amount of bases present in water, mainly bicarbonate, carbonate, and hydroxide ions. Alkalinity can buffer the water against pH changes and acidification. Alkalinity is related to hardness through the common ions of calcium and magnesium that are associated with bicarbonate and carbonate.
- To measure the hardness and alkalinity of water, titration methods are commonly used. Titration is a technique that involves adding a known amount of a solution with a known concentration (titrant) to a sample with an unknown concentration (analyte) until a color change or an endpoint is reached.
- To measure the hardness of water, a titrant of ethylenediaminetetraacetic acid (EDTA) is used, which forms a complex with the hardness ions and changes the color of an indicator (such as eriochrome black T) from red to blue at the endpoint. The amount of EDTA used is proportional to the hardness of the water.
- To measure the alkalinity of water, a titrant of sulfuric acid (H2SO4) is used, which neutralizes the bases in the water and changes the color of an indicator (such as phenolphthalein or bromcresol green) from pink or blue to colorless at the endpoint. The amount of H2SO4 used is proportional to the alkalinity of the water.
- The units of hardness and alkalinity are usually expressed as milligrams per liter (mg/L) or parts per million (ppm) of calcium carbonate (CaCO3) equivalent . The classification of water hardness is as follows:

  - Soft: 0 to 60 mg/L as CaCO3
  - Moderately hard: 61 to 120 mg/L as CaCO3
  - Hard: 121 to 180 mg/L as CaCO3
  - Very hard: more than 180 mg/L as CaCO3

- The classification of water alkalinity is as follows:

  - Low: less than 20 mg/L as CaCO3
  - Moderate: 20 to 200 mg/L as CaCO3
  - High: more than 200 mg/L as CaCO3
