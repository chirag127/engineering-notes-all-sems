# 3. To enable the students to understand about the measure of pH, surface tension and viscosity of

- pH is a measure of the acidity or alkalinity of a solution. It is defined as the negative logarithm of the hydrogen ion concentration in moles per liter. The pH scale ranges from 0 to 14, with 7 being neutral, lower values being acidic, and higher values being basic. Examples of solutions with different pH values are lemon juice (pH 2), vinegar (pH 3), water (pH 7), baking soda (pH 9), and ammonia (pH 11).
- Surface tension is a measure of the energy required to increase the surface area of a liquid, or the force required to increase the length of a liquid surface by a given amount. It is caused by the cohesive forces between molecules at the surface of a liquid, which tend to minimize the surface area. Surface tension is expressed in units of joules per square meter (J/m^2) or newtons per meter (N/m). Examples of liquids with different surface tensions are water (72.8 mN/m), ethanol (22.3 mN/m), and mercury (485 mN/m).
- Viscosity is a measure of the resistance of a fluid to flow. It is caused by the frictional forces between the molecules of a fluid, which oppose their relative motion. Viscosity is expressed in units of pascal-seconds (Pa·s) or poise (P). Examples of fluids with different viscosities are honey (10 Pa·s), water (0.001 Pa·s), and air (0.000018 Pa·s).
