### 15. Determination of Rate constant of hydrolysis of esters

- Esters are organic compounds that have the general formula RCOOR', where R and R' are alkyl or aryl groups.
- Esters can be hydrolyzed by water in the presence of an acid or a base catalyst to form carboxylic acids and alcohols.
- The hydrolysis of esters is a reversible reaction and follows the first-order kinetics with respect to the ester concentration.
- The rate constant of hydrolysis of esters can be determined by measuring the change in the concentration of the ester or the products over time, using various methods such as titration, spectrophotometry, or conductometry.
- One of the common methods to determine the rate constant of hydrolysis of esters is by titrating the hydrolyzed solution with a standard alkali solution and using phenolphthalein as an indicator.
- The procedure for this method is as follows:

  - Prepare a known volume and concentration of the ester solution in a conical flask and add a few drops of phenolphthalein indicator.
  - Add a known volume and concentration of the acid catalyst (usually HCl) to the flask and start the stopwatch.
  - Swirl the flask gently and periodically titrate a small aliquot of the solution with a standard alkali solution (usually NaOH) until a faint pink color persists for 30 seconds.
  - Record the volume of the alkali solution used and the time elapsed for each titration.
  - Repeat the titration at regular intervals until the ester is completely hydrolyzed.
  - Plot a graph of the volume of the alkali solution used versus the time elapsed.
  - The slope of the graph gives the rate of hydrolysis of the ester, which is equal to the rate constant multiplied by the initial concentration of the ester.
  - The rate constant can be calculated by dividing the slope by the initial concentration of the ester.
  - The rate constant can also be calculated by using the integrated rate equation for the first-order reaction:

    - ln([ester]t/[ester]0) = -kt
    - where [ester]t is the concentration of the ester at time t, [ester]0 is the initial concentration of the ester, k is the rate constant, and t is the time elapsed.
    - By plotting a graph of ln([ester]t/[ester]0) versus t, the slope gives the negative value of the rate constant.
