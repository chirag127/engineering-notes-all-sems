### 1. Calibration of Analytical Equipment and apparatus

- Calibration is the process of evaluating and adjusting the precision and accuracy of measurement equipment.
- Calibration ensures that the equipment and apparatus produce valid and reliable results that are consistent with the standards and specifications .
- Calibration involves comparing the output of the equipment and apparatus with a known reference value, such as a certified standard or a traceable measurement .
- Calibration can be done internally or externally, depending on the type and complexity of the equipment and apparatus, the availability of reference standards, and the quality requirements .
- Calibration should be done at regular intervals, based on the frequency of use, the environmental conditions, the stability of the equipment and apparatus, and the manufacturer's recommendations  .
- Calibration should be documented and recorded, with the date, the reference value, the measured value, the uncertainty, the correction factor, and the signature of the person who performed the calibration .
- Calibration should be verified and validated, by checking the performance of the equipment and apparatus against a known sample, a control chart, or a proficiency test .
- Calibration should be followed by maintenance and repair, if needed, to ensure the proper functioning and accuracy of the equipment and apparatus .
