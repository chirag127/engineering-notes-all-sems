# Determination of pH by titrimetric method

- Titrimetric method is a technique of quantitative analysis that involves measuring the volume of a solution of known concentration (titrant) that is required to react completely with a solution of unknown concentration (analyte) .
- pH is a measure of the acidity or alkalinity of a solution, defined as the negative logarithm of the hydrogen ion concentration: pH = -log[H3O+] .
- Determination of pH by titrimetric method involves using a pH meter to monitor the change in pH of the analyte solution as the titrant is added  .
- A pH meter consists of a glass electrode that responds to the hydrogen ion activity in the solution, and a reference electrode that provides a constant potential .
- The potential difference between the two electrodes is proportional to the pH of the solution, according to the Nernst equation .
- A plot of the pH of the solution versus the volume of the titrant added is called a titration curve, which shows the changes in pH during the titration .
- The titration curve can be used to determine the endpoint or equivalence point of the titration, where the moles of titrant are equal to the moles of analyte .
- The shape and features of the titration curve depend on the type and strength of the acid and base involved in the titration .
- For example, a titration of a strong acid with a strong base produces a titration curve that has a steep rise near the equivalence point, which is at pH 7 .
- A titration of a weak acid with a strong base produces a titration curve that has a buffer region before the equivalence point, where the pH changes gradually, and a sharp rise at the equivalence point, which is above pH 7 .
- A titration of a weak base with a strong acid produces a titration curve that has a buffer region before the equivalence point, where the pH changes gradually, and a sharp drop at the equivalence point, which is below pH 7 .
- A titration of a polyprotic acid or base produces a titration curve that has multiple equivalence points, corresponding to the successive deprotonation or protonation of the analyte .
- The advantages of titrimetric method for pH determination are that it is accurate, precise, and applicable to a wide range of acid-base reactions .
- The disadvantages of titrimetric method for pH determination are that it requires a calibrated pH meter, a suitable indicator or endpoint detection method, and a standard titrant solution .
