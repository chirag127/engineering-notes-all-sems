# K3

- K3 is a type of surface in mathematics that is named after three mathematicians: Kummer, Kähler, and Kodaira.
- K3 surfaces are complex algebraic surfaces that have trivial canonical bundle and no holomorphic 1-forms.
- K3 surfaces are also Calabi-Yau manifolds, meaning that they have a Ricci-flat Kähler metric and a holonomy group that is a subgroup of SU(2).
- K3 surfaces have many interesting properties and applications in geometry, topology, number theory, and physics.
- Some examples of K3 surfaces are:

  - The quartic surface in P^3 defined by a homogeneous polynomial of degree 4.
  - The double cover of P^2 branched along a smooth sextic curve.
  - The Kummer surface, which is obtained by taking the quotient of a 2-torus by an involution and resolving the 16 singular points.
  - The resolution of the singularities of the Fermat quartic surface, which is defined by x^4 + y^4 + z^4 + w^4 = 0 in P^3.
