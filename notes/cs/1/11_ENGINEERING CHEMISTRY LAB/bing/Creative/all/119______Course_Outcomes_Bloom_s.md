# Course Outcomes Bloom's Taxonomy

- Course outcomes are brief statements that describe what students will be expected to learn by the end of the course.
- Course outcomes should be aligned with the course content, activities, and assessments, and should reflect the level of cognitive skills that students need to demonstrate.
- Bloom's taxonomy is a model of cognitive skills that can be used to classify educational learning objectives and to design course outcomes that support student learning at multiple levels of cognition .
- Bloom's taxonomy consists of six levels of cognitive skills, from lower-order to higher-order: remember, understand, apply, analyze, evaluate, and create .
- Remembering involves recalling facts, terms, concepts, or procedures from memory .
- Understanding involves explaining, interpreting, summarizing, or paraphrasing the meaning of information .
- Applying involves using knowledge or skills to solve problems or perform tasks in new situations .
- Analyzing involves breaking down information into parts, identifying relationships, or examining causes and effects .
- Evaluating involves judging the quality, value, or relevance of information, arguments, or solutions based on criteria or standards .
- Creating involves generating, designing, planning, or producing new or original ideas, products, or solutions .
- Each level of Bloom's taxonomy requires a different type of verb to describe the learning outcome. For example, verbs for remembering include: define, list, name, recall, recognize, etc. Verbs for creating include: compose, construct, design, develop, invent, etc .
- Course outcomes should use verbs that match the level of cognitive skills that students are expected to achieve by the end of the course. For example, a course outcome that uses the verb "describe" implies that students will only need to remember or understand the information, while a course outcome that uses the verb "analyze" implies that students will need to apply or analyze the information .
- Course outcomes should also specify the content or context of the learning outcome, such as the topic, concept, skill, or situation that students will need to demonstrate their learning. For example, a course outcome that uses the verb "apply" should also indicate what knowledge or skill students will apply, and to what problem or task .
- Course outcomes should be measurable, observable, and achievable, meaning that they can be assessed using appropriate methods and tools, and that they are realistic and attainable for the students and the course .
- Course outcomes should be written from the perspective of the students, using phrases such as "students will be able to" or "by the end of the course, students will" to indicate the expected learning outcomes .
- Course outcomes should be reviewed and revised periodically to ensure that they are aligned with the course content, activities, and assessments, and that they reflect the current expectations and standards of the discipline and the institution .
