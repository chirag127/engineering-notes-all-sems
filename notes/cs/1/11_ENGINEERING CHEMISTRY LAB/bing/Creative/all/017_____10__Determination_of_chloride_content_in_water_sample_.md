# 10. Determination of chloride content in water sample

- Chloride ions are one of the major inorganic anions in water and wastewater. They can originate from natural sources such as seawater, salt deposits, and mineral springs, or from anthropogenic sources such as industrial effluents, agricultural runoff, and road deicing.
- Chloride ions can affect the taste, corrosion, and biological activity of water. Therefore, it is important to monitor and control the chloride content in water for various purposes such as drinking, irrigation, and industrial use.
- There are several methods for determining the chloride content in water, such as argentometric, potentiometric, colorimetric, and ion chromatographic methods. Among them, the argentometric method is the most common and simple one, which is based on the precipitation and titration of chloride ions with silver nitrate solution using a suitable indicator.
- The argentometric method can be classified into three types: Mohr's method, Volhard's method, and Fajans' method. Each method uses a different indicator and has a different pH range for the titration. In this experiment, we will use Mohr's method, which uses potassium chromate as the indicator and works best at a pH of 7-8.
- The principle of Mohr's method is as follows: When a water sample containing chloride ions is titrated with silver nitrate solution, the chloride ions are precipitated as white silver chloride according to the reaction:

  $$\text{Cl}^- + \text{Ag}^+ \rightarrow \text{AgCl} \downarrow$$

- The endpoint of the titration is detected by the formation of a reddish-brown precipitate of silver chromate, which indicates that all the chloride ions have been consumed and the excess silver ions have reacted with the chromate ions from the indicator according to the reaction:

  $$\text{2Ag}^+ + \text{CrO}_4^{2-} \rightarrow \text{Ag}_2\text{CrO}_4 \downarrow$$

- The amount of chloride ions in the water sample can be calculated from the volume and concentration of the silver nitrate solution used in the titration.

- The procedure for determining the chloride content in water by Mohr's method is as follows   :

  1. Take 50 mL of the water sample (V mL) and dilute it to 100 mL with distilled water in a volumetric flask. If the sample is highly colored, add 3 mL of aluminum hydroxide and shake well, allow to settle, filter, wash and collect the filtrate.
  2. Transfer 25 mL of the diluted sample to a conical flask and add a few drops of phenolphthalein indicator. If the solution turns pink, add 0.1 M hydrochloric acid dropwise until the color disappears. This is to adjust the pH of the sample to 7-8.
  3. Add 1 mL of 5% potassium chromate indicator to the sample and mix well. The solution should have a pale yellow color.
  4. Titrate the sample with 0.02 M silver nitrate solution from a burette until a reddish-brown precipitate of silver chromate is obtained. Note the final burette reading (V1 mL).
  5. Repeat the titration with two more aliquots of the diluted sample and record the average burette reading (V2 mL).
  6. Calculate the concentration of chloride ions in the water sample using the following formula:

    $$\text{Cl}^- \text{(mg/L)} = \frac{V_2 \times N \times 35.45 \times 1000}{V}$$

    where V2 is the average volume of silver nitrate solution used in mL, N is the normality of silver nitrate solution, 35.45 is the equivalent weight of chloride ion in g, and V is the volume of the water sample taken in mL.

  7. Report the result as mg/L of chloride ion in the water sample.
