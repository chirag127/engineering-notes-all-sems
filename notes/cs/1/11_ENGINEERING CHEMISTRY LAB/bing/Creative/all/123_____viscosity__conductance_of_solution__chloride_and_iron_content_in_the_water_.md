# Viscosity, Conductance of Solution, Chloride and Iron Content in the Water

## Viscosity
- Viscosity is a measure of the resistance of a fluid to deformation under shear stress.
- Viscosity depends on the intermolecular forces, temperature, pressure, and composition of the fluid.
- Viscosity of water decreases with increasing temperature and decreases with increasing salinity.
- Viscosity of water at 20°C is 1.002 mPa s.
- Viscosity of water at 100°C is 0.282 mPa s.
- Viscosity of seawater at 20°C is 1.08 mPa s.

## Conductance of Solution
- Conductance of solution is a measure of the ability of a solution to conduct electric current.
- Conductance of solution depends on the concentration, type, and mobility of the ions in the solution, as well as the temperature and pressure of the solution.
- Conductance of solution increases with increasing concentration and temperature of the solution.
- Conductance of solution is inversely proportional to the viscosity of the solution.
- Conductance of solution is measured in Siemens per meter (S/m) or microsiemens per centimeter (µS/cm).
- Conductance of pure water at 25°C is about 0.05 µS/cm.
- Conductance of drinking water at 25°C is in the range of 200–800 µS/cm.
- Conductance of seawater at 25°C is about 50 mS/cm (or 0.05 S/cm).

## Chloride and Iron Content in the Water
- Chloride and iron are two common dissolved solids in water that affect its quality and properties.
- Chloride is a negatively charged ion (Cl-) that comes from natural sources such as seawater, rocks, and volcanic activity, or from human activities such as agriculture, industry, and wastewater.
- Chloride increases the salinity, conductivity, and corrosivity of water, and can affect the taste and odor of water.
- Chloride can also interfere with the disinfection process and cause the formation of harmful byproducts such as chloramines and trihalomethanes.
- Chloride concentration in water is measured in milligrams per liter (mg/L) or parts per million (ppm).
- Chloride concentration in freshwater is usually less than 250 mg/L, while in seawater it is about 19,000 mg/L.
- Chloride concentration in drinking water is regulated by the Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) to be less than 250 mg/L for aesthetic reasons.
- Iron is a positively charged ion (Fe2+ or Fe3+) that comes from natural sources such as soil, rocks, and groundwater, or from human activities such as mining, industry, and corrosion of pipes and fittings.
- Iron increases the hardness, color, and turbidity of water, and can cause staining of plumbing fixtures, laundry, and dishes.
- Iron can also affect the taste and odor of water, and promote the growth of iron bacteria that can clog pipes and filters.
- Iron concentration in water is measured in milligrams per liter (mg/L) or parts per million (ppm).
- Iron concentration in freshwater is usually less than 0.3 mg/L, while in seawater it is about 0.01 mg/L.
- Iron concentration in drinking water is regulated by the Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) to be less than 0.3 mg/L for aesthetic reasons.
