# Determination of Hardness of Water by EDTA Method

- Hardness of water is a measure of the concentration of calcium and magnesium ions in water.
- EDTA (ethylenediaminetetraacetic acid) is a chelating agent that can form stable complexes with metal ions, such as Ca2+ and Mg2+, in a 1:1 ratio.
- Eriochrome Black T is an indicator that changes color from wine red to blue when it binds to free metal ions in water.
- The principle of the EDTA method is to titrate a water sample with a standard EDTA solution until all the metal ions are complexed and the indicator changes color.
- The procedure for the EDTA method is as follows   :

  1. Take a sample volume of 20 ml (V ml) and transfer it to an Erlenmeyer flask.
  2. Dilute the sample to 40 ml by adding 20 ml of distilled water.
  3. Add 1 ml of ammonia buffer to adjust the pH to 10 ± 0.1.
  4. Add 1 or 2 drops of Eriochrome Black T indicator solution.
  5. Titrate the sample with a standard EDTA solution of known concentration (C mg/ml) until the wine red color just turns blue.
  6. Record the volume of EDTA solution used (T ml).
  7. Repeat the titration with a blank (distilled water) and a standard (hard water of known hardness) to check the accuracy and precision of the method.

- The calculation of the hardness of water by the EDTA method is based on the following formula   :

  - Hardness (mg/L as CaCO3) = (C × T × 1000) / V
  - Where C is the concentration of EDTA solution in mg/ml, T is the volume of EDTA solution used in ml, and V is the volume of water sample in ml.
  - The hardness can be reported as total hardness or calcium hardness, depending on the type of metal ions present in the water sample.
