# Course Outcomes Bloom's Taxonomy

- Course outcomes are brief statements that describe what students will be expected to learn by the end of the course.
- Course outcomes should be aligned with the course content, activities, and assessments, and should reflect the level of cognitive skills that students need to demonstrate.
- Bloom's taxonomy is a model of cognitive skills that can be used to classify educational learning objectives and to design course outcomes.
- Bloom's taxonomy consists of six levels of cognition, from lower-order to higher-order: remember, understand, apply, analyze, evaluate, and create.
- Each level of cognition has a set of verbs that can be used to write course outcomes that indicate the expected level of student performance.
- For example, at the remember level, verbs such as define, list, recall, and identify can be used to write course outcomes that require students to recall factual information.
- At the create level, verbs such as design, construct, produce, and synthesize can be used to write course outcomes that require students to generate new ideas or products.
- Course outcomes should be specific, measurable, achievable, relevant, and time-bound (SMART) to ensure clarity and accountability.
- Course outcomes should also be aligned with the program outcomes and the institutional mission and vision.
- Course outcomes should be communicated to students at the beginning of the course and throughout the course to help them monitor their progress and self-regulate their learning.
