# Determination of chloride content in water sample

- Chloride is one of the major anions found in natural waters and wastewaters. It originates from various sources such as seawater intrusion, industrial effluents, domestic sewage, road salt, fertilizers, etc.
- High concentrations of chloride can have adverse effects on aquatic life, corrosion of pipes and fittings, taste and odour of drinking water, and salinity of soil and crops.
- Therefore, it is important to monitor and control the chloride content in water samples for environmental and health reasons.
- One of the common methods for determining the chloride content in water samples is the Mohr method, which is based on the precipitation and titration of chloride ions with silver nitrate solution using potassium chromate as an indicator.
- The principle of the Mohr method is as follows:

  - If water containing chlorides is titrated with silver nitrate solution, chlorides are precipitated as white silver chloride according to the reaction:

    `Cl- (aq) + Ag+ (aq) -> AgCl (s)`

  - Potassium chromate is used as an indicator, which supplies chromate ions that react with excess silver ions to form a reddish-brown precipitate of silver chromate according to the reaction:

    `CrO4 2- (aq) + 2Ag+ (aq) -> Ag2CrO4 (s)`

  - The end point of the titration is marked by the appearance of the first permanent reddish-brown precipitate, indicating that all the chloride ions have been precipitated and the silver nitrate solution is in excess.

- The procedure for the Mohr method is as follows  :

  - Take 50 mL of sample (V mL) and dilute to 100 mL with distilled water in a volumetric flask.
  - If the sample is highly coloured, add 3 mL of aluminium hydroxide and shake well, allow to settle, filter, wash and collect filtrate.
  - Bring the sample pH to 7-8 by adding acid or alkali if necessary. A pH meter or pH paper can be used to check the pH.
  - Add 1 mL of indicator (potassium chromate) to the sample in a conical flask.
  - Titrate the solution against standard silver nitrate solution (0.01 M or 0.02 M) from a burette until a reddish-brown precipitate is obtained. Record the volume of silver nitrate solution used (V1 mL).
  - Repeat the titration with two more aliquots of the sample and take the average volume of silver nitrate solution used (V2 mL).

- The calculation for the chloride content in water sample is as follows  :

  - The concentration of chloride ions in the sample (C mg/L) can be calculated from the following formula:

    `C = (V2 x N x 35.45 x 1000) / V`

    where V2 is the average volume of silver nitrate solution used (mL), N is the normality of silver nitrate solution, 35.45 is the equivalent weight of chloride ion, and V is the volume of sample taken (mL).

  - The percentage of chloride in the sample (% Cl) can be calculated from the following formula:

    `% Cl = (C x 100) / 1000`

    where C is the concentration of chloride ions in the sample (mg/L).

  - The results should be reported to the nearest 0.1 mg/L or 0.01 % as appropriate.
