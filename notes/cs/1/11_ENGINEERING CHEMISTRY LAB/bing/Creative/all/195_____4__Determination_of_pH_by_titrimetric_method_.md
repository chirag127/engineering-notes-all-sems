# 4. Determination of pH by titrimetric method

- Titrimetric method is a technique of quantitative analysis that involves measuring the volume of a solution of known concentration (titrant) that is required to react completely with a measured volume of a solution of unknown concentration (titrand) or a known mass of an analyte.
- pH is a measure of the acidity or alkalinity of a solution, defined as the negative logarithm of the hydrogen ion concentration: pH = -log[H+].
- Determination of pH by titrimetric method involves using a suitable indicator that changes color at a specific pH value (equivalence point) that corresponds to the stoichiometric point of the titration reaction.
- The indicator is added to the titrand solution and the titrant is added gradually from a burette until the color change occurs, indicating the end point of the titration.
- The volume of the titrant used is noted and the pH of the solution at the equivalence point is calculated using the titration equation and the known concentration of the titrant.
- The choice of the indicator depends on the type and strength of the acid and base involved in the titration. Some common indicators are phenolphthalein, methyl orange, bromothymol blue, and litmus.
- The titration curve is a plot of pH versus volume of titrant added, which shows the change in pH during the titration. The shape of the curve depends on the nature and strength of the acid and base and the indicator used.
- The titration method can be used to determine the pH of various solutions, such as vinegar, lemon juice, milk, soap, antacid, etc. by using appropriate titrants and indicators.
