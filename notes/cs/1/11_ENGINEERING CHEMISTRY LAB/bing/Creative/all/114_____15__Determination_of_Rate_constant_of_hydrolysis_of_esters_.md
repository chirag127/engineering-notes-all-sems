# 15. Determination of rate constant of hydrolysis of esters

- Hydrolysis of esters is the reaction of esters with water to produce carboxylic acids and alcohols.
- Hydrolysis of esters is catalyzed by either an acid or a base. Acidic hydrolysis is the reverse of esterification, while basic hydrolysis is also known as saponification.
- The rate of hydrolysis of esters depends on the nature of the ester, the concentration of the catalyst, the temperature, and the solvent.
- The rate law for the hydrolysis of esters can be written as:

  `rate = k[ester][H2O][H+]` for acidic hydrolysis

  `rate = k[ester][OH-]` for basic hydrolysis

  where k is the rate constant, and [ ] denotes the concentration of the species.
- The rate constant k can be determined experimentally by measuring the rate of the reaction under different conditions, such as varying the concentration of the catalyst, the ester, or the water, or changing the temperature or the solvent.
- One method to determine the rate constant of acid-catalyzed hydrolysis of an ester is based on the following steps:

  - Prepare a solution of the ester and a large excess of water containing a known concentration of a strong acid, such as hydrochloric acid, in a conical flask.
  - Heat the flask in a water bath at a constant temperature and shake it occasionally.
  - At regular intervals of time, withdraw a small sample of the reaction mixture and titrate it against a standard solution of sodium hydroxide, using phenolphthalein as an indicator.
  - Record the volume of sodium hydroxide required to reach the end point of the titration, and the corresponding time elapsed since the start of the reaction.
  - Repeat the procedure until the volume of sodium hydroxide becomes constant, indicating that the reaction has reached equilibrium.
  - Plot a graph of log((V∞ - V0) / (V∞ - Vt)) versus t, where V∞ is the final volume of sodium hydroxide at equilibrium, V0 is the initial volume of sodium hydroxide at the start of the reaction, and Vt is the volume of sodium hydroxide at time t.
  - The slope of the graph is equal to 2.303/k, where k is the rate constant of the reaction.
