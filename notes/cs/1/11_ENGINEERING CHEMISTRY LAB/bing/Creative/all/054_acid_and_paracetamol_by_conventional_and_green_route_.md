# Acid and Paracetamol by Conventional and Green Route

- Paracetamol is a common painkiller used to treat aches and pain. It can also be used to reduce fever. Its chemical name is N-acetyl-p-aminophenol (PAR) or acetaminophen.
- The conventional route for the synthesis of paracetamol involves the following steps :
  - Nitration of phenol with nitric acid to form p-nitrophenol.
  - Reduction of p-nitrophenol with tin and hydrochloric acid to form p-aminophenol.
  - Acetylation of p-aminophenol with acetic anhydride to form paracetamol.
- The conventional route has some drawbacks, such as:
  - Low yield of p-nitrophenol due to unwanted ortho-nitration and oxidation of phenol.
  - Generation of toxic and corrosive by-products, such as nitrous acid, tin chloride and acetic acid.
  - Use of large amounts of solvents, such as water, ethanol and ether, which increase the waste and energy consumption.
- The green route for the synthesis of paracetamol involves the following steps  :
  - Direct amidation of hydroquinone with acetic anhydride and ammonium acetate in a solvent-free microwave-assisted reaction to form paracetamol.
  - Alternatively, direct acetylation of p-aminophenol with acetic acid and sodium acetate in a solvent-free microwave-assisted reaction to form paracetamol.
- The green route has some advantages, such as:
  - High yield and selectivity of paracetamol due to the use of microwave irradiation, which enhances the reaction rate and reduces the side reactions.
  - Elimination of the nitration and reduction steps, which simplifies the process and reduces the environmental impact.
  - Avoidance of the use of solvents, which minimizes the waste and energy consumption.
