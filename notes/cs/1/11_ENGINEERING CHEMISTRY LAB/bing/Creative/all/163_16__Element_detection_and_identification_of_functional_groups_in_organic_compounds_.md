# Element detection and identification of functional groups in organic compounds

- Organic compounds are those that contain carbon atoms, usually bonded to hydrogen, oxygen, nitrogen, sulfur, or halogens.
- The structure and properties of organic compounds are largely determined by the functional groups, which are specific groups of atoms or bonds that have characteristic chemical behavior.
- Functional groups can be classified into different categories, such as alcohols, aldehydes, ketones, carboxylic acids, amines, etc.
- To detect and identify the elements and functional groups in organic compounds, various qualitative and quantitative methods can be used, such as:

## Element detection
- Carbon and hydrogen: These elements are present in almost all organic compounds. They can be detected by heating the compound with copper oxide in a test tube. The carbon will react with oxygen to form carbon dioxide, which can be tested with lime water. The hydrogen will react with oxygen to form water, which can be tested with anhydrous copper sulfate.
- Nitrogen: This element is present in many organic compounds, such as amines, amides, nitriles, etc. It can be detected by the Lassaigne's test, which involves heating the compound with sodium metal in a fusion tube. The sodium will react with nitrogen to form sodium cyanide, which can be extracted with water and tested with iron(II) sulfate and iron(III) chloride solutions. The formation of Prussian blue color indicates the presence of nitrogen.
- Halogens: These elements are present in many organic compounds, such as alkyl halides, aryl halides, etc. They can be detected by the Beilstein test, which involves heating the compound with a copper wire in a flame. The halogens will react with copper to form volatile copper halides, which impart a characteristic color to the flame. Chlorine gives a green flame, bromine gives a brown flame, and iodine gives a violet flame.
- Sulfur: This element is present in many organic compounds, such as thiols, sulfides, sulfones, etc. It can be detected by the sodium fusion test, similar to the nitrogen detection. The sodium will react with sulfur to form sodium sulfide, which can be extracted with water and tested with lead acetate solution. The formation of a black precipitate of lead sulfide indicates the presence of sulfur.
- Oxygen: This element is present in many organic compounds, such as alcohols, ethers, aldehydes, ketones, carboxylic acids, etc. It can be detected by the sodium fusion test, similar to the nitrogen and sulfur detection. The sodium will react with oxygen to form sodium oxide, which can be extracted with water and tested with phenolphthalein indicator. The formation of a pink color indicates the presence of oxygen.

## Functional group identification
- Alcoholic -OH group: This functional group is present in compounds such as alcohols, phenols, etc. It can be detected by the following tests:
  - Lucas test: This test involves adding a few drops of the compound to a solution of zinc chloride in concentrated hydrochloric acid. The formation of a turbid or cloudy solution indicates the presence of an alcoholic -OH group. The rate of turbidity depends on the type of alcohol. Primary alcohols give turbidity after several minutes, secondary alcohols give turbidity within a few minutes, and tertiary alcohols give turbidity immediately.
  - Esterification test: This test involves heating the compound with acetic acid and a few drops of concentrated sulfuric acid. The formation of a fruity smell indicates the presence of an alcoholic -OH group. The smell is due to the formation of an ester, which is a compound with the general formula RCOOR'.
