# Determination of Available Chlorine in Bleaching Powder

- Bleaching powder is a white powder that contains calcium hypochlorite (Ca(OCl)2) and calcium chloride (CaCl2) as the main components.
- Bleaching powder is used as a disinfectant, bleaching agent, and deodorizer in various industries and households.
- The effectiveness of bleaching powder depends on the amount of chlorine that can be released when it reacts with water or acids. This is called the available chlorine.
- The available chlorine in bleaching powder can be determined by the iodometric method, which involves the following steps:

  - A known mass of bleaching powder is dissolved in water and filtered to obtain a clear solution.
  - A measured volume of the solution is taken in a conical flask and an excess of potassium iodide (KI) solution is added. This causes the available chlorine to oxidize the iodide ions to iodine (I2) according to the equation:

    Ca(OCl)2 + 4KI + 2HCl -> CaCl2 + 2KCl + 2I2 + 2H2O

  - The liberated iodine is then titrated with a standard solution of sodium thiosulfate (Na2S2O3) using starch as an indicator. The starch forms a blue complex with iodine, which disappears at the end point. The equation for the titration is:

    I2 + 2Na2S2O3 -> 2NaI + Na2S4O6

  - The volume of sodium thiosulfate used is noted and the percentage of available chlorine in the bleaching powder is calculated using the formula:

    % available chlorine = (V x N x 35.5 x 100) / (W x 1000)

    where V is the volume of sodium thiosulfate in mL, N is the normality of sodium thiosulfate, W is the mass of bleaching powder in g, and 35.5 is the equivalent mass of chlorine.
