# 2. Determination of Hardness of water sample by EDTA method

Hardness of water is a measure of the concentration of calcium and magnesium ions in water. These ions can form insoluble deposits in pipes, boilers, and other appliances that use water. Hardness of water can be determined by titrating a water sample with a standard solution of ethylenediaminetetraacetic acid (EDTA), which forms stable complexes with calcium and magnesium ions. The end point of the titration is detected by using an indicator that changes color when all the calcium and magnesium ions have been complexed by EDTA.

The procedure for determining the hardness of water by EDTA method is as follows   :

- Take a sample volume of 20 mL (V mL) of water in a clean Erlenmeyer flask.
- Dilute the sample to 40 mL by adding 20 mL of distilled water.
- Add 1 mL of ammonia buffer to adjust the pH to 10 ± 0.1. The buffer prevents the formation of insoluble hydroxides of calcium and magnesium.
- Add 1 or 2 drops of Eriochrome Black T indicator, which forms a wine-red complex with calcium and magnesium ions in the water sample.
- Titrate the sample with a standard solution of EDTA of known concentration (C M) from a burette, with constant stirring, until the color changes from wine-red to blue. This indicates that all the calcium and magnesium ions have been complexed by EDTA. Record the volume of EDTA used as V E mL.
- Repeat the titration with two more samples of water and take the average of the three readings of V E.

The hardness of water can be calculated by using the following formula  :

Hardness of water (mg/L as CaCO3) = (V E x C M x 1000) / V

where V E is the volume of EDTA used in mL, C M is the concentration of EDTA in mg CaCO3 per mL, and V is the volume of water sample in mL.

The hardness of water can be expressed in different units, such as grains per gallon (gpg), parts per million (ppm), or milligrams per liter (mg/L). One grain per gallon is equivalent to 17.1 ppm or 17.1 mg/L of hardness as CaCO3. The hardness of water can be classified as follows:

- Soft water: 0 - 60 mg/L as CaCO3
- Moderately hard water: 61 - 120 mg/L as CaCO3
- Hard water: 121 - 180 mg/L as CaCO3
- Very hard water: > 180 mg/L as CaCO3
