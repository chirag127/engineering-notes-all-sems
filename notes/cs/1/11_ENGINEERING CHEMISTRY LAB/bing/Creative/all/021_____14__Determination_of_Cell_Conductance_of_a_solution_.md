# 14. Determination of Cell Conductance of a Solution

- Cell conductance of a solution is the measure of how well the solution can conduct electric current. It is the reciprocal of the resistance of the solution.
- Cell conductance of a solution depends on the concentration, charge, and mobility of the ions present in the solution.
- To measure the cell conductance of a solution, a special device called a conductance cell is used. A conductance cell consists of two electrodes of known area and distance, immersed in the solution.
- The resistance of the solution between the electrodes is measured by using a Wheatstone bridge or a conductivity meter. The resistance is inversely proportional to the conductance of the solution.
- The cell conductance of a solution is also related to the cell constant, which is the ratio of the distance between the electrodes to the area of the electrodes. The cell constant is a characteristic of the conductance cell and can be determined by using a solution of known conductance, such as potassium chloride.
- The cell conductance of a solution can be calculated by using the following formula:

  Cell conductance = Cell constant / Resistance

- The cell conductance of a solution is expressed in units of siemens per meter (S/m) or mho per meter (mho/m). The cell conductance of a solution is also called the conductivity of the solution.
