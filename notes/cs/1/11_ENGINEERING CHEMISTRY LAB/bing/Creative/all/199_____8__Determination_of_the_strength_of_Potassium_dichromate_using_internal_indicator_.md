# 8. Determination of the strength of Potassium dichromate using internal indicator.

- Potassium dichromate is a primary standard substance that can be used to determine the strength of a reducing agent such as ferrous ammonium sulphate.
- The reaction between potassium dichromate and ferrous ammonium sulphate is given by:

  $$K_2Cr_2O_7 + 6FeSO_4 + 7H_2SO_4 \rightarrow Cr_2(SO_4)_3 + 3K_2SO_4 + 6Fe_2(SO_4)_3 + 7H_2O$$

- The end point of this titration can be detected by using an internal indicator, such as diphenylamine sulphonic acid or barium diphenylamine sulphonate, which changes color from colorless to blue or violet when the ferrous ions are completely oxidized to ferric ions by the dichromate ions.
- The procedure for the determination of the strength of potassium dichromate using internal indicator is as follows:

  - Weigh accurately about 0.5 g of potassium dichromate and dissolve it in 100 mL of distilled water in a volumetric flask. This is the standard solution of potassium dichromate.
  - Pipette out 10 mL of the standard solution into a conical flask and add 10 mL of dilute sulphuric acid and a few drops of the internal indicator.
  - Titrate the solution with the given ferrous ammonium sulphate solution from a burette until the color changes from colorless to blue or violet. Note the burette reading. Repeat the titration for concordant readings.
  - Calculate the strength of the ferrous ammonium sulphate solution using the formula:

    $$\frac{W_1 \times V_1 \times N_1}{W_2 \times V_2 \times N_2} = 1$$

    where W_1 and W_2 are the weights of potassium dichromate and ferrous ammonium sulphate respectively, V_1 and V_2 are the volumes of potassium dichromate and ferrous ammonium sulphate respectively, and N_1 and N_2 are the normalities of potassium dichromate and ferrous ammonium sulphate respectively.
