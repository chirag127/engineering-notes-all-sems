# 8. Determination of the strength of Potassium dichromate using internal indicator.

- Potassium dichromate is a primary standard substance that can be used to determine the strength of a solution of an oxidizing or reducing agent by titration.
- The internal indicator is the color change of the solution due to the presence of chromium ions, which change from orange to green as they are reduced by the titrant.
- The procedure is as follows:

  - Prepare a standard solution of potassium dichromate by dissolving a known mass of the salt in distilled water and making up the volume to a known mark in a volumetric flask.
  - Pipette out a known volume of the potassium dichromate solution into a conical flask and add a few drops of dilute sulfuric acid to acidify the solution.
  - Titrate the solution with the unknown solution of the oxidizing or reducing agent using a burette until the color change from orange to green is observed. Note the final burette reading.
  - Repeat the titration until concordant results are obtained. Calculate the average titre value.
  - Use the formula M1V1 = M2V2 to calculate the strength of the unknown solution, where M1 and M2 are the molarities and V1 and V2 are the volumes of the potassium dichromate and the unknown solution, respectively.
