# 2. Determination of Hardness of water sample by EDTA method

- Hardness of water is the measure of the concentration of calcium and magnesium ions in water.
- EDTA (Ethylenediaminetetraacetic acid) is a chelating agent that can form stable complexes with metal ions, such as Ca2+ and Mg2+.
- EDTA titration is a method of determining the hardness of water by titrating a water sample with a standard solution of EDTA in the presence of a suitable indicator.
- The indicator used for EDTA titration is Eriochrome Black T, which is a metallochromic dye that changes color depending on the metal ion it binds to.
- Eriochrome Black T forms a wine-red complex with Ca2+ and Mg2+ ions at pH 10, but turns blue when these ions are replaced by EDTA.
- The end point of the titration is the color change from wine-red to blue, indicating that all the Ca2+ and Mg2+ ions in the water sample have been complexed by EDTA.
- The hardness of water can be calculated from the volume of EDTA used and its concentration, which is expressed in terms of mg of CaCO3 equivalent per mL of EDTA.

## Procedure for determination of hardness of water by EDTA titration   

1. Take a sample volume of 20 mL (V mL) of water in a clean Erlenmeyer flask.
2. Dilute the sample to 40 mL by adding 20 mL of distilled water.
3. Add 1 mL of ammonia buffer to bring the pH to 10±0.1. The buffer consists of a mixture of ammonium chloride and ammonia solution.
4. Add 1 or 2 drops of Eriochrome Black T indicator solution. The solution should turn wine-red due to the formation of metal-indicator complexes.
5. Titrate the sample with a standard solution of EDTA of known concentration (C mg/mL) from a burette, with constant stirring, until the color changes from wine-red to blue. Note the final burette reading (B mL).
6. Repeat the titration with two more samples and take the average of the three readings.

## Calculation of hardness of water by EDTA titration    

- The hardness of water (H mg/L) can be calculated from the following formula:

H = (C × B × 1000) / V

where C is the concentration of EDTA in mg of CaCO3 equivalent per mL, B is the average volume of EDTA used in mL, and V is the volume of water sample taken in mL.

- The hardness of water can also be expressed in terms of degrees of hardness, which are defined as follows:

1 degree Clark = 14.3 mg/L of CaCO3 equivalent
1 degree French = 10 mg/L of CaCO3 equivalent
1 degree German = 17.8 mg/L of CaCO3 equivalent

- To convert the hardness of water from mg/L to degrees, divide the value in mg/L by the corresponding factor for the desired unit. For example, to convert 200 mg/L to degrees Clark, divide 200 by 14.3, which gives 13.99 degrees Clark.
