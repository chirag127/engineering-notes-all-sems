# CO-3 Measure the hardness and alkalinity of the water. K3

- Hardness of water is the measure of the amount of calcium and magnesium salts dissolved in it. It is expressed in terms of equivalent calcium carbonate (CaCO3) concentration in mg/L or ppm.
- Alkalinity of water is the measure of the capacity of water to neutralize acids. It is mainly due to the presence of bicarbonates, carbonates and hydroxides of calcium, magnesium and other metals. It is expressed in terms of equivalent calcium carbonate (CaCO3) concentration in mg/L or ppm.
- Hardness and alkalinity of water are important parameters for water quality assessment, as they affect the suitability of water for domestic, industrial and agricultural purposes.
- Hardness and alkalinity of water can be measured by titration methods using standard solutions of acids and bases, and suitable indicators.
- The common methods for measuring hardness and alkalinity of water are:
  - EDTA method for total hardness and calcium hardness
  - Soap method for total hardness
  - Phenolphthalein and methyl orange methods for total alkalinity and phenolphthalein alkalinity
  - Borax method for total alkalinity
  - Hydroxylamine hydrochloride method for carbonate and bicarbonate alkalinity
  - Potentiometric method for total alkalinity and carbonate alkalinity
- The steps involved in each method are:
  - EDTA method: A known volume of water sample is taken in a conical flask and a few drops of Eriochrome Black T indicator are added. The solution turns wine red due to the formation of a complex between the indicator and the metal ions. The solution is then titrated with a standard solution of ethylenediaminetetraacetic acid (EDTA) until the end point is reached, indicated by a color change from wine red to blue. The volume of EDTA used is noted and the total hardness is calculated. For calcium hardness, the same procedure is repeated after adding a buffer solution of ammonia and ammonium chloride, which masks the magnesium ions. The difference between the total hardness and the calcium hardness gives the magnesium hardness.
  - Soap method: A known volume of water sample is taken in a stoppered bottle and a few drops of phenolphthalein indicator are added. If the solution turns pink, it indicates the presence of alkalinity and a few drops of sulfuric acid are added to neutralize it. Then, a standard solution of soap is added drop by drop with shaking until a lather is formed that persists for at least 5 minutes. The volume of soap solution used is noted and the total hardness is calculated.
  - Phenolphthalein and methyl orange methods: A known volume of water sample is taken in a conical flask and a few drops of phenolphthalein indicator are added. If the solution turns pink, it indicates the presence of phenolphthalein alkalinity, which is due to the hydroxides and half of the carbonates. The solution is then titrated with a standard solution of sulfuric acid until the end point is reached, indicated by a color change from pink to colorless. The volume of acid used is noted and the phenolphthalein alkalinity is calculated. Then, a few drops of methyl orange indicator are added to the same solution, which turns yellow. The solution is further titrated with the same acid until the end point is reached, indicated by a color change from yellow to orange. The volume of acid used is noted and the total alkalinity is calculated. The difference between the total alkalinity and the phenolphthalein alkalinity gives the methyl orange alkalinity, which is due to the bicarbonates and the remaining carbonates.
  - Borax method: A known volume of water sample is taken in a conical flask and a few drops of phenolphthalein indicator are added. If the solution turns pink, it indicates the presence of alkalinity and a few drops of sulfuric acid are added to neutralize it. Then, a standard solution of sodium tetraborate (borax) is added with constant stirring until the end point is reached, indicated by a color change from colorless to pink. The volume of borax used is noted and the total alkalinity is calculated.
  - Hydroxylamine hydrochloride method: A known volume of water sample is taken in a conical flask and a few drops of phenolphthalein indicator are added.
