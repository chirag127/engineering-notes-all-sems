# 6. Determination of Viscosity of a given liquid by viscometer

- Viscosity is a measure of the resistance of a fluid to flow or deform under an applied force.
- Viscosity depends on the intermolecular forces, temperature and pressure of the fluid.
- A viscometer is a device that measures the viscosity of a fluid by observing its flow through a narrow tube or a capillary.
- The flow rate of the fluid depends on the pressure difference across the tube, the length and radius of the tube, and the viscosity of the fluid.
- The most common type of viscometer is the Ostwald viscometer, which consists of a U-shaped glass tube with two bulbs and a constant diameter capillary connecting them.
- The fluid is filled in the lower bulb and then sucked up to a higher level in the upper bulb using a suction pump or a rubber bulb.
- The fluid is then allowed to flow down through the capillary under the influence of gravity, and the time taken for the fluid to flow between two marked points on the tube is measured.
- The viscosity of the fluid can be calculated using the following formula:

$$\eta = \frac{\pi r^4 \rho g (t_1 - t_2)}{8 l (V_1 - V_2)}$$

where $\eta$ is the viscosity, $r$ is the radius of the capillary, $\rho$ is the density of the fluid, $g$ is the acceleration due to gravity, $t_1$ and $t_2$ are the times taken for the fluid to flow between the two marked points, $l$ is the length of the capillary, and $V_1$ and $V_2$ are the volumes of the two bulbs.

- The viscosity of the fluid can also be expressed in terms of the relative viscosity, which is the ratio of the viscosity of the fluid to the viscosity of a reference fluid, usually water at the same temperature.
- The relative viscosity can be calculated using the following formula:

$$\frac{\eta}{\eta_0} = \frac{t}{t_0}$$

where $\eta_0$ and $t_0$ are the viscosity and the time taken for the reference fluid to flow through the viscometer, respectively.

- The relative viscosity can also be converted to the specific viscosity, which is the difference between the relative viscosity and 1, or the percentage viscosity, which is the specific viscosity multiplied by 100.
- The specific viscosity and the percentage viscosity can be calculated using the following formulas:

$$\eta_s = \frac{\eta}{\eta_0} - 1$$

$$\eta_p = 100 \eta_s$$

- The viscosity of a fluid can also be affected by the presence of solutes or impurities in the fluid, which can increase or decrease the intermolecular forces and the flow resistance of the fluid.
- The effect of solutes or impurities on the viscosity of a fluid can be quantified by the viscosity coefficient, which is the ratio of the percentage viscosity of the solution to the percentage concentration of the solute or impurity.
- The viscosity coefficient can be calculated using the following formula:

$$k = \frac{\eta_p}{c}$$

where $c$ is the percentage concentration of the solute or impurity.

- The viscosity coefficient can be used to compare the effect of different solutes or impurities on the viscosity of a fluid, or to determine the concentration of an unknown solute or impurity in a fluid by measuring its viscosity.
