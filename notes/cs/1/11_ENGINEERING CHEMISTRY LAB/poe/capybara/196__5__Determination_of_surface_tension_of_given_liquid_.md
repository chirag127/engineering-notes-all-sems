### 5. Determination of surface tension of given liquid.

Surface tension is the property of a liquid that allows it to resist an external force. It is defined as the force per unit length acting perpendicular to an imaginary line drawn on the surface of the liquid. The surface tension of a liquid depends on various factors such as temperature, pressure, and the nature of the liquid.

The determination of surface tension of a given liquid can be done through the following methods:

1. Capillary rise method: In this method, a capillary tube is immersed in the liquid and the rise of the liquid in the tube is observed. The height of the rise is directly proportional to the surface tension of the liquid.

2. Drop weight method: In this method, a drop of the liquid is allowed to fall from a certain height and the time taken by the drop to fall a certain distance is measured. The surface tension of the liquid can be calculated using the formula T = 4πr^2g/3h, where T is the surface tension, r is the radius of the drop, g is the acceleration due to gravity, and h is the height of the fall.

3. Drop volume method: In this method, a drop of known volume is allowed to fall from a certain height and the diameter of the drop is measured. The surface tension of the liquid can be calculated using the formula T = 2ΔpD/3r, where T is the surface tension, Δp is the pressure difference across the drop, D is the diameter of the drop, and r is the radius of the capillary.

4. Maximum bubble pressure method: In this method, a soap film is formed between two rings and the pressure required to burst the film is measured. The surface tension of the liquid can be calculated using the formula T = 4ΔpR/3, where T is the surface tension, Δp is the pressure difference across the film, and R is the radius of the ring.

In conclusion, the determination of surface tension of a given liquid can be done through various methods such as capillary rise method, drop weight method, drop volume method, and maximum bubble pressure method. Each method has its own advantages and disadvantages and the choice of method depends on the nature of the liquid and the accuracy required.
