
### CO-3 Measure the hardness and alkalinity of the water. K3

1. Hardness of water is a measure of the total amount of dissolved minerals, primarily calcium and magnesium, in the water. 
2. Alkalinity is a measure of the capacity of water to resist changes in pH. 
3. Hardness is commonly measured in milligrams per liter (mg/L) or parts per million (ppm) of calcium carbonate (CaCO3). 
4. Alkalinity is usually measured in milligrams per liter (mg/L) of calcium carbonate (CaCO3). 
5. Hardness can be determined by titration with a standard EDTA solution. 
6. Alkalinity can be determined by titration with a standard acid solution. 
7. Hardness of water is important because it affects the taste and can cause scale buildup in pipes and water-using appliances. 
8. Alkalinity of water is important because it helps to buffer pH changes and protect aquatic life.
