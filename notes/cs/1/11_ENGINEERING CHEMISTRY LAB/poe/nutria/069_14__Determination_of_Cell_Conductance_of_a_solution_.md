
# 14. Determination of Cell Conductance of a Solution

1. Cell conductance is a measure of the ability of a solution to conduct electrical current.
2. It is the reciprocal of the resistance of the solution, which is the opposition to the flow of electric current.
3. Cell conductance is usually measured in siemens (S) or millisiemens (mS).
4. Cell conductance can be determined by measuring the voltage across a known resistance in the solution.
5. The cell conductance of a solution is affected by factors such as temperature, concentration, and the presence of ions in the solution.
6. The cell conductance of a solution can be used to calculate the equivalent conductance of the ions in the solution.
7. The equivalent conductance of the ions in a solution is the sum of the individual ionic conductances of each ion present in the solution.
8. The total cell conductance of a solution is the sum of the individual ionic conductances, plus the contribution of the solvent.
