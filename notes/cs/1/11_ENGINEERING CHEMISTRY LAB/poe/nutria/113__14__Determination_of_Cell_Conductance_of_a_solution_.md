

### 14. Determination of Cell Conductance of a Solution

1. Cell conductance is a measure of the ability of an electrolyte solution to conduct electricity. 
2. It is expressed as the reciprocal of the resistance of a solution to the flow of electricity, usually expressed in siemens per meter (S/m).
3. Cell conductance is determined by measuring the current passing through a solution of known volume and concentration.
4. The conductance of a solution is affected by the number of ions present in the solution, as well as the mobility of the ions.
5. The conductance of a solution can also be affected by temperature, pressure, and the concentration of other substances in the solution.
6. It is important to note that the cell conductance of a solution is not constant, and can vary with time.
7. The cell conductance of a solution can be used to measure the concentration of ions in a solution, as well as the rate of ion movement.
8. It can also be used to determine the rate of diffusion of ions in a solution.
