
### 4. Determination of pH by Titrimetric Method

- Titrimetric methods involve the use of a titrant and an indicator to determine the end point of a titration.
- The titrant is a solution of known concentration, usually a base or an acid, which is added to a solution of unknown concentration.
- The indicator is a chemical that changes color when the solution reaches a certain pH.
- In titrimetric methods, the amount of titrant added is determined by measuring the pH of the solution at the end point of the titration.
- The end point of the titration is typically identified by the color change of the indicator.
- The most common type of titration used in the determination of pH is the acid-base titration.
- In acid-base titrations, the pH of the solution is determined by the amount of base that has been added to neutralize the acid.
- The end point of the titration is typically identified by the color change of the indicator.
- Another type of titration used in the determination of pH is the redox titration.
- In redox titrations, the amount of oxidizing or reducing agent added is determined by measuring the potential of the solution at the end point of the titration.
- The end point of the titration is typically identified by the color change of the indicator.
- The pH of the solution can also be determined by using a pH meter.
- The pH meter is a device that measures the electrical potential of a solution and converts it into a pH value.
- The pH meter is a more accurate method of determining pH than titrimetric methods.
