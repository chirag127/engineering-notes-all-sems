
### 4. Determination of pH by titrimetric method

- Titrimetric method is a process of determining the concentration of a substance in a solution by adding a standard solution of known concentration.
- The process involves adding a standard solution of known concentration to the solution containing the unknown concentration of the substance.
- The pH of the solution is then determined by measuring the amount of standard solution required to reach the equivalence point.
- The equivalence point is the point where the number of moles of the titrant (standard solution) is equal to the number of moles of the analyte (unknown solution).
- The pH of the solution can be calculated by using the formula, pH = -log[H+].
- The titrimetric method is an accurate and reliable method for determining the pH of a solution. It can be used for both strong and weak acids and bases.
- The method is also useful for determining the pH of complex solutions such as buffers, where the pH can be affected by the presence of other ions.
