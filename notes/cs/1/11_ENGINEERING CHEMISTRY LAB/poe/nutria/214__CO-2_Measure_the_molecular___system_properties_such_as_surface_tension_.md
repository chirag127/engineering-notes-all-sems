
### CO-2 Measure the Molecular / System Properties such as Surface Tension

- Carbon dioxide (CO2) is an important component of the Earth's atmosphere, and its concentration affects many physical and chemical properties of the atmosphere.
- CO2 molecules are non-polar and have a linear structure, with two oxygen atoms on either side of the carbon atom.
- The surface tension of CO2 is determined by the molecules' ability to form hydrogen bonds with other molecules. 
- The surface tension of CO2 is affected by temperature, pressure, and the presence of other molecules in the system.
- At standard temperature and pressure (STP), the surface tension of CO2 is 32.1 mN/m.
- At higher temperatures, the surface tension of CO2 decreases, as the molecules become more mobile and more likely to break hydrogen bonds.
- At lower temperatures, the surface tension of CO2 increases, as the molecules become less mobile and more likely to form hydrogen bonds.
- The surface tension of CO2 is also affected by the presence of other molecules in the system. For example, the presence of water molecules in the system can increase the surface tension of CO2.
