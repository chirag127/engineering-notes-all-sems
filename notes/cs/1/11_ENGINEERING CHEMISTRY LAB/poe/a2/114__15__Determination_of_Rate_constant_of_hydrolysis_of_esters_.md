### 15. Determination of Rate constant of hydrolysis of esters.

1. Esters hydrolyse in aqueous solutions to give carboxylic acids and alcohols. The rate of hydrolysis can be determined by measuring the amount of ester reacted at different time intervals.
2. The rate of reaction is directly proportional to the concentration of ester. At constant temperature, the rate of reaction is given by Rate = k [Ester], where k is the rate constant.
3. To determine k, measurements are made of ester concentration, [Ester] at regular time intervals (say t1, t2, t3....tn). The integrated rate law is applied:
    1/[Ester] = 1/[Ester]0 + k.t
    By plotting 1/[Ester] vs t, k can be determined from the slope of the graph.
4. The effect of temperature, pH and catalysts on the rate of hydrolysis can also be studied to get more information about the reaction mechanism. An increase in temperature, pH and addition of acid/base catalyst increases the rate of hydrolysis.
5. The value of k can be used to compare the relative reactivity of different esters towards hydrolysis. tertiary esters are most reactive followed by secondary and primary esters.
