# CO2: Measure the molecular / system properties such as surface tension

## Surface Tension
- Surface tension refers to the tendency of liquid surfaces to shrink into the minimum surface area possible.
- It is caused by cohesion between molecules in the liquid.
- Surface tension is measured in units of force per unit length (N/m).
- For CO2, the surface tension is 0.037 N/m at 0°C and 1 atm pressure.
- Surface tension decreases with increasing temperature as intermolecular forces weaken.

## Other Properties
- Density: Liquid CO2 has a density of 0.976 g/mL at 20°C and 1 atm. Density decreases with increasing temperature.
- Viscosity: The viscosity of liquid CO2 is 0.077 cP at 20°C and 1 atm. Viscosity decreases with increasing temperature as molecular motion increases.
- Dipole Moment: CO2 is nonpolar and has a dipole moment of 0 Debye. It has no permanent charge separation.
- Critical Point: CO2 reaches a critical point at 31°C and 73 atm, above which it cannot be liquefied.
