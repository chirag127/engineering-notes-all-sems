# Course Outcomes (Bloom's)

1. Remembering: Recall or recognize terms, facts, techniques, theories, principles, etc.
- Students will be able to list the levels of Bloom's taxonomy.
- Students will be able to recall the purpose of each level of Bloom's taxonomy.

2. Understanding: Grasp the meaning of information, explain ideas or concepts, translate knowledge into own words.
- Students will be able to explain the meaning and purpose of each level of Bloom's taxonomy in their own words.
- Students will be able to provide examples of learning objectives and assessments targeting each level of Bloom's taxonomy.

3. Applying: Use information, procedures, methods, techniques, etc. in new situations.
- Students will be able to write learning objectives and assessment questions targeting specific levels of Bloom's taxonomy.
- Students will be able to categorize given learning objectives or assessment questions into the appropriate levels of Bloom's taxonomy.

4. Analyzing: Break down information into constituent parts, examine and try to understand the organizational structure of information.
- Students will be able to analyze learning objectives and assessments to determine how well they reflect the levels of Bloom's taxonomy.
- Students will be able to evaluate the distribution of learning objectives and assessments across the levels of Bloom's taxonomy in a course.

5. Evaluating: Make judgments based on internal evidence or externally supplied criteria.
- Students will be able to evaluate the effectiveness of learning objectives and assessments targeting each level of Bloom's taxonomy.
- Students will be able to justify evaluations made of the learning objectives and assessments.

6. Creating: Put elements together in a new pattern or structure, make something new.
- Students will be able to write learning objectives and assessment questions that require higher-order thinking skills (analyzing, evaluating, creating).
- Students will be able to create a lesson or short course with learning objectives and assessments targeted at specific levels of Bloom's taxonomy.
