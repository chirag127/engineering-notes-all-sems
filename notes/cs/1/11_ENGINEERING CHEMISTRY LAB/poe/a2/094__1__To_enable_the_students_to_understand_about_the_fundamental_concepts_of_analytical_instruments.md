### 1. To enable the students to understand about the fundamental concepts of analytical instruments.

1. Analytical instruments are tools used to analyze the chemical composition of a sample. Some key analytical instruments are:
- Spectrophotometers: Used to measure how much light is absorbed by a sample at specific wavelengths. This can reveal information about the sample's chemical composition.
- Chromatography instruments: Used to separate mixtures into their individual components. The separated components can then be analyzed to identify the chemicals present.
- Mass spectrometers: Used to measure the mass-to-charge ratio of ions to identify the chemical structure of a compound.
- NMR spectrometers: Used to analyze the magnetic properties of atomic nuclei in a sample to determine molecular structure.

2. These instruments work by detecting and measuring physical or chemical properties of a sample. Based on the specific property being measured and how it's measured, we can then draw conclusions about the sample's chemical composition. A deep understanding of how these instruments work is crucial for proper use and interpretation of results.

3. Key things to understand about analytical instruments are:
- What property of the sample is being measured (e.g. light absorbance, mass-to-charge ratio of ions, nuclear magnetic resonance)
- How the property is being measured (e.g. using light of specific wavelengths, magnetic fields, electric fields)
- How the measurement results can be interpreted to determine the sample's composition
- Errors and limitations of the analytical approach
- Proper setup, calibration, and operation of the instrument to get accurate results
