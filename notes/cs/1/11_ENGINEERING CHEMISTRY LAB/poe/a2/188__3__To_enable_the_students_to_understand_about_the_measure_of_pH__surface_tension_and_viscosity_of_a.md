### 3. To enable the students to understand about the measure of pH, surface tension and viscosity of a liquid.

1. pH: pH is a measure of how acidic/basic a liquid is. It is measured on a scale of 0 to 14 with 0 being highly acidic, 7 being neutral and 14 being highly basic. The pH can be measured using a pH meter or pH paper which changes color depending on the pH. Lower pH indicates higher H+ ion concentration and higher pH indicates higher OH- ion concentration.

2. Surface Tension: Surface tension is a measure of how difficult it is to stretch the surface of a liquid to increase its surface area. Liquids with high surface tension have a strong cohesive force between molecules. Surface tension can be measured using a du Nouy ring or a drop weight method. High surface tension liquids like water form droplets to minimize surface area. Low surface tension liquids spread out and wet surfaces easily.

3. Viscosity: Viscosity refers to the resistance of a liquid to flow. Liquids with high viscosity are thick and flow slowly while liquids with low viscosity are thin and flow fast. Viscosity can be measured using devices like viscometers which apply shear stress to the liquid and measure its flow rate. Highly viscous liquids like honey have stronger intermolecular forces between molecules resulting in slower flow. Low viscous liquids like alcohol have weaker intermolecular forces allowing faster flow.

The properties of pH, surface tension and viscosity are useful to understand the behavior and applications of different liquids. They can be used to determine the suitability of a liquid for a particular purpose.
