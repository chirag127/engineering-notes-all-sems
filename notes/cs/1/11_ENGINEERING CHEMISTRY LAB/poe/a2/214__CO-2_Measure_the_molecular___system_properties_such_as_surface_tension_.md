### CO-2 Measure the molecular / system properties such as surface tension,

1. Surface tension: Surface tension is a property of the surface of a liquid that causes it to behave as an elastic sheet. It arises due to cohesion of similar molecules. Surfactants reduce surface tension and are used in detergents to enable clothes washing.

2. Viscosity: Viscosity is a measure of a fluid's resistance to flow. It depends on intermolecular forces between molecules and affects fluid flow. High viscosity indicates a thick liquid while low viscosity indicates a thin liquid.

3. Density: Density is a measure of how tightly packed the molecules are in a substance. It is calculated as mass per unit volume and affects whether a substance floats or sinks in another liquid or gas. Higher density means molecules are more tightly packed.

4. Boiling and melting points: The boiling point is the temperature at which a liquid changes to a gas while the melting point is the temperature at which a solid changes to a liquid. These depend on the strength of intermolecular attractions and molecular weight. Stronger attractions lead to higher boiling/melting points.

5. Solubility: Solubility refers to the ability of a substance to dissolve in a solvent. It depends on the properties of both the solute and solvent and how well their molecules interact. Similar molecules interact better leading to higher solubility. Solubility affects rates of chemical reactions and biological processes.
