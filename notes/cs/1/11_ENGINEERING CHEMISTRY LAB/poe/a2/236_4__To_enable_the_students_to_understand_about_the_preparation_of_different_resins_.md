### 4. To enable the students to understand about the preparation of different resins.

1. Phenol-formaldehyde resin (Bakelite):
- Phenol and formaldehyde are heated together in the presence of a catalyst such as sodium hydroxide.
- The mixture is then heated under pressure.
- During the process, the phenol and formaldehyde undergo condensation to form a 3D network polymer.
- The resin is then moulded into the required shape.
- Bakelite is hard and resistant to heat, chemicals and electricity.

2. Melamine-formaldehyde resin:
- Melamine and formaldehyde are mixed and heated.
- Melamine acts as a crosslinking agent to produce a three-dimensional network structure.
- The properties depend on the ratio of melamine to formaldehyde.
- It is resistant to heat, scratches and moisture. Used to produce kitchenware and laminates.

3. Urea-formaldehyde resin:
- Urea and formaldehyde are heated in the presence of an acid catalyst.
- The properties can be controlled by varying the ratio of the reactants and catalyst concentration.
- It is used to produce adhesives and particle boards but has some toxicity issues.
