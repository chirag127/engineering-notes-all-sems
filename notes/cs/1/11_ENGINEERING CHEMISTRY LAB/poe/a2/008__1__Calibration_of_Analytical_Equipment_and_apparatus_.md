### 1. Calibration of Analytical Equipment and apparatus.

1. Calibration is essential to ensure accuracy and precision of measurements. It is a process of determining and adjusting the accuracy of the measuring instrument by comparing its measurements with those of a standard over the appropriate range of measurements.
2. Calibration is done using certified reference materials (CRMs) whose physical properties are well characterized. The calibration establishes a relationship between the instrument response and the property being measured and is usually done by linear regression analysis.
3. For most analytical equipment like pH meters, spectrophotometers, balances, etc., calibration involves determining the values corresponding to two or more points across the range of measurements and fitting a linear regression line. This line is then used to determine unknown values.
4. For some instruments like gas and liquid chromatography equipment, computer-controlled calibration procedures may be used. The retention times and peak widths are recorded for standards and used to construct a calibration curve.
5. It is recommended to calibrate instruments before first use and then routinely as per the manufacturer's recommendations or internal laboratory protocols to account for drift. Calibration data should be properly documented for each instrument.
6. Uncalibrated or improperly calibrated instruments can give erroneous results and affect accuracy, precision, and reliability of measurements. Hence, calibration is a critical laboratory practice.
