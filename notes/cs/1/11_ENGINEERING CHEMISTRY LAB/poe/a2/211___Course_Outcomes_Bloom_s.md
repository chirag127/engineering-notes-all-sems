###  Course Outcomes Bloom’s

1. Knowledge: Recall or recognize specific facts, terms, concepts, or criteria
- Students will be able to recall key terminology and definitions related to Bloom's taxonomy and course objectives.
- Students will be able to recall the names and sequence of the six levels in Bloom's taxonomy.

2. Comprehension: Understand the meaning of information or concepts
- Students will be able to explain the purpose and applicability of Bloom's taxonomy to developing course objectives and assessments.
- Students will be able to explain the meaning of each level in Bloom's taxonomy in their own words.

3. Application: Use information or concepts in new situations
- Students will be able to apply Bloom's taxonomy levels to evaluate existing course objectives and assessments and identify areas for improvement.
- Students will be able to write objectives and assessments at multiple levels of Bloom's taxonomy for their course.

4. Analysis: Break down information into its component parts
- Students will be able to analyze how well course objectives and assessments align with the levels of Bloom's taxonomy.
- Students will be able to analyze sample objectives or assessments and identify which level of Bloom's taxonomy they align with.

5. Synthesis: Create something new by combining parts or elements
- Students will be able to synthesize a set of course objectives and assessments that incorporate multiple levels of Bloom's taxonomy.
- Students will be able to synthesize feedback on objectives and assessments to make improvements that better meet the goals of their course.

6. Evaluation: Make judgments or decisions based on criteria
- Students will be able to evaluate draft course objectives and assessments against the levels of Bloom's taxonomy and make recommendations for improvement.
- Students will be able to reflect on and evaluate their own understanding of Bloom's taxonomy and its application to their course.
