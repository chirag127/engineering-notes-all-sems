### CO-5 Estimate the rate constant of reaction. K3

1. The rate constant (k) of a chemical reaction can be estimated from the slope of the line obtained by plotting concentration of reactant versus time on a graph. The units of rate constant are always M-1s-1 as the rate depends on concentration of the reactant.

2. For a first order reaction, the rate law can be written as, rate = k[A]
Where, [A] is the concentration of reactant. Integrating the rate law gives, k = 2.303/t log([A]0/[A])
Where, [A]0 is the initial concentration of reactant. A plot of log([A]0/[A]) versus time (t) gives a straight line with slope equal to -k/2.303. The value of rate constant (k) can be calculated from the slope.

3. For a second order reaction, the rate law can be written as, rate = k[A]2
Integrating the rate law gives, 1/k = 1/([A]0 - [A])
A plot of 1/[A] versus time gives a straight line with slope k. The rate constant (k) can be calculated from the slope.

4. The collision theory can be used to theoretically estimate the order of reaction and pre-exponential factor (A) in the rate law, rate = A e-Ea/RT where, Ea is the activation energy, R is the gas constant and T is temperature. The rate constant can be estimated from the slope of the plot of log k versus 1/T (Arrhenius plot).
