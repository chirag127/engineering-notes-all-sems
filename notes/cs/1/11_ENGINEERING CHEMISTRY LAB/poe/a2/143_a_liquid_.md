# Liquids

- A liquid is a state of matter with no fixed shape that takes on the shape of its container.
- Liquids have a defined volume but not a defined shape. They flow and take on the shape of their container.
- The intermolecular forces between liquid molecules are weaker than solid molecules but stronger than gas molecules. This allows liquids to flow while still maintaining volume.
- Common properties of liquids include:
-- Defined volume
-- Indefinite shape
-- Ability to flow
-- Being condensed (tightly packed particles)
- Water is a common example of a liquid. Other examples include mercury, ethanol, gasoline, and molten metals like gold or silver.
- Liquids change to gases through the process of evaporation or vaporization. This occurs when molecules gain enough energy to overcome intermolecular forces and escape the liquid.
- Liquids change to solids through the process of freezing or solidification. This occurs when molecules lose energy and intermolecular forces pull molecules closer together, restricting motion.
