1. Surface tension: Surface tension is a property of liquids that causes the surface layer of molecules to act as an elastic skin. It arises due to cohesive forces between molecules. The surface tension of a liquid depends on its molecular interactions and can be measured using a surface tensiometer.

Viscosity is primarily dependent on intermolecular forces between molecules and can be measured using devices such as a viscometer, Ostwald viscometer or Saybolt viscometer.

3. Boiling and melting points: The boiling and melting points of a substance are physical properties that depend on the strength of intermolecular forces between its molecules. They can be measured using devices such as a melting point apparatus or boiling point apparatus. Stronger intermolecular forces lead to higher boiling and melting points.

4. Vapour pressure: Vapour pressure refers to the pressure exerted by a vapour in equilibrium with its condensed phase at a given temperature. It depends on the strength of intermolecular forces between molecules and can be measured using devices such as a vapour pressure thermometer. Substances with weaker intermolecular forces have higher vapour pressures.
