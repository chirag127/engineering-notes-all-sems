### 9. Determination of available chlorine in bleaching powder.

1. Bleaching powder is calcium hypochlorite (Ca(OCl)2) and contains 35-40% available chlorine.
2. The available chlorine in bleaching powder can be determined by titration with sodium thiosulfate (Na2S2O3) using starch as indicator.
3. Procedure:
- Take accurately weighed 1 g of bleaching powder sample in a conical flask.
- Add 50 mL of distilled water and 2 mL of potassium iodide (KI) solution.
- Keep the flask stoppered for 2-3 min for the reaction to complete. The iodine liberated is equivalent to the available chlorine in bleaching powder.
I2 (aq) + 2KI (aq) → K2I2
- Titrate the iodine with 0.1 M sodium thiosulfate (Na2S2O3) using starch as an indicator.
- Note the burette reading (V1 mL) for the end point.
- Repeat the experiment with another sample and note the burette reading (V2 mL).
- % available chlorine = (V1-V2) × molarity × 100/ weight of sample taken
4. Precautions:
- Avoid exposure to iodine vapours. Perform the experiment in a well-ventilated place.
- Standardize the sodium thiosulfate solution against potassium dichromate.
- Use freshly prepared starch solution as an indicator.
