### 9. Determination of available chlorine in bleaching powder.

- Bleaching powder is a yellowish-white powder with a characteristic smell. It is calcium oxychloride, Ca(OCl)Cl. It contains a mixture of calcium chloride and calcium hypochlorite.
- The available chlorine in bleaching powder can be determined by the following methods:
1. Iodine method: Bleaching powder is reacted with a known excess of potassium iodide solution. The liberated iodine is titrated against sodium thiosulphate solution. One ml of N/10 sodium thiosulphate is equivalent to 0.003527 g of available chlorine.
2. Sodium arsenite method: Bleaching powder is reacted with excess of sodium arsenite solution. The unreacted sodium arsenite is titrated against iodine solution. One ml of N/10 iodine solution is equivalent to 0.003527 g of available chlorine.
3. Hydrogen peroxide method: Bleaching powder reacts with dilute sulphuric acid and hydrogen peroxide to liberate oxygen. The volume of oxygen liberated at S.T.P. is measured. One litre of oxygen is equivalent to 0.00441 g of available chlorine.
- The methods 1 and 2 are more accurate. Method 3 requires some corrections. The results are generally lower than the true value due to the instability of hydrogen peroxide.
