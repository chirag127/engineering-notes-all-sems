### 1. Calibration of Analytical Equipment and apparatus.

1. Calibration is the process of adjusting an instrument to produce accurate results. It is essential for analytical instruments to produce precise and accurate results.
2. Calibration is done using standard solutions of known concentration. The instrument reading for the standard solution is adjusted to the known value. This establishes a relation between the instrument reading and the analyte concentration.
3. The frequency of calibration depends on the stability and precision of the instrument. More precise instruments may require less frequent calibration. Environmental factors like temperature can also affect calibration.
4. Internal calibration is done using an internal standard. External calibration is done using external standards. Internal calibration is more convenient but external calibration is more accurate.
5. Calibration curves are plotted using multiple standards to get a regression equation. The regression equation is then used to determine unknown concentrations from instrument readings.
6. It is necessary to calibrate instruments before use and verify calibration periodically to ensure accuracy of results. Calibration certificates should be obtained and controls/standards run to verify calibration.
