### 16. Element detection and identification of functional groups in organic compounds.

1. Element detection

- Carbon and hydrogen: Combustion method. The organic compound is burnt in oxygen to produce carbon dioxide and water. Carbon dioxide is absorbed in sodium hydroxide and the water is collected.
- Nitrogen: Kjeldahl's method. The organic compound is heated with concentrated sulphuric acid and sodium sulphate. Ammonium sulphate is formed which is decomposed by sodium hydroxide to give ammonia. Ammonia is absorbed in acid and determined volumetrically.
- Halogen: Fusion with metallic sodium and examination of the residue. The residue contains sodium halide which is identified by appropriate tests.
- Sulphur: Oxidation with nitric acid and examination of the product. The sulphur is oxidised to sulphuric acid which is identified by barium chloride test.

2. Identification of functional groups

- Alcohols: Iodoform test, oxidation to aldehydes or ketones and reduction to hydrocarbons.
- Aldehydes and ketones: Reduction to alcohols, oxidation to acids, 2,4-dinitrophenylhydrazine test.
- Carboxylic acids: Reaction with ammonia to form amides, reduction to primary alcohols.
- Amines: Reaction with nitrous acid, carboxylic acids and aldehydes.
- Alkenes and alkynes: Addition reactions, hydrogenation.
