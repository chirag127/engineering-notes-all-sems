### 8. Determination of the strength of Potassium dichromate using internal indicator.

1. Take about 0.5 g of potassium dichromate in a dry conical flask.
2. Add 25 mL distilled water and shake well to dissolve the solid.
3. Add 2-3 drops of potassium iodide solution to the dichromate solution. A red precipitate of chromium(III) iodide is formed.
4. Titrate the solution with 0.1 M sodium thiosulfate solution using starch as an internal indicator.
5. A blue-black precipitate is formed when all the dichromate has been reduced to chromium(III). This indicates the end point.
6. Repeat the titration twice or thrice and take the average of the volume of sodium thiosulfate solution used.
7. Calculate the molarity of potassium dichromate solution using the formula:
   `Molarity = (Moles of K2Cr2O7)/(Volume in L)`
   Since `1 mL of 0.1 M sodium thiosulfate ~ 0.00392 moles of K2Cr2O7`
   `Moles of K2Cr2O7 = Volume of thiosulfate solution used × 0.00392`
