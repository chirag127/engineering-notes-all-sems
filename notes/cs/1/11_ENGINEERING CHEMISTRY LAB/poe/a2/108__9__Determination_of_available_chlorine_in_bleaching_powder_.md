### 9. Determination of available chlorine in bleaching powder.

1. Bleaching powder is a yellowish-white powder with a characteristic smell of hypochlorous acid. It is a mixture of calcium chloride (CaCl<sub>2</sub>) and calcium hypochlorite (Ca(OCl)<sub>2</sub>).

2. The 'available chlorine' in bleaching powder refers to the amount of chlorine which is present in the forms of hypochlorous acid (HOCl) and hypochlorite ions (OCl<sup>-</sup>). It can be determined by titrating it against standard potassium permanganate (KMnO<sub>4</sub>) solution.

3. Procedure:

- Take about 0.5 g of bleaching powder and mix it with 50 mL of distilled water in a conical flask.
- Add 5 mL of 5M sulfuric acid (H<sub>2</sub>SO<sub>4</sub>) and shake well. This converts the hypochlorites to hypochlorous acid.
- Titrate the solution with 0.05M potassium permanganate solution till the first permanent pink color appears.

4. The reactions occurring are:

HOCl + 2KMnO<sub>4</sub> + 3H<sub>2</sub>SO<sub>4</sub> → K<sub>2</sub>SO<sub>4</sub> + 2MnSO<sub>4</sub> + 3H<sub>2</sub>O + Cl<sub>2</sub>

5MnO<sub>4</sub> + 8H<sup>+</sup> + 5e<sup>-</sup> → Mn<sup>2+</sup> + 4H<sub>2</sub>O

5. Calculation:

Available chlorine (%) = (Volume of KMnO<sub>4</sub> x Molarity of KMnO<sub>4</sub> x Equivalent weight of Cl) / Weight of bleaching powder x 100
