### 11. Preparation of Phenol formaldehyde (PF) resin

Phenol formaldehyde (PF) resin is a thermosetting polymer that is widely used in the manufacturing of various products, including plywood, molded plastic, and laminates. Its preparation involves several steps, as outlined below:

1. Preparation of Phenol: Phenol is prepared by the cumene process, which involves the conversion of cumene to cumene hydroperoxide, followed by its decomposition to phenol and acetone.

2. Preparation of Formaldehyde: Formaldehyde is prepared by the oxidation of methanol with air or oxygen, using a silver catalyst.

3. Mixing Phenol and Formaldehyde: Phenol and formaldehyde are mixed in the presence of an acidic catalyst, such as hydrochloric acid or sulfuric acid. The mixture is heated to around 70-80°C, which initiates the reaction between phenol and formaldehyde.

4. Formation of Resin: The reaction between phenol and formaldehyde results in the formation of a resinous material, which can be further processed to obtain the desired properties. The degree of polymerization and cross-linking can be controlled by adjusting the pH, temperature, and concentration of the reactants.

5. Post-Processing: The PF resin is then cooled and washed to remove any unreacted phenol or formaldehyde. It is then dried and ground to a fine powder or dissolved in a suitable solvent to obtain a liquid resin.

6. Application: The PF resin can be used in various applications, such as adhesive, binder, coating, and molding compound. Its properties can be modified by incorporating additives, such as fillers, plasticizers, and curing agents.

In conclusion, the preparation of Phenol formaldehyde (PF) resin involves the mixing of phenol and formaldehyde in the presence of an acidic catalyst, followed by the formation of a resinous material. The properties of the resin can be modified by adjusting the processing parameters and incorporating additives.
