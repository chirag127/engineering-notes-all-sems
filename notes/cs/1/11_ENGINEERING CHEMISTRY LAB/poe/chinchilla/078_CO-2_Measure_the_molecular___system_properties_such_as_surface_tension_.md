# CO-2 Measure the molecular / system properties such as surface tension

When studying the properties of molecules and systems, it is important to be able to measure their various characteristics accurately. One such property that is often studied is surface tension. Surface tension refers to the tendency of a liquid surface to minimize its area, which results in the formation of a spherical shape. In this section, we will explore how to measure the molecular and system properties such as surface tension of CO-2.

### What is Surface Tension?

Before we dive into measuring surface tension, let's briefly discuss what it is. Surface tension arises due to the cohesive forces between the molecules in a liquid. These cohesive forces cause the molecules in the bulk of the liquid to attract each other, resulting in a net force that pulls the molecules at the surface inward. This inward force causes the surface of the liquid to contract and form a spherical shape. The magnitude of this force per unit length is known as the surface tension.

### Measuring Surface Tension

There are several methods for measuring surface tension, including capillary rise, drop weight, and pendant drop methods. In the context of CO-2, the most common method is the pendant drop method. In this method, a drop of CO-2 is suspended from a needle or a capillary tube, and an image of the drop is captured using a high-speed camera. From this image, the shape of the drop can be analyzed to determine the surface tension.

The pendant drop method involves the following steps:

1. Prepare a sample of CO-2 in a high-pressure cell.
2. Fill a capillary tube or needle with the CO-2 sample and suspend a droplet of CO-2 from the end.
3. Capture an image of the droplet using a high-speed camera.
4. Analyze the image to determine the shape of the droplet.
5. Use the shape of the droplet to calculate the surface tension of the CO-2 sample.

Measuring the molecular and system properties such as surface tension of CO-2 is an important part of studying its behavior.
