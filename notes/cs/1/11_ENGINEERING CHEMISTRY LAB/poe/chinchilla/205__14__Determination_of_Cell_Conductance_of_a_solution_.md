### 14. Determination of Cell Conductance of a solution.

In the study of electrochemistry, it is essential to determine the cell conductance of a solution. The cell conductance is the measure of the ability of a solution to conduct electricity. This measurement is significant because it helps to determine the strength of an electrolyte solution and its ability to conduct electricity.

Here are the steps involved in determining the cell conductance of a solution:

1. Set up the experimental apparatus: To determine the cell conductance of a solution, set up the experimental apparatus. This apparatus consists of two electrodes, a voltmeter, and an ammeter. The two electrodes are placed in the solution, and the voltmeter and ammeter are connected to them to measure the voltage and current, respectively.

2. Clean the electrodes: Clean the electrodes before placing them in the solution. This ensures that there is no contamination that could affect the results of the experiment.

3. Measure the resistance: Measure the resistance of the solution using the voltmeter and ammeter. Apply a small potential difference across the electrodes and measure the current that flows through the solution. Use Ohm's law to calculate the resistance of the solution.

4. Calculate the conductance: Once the resistance is calculated, the conductance of the solution can be determined. The conductance is the reciprocal of the resistance.

5. Determine the specific conductance: The specific conductance of the solution is the conductance of the solution divided by the distance between the electrodes.

6. Calculate the molar conductance: Finally, the molar conductance of the solution can be calculated by dividing the specific conductance by the concentration of the solution. The molar conductance is a measure of the ability of the solution to conduct electricity, and it is expressed in units of siemens per meter squared per mole (S m2 mol-1).

In conclusion, the determination of the cell conductance of a solution is an essential aspect of electrochemistry. By following the steps outlined above, the strength of an electrolyte solution and its ability to conduct electricity can be measured accurately.
