# Acid and Paracetamol by Conventional and Green Route

Acid and paracetamol are widely used drugs that are used to treat various illnesses such as pain, fever, and inflammation. These drugs can be produced using conventional and green routes. In this article, we will discuss the production of acid and paracetamol by conventional and green routes.

## Conventional Route

### Production of Acid

1. The conventional route for the production of acid involves the oxidation of hydrocarbons such as naphtha, propane, and butane.
2. The oxidation reaction is carried out using a catalyst such as vanadium pentoxide.
3. The reaction produces a mixture of various carboxylic acids, which are then separated using a fractionation process.
4. The separated acid is further purified through a series of processes such as distillation, crystallization, and filtration to obtain the final product.

### Production of Paracetamol

1. The conventional route for the production of paracetamol involves the reaction of p-aminophenol with acetic anhydride.
2. The reaction is carried out in the presence of a catalyst such as sulfuric acid or hydrochloric acid.
3. The reaction produces crude paracetamol, which is then purified through a series of processes such as crystallization, filtration, and drying to obtain the final product.

## Green Route

### Production of Acid

1. The green route for the production of acid involves the use of renewable resources such as biomass and waste materials.
2. The process involves the fermentation of sugars and other organic compounds present in biomass and waste materials.
3. The fermentation process produces carboxylic acids, which are then separated using a series of processes such as distillation, crystallization, and filtration to obtain the final product.

### Production of Paracetamol

1. The green route for the production of paracetamol involves the use of renewable resources such as lignin and cellulose.
2. The process involves the extraction of lignin and cellulose from biomass sources such as wood and agricultural waste.
3. The extracted lignin and cellulose are then converted into p-aminophenol through a series of processes such as hydrolysis, reduction, and nitration.
4. The p-aminophenol is then reacted with acetic anhydride to produce paracetamol.

In conclusion, both conventional and green routes are used for the production of acid and paracetamol. The choice of route depends on various factors such as cost, availability of resources, and environmental impact. However, the green route offers a more sustainable and environmentally friendly option for the production of these drugs.
