### 2. Determination of Hardness of Water Sample by EDTA Method

Water hardness is a common term used to describe the amount of dissolved minerals in water. These minerals, primarily calcium and magnesium ions, can lead to scaling and formation of deposits in pipes, appliances, and hot water systems. Therefore, determining the hardness of water is important in maintaining the quality of water for various applications.

One of the widely used methods for determining water hardness is the EDTA (Ethylenediaminetetraacetic acid) method. In this method, EDTA is used as a chelating agent to complex with the calcium and magnesium ions present in the water sample. The endpoint of the reaction is determined using a metal indicator, such as Eriochrome Black T (EBT), which changes color upon binding with the metal ions.

The determination of hardness of water sample by EDTA method involves the following steps:

1. Preparation of standard EDTA solution: Prepare a standard solution of EDTA by dissolving a known amount of EDTA in distilled water. The concentration of EDTA solution should be such that it can chelate with the expected amount of calcium and magnesium ions present in the water sample.

2. Preparation of buffer solution: Prepare a buffer solution of pH 10 by dissolving a known amount of disodium hydrogen phosphate and sodium hydroxide in distilled water. The buffer solution helps to maintain a constant pH during the titration process.

3. Calibration of the EDTA solution: Using a standardized calcium solution, titrate the EDTA solution until the endpoint is reached. Calculate the concentration of the EDTA solution using the known concentration of the calcium solution.

4. Sample preparation: Take a known volume of the water sample in a conical flask and add a few drops of EBT indicator. The water sample should be free from any suspended particles or turbidity.

5. Titration: Add a few milliliters of the buffer solution to the water sample and titrate with the standardized EDTA solution until the color changes from wine red to blue. The volume of EDTA solution required to reach the endpoint is noted.

6. Calculation: Calculate the hardness of the water sample in ppm (parts per million) or mg/L using the following formula:

Hardness = (Volume of EDTA solution x Concentration of EDTA solution x 1000) / Volume of water sample

7. Repeat the titration process for duplicate or triplicate samples to ensure the accuracy of the results.

In conclusion, the determination of hardness of water sample by EDTA method is a simple and reliable method that can be used to measure the concentration of calcium and magnesium ions present in water. It is an essential test for industries, laboratories, and households that rely on high-quality water for various applications.
