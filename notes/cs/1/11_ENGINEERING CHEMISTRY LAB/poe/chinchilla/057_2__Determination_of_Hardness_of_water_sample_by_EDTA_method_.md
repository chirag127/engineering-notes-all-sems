## Determination of Hardness of water sample by EDTA method

Hardness of water refers to the presence of dissolved minerals, primarily calcium and magnesium ions. The measurement of water hardness is important in determining its potential to cause scaling or other undesirable effects.

One common method for determining water hardness is the EDTA titration method. EDTA (ethylene diamine tetraacetic acid) is a chelating agent that can bind with metal ions, including calcium and magnesium.

The following steps can be followed to determine the hardness of a water sample using the EDTA titration method:

1. Collect a representative water sample in a clean, dry container. The sample should be at room temperature.

2. Add a small amount of buffer solution to the water sample to adjust its pH to around 10. This helps to ensure that the metal ions in the water will react with the EDTA.

3. Add a few drops of indicator solution to the water sample. The indicator used is typically Eriochrome Black T, which turns from blue to pink as the EDTA reacts with the metal ions.

4. Prepare a standard solution of EDTA by dissolving a known amount in distilled water. The concentration of the EDTA solution should be known.

5. Titrate the water sample with the EDTA solution until the indicator changes from blue to pink. This indicates that all of the metal ions in the water sample have reacted with the EDTA.

6. Record the volume of EDTA solution used in the titration.

7. Repeat the titration with additional water samples to ensure accuracy and reproducibility.

8. Calculate the hardness of the water sample in terms of calcium carbonate (CaCO3) using the following formula:

   Hardness (CaCO3) = (Volume of EDTA solution used x Molarity of EDTA solution x 1000) / Volume of water sample taken

   Molarity of EDTA solution = (Weight of EDTA used in grams / Molecular weight of EDTA) / Volume of EDTA solution in liters

The determination of water hardness by EDTA titration method is a common analytical technique used in the field of water treatment and can be used to ensure that water is safe for consumption and other purposes, while also preventing scaling or other undesirable effects.
