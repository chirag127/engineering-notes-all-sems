### 7. Determination of the strength of Ferrous ammonium sulfate using external indicator.

Ferrous ammonium sulfate, commonly known as Mohr's salt, is a chemical compound that is widely used in analytical chemistry. It is a white crystalline solid that is highly soluble in water and has a molecular formula of Fe(NH4)2(SO4)2·6H2O. One of the most common uses of Ferrous ammonium sulfate is as a primary standard for the calibration of analytical methods.

The strength of Ferrous ammonium sulfate can be determined using an external indicator. The external indicator method involves the use of a dye that reacts with the Ferrous ammonium sulfate to produce a color change. The intensity of the color change is proportional to the strength of the Ferrous ammonium sulfate.

The following steps can be followed to determine the strength of Ferrous ammonium sulfate using an external indicator:

1. Prepare a standard solution of Ferrous ammonium sulfate by dissolving a known amount of the compound in water. The solution should be standardized by titration with a standard solution of potassium permanganate.

2. Add a few drops of the external indicator to the standard solution. The external indicator should be chosen based on its ability to produce a clear, intense color change with Ferrous ammonium sulfate.

3. Titrate the standard solution with a standard solution of potassium permanganate until the color of the solution changes from the initial color to the endpoint color. The endpoint is reached when the external indicator changes color.

4. Record the volume of the standard solution of potassium permanganate used in the titration.

5. Repeat the titration process for at least two more times to ensure the accuracy of the results.

6. Calculate the strength of the Ferrous ammonium sulfate by using the formula:

    Strength of Ferrous ammonium sulfate = (Volume of potassium permanganate used in titration * Normality of potassium permanganate * Molecular weight of Ferrous ammonium sulfate) / (Volume of Ferrous ammonium sulfate taken in the titration * 1,000)

    Where:
    - Normality of potassium permanganate is the concentration of the standard solution of potassium permanganate in terms of moles per liter.
