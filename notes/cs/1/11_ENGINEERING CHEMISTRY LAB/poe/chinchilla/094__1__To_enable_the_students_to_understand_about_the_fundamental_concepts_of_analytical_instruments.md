### 1. To enable the students to understand about the fundamental concepts of analytical instruments.

Analytical instruments are widely used in various fields of science and technology to analyze the properties of different materials. To understand the principles and working of analytical instruments, it is important to learn about the fundamental concepts that govern their operation. The following points provide an overview of the basic concepts related to analytical instruments:

1. Measurement: The primary function of analytical instruments is to measure physical and chemical properties of materials. These measurements are often quantitative, i.e., they provide numerical values that can be used to compare different samples and draw conclusions.

2. Sensitivity: The sensitivity of an analytical instrument refers to its ability to detect small changes in the property being measured. Instruments with high sensitivity are preferred as they can detect even minor changes in the sample.

3. Selectivity: The selectivity of an instrument refers to its ability to measure specific properties of a sample without interference from other components. Selective instruments are preferred as they can provide accurate measurements of specific properties.

4. Calibration: Calibration is the process of determining the relationship between the output of an instrument and the property being measured. Calibration is important to ensure the accuracy and reliability of measurements.

5. Detection limit: The detection limit of an instrument is the minimum amount of a substance that can be detected by the instrument. Instruments with low detection limits are preferred as they can detect even trace amounts of substances.

6. Precision: Precision refers to the degree of repeatability of measurements made with an instrument. An instrument with high precision will provide similar results each time it is used.

7. Accuracy: Accuracy refers to the degree of agreement between the measured value and the true value of a property. An instrument with high accuracy will provide measurements that are close to the true value.

8. Signal-to-Noise ratio: The signal-to-noise ratio of an instrument refers to the ratio of the signal (i.e., the property being measured) to the noise (i.e., unwanted electrical or environmental interference). Instruments with high signal-to-noise ratios are preferred as they provide more accurate measurements.

9. Sensing mechanism: The sensing mechanism of an instrument refers to the physical or chemical process by which the property being measured is converted into a measurable signal. Understanding the sensing mechanism is important to interpret the measurements made by an instrument.

By understanding these fundamental concepts, students can gain a better understanding of how analytical instruments work and how to use them effectively in their research or work.
