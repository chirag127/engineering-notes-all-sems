### 2. Determination of Hardness of water sample by EDTA method

The hardness of water is defined as the amount of calcium and magnesium ions present in water. Water hardness can cause scaling on pipes and appliances, and can also lead to the formation of soap scum. EDTA (ethylenediaminetetraacetic acid) is a chelating agent that is commonly used to determine the hardness of water samples. The following points describe the determination of hardness of water sample by EDTA method:

1. Take a water sample of known volume in a conical flask.
2. Add a buffer solution to the water sample to maintain a constant pH.
3. Add a small amount of indicator solution to the water sample. The indicator used is typically Eriochrome Black T (EBT), which forms a complex with calcium and magnesium ions.
4. Titrate the water sample with a standard solution of EDTA until the color of the indicator changes from wine red to blue. The titration should be carried out slowly with constant stirring.
5. Note down the volume of EDTA solution used in the titration.
6. Repeat the titration process with a blank solution (water sample with buffer and indicator but without the addition of EDTA) to determine the amount of EDTA required to react with the buffer and indicator.
7. Calculate the hardness of the water sample using the formula:

Hardness (in mg/L) = (volume of EDTA used in sample titration – volume of EDTA used in blank titration) x normality of EDTA x 1000 / volume of water sample

8. The hardness of water can be classified as soft (0-75 mg/L), moderately hard (75-150 mg/L), hard (150-300 mg/L), and very hard (above 300 mg/L).

It is important to note that the EDTA method only measures the total hardness of water, which includes both calcium and magnesium ions. To determine the individual concentrations of calcium and magnesium ions, additional tests must be performed. The EDTA method is a widely used and reliable method for determining the hardness of water samples in laboratories and industries.
