### 10. Determination of chloride content in water sample.

In analytical chemistry, it is essential to determine the amount of chloride present in a water sample. Here are the steps to follow for the determination of chloride content in a water sample:

1. Take a water sample of 100 mL in a clean beaker.

2. Add a few drops of nitric acid (HNO3) to the water sample to oxidize any interfering substances.

3. Add a few drops of silver nitrate (AgNO3) solution to the water sample. The addition of silver nitrate solution will result in the formation of a white precipitate of silver chloride (AgCl).

4. Stir the mixture and allow it to settle for a few minutes.

5. Filter the mixture through a filter paper to separate the precipitated silver chloride from the solution.

6. Wash the precipitate with distilled water to remove any soluble impurities.

7. Transfer the precipitate to a crucible and dry it at a low temperature.

8. Heat the crucible containing the dried precipitate over a flame until the silver chloride turns into a reddish-brown color.

9. Cool the crucible and weigh it accurately.

10. Calculate the amount of chloride present in the water sample using the following formula:

   Chloride content (mg/L) = (weight of AgCl × 0.00035 × 1000)/volume of water sample (mL)

   *Note: The factor 0.00035 is the conversion factor to convert the weight of AgCl to the amount of chloride present in mg/L.*

In conclusion, the determination of chloride content in a water sample is an important analytical procedure. By following the above steps, we can accurately determine the amount of chloride present in a water sample.
