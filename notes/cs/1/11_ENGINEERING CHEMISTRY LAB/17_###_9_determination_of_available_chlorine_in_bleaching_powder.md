### 9. Determination of available chlorine in bleaching powder.

Determination of available chlorine in bleaching powder:

Available chlorine is a measure of the amount of active chlorine in a bleaching powder. It is a key parameter for evaluating the efficacy of bleaching powders in applications such as water treatment, textiles, and food processing.

The following steps can be used to determine the available chlorine in a bleaching powder:

1. Preparation of sample solution: A weighed amount of bleaching powder is dissolved in distilled water to form a sample solution. The concentration of the sample solution is typically expressed as a weight-to-volume ratio, such as grams per liter.

2. Titration: The sample solution is titrated with a standardized solution of sodium thiosulfate (Na2S2O3) using a burette. The reaction between the available chlorine in the sample solution and the sodium thiosulfate results in the formation of chloride ions (Cl-) and sulfite ions (SO32-). The endpoint of the titration is indicated by the appearance of a yellow color, which is produced by the formation of a yellow compound, sulfur dioxide (SO2).

3. Calculation of available chlorine: The amount of available chlorine in the sample solution can be calculated from the volume of sodium thiosulfate required to reach the endpoint of the titration. The calculation is based on the stoichiometry of the reaction between the available chlorine and the sodium thiosulfate.

In conclusion, the determination of available chlorine in a bleaching powder involves dissolving a weighed amount of bleaching powder in distilled water to form a sample solution, titrating the sample solution with a standardized solution of sodium thiosulfate, and calculating the amount of available chlorine from the volume of sodium thiosulfate required to reach the endpoint of the titration. The available chlorine is a measure of the amount of active chlorine in a bleaching powder and is an important parameter for evaluating its efficacy in various applications.
