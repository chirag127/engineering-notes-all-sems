### 2. Determination of Hardness of water sample by EDTA method.

The EDTA (ethylene diamine tetraacetic acid) method is a commonly used method for determining the hardness of water samples. Hardness in water is caused by the presence of cations such as calcium and magnesium, which interfere with the performance of soap and other household cleaning products.

To determine the hardness of a water sample using the EDTA method, the following steps are typically followed:

1. Prepare a solution of EDTA and a buffer to control the pH.

2. Add a known volume of the water sample to the EDTA solution and mix well.

3. Add a reagent that reacts with the EDTA to form a complex with the calcium and magnesium ions in the water sample.

4. Measure the change in color of the solution, which indicates the amount of calcium and magnesium ions present in the water sample.

5. Calculate the hardness of the water sample based on the amount of calcium and magnesium ions present.

6. Repeat the experiment several times to obtain an average value for the hardness.

7. Compare the average value obtained with the known values for the hardness of water to determine the accuracy of the experiment.

8. If necessary, make adjustments to the experiment and repeat the steps to obtain a more accurate result.

The EDTA method is a simple and reliable method for determining the hardness of water samples and is widely used in water treatment and analysis.
