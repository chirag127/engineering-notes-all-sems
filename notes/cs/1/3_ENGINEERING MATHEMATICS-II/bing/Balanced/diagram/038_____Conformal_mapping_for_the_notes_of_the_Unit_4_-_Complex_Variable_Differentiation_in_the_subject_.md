### Conformal mapping

- A conformal mapping is a function defined on the complex plane that transforms a given curve or points on a plane, preserving each angle of that curve.
- A conformal mapping is also called a conformal transformation or a conformal map projection.
- A conformal mapping is differentiable at every point in its domain and has a nonzero derivative.
- A conformal mapping is useful for studying the properties of complex functions, harmonic functions, and potential flows.
- Examples of conformal mappings are:
  - Similarity transformations, which are mappings that preserve the shape and size of figures, such as rotations, translations, and scaling.
  - The exponential function, which maps the horizontal strips in the z-plane to the sectors of the w-plane.
  - The logarithmic function, which maps the sectors of the z-plane to the horizontal strips of the w-plane.
  - The Joukowsky transformation, which maps a circle in the z-plane to an airfoil in the w-plane.
  - The Mercator map, which maps the surface of the earth to a rectangular map that preserves compass directions.
