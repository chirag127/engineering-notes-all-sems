### Convergence of series

- A series is an expression of the form `∑ i = 1 ∞ a i`, where `a i` are the terms of a sequence.
- A series is convergent if the sequence of its partial sums `s n = ∑ i = 1 n a i` tends to a limit as `n` goes to infinity. That means that the partial sums become closer and closer to a given number `s`, which is called the sum of the series .
- A series is divergent if the sequence of its partial sums does not tend to a limit as `n` goes to infinity. That means that the partial sums either grow without bound or oscillate between different values .
- A series can be tested for convergence or divergence using various methods, such as the nth term test, the comparison test, the ratio test, the root test, the integral test, the alternating series test, and others.
- The convergence or divergence of a series depends on the behavior of the terms `a i` as `i` goes to infinity. For example, if `a i` converges to zero as `i` goes to infinity, then the series may or may not converge, depending on how fast `a i` approaches zero. However, if `a i` does not converge to zero as `i` goes to infinity, then the series must diverge .
- The convergence or divergence of a series has important implications for the properties and applications of the series, such as its continuity, differentiability, integrability, and representation of functions .
