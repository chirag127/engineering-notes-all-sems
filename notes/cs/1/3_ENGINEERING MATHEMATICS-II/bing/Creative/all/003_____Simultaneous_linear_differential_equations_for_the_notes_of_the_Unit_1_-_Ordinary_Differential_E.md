# Simultaneous Linear Differential Equations

- A simultaneous differential equation is one of the mathematical equations for an indefinite function of one or more than one variables that relate the values of the function.
- A simultaneous linear differential equation is a system of two or more linear differential equations with a single independent variable and two or more dependent variables.
- A general form of a simultaneous linear differential equation with two dependent variables is:

$$
\begin{cases}
a_1(x) \frac{dy}{dx} + b_1(x) y + c_1(x) z = f_1(x) \\
a_2(x) \frac{dz}{dx} + b_2(x) z + c_2(x) y = f_2(x)
\end{cases}
$$

- where $a_1, b_1, c_1, a_2, b_2, c_2, f_1, f_2$ are given functions of $x$ and $y$ and $z$ are the unknown functions of $x$.
- A solution of a simultaneous linear differential equation is a pair of functions $(y(x), z(x))$ that satisfy both equations for all values of $x$ in a given interval.
- A method to solve a simultaneous linear differential equation is to eliminate one of the dependent variables by using the substitution method, the elimination method, or the matrix method.
- Another method to solve a simultaneous linear differential equation is to use the Laplace transform, which converts the system of differential equations into a system of algebraic equations that can be solved by using the inverse Laplace transform.
- Simultaneous linear differential equations can be used to model real-life problems such as electric circuits, mechanical vibrations, population dynamics, and chemical reactions .
