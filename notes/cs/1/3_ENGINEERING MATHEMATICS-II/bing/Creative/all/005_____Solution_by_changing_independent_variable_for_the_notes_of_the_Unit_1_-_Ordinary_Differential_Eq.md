# Solution by changing independent variable for the notes of the Unit 1 - Ordinary Differential Equation of Higher Order in the subject of ENGINEERING MATHEMATICS-II

- A solution of a differential equation is a function that satisfies the equation when substituted into it.
- A general solution of a differential equation is a solution that contains one or more arbitrary constants.
- A particular solution of a differential equation is a solution that is obtained by assigning specific values to the arbitrary constants in the general solution.
- A change of variable is a technique that transforms a differential equation into another one that is easier to solve.
- A change of variable can involve changing the independent variable, the dependent variable, or both.
- A change of variable can be used to solve homogeneous differential equations, linear differential equations, separable differential equations, and other types of differential equations  .
- To use a change of variable, one must find a suitable substitution that simplifies the equation, and then apply the chain rule to express the derivatives in terms of the new variables .
- After solving the transformed equation, one must reverse the substitution to obtain the solution in terms of the original variables.
- An example of a change of variable involving the independent variable is to replace x with u = x + a, where a is a constant. This can be used to solve differential equations of the form y'' + p(x)y' + q(x)y = 0, where p and q are constants or functions of x.
- An example of a change of variable involving the dependent variable is to replace y with z = y/x, where x is the independent variable. This can be used to solve differential equations of the form y' = f(x,y), where f is a homogeneous function of the same degree in x and y .
