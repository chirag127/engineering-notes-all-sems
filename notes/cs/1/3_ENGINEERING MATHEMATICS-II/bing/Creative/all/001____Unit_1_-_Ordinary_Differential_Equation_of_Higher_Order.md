# Unit 1 - Ordinary Differential Equation of Higher Order

- An ordinary differential equation (ODE) is an equation that involves one or more derivatives of an unknown function with respect to a single independent variable.
- The order of an ODE is the highest order of the derivative that appears in the equation.
- A higher-order ODE is an ODE of order two or more.
- A higher-order ODE can be written in the form:

$$
a_n(x) \frac{d^n y}{dx^n} + a_{n-1}(x) \frac{d^{n-1} y}{dx^{n-1}} + \cdots + a_1(x) \frac{dy}{dx} + a_0(x) y = f(x)
$$

where $a_n(x), a_{n-1}(x), \ldots, a_0(x)$ and $f(x)$ are given functions of $x$, and $y$ is the unknown function to be determined.

- A higher-order ODE is called linear if the coefficients $a_n(x), a_{n-1}(x), \ldots, a_0(x)$ are constants or linear functions of $x$, and $f(x)$ is any function of $x$.
- A higher-order ODE is called homogeneous if $f(x) = 0$, and nonhomogeneous otherwise.
- A higher-order ODE is called separable if it can be written in the form:

$$
\frac{d^n y}{dx^n} = g(x) h(y)
$$

where $g(x)$ and $h(y)$ are given functions of $x$ and $y$, respectively.

- A higher-order ODE is called exact if it can be written in the form:

$$
\frac{d^n y}{dx^n} = \frac{\partial^n F}{\partial x^n}
$$

where $F(x,y)$ is a given function of $x$ and $y$, such that $\frac{\partial^n F}{\partial x^n}$ is independent of $y$.

- A higher-order ODE is called autonomous if it can be written in the form:

$$
\frac{d^n y}{dx^n} = g(y)
$$

where $g(y)$ is a given function of $y$, independent of $x$.

- The general solution of a higher-order ODE is a function that satisfies the equation for any values of the arbitrary constants that appear in the solution.
- The particular solution of a higher-order ODE is a function that satisfies the equation and some given initial or boundary conditions.
- The general solution of a linear homogeneous higher-order ODE can be obtained by finding a set of linearly independent solutions and forming a linear combination of them.
- The general solution of a linear nonhomogeneous higher-order ODE can be obtained by adding a particular solution of the nonhomogeneous equation to the general solution of the corresponding homogeneous equation.
- The general solution of a separable higher-order ODE can be obtained by separating the variables and integrating both sides of the equation.
- The general solution of an exact higher-order ODE can be obtained by finding a function $F(x,y)$ such that $\frac{\partial^n F}{\partial x^n} = \frac{d^n y}{dx^n}$, and setting $F(x,y) = C$, where $C$ is an arbitrary constant.
- The general solution of an autonomous higher-order ODE can be obtained by reducing the order of the equation by introducing a new variable $z = \frac{dy}{dx}$, and solving the resulting first-order ODE.
