### Cauchy- Integral theorem

- The Cauchy integral theorem is a statement about line integrals of holomorphic functions in the complex plane.
- A holomorphic function is a complex-valued function that is differentiable at every point in its domain.
- The Cauchy integral theorem says that if a function f(z) is holomorphic in a simply connected domain D, and C is a closed contour in D, then the line integral of f(z) along C is zero  .
- A simply connected domain is a region that has no holes or gaps in it.
- A closed contour is a curve that starts and ends at the same point.
- The line integral of f(z) along C is defined as the sum of f(z) times the infinitesimal change in z along C.
- The Cauchy integral theorem can be written as

$$\oint_C f(z) dz = 0$$

- The Cauchy integral theorem can be derived from Stokes' theorem, which relates the line integral of a vector field to the flux of its curl through a surface.
- The Cauchy integral theorem is a powerful tool for evaluating line integrals of holomorphic functions, since it allows us to choose any convenient contour that encloses the region of interest  .
- The Cauchy integral theorem also implies that the value of a holomorphic function at any point inside a closed contour is determined by its values on the boundary of the contour. This is known as the Cauchy integral formula.
