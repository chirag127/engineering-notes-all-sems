### Existence theorem for the notes of the Unit 2 - Laplace Transform in the subject of ENGINEERING MATHEMATICS-II

- The existence theorem of Laplace transform states the conditions under which a function has a Laplace transform.
- A function f(t) has a Laplace transform F(s) if and only if
  - f(t) is piecewise continuous on every finite interval in [0, ∞), i.e., f(t) has only a finite number of discontinuities and jumps in any interval [0, T].
  - f(t) is of exponential order, i.e., there exist constants M, c, and T such that |f(t)| ≤ Me<sup>ct</sup> for all t ≥ T.
- The constants M, c, and T depend on the function f(t) and may not be unique.
- The parameter s in the Laplace transform F(s) must be greater than c for the integral to converge.
- The existence theorem ensures that the Laplace transform is well-defined for a wide class of functions, such as polynomials, exponentials, trigonometric functions, etc.
- The existence theorem also implies that the Laplace transform is unique, i.e., if two functions have the same Laplace transform, then they are equal except for a finite number of points.
