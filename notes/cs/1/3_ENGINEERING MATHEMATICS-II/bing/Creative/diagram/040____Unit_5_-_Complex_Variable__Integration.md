## Unit 5 - Complex Variable –Integration

- Complex variable integration is the process of finding the value of a complex function along a curve in the complex plane.
- The curve can be either closed or open, and can be either smooth or piecewise smooth.
- A smooth curve is one that has a continuous derivative at every point, and a piecewise smooth curve is one that is composed of finitely many smooth curves joined at their endpoints.
- The value of the complex integral depends on both the function and the curve, and is denoted by

$$\int_C f(z) dz$$

where $C$ is the curve and $f(z)$ is the complex function.

- There are some basic properties of complex integration that are similar to those of real integration, such as linearity, additivity, and independence of parameterization.
- Linearity means that for any complex constants $a$ and $b$, and any complex functions $f(z)$ and $g(z)$,

$$\int_C (af(z) + bg(z)) dz = a \int_C f(z) dz + b \int_C g(z) dz$$

- Additivity means that for any curve $C$ that can be split into two subcurves $C_1$ and $C_2$, such that $C = C_1 + C_2$, and any complex function $f(z)$,

$$\int_C f(z) dz = \int_{C_1} f(z) dz + \int_{C_2} f(z) dz$$

- Independence of parameterization means that for any curve $C$ that can be represented by different parametric equations, and any complex function $f(z)$,

$$\int_C f(z) dz = \int_a^b f(z(t)) z'(t) dt$$

where $z(t)$ is any parametric equation for $C$, and $a$ and $b$ are the corresponding limits of integration.

- There are also some important theorems that relate complex integration to complex analysis, such as Cauchy's integral theorem, Cauchy's integral formula, and the residue theorem.
- Cauchy's integral theorem states that for any closed curve $C$ that lies in a simply connected domain $D$, and any complex function $f(z)$ that is analytic in $D$,

$$\int_C f(z) dz = 0$$

- Cauchy's integral formula states that for any closed curve $C$ that encloses a point $z_0$ in a simply connected domain $D$, and any complex function $f(z)$ that is analytic in $D$,

$$f(z_0) = \frac{1}{2\pi i} \int_C \frac{f(z)}{z - z_0} dz$$

- The residue theorem states that for any closed curve $C$ that encloses a finite number of isolated singularities of a complex function $f(z)$ in a simply connected domain $D$,

$$\int_C f(z) dz = 2\pi i \sum_{k=1}^n \text{Res}(f, z_k)$$

where $\text{Res}(f, z_k)$ is the residue of $f(z)$ at the singularity $z_k$.

- These theorems are useful for evaluating complex integrals that are otherwise difficult or impossible to compute by direct methods. They also have applications in various fields of mathematics, physics, and engineering.
