### Functions of complex variable

- A complex function is a function that maps complex numbers to complex numbers, i.e., $w=f(z)$ where $w$ and $z$ are complex numbers.
- A complex number $z$ can be written as $z=x+iy$ where $x$ and $y$ are real numbers and $i$ is the imaginary unit such that $i^2=-1$.
- A complex function can also be written as $w=u+iv$ where $u$ and $v$ are real functions of $x$ and $y$, i.e., $u=u(x,y)$ and $v=v(x,y)$.
- A complex function is said to be **differentiable** at a point $z_0$ if the limit $\lim_{\Delta z \to 0} \frac{f(z_0+\Delta z)-f(z_0)}{\Delta z}$ exists and is independent of the direction of $\Delta z$.
- A complex function is said to be **analytic** or **holomorphic** at a point $z_0$ if it is differentiable at $z_0$ and at every point in some neighborhood of $z_0$.
- A complex function is said to be **entire** if it is analytic at every point in the complex plane.
- A complex function is said to be **harmonic** if its real and imaginary parts satisfy the Laplace equation, i.e., $\nabla^2 u = \nabla^2 v = 0$.
- Some examples of complex functions are:
  - $f(z)=z^2=(x+iy)^2=x^2-y^2+2ixy$
  - $f(z)=e^z=e^{x+iy}=e^x(\cos y + i \sin y)$
  - $f(z)=\log z=\log |z| + i \arg z$
  - $f(z)=\sin z=\sin (x+iy)=\sin x \cosh y + i \cos x \sinh y$
- Some properties of complex functions are:
  - If $f(z)$ and $g(z)$ are analytic, then so are $f(z)+g(z)$, $f(z)-g(z)$, $f(z)g(z)$, and $\frac{f(z)}{g(z)}$ (if $g(z) \neq 0$).
  - If $f(z)$ is analytic and $g(z)$ is a function of $f(z)$, then the composite function $g(f(z))$ is also analytic.
  - If $f(z)$ is analytic and non-constant, then $f(z)$ is an open mapping, i.e., it maps open sets to open sets.
  - If $f(z)$ is analytic in a domain $D$, then $f'(z)$ is also analytic in $D$ and $f(z)$ has an infinite number of derivatives in $D$.
  - If $f(z)$ and $g(z)$ are analytic and $f(z)=g(z)$ for all $z$ in some open set, then $f(z)=g(z)$ for all $z$ in the domain of $f(z)$ and $g(z)$.
  - If $f(z)$ is analytic and $f'(z)=0$ for all $z$ in some open set, then $f(z)$ is constant in that open set.
