## Unit 3 - Sequence and Series

A **sequence** is a list of numbers or objects that follow a certain rule or pattern. For example, 2, 4, 6, 8, ... is a sequence of even numbers.

A **series** is the sum of the terms of a sequence. For example, 2 + 4 + 6 + 8 + ... is a series of even numbers.

Some common types of sequences and series are:

- **Arithmetic sequence and series**: A sequence where each term is obtained by adding or subtracting a constant value to the previous term. For example, 3, 7, 11, 15, ... is an arithmetic sequence with a common difference of 4. The series is the sum of the terms, such as 3 + 7 + 11 + 15 + ...
- **Geometric sequence and series**: A sequence where each term is obtained by multiplying or dividing a constant value to the previous term. For example, 2, 6, 18, 54, ... is a geometric sequence with a common ratio of 3. The series is the product of the terms, such as 2 * 6 * 18 * 54 * ...
- **Fibonacci sequence and series**: A sequence where each term is obtained by adding the previous two terms. For example, 1, 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13, ... is a Fibonacci sequence. The series is the sum of the terms, such as 1 + 1 + 2 + 3 + 5 + 8 + 13 + ...
- **Harmonic sequence and series**: A sequence where each term is the reciprocal of a positive integer. For example, 1, 1/2, 1/3, 1/4, ... is a harmonic sequence. The series is the sum of the terms, such as 1 + 1/2 + 1/3 + 1/4 + ...

Some important concepts and formulas related to sequences and series are:

- **Term**: The individual element of a sequence. For example, in the sequence 2, 4, 6, 8, ..., the first term is 2, the second term is 4, and so on.
- **Index**: The position of a term in a sequence. For example, in the sequence 2, 4, 6, 8, ..., the index of 2 is 1, the index of 4 is 2, and so on.
- **General term**: A formula that gives the value of any term in a sequence based on its index. For example, in the arithmetic sequence 3, 7, 11, 15, ..., the general term is a_n = 3 + 4(n - 1), where n is the index of the term.
- **Partial sum**: The sum of the first n terms of a series. For example, in the arithmetic series 3 + 7 + 11 + 15 + ..., the partial sum of the first 4 terms is S_4 = 3 + 7 + 11 + 15 = 36.
- **Infinite series**: A series that has infinitely many terms. For example, 1 + 1/2 + 1/4 + 1/8 + ... is an infinite series.
- **Convergence and divergence**: The behavior of an infinite series as the number of terms increases. A series is said to **converge** if the partial sums approach a finite limit. A series is said to **diverge** if the partial sums do not approach a finite limit. For example, the series 1 + 1/2 + 1/4 + 1/8 + ... converges to 2, while the series 1 + 2 + 3 + 4 + ... diverges to infinity.
