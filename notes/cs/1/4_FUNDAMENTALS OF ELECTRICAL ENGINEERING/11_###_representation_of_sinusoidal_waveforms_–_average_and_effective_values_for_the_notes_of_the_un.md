### Representation of Sinusoidal waveforms – Average and effective values for the notes of the Unit 2 - Steady State Analysis of Single Phase AC Circuits in the subject of FUNDAMENTALS OF ELECTRICAL ENGINEERING

Sinusoidal waveforms are periodic waveforms that are commonly used to represent alternating current (AC) signals in electrical engineering. In the subject of Fundamentals of Electrical Engineering, Unit 2 - Steady State Analysis of Single Phase AC Circuits, the representation of sinusoidal waveforms is an important topic. The average value of a sinusoidal waveform is the average of its values over one period and is used to represent the average power of a signal. The effective value, also known as the root-mean-square (RMS) value, is a measure of the magnitude of a sinusoidal waveform and is used to represent the effective power of a signal. The effective value of a sinusoidal waveform is equal to the square root of the average of the square of its values over one period. Understanding the representation of sinusoidal waveforms and their average and effective values is essential for students pursuing electrical engineering degrees, as it forms the basis for many advanced topics in electrical engineering, including power distribution and control systems.
