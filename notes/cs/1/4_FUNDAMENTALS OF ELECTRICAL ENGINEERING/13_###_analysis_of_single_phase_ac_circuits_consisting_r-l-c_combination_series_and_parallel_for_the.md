### Analysis of single phase AC Circuits consisting R-L-C combination (Series and Parallel) for the notes of the Unit 2 - Steady State Analysis of Single Phase AC Circuits in the subject of FUNDAMENTALS OF ELECTRICAL ENGINEERING

Single-phase AC circuits are composed of resistors, inductors, and capacitors. These components can be arranged in either a series or parallel configuration.

Series R-L-C Circuit:
In a series R-L-C circuit, the components are connected in a series, with the current flowing through each component in turn. The impedance of the circuit is equal to the sum of the impedances of each component. The voltage across each component is proportional to its impedance.

Parallel R-L-C Circuit:
In a parallel R-L-C circuit, the components are connected in parallel, with the voltage across each component being the same. The impedance of the circuit is equal to the reciprocal of the sum of the reciprocals of the impedances of each component. The current flowing through each component is proportional to its impedance.

Steady-state analysis of single-phase AC circuits involves finding the voltages and currents in the circuit at a given frequency. This can be done using phasor analysis, which involves representing the voltages and currents in the circuit as complex numbers. The magnitude and phase angle of these complex numbers can be used to determine the voltages and currents in the circuit.

In conclusion, the analysis of single-phase AC circuits consisting of R-L-C combinations is important for understanding the behavior of these circuits in steady-state conditions.
