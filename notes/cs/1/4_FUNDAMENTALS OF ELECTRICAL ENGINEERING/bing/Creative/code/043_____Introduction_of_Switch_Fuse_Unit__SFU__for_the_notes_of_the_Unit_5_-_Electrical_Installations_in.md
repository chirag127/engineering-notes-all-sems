### Introduction of Switch Fuse Unit (SFU) for the notes of the Unit 5 - Electrical Installations in the subject of FUNDAMENTALS OF ELECTRICAL ENGINEERING

- A switch fuse unit (SFU) is a type of low voltage switchgear that combines a switch and a fuse in a single unit.
- A switch fuse unit is used to protect electrical circuits from short circuits, overloads, and faults by interrupting the current flow when the fuse blows.
- A switch fuse unit can also be used to isolate and control electrical circuits by manually opening and closing the switch.
- A switch fuse unit is made of iron and is known as an ironclad switch. It can be double pole for controlling single phase two-wire circuits, triple pole for controlling three-phase, 3-wire circuits, or triple pole with neutral link for controlling 3-phase, 4-wire circuits. The respective switches are known as double pole ironclad (DPIC), triple pole ironclad (TPIC), and triple pole with neutral link ironclad (TPNIC) switches  .
- A switch fuse unit has a switch unit and a fuse unit. The switch unit consists of a handle, a contactor, and a spring mechanism. The fuse unit consists of a fuse base, a fuse carrier, and a fuse link. The fuse link is a metal wire or strip that melts when the current exceeds a certain value. The fuse link can be replaced after it blows .
- A switch fuse unit has a rated current and a rated voltage. The rated current is the maximum current that the switch fuse unit can carry without overheating. The rated voltage is the maximum voltage that the switch fuse unit can withstand without breaking down. The rated current and voltage of the switch fuse unit should match the characteristics of the electrical circuit that it protects .
- A switch fuse unit has some advantages and disadvantages. Some advantages are:

  - It is simple and cheap to install and maintain.
  - It provides reliable and fast protection against short circuits and overloads.
  - It allows manual control and isolation of electrical circuits.
  - It can be used for different types of loads and circuits.

  Some disadvantages are:

  - It does not provide protection against earth faults or leakage currents.
  - It does not have an indication of the fuse status or the fault location.
  - It may cause arc flash or fire hazards when the fuse blows.
  - It may have a lower breaking capacity than other types of switchgear .
