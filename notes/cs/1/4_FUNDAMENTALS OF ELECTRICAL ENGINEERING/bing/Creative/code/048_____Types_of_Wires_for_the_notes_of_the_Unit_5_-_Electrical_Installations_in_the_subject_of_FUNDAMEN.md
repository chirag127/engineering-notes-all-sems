### Types of Wires for the notes of the Unit 5 - Electrical Installations in the subject of FUNDAMENTALS OF ELECTRICAL ENGINEERING

- A wire is a single, flexible strand or rod of metal that can carry electric current when connected to a power source.
- Wires are used to connect electrical devices, such as switches, outlets, lights, appliances, and machines, to the electrical system of a building or a vehicle.
- Wires can be classified by their material, size, shape, insulation, and function.

#### Material
- The most common materials for wires are copper and aluminum, which have high electrical conductivity and can be easily bent and shaped.
- Copper wires are more expensive, but have higher tensile strength, ductility, and resistance to corrosion than aluminum wires.
- Aluminum wires are lighter, cheaper, and more suitable for long-distance transmission, but have lower conductivity, higher resistance, and more susceptibility to oxidation than copper wires.
- Other materials, such as steel, iron, nickel, silver, gold, and platinum, can also be used for wires, but they have lower conductivity, higher cost, or special applications.

#### Size
- The size of a wire is measured by its cross-sectional area or diameter, which affects its current-carrying capacity and resistance.
- The larger the cross-sectional area or diameter, the more current the wire can carry and the lower its resistance.
- The size of a wire is usually expressed by its gauge, which is a standardized system of numbers that indicate the diameter or cross-sectional area of the wire.
- The most common gauge systems are the American Wire Gauge (AWG) and the Metric Wire Gauge (MWG).
- The AWG system uses a logarithmic scale, where the lower the gauge number, the larger the wire size. For example, a 10 AWG wire has a diameter of 2.588 mm and a cross-sectional area of 5.26 mm2, while a 20 AWG wire has a diameter of 0.812 mm and a cross-sectional area of 0.52 mm2.
- The MWG system uses a linear scale, where the gauge number is equal to the diameter of the wire in millimeters. For example, a 10 MWG wire has a diameter of 10 mm and a cross-sectional area of 78.54 mm2, while a 20 MWG wire has a diameter of 20 mm and a cross-sectional area of 314.16 mm2.

#### Shape
- The shape of a wire can be round, square, rectangular, or flat, depending on the application and the manufacturing process.
- Round wires are the most common and versatile shape, as they can be easily twisted, coiled, or bent into various forms.
- Square and rectangular wires are used for specialized purposes, such as winding coils, transformers, or electromagnets, where they can fit more closely and efficiently than round wires.
- Flat wires are used for ribbon cables, flexible printed circuits, or heating elements, where they can provide a large surface area and a thin profile.

#### Insulation
- The insulation of a wire is the material that covers the metal conductor to prevent electric shock, short circuit, or fire.
- The insulation of a wire can be made of various materials, such as rubber, plastic, PVC, Teflon, or fiberglass, depending on the voltage, temperature, and environmental conditions of the application.
- The insulation of a wire can also have different colors, which are used to identify the function or polarity of the wire. For example, in the US, black wires are usually used for hot wires, white wires for neutral wires, and green wires for grounding wires.
- The insulation of a wire can also have different ratings, which indicate the maximum voltage, current, or temperature that the wire can withstand. For example, a wire with a 600 V rating can handle up to 600 volts of electric potential, while a wire with a 90°C rating can operate up to 90 degrees Celsius of temperature.

#### Function
- The function of a wire is the purpose or role that the wire plays in an electrical circuit or system.
- The function of a wire can be classified by its type, such as power wire, signal wire, or ground wire.
- Power wires are used to carry electric current from the power source to the electrical devices, such as lights, appliances, or machines.
