## Unit 5 - Electrical Installations

- Electrical installations are the construction or installation of electrical wiring and the permanent attachment or installation of electrical products in or on any structure that is not itself an electrical product.
- Electrical installations also include the installation, repair, alteration and maintenance of electrical conductors, fittings, devices and fixtures for heating, lighting or power purposes, regardless of the voltage.
- Electrical installations must comply with the International Electrotechnical Commission (IEC) standards, which are the global standards for electrical engineering.
- Electrical installations require professional and licensed installers who are insured and background checked, to ensure safety and quality of work.
- Electrical installations can be classified into different types, such as residential, commercial, industrial, or public, depending on the purpose and location of the installation.
- Electrical installations can also be categorized by the voltage level, such as low voltage (LV), medium voltage (MV), or high voltage (HV), depending on the amount of electrical power transmitted or distributed.
- Electrical installations involve various components, such as wires, cables, switches, sockets, outlets, circuit breakers, fuses, transformers, generators, motors, lights, appliances, and more.
- Electrical installations must follow the principles of electrical engineering, such as Ohm's law, Kirchhoff's laws, power factor, voltage drop, resistance, capacitance, inductance, impedance, and more.
- Electrical installations must also adhere to the codes and regulations of the local authorities, such as the National Electrical Code (NEC) in the United States, the British Standard (BS) in the United Kingdom, or the Canadian Electrical Code (CEC) in Canada.
- Electrical installations are essential for providing electricity to homes, businesses, industries, and public facilities, and for enabling various electrical applications and functions.
