### Three phase balanced circuits

- A three phase balanced circuit is a type of alternating current (AC) power system that consists of three voltage sources and three loads connected in either star (Y) or delta (Δ) configuration.
- In a balanced system, each of the three instantaneous voltages have equal amplitudes but are separated from the other voltages by a phase angle of 120°. The three voltages (or phases) are typically labeled a, b and c .
- The advantages of using a three phase balanced circuit over a single phase circuit are:
  - Higher power transfer capability: A three phase circuit can deliver three times the power of a single phase circuit with the same voltage and current ratings.
  - Higher efficiency: A three phase circuit can reduce the conductor size and the power losses by using less copper and iron in the transmission lines and transformers.
  - Higher reliability: A three phase circuit can maintain the power supply even if one of the phases fails or is disconnected.
- The analysis of a three phase balanced circuit can be simplified by using the following steps :
  - Choose a reference phase, usually phase a, and draw the corresponding single phase equivalent circuit.
  - Apply Kirchhoff's voltage and current laws to the single phase circuit and solve for the unknown variables.
  - Use the phase relationships and the star-delta transformations to find the voltages and currents in the other phases and the neutral wire.
  - Use the power factor, the apparent power, the real power and the reactive power to calculate the total power delivered or consumed by the circuit.
- The power factor of a three phase balanced circuit is defined as the ratio of the real power to the apparent power. It is a measure of how efficiently the circuit uses the supplied power. A power factor of 1 indicates that the circuit only consumes real power and no reactive power. A power factor of 0 indicates that the circuit only consumes reactive power and no real power. A power factor between 0 and 1 indicates that the circuit consumes both real and reactive power.
- The apparent power of a three phase balanced circuit is the product of the line voltage and the line current. It is measured in volt-amperes (VA) or kilovolt-amperes (kVA). The apparent power is the total power that the circuit draws from the source.
- The real power of a three phase balanced circuit is the product of the line voltage, the line current and the power factor. It is measured in watts (W) or kilowatts (kW). The real power is the useful power that the circuit delivers to the load.
- The reactive power of a three phase balanced circuit is the product of the line voltage, the line current and the sine of the phase angle between the voltage and the current. It is measured in volt-amperes reactive (VAR) or kilovolt-amperes reactive (kVAR). The reactive power is the power that the circuit exchanges with the source due to the presence of inductors and capacitors.
