# ACB

- ACB stands for Air Circuit Breaker, which is an electrical device used to provide overcurrent and short-circuit protection for electric circuits over 800 Amps to 10kA   .
- ACB operates at atmospheric pressure in air, where air-blast as an arc quenching medium .
- ACB is used in low voltage applications below 450V, such as distribution panels , and also in high voltage applications up to 15kV.
- ACB has three main components: the contacts, the arc chute, and the operating mechanism .
- The contacts are the conductive parts that carry the current and open or close the circuit. The arc chute is the chamber that extinguishes the arc when the contacts separate. The operating mechanism is the device that controls the movement of the contacts .
- ACB can be classified into two types: plain air circuit breaker and air blast circuit breaker .
- Plain air circuit breaker uses the natural process of air flow to quench the arc. It has two pairs of contacts: the main contacts and the arcing contacts. The main contacts carry the normal current and open first. The arcing contacts carry the arc current and open later. The arc is drawn into the arc chute and cooled by the air .
- Air blast circuit breaker uses a blast of compressed air to quench the arc. It has only one pair of contacts that open and close rapidly. The arc is blown away by the air blast into the arc chute and extinguished .
- ACB has several advantages, such as high speed of operation, low maintenance, high reliability, and easy replacement of parts  .
- ACB also has some disadvantages, such as high cost, high noise, and high power consumption for the air compressor  .
- ACB is suitable for applications where frequent switching and high fault currents are expected, such as industrial plants, power stations, and substations  .
