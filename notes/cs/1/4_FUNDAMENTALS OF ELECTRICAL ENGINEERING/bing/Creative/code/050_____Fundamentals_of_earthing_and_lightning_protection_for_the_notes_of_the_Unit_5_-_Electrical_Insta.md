### Fundamentals of earthing and lightning protection

- Earthing and lightning protection are important for everyone that uses electrical equipment and that includes the large majority of the world.
- Earthing is the process of connecting the metallic parts of an electrical system or appliance to the earth. This provides a low-resistance path for fault currents and protects the system and the users from electric shock.
- Lightning protection is the process of intercepting or diverting lightning and providing a certain path for conducting the surges safely to the ground by adequate down conductors to grounding electrodes. This helps prevent disastrous events like fires, injuries, and deaths .
- The basic principles of earthing and lightning protection are:
  - Provide a low-impedance path for fault currents and lightning currents to the earth.
  - Minimize the potential difference between the earthed parts and the surrounding soil.
  - Avoid dangerous touch and step voltages for humans and animals.
  - Reduce electromagnetic interference and noise in the electrical system.
  - Enhance the performance and reliability of the electrical system .
- The types and components of earthing and lightning protection systems are:
  - Earthing electrodes: These are metal rods, plates, or wires that are buried in the soil and connected to the earthed parts of the electrical system. They provide a physical contact with the earth and dissipate the fault currents and lightning currents into the soil .
  - Earthing conductors: These are metal wires or cables that connect the earthing electrodes to the earthed parts of the electrical system. They provide a low-resistance path for the fault currents and lightning currents to flow to the earth .
  - Earthing bonds: These are metal connections that join the metallic parts of the electrical system that are not directly connected to the earthing conductors. They ensure that all the metallic parts are at the same potential and prevent circulating currents and sparks .
  - Lightning rods: These are metal rods or wires that are installed on the top of buildings or structures that are exposed to lightning strikes. They intercept the lightning and divert it to the earthing system .
  - Lightning down conductors: These are metal wires or cables that connect the lightning rods to the earthing system. They provide a low-impedance path for the lightning currents to flow to the earth .
  - Lightning air terminals: These are metal rods or wires that are installed along the edges or corners of buildings or structures that are exposed to lightning strikes. They provide additional protection to the lightning rods by creating a zone of protection around them .
  - Surge arresters: These are devices that are installed on the electrical system to limit the overvoltages caused by lightning or switching surges. They divert the excess voltage to the earthing system and protect the electrical equipment from damage .
