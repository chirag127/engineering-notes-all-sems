### Losses in Transformers

Transformers are devices that transfer electrical energy from one circuit to another by electromagnetic induction. They are used to increase or decrease the voltage and current levels of AC power systems. However, transformers are not ideal and they incur some losses during the energy conversion process. These losses reduce the efficiency and performance of the transformers. The main types of losses in transformers are:

- **Copper loss**: This is the power loss due to the resistance of the transformer windings. It is proportional to the square of the current flowing through the windings and the resistance of the windings. Copper loss can be reduced by using thicker wires or better conductors for the windings. Copper loss is also called ohmic loss, variable loss or resistive loss  .

- **Core loss**: This is the power loss due to the magnetization and demagnetization of the transformer core. It consists of two components: hysteresis loss and eddy current loss. Hysteresis loss is caused by the repeated reversal of the magnetic domains in the core material as the alternating magnetic flux changes direction. It depends on the type and quality of the core material, the frequency and the peak value of the magnetic flux. Eddy current loss is caused by the induced currents in the core due to the changing magnetic flux. It depends on the conductivity, thickness and shape of the core material, the frequency and the peak value of the magnetic flux. Core loss can be reduced by using low-loss core materials, laminating the core into thin sheets, and minimizing the air gaps in the core. Core loss is also called iron loss or constant loss   .

- **Stray loss**: This is the power loss due to the leakage of the magnetic flux from the transformer. It occurs when some of the magnetic flux does not link with both the primary and secondary windings, but instead escapes to the surrounding air or other nearby objects. It depends on the design and construction of the transformer, the load and the operating conditions. Stray loss can be reduced by using better insulation, shielding and cooling methods for the transformer. Stray loss is also called leakage loss  .

- **Dielectric loss**: This is the power loss due to the heating of the transformer insulation by the electric field. It occurs when the insulation material has some electrical conductivity or polarization, which causes it to dissipate some energy as heat when subjected to an alternating electric field. It depends on the type and quality of the insulation material, the frequency and the peak value of the electric field. Dielectric loss can be reduced by using low-loss insulation materials, minimizing the thickness and area of the insulation, and maintaining the insulation in a dry and clean condition. Dielectric loss is also called insulation loss .

The total loss in a transformer is the sum of the copper loss, the core loss, the stray loss and the dielectric loss. The efficiency of a transformer is the ratio of the output power to the input power, which is equal to one minus the ratio of the total loss to the input power. The efficiency of a transformer can be improved by minimizing the losses in the transformer. The losses in a transformer can be calculated by using various methods, such as the open-circuit test, the short-circuit test, the load test and the phasor diagram analysis  .
