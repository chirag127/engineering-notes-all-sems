### Ideal and Practical Transformer

- An ideal transformer is a hypothetical transformer that has no energy losses and is 100% efficient .
- A practical transformer is a real transformer that has some energy losses and is less than 100% efficient .
- The main differences between an ideal and practical transformer are:

| Ideal Transformer | Practical Transformer |
| ----------------- | --------------------- |
| The resistance of the windings is zero or negligible  | The resistance of the windings is finite and causes copper losses  |
| The magnetic circuit has infinite permeability and zero core loss | The magnetic circuit has finite permeability and causes hysteresis and eddy current losses |
| The leakage flux is zero or negligible | The leakage flux is finite and causes voltage drops and regulation problems |
| The efficiency is always 100% and does not depend on any parameter | The efficiency is less than 100% and depends on the power factor and loading of the transformer |
| The voltage ratio is equal to the turns ratio | The voltage ratio is not equal to the turns ratio due to voltage drops |

- An ideal transformer is used as a conceptual model for the analysis and design of a practical transformer.
- A practical transformer is used for various applications such as power transmission, voltage conversion, isolation, impedance matching, etc.
