# Types of Batteries

Batteries are devices that store electrical energy in the form of chemical energy and convert it into electrical current when needed. Batteries are widely used in electrical and electronic applications, such as mobile phones, laptops, cars, flashlights, etc.

There are mainly two categories of batteries: primary batteries and secondary batteries. A primary battery is a disposable kind of battery. Once used, it cannot be recharged. A secondary battery is a rechargeable battery. Once empty, it can be recharged again and again.

There are also other types of batteries, such as reserve cells, fuel cells, and flow batteries, that have different characteristics and applications.

## Primary Batteries

Primary batteries are also known as non-rechargeable batteries. They are designed to be used once and then discarded. They have a high energy density, meaning they can store a lot of energy in a small volume. They are also cheap and easy to manufacture. However, they have a limited shelf life and capacity, meaning they lose their charge over time and cannot provide a high current for a long time.

Some examples of primary batteries are:

- Alkaline batteries: These are the most common type of primary batteries. They use zinc and manganese dioxide as the electrodes and potassium hydroxide as the electrolyte. They have a nominal voltage of 1.5 V and can provide a steady current for a long time. They are used in many household devices, such as toys, clocks, radios, etc.
- Zinc-carbon batteries: These are the oldest type of primary batteries. They use zinc and carbon as the electrodes and ammonium chloride or zinc chloride as the electrolyte. They have a nominal voltage of 1.5 V and can provide a low current for a short time. They are used in low-power devices, such as flashlights, remote controls, etc.
- Lithium batteries: These are the most powerful type of primary batteries. They use lithium and various metal oxides as the electrodes and organic solvents as the electrolyte. They have a nominal voltage of 3 V and can provide a high current for a long time. They are used in high-performance devices, such as cameras, medical equipment, etc.

## Secondary Batteries

Secondary batteries are also known as rechargeable batteries. They are designed to be used multiple times by recharging them with an external power source. They have a lower energy density, meaning they can store less energy in a given volume. They are also more expensive and complex to manufacture. However, they have a longer shelf life and capacity, meaning they can retain their charge for a long time and can provide a high current for a long time.

Some examples of secondary batteries are:

- Lead-acid batteries: These are the oldest type of secondary batteries. They use lead and lead dioxide as the electrodes and sulfuric acid as the electrolyte. They have a nominal voltage of 2 V and can provide a high current for a short time. They are used in automotive applications, such as starting, lighting, and ignition (SLI) systems, as well as in backup power systems, such as uninterruptible power supplies (UPS).
- Nickel-cadmium batteries (Ni-Cd): These are the first type of secondary batteries to use metal electrodes. They use nickel and cadmium as the electrodes and potassium hydroxide as the electrolyte. They have a nominal voltage of 1.2 V and can provide a moderate current for a long time. They are used in portable applications, such as cordless phones, power tools, etc. However, they suffer from memory effect, meaning they lose their capacity if they are not fully discharged before recharging, and environmental issues, meaning they contain toxic cadmium that can pollute the soil and water.
- Nickel-metal hydride batteries (Ni-MH): These are the improved version of Ni-Cd batteries. They use nickel and a metal alloy as the electrodes and potassium hydroxide as the electrolyte. They have a nominal voltage of 1.2 V and can provide a high current for a long time. They are used in similar applications as Ni-Cd batteries, but they have a higher capacity, meaning they can store more energy, and a lower environmental impact, meaning they contain less toxic metals.
