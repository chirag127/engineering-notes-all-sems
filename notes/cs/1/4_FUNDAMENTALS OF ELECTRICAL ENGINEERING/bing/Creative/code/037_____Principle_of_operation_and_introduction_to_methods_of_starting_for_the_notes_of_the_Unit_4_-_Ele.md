### Principle of operation and introduction to methods of starting for the notes of the Unit 4 - Electrical machines in the subject of FUNDAMENTALS OF ELECTRICAL ENGINEERING

- Electrical machines are devices that convert electrical energy into mechanical energy or vice versa. They can be classified into two main categories: direct current (DC) machines and alternating current (AC) machines.
- DC machines operate on the principle of electromagnetic induction, which states that when a current-carrying conductor is placed in a magnetic field, it experiences a force that tends to rotate it. This force is called torque and it is proportional to the current, the magnetic field and the angle between them. The direction of the torque can be determined by Fleming's left-hand rule .
- DC machines can be further divided into DC generators and DC motors. DC generators convert mechanical energy into electrical energy by rotating a coil of wire in a magnetic field. The induced voltage in the coil depends on the speed of rotation, the number of turns and the magnetic flux. DC motors convert electrical energy into mechanical energy by supplying a current to a coil of wire that rotates in a magnetic field. The speed of rotation depends on the applied voltage, the load torque and the armature resistance.
- DC machines can have different types of field windings, which are coils of wire that produce the magnetic field. The field windings can be connected in series, parallel or separately with the armature, which is the rotating part of the machine. The field windings can also be replaced by permanent magnets, which are called permanent magnet DC (PMDC) machines.
- The methods of starting a DC machine depend on the type of field winding and the application. The most common methods are:
  - Direct-on-line (DOL) starting: This method involves connecting the machine directly to the supply voltage. It is simple and cheap, but it causes a high starting current and a high mechanical stress on the machine. It is suitable for small machines with low inertia and high resistance.
  - Resistance starting: This method involves inserting a variable resistance in series with the armature or the field winding. The resistance reduces the starting current and the torque, and it is gradually decreased as the machine reaches the desired speed. It is suitable for medium-sized machines with moderate inertia and low resistance.
  - Rheostatic starting: This method involves using a rheostat, which is a variable resistor that can be controlled manually or automatically. The rheostat is connected in series with the armature and it regulates the speed of the machine by varying the armature voltage. It is suitable for large machines with high inertia and low resistance.
  - Electronic starting: This method involves using an electronic device, such as a thyristor, a transistor or a chopper, to control the armature voltage and current. The electronic device can be programmed to provide a smooth and efficient starting of the machine. It is suitable for any size of machine and it can also provide speed control and braking.

- AC machines operate on the principle of rotating magnetic field, which states that when a set of coils are supplied with alternating currents that are out of phase with each other, they produce a magnetic field that rotates at a constant speed. The speed of the rotating magnetic field depends on the frequency of the supply and the number of poles of the machine. The direction of the rotation can be reversed by changing the phase sequence of the supply.
- AC machines can be further divided into synchronous machines and induction machines. Synchronous machines have a rotor that rotates at the same speed as the rotating magnetic field. The rotor can be a permanent magnet, a wound field or a salient pole. Synchronous machines can operate as generators or motors, depending on the direction of the power flow. Synchronous generators produce a constant voltage and frequency, and they are used in power plants and grid applications. Synchronous motors have a constant speed and a high power factor, and they are used in applications that require precise speed control and high efficiency.
- Induction machines have a rotor that rotates at a slightly lower speed than the rotating magnetic field. The rotor can be a squirrel cage or a wound rotor. Induction machines operate as motors only, and they are the most widely used type of AC machines. Induction motors have a simple and robust construction, a low cost and a high reliability. They are used in applications that require variable speed and torque, such as fans, pumps, compressors and conveyors.
