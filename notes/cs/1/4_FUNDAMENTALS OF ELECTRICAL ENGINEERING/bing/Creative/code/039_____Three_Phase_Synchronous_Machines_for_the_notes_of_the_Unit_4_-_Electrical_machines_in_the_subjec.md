### Three Phase Synchronous Machines

- A three phase synchronous machine is an AC machine that operates at a constant speed and converts mechanical energy into electrical energy or vice versa .
- A three phase synchronous machine consists of two main parts: a stator and a rotor .
- The stator is the stationary part of the machine that carries a three phase winding, which is connected to the AC supply. The stator winding produces a rotating magnetic field that rotates at a synchronous speed .
- The rotor is the rotating part of the machine that carries a DC field winding, which is excited by a DC source. The rotor field produces a constant magnetic field that aligns with the stator field .
- The rotor can be either a salient pole type or a cylindrical type. A salient pole rotor has projecting poles that create a non-uniform air gap between the stator and the rotor. A cylindrical rotor has a smooth surface that creates a uniform air gap.
- A three phase synchronous machine can operate as a generator or a motor, depending on the direction of the power flow. A synchronous generator converts mechanical power from a prime mover into electrical power. A synchronous motor converts electrical power from the AC supply into mechanical power.
- A three phase synchronous machine has the following advantages:
  - It can operate at high efficiency and power factor.
  - It can control the voltage and frequency of the AC supply.
  - It can provide reactive power support to the power system.
- A three phase synchronous machine has the following disadvantages:
  - It requires a separate DC source for field excitation.
  - It has a high starting current and torque.
  - It has a poor speed regulation and cannot adjust to load changes.
- A three phase synchronous machine can be started by the following methods:
  - Using a separate induction motor or a DC motor to bring the rotor to the synchronous speed.
  - Using a damper winding or a cage winding on the rotor to create an induction torque.
  - Using a variable frequency converter to vary the supply frequency and voltage .
