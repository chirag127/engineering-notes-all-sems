# Magnetic circuits

- A magnetic circuit is a closed path to which a magnetic field, represented as lines of magnetic flux, is confined .
- A magnetic circuit is usually made up of one or more loops containing a magnetic flux, which is generated by permanent magnets or electromagnets and confined to the path by magnetic cores consisting of ferromagnetic materials like iron.
- Some examples of magnetic circuits are: horseshoe magnet with iron keeper (low-reluctance circuit), horseshoe magnet with no keeper (high-reluctance circuit), electric motor (variable-reluctance circuit), and some types of pickup cartridge (variable-reluctance circuits).
- A magnetic circuit can be analyzed using the analogy of an electric circuit, where the magnetic flux is analogous to the electric current, the magnetic potential difference (or magnetomotive force) is analogous to the voltage, and the magnetic reluctance is analogous to the resistance .
- The basic equation for a magnetic circuit is:

  $$\Phi = \frac{F}{R}$$

  where $\Phi$ is the magnetic flux, $F$ is the magnetic potential difference, and $R$ is the magnetic reluctance .
- The magnetic potential difference $F$ is the product of the current $I$ and the number of turns $N$ of the coil that produces the magnetic field, i.e., $F = NI$ .
- The magnetic reluctance $R$ is the ratio of the magnetic potential difference $F$ to the magnetic flux $\Phi$, i.e., $R = \frac{F}{\Phi}$ .
- The magnetic reluctance $R$ depends on the length $l$, the cross-sectional area $A$, and the permeability $\mu$ of the magnetic material, i.e., $R = \frac{l}{\mu A}$ .
- The permeability $\mu$ is a measure of how easily a magnetic material can be magnetized. It is the product of the relative permeability $\mu_r$ and the permeability of free space $\mu_0$, i.e., $\mu = \mu_r \mu_0$ .
- The permeability of free space $\mu_0$ is a constant value of $4\pi \times 10^{-7}$ H/m. The relative permeability $\mu_r$ is a dimensionless quantity that varies for different materials. For air, $\mu_r = 1$. For ferromagnetic materials, $\mu_r$ is much greater than 1 .
- A magnetic circuit may have more than one loop or branch, in which case the total magnetic flux is the sum of the fluxes in each loop or branch, and the total magnetic reluctance is the equivalent reluctance of the parallel or series combination of the reluctances in each loop or branch .
- A magnetic circuit may also have air gaps or other materials in the path, which increase the magnetic reluctance and reduce the magnetic flux .
- A magnetic circuit can be used to model the behavior of devices such as transformers and relays, which use magnetic fields to transfer energy or control signals.
