# Unit 1 - DC Circuits

## Introduction

A DC circuit is a circuit that uses direct current (DC) as the source of electric power. DC is the type of current that flows in one direction only, unlike alternating current (AC) that changes direction periodically. DC circuits are commonly used in batteries, solar cells, and electronic devices.

## DC Current

DC current is the unidirectional flow of electric charge. The amount of charge that passes through a point in a circuit per unit time is called the electric current, measured in amperes (A). The direction of the current is defined as the direction that positive charges would flow, from the positive terminal to the negative terminal of the source.

## DC Circuit Types

There are three main types of DC circuits: series, parallel, and series-parallel. Each type has different characteristics and rules for calculating the voltage, current, and resistance in the circuit.

### Series DC Circuit

A series DC circuit is a circuit where the components are connected end to end, forming a single path for the current to flow. The current is the same in every component, and the total voltage across the circuit is equal to the sum of the voltages across each component. The total resistance in the circuit is equal to the sum of the resistances of each component.

### Parallel DC Circuit

A parallel DC circuit is a circuit where the components are connected in separate branches, forming multiple paths for the current to flow. The voltage is the same across every branch, and the total current in the circuit is equal to the sum of the currents in each branch. The total resistance in the circuit is equal to the reciprocal of the sum of the reciprocals of the resistances of each branch.

### Series-Parallel DC Circuit

A series-parallel DC circuit is a circuit that combines both series and parallel connections of components. The circuit can be simplified by applying the rules for series and parallel circuits to each sub-circuit, and then combining the results to find the total voltage, current, and resistance in the circuit.

## DC Circuit Formulas

The following formulas can be used to calculate the voltage, current, and resistance in DC circuits:

- Ohm's law: V = IR, where V is the voltage, I is the current, and R is the resistance.
- Kirchhoff's voltage law: The sum of the voltages around any closed loop in a circuit is zero.
- Kirchhoff's current law: The sum of the currents entering any node in a circuit is equal to the sum of the currents leaving that node.
- Series circuit: V<sub>T</sub> = V<sub>1</sub> + V<sub>2</sub> + ... + V<sub>n</sub>, I<sub>T</sub> = I<sub>1</sub> = I<sub>2</sub> = ... = I<sub>n</sub>, R<sub>T</sub> = R<sub>1</sub> + R<sub>2</sub> + ... + R<sub>n</sub>
- Parallel circuit: V<sub>T</sub> = V<sub>1</sub> = V<sub>2</sub> = ... = V<sub>n</sub>, I<sub>T</sub> = I<sub>1</sub> + I<sub>2</sub> + ... + I<sub>n</sub>, 1/R<sub>T</sub> = 1/R<sub>1</sub> + 1/R<sub>2</sub> + ... + 1/R<sub>n</sub>
