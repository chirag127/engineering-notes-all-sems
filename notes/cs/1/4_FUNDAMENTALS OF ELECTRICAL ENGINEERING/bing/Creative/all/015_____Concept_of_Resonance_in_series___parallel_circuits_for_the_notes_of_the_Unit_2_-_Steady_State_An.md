# Concept of Resonance in Series and Parallel Circuits

- Resonance is a condition that occurs in AC circuits when the reactive power of the capacitors and inductors become equal.
- Resonance can occur in both series and parallel circuits, but the characteristics and effects are different.
- In a series circuit, resonance occurs when the inductive reactance (XL) and the capacitive reactance (XC) are equal in magnitude, but opposite in phase. This results in a minimum impedance (Z) and a maximum current (I) at the resonant frequency (fr).
- In a parallel circuit, resonance occurs when the admittance (Y) of the inductor and the capacitor are equal in magnitude, but opposite in phase. This results in a maximum impedance (Z) and a minimum current (I) at the resonant frequency (fr).
- The series resonance circuit is also known as the acceptor circuit, because it accepts the maximum current at the resonant frequency and rejects the current at other frequencies. The parallel resonance circuit is also known as the rejector circuit, because it rejects the current at the resonant frequency and accepts the current at other frequencies.
- The series resonance circuit magnifies the voltage across the capacitor and the inductor, while the parallel resonance circuit magnifies the current through the capacitor and the inductor.
- The quality factor (Q) of a resonant circuit is a measure of how sharp the resonance is. It is defined as the ratio of the resonant frequency to the bandwidth, where the bandwidth is the range of frequencies for which the current or voltage is above a certain fraction of the maximum value.

- The Q of a resonant circuit affects the selectivity and the power loss. A higher Q means a narrower bandwidth, a higher selectivity, and a lower power loss. A lower Q means a wider bandwidth, a lower selectivity, and a higher power loss.

- The resonance frequency of a circuit can be changed by varying the capacitance or the inductance. This can be done by using variable capacitors or inductors, or by using switches or relays to connect or disconnect different values of capacitors or inductors.
