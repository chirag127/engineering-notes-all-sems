# Single Phase Induction Motor

A single phase induction motor is a type of electric motor that operates on single phase alternating current. It is similar to a three phase induction motor in construction, except that the stator has only one winding instead of three. The rotor is usually a squirrel cage type, which rotates freely in the air gap between the stator and the rotor.

## Construction of Single Phase Induction Motor

The main parts of a single phase induction motor are:

- **Stator**: The stator is the stationary part of the motor, which consists of a laminated steel core with slots. The slots contain the stator winding, which is connected to the single phase AC supply. The stator winding produces a pulsating magnetic field, which does not rotate.
- **Rotor**: The rotor is the rotating part of the motor, which consists of a cylindrical steel core with slots. The slots contain the rotor bars, which are short-circuited at both ends by end rings. The rotor bars and end rings form a squirrel cage, which acts as a secondary winding of a transformer. The rotor is mounted on a shaft, which is supported by bearings at both ends.
- **End shields**: The end shields are the metal covers that enclose the stator and the rotor. They also provide the mounting base for the motor and the bearing housings.
- **Fan**: The fan is attached to the rotor shaft at one end, which provides cooling for the motor by blowing air over the stator and the rotor.

## Working Principle of Single Phase Induction Motor

The working principle of a single phase induction motor is based on the concept of a rotating magnetic field, which is produced by two or more alternating currents that are out of phase with each other. However, a single phase AC supply cannot produce a rotating magnetic field by itself. Therefore, some methods are used to create a phase difference between the currents in the stator winding, which results in a rotating magnetic field.

The most common methods of creating a phase difference in a single phase induction motor are:

- **Split-phase method**: In this method, the stator winding is split into two parts: a main winding and an auxiliary winding. The main winding is connected directly to the single phase AC supply, while the auxiliary winding is connected through a capacitor or a resistor. The capacitor or the resistor introduces a phase shift between the currents in the two windings, which creates a rotating magnetic field. The auxiliary winding is only used for starting the motor, and is disconnected by a centrifugal switch or a relay once the motor reaches a certain speed. This type of motor is called a split-phase motor or a capacitor-start motor.
- **Permanent-split capacitor method**: In this method, the stator winding is also split into two parts: a main winding and an auxiliary winding. However, the auxiliary winding is permanently connected to the single phase AC supply through a capacitor, which creates a phase shift between the currents in the two windings. The capacitor also reduces the current in the auxiliary winding, which improves the efficiency and the power factor of the motor. This type of motor is called a permanent-split capacitor motor or a capacitor-run motor.
- **Shaded-pole method**: In this method, the stator winding is not split, but has some slots that are partially covered by copper rings or bands. These copper rings or bands are called shaded poles, which act as short-circuited secondary windings. The shaded poles create a phase shift between the currents in the shaded and unshaded parts of the stator winding, which produces a rotating magnetic field. This type of motor is called a shaded-pole motor.

In all these methods, the rotating magnetic field induces an electromotive force (emf) in the rotor bars, which causes a current to flow in the rotor circuit. The current in the rotor circuit interacts with the magnetic field in the air gap, which produces a torque on the rotor. The torque causes the rotor to rotate in the same direction as the magnetic field. The speed of the rotor is slightly less than the synchronous speed of the magnetic field, which is called the slip speed. The slip speed is proportional to the load on the motor.
