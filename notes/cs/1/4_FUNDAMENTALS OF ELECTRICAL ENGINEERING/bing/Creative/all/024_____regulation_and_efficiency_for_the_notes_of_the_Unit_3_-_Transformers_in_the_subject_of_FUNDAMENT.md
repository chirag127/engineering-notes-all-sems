# Regulation and Efficiency of Transformers

- Regulation of a transformer is the measure of how well the output voltage of the transformer remains constant under varying load conditions.
- Regulation is expressed as a percentage of the no-load voltage, and is calculated as:

`% Regulation = [(VNL - VFL) / VFL] x 100`

where VNL is the no-load voltage, and VFL is the full-load voltage.

- A transformer with low regulation has a small change in output voltage when the load changes, and is desirable for most applications.
- A transformer with high regulation has a large change in output voltage when the load changes, and may cause problems for sensitive loads.

- Efficiency of a transformer is the ratio of the output power to the input power, expressed as a percentage.
- Efficiency is calculated as:

`% Efficiency = (Pout / Pin) x 100`

where Pout is the output power, and Pin is the input power.

- A transformer with high efficiency has a small loss of power in the form of heat, and is desirable for energy saving and environmental reasons.
- A transformer with low efficiency has a large loss of power in the form of heat, and may cause overheating and fire hazards.

- The efficiency of a transformer depends on several factors, such as the design, the materials, the frequency, the load, and the ambient temperature.
- The efficiency of a transformer is not constant, but varies with the load. The efficiency is maximum at a certain load, called the optimum load, which is usually around 50% to 75% of the rated load.

- The Department of Energy (DOE) has established minimum efficiency standards for transformers sold in the United States, to improve the reliability of the power grid, lower the electricity costs, and reduce the greenhouse gas emissions.
- The DOE 2016 efficiency standards are the latest and most stringent requirements, which apply to three categories of distribution transformers: low-voltage dry-type, liquid-immersed, and medium-voltage dry-type.
- The DOE 2016 efficiency standards increase the minimum efficiency levels from the previous DOE 2010 standards, which were already higher than the international standards. The DOE 2016 standards aim for 98.70% to 99.55% transformer efficiency ratings, depending on the type and size of the transformer.
