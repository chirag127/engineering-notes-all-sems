# Principle of operation of alternator and synchronous motor

- An **alternator** or **synchronous generator** is a device that converts mechanical energy into electrical energy by producing alternating current (AC).
- A **synchronous motor** is a device that converts electrical energy into mechanical energy by rotating at a constant speed in synchronism with the AC supply frequency.
- Both devices work on the principle of **electromagnetic induction**, i.e., when the flux linking a conductor changes, an EMF is induced in the conductor .

## Alternator

- An alternator consists of a **stator** and a **rotor**. The stator is the stationary part that contains a set of coils called the **armature winding**. The rotor is the rotating part that contains a set of magnets or electromagnets called the **field winding**.
- The rotor is driven by a prime mover, such as a turbine, engine, or water wheel, and creates a **rotating magnetic field** that induces an EMF in the stator coils.
- The EMF generated in the stator coils depends on the **speed of rotation** of the rotor, the **number of poles** on the rotor, the **strength of the magnetic field** on the rotor, and the **number of turns** on the stator coils.
- The EMF generated in the stator coils is **alternating** in nature, i.e., it changes its direction and magnitude periodically. The frequency of the EMF is equal to the **product of the speed of rotation and the number of poles** divided by 120.

## Synchronous motor

- A synchronous motor consists of a **stator** and a **rotor**. The stator is the stationary part that contains a set of coils called the **stator winding**. The rotor is the rotating part that contains a set of magnets or electromagnets called the **rotor winding**.
- The stator winding is connected to a **three-phase AC supply** that creates a **rotating magnetic field** in the stator. The rotor winding is connected to a **DC supply** that creates a **constant magnetic field** in the rotor.
- The rotor is attracted by the stator magnetic field and tries to align itself with it. When the rotor reaches the **synchronous speed**, i.e., the speed at which the stator magnetic field rotates, the rotor locks in with the stator magnetic field and rotates at a **constant speed**.
- The speed of the synchronous motor depends on the **frequency of the AC supply** and the **number of poles** on the stator and the rotor. The speed of the synchronous motor is equal to the **product of the frequency and 120** divided by the **number of poles** .
