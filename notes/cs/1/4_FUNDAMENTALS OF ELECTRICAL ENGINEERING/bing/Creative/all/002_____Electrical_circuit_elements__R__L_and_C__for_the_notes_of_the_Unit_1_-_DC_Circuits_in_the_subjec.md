# Electrical circuit elements (R, L and C)

- An **electrical circuit** is a closed loop of conductors and components that allows the flow of electric current and energy.
- An **electrical circuit element** is a device or component that has a specific function or property in a circuit.
- The most fundamental and passive electrical circuit elements are **resistor (R)**, **inductor (L)**, and **capacitor (C)**.
- A **resistor** is a circuit element that **opposes** the flow of current and **dissipates** electrical energy as heat. The resistance of a resistor is measured in **ohms (Ω)** and is given by the ratio of voltage across it to the current through it, according to **Ohm's law**.
- An **inductor** is a circuit element that **stores** electrical energy in a **magnetic field** when current flows through it. The inductance of an inductor is measured in **henries (H)** and is given by the ratio of the magnetic flux linkage to the current, according to **Faraday's law**.
- A **capacitor** is a circuit element that **stores** electrical energy in an **electric field** between two conductive plates separated by an insulator. The capacitance of a capacitor is measured in **farads (F)** and is given by the ratio of the electric charge on the plates to the voltage across them, according to **Gauss's law**.
- An **RLC circuit** is an electrical circuit that consists of a resistor, an inductor, and a capacitor, connected in **series** or in **parallel**. The name of the circuit is derived from the letters that are used to denote the constituent components of this circuit, where the sequence of the components may vary from RLC.
- An RLC circuit can exhibit **resonance**, which is a phenomenon where the circuit responds with maximum amplitude to an input signal of a specific frequency, called the **resonant frequency**. The resonant frequency of an RLC circuit depends on the values of the resistance, inductance, and capacitance, and whether the circuit is series or parallel.
- An RLC circuit can also exhibit **transient** and **steady-state** behaviors, which are the responses of the circuit to a change in the input signal or the initial conditions. The transient behavior is the short-term response that occurs immediately after the change, while the steady-state behavior is the long-term response that occurs after the transient behavior has died out.
- The analysis of RLC circuits involves applying **Kirchhoff's laws**, which are the laws of conservation of charge and energy in a circuit, and solving the resulting **differential equations** that describe the voltage and current in the circuit elements. The solutions can be expressed in terms of **complex numbers**, **phasors**, or **Laplace transforms**, depending on the type and frequency of the input signal.
