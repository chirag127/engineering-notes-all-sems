## Introduction

Electrical machines are devices that convert electrical energy into mechanical energy or vice versa. They are classified into two main categories: direct current (DC) machines and alternating current (AC) machines. DC machines operate on a constant voltage and current, while AC machines operate on a sinusoidal voltage and current that vary in magnitude and direction. DC machines are mainly used for applications that require speed control, such as electric vehicles, elevators, and cranes. AC machines are mainly used for applications that require constant speed, such as fans, pumps, and compressors.

## Principle of operation

The principle of operation of electrical machines is based on the interaction between electric currents and magnetic fields. When a current-carrying conductor is placed in a magnetic field, it experiences a force that is perpendicular to both the current and the field. This force is called the Lorentz force and it can be used to rotate the conductor or the magnetic field. The direction of the force can be determined by the right-hand rule: if the thumb, index finger, and middle finger of the right hand are aligned with the current, field, and force respectively, then the fingers form a right angle.

In a DC machine, the magnetic field is produced by permanent magnets or electromagnets that are fixed to the stator (the stationary part of the machine). The current is supplied to the rotor (the rotating part of the machine) through brushes and commutators that switch the direction of the current as the rotor rotates. This ensures that the torque (the rotational force) on the rotor is always in the same direction and the rotor can rotate continuously.

In an AC machine, the magnetic field is produced by alternating currents that are supplied to the stator through a three-phase or single-phase power source. The rotor can be either a squirrel-cage type or a wound type. A squirrel-cage rotor consists of copper or aluminum bars that are short-circuited by end rings. A wound rotor consists of coils that are connected to slip rings and brushes. The current in the rotor is induced by the magnetic field of the stator through electromagnetic induction. The torque on the rotor depends on the phase difference between the stator and rotor currents. The rotor can rotate synchronously (at the same speed as the stator field) or asynchronously (at a different speed than the stator field).

## Methods of starting

The methods of starting electrical machines depend on the type and size of the machine, the load characteristics, and the power supply. Some of the common methods of starting are:

- Full voltage or direct-on-line (DOL) starting: This is the simplest and most economical method of starting, where the machine is directly connected to the full voltage of the power supply. This method is suitable for small and medium-sized machines that have low starting currents and high starting torques. However, this method can cause high inrush currents, voltage dips, and mechanical stresses on the machine and the load.

- Reduced voltage or star-delta (Y-Δ) starting: This is a method of starting where the stator windings of a three-phase AC machine are initially connected in a star (Y) configuration and then switched to a delta (Δ) configuration after the machine reaches a certain speed. This method reduces the starting current and voltage by a factor of √3, but also reduces the starting torque by a factor of 3. This method is suitable for medium and large-sized machines that have high starting currents and low starting torques.

- Soft starting: This is a method of starting where the voltage and frequency of the power supply are gradually increased from zero to the rated values using a device called a soft starter. This method reduces the starting current and voltage, and provides a smooth acceleration of the machine and the load. This method is suitable for large and sensitive machines that require a gentle start.

- Variable frequency drive (VFD) starting: This is a method of starting where the voltage and frequency of the power supply are varied according to the speed and torque requirements of the machine and the load using a device called a variable frequency drive. This method provides the best control over the starting current, voltage, torque, and speed of the machine and the load. This method is suitable for applications that require variable speed and torque, such as fans, pumps, and conveyors.
