# Kirchhoff's laws for DC circuits

Kirchhoff's laws are two principles that govern the analysis of electric circuits. They are named after Gustav Kirchhoff, a German physicist who formulated them in 1845. They are:

- Kirchhoff's current law (KCL): The algebraic sum of the currents entering and leaving any node in a circuit is zero. This means that the total charge flowing into a node is equal to the total charge flowing out of it. This law is also known as the junction rule or the conservation of charge principle.
- Kirchhoff's voltage law (KVL): The algebraic sum of the voltages around any closed loop in a circuit is zero. This means that the total energy gained or lost by the charges in a loop is zero. This law is also known as the loop rule or the conservation of energy principle.

## Applications of Kirchhoff's laws

Kirchhoff's laws are used to find:

- The values of current, voltage and internal resistance in DC circuits.
- The unknown resistance in a circuit using methods such as Wheatstone bridge, mesh analysis or node analysis.
- The equivalent resistance of a complex circuit by simplifying it into series and parallel combinations of resistors.
- The power dissipated or delivered by each element in a circuit.

## Limitations of Kirchhoff's laws

Kirchhoff's laws are valid only for:

- Linear circuits, where the voltage and current are proportional to each other for each element.
- Time-invariant circuits, where the values of the elements do not change with time.
- Lumped circuits, where the physical dimensions of the elements are negligible compared to the wavelength of the signals.

Kirchhoff's laws do not apply to:

- Nonlinear circuits, where the voltage and current are not proportional to each other for some elements, such as diodes, transistors, etc.
- Time-varying circuits, where the values of the elements change with time, such as capacitors, inductors, etc.
- Distributed circuits, where the physical dimensions of the elements are comparable to or larger than the wavelength of the signals, such as transmission lines, antennas, etc.
