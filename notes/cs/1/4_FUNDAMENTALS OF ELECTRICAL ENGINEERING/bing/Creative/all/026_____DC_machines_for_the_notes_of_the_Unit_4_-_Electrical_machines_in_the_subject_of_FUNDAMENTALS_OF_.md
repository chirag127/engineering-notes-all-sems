# DC Machines

## Introduction

- A DC machine is an electro-mechanical energy conversion device that can either convert mechanical power to electrical power (DC generator) or electrical power to mechanical power (DC motor).
- The principle of operation of a DC machine is based on the interaction of a current-carrying conductor and a magnetic field.
- A DC machine consists of two main parts: a stationary part called the stator and a rotating part called the rotor.
- The stator provides the magnetic field by means of permanent magnets or electromagnets, and the rotor carries the conductors that cut the magnetic field and induce the emf or torque.
- The rotor conductors are connected to a commutator, which is a device that reverses the direction of the current in the conductors every half rotation, so that the output voltage or torque is unidirectional.

## Construction of a DC Machine

- The main components of a DC machine are:

  - **Yoke**: The outer frame of the machine that supports and protects the other parts. It is usually made of cast iron or steel. It also acts as a part of the magnetic circuit and provides a low reluctance path for the magnetic flux.
  - **Poles**: The projections on the inner side of the yoke that carry the field windings. They are usually made of laminated steel to reduce eddy current losses. They have pole shoes at their ends to increase the area of contact with the air gap and distribute the flux uniformly.
  - **Field windings**: The coils of wire wound on the poles that produce the magnetic field when energized by a DC source. They can be connected in series or parallel with the armature winding, depending on the type of the machine.
  - **Armature**: The rotating part of the machine that carries the armature windings. It is usually made of laminated steel to reduce eddy current losses. It has slots on its surface to accommodate the armature conductors.
  - **Armature windings**: The coils of wire wound on the armature slots that cut the magnetic field and induce the emf or torque. They are connected in series-parallel arrangement to form a closed loop. They are also connected to the commutator segments by means of brushes.
  - **Commutator**: A cylindrical device mounted on the shaft of the armature that consists of a number of segments (equal to the number of armature coils) insulated from each other. It reverses the direction of the current in the armature coils every half rotation, so that the output voltage or torque is unidirectional.
  - **Brushes**: The sliding contacts that connect the armature windings to the external circuit. They are usually made of carbon or graphite and are pressed against the commutator segments by means of springs. They collect the current from the commutator and transfer it to the load.

## Types of DC Machines

- DC machines can be classified into three types based on the connection of the field windings to the armature windings:

  - **Series DC machine**: The field winding is connected in series with the armature winding. The current in the field winding is the same as the current in the armature winding. The field flux is proportional to the armature current. This type of machine has a high starting torque and a variable speed. It is used for applications that require high torque at low speed, such as cranes, hoists, traction, etc.
  - **Shunt DC machine**: The field winding is connected in parallel with the armature winding. The current in the field winding is independent of the current in the armature winding. The field flux is constant and independent of the load. This type of machine has a low starting torque and a constant speed. It is used for applications that require constant speed and low torque, such as fans, blowers, pumps, etc.
  - **Compound DC machine**: The field winding is divided into two parts: a series field winding and a shunt field winding. The series field winding is connected in series with the armature winding, and the shunt field winding is connected in parallel with the armature winding. The field flux is the sum of the series field flux and the shunt field flux. This type of machine has a moderate starting torque and a variable speed. It is used for applications that require a combination of high torque and constant speed, such as generators, compressors, elevators, etc.
