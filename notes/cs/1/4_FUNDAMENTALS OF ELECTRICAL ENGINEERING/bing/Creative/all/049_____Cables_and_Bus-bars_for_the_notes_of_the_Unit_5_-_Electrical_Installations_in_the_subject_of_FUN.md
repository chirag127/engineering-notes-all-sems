# Cables and Bus-bars

## Introduction

- Cables and bus-bars are two methods of transporting electrical energy in distribution systems.
- Cables are insulated conductors that are usually made of copper or aluminum and can be run in trays or conduit.
- Bus-bars are metal bars that are usually made of copper or aluminum and are used to connect power sources and loads in an enclosure.
- Both cables and bus-bars have advantages and disadvantages depending on the application and design requirements.

## Comparison of Cables and Bus-bars

- Cost: Bus-bars are generally cheaper per ampere carried than cables, as they have no insulation and are not made of thin strands. However, the cost of installation and maintenance may vary depending on the complexity and accessibility of the system.
- Space: Bus-bars use less space than cables, as they have a compact design and do not require large bend radii. This is especially beneficial when dealing with high currents and voltages. Cables may take up more space and create more clutter in the system.
- Reliability: Bus-bars are more reliable than cables, as they have less resistance and voltage drop, and are less prone to overheating and short circuits. Cables may have more losses and faults due to insulation damage, corrosion, or mechanical stress.
- Flexibility: Cables are more flexible than bus-bars, as they can be easily routed and modified to suit different layouts and configurations. Bus-bars are more rigid and fixed, and may require more planning and engineering to change or expand the system.
- Safety: Bus-bars are safer than cables, as they are enclosed and isolated from the environment and human contact. Cables may pose more hazards such as electric shock, fire, or electromagnetic interference.
