# Concept of active and passive elements

- Active and passive elements are the two main types of electronic circuit elements that have different characteristics and functions.
- Active elements are the ones that can supply energy to a circuit or control the flow of current in a circuit. They can also provide amplification or gain for electronic signals. Examples of active elements are batteries, generators, transistors, op-amps, etc.
- Passive elements are the ones that can only receive, store, or dissipate energy in a circuit. They cannot control the current direction or provide amplification. They are mainly used for energy storage, discharge, oscillation, filtering, etc. Examples of passive elements are resistors, capacitors, inductors, etc.
- The main difference between active and passive elements is that active elements can deliver more power to a circuit than they receive, while passive elements can only deliver less power than they receive.
- Some of the criteria to distinguish between active and passive elements are:

| Criteria | Active Element | Passive Element |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Energy source | Has an internal or external energy source | Does not have an energy source |
| Current direction | Can control the current direction | Cannot control the current direction |
| Power gain | Can provide power gain for signals | Cannot provide power gain for signals |
| Function | Used for current and voltage control, signal processing, etc. | Used for energy storage, discharge, oscillation, filtering, etc. |
| Symbol | Usually has an arrow to indicate the direction of current or voltage | Usually does not have an arrow |

- The concept of active and passive elements is important for understanding the behavior and analysis of electrical circuits. Active elements can modify the input signals and perform various functions, while passive elements can store or release energy and affect the impedance and frequency response of the circuit.
