# Principle and Construction of Electrical Machines

Electrical machines are devices that convert mechanical energy to electrical energy and vice versa. They are classified into two main categories: direct current (DC) machines and alternating current (AC) machines. DC machines operate on direct current, while AC machines operate on alternating current.

## DC Machines

DC machines are further divided into DC generators and DC motors. DC generators produce direct current by converting mechanical power to electrical power, while DC motors consume direct current by converting electrical power to mechanical power.

### Principle of DC Machines

The working principle of a DC machine is based on the Lorentz force law, which states that when a current-carrying conductor is placed in a magnetic field, it experiences a force that is perpendicular to both the current and the magnetic field. This force causes the conductor to rotate, which is called torque. The direction of the force can be determined by Fleming's left-hand rule for motors and Fleming's right-hand rule for generators.

### Construction of DC Machines

The main components of a DC machine are:

- Yoke: The outer frame of the machine that supports and protects the other parts. It is usually made of cast iron or steel. It also provides a low reluctance path for the magnetic flux.
- Poles: The parts of the machine that produce the magnetic field. They consist of pole cores and pole shoes. The pole cores are made of laminated steel and carry the field coils. The pole shoes are attached to the pole cores and spread the flux over the armature.
- Field coils: The coils of wire that carry direct current and generate the magnetic flux. They are wound on the pole cores and connected in series or parallel.
- Armature: The rotating part of the machine that carries the armature conductors. It consists of armature core and armature winding. The armature core is made of laminated steel and provides a low reluctance path for the flux. The armature winding is the arrangement of conductors that carry the induced current in generators or the input current in motors. The conductors are insulated from each other and from the core.
- Commutator: The cylindrical device that connects the armature winding to the external circuit. It consists of segments of copper or brass that are insulated from each other and from the shaft. The commutator reverses the direction of the current in the armature conductors every half rotation, so that the torque remains constant.
- Brushes: The stationary contacts that slide on the commutator and transfer the current between the armature and the external circuit. They are usually made of carbon or graphite and are spring-loaded to maintain contact with the commutator.

## AC Machines

AC machines are further divided into transformers, induction machines, synchronous machines, and special machines. Transformers are static devices that transfer electrical power from one circuit to another by electromagnetic induction. Induction machines are asynchronous machines that operate on the principle of electromagnetic induction. Synchronous machines are machines that operate at a constant speed and are synchronized with the supply frequency. Special machines are machines that have specific applications or characteristics, such as stepper motors, brushless DC motors, switched reluctance motors, etc.

### Principle of AC Machines

The working principle of an AC machine is based on the Faraday's law of electromagnetic induction, which states that when a conductor is moved in a magnetic field, an electromotive force (emf) is induced in it. The direction of the induced emf can be determined by Lenz's law, which states that the induced emf opposes the cause of its production. The magnitude of the induced emf depends on the rate of change of flux linkage, which is the product of the flux and the number of turns of the conductor.

### Construction of AC Machines

The main components of an AC machine are:

- Stator: The stationary part of the machine that carries the stator winding. It is usually made of laminated steel and provides a low reluctance path for the magnetic flux.
- Rotor: The rotating part of the machine that carries the rotor winding or the rotor bars. It is also made of laminated steel and may have salient or non-salient poles. The rotor winding or the rotor bars are connected to slip rings or short-circuited at the ends.
- Stator winding: The arrangement of coils that carry alternating current and generate the rotating magnetic field. The stator winding is connected to the supply voltage and may have single-phase, three-phase, or poly-phase configuration. The stator winding may also have different types of connections, such as star, delta, zig-zag, etc.
