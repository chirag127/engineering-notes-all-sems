# Fundamentals of Earthing and Lightning Protection

## Introduction

Earthing and lightning protection are important for everyone that uses electrical equipment and that includes the large majority of the world. Earthing, Lighting Arrestors and Surge Protection Devices are the leading devices that protect devices against current shock. Lightning protection grounding helps protect structures and equipment from direct strikes. Overhead ground wires and surge arresters, connected to ground, can limit dangerous system overvoltages to safe values.

## Objectives of Earthing and Lightning Protection

The main objectives of earthing and lightning protection are:

- To provide a low resistance path for fault currents to flow to the earth and clear the fault.
- To prevent electric shock to humans and animals by limiting the touch and step voltages to safe levels.
- To protect electrical and electronic equipment from damage due to lightning surges and transient overvoltages.
- To reduce electromagnetic interference and noise in sensitive circuits and devices.
- To stabilize the voltage of the electrical system with respect to the earth.

## Types of Earthing Systems

There are different types of earthing systems depending on the configuration of the source, the load, and the earth connection. Some common types are:

- TN system: The source neutral is connected to the earth and the load is directly connected to the source neutral. The exposed metal parts of the load are also connected to the source neutral or earth. There are three subtypes of TN system: TN-C, TN-S, and TN-C-S.
- TT system: The source neutral is connected to the earth and the load is connected to a separate earth electrode. The exposed metal parts of the load are also connected to the load earth electrode.
- IT system: The source neutral is isolated from the earth or connected through a high impedance. The load is connected to a separate earth electrode. The exposed metal parts of the load are also connected to the load earth electrode.

## Components of Earthing System

The main components of an earthing system are:

- Earth electrode: A conductor or a group of conductors that are buried in the soil and provide a low resistance contact with the earth. The common types of earth electrodes are rods, plates, pipes, and meshes.
- Earth conductor: A conductor that connects the earth electrode to the electrical equipment or system that needs to be earthed. The earth conductor should have adequate cross-sectional area, mechanical strength, and corrosion resistance.
- Earth clamp: A device that securely connects the earth conductor to the earth electrode or the equipment. The earth clamp should have good electrical contact, low resistance, and high durability.
- Earth pit: A hole in the ground that contains the earth electrode and the earth clamp. The earth pit should be filled with suitable soil or material that enhances the conductivity and moisture retention of the earth electrode.

## Principles of Lightning Protection

Lightning protection is the process of designing and installing a system that intercepts or diverts lightning and provides a certain path for conducting the surges safely to the ground by adequate down conductors to grounding electrodes. The main principles of lightning protection are:

- Capture: A lightning protection system should have one or more air terminals or rods that are located at the highest points of the structure or equipment to be protected. The air terminals should be made of conductive material and have sharp points to attract and capture the lightning strike.
- Conduction: A lightning protection system should have one or more down conductors or cables that connect the air terminals to the grounding electrodes. The down conductors should have low resistance, high current carrying capacity, and minimal bends or joints.
- Dissipation: A lightning protection system should have one or more grounding electrodes or rods that are buried in the soil and provide a low impedance path for the lightning current to flow to the earth. The grounding electrodes should have large surface area, good contact with the soil, and adequate spacing from each other and other underground utilities.
