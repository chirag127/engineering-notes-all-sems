### Types of Wires

- Wires are conductors that carry electric current from a source to a load.
- Wires can be classified based on their material, size, insulation, and wiring system.
- The material of a wire affects its conductivity, resistance, durability, and cost. The most common materials are copper and aluminum, but other metals such as steel, iron, and silver can also be used.
- The size of a wire is measured by its gauge or cross-sectional area. The smaller the gauge, the larger the diameter and the higher the current-carrying capacity. The American Wire Gauge (AWG) system is widely used to specify wire sizes.
- The insulation of a wire is the covering that protects it from physical damage, moisture, heat, and electric shock. The insulation can be made of plastic, rubber, paper, or other materials. The insulation also determines the voltage rating of a wire, which is the maximum voltage that can be safely applied to it.
- The wiring system of a wire is the method of arranging and installing wires in a building or a device. There are different types of wiring systems, each with its own advantages and disadvantages. Some of the common types are  :

  - Cleat wiring: This is a temporary and cheap wiring system that uses porcelain or plastic cleats to hold wires on wooden boards or walls. The wires are exposed and can be easily damaged or tampered with.
  - Wooden casing and capping wiring: This is a permanent and durable wiring system that uses wooden casing and capping to enclose wires on wooden boards or walls. The wires are protected from mechanical stress and moisture, but the wood can catch fire or decay over time.
  - CTS or TRS or PVC sheath wiring: This is a common and economical wiring system that uses rubber or plastic insulated wires that are covered with a PVC sheath. The wires are flexible and can be installed in any direction, but they are not suitable for high temperatures or corrosive environments.
  - Lead sheathed or metal sheathed wiring: This is a safe and reliable wiring system that uses lead or metal sheathed wires that are covered with a PVC sheath. The wires are protected from moisture, fire, and chemical damage, but they are heavy and expensive.
  - Conduit wiring: This is a secure and aesthetic wiring system that uses metal or plastic pipes or tubes to enclose wires. The wires are hidden from view and can be easily replaced or repaired, but the installation is complex and costly.

- Apart from these types, there are also other types of wires that are used for specific purposes, such as :

  - Low-voltage wiring: This is used for circuits that require 50 volts or less, such as landscape lighting, sprinkler systems, doorbells, speakers, and thermostats. The wires are usually thin and made of copper or aluminum.
  - Coaxial cable: This is used for transmitting high-frequency signals, such as television, radio, and internet. The cable consists of a central conductor surrounded by a dielectric insulator, a metallic shield, and an outer jacket. The cable is resistant to interference and noise, but it is bulky and rigid.
  - Twisted pair cable: This is used for transmitting low-frequency signals, such as telephone, data, and audio. The cable consists of two insulated wires twisted together to reduce crosstalk and electromagnetic interference. The cable is flexible and easy to install, but it is susceptible to noise and attenuation.
  - Fiber optic cable: This is used for transmitting high-speed and high-bandwidth signals, such as video, data, and voice. The cable consists of a core of glass or plastic fibers that carry light pulses, surrounded by a cladding, a buffer, and a jacket. The cable is immune to interference and noise, but it is fragile and expensive.
