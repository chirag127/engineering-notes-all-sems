### Three phase balanced circuits

- A three phase balanced circuit is a type of alternating current (AC) power system that consists of three voltage sources and three loads connected in either star (Y) or delta (Δ) configuration.
- In a balanced system, each of the three instantaneous voltages have equal amplitudes but are separated from the other voltages by a phase angle of 120°. The three voltages (or phases) are typically labeled a, b and c .
- The advantages of using three phase balanced circuits over single phase circuits are higher power transmission, higher efficiency, smoother torque in motors, and simpler transformer design.
- The solution of a balanced three phase circuit is easily carried out by solving the single-phase network corresponding to the reference phase. The reference phase is usually chosen as the phase with the highest voltage magnitude.
- The power delivered by a balanced three phase circuit can be calculated by multiplying the line voltage by the line current by the power factor by the square root of three. The power factor is the cosine of the angle between the voltage and the current.
- The power delivered by a balanced three phase circuit can also be calculated by adding the powers delivered by each phase. The power delivered by each phase is the product of the phase voltage, the phase current, and the power factor.
- The power factor of a balanced three phase circuit depends on the type and the impedance of the load. Resistive loads have a power factor of 1, inductive loads have a power factor less than 1, and capacitive loads have a power factor greater than 1.
- The power factor of a balanced three phase circuit can be improved by adding capacitors or inductors in parallel or series with the load. This reduces the reactive power and increases the active power of the circuit.
