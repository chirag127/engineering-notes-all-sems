### Three Phase Synchronous Machines

- A three phase synchronous machine is an AC machine that operates at a constant speed and converts mechanical energy into electrical energy or vice versa .
- A three phase synchronous machine consists of two main parts: the stator and the rotor .
- The stator is the stationary part of the machine that contains a three phase winding, which is connected to the AC supply. The stator winding produces a rotating magnetic field that rotates at a speed called the synchronous speed .
- The rotor is the rotating part of the machine that contains a direct current (DC) field winding, which is excited by a DC source. The rotor winding produces a constant magnetic field that aligns with the stator field .
- The electrical power is transferred to or from the rotor through slip rings and brushes, which are mounted on the stator and the rotor respectively .
- A three phase synchronous machine can operate as a generator or a motor, depending on the direction of the power flow and the relative speed of the rotor and the stator field.
- A three phase synchronous generator converts mechanical energy into electrical energy by rotating the rotor faster than the stator field. The rotor field induces an electromotive force (EMF) in the stator winding, which generates a three phase AC output .
- A three phase synchronous motor converts electrical energy into mechanical energy by rotating the rotor at the same speed as the stator field. The stator field induces a torque on the rotor, which drives the mechanical load .
- A three phase synchronous machine can have a salient pole or a round rotor, depending on the shape and distribution of the rotor poles.
- A salient pole rotor has projecting poles that are mounted on a laminated core. A salient pole rotor is used for low speed and high torque applications, such as hydro-generators and single phase synchronous motors.
- A round rotor has a smooth cylindrical surface that is uniformly magnetized. A round rotor is used for high speed and low torque applications, such as turbo-generators and large synchronous motors.
