### ACB

- ACB stands for Air Circuit Breaker, which is an electrical device used to provide overcurrent and short-circuit protection for electric circuits over 800 Amps to 10kA   .
- ACB operates at atmospheric pressure in air, where air-blast as an arc quenching medium .
- ACB is used in low voltage applications below 450V, such as distribution panels , and also in high voltage applications up to 15kV.
- ACB has three main components: contacts, arc chute and trip coil  .
- Contacts are the conductive parts that carry the current and open or close the circuit. They consist of a fixed contact and a moving contact  .
- Arc chute is the chamber that contains a series of metal plates or splitters that divide and cool the arc when the contacts separate  .
- Trip coil is the electromagnet that releases the latch mechanism and allows the moving contact to open under the action of a spring when a fault is detected  .
- ACB can be classified into two types: plain air circuit breaker and air blast circuit breaker .
- Plain air circuit breaker is the simplest form of ACB, where the arc is extinguished naturally by the lengthening and cooling effect of air .
- Air blast circuit breaker is the advanced form of ACB, where the arc is extinguished by a blast of compressed air that is directed along the arc or across the arc .
- ACB has several advantages, such as high speed of operation, low maintenance cost, high breaking capacity, and easy replacement of contacts  .
- ACB also has some disadvantages, such as high installation cost, high noise level, and possibility of fire hazard due to air blast  .
