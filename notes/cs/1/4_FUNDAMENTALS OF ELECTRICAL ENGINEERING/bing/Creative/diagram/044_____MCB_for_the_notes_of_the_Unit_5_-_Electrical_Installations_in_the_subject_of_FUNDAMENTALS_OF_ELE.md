### MCB for the notes of the Unit 5 - Electrical Installations in the subject of FUNDAMENTALS OF ELECTRICAL ENGINEERING

- MCB stands for **Miniature Circuit Breaker**, which is an automatically operated electrical switch that protects an electrical circuit from damage caused by excess current from an overload or short circuit.
- MCBs are designed to **trip** (interrupt the current flow) after a certain amount of time depending on the overcurrent level.
- MCBs are more sensitive and reliable than fuses, and can be reset manually or automatically after a fault is cleared.
- MCBs are classified into different types based on their **tripping characteristics**, such as Type B, Type C, and Type D.
- MCBs are also rated according to their **current carrying capacity**, such as 6A, 10A, 16A, 20A, 32A, etc.
- MCBs are commonly used in low voltage electrical networks, such as residential, commercial, and industrial applications.
- To install an MCB, the following steps are recommended :
  - Step 1: Identify the spot to install the MCB. Turn off the power supply to the electric panel and look for an unused location to accommodate a circuit breaker. If the unused location has a knockout plate, remove it before the installation.
  - Step 2: Place the MCB in the panel. Select the correct circuit breaker that is allowed to be installed in the panel. Place the new circuit breaker next to an existing MCB. Align the clips on the back of the MCB with the slots on the hot bus bar. Push the MCB into place until it snaps securely .
  - Step 3: Connect the circuit wires to the MCB. Strip about 1/2 inch of insulation from the end of the black (hot) wire and insert it into the terminal screw on the MCB. Tighten the screw to secure the wire. Repeat the same for the white (neutral) wire and the green (ground) wire, connecting them to the neutral bus bar and the ground bus bar respectively.
  - Step 4: Test the MCB. Turn on the power supply to the electric panel and use a test light or meter to verify that the MCB is working properly. If the MCB trips immediately, there may be a short circuit or a faulty MCB. If the MCB does not trip, the installation is successful.
