### Principle of operation and introduction to methods of starting for the notes of the Unit 4 - Electrical machines in the subject of FUNDAMENTALS OF ELECTRICAL ENGINEERING

- Electrical machines are devices that convert electrical energy into mechanical energy or vice versa. They are classified into two main types: direct current (DC) machines and alternating current (AC) machines .
- DC machines operate on the principle of electromagnetic induction, which states that when a current carrying conductor is placed in a magnetic field, it experiences a force that tends to rotate it. This force is called torque and it is proportional to the current, the magnetic field and the angle between them .
- DC machines can be further divided into DC generators and DC motors. DC generators convert mechanical energy into electrical energy by rotating a coil of wire in a magnetic field. DC motors convert electrical energy into mechanical energy by supplying current to a coil of wire that rotates in a magnetic field .
- DC machines can have different types of field windings, such as shunt, series, compound and permanent magnet. The field winding is the part of the machine that produces the magnetic field. The type of field winding affects the performance and characteristics of the machine .
- DC machines require a starter to limit the initial current and protect the machine from damage. A starter is a device that reduces the voltage or resistance in the circuit of the machine during starting. There are different types of starters, such as rheostatic, three-point, four-point and electronic starters.
- AC machines operate on the principle of rotating magnetic field, which states that when a set of coils are supplied with AC currents of different phases, they produce a magnetic field that rotates at a constant speed. This speed is called synchronous speed and it is proportional to the frequency of the AC supply and the number of poles of the machine .
- AC machines can be further divided into synchronous machines and induction machines. Synchronous machines have a rotor that rotates at the same speed as the rotating magnetic field. Induction machines have a rotor that rotates at a slightly lower speed than the rotating magnetic field. This difference is called slip and it is proportional to the load on the machine .
- Synchronous machines can be either generators or motors. Synchronous generators convert mechanical energy into electrical energy by rotating a magnet or a coil of wire in a rotating magnetic field. Synchronous motors convert electrical energy into mechanical energy by supplying AC currents to a magnet or a coil of wire that rotates in a rotating magnetic field .
- Induction machines can be either motors or generators. Induction motors convert electrical energy into mechanical energy by inducing currents in a rotor that rotates in a rotating magnetic field. Induction generators convert mechanical energy into electrical energy by rotating a rotor that induces currents in a stator that is connected to an AC supply .
- Induction machines can have different types of rotors, such as squirrel cage and wound rotor. The rotor is the part of the machine that rotates and interacts with the rotating magnetic field. The type of rotor affects the performance and characteristics of the machine .
- Induction machines require a starter to limit the initial current and protect the machine from damage. A starter is a device that reduces the voltage or frequency in the circuit of the machine during starting. There are different types of starters, such as direct-on-line, star-delta, autotransformer, soft starter and variable frequency drive starters.
