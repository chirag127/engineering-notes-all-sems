### Single Phase Induction Motor

A single phase induction motor is a type of AC motor that operates on single phase power supply. It is similar to a three phase induction motor in construction, except that it has only one stator winding instead of three. The stator winding produces a pulsating magnetic field, which does not rotate by itself. The rotor is a squirrel cage type, which is free to rotate inside the stator. The rotor does not receive any electrical power from the stator, but it is induced by the magnetic field.

Some of the main points to note about single phase induction motor are:

- It has no starting torque, because the pulsating magnetic field does not create any net force on the rotor. Therefore, it needs some external means to start rotating, such as a capacitor, a shaded pole, or a split phase winding.
- Once the rotor starts rotating, it develops a torque due to the interaction of the induced currents in the rotor bars and the stator magnetic field. The direction of rotation depends on the initial push given to the rotor.
- The speed of the rotor is slightly less than the synchronous speed of the stator magnetic field. The difference is called the slip, and it determines the amount of induced voltage and current in the rotor. The slip also affects the power factor and efficiency of the motor.
- The power rating of single phase induction motor is usually lower than that of three phase induction motor, because of the lower power factor and higher losses. The typical range of power rating is from a few watts to a few kilowatts.
- The applications of single phase induction motor include fans, blowers, pumps, washing machines, refrigerators, air conditioners, and other domestic and commercial appliances.
