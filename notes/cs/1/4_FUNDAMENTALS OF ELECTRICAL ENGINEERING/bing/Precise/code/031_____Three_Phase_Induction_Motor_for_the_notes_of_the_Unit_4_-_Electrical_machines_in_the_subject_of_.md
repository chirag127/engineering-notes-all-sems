### Three Phase Induction Motor

A three-phase induction motor is an AC electric motor that operates on three-phase power. It is one of the most common types of electric motors used in industry, due to its simplicity, reliability, and low cost.

Some key points to note about three-phase induction motors are:

1. The stator of a three-phase induction motor is made up of a series of windings that are energized by a three-phase AC power source. This creates a rotating magnetic field within the motor.

2. The rotor of a three-phase induction motor is typically made of a series of conductive bars that are shorted together at either end by end rings. This is known as a "squirrel cage" rotor.

3. When the rotating magnetic field created by the stator windings passes over the rotor bars, it induces a current in the bars. This current creates its own magnetic field, which interacts with the stator's magnetic field to produce torque.

4. The speed of a three-phase induction motor is determined by the frequency of the AC power source and the number of poles in the motor. The synchronous speed of the motor can be calculated using the formula: Ns = 120f/P, where Ns is the synchronous speed in RPM, f is the frequency of the AC power source in Hz, and P is the number of poles in the motor.

5. The actual speed of a three-phase induction motor will be slightly less than its synchronous speed, due to slip. Slip is the difference between the synchronous speed and the actual speed of the motor, expressed as a percentage of the synchronous speed.

6. Three-phase induction motors are available in a wide range of sizes and power ratings, from fractional horsepower to several thousand horsepower.

7. Three-phase induction motors are commonly used in applications such as pumps, fans, compressors, and conveyor systems.
