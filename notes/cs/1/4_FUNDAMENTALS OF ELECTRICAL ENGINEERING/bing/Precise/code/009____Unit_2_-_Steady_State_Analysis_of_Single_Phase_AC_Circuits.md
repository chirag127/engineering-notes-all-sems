## Unit 2 - Steady State Analysis of Single Phase AC Circuits

1. **Introduction:** Steady state analysis of single phase AC circuits involves the calculation of current, voltage, and power in circuits that are powered by a single phase AC source.
2. **Sinusoidal Steady State:** In sinusoidal steady state, all the circuit variables such as current, voltage, and power are sinusoidal functions of time with the same frequency as the source.
3. **Phasor Representation:** Phasors are complex numbers that represent the magnitude and phase of sinusoidal signals. They are used to simplify the analysis of AC circuits.
4. **Impedance and Admittance:** Impedance is the opposition to the flow of current in an AC circuit. It is a complex quantity that takes into account both resistance and reactance. Admittance is the reciprocal of impedance.
5. **Power in AC Circuits:** In AC circuits, power is the product of voltage and current. It can be calculated using the phasor representation of voltage and current.
6. **Power Factor:** Power factor is the ratio of real power to apparent power. It is an important parameter in the analysis of AC circuits as it affects the efficiency of power transmission.
7. **Series and Parallel AC Circuits:** The analysis of series and parallel AC circuits is similar to that of DC circuits. However, the use of phasors and complex impedance makes the calculations easier.
8. **Resonance in AC Circuits:** Resonance occurs in AC circuits when the reactance of the circuit is zero. At resonance, the circuit behaves as a purely resistive circuit and the current is maximum.
