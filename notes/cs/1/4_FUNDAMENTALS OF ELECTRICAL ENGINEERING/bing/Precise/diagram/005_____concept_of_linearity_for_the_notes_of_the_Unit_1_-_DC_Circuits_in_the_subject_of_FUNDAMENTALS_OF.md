### Concept of Linearity

Linearity is a property of a mathematical relationship or function that can be graphically represented as a straight line. In the context of electrical engineering, linearity refers to the behavior of an electrical circuit or component in which the output is directly proportional to the input.

Here are some key points to remember about linearity in the context of DC circuits:

1. A linear circuit is one in which the output is directly proportional to the input. This means that if the input is doubled, the output will also double.
2. Linear circuits obey the superposition principle, which states that the response of a linear circuit to multiple inputs is the sum of the responses to each individual input.
3. Ohm's law, which states that the current through a conductor between two points is directly proportional to the voltage across the two points, is an example of a linear relationship in a DC circuit.
4. Linear circuits are easier to analyze and design than non-linear circuits because their behavior can be predicted using simple mathematical equations.
5. Common linear components in DC circuits include resistors, capacitors, and inductors.
