### Concept of Resonance in Series & Parallel Circuits

Resonance is a phenomenon that occurs in both series and parallel AC circuits. It is a condition where the circuit's impedance is at a minimum or maximum value, depending on whether the circuit is a series or parallel circuit.

#### Series Resonance

In a series circuit, resonance occurs when the inductive reactance (XL) is equal to the capacitive reactance (XC). At this point, the circuit's impedance is at a minimum value, and the current flowing through the circuit is at its maximum value.

The resonant frequency (fr) of a series circuit can be calculated using the formula:

fr = 1 / (2π√(LC))

Where L is the inductance of the inductor and C is the capacitance of the capacitor.

#### Parallel Resonance

In a parallel circuit, resonance occurs when the admittance of the circuit is at a minimum value. This happens when the conductance (G) is equal to the susceptance (B).

The resonant frequency (fr) of a parallel circuit can be calculated using the formula:

fr = 1 / (2π√(LC))

Where L is the inductance of the inductor and C is the capacitance of the capacitor.

#### Applications of Resonance

Resonance is an important concept in the steady-state analysis of single-phase AC circuits. It is used in the design of filters, oscillators, and other electronic circuits. It is also used in the tuning of radio and television receivers.
