### Slip-torque characteristics

The slip-torque characteristics of an induction motor describe the relationship between the slip and the torque produced by the motor. The slip is defined as the difference between the synchronous speed of the rotating magnetic field and the rotor speed, expressed as a percentage of the synchronous speed.

The torque produced by an induction motor is directly proportional to the slip, up to a certain point. This point is known as the maximum torque point, and it occurs at a slip value known as the maximum torque slip. Beyond this point, the torque decreases as the slip increases.

The shape of the slip-torque curve is determined by the design of the motor, and it can be altered by changing the rotor resistance. A high rotor resistance results in a steep slip-torque curve, with a high starting torque and a low maximum torque. A low rotor resistance results in a flatter slip-torque curve, with a lower starting torque and a higher maximum torque.

In summary, the slip-torque characteristics of an induction motor describe the relationship between the slip and the torque produced by the motor. The shape of the slip-torque curve can be altered by changing the rotor resistance, and it determines the starting and maximum torque of the motor.
