# Magnetic Circuits

- A magnetic circuit is a closed path to which a magnetic field, represented as lines of magnetic flux, is confined .
- A magnetic circuit is usually made up of one or more loops containing a magnetic flux, which is generated by permanent magnets or electromagnets and confined to the path by magnetic cores consisting of ferromagnetic materials like iron.
- Some examples of magnetic circuits are: horseshoe magnet with iron keeper (low-reluctance circuit), horseshoe magnet with no keeper (high-reluctance circuit), electric motor (variable-reluctance circuit), and some types of pickup cartridge (variable-reluctance circuits).
- A magnetic circuit can be analyzed using the analogy of an electric circuit, where the magnetic flux is analogous to the electric current, the magnetic field intensity is analogous to the electric potential difference, and the magnetic reluctance is analogous to the electric resistance .
- The magnetic flux in a magnetic circuit is given by the following equation, where N is the number of turns of the coil, I is the current in the coil, and R is the total reluctance of the magnetic circuit :

  $$\Phi = \frac{NI}{R}$$

- The magnetic field intensity in a magnetic circuit is given by the following equation, where l is the length of the magnetic path, and A is the cross-sectional area of the magnetic path :

  $$H = \frac{NI}{l}$$

- The magnetic reluctance in a magnetic circuit is given by the following equation, where μ is the permeability of the magnetic material, and μ0 is the permeability of free space :

  $$R = \frac{l}{\mu A} = \frac{l}{\mu_0 \mu_r A}$$

- The permeability of a magnetic material is a measure of how easily it can be magnetized by a magnetic field. The permeability of free space is a constant value, and the relative permeability is a dimensionless ratio that depends on the type of material .
- The magnetic flux density in a magnetic circuit is given by the following equation, where B is the magnetic flux density, and μ is the permeability of the magnetic material :

  $$B = \mu H$$

- The magnetic flux density is also related to the magnetic flux by the following equation, where A is the cross-sectional area of the magnetic path :

  $$\Phi = BA$$

- A magnetic circuit can have different types of magnetic materials and air gaps in its path, which affect the magnetic flux and the magnetic reluctance. The magnetic flux is conserved in a magnetic circuit, meaning that the total flux entering a node is equal to the total flux leaving the node .
- A magnetic circuit can also have different types of sources of magnetic flux, such as permanent magnets or electromagnets. The magnetic flux generated by a permanent magnet is constant and independent of the magnetic circuit, while the magnetic flux generated by an electromagnet is proportional to the current in the coil and depends on the magnetic circuit .
- A magnetic circuit can be used for various applications, such as transformers, relays, motors, generators, sensors, and actuators. A transformer is a device that transfers electrical energy from one circuit to another through a magnetic circuit, by using the principle of electromagnetic induction. A relay is a device that uses a magnetic circuit to switch an electric circuit on or off, by using the principle of electromagnetism. A motor is a device that converts electrical energy into mechanical energy, by using the principle of electromagnetic force. A generator is a device that converts mechanical energy into electrical energy, by using the principle of electromagnetic induction. A sensor is a device that detects a physical quantity, such as temperature, pressure, or magnetic field, and converts it into an electrical signal, by using the principle of electromagnetism. An actuator is a device that converts an electrical signal into a physical action, such as movement, rotation, or sound, by using the principle of electromagnetism .
