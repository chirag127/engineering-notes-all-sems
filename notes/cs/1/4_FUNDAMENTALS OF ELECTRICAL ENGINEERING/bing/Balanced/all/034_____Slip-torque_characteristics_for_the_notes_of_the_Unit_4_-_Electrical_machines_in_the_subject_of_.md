# Slip-torque characteristics of induction motor

- Slip is the difference between the synchronous speed and the rotor speed of an induction motor, expressed as a fraction of the synchronous speed.
- Torque is the rotational force produced by the motor on the load.
- The slip-torque characteristic of an induction motor is the relationship between the slip and the torque for a given value of rotor resistance.
- The slip-torque characteristic can be derived from the equivalent circuit of the induction motor, by equating the mechanical power output to the electrical power input to the rotor.

- The slip-torque characteristic can be divided into three regions, depending on the value of slip:
  - Low slip region: This is the region where the slip is very small, and the rotor speed is near the synchronous speed. In this region, the torque is proportional to the slip, and the rotor resistance is negligible compared to the rotor reactance. The torque is low and increases linearly with slip.
  - Medium slip region: This is the region where the slip is moderate, and the rotor speed is below the synchronous speed. In this region, the torque is proportional to the square of the slip, and the rotor resistance is comparable to the rotor reactance. The torque is higher and increases rapidly with slip, reaching a maximum value at a certain slip, called the pull-out slip or the critical slip.
  - High slip region: This is the region where the slip is high, and the rotor speed is much lower than the synchronous speed. In this region, the torque is inversely proportional to the slip, and the rotor resistance is dominant over the rotor reactance. The torque is lower and decreases with slip, becoming zero at the standstill condition, where the slip is unity.

- The maximum torque, also called the breakdown torque or the pull-out torque, occurs when the rotor resistance is equal to the rotor reactance multiplied by the slip. The value of the maximum torque is independent of the rotor resistance, and depends only on the supply voltage and the motor parameters. The value of the slip at the maximum torque, however, depends on the rotor resistance, and increases with the increase in the rotor resistance.
- The slip-torque characteristic can be modified by changing the rotor resistance, either by inserting external resistors in the rotor circuit, or by using a wound rotor with variable taps. By increasing the rotor resistance, the maximum torque can be shifted to a lower speed, and the starting torque can be improved. However, this also increases the rotor losses and reduces the efficiency of the motor.
