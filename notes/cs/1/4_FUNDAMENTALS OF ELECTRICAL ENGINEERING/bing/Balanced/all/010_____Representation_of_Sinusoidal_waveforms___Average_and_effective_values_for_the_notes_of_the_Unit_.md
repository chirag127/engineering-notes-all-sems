# Representation of Sinusoidal waveforms – Average and effective values

- A sinusoidal waveform is a periodic function that oscillates between positive and negative peak values with a constant frequency and amplitude.
- The general equation of a sinusoidal waveform is given by: v(t) = Vm sin(ωt + θ), where Vm is the peak value, ω is the angular frequency, t is the time, and θ is the phase angle.
- The average value of a sinusoidal waveform is the arithmetic mean of the positive and negative half-cycles. It is calculated by multiplying the peak value by 0.637.
- The average value of a sinusoidal waveform is zero if it is symmetrical about the x-axis, meaning that the positive and negative areas are equal.
- The effective value or root mean square (RMS) value of a sinusoidal waveform is the equivalent DC value that would produce the same heating effect in a resistor as the AC waveform.
- The RMS value of a sinusoidal waveform is calculated by multiplying the peak value by 0.707.
- The RMS value of a sinusoidal waveform is always greater than or equal to the average value, except for a square wave, where they are equal.
- The form factor of a sinusoidal waveform is the ratio of the RMS value to the average value. For a pure sine wave, the form factor is 1.11.
- The peak factor of a sinusoidal waveform is the ratio of the peak value to the RMS value. For a pure sine wave, the peak factor is 1.414.
- A phasor is a rotating vector that represents the magnitude and phase of a sinusoidal waveform.
- A phasor can be expressed in rectangular form as V = Vm cos θ + j Vm sin θ, or in polar form as V = Vm ∠ θ, where j is the imaginary unit.
- A resistor is a passive element that opposes the flow of current and dissipates energy as heat.
- A resistor obeys Ohm's law, which states that the voltage across a resistor is proportional to the current through it: v = iR, where R is the resistance.
- A resistor does not change the shape or frequency of a sinusoidal waveform, only its amplitude and phase.
- The voltage and current phasors of a resistor are in phase, meaning that they have the same phase angle.
