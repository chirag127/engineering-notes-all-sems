# Introduction of Switch Fuse Unit (SFU)

- A switch fuse unit (SFU) is a type of low voltage switchgear that combines a switch and a fuse in one unit.
- It is used to protect and isolate electrical circuits from short circuits, overloads, and faults.
- It consists of a switch unit and a fuse unit. The switch unit can be operated manually to open or close the contacts. The fuse unit contains a fuse link that melts when the current exceeds a certain value, interrupting the circuit.
- SFUs are made of iron and are known as ironclad switches. They can be classified according to the number of poles and the presence of a neutral link.
- A double pole ironclad (DPIC) switch fuse unit is used to control single phase two-wire circuits. It has two poles, one for each wire.
- A triple pole ironclad (TPIC) switch fuse unit is used to control three-phase, three-wire circuits. It has three poles, one for each phase.
- A triple pole with neutral link ironclad (TPNIC) switch fuse unit is used to control three-phase, four-wire circuits. It has three poles and a neutral link, which connects the neutral wire to the fuse unit.
- The fuse rating of the SFU should be less than the switch rating, and should be selected based on the connected load. For example, a 32 A fuse can be used for a 63 A SFU.
