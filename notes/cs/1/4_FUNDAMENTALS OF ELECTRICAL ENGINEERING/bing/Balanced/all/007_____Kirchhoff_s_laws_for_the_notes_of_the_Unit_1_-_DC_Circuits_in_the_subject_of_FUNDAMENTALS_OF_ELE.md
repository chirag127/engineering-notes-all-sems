# Kirchhoff‟s laws

- Kirchhoff‟s laws are two rules that can be used to analyze any electrical circuit, whether it is simple or complex.
- Kirchhoff‟s laws are based on the conservation of charge and energy in a circuit.
- Kirchhoff‟s laws are also known as Kirchhoff‟s circuit laws or Kirchhoff‟s rules.

## Kirchhoff‟s current law (KCL)

- Kirchhoff‟s current law states that the algebraic sum of the currents entering any node (or junction) in a circuit is zero.
- Mathematically, KCL can be expressed as:

$$\sum_{k=1}^n I_k = 0$$

- Where $I_k$ is the current entering or leaving the node, and $n$ is the number of branches connected to the node.
- KCL is based on the principle of conservation of charge, which means that charge cannot be created or destroyed in a circuit.
- KCL can be used to find the unknown currents in a circuit, by applying it to each node and forming a system of linear equations.

## Kirchhoff‟s voltage law (KVL)

- Kirchhoff‟s voltage law states that the algebraic sum of the voltages around any closed loop (or mesh) in a circuit is zero.
- Mathematically, KVL can be expressed as:

$$\sum_{k=1}^n V_k = 0$$

- Where $V_k$ is the voltage across the kth element in the loop, and $n$ is the number of elements in the loop.
- KVL is based on the principle of conservation of energy, which means that energy cannot be created or destroyed in a circuit.
- KVL can be used to find the unknown voltages in a circuit, by applying it to each loop and forming a system of linear equations.
