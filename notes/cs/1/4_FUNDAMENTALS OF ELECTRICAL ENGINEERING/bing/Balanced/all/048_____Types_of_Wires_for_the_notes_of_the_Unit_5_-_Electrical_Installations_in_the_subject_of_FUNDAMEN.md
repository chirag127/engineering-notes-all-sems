# Types of Wires

Wires are conductors that carry electric current from a source to a load. They are usually made of metal, such as copper or aluminum, and have different sizes, shapes, and insulation materials. The type of wire used for a particular application depends on the voltage, current, and environmental conditions.

Some of the common types of wires used in electrical engineering are:

- **Cleat wiring**: This is a temporary wiring system that uses porcelain or plastic cleats to hold wires on wooden boards or walls. The wires are insulated with rubber or cotton and are not protected from mechanical damage or moisture. This type of wiring is cheap and easy to install, but it is not suitable for high voltages or permanent use .

- **Wooden casing and capping wiring**: This is a semi-permanent wiring system that uses wooden casing and capping to enclose wires on walls or ceilings. The wires are insulated with rubber or cotton and are protected from dust and moisture. This type of wiring is more durable and neat than cleat wiring, but it is still prone to fire hazards and termite attacks .

- **CTS or TRS or PVC sheath wiring**: This is a permanent wiring system that uses cables with tough rubber sheath (TRS) or polyvinyl chloride (PVC) sheath to cover wires. The cables are fixed on wooden battens or metal clips and are insulated from each other and the surroundings. This type of wiring is safe, flexible, and economical, but it may deteriorate over time due to heat and chemical reactions .

- **Lead sheathed or metal sheathed wiring**: This is a permanent wiring system that uses cables with lead or metal sheath to cover wires. The cables are laid in metal conduits or pipes and are insulated from each other and the surroundings. This type of wiring is very durable, fire-resistant, and corrosion-resistant, but it is expensive and heavy .

- **Conduit wiring**: This is a permanent wiring system that uses metal or plastic pipes or tubes to enclose wires. The wires are insulated with rubber or PVC and are protected from mechanical damage and moisture. This type of wiring is very safe, neat, and versatile, but it is also costly and complex to install .

- **Low-voltage wiring**: This is a wiring system that uses wires with low voltage (50 volts or less) to power devices such as landscape lighting, sprinkler systems, doorbells, speakers, and thermostats. The wires are usually thin and have different colors to indicate their functions. This type of wiring is easy to install and does not require special protection, but it may not be compatible with some devices or codes.

- **Hot, neutral, and grounding wires**: These are the three types of wires that are used in most household and commercial circuits. Hot wires are black or red and carry electric current from the source to the load. Neutral wires are white or light gray and return the current from the load to the source. Grounding wires are bare copper or green and connect the circuit to the earth or a metal object for safety. These wires are insulated with rubber or PVC and have different sizes and ratings depending on the voltage and current.
