## Unit 5 - Electrical Installations

- Electrical installations are the systems and equipment that provide electrical power and lighting to buildings and other structures.
- Electrical installations include the following components:
  - Power sources: These are the devices that generate or supply electrical energy, such as generators, transformers, batteries, solar panels, etc.
  - Distribution systems: These are the cables, wires, conduits, switches, circuit breakers, fuses, etc. that carry electrical energy from the power sources to the loads.
  - Loads: These are the devices that consume electrical energy, such as lights, appliances, motors, heaters, etc.
  - Control devices: These are the devices that regulate the flow of electrical energy, such as switches, dimmers, relays, timers, sensors, etc.
  - Protection devices: These are the devices that prevent or limit the damage caused by electrical faults, such as circuit breakers, fuses, surge protectors, grounding rods, etc.
  - Communication devices: These are the devices that transmit or receive information using electrical signals, such as telephones, intercoms, alarms, CCTV, etc.
- Electrical installations must be designed, installed, maintained, and tested according to the relevant standards and codes of practice, such as the National Electrical Code (NEC) in the US, the British Standard BS 7671 in the UK, the International Electrotechnical Commission (IEC) standards, etc.
- Electrical installations must also comply with the safety and environmental regulations and requirements, such as the Occupational Safety and Health Administration (OSHA) in the US, the Health and Safety Executive (HSE) in the UK, the European Union directives, etc.
- Electrical installations must be inspected and tested periodically to ensure their proper functioning and safety. Some of the common tests and inspections include:
  - Visual inspection: This involves checking the physical condition and appearance of the components, such as the insulation, connections, labels, etc.
  - Continuity test: This involves measuring the resistance of the conductors and verifying that there are no open or short circuits.
  - Insulation resistance test: This involves applying a high voltage to the conductors and measuring the leakage current to check the quality of the insulation.
  - Earth fault loop impedance test: This involves measuring the impedance of the path from the power source to the load and back to the source through the earth, to ensure that the fault current can be safely interrupted by the protection devices.
  - Polarity test: This involves verifying that the live, neutral, and earth wires are correctly connected and identified.
  - Functional test: This involves operating the loads and control devices and checking their performance and response.
