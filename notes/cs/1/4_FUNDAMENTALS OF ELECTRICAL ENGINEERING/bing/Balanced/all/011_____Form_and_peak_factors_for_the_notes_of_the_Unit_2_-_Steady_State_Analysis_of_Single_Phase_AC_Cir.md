# Form and peak factors for the notes of the Unit 2 - Steady State Analysis of Single Phase AC Circuits in the subject of FUNDAMENTALS OF ELECTRICAL ENGINEERING

- An alternating current (AC) waveform is a periodic function of time that varies in magnitude and direction.
- The peak value of an AC waveform is the maximum absolute value that it attains in one cycle.
- The root mean square (RMS) value of an AC waveform is the effective value that produces the same heating effect as a direct current (DC) of the same magnitude.
- The average value of an AC waveform is the arithmetic mean of the absolute values over one cycle.
- The form factor of an AC waveform is the ratio of the RMS value to the average value. It indicates how much the waveform deviates from a pure sine wave, which has a form factor of 1.11.
- The peak factor of an AC waveform is the ratio of the peak value to the RMS value. It indicates how much the waveform peaks compared to its effective value. For a pure sine wave, the peak factor is 1.414.
- The form factor and the peak factor are related by the equation: form factor = peak factor x (average value / peak value).
- The form factor and the peak factor are the same and equal to one for a square waveform, which has a constant magnitude and direction.
- The form factor and the peak factor are useful for analyzing the power and efficiency of AC circuits, as well as for designing and testing AC devices and instruments.
