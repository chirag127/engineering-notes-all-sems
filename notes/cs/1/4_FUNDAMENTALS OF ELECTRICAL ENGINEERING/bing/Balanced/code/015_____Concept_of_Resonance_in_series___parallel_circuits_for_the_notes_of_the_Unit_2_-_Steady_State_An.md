### Concept of Resonance in series & parallel circuits

- Resonance is a phenomenon that occurs in AC circuits when the frequency of the applied voltage is such that the impedance of the circuit is either minimum or maximum.
- Resonance can be classified into two types: series resonance and parallel resonance.
- Series resonance occurs when the circuit consists of a resistor, an inductor and a capacitor connected in series. At resonance, the inductive reactance and the capacitive reactance are equal in magnitude and opposite in phase, so they cancel each other. The impedance of the circuit is then equal to the resistance, which is minimum. The current in the circuit is maximum and the voltage across each element is proportional to its impedance. The resonant frequency is given by:

  $$f_r = \frac{1}{2\pi\sqrt{LC}}$$

- Parallel resonance occurs when the circuit consists of a resistor, an inductor and a capacitor connected in parallel. At resonance, the admittance of the circuit is minimum and the impedance is maximum. The current in each branch is proportional to its admittance and the voltage across the circuit is maximum. The resonant frequency is given by:

  $$f_r = \frac{1}{2\pi\sqrt{LC}}$$

- The difference between series and parallel resonance is that in series resonance, the impedance is minimum and the current is maximum, while in parallel resonance, the impedance is maximum and the current is minimum. Also, in series resonance, the voltage across the capacitor and the inductor are maximum, while in parallel resonance, the current through the capacitor and the inductor are maximum.
- Resonance can be used for various applications, such as tuning radio receivers, filtering unwanted frequencies, generating oscillations, etc.
