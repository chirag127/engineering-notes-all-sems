### Principle and Construction of Electrical Machines

- Electrical machines are devices that convert mechanical energy to electrical energy and vice versa.
- The principle of operation of electrical machines is based on the interaction of magnetic fields and electric currents.
- The main types of electrical machines are DC machines, AC machines, and special purpose machines.
- DC machines are those that operate on direct current (DC) and have a commutator to change the direction of current in the armature coils.
- AC machines are those that operate on alternating current (AC) and have a rotating magnetic field in the stator and a rotating armature in the rotor.
- Special purpose machines are those that have specific applications and characteristics, such as stepper motors, servo motors, linear motors, etc.

#### Construction of DC Machines

- The main parts of a DC machine are the yoke, the poles, the armature, the commutator, and the brushes .
- The yoke is the outer frame of the machine that supports and protects the other parts. It is usually made of cast iron or steel.
- The poles are the projections on the inner side of the yoke that carry the field coils. The field coils are wound on the pole cores and produce the magnetic field in the air gap.
- The armature is the rotating part of the machine that carries the armature coils. The armature coils are connected to the commutator segments and are placed in the slots of the armature core. The armature core is made of laminated steel to reduce eddy current losses.
- The commutator is a cylindrical structure that consists of insulated copper segments. It is mounted on the shaft of the armature and rotates with it. The commutator acts as a mechanical switch that reverses the direction of current in the armature coils every half cycle.
- The brushes are the stationary contacts that press against the commutator and transfer the current to and from the external circuit. The brushes are usually made of carbon or graphite and are mounted on the brush holders.

#### Working of DC Machines

- The working of a DC machine is based on the principle of electromagnetic induction and Lorentz force .
- When a current carrying conductor is placed in a magnetic field, it experiences a force that is perpendicular to both the current and the field. This force is called the Lorentz force and it causes the conductor to move.
- When a conductor moves in a magnetic field, it cuts the magnetic flux and induces an emf in it. This emf is called the induced emf and it opposes the cause of its production. This is known as Faraday's law of electromagnetic induction.
- In a DC machine, the armature coils are the current carrying conductors that rotate in the magnetic field of the poles. The armature coils cut the magnetic flux and induce an emf in them. The induced emf is collected by the commutator and the brushes and is delivered to the external circuit.
- Depending on the direction of the current and the magnetic field, the armature coils experience a torque that makes them rotate. The torque is proportional to the product of the current and the flux. The speed of the armature is proportional to the induced emf and inversely proportional to the flux.
- A DC machine can work as a generator or a motor depending on the direction of the power flow. In a generator, the mechanical input is converted to electrical output. In a motor, the electrical input is converted to mechanical output.
