### Electrical circuit elements (R, L and C)

- An electrical circuit is a combination of electrical components that are connected by wires to allow electric current to flow through them.
- The basic electrical components are resistors, inductors and capacitors, which are denoted by R, L and C respectively.
- A resistor is a device that opposes the flow of electric current and converts electrical energy into heat. The resistance of a resistor is measured in ohms (Ω) and depends on its material, shape and temperature. The voltage across a resistor is proportional to the current through it, according to Ohm's law: V = IR, where V is the voltage, I is the current and R is the resistance.
- An inductor is a device that stores electrical energy in a magnetic field when electric current flows through it. The inductance of an inductor is measured in henries (H) and depends on its shape, size and number of turns of wire. The voltage across an inductor is proportional to the rate of change of current through it, according to Faraday's law: V = L(dI/dt), where V is the voltage, L is the inductance and dI/dt is the derivative of current with respect to time.
- A capacitor is a device that stores electrical energy in an electric field when a voltage is applied across it. The capacitance of a capacitor is measured in farads (F) and depends on its shape, size and the type of material between its plates. The voltage across a capacitor is proportional to the charge stored on it, according to the definition of capacitance: Q = CV, where Q is the charge, C is the capacitance and V is the voltage.
- The behavior of resistors, inductors and capacitors in a circuit depends on whether the circuit is powered by a direct current (DC) source or an alternating current (AC) source. In a DC circuit, the current is constant and the voltage is steady, while in an AC circuit, the current and voltage vary sinusoidally with time and frequency.
- In a DC circuit, a resistor behaves as a linear element, meaning that its voltage-current relationship is a straight line that passes through the origin. An inductor behaves as a short circuit, meaning that it has zero resistance and allows maximum current to flow through it. A capacitor behaves as an open circuit, meaning that it has infinite resistance and blocks any current from flowing through it.
- In an AC circuit, a resistor still behaves as a linear element, but an inductor and a capacitor behave as frequency-dependent elements, meaning that their impedance (or opposition to current) varies with the frequency of the AC source. The impedance of an inductor is given by Z_L = jωL, where j is the imaginary unit, ω is the angular frequency and L is the inductance. The impedance of a capacitor is given by Z_C = 1/jωC, where C is the capacitance. The impedance of a resistor is given by Z_R = R, where R is the resistance. The impedance of a series combination of R, L and C is given by Z = Z_R + Z_L + Z_C, while the impedance of a parallel combination of R, L and C is given by 1/Z = 1/Z_R + 1/Z_L + 1/Z_C.
