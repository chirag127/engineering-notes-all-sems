# Applications of DC motors (simple numerical problems)

DC motors are electric motors that are powered by direct current (DC), such as from a battery or DC power supply. They have different types, features and applications depending on their design and construction. Here are some of the common applications of DC motors and some simple numerical problems to illustrate their working principles.

## Series DC motors

Series DC motors are motors where the armature and field windings are connected in series. They have high starting torque and can vary their speed according to the load. They are suitable for applications where the load changes frequently and the speed regulation is not important. Some examples of series DC motors are:

- Traction systems: Series DC motors are used in electric trains, trams and trolleys. They can provide high torque to overcome the inertia and friction of the vehicles and can adjust their speed according to the track conditions and the speed limit. For example, a series DC motor with a rated voltage of 600 V, a rated current of 200 A and a rated power of 120 kW is used to drive a train. The resistance of the armature and the field windings are 0.1 ohm and 0.05 ohm respectively. The speed of the motor is 1000 rpm when the train is running at 60 km/h. What is the torque of the motor and the back emf generated by the motor?

  - Solution: The torque of the motor is given by the formula:

    `T = P / (2 * pi * N / 60)`

    where T is the torque in Nm, P is the power in W, N is the speed in rpm and pi is 3.14.

    Substituting the values, we get:

    `T = 120000 / (2 * 3.14 * 1000 / 60)`

    `T = 1146.4 Nm`

    The back emf of the motor is given by the formula:

    `E = V - I * (Ra + Rf)`

    where E is the back emf in V, V is the supply voltage in V, I is the current in A, Ra is the armature resistance in ohm and Rf is the field resistance in ohm.

    Substituting the values, we get:

    `E = 600 - 200 * (0.1 + 0.05)`

    `E = 570 V`

- Cranes: Series DC motors are used in cranes to lift heavy loads. They can provide high torque to overcome the weight of the load and can vary their speed according to the height of the load. For example, a series DC motor with a rated voltage of 240 V, a rated current of 50 A and a rated power of 12 kW is used to lift a load of 1000 kg. The resistance of the armature and the field windings are 0.2 ohm and 0.1 ohm respectively. The speed of the motor is 500 rpm when the load is at the ground level. What is the torque of the motor and the back emf generated by the motor?

  - Solution: The torque of the motor is given by the formula:

    `T = P / (2 * pi * N / 60)`

    Substituting the values, we get:

    `T = 12000 / (2 * 3.14 * 500 / 60)`

    `T = 229.2 Nm`

    The back emf of the motor is given by the formula:

    `E = V - I * (Ra + Rf)`

    Substituting the values, we get:

    `E = 240 - 50 * (0.2 + 0.1)`

    `E = 225 V`
