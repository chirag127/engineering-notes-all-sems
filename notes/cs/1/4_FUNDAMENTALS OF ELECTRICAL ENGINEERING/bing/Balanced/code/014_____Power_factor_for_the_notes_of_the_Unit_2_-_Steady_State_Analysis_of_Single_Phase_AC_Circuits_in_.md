### Power factor

- Power factor is a dimensionless quantity that measures how efficiently electrical power is converted into useful work output. 
- Power factor is defined as the ratio of real power (P) to apparent power (S) in an AC circuit. Real power is the power that performs useful work, such as turning a motor or lighting a bulb. Apparent power is the product of the voltage and current in the circuit, regardless of the phase angle between them.   
- Power factor can be expressed as a decimal number or a percentage, ranging from 0 to 1 or 0% to 100%. A power factor of 1 or 100% means that all the power supplied by the source is consumed by the load. A power factor of 0 or 0% means that none of the power supplied by the source is consumed by the load.   
- Power factor can also be calculated as the cosine of the phase angle (θ) between the voltage and current waveforms in the circuit. The phase angle represents the time difference between the voltage and current peaks. A phase angle of 0° means that the voltage and current are in phase, and the power factor is 1. A phase angle of 90° means that the voltage and current are out of phase, and the power factor is 0.  
- The power factor formula is: 

    `power factor = P / S = kW / kVA = cos θ`    

- Power factor is affected by the presence of reactive components, such as capacitors and inductors, in the circuit. Reactive components store and release energy in the form of electric or magnetic fields, causing the current to lag or lead the voltage. This results in a lower power factor, as some of the apparent power is not converted into real power.  
- Power factor can be improved by adding power factor correction devices, such as capacitors or synchronous motors, to the circuit. These devices provide or consume reactive power, thereby reducing the phase angle and increasing the power factor.  
- Power factor is important for both the consumers and the suppliers of electrical power. A low power factor means that more current is required to deliver the same amount of real power, resulting in higher losses, lower efficiency, and higher costs. A high power factor means that less current is required to deliver the same amount of real power, resulting in lower losses, higher efficiency, and lower costs.
