### Bandwidth and Quality Factor for AC Circuits

- Bandwidth (BW) is the range of frequencies over which the current or voltage in a resonant circuit is greater than 70.7% of its maximum value .
- Quality factor (Q) is a measure of how sharp or selective the resonance is in a resonant circuit . It is defined as the ratio of the resonant frequency (fr) to the bandwidth    :
  - Q = fr / BW
- A high Q resonant circuit has a narrow bandwidth and a low Q resonant circuit has a wide bandwidth .
- The Q factor and the bandwidth are inversely proportional to each other   :
  - BW = fr / Q
- The Q factor and the bandwidth depend on the type of resonant circuit (series or parallel) and the values of the components (resistance, inductance, and capacitance)  .
- The formulas for calculating the Q factor and the bandwidth for series and parallel resonant circuits are different :
  - For series resonant circuit:
    - Q = XL / R = XC / R
    - BW = R / L = 1 / (RC)
  - For parallel resonant circuit:
    - Q = R / XL = R / XC
    - BW = L / RC = 1 / (LC)
- The Q factor and the bandwidth are related to the power dissipation and the voltage gain in a resonant circuit  :
  - A high Q resonant circuit has low power dissipation and high voltage gain at resonance
  - A low Q resonant circuit has high power dissipation and low voltage gain at resonance
- The Q factor and the bandwidth are important parameters for designing and analyzing resonant circuits for various applications, such as filters, oscillators, and amplifiers  .
