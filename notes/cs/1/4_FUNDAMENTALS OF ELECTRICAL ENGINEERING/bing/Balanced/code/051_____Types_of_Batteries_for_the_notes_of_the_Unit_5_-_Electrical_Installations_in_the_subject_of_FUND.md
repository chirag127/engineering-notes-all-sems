### Types of Batteries

A battery is a device that converts chemical energy into electrical energy. It consists of one or more cells, each containing a positive and a negative electrode, and an electrolyte that allows the flow of electric charge between them.

There are four main types of batteries:

- **Primary batteries**: These are non-rechargeable batteries that can only be used once. They have a high energy density and a low self-discharge rate. They are suitable for applications that require a constant and reliable power source, such as flashlights, toys, and remote controls. Examples of primary batteries are alkaline, zinc-carbon, and lithium batteries .
- **Secondary batteries**: These are rechargeable batteries that can be used multiple times. They have a lower energy density and a higher self-discharge rate than primary batteries. They are suitable for applications that require a high power output and frequent recharging, such as electric vehicles, laptops, and smartphones. Examples of secondary batteries are lead-acid, nickel-cadmium, nickel-metal hydride, and lithium-ion batteries  .
- **Reserve batteries**: These are batteries that are activated only when needed. They have a long shelf life and a high power output. They are suitable for applications that require a backup or emergency power source, such as military, medical, and aerospace equipment. Examples of reserve batteries are thermal, water-activated, and gas-generating batteries .
- **Fuel cells**: These are batteries that generate electricity from the reaction of a fuel and an oxidant. They have a high efficiency and a low environmental impact. They are suitable for applications that require a continuous and clean power source, such as stationary, portable, and transportation devices. Examples of fuel cells are hydrogen, methanol, and microbial fuel cells .
