### Form and peak factors for the notes of the Unit 2 - Steady State Analysis of Single Phase AC Circuits in the subject of FUNDAMENTALS OF ELECTRICAL ENGINEERING

- Form factor is a parameter used to describe AC waveforms and is defined as the ratio of the RMS value to the average value of an alternating quantity.
- RMS value is the effective value of an AC quantity that produces the same heating effect as a DC quantity of the same value.
- Average value is the arithmetic mean of the positive half cycle of an AC quantity.
- Form factor can be used to determine the purity of a sine wave, which has a form factor of 1.11.
- Peak factor or crest factor is another parameter used to describe AC waveforms and is defined as the ratio of the peak value to the RMS value of an alternating quantity.
- Peak value is the maximum amplitude of an AC quantity.
- Peak factor indicates the sharpness of the peaks of an AC waveform.
- For a pure sine wave, the peak factor is 1.414.
- The form factor and peak factor are related by the following equation:

  `Form factor = Peak factor * (Average value / Peak value)`

- The form factor and peak factor are equal to one for a square waveform.
- The form factor and peak factor are different for other waveforms, such as triangular, sawtooth, or half-wave rectified.
- The form factor and peak factor are important for the analysis of power in AC circuits, as they affect the power factor, which is the ratio of the average power to the apparent power.
- The power factor depends on the phase angle between the current and voltage in an AC circuit, and it determines the efficiency and quality of the power delivery.
