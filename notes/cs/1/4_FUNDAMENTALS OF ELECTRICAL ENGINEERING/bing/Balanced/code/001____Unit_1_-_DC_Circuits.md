## Unit 1 - DC Circuits

DC circuits are electrical circuits that use direct current (DC) as the source of power. DC is a type of electric current that flows in one direction only, unlike alternating current (AC) that changes direction periodically. DC circuits are commonly used in batteries, solar cells, and electronic devices.

Some of the topics covered in this unit are:

- Basic concepts of electric charge, current, voltage, power, and energy.
- Ohm's law, which relates the voltage, current, and resistance in a DC circuit.
- Kirchhoff's laws, which are two rules that help analyze complex DC circuits.
- Series and parallel circuits, which are two ways of connecting multiple components in a DC circuit.
- Voltage and current dividers, which are formulas that help find the voltage or current across a component in a series or parallel circuit.
- Equivalent resistance, which is a way of simplifying a complex DC circuit by replacing multiple resistors with a single resistor.
- Wheatstone bridge, which is a device that measures unknown resistances using a balanced circuit.
- Potentiometer, which is a variable resistor that can be used to control the voltage or current in a circuit.
- Capacitors, which are components that store electric charge and energy in a DC circuit.
- RC circuits, which are circuits that contain resistors and capacitors and have a transient behavior when the voltage source is switched on or off.
