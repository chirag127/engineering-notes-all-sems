### Magnetic circuits

- A magnetic circuit is a closed path to which a magnetic field, represented as lines of magnetic flux, is confined.
- A magnetic circuit is analogous to an electric circuit, but instead of electric charge flowing, the magnetic flux is the quantity of interest.
- A magnetic circuit consists of a structure composed for the most part of high permeability magnetic material, such as iron, which guides the magnetic flux.
- The presence of high permeability material causes the magnetic flux to be confined to the paths defined by the structure, much as currents are confined to the conductors of an electric circuit.
- Some examples of magnetic circuits are: horseshoe magnet with iron keeper (low-reluctance circuit), horseshoe magnet with no keeper (high-reluctance circuit), electric motor (variable-reluctance circuit), and transformer (fixed-reluctance circuit).
- The main parameters of a magnetic circuit are: magnetic flux, magnetic flux density, magnetic field intensity, magnetic potential difference, magnetic reluctance, and magnetic permeability.
- The magnetic flux is the total amount of magnetic field passing through a given area, measured in webers (Wb) or maxwells (Mx).
- The magnetic flux density is the magnetic flux per unit area, measured in teslas (T) or gauss (G).
- The magnetic field intensity is the magnetic force per unit length exerted on a unit magnetic pole, measured in amperes per meter (A/m) or oersteds (Oe).
- The magnetic potential difference is the work done in moving a unit magnetic pole around a magnetic circuit, measured in ampere-turns (At) or gilberts (Gb).
- The magnetic reluctance is the opposition to the magnetic flux in a magnetic circuit, measured in ampere-turns per weber (At/Wb) or oersteds per maxwell (Oe/Mx).
- The magnetic permeability is the measure of how easily a material can support a magnetic field, measured in henries per meter (H/m) or newtons per ampere squared (N/A^2^).
- The basic laws of a magnetic circuit are: Ohm's law for magnetic circuits, Kirchhoff's laws for magnetic circuits, and Faraday's law of electromagnetic induction.
- Ohm's law for magnetic circuits states that the magnetic potential difference around a closed magnetic circuit is equal to the product of the magnetic flux and the total magnetic reluctance of the circuit.
- Kirchhoff's laws for magnetic circuits state that the sum of the magnetic potential differences around any closed loop of a magnetic circuit is zero, and the sum of the magnetic fluxes entering any node of a magnetic circuit is equal to the sum of the magnetic fluxes leaving that node.
- Faraday's law of electromagnetic induction states that the induced electromotive force (emf) in a coil is equal to the rate of change of magnetic flux through the coil.
