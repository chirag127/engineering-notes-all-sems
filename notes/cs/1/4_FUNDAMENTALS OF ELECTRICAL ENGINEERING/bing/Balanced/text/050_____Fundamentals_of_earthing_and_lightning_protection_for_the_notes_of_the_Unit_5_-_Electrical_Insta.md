### Fundamentals of earthing and lightning protection

- Earthing and lightning protection are important for everyone that uses electrical equipment and that includes the large majority of the world.
- Earthing is the process of connecting the metallic parts of an electrical system or appliance to the earth. This provides a low-resistance path for fault currents and protects the system and the users from electric shock.
- Lightning protection is the process of intercepting or diverting lightning and providing a certain path for conducting the surges safely to the ground by adequate down conductors to grounding electrodes. This helps prevent disastrous events like fires, injuries, and deaths.
- The basic principles of earthing and lightning protection are:
  - Providing an alternative path for the lightning current to flow to ground and ensure that:
    - Lightning current flowing in the lightning protection system (LPS) does not induce dangerous currents in any parallel metallic systems near the LPS, and
    - The potential difference between the LPS and the earth is minimized.
  - Providing a low-impedance path for the fault currents to flow to ground and ensure that:
    - The fault current is quickly cleared by the protective devices, and
    - The touch and step voltages are kept below the safe limits.
- The types and components of earthing and lightning protection systems are:
  - Earthing system: consists of earth electrodes, earth conductors, earth pits, and bonding conductors. The earth electrodes are buried in the soil and provide a physical connection to the earth. The earth conductors connect the earth electrodes to the metallic parts of the electrical system or appliance. The earth pits are filled with conductive materials to improve the soil resistivity and moisture retention. The bonding conductors connect the metallic parts of the electrical system or appliance to each other and to the earth conductors.
  - Lightning protection system: consists of air terminals, down conductors, and grounding electrodes. The air terminals are metal rods or wires that are installed at the highest points of a structure or equipment to intercept the lightning. The down conductors connect the air terminals to the grounding electrodes. The grounding electrodes are buried in the soil and provide a low-impedance path for the lightning current to dissipate into the earth.
  - Surge protection devices: are devices that limit the voltage surges or transients that may occur due to lightning or switching operations. They are installed at the point of entry of the electrical supply or at the sensitive equipment. They can be classified into primary, secondary, and tertiary surge protection devices based on their location and function.
