## Unit 1 - DC Circuits

- A DC circuit is a circuit that carries direct current (DC), which is a type of electric current that flows in one direction only.
- The main components of a DC circuit are a voltage source, such as a battery or a generator, and a load, such as a resistor, a capacitor, an inductor, or a combination of them.
- The voltage source provides the electric potential difference that drives the current through the circuit, while the load consumes the electric power and converts it into other forms of energy, such as heat, light, or motion.
- The basic laws that govern the behavior of DC circuits are Ohm's law, Kirchhoff's current law, and Kirchhoff's voltage law.
- Ohm's law states that the current (I) through a resistor (R) is proportional to the voltage (V) across it, or I = V/R.
- Kirchhoff's current law states that the algebraic sum of the currents entering and leaving any node in a circuit is zero, or ∑I = 0.
- Kirchhoff's voltage law states that the algebraic sum of the voltages around any closed loop in a circuit is zero, or ∑V = 0.
- These laws can be used to analyze and solve DC circuits by applying various methods, such as nodal analysis, mesh analysis, superposition, Thevenin's theorem, Norton's theorem, and maximum power transfer.
