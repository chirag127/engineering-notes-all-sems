### Regulation and Efficiency of Transformers

- Regulation of a transformer is the measure of how well the output voltage of the transformer remains constant under varying load conditions.
- Regulation is expressed as a percentage of the no-load voltage, and is calculated as:

```
% Regulation = [(VNL - VFL) / VFL] x 100
```

where VNL is the no-load voltage, and VFL is the full-load voltage.

- A transformer with low regulation has a small change in output voltage when the load changes, and is desirable for most applications.
- A transformer with high regulation has a large change in output voltage when the load changes, and may cause problems for sensitive equipment.

- Efficiency of a transformer is the ratio of the output power to the input power, expressed as a percentage.
- Efficiency is calculated as:

```
% Efficiency = [(Pout / Pin) x 100
```

where Pout is the output power, and Pin is the input power.

- A transformer with high efficiency has a small loss of power in the form of heat, noise, and leakage flux, and is desirable for energy saving and environmental reasons.
- A transformer with low efficiency has a large loss of power in the form of heat, noise, and leakage flux, and may cause overheating, fire hazards, and increased carbon emissions.

- The efficiency of a transformer depends on several factors, such as the design, construction, material, and operating conditions of the transformer.
- The Department of Energy (DOE) has established minimum efficiency standards for different types of transformers sold in the United States, to improve the reliability of the power grid, lower the utility bills, and reduce the greenhouse gas emissions  .
- The most recent of these standards are the DOE 2016 efficiency requirements, which increased the expected efficiency ratings from 98.36 to 99.49% (DOE 2010) to 98.70 to 99.55% (DOE 2016) for various categories of transformers.
