### Applications of DC motors (simple numerical problems)

DC motors are electric motors that are powered by direct current (DC), such as from a battery or DC power supply. They have different types, features and applications depending on their design and construction. Here are some of the common applications of DC motors:

- **Computer equipment**: DC motors are used for CPU cooling fans and the drive motors for HDDs and CD-ROM drives. They provide low noise, high speed and precise control. 
- **Audio and video equipment**: DC motors are also used in audio CD, DVD, and Blu-ray players. They offer smooth rotation, low vibration and accurate positioning. 
- **Home appliances**: DC motors are used in various home appliances, such as vacuum cleaners, sewing machines, electric fans, hair dryers, etc. They provide high torque, variable speed and low power consumption.   
- **Automobiles**: DC motors are used in many parts of automobiles, such as wiper motors, power seat motors, power window motors, etc. They offer high reliability, durability and efficiency.  
- **Industrial machinery and medical equipment**: DC motors are used as servo motors in industrial robots, CNC machines, etc. They provide high precision, fast response and wide range of speed. DC motors are also used in fan motors in respirators and oxygen concentrators. They offer low noise, long life and stable operation.  

Depending on the type of DC motor, the applications may vary. There are four main types of DC motors: permanent magnet, series, shunt and compound. 

- **Permanent magnet DC motor**: This type of motor has a permanent magnet to create the magnetic field. It has a simple structure, low cost and high efficiency. It is suitable for low power and constant speed applications, such as toys, electric toothbrushes, etc. 
- **Series DC motor**: This type of motor has the field winding connected in series with the armature winding. It has a high starting torque and variable speed. It is suitable for high power and variable load applications, such as traction systems, cranes, air compressors, etc.   
- **Shunt DC motor**: This type of motor has the field winding connected in parallel with the armature winding. It has a low starting torque and constant speed. It is suitable for low power and constant load applications, such as fans, blowers, pumps, etc.  
- **Compound DC motor**: This type of motor has both series and shunt field windings. It has a combination of characteristics of both series and shunt motors. It can be adjusted for different speed and torque requirements. It is suitable for medium power and variable load applications, such as elevators, lathes, printing presses, etc.  

To solve simple numerical problems involving DC motors, we need to know some basic formulas and concepts, such as:

- **Back emf**: This is the voltage induced in the armature winding due to the rotation of the armature in the magnetic field. It opposes the applied voltage and limits the current in the circuit. It is given by:

  `E_b = PΦZN / 60A`

  where `E_b` is the back emf, `P` is the number of poles, `Φ` is the flux per pole, `Z` is the number of armature conductors, `N` is the speed in rpm, and `A` is the number of parallel paths in the armature. 

- **Torque**: This is the rotational force produced by the motor. It is proportional to the product of the armature current and the flux per pole. It is given by:

  `T = PΦI_a / 2πA`

  where `T` is the torque, `I_a` is the armature current. 

- **Power**: This is the rate of doing work by the motor. It is proportional to the product of the torque and the speed. It is given by:

  `P = 2πNT / 60`

  where `P` is the power.
