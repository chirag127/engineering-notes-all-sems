### Introduction of Switch Fuse Unit (SFU) for the notes of the Unit 5 - Electrical Installations in the subject of FUNDAMENTALS OF ELECTRICAL ENGINEERING

- A switch fuse unit (SFU) is a type of low voltage switchgear that combines a switch and a fuse in a single unit.
- SFU is used to protect and isolate electrical circuits from short circuits, overloads, and faults.
- SFU has one switch unit and one fuse unit. When the switch is operated, the contacts will get closed or opened, and then the supply will pass through or disconnect from the fuse unit to the output.
- SFU is also known as an ironclad switch, being made of iron. It may be double pole for controlling single phase two-wire circuits or triple pole for controlling three-phase, 3-wire circuits or triple pole with neutral link for controlling 3-phase, 4-wire circuits.
- The respective switches are known as double pole ironclad (DPIC), triple pole ironclad (TPIC), and triple pole with neutral link ironclad (TPNIC) switches.
- SFU can be mounted on a panel board, a distribution board, or a wall. It has a handle for manual operation and an indicator for showing the status of the switch and the fuse.
- SFU usually uses high rupturing capacity (HRC) fuses as back up fuses in this kind of units. The fuse rating can be selected based on the connected load and should be less than the switch rating. For example, a 32A fuse can be used for a 63A rating SFU unit.
- SFU provides a reliable and economical way of protecting and controlling electrical circuits in low voltage applications. It also reduces the maintenance and replacement costs of the switch and the fuse.
