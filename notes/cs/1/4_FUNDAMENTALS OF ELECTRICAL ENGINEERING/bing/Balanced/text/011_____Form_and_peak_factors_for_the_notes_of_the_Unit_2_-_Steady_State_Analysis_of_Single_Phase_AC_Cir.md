### Form and peak factors for the notes of the Unit 2 - Steady State Analysis of Single Phase AC Circuits in the subject of FUNDAMENTALS OF ELECTRICAL ENGINEERING

- An alternating current (AC) waveform is a periodic function that varies in magnitude and direction with time. The waveform can have different shapes, such as sinusoidal, triangular, square, etc.
- The peak value of an AC waveform is the maximum absolute value that it attains in one cycle. The peak value is denoted by Vp for voltage and Ip for current.
- The root mean square (RMS) value of an AC waveform is the effective value that produces the same heating effect as a direct current (DC) of the same magnitude. The RMS value is denoted by Vrms for voltage and Irms for current. The RMS value can be calculated by taking the square root of the average of the squares of the instantaneous values over one cycle.
- The average value of an AC waveform is the arithmetic mean of the instantaneous values over one cycle. The average value is denoted by Vavg for voltage and Iavg for current. The average value can be calculated by integrating the instantaneous values over one cycle and dividing by the period.
- The form factor of an AC waveform is the ratio of the RMS value to the average value. The form factor is denoted by Ff for voltage and current. The form factor can be used to describe the shape of the AC waveform. The form factor for a pure sinusoidal waveform is 1.11, while the form factor for a square waveform is 1.
- The peak factor of an AC waveform is the ratio of the peak value to the RMS value. The peak factor is denoted by Fp for voltage and current. The peak factor can be used to measure the peakiness or crest of the AC waveform. The peak factor for a pure sinusoidal waveform is 1.414, while the peak factor for a square waveform is 1.
