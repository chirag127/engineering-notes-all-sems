### Voltage and Current Sources

- A voltage source is a device that provides a constant voltage across its terminals, regardless of the current drawn from it . An example of a voltage source is a battery.
- A current source is a device that provides a constant current through its terminals, regardless of the voltage across it . An example of a current source is a solar cell.
- Both voltage and current sources are idealized models that do not exist in reality. In practice, every source has some internal resistance that limits its ability to maintain a constant voltage or current.
- The internal resistance of a voltage source is the resistance in series with the ideal voltage source. The internal resistance of a current source is the resistance in parallel with the ideal current source.
- The voltage and current sources can be classified into two types: independent and dependent. Independent sources are those that do not depend on any other quantity in the circuit. Dependent sources are those that depend on some other voltage or current in the circuit .

- The voltage and current sources are the basic elements that supply energy to the electrical circuits. The voltage and current sources can be used to analyze the behavior of the circuits using various methods such as Kirchhoff's laws, mesh analysis, nodal analysis, etc.
