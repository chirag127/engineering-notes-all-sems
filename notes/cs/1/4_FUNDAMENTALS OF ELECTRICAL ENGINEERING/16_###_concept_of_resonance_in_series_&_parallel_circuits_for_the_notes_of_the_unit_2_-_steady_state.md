### Concept of Resonance in series & parallel circuits for the notes of the Unit 2 - Steady State Analysis of Single Phase AC Circuits in the subject of FUNDAMENTALS OF ELECTRICAL ENGINEERING

Resonance in electrical circuits refers to the condition where the impedance of the circuit is at a minimum and the circuit is said to be in resonance. In series resonance circuits, the impedance of the circuit is at a minimum when the frequency of the applied voltage is equal to the natural frequency of the circuit. In parallel resonance circuits, the impedance of the circuit is at a minimum when the frequency of the applied voltage is equal to the resonant frequency of the circuit. The resonant frequency is determined by the values of the inductance and capacitance in the circuit. The resonant frequency is the frequency at which the circuit is most responsive to the applied voltage. The resonant frequency is also the frequency at which the energy stored in the circuit is at a maximum. The resonant frequency is an important concept in the analysis of AC circuits.
