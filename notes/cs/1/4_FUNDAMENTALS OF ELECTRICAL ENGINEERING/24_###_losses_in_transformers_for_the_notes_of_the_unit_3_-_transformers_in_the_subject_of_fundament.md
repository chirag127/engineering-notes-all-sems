### losses in transformers for the notes of the Unit 3 - Transformers in the subject of FUNDAMENTALS OF ELECTRICAL ENGINEERING

Transformers are electrical devices used to transfer electrical energy from one circuit to another by means of electromagnetic induction. In Unit 3 - Transformers of the subject of Fundamentals of Electrical Engineering, students learn about the various losses that occur in transformers. These losses include copper losses, iron losses, and stray losses. Copper losses are caused by the resistance of the transformer's windings, and result in a loss of energy due to heating of the windings. Iron losses are caused by the magnetic hysteresis and eddy currents in the transformer's core, and result in a loss of energy due to heating of the core. Stray losses are caused by the leakage of magnetic flux outside the transformer's core, and result in a loss of energy due to heating of the surrounding components. Understanding the sources of losses in transformers is important for students pursuing electrical engineering degrees, as it forms the basis for many advanced topics in electrical engineering, including power system analysis and design.
