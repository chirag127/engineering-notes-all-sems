### Cables and Bus-bars for the notes of the Unit 5 - Electrical Installations in the subject of FUNDAMENTALS OF ELECTRICAL ENGINEERING

Cables: Cables are conductors used to transmit electrical energy from one point to another. They are made of copper, aluminum, or other conductive materials and are covered with insulation to prevent electrical shock and short circuits.

Properties of Cables:
1. Conductivity: Cables must have high electrical conductivity to minimize energy losses.
2. Insulation: Cables must have proper insulation to prevent electrical shock and short circuits.
3. Flexibility: Cables must be flexible enough to be easily installed and bent, but not so flexible that they break easily.
4. Durability: Cables must be durable enough to withstand environmental conditions and mechanical stress.

Types of Cables:
1. Power cables: Used for transmitting high-voltage electrical energy.
2. Control cables: Used for transmitting low-voltage electrical signals.
3. Instrumentation cables: Used for transmitting electrical signals in instrumentation systems.

Bus-bars: Bus-bars are conductors used to distribute electrical energy within electrical systems. They are made of copper, aluminum, or other conductive materials and are designed to carry high currents with low resistance.

Properties of Bus-bars:
1. Conductivity: Bus-bars must have high electrical conductivity to minimize energy losses.
2. Strength: Bus-bars must be strong enough to carry the high currents they are designed for.
3. Resistance to corrosion: Bus-bars must be resistant to corrosion to ensure a long service life.

Types of Bus-bars:
1. Rigid bus-bars: Used for high-current applications where stability is required.
2. Flexible bus-bars: Used for applications where flexibility is required, such as in mobile electrical systems.
