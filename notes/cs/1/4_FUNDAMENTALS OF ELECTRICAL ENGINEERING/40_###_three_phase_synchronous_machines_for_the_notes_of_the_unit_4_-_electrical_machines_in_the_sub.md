### Three Phase Synchronous Machines for the notes of the Unit 4 - Electrical machines in the subject of FUNDAMENTALS OF ELECTRICAL ENGINEERING

Three-phase synchronous machines are AC electrical machines that operate on the principle of rotating magnetic fields. They are classified into two types: generator and motor. Generators convert mechanical energy into electrical energy, while motors convert electrical energy into mechanical energy. Three-phase synchronous machines have three stator windings, each displaced 120 degrees from each other, which produce a rotating magnetic field. The rotor, which is mounted on a shaft, is either a permanent magnet or has field windings that are supplied with DC current. The interaction between the stator and rotor magnetic fields results in torque production in the rotor, causing it to rotate. The speed of the rotor is synchronous with the frequency of the AC supply, hence the name “synchronous”. These machines are widely used in power generation, industrial drives, and renewable energy systems.
