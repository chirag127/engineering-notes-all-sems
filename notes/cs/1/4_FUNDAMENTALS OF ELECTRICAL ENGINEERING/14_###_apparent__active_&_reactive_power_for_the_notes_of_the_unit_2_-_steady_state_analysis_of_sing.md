### Apparent, active & reactive power for the notes of the Unit 2 - Steady State Analysis of Single Phase AC Circuits in the subject of FUNDAMENTALS OF ELECTRICAL ENGINEERING

Apparent, active, and reactive power are three important concepts in electrical engineering and are covered in Unit 2 - Steady State Analysis of Single Phase AC Circuits in the subject of Fundamentals of Electrical Engineering. Apparent power is the total power in an AC circuit, including both active power, which is the power used to perform work, and reactive power, which is the power used to maintain the electric field in an inductor or capacitor. Active power is measured in watts (W) and is the power used to perform work. Reactive power is measured in volt-amperes reactive (VAR) and is the power used to maintain the electric field in an inductor or capacitor. The apparent power is measured in volt-amperes (VA) and is the vector sum of the active and reactive power. Understanding the concept of apparent, active, and reactive power is essential for students pursuing electrical engineering degrees, as it forms the basis for many advanced topics in electrical engineering, including power system analysis and design.
