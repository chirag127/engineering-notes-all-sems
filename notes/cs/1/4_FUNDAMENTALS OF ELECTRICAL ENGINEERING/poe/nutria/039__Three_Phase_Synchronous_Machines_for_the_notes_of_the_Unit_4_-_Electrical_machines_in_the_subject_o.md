
### Three Phase Synchronous Machines 

* Synchronous machines are electrical machines used to convert electrical energy into mechanical energy or vice versa. 
* These machines are driven by direct current or alternating current, and they have a synchronous speed which is determined by the frequency of the applied voltage. 
* Synchronous machines can be classified into two main types: three-phase synchronous machines and single-phase synchronous machines. 
* Three-phase synchronous machines are the most common type of synchronous machines used in electrical engineering. 
* These machines are composed of three-phase windings which are connected in either star or delta configuration. 
* The three-phase synchronous machines can be further classified into two types: salient-pole and non-salient-pole machines. 
* Salient-pole machines have a rotor with salient poles, while non-salient-pole machines have a rotor with smooth surface. 
* The three-phase synchronous machines can be further classified into two types based on their operation: synchronous generators and synchronous motors. 
* Synchronous generators are used to convert mechanical energy into electrical energy, while synchronous motors are used to convert electrical energy into mechanical energy. 
* The three-phase synchronous machines can be further classified into two types based on their construction: wound-rotor and permanent magnet machines. 
* Wound-rotor machines have a rotor with windings, while permanent magnet machines have a rotor with permanent magnets. 
* The three-phase synchronous machines can be further classified into two types based on their applications: synchronous condensers and synchronous motors. 
* Synchronous condensers are used to provide reactive power support to the electrical grid, while synchronous motors are used in various applications such as pumps, fans, compressors and other industrial applications. 
* The three-phase synchronous machines can be further classified into two types based on their excitation systems: brushless and brush-type machines. 
* Brushless machines are self-excited, while brush-type machines require an external source of excitation. 
* The three-phase synchronous machines can be further classified into two types based on their control systems: direct-on-line and variable-frequency drives. 
* Direct-on-line machines are operated at a fixed speed, while variable-frequency drives are used to control the speed of the machine.
