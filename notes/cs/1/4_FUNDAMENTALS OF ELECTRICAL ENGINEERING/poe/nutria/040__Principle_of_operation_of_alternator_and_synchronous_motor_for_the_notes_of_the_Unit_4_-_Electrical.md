
### Principle of Operation of Alternator and Synchronous Motor

* An alternator is an electrical machine that converts mechanical energy into alternating current (AC) electrical energy. It works by using a rotating magnetic field to induce an electric current in a stationary set of windings.

* A synchronous motor is an AC motor in which, at steady state, the rotation of the shaft is synchronized with the frequency of the supply current. The stator windings are energized with a current that has the same frequency as the supply current, and the rotor windings are energized by a current that lags the stator current by a small amount. This lag is called the “synchronous reactance”.

* The principle of operation of an alternator is based on Faraday’s Law of Electromagnetic Induction. This law states that when a conductor moves in a magnetic field, an electric current is induced in the conductor. In an alternator, the conductor is a coil of wire (called a “stator”) that is mounted on the alternator’s rotor. As the rotor spins, the stator is exposed to the rotating magnetic field of the rotor’s magnets. This induces an electric current in the stator windings. 

* The principle of operation of a synchronous motor is based on the concept of “magnetic locking”. The stator windings are energized with a current that has the same frequency as the supply current, and the rotor windings are energized by a current that lags the stator current by a small amount. This lag is called the “synchronous reactance”. This lag causes the rotor to be “locked” in phase with the stator, and the rotor will then rotate in synchronism with the stator.
