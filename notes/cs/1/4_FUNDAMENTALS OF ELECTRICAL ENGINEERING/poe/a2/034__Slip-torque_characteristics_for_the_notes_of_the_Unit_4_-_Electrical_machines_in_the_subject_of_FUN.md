### Slip-torque characteristics

1. The torque developed by an induction motor depends upon the slip (s) between the rotor speed (N<sub>r</sub>) and the synchronous speed (N<sub>s</sub>).
2. The torque-slip characteristic for a 3-phase induction motor is shown in the figure below.

![Torque-slip characteristics of a 3-phase induction motor](https://www.electrical4u.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/01/torque-slip-characteristics.jpg)

3. When the slip is zero, i.e. N<sub>r</sub> = N<sub>s</sub>, the motor develops maximum torque called the pull-out torque or breakdown torque.
4. If the load torque is gradually increased from zero, the slip will increase resulting in a decrease of torque.
5. When the load torque equals the maximum torque, the slip becomes maximum. This corresponds to the maximum power output from the motor.
6. If the load torque is further increased, the motor comes to rest as the torque developed becomes zero at s = 1.
7. The flat portion of the characteristic indicates that the torque remains almost constant over a range of slip. This results in a uniform speed of rotation under varying loads. This is the normal working range of the motor.
