# FUNDAMENTALS OF ELECTRICAL ENGINEERING

1. Electric Charge: Electric charge is a fundamental property of matter that causes it to experience a force when placed in an electromagnetic field. Positively charged substances have fewer electrons than protons while negatively charged substances have more electrons than protons.

2. Electric Current: The flow of electric charge is an electric current. It is measured in amperes (A) and flows through a complete circuit from the negative terminal to the positive terminal. The amount of current depends on the voltage and resistance in the circuit.

3. Voltage: Voltage is the amount of "push" that drives the electric current. It is measured in volts (V) and is provided by a voltage source like a battery or power outlet. Higher voltage leads to higher current when resistance is constant.

4. Resistance: Resistance opposes the flow of electric current and is measured in ohms (Ω). It depends on the material, length, and cross-sectional area of the object. Resistance causes the electrical energy to be converted into heat and light. Lower resistance leads to higher current when voltage is constant.

5. Ohm's Law: Ohm's law states that current is directly proportional to voltage and inversely proportional to resistance. It is expressed as I=V/R where I is current in A, V is voltage in V, and R is resistance in Ω. Ohm's law is a basic law that describes how voltage, current, and resistance relate to each other in an electrical circuit.
