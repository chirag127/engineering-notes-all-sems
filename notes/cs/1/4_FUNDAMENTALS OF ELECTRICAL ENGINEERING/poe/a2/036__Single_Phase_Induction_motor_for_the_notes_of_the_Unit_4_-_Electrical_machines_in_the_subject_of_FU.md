### Single Phase Induction motor

- Single phase induction motors are widely used for lower power applications like fans, blowers, centrifugal pumps, washers, grinders etc. where split phase motors are used.
- These motors are more efficient, rugged, reliable and economical.
- The single phase induction motor is not self starting. It requires some starting mechanism to provide a rotating magnetic field. The two common types are:
- Split phase motor: It uses a starter winding or auxiliary winding in addition to the main winding. The starter winding has a higher resistance and inductance. Due to the phase difference between the currents in the two windings, a rotating magnetic field is produced which starts the motor. Once the motor picks up speed, the starter winding is disconnected using a centrifugal switch.
- Capacitor start motor: Here a capacitor is connected in series with the starter winding. The capacitor introduces a lead in the current of the starter winding which produces phase difference and resulting rotating magnetic field to start the motor. The capacitor is disconnected using a centrifugal switch once the motor accelerates to a certain speed.
- The speed of a single phase induction motor depends on the supply frequency and number of poles. At no load, the speed is slightly more than the synchronous speed. The performance of these motors is inferior to 3-phase induction motors.
