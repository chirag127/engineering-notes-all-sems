### Power factor for the notes of the Unit 2 - Steady State Analysis of Single Phase AC Circuits in the subject of FUNDAMENTALS OF ELECTRICAL ENGINEERING

- Power factor (PF) is the ratio of actual power to apparent power in an AC circuit.
- Apparent power (S) is the product of RMS voltage and RMS current. It is the total VA drawn from the supply.
- Actual power (P) is the power dissipated or delivered to the load.
- PF = P/S
- A PF of 1 indicates actual power equals apparent power, which means the circuit is fully utilizing the supply voltage and current. This occurs when the load is purely resistive.
- A PF of less than 1 indicates apparent power is greater than actual power, which means the circuit is not fully utilizing the supply voltage and current. This occurs when the load has reactive components like inductors or capacitors.
- A low PF means extra current is drawn from the supply, leading to higher losses and less efficient utilization of power.
- PF can be improved using power factor correction techniques like using capacitors or active power filters.
