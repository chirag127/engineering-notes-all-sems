### Applications (Numerical problems related to slip only)

In this section, we will discuss the applications of slip in electrical machines. Slip is an important parameter that determines the performance of electrical machines. We will solve some numerical problems related to slip to understand its practical applications. 

Here are some examples:

1. A 3-phase, 50 Hz, 4-pole induction motor runs at a speed of 1440 rpm. The stator winding is connected to a 3-phase supply of 400 V. Calculate the slip of the motor.
   - Solution:
     - Speed of the motor = 1440 rpm
     - Synchronous speed = (120 x frequency) / number of poles = (120 x 50) / 4 = 1500 rpm
     - Slip = (Synchronous speed - Actual speed) / Synchronous speed = (1500 - 1440) / 1500 = 0.04 or 4%

2. A 3-phase, 60 Hz, 6-pole induction motor has a full load speed of 1150 rpm. Calculate the slip of the motor.
   - Solution:
     - Synchronous speed = (120 x frequency) / number of poles = (120 x 60) / 6 = 1200 rpm
     - Slip = (Synchronous speed - Actual speed) / Synchronous speed = (1200 - 1150) / 1200 = 0.042 or 4.2%

3. An induction motor has a rotor resistance of 0.2 ohms and a rotor reactance of 0.5 ohms. The stator voltage is 220 V and the frequency is 50 Hz. The motor is drawing a current of 10 A at a power factor of 0.8 lagging. Calculate the slip and the rotor current.
   - Solution:
     - Impedance of the rotor = R + jX = 0.2 + j0.5 ohms
     - Voltage across the rotor = Stator voltage / (1 - s) = 220 / (1 - s)
     - Rotor current = V / Z = (220 / (1 - s)) / (0.2 + j0.5) = (220 / (1 - s)) / (0.559 + j1.396)
     - Magnitude of rotor current = 10 A / 0.8 = 12.5 A
     - Power factor angle = cos^-1(0.8) = 36.87 degrees
     - Real power = 220 x 10 x 0.8 = 1760 W
     - Reactive power = 220 x 10 x sin(36.87) = 1325.8 VAR
     - Apparent power = 220 x 10 / 0.8 = 2750 VA
     - Current angle = tan^-1(1325.8 / 1760) = 35.77 degrees
     - Total current = 10 / cos(35.77) = 12.5 A / 0.809 = 15.43 A
     - Slip = (synchronous speed - actual speed) / synchronous speed
     - Actual speed = (120 x frequency) / number of poles x (1 - s)
     - Solving the equations, we get slip = 0.04 or 4% and rotor current = 9.029 A

By solving such numerical problems, we can understand the practical applications of slip in electrical machines. These problems are important for the exams and for understanding the concepts of electrical machines.
