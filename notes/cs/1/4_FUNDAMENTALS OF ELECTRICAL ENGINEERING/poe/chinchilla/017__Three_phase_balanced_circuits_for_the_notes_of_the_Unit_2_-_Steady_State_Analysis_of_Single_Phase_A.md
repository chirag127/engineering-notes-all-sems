### Three Phase Balanced Circuits

Three phase circuits are extensively used in electrical power systems due to their advantages over single-phase circuits. These circuits have three conductors, each carrying an alternating current of the same magnitude and frequency, but with a phase difference of 120 degrees.

A balanced three-phase circuit is one in which all three phases have equal voltages and currents, and the loads are evenly distributed across the three phases. The analysis of such circuits is simpler as compared to unbalanced circuits.

Here are some important points to remember about three-phase balanced circuits:

- In a balanced three-phase circuit, the line voltage is equal to the phase voltage multiplied by the square root of three.
- The line current is equal to the phase current.
- The power in a balanced three-phase circuit is given by P = √3 V L I L cos(θ), where V L is the line voltage, I L is the line current, and cos(θ) is the power factor.
- The total power in a balanced three-phase circuit is the sum of the power in each phase.
- The power factor of a balanced three-phase circuit is the same as the power factor of each phase.
- The power in a balanced three-phase circuit can be measured using a three-phase wattmeter.
- The three-phase wattmeter measures the total power in a balanced three-phase circuit by using two wattmeters, one for measuring the power in two phases and another for measuring the power in the third phase.
- The total power measured by the two wattmeters is added to get the total power in the circuit.

In conclusion, three-phase balanced circuits are important in electrical power systems and their analysis is simpler as compared to unbalanced circuits. Understanding the concepts related to these circuits is crucial for the study of electrical engineering.
