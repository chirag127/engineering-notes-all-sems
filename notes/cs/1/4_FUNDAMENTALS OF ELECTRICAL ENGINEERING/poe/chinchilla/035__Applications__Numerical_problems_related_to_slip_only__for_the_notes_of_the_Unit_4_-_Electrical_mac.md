### Applications (Numerical Problems related to Slip only)

In the study of electrical machines, the concept of slip plays a vital role in understanding the performance of induction motors. Slip is defined as the difference between synchronous speed and actual rotor speed, expressed as a percentage of synchronous speed. In this section, we will discuss some numerical problems related to slip that are commonly encountered in the field of electrical engineering.

1. Calculation of Slip: One of the most basic numerical problems related to slip is the calculation of slip itself. This can be done using the following formula:

   `Slip = (Ns - Nr) / Ns * 100%`

   Where Ns is the synchronous speed of the motor in revolutions per minute (RPM), and Nr is the actual rotor speed in RPM.

2. Calculation of Rotor Speed: Another common numerical problem is the calculation of rotor speed given the synchronous speed and slip. This can be done using the following formula:

   `Nr = (1 - Slip/100) * Ns`

   Where Ns is the synchronous speed in RPM, and Slip is the slip percentage.

3. Calculation of Rotor Resistance: The rotor resistance is an important parameter in the performance of induction motors. It can be calculated using the following formula:

   `R2 = (Vt / It) / (1 - s)`

   Where Vt is the terminal voltage of the motor, It is the total current drawn by the motor, and s is the slip percentage.

4. Calculation of Torque: The torque produced by an induction motor is proportional to the square of the current and slip. Therefore, the torque can be calculated using the following formula:

   `T = (3 * Vt^2 * R2 / s) / (2 * pi * f)`

   Where Vt is the terminal voltage, R2 is the rotor resistance, s is the slip percentage, f is the frequency of the power supply, and pi is the mathematical constant pi.

5. Calculation of Efficiency: The efficiency of an induction motor is defined as the ratio of output power to input power. It can be calculated using the following formula:

   `Efficiency = (Output power / Input power) * 100%`

   Where Output power is the mechanical power produced by the motor, and Input power is the electrical power supplied to the motor. The output power can be calculated as:

   `Output power = T * Nr * 2 * pi / 60`

   Where T is the torque produced by the motor, Nr is the rotor speed in RPM, and pi is the mathematical constant pi.

These are some of the common numerical problems related to slip that are encountered in the study of electrical machines. By understanding these problems and their solutions, students can gain a deeper understanding of the performance characteristics of induction motors and their applications in various industries.
