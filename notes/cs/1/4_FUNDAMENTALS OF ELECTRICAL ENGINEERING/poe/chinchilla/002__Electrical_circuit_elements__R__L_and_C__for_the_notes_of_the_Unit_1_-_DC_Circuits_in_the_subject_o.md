### Electrical circuit elements (R, L and C)

In the study of electrical engineering, understanding the behavior of electrical circuit elements is crucial. In this unit, we will focus on the behavior of three passive circuit elements: resistors (R), inductors (L), and capacitors (C). Here are some important points to keep in mind:

#### Resistors (R)
- A resistor is a passive circuit element that opposes the flow of electrical current.
- The unit of resistance is the ohm (Ω).
- Resistors can be connected in series or parallel, and their combined resistance can be calculated using Ohm's Law.
- Resistors are used in circuits to control the flow of current, limit voltage, and dissipate power.

#### Inductors (L)
- An inductor is a passive circuit element that opposes changes in current.
- The unit of inductance is the henry (H).
- Inductors can store energy in a magnetic field, and their behavior is governed by Faraday's Law of Induction.
- The impedance of an inductor increases with frequency.
- Inductors are used in circuits to filter signals, store energy, and control the behavior of motors and generators.

#### Capacitors (C)
- A capacitor is a passive circuit element that stores electrical charge.
- The unit of capacitance is the farad (F).
- Capacitors can store energy in an electric field, and their behavior is governed by Coulomb's Law.
- The impedance of a capacitor decreases with frequency.
- Capacitors are used in circuits to filter signals, store energy, and control the behavior of motors and generators.

#### Conclusion
Understanding the behavior of resistors, inductors, and capacitors is fundamental to the study of electrical circuits. These elements can be combined in a variety of ways to create complex circuits that exhibit interesting and useful behaviors. In the next unit, we will explore the behavior of these elements in AC circuits.
