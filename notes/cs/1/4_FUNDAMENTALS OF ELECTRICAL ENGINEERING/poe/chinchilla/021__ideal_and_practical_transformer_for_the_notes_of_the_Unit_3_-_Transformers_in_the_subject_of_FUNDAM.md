### Ideal and Practical Transformer

Transformers are an essential component of the electrical power system as they help in the efficient transmission and distribution of electrical power. A transformer is a device that transfers electrical energy from one circuit to another through electromagnetic induction. It is designed to change the voltage level of an alternating current (AC) power supply.

There are two types of transformers - ideal transformer and practical transformer. In this unit, we will discuss the differences between the two and how they operate.

#### Ideal Transformer

An ideal transformer is a theoretical concept of a transformer that operates without any losses. It is designed to have 100% efficiency, which means that all the electrical power input to the transformer is transferred to the output without any losses. Some of the characteristics of an ideal transformer are:

- It has no resistance in its windings, which means that there are no copper losses.
- It has no magnetic losses, which means that there are no iron losses.
- It has perfect coupling between the primary and secondary windings, which means that all the magnetic flux generated by the primary winding passes through the secondary winding.

#### Practical Transformer

A practical transformer is a real transformer that operates with some losses due to the physical limitations of the materials used in its construction. Some of the characteristics of a practical transformer are:

- It has resistance in its windings, which means that there are copper losses due to the flow of current through the windings.
- It has magnetic losses due to the hysteresis and eddy currents in the core material, which results in iron losses.
- It has imperfect coupling between the primary and secondary windings due to leakage flux, which results in losses.

#### Differences between Ideal and Practical Transformer

The following are the main differences between an ideal transformer and a practical transformer:

- Efficiency: An ideal transformer has 100% efficiency, whereas a practical transformer has some losses, and its efficiency is always less than 100%.
- Resistance: An ideal transformer has no resistance in its windings, whereas a practical transformer has resistance in its windings, resulting in copper losses.
- Magnetic losses: An ideal transformer has no magnetic losses, whereas a practical transformer has magnetic losses due to hysteresis and eddy currents in the core material.
- Coupling: An ideal transformer has perfect coupling between the primary and secondary windings, whereas a practical transformer has imperfect coupling due to leakage flux, resulting in losses.

#### Conclusion

In conclusion, transformers are an essential component of the electrical power system, and there are two types of transformers - ideal transformer and practical transformer. An ideal transformer is a theoretical concept that operates without any losses, whereas a practical transformer is a real transformer that operates with some losses due to physical limitations. Understanding the characteristics and differences between the two types of transformers is crucial in designing and operating electrical power systems efficiently.
