### Apparent, Active & Reactive Power

In single-phase AC circuits, we measure three types of power: apparent power, active power, and reactive power. These three types of power are essential to understand the power consumption and power factor of electrical devices.

#### Apparent Power

Apparent power is the total amount of power consumed by an AC circuit. It is the product of the rms voltage and rms current of the circuit. The unit of apparent power is volt-ampere (VA). Apparent power includes both active power and reactive power.

#### Active Power

Active power is the actual power consumed by an AC circuit. It is the product of the rms voltage, rms current, and the cosine of the phase angle between them. The unit of active power is watt (W). Active power is also known as real power.

#### Reactive Power

Reactive power is the power consumed by an AC circuit that is not used to do work. It is the product of the rms voltage, rms current, and the sine of the phase angle between them. The unit of reactive power is volt-ampere reactive (VAR). Reactive power is required to maintain the magnetic fields in inductive devices like motors and transformers.

#### Power Factor

Power factor is the ratio of active power to the apparent power of an AC circuit. It is a measure of how effectively the circuit is using the supplied power. Power factor ranges from 0 to 1, where 1 indicates that the circuit is using all the supplied power efficiently. A low power factor indicates that the circuit is not using the supplied power efficiently, resulting in energy losses and increased power consumption.

#### Conclusion

Understanding the concepts of apparent power, active power, reactive power, and power factor is essential in the analysis and design of electrical circuits. These concepts help in determining the power consumption and efficiency of electrical devices.
