### Power factor for the notes of the Unit 2 - Steady State Analysis of Single Phase AC Circuits in the subject of FUNDAMENTALS OF ELECTRICAL ENGINEERING

Power factor is a measure of how effectively an AC electrical power system uses the available power. In Fundamentals of Electrical Engineering, Unit 2 on Steady State Analysis of Single Phase AC Circuits, it is important to understand the concept of power factor.

1. Definition: Power factor is defined as the ratio of real power (P) to apparent power (S) in an AC electrical power system. It is a dimensionless quantity and is expressed as a decimal or a percentage.

PF = P/S

2. Importance: Power factor is important because it affects the efficiency of an AC electrical power system. A low power factor means that a significant portion of the available power is being wasted and not used effectively.

3. Types of Power Factor: There are two types of power factor: leading power factor and lagging power factor. Leading power factor occurs when the current leads the voltage, while lagging power factor occurs when the current lags the voltage.

4. Correction of Power Factor: Power factor correction is the process of improving the power factor of an AC electrical power system. This can be done by adding capacitors or inductors to the system to shift the phase angle between the current and voltage.

Applications of Power Factor:

1. Electrical Utilities: Power factor is important for electrical utilities because it affects the amount of current that must be supplied to meet the demands of customers. A low power factor means that the utility must supply more current, which increases the cost of electricity.

2. Industrial Plants: Power factor is important for industrial plants because it affects the efficiency of their electrical systems. A low power factor means that more energy is wasted, which increases the cost of electricity.

3. Electrical Motors: Power factor is important for electrical motors because it affects their efficiency. A low power factor means that more energy is wasted, which increases the cost of electricity and reduces the life of the motor.
