The Electron Transport System (ETS) and Oxidative Phosphorylation are key components of cellular respiration, the process by which cells produce energy from food molecules. The ETS is a series of protein complexes in the inner membrane of mitochondria that transfer electrons from NADH and FADH2 to oxygen, resulting in the production of ATP.

Oxidative Phosphorylation is the process by which ATP is produced through the transfer of electrons in the ETS, which drives the production of ATP. The transfer of electrons creates a proton gradient across the inner mitochondrial membrane, which is used to generate ATP through the action of ATP synthase.

The ETS and Oxidative Phosphorylation are integral components of cellular respiration, which also includes glycolysis, the formation of acetyl co-A, and the Krebs cycle. These processes work together to produce ATP, the main energy currency of cells.

Factors that can affect respiration include the availability of oxygen, the presence of inhibitors, and changes in temperature and pH.
