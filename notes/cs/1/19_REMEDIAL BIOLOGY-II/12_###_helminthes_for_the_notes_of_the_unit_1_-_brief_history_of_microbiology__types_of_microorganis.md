Helminths:
- Helminths are a group of multicellular parasitic worms that include roundworms (nematodes), flatworms (trematodes), and tapeworms (cestodes).
- Helminths can infect humans and other animals, causing a range of diseases such as intestinal worms and blood flukes.
- Helminths have a complex life cycle that often involves multiple hosts, with the adult worms living in the intestines or other organs of the host.
- Helminths can have a significant impact on human health, causing malnutrition, anemia, and other health problems.
- Helminths are treated with antiparasitic drugs, but drug resistance is a growing problem.
- Helminths are also being studied for their potential to be used in the treatment of autoimmune and inflammatory diseases.
- Understanding the biology and life cycle of helminths is important for the development of effective treatment and control strategies.
