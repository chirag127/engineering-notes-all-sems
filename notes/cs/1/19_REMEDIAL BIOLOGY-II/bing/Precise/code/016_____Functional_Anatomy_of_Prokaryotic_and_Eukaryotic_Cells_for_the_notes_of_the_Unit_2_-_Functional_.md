### Functional Anatomy of Prokaryotic and Eukaryotic Cells

Unit 2 - Functional Anatomy of Prokaryotic and Eukaryotic Cells: Size, shape, and arrangement of bacterial cells. Structure and function of cells in the subject of REMEDIAL BIOLOGY-II KCS

- Prokaryotic cells are generally smaller and simpler in structure than eukaryotic cells.
- Prokaryotic cells lack a nucleus and other membrane-bound organelles.
- The genetic material of prokaryotic cells is contained in a single, circular chromosome located in the nucleoid region of the cell.
- Prokaryotic cells have a cell wall that provides shape and protection.
- The size, shape, and arrangement of bacterial cells can vary greatly. Some common shapes include cocci (spherical), bacilli (rod-shaped), and spirilla (spiral-shaped).
- Eukaryotic cells are generally larger and more complex than prokaryotic cells.
- Eukaryotic cells have a nucleus and other membrane-bound organelles, such as mitochondria, endoplasmic reticulum, and Golgi apparatus.
- The genetic material of eukaryotic cells is contained in multiple, linear chromosomes located in the nucleus.
- Some eukaryotic cells have a cell wall, while others do not.
- The structure and function of cells can vary greatly among different types of eukaryotes, such as animals, plants, fungi, and protists.
