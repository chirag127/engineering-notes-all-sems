### Enzymes

Enzymes are biological catalysts that speed up chemical reactions in living organisms. They are proteins that are folded into complex shapes, allowing smaller molecules to fit into them. The place where these substrate molecules fit is called the active site.

Some key points to remember about enzymes:

1. Enzymes are specific to a particular substrate due to their shape.
2. They can be denatured by changes in temperature or pH, which alters their shape.
3. Enzyme activity can be affected by the presence of inhibitors or activators.

In the context of catabolic and anabolic reactions, enzymes play a crucial role in breaking down larger molecules into smaller ones (catabolism) or building up smaller molecules into larger ones (anabolism). This is important for energy production and carbohydrate metabolism.

Lipid and protein catabolism also involve the action of enzymes to break down these macromolecules into their constituent parts. The energy produced from these reactions can then be used by the cell in various metabolic pathways.

Overall, enzymes are essential for the integration of metabolism and the efficient use of energy within living organisms. They are a key component of the subject of Remedial Biology-II KCS, specifically in the study of Unit 3 - Catabolic & Anabolic Reactions.
