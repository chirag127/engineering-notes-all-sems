### ATP

- ATP stands for Adenosine Triphosphate.
- ATP is a molecule that carries energy within cells.
- It is the main source of energy for most cellular processes.
- The structure of ATP consists of an adenine molecule, a ribose sugar, and three phosphate groups.
- The energy stored in ATP is released when the bond between the second and third phosphate groups is broken, forming ADP (Adenosine Diphosphate) and a free phosphate group.
- This process is called hydrolysis and releases energy that can be used by the cell.
- ATP can be regenerated from ADP and a free phosphate group through a process called phosphorylation.
- The formation of ATP occurs in the mitochondria through cellular respiration, which includes processes such as glycolysis, the formation of acetyl co-A, the Krebs cycle, the electron transport system, and oxidative phosphorylation.
- Factors that can affect cellular respiration and the production of ATP include the availability of oxygen, nutrients, and the presence of inhibitors or uncouplers.
