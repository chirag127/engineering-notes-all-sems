## Unit 2 - Functional Anatomy of Prokaryotic and Eukaryotic Cells

### Size, shape, and arrangement of bacterial cells

- Bacterial cells are typically small, with a size range of 0.2-2.0 micrometers in diameter.
- Bacterial cells come in a variety of shapes, including spherical (cocci), rod-shaped (bacilli), and spiral (spirilla).
- The arrangement of bacterial cells is determined by their plane of division and can include pairs (diplococci), chains (streptococci), clusters (staphylococci), and tetrads (groups of four cells).
- The shape and arrangement of bacterial cells can be used to help identify and classify different species of bacteria.

### Structure and function of cells

- Cells are the basic unit of life and are responsible for carrying out all of the functions necessary for an organism to survive.
- Cells can be divided into two main categories: prokaryotic and eukaryotic.
- Prokaryotic cells, such as bacteria, are simple in structure and lack a nucleus and other membrane-bound organelles.
- Eukaryotic cells, such as those found in plants and animals, are more complex and contain a nucleus and other membrane-bound organelles.
- The structure of a cell is closely related to its function. For example, cells that are involved in movement, such as muscle cells, have a large number of mitochondria to provide the energy needed for movement.
- Cells also have specialized structures, such as the cell wall in plant cells, that help them carry out their specific functions.
