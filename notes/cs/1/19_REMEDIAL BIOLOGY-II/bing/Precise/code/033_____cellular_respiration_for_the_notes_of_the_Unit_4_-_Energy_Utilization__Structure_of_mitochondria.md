### Cellular Respiration

Cellular respiration is the process by which cells convert nutrients into energy that the cell can use to perform its functions. This process takes place in the mitochondria, which are organelles found in the cytoplasm of eukaryotic cells.

#### Structure of Mitochondria

- Mitochondria are double-membrane-bound organelles.
- The outer membrane is smooth and covers the organelle.
- The inner membrane is folded into cristae, which increases the surface area for cellular respiration.
- The space between the two membranes is called the intermembrane space.
- The space enclosed by the inner membrane is called the matrix.

#### Cellular Respiration

- Cellular respiration can be divided into four stages: Glycolysis, Formation of Acetyl Co-A, Krebs Cycle, and Electron Transport System and Oxidative Phosphorylation.
- Glycolysis takes place in the cytoplasm and involves the breakdown of glucose into two molecules of pyruvate.
- The pyruvate is then transported into the mitochondria, where it is converted into acetyl Co-A.
- Acetyl Co-A then enters the Krebs Cycle, which takes place in the matrix of the mitochondria.
- The Krebs Cycle produces ATP, NADH, and FADH2.
- The NADH and FADH2 then enter the Electron Transport System, which takes place in the inner mitochondrial membrane.
- The Electron Transport System produces ATP through the process of Oxidative Phosphorylation.

#### Relationship of Carbohydrate Metabolism to Other Compounds

- Carbohydrate metabolism is closely related to the metabolism of other compounds, such as lipids and proteins.
- When the body has excess glucose, it can be stored as glycogen or converted into fat.
- When the body needs energy, it can break down glycogen or fat to produce glucose.
- Proteins can also be broken down to produce glucose, but this is not the preferred source of energy for the body.

#### ATP

- ATP (adenosine triphosphate) is the main energy currency of the cell.
- ATP is produced during cellular respiration and is used to power cellular processes.
- ATP is composed of an adenine base, a ribose sugar, and three phosphate groups.
- The energy stored in ATP is released when the bond between the second and third phosphate groups is broken.

#### Factors Affecting Respiration

- Several factors can affect the rate of cellular respiration, including temperature, oxygen availability, and the availability of nutrients.
- As temperature increases, the rate of cellular respiration increases until an optimum temperature is reached.
- Oxygen is required for the Electron Transport System, so a lack of oxygen can slow down or stop cellular respiration.
- The availability of nutrients, such as glucose, can also affect the rate of cellular respiration.
