### Nonproteobacteria Gram –ve

- The majority of the gram-negative bacteria belong to the phylum Proteobacteria. Those that do not are called the nonproteobacteria.
- Nonproteobacteria can be either Gram-negative or Gram-positive.
- Metabolically, nonproteobacteria are either photoautotrophs or chemoheterotrophs.
- Cyanobacteria are photoautotrophic nonproteobacteria.
- Chemoheterotrophic nonproteobacteria can be either motile or nonmotile.
- Four classes of gram-negative nonproteobacteria are Chlamydia, the spirochetes, the CFB group, and the Planctomycetes.
- The gram-negative nonproteobacteria of the genera Cytophaga, Fusobacterium, and Bacteroides are classified together as a phylum and called the CFB group.
- Although they are phylogenetically diverse, bacteria of the CFB group share some similarities in the sequence of nucleotides in their DNA.
- Gram-negative nonproteobacteria include the taxa spirochetes; the Cytophaga, Fusobacterium, Bacteroides group; Planctomycetes; and many representatives of phototrophic bacteria.
- Spirochetes are motile, spiral bacteria with a long, narrow body; they are difficult or impossible to culture.
