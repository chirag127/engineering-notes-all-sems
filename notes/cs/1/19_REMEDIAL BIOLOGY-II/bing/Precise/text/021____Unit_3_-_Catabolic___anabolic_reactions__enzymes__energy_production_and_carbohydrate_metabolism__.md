## Unit 3 - Catabolic & Anabolic Reactions: Enzymes, Energy Production and Carbohydrate Metabolism. Lipid & Protein Catabolism, Energy Production Mechanism, Metabolic Diversity & Pathways of Energy Use. Integration of Metabolism

1. **Catabolic reactions** are the set of metabolic pathways that break down molecules into smaller units and release energy. These reactions involve the breakdown of complex organic molecules, such as carbohydrates, lipids, and proteins, into simpler molecules, such as carbon dioxide, water, and ammonia.

2. **Anabolic reactions**, on the other hand, are the set of metabolic pathways that construct molecules from smaller units and require energy. These reactions involve the synthesis of complex molecules from simpler ones, such as the synthesis of proteins from amino acids.

3. **Enzymes** are biological catalysts that speed up chemical reactions in the body. They are essential for both catabolic and anabolic reactions, as they lower the activation energy required for the reaction to occur.

4. **Energy production** in the body occurs through the process of cellular respiration, which involves the breakdown of glucose to produce ATP (adenosine triphosphate), the main source of energy for cells.

5. **Carbohydrate metabolism** refers to the various biochemical processes responsible for the formation, breakdown, and interconversion of carbohydrates in living organisms. The most important carbohydrate is glucose, which can be broken down via glycolysis, the citric acid cycle, and oxidative phosphorylation to produce ATP.

6. **Lipid catabolism** involves the breakdown of lipids, such as triglycerides, into glycerol and fatty acids. These fatty acids can then be further broken down through beta-oxidation to produce ATP.

7. **Protein catabolism** involves the breakdown of proteins into amino acids, which can then be used for energy production or for the synthesis of new proteins.

8. **Energy production mechanisms** vary among different organisms. Some organisms, such as plants, use photosynthesis to convert sunlight into chemical energy. Others, such as animals, use cellular respiration to produce ATP from glucose.

9. **Metabolic diversity** refers to the variety of metabolic pathways and processes that exist among different organisms. This diversity allows for the utilization of a wide range of energy sources and the production of a wide range of metabolic products.

10. **Pathways of energy use** refer to the various ways in which cells can use energy, such as for the synthesis of new molecules, the transport of molecules across membranes, and the performance of mechanical work.

11. **Integration of metabolism** refers to the coordination and regulation of the various metabolic pathways in the body. This integration ensures that the body's energy needs are met and that the various metabolic processes are balanced.
