### Brief history of microbiology
- Microbiology is the study of microorganisms, which are unicellular or cell-cluster microscopic organisms.
- The field of microbiology can be traced back to the invention of the microscope in the 17th century.
- Antonie van Leeuwenhoek is considered the father of microbiology for his pioneering work in the field.
- Louis Pasteur and Robert Koch made significant contributions to the field in the 19th century, including the development of the germ theory of disease.

### Types of microorganisms
- Microorganisms can be classified into several groups, including bacteria, archaea, fungi, protozoa, algae, and viruses.
- Bacteria and archaea are prokaryotes, while fungi, protozoa, and algae are eukaryotes.
- Viruses are not considered living organisms and are not classified as prokaryotes or eukaryotes.

### Basic idea of domain bacteria
- Bacteria are single-celled prokaryotic organisms.
- They are found in nearly every environment on Earth, including soil, water, and the human body.
- Bacteria play important roles in various ecosystems, including decomposition and nitrogen fixation.

### Proteobacteria
- Proteobacteria is a major phylum of gram-negative bacteria.
- They include a wide variety of pathogens, such as Escherichia coli and Salmonella.
- Proteobacteria also include many important nitrogen-fixing bacteria.

### Non-proteobacteria Gram –ve and Gram +ve bacteria
- Non-proteobacteria gram-negative bacteria include several important phyla, such as Bacteroidetes and Cyanobacteria.
- Gram-positive bacteria are characterized by a thick peptidoglycan layer in their cell walls.
- They include several important genera, such as Staphylococcus and Streptococcus.

### Lichens
- Lichens are symbiotic associations between a fungus and a photosynthetic partner, usually an alga or a cyanobacterium.
- They are found in a wide variety of environments, including deserts, tundras, and rainforests.
- Lichens play important roles in their ecosystems, including soil formation and nutrient cycling.

### Algae
- Algae are a diverse group of aquatic photosynthetic organisms.
- They range in size from single-celled organisms to large multicellular forms, such as seaweeds.
- Algae play important roles in aquatic ecosystems, including oxygen production and serving as a food source for other organisms.

### Protozoa
- Protozoa are single-celled eukaryotic organisms.
- They are found in a wide variety of environments, including soil, water, and the human body.
- Some protozoa are parasites, while others are free-living.

### Helminthes
- Helminthes are parasitic worms, including roundworms, tapeworms, and flukes.
- They are found in a wide variety of hosts, including humans and other animals.
- Helminthes can cause a variety of diseases, including intestinal infections and tissue damage.

### Viral structures
- Viruses are non-living infectious agents.
- They consist of a nucleic acid genome surrounded by a protein coat, called a capsid.
- Some viruses also have an outer envelope composed of lipids and proteins.

### Viral multiplication
- Viruses can only replicate inside a host cell.
- The virus attaches to the host cell and injects its genetic material into the cell.
- The host cell's machinery is then used to replicate the virus's genetic material and produce new virus particles.

### Role of microorganisms in the production of industrial chemicals and pharmaceuticals
- Microorganisms play important roles in the production of various industrial chemicals and pharmaceuticals.
- For example, bacteria are used to produce antibiotics, such as penicillin and streptomycin.
- Microorganisms are also used in the production of various chemicals, such as ethanol and acetone.
