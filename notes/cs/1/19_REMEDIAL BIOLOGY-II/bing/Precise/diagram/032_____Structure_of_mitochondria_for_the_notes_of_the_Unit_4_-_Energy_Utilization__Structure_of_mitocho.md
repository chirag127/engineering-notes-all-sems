### Structure of Mitochondria

- Mitochondria are small organelles, often between 0.75 and 3 micrometers, and are not visible under the microscope unless they are stained.
- They have a double membrane structure, an outer membrane and an inner membrane, both made of phospholipid layers .
- The inner membrane is folded into cristae, where the reactions creating mitochondrial energy take place.
- The space between the inner and outer membrane is called the intermembrane space.
- Inside the inner membrane is a gel-like material called the matrix.
- Mitochondria are found in the cells of nearly every eukaryotic organism, including plants and animals.
- Cells that require a lot of energy, such as muscle cells, can contain hundreds or thousands of mitochondria.
- A few types of cells, such as red blood cells, lack mitochondria entirely.
- Mitochondria use aerobic respiration to generate adenosine triphosphate (ATP), which is used throughout the cell as a source of chemical energy.
