### Arrangement of Bacterial Cells

Bacterial cells can be arranged in several ways, depending on their shape and the plane of division during cell growth. Here are some common arrangements of bacterial cells:

1. **Singles**: Single cells that are not attached to each other.
2. **Pairs**: Two cells attached to each other.
3. **Chains**: Cells attached end-to-end, forming a chain.
4. **Clusters**: Cells attached in a grape-like cluster.
5. **Tetrads**: Four cells arranged in a square.
6. **Sarcinae**: Eight cells arranged in a cube.

These arrangements are commonly observed in cocci (spherical) bacteria. Rod-shaped bacteria, on the other hand, are usually found in singles or chains.

It is important to note that the arrangement of bacterial cells can provide valuable information for their identification and classification. For example, Streptococcus bacteria are known for their chain arrangement, while Staphylococcus bacteria are known for their cluster arrangement.
