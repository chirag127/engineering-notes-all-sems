### Electron Transport System and Oxidative Phosphorylation

The electron transport system (ETS) and oxidative phosphorylation are the final stages of cellular respiration, which occurs in the mitochondria. These processes are responsible for the majority of ATP production in the cell.

1. **Electron Transport System (ETS):** The ETS is a series of protein complexes and electron carriers located in the inner mitochondrial membrane. Electrons from NADH and FADH2, produced during glycolysis, the formation of acetyl CoA, and the Krebs cycle, are transferred to the ETS. As the electrons move through the ETS, they release energy, which is used to pump protons across the inner mitochondrial membrane, creating a proton gradient.

2. **Oxidative Phosphorylation:** This process uses the energy from the proton gradient to drive the production of ATP. The protons flow back across the inner mitochondrial membrane through an enzyme called ATP synthase, which uses the energy to synthesize ATP from ADP and inorganic phosphate.

3. **ATP Production:** The ETS and oxidative phosphorylation can produce up to 34 molecules of ATP per molecule of glucose. This is in addition to the 4 ATP molecules produced during glycolysis and the Krebs cycle, bringing the total ATP yield to 38 molecules per molecule of glucose.

4. **Factors Affecting Respiration:** The rate of cellular respiration can be affected by various factors, including the availability of oxygen, the concentration of substrates such as glucose, and the activity of enzymes involved in the process.

In summary, the electron transport system and oxidative phosphorylation are essential processes in cellular respiration, responsible for the majority of ATP production in the cell. These processes occur in the mitochondria and are regulated by various factors.
