### Shape - Unit 2: Functional Anatomy of Prokaryotic and Eukaryotic Cells

- The shape of bacterial cells is determined by their cell wall.
- Bacterial cells can have a variety of shapes, including spherical (coccus), rod-shaped (bacillus), and spiral (spirillum).
- The shape of a bacterial cell can provide clues about its lifestyle and behavior.
- For example, rod-shaped bacteria are often motile, while spherical bacteria are often found in clusters or chains.
- The arrangement of bacterial cells can also provide information about their behavior.
- For example, bacteria that form clusters or chains are often found in environments where they need to stick together for protection or to access nutrients.
- The structure and function of cells are important topics in the study of REMEDIAL BIOLOGY-II KCS. Understanding the shape, size, and arrangement of bacterial cells can provide valuable insights into their behavior and interactions with their environment.
