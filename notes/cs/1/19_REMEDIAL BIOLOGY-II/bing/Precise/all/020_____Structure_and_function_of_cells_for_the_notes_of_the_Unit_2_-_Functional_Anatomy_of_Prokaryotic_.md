# Structure and function of cells

Unit 2 - Functional Anatomy of Prokaryotic and Eukaryotic Cells: Size, shape, and arrangement of bacterial cells. Structure and function of cells in the subject of REMEDIAL BIOLOGY-II KCS

- Cells are the basic unit of life and are responsible for carrying out all the functions necessary for an organism to survive.
- Prokaryotic cells are simple in structure and are found in organisms such as bacteria. They lack a nucleus and other membrane-bound organelles.
- Eukaryotic cells are more complex in structure and are found in organisms such as plants and animals. They have a nucleus and other membrane-bound organelles.
- The size, shape, and arrangement of bacterial cells can vary greatly. Some bacteria are spherical, while others are rod-shaped or spiral. Bacteria can also be arranged in clusters, chains, or pairs.
- The structure and function of cells are closely related. For example, the cell membrane controls what enters and exits the cell, while the mitochondria produce energy for the cell.
- Other important organelles in eukaryotic cells include the endoplasmic reticulum, which is involved in protein synthesis, and the Golgi apparatus, which modifies and sorts proteins.
- In prokaryotic cells, the cell wall provides support and protection, while the flagella help the cell move.
- Understanding the structure and function of cells is essential for understanding the biology of living organisms.
