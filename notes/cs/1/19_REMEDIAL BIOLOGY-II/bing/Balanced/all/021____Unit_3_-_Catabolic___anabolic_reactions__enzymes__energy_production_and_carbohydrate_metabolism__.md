# Unit 3 - Catabolic & anabolic reactions: enzymes, energy production and carbohydrate metabolism. Lipid & protein catabolism, Energy production mechanism, metabolic diversity & pathways of energy use. Integration of metabolism

- Catabolic and anabolic reactions are two types of metabolic reactions that occur in living cells.
- Catabolic reactions break down complex molecules into simpler ones, releasing energy and reducing power (e.g., ATP, NADH, FADH2) that can be used for other cellular processes    .
- Examples of catabolic reactions are glycolysis, the citric acid cycle, oxidative phosphorylation, fatty acid oxidation, and amino acid degradation    .
- Anabolic reactions use energy and reducing power to synthesize complex molecules from simpler ones, such as proteins, carbohydrates, lipids, and nucleic acids    .
- Examples of anabolic reactions are gluconeogenesis, glycogenesis, the pentose phosphate pathway, fatty acid synthesis, and amino acid synthesis    .
- Enzymes are biological catalysts that speed up the rate of metabolic reactions by lowering the activation energy required for the reactions to occur    .
- Enzymes are usually proteins that have specific active sites where substrates bind and undergo chemical transformation    .
- Enzymes can be regulated by various factors, such as temperature, pH, substrate concentration, inhibitors, activators, and feedback mechanisms    .
- Energy production is the process of converting the chemical energy stored in fuel molecules (such as glucose, fatty acids, and amino acids) into a more usable form of energy (such as ATP) that can power cellular work    .
- Energy production involves three main stages: glycolysis, the citric acid cycle, and oxidative phosphorylation    .
- Glycolysis is the breakdown of glucose into two molecules of pyruvate, generating two molecules of ATP and two molecules of NADH per glucose molecule    .
- The citric acid cycle is the oxidation of acetyl-CoA (derived from pyruvate or fatty acids) into carbon dioxide, generating two molecules of ATP, six molecules of NADH, and two molecules of FADH2 per acetyl-CoA molecule    .
- Oxidative phosphorylation is the transfer of electrons from NADH and FADH2 to oxygen, generating a proton gradient across the inner mitochondrial membrane that drives the synthesis of ATP by ATP synthase    .
- Carbohydrate metabolism is the set of metabolic pathways that involve the synthesis and degradation of carbohydrates, such as glucose, glycogen, and pentoses    .
- Carbohydrate metabolism includes catabolic pathways, such as glycolysis, the citric acid cycle, and the pentose phosphate pathway, and anabolic pathways, such as gluconeogenesis and glycogenesis    .
