# Cellular Respiration

- Cellular respiration is the process by which biological fuels are oxidised in the presence of an inorganic electron acceptor such as oxygen to produce large amounts of energy, to drive the bulk production of adenosine triphosphate (ATP).
- ATP is the main energy currency of the cell, and it is used to power various cellular processes such as biosynthesis, transport, and movement.
- Cellular respiration can be divided into four main stages: glycolysis, pyruvate oxidation, the citric acid or Krebs cycle, and oxidative phosphorylation.

## Structure of mitochondria

- Mitochondria are the organelles where most of the cellular respiration takes place in eukaryotic cells.
- Mitochondria have a double membrane structure: an outer membrane that surrounds the organelle, and an inner membrane that forms folds called cristae.
- The cristae increase the surface area of the inner membrane, where many of the enzymes and proteins involved in cellular respiration are located.
- The space between the outer and inner membranes is called the intermembrane space, and the space enclosed by the inner membrane is called the matrix.
- The matrix contains the mitochondrial DNA, ribosomes, and some enzymes that catalyse reactions in the citric acid cycle.

## Cellular respiration

- The overall equation for cellular respiration is:

C6H12O6 + 6O2 -> 6CO2 + 6H2O + ATP

- This means that glucose (C6H12O6) is oxidised by oxygen (O2) to produce carbon dioxide (CO2), water (H2O), and ATP.
- The oxidation of glucose involves the transfer of electrons from glucose to oxygen, which is mediated by a series of electron carriers.
- The electron carriers are reduced when they accept electrons, and oxidised when they donate electrons.
- The electron carriers can be divided into two groups: nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide (NAD+) and flavin adenine dinucleotide (FAD), which are coenzymes that act as mobile electron carriers, and the electron transport chain (ETC), which is a series of protein complexes embedded in the inner mitochondrial membrane that transfer electrons from NADH and FADH2 to oxygen.
- The ETC also pumps protons (H+) from the matrix to the intermembrane space, creating a proton gradient that drives the synthesis of ATP by a protein complex called ATP synthase.

## Glycolysis

- Glycolysis is the first stage of cellular respiration, and it takes place in the cytoplasm of the cell.
- Glycolysis involves the breakdown of one molecule of glucose into two molecules of pyruvate, a three-carbon compound.
- Glycolysis also produces two molecules of ATP and two molecules of NADH per glucose molecule.
- Glycolysis can be divided into two phases: the energy investment phase, where two molecules of ATP are used to phosphorylate glucose and its intermediates, and the energy payoff phase, where four molecules of ATP and two molecules of NADH are generated by the oxidation of the intermediates.
- The net equation for glycolysis is:

Glucose + 2NAD+ + 2ADP + 2Pi -> 2Pyruvate + 2NADH + 2ATP + 2H2O

- Pi stands for inorganic phosphate, and H2O stands for water.
