# Relationship of carbohydrate metabolism to other compounds

- Carbohydrates are organic molecules composed of carbon, hydrogen, and oxygen atoms. The family of carbohydrates includes both simple and complex sugars. Glucose and fructose are examples of simple sugars, and starch, glycogen, and cellulose are all examples of complex sugars.
- Carbohydrate metabolism is the process of breaking down carbohydrates into simpler molecules, such as glucose, and using them to generate energy or synthesize other compounds. Carbohydrate metabolism involves several pathways, such as glycolysis, the formation of acetyl CoA, the Krebs cycle, the electron transport system, and oxidative phosphorylation.
- Carbohydrate metabolism is connected to the metabolism of other compounds, such as proteins and lipids, through several intermediates and products. These connections allow the cells to use different substrates for energy production and biosynthesis, depending on the availability and demand  .
- The carbohydrates that can also feed into glucose catabolism include galactose, fructose, glycogen, and pentose. These are catabolized during glycolysis, which is the first step of carbohydrate metabolism. Glycolysis converts glucose into two molecules of pyruvate, which can then enter the mitochondrial matrix and be converted into acetyl CoA.
- The amino acids from proteins can also connect with glucose catabolism through pyruvate, acetyl CoA, and components of the Krebs cycle. The Krebs cycle is the second step of carbohydrate metabolism, which oxidizes acetyl CoA into carbon dioxide and generates reduced coenzymes, such as NADH and FADH2. Some amino acids can be deaminated and converted into pyruvate or acetyl CoA, while others can be converted into intermediates of the Krebs cycle, such as oxaloacetate, alpha-ketoglutarate, succinyl CoA, or fumarate  .
- The fatty acids from lipids can also connect with glucose catabolism through acetyl CoA. The fatty acids are broken down into acetyl CoA units by a process called beta-oxidation, which occurs in the mitochondrial matrix. The acetyl CoA units can then enter the Krebs cycle and be oxidized further .
- The electron transport system and oxidative phosphorylation are the final steps of carbohydrate metabolism, which use the reduced coenzymes from glycolysis and the Krebs cycle to generate ATP, the main energy currency of the cell. The electron transport system consists of a series of protein complexes and electron carriers that transfer electrons from NADH and FADH2 to oxygen, forming water. The energy released by this process is used to pump protons across the inner mitochondrial membrane, creating a proton gradient. The oxidative phosphorylation uses this proton gradient to drive the synthesis of ATP by a protein called ATP synthase.
- The ATP generated by carbohydrate metabolism can be used for various cellular processes, such as biosynthesis, transport, movement, and signaling. The ATP can also be stored as glycogen or converted into other compounds, such as nucleotides, amino acids, or lipids.
