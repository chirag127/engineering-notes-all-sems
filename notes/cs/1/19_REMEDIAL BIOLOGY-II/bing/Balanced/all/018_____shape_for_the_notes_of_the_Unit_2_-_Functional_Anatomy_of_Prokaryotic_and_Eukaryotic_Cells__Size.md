# Size, shape, and arrangement of bacterial cells

- Bacterial cells are microscopic organisms that cannot be seen by the naked eye. They are measured in micrometers (µm), which are one-thousandth of a millimeter. The average size of a bacterial cell is between 0.2 and 2.0 µm .
- Bacterial cells have different shapes and arrangements that help them adapt to different environments and functions. The most common shapes are:
  - Cocci: spherical or oval-shaped bacteria. They can be arranged in pairs (diplococci), chains (streptococci), clusters (staphylococci), or tetrads (groups of four).
  - Bacilli: rod-shaped or cylindrical bacteria. They can be arranged in singles, pairs (diplobacilli), chains (streptobacilli), or palisades (parallel rows).
  - Spirilla: spiral-shaped or curved bacteria. They can be rigid (spirilla) or flexible (spirochetes). They are usually arranged in singles or loose coils.
- Some bacterial cells have special structures or appendages that modify their shape or arrangement, such as:
  - Capsules: slimy layers of polysaccharides or proteins that surround some bacterial cells and protect them from drying out, phagocytosis, or antibiotics.
  - Flagella: long, whip-like structures that protrude from some bacterial cells and enable them to move or swim.
  - Pili: short, hair-like structures that extend from some bacterial cells and allow them to attach to surfaces, exchange genetic material, or scavenge nutrients .
- Some examples of bacterial cells with different size, shape, and arrangement are:
  - Staphylococcus aureus: a cocci bacterium that forms grape-like clusters and causes skin infections, food poisoning, and toxic shock syndrome.
  - Escherichia coli: a bacillus bacterium that is normally found in the human intestine and can cause diarrhea, urinary tract infections, or meningitis .
  - Treponema pallidum: a spirochete bacterium that causes syphilis, a sexually transmitted disease.
  - Mycoplasma genitalium: one of the smallest known bacteria, with a length of only 323 nm and a width of 242 nm. It has no cell wall and causes genital infections.
