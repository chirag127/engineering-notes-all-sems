# Functional Anatomy of Prokaryotic and Eukaryotic Cells

- Prokaryotic and eukaryotic cells are the two basic types of cells that make up all living organisms.
- Prokaryotic cells are simpler and smaller than eukaryotic cells, and lack a nucleus and other membrane-bound organelles.
- Eukaryotic cells are more complex and larger than prokaryotic cells, and have a nucleus and other membrane-bound organelles that perform specific functions.
- The main differences between prokaryotic and eukaryotic cells are summarized in the table below:

| Feature | Prokaryotic cells | Eukaryotic cells |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Size | 0.1-5 micrometers | 10-100 micrometers |
| Shape | Mostly spherical, rod-shaped, or spiral | Variable, depending on the type of cell |
| Arrangement | Single-celled or in chains, clusters, or filaments | Single-celled or multicellular |
| DNA | Circular, in the cytoplasm, not associated with histones | Linear, in the nucleus, associated with histones |
| Organelles | No membrane-bound organelles, except ribosomes | Many membrane-bound organelles, such as mitochondria, chloroplasts, endoplasmic reticulum, Golgi apparatus, etc. |
| Cell wall | Present in most prokaryotes, composed of peptidoglycan or other polysaccharides | Present in some eukaryotes, such as plants and fungi, composed of cellulose or chitin |
| Cell membrane | Phospholipid bilayer with embedded proteins | Phospholipid bilayer with embedded proteins and carbohydrates |
| Cell division | Binary fission, a simple process of splitting into two identical cells | Mitosis or meiosis, complex processes of dividing the nucleus and the cytoplasm into two or four daughter cells |
| Reproduction | Asexual, by binary fission or budding | Asexual or sexual, by mitosis or meiosis and fertilization |

- The structure and function of some of the common features of prokaryotic and eukaryotic cells are explained below:

## Plasma membrane

- The plasma membrane is the outermost layer of the cell that separates it from the external environment and regulates the movement of substances into and out of the cell.
- The plasma membrane is composed of a phospholipid bilayer, which consists of two layers of phospholipids, each with a hydrophilic (water-loving) head and a hydrophobic (water-fearing) tail.
- The phospholipid bilayer is fluid and flexible, and allows the membrane to change shape and adapt to different conditions.
- The plasma membrane also contains proteins and carbohydrates that are embedded in or attached to the phospholipid bilayer. These molecules perform various functions, such as transport, signaling, recognition, and adhesion.

## Cytoplasm

- The cytoplasm is the gel-like substance that fills the interior of the cell and contains the DNA, ribosomes, and other molecules and structures that are involved in the cell's metabolism, growth, and reproduction.
- The cytoplasm of prokaryotic cells is mostly water, with dissolved salts, sugars, amino acids, and other organic molecules. It also contains the nucleoid, which is the region where the circular DNA is located, and plasmids, which are small, circular, extra-chromosomal DNA molecules that can carry genes for antibiotic resistance, virulence, or other traits.
- The cytoplasm of eukaryotic cells is more complex and organized than that of prokaryotic cells. It contains the nucleus, which is the membrane-bound compartment that houses the linear DNA, and various organelles, which are specialized structures that perform specific functions for the cell. The cytoplasm also contains the cytoskeleton, which is a network of protein fibers that provide shape, support, and movement to the cell.

## Ribosomes

- Ribosomes are the structures that synthesize proteins from amino acids, using the information encoded in the DNA and the messenger RNA (mRNA).
- Ribosomes are composed of two subunits, one large and one small, each made of ribosomal RNA (rRNA) and proteins.
