# ATP and Cellular Respiration

- ATP (adenosine triphosphate) is the energy-carrying molecule used in cells because it can release energy very quickly.
- Cellular respiration refers to the breakdown of glucose and other respiratory substrates to make ATP in the presence of an inorganic electron acceptor such as oxygen .
- Cellular respiration is a series of chemical reactions that involve three main steps: glycolysis, the citric acid cycle, and oxidative phosphorylation .
- Glycolysis is the first step of cellular respiration that occurs in the cytoplasm. It splits one molecule of glucose into two molecules of pyruvate, producing a net of 2 ATP and 2 NADH (a reduced form of nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide, an electron carrier) per glucose molecule .
- Pyruvate is then transported into the mitochondria, where it is converted into acetyl coenzyme A (acetyl-CoA) by a complex of enzymes called pyruvate dehydrogenase. This process also produces 1 NADH and 1 CO2 per pyruvate molecule .
- Acetyl-CoA enters the citric acid cycle (also known as the Krebs cycle or the tricarboxylic acid cycle) in the mitochondrial matrix. It combines with oxaloacetate to form citrate, which then goes through a series of reactions that regenerate oxaloacetate and release 2 CO2, 3 NADH, 1 FADH2 (a reduced form of flavin adenine dinucleotide, another electron carrier), and 1 ATP per acetyl-CoA molecule .
- The NADH and FADH2 produced in glycolysis, pyruvate oxidation, and the citric acid cycle donate their electrons to the electron transport chain (ETC) in the inner mitochondrial membrane. The ETC consists of a series of protein complexes and electron carriers that transfer the electrons from one to another, releasing energy along the way. The energy is used to pump protons (H+) from the mitochondrial matrix to the intermembrane space, creating a proton gradient across the membrane .
- The proton gradient drives the synthesis of ATP by a protein complex called ATP synthase, which allows the protons to flow back into the matrix through its channel. As the protons pass through, they cause the rotation of a part of the ATP synthase that catalyzes the formation of ATP from ADP and a phosphate group. This process is called oxidative phosphorylation because it involves the oxidation of NADH and FADH2 and the phosphorylation of ADP .
- The final electron acceptor in the ETC is oxygen, which combines with the electrons and protons to form water. This is why cellular respiration requires oxygen and produces water as a by-product .
- The total number of ATP molecules produced by cellular respiration varies depending on the efficiency of the ETC and the transport of NADH and FADH2 from the cytoplasm to the mitochondria. A common estimate is that one molecule of glucose can produce a net of 30-32 ATP molecules.
- The relationship of carbohydrate metabolism to other compounds is that carbohydrates can be converted into other molecules such as amino acids, fatty acids, and nucleotides, or vice versa, depending on the cellular needs and availability of substrates. These conversions involve various enzymes and intermediates that link the pathways of glycolysis, the citric acid cycle, and oxidative phosphorylation with other metabolic pathways.
- The factors affecting respiration in cells include the availability of oxygen, glucose, and other respiratory substrates, the demand for ATP and other products of respiration, the activity and regulation of the enzymes involved in respiration, and the environmental conditions such as temperature and pH.
