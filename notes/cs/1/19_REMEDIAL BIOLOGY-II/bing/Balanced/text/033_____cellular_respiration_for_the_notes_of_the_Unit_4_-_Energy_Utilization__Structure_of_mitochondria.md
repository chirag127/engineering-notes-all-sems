### Cellular Respiration

- Cellular respiration is a metabolic pathway that breaks down glucose and produces ATP, the energy currency of the cell.
- Cellular respiration can be divided into four stages: glycolysis, pyruvate oxidation, the Krebs cycle, and oxidative phosphorylation .
- Cellular respiration is regulated by the availability of substrates and products, such as glucose, oxygen, ATP, ADP, and NADH.
- Cellular respiration can be used by engineers to clean up environmental pollution through a process called bioremediation, which involves microorganisms, fungi, and plants that can degrade contaminants for energy.

#### Structure of Mitochondria

- Mitochondria are organelles that are the site of most of the reactions of cellular respiration in eukaryotic cells.
- Mitochondria have a double membrane structure: an outer membrane that surrounds the organelle, and an inner membrane that forms folds called cristae.
- The space between the outer and inner membranes is called the intermembrane space, and the space inside the inner membrane is called the matrix.
- The inner membrane contains many proteins that are involved in electron transport and oxidative phosphorylation, while the matrix contains enzymes that catalyze the Krebs cycle and pyruvate oxidation.

#### Glycolysis

- Glycolysis is the first stage of cellular respiration, and it occurs in the cytoplasm of the cell.
- Glycolysis involves the splitting of a six-carbon glucose molecule into two three-carbon pyruvate molecules, with the net production of two ATP and two NADH molecules.
- Glycolysis can be divided into two phases: an energy investment phase, where two ATP are used to activate glucose, and an energy payoff phase, where four ATP and two NADH are generated.
- Glycolysis does not require oxygen, and it can occur in both aerobic and anaerobic conditions.

#### Formation of Acetyl Co-A

- Pyruvate oxidation is the second stage of cellular respiration, and it occurs in the mitochondrial matrix.
- Pyruvate oxidation involves the conversion of pyruvate into acetyl coenzyme A (acetyl Co-A), with the release of carbon dioxide and the reduction of NAD+ to NADH.
- Pyruvate oxidation is catalyzed by a complex of enzymes called the pyruvate dehydrogenase complex (PDC).
- Pyruvate oxidation requires oxygen, and it links glycolysis to the Krebs cycle.

#### Krebs Cycle

- The Krebs cycle, also called the citric acid cycle, is the third stage of cellular respiration, and it occurs in the mitochondrial matrix .
- The Krebs cycle involves the oxidation of acetyl Co-A into carbon dioxide, with the production of ATP, NADH, and FADH2 .
- The Krebs cycle consists of eight steps, each catalyzed by a specific enzyme .
- The Krebs cycle completes the oxidation of glucose, and it provides electrons for the electron transport chain .

#### Electron Transport System and Oxidative Phosphorylation

- Electron transport and oxidative phosphorylation are the fourth and final stage of cellular respiration, and they occur in the inner mitochondrial membrane .
- Electron transport involves the transfer of electrons from NADH and FADH2 to a series of protein complexes and electron carriers, called the electron transport chain (ETC) .
- Electron transport creates a proton gradient across the inner mitochondrial membrane, which drives the synthesis of ATP by a protein called ATP synthase .
- Oxidative phosphorylation is the process of using the energy from the proton gradient to phosphorylate ADP into ATP .
- Electron transport and oxidative phosphorylation require oxygen as the final electron acceptor, and they produce the most ATP in cellular respiration .
