### Functional Anatomy of Prokaryotic and Eukaryotic Cells

- Prokaryotic and eukaryotic cells are the two basic types of cells that make up all living organisms .
- Prokaryotic cells are simpler and smaller than eukaryotic cells, and lack a nucleus and other membrane-bound organelles  .
- Prokaryotic cells are found in the domains Bacteria and Archaea, and are involved in many important biological processes, such as decomposition, nitrogen fixation, and photosynthesis .
- Eukaryotic cells are more complex and larger than prokaryotic cells, and have a nucleus that contains the genetic material and other membrane-bound organelles that perform specific functions  .
- Eukaryotic cells are found in the domain Eukarya, and include animals, plants, fungi, and protists .
- Eukaryotic cells have more diversity and specialization than prokaryotic cells, and can form multicellular organisms with different types of tissues and organs .

#### Size, shape, and arrangement of bacterial cells

- Bacterial cells are prokaryotic cells that belong to the domain Bacteria .
- Bacterial cells are typically much smaller than eukaryotic cells, ranging from 0.2 to 10 micrometers in length.
- Bacterial cells have different shapes, such as cocci (spherical), bacilli (rod-shaped), spirilla (spiral), and vibrios (comma-shaped).
- Bacterial cells can also form different arrangements, such as diplococci (pairs of cocci), streptococci (chains of cocci), staphylococci (clusters of cocci), diplobacilli (pairs of bacilli), streptobacilli (chains of bacilli), and palisades (side-by-side bacilli).
- Bacterial cells have a cell wall that provides shape and protection, and can be classified as gram-positive or gram-negative based on the structure and composition of the cell wall.
