### Kreb cycle

- The Kreb cycle, also known as the citric acid cycle, is a series of chemical reactions that occur in the mitochondria of aerobic organisms  .
- The Kreb cycle is part of the cellular respiration process, which converts glucose and oxygen into carbon dioxide, water, and energy (ATP)  .
- The Kreb cycle requires the input of acetyl-CoA, which is derived from the oxidation of pyruvate, the end product of glycolysis  .
- The Kreb cycle consists of eight steps, each catalyzed by a specific enzyme   :
  - Step 1: Acetyl-CoA combines with oxaloacetate (a four-carbon molecule) to form citrate (a six-carbon molecule) and release coenzyme A.
  - Step 2: Citrate is rearranged to form isocitrate, a more reactive form of citrate.
  - Step 3: Isocitrate is oxidized to form alpha-ketoglutarate (a five-carbon molecule) and release carbon dioxide and NADH (a high-energy electron carrier).
  - Step 4: Alpha-ketoglutarate is oxidized to form succinyl-CoA (a four-carbon molecule) and release carbon dioxide and NADH.
  - Step 5: Succinyl-CoA is converted to succinate and release coenzyme A and GTP (a high-energy phosphate carrier).
  - Step 6: Succinate is oxidized to form fumarate and release FADH2 (another high-energy electron carrier).
  - Step 7: Fumarate is hydrated to form malate, a more reactive form of fumarate.
  - Step 8: Malate is oxidized to form oxaloacetate and release NADH.
- The Kreb cycle produces two molecules of carbon dioxide, three molecules of NADH, one molecule of FADH2, and one molecule of GTP per acetyl-CoA molecule   .
- The Kreb cycle also provides precursors for the synthesis of certain amino acids, such as aspartate, glutamate, and alanine .
- The Kreb cycle is regulated by the availability of substrates (such as acetyl-CoA, NAD+, and oxaloacetate) and the feedback inhibition of enzymes (such as citrate synthase, isocitrate dehydrogenase, and alpha-ketoglutarate dehydrogenase) by the products (such as ATP, NADH, and succinyl-CoA)   .
- The Kreb cycle is linked to the electron transport chain and oxidative phosphorylation, which use the electrons from NADH and FADH2 to generate a proton gradient across the inner mitochondrial membrane and drive the synthesis of ATP by ATP synthase   .
- The Kreb cycle is one of the oldest metabolic pathways, as it is found in almost all living organisms that use oxygen for respiration  . It is thought to have evolved from an ancient anaerobic pathway that used sulfur instead of oxygen as the final electron acceptor .
