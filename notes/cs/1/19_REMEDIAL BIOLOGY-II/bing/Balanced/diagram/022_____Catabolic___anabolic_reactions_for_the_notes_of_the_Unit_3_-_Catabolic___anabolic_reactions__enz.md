# Catabolic and Anabolic Reactions

- Catabolic and anabolic reactions are two types of metabolic reactions that occur in living cells.
- Catabolic reactions are those that break down complex molecules into simpler ones, releasing energy in the process. Examples of catabolic reactions are glycolysis, beta-oxidation, and proteolysis.
- Anabolic reactions are those that build up complex molecules from simpler ones, using energy in the process. Examples of anabolic reactions are protein synthesis, photosynthesis, and lipogenesis.
- Catabolic and anabolic reactions are often coupled, meaning that the energy released by catabolic reactions is used to power anabolic reactions. A common molecule that mediates this coupling is adenosine triphosphate (ATP), which is produced by catabolic reactions and consumed by anabolic reactions.
- Catabolic and anabolic reactions are regulated by various factors, such as hormones, enzymes, and feedback mechanisms, to maintain the balance between energy production and consumption, and to ensure the proper functioning of the cell and the organism.
