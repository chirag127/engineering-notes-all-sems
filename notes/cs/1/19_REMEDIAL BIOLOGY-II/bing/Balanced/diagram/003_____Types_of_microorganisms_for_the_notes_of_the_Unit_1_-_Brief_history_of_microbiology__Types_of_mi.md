### Types of microorganisms

Microorganisms or microbes are microscopic organisms that exist as unicellular, multicellular, or cell clusters. Microorganisms are widespread in nature and are beneficial to life, but some can cause serious harm. They can be divided into six major types :

- **Bacteria**: These are prokaryotic organisms that have a simple cell structure and lack a nucleus and membrane-bound organelles. They can be classified into two domains: eubacteria and archaea. Eubacteria are the most common and diverse group of bacteria, and include Gram-positive and Gram-negative bacteria. Archaea are bacteria that live in extreme environments, such as hot springs, salt lakes, and deep-sea vents. They have unique biochemical and genetic features that distinguish them from eubacteria.
- **Algae**: These are eukaryotic organisms that have a complex cell structure and contain a nucleus and membrane-bound organelles. They are photosynthetic and produce oxygen and organic compounds from carbon dioxide and water. They can be unicellular or multicellular, and vary in size, shape, and pigmentation. They are found in aquatic and moist environments, and some can live symbiotically with other organisms, such as lichens and corals.
- **Fungi**: These are eukaryotic organisms that have a complex cell structure and contain a nucleus and membrane-bound organelles. They are heterotrophic and obtain organic compounds from other sources, such as dead or living organisms. They can be unicellular or multicellular, and form filamentous structures called hyphae that make up the body of the fungus. They are found in soil, water, air, and on plants and animals. They play important roles in decomposition, nutrient cycling, and symbiosis, but some can also cause diseases and spoilage.
- **Protozoa**: These are eukaryotic organisms that have a complex cell structure and contain a nucleus and membrane-bound organelles. They are unicellular and motile, and can move by various means, such as flagella, cilia, or pseudopodia. They are found in aquatic and moist environments, and some can live as parasites or symbionts in other organisms. They can be classified into four major groups based on their mode of locomotion: flagellates, ciliates, amoebae, and sporozoans.
- **Viruses**: These are acellular entities that are not composed of cells. They consist of a nucleic acid (DNA or RNA) enclosed in a protein coat called a capsid. Some viruses also have an outer envelope derived from the host cell membrane. They are obligate intracellular parasites, meaning they can only replicate inside a living host cell. They can infect all types of cells, from bacteria to humans, and cause various diseases, such as influenza, AIDS, and COVID-19.
- **Multicellular animal parasites**: These are eukaryotic organisms that have a complex cell structure and contain a nucleus and membrane-bound organelles. They are multicellular and have specialized tissues and organs for different functions. They are heterotrophic and obtain organic compounds from other sources, such as living organisms. They are also called helminths, and include worms, flukes, and tapeworms. They can infect humans and animals and cause diseases, such as schistosomiasis, hookworm, and cysticercosis.
