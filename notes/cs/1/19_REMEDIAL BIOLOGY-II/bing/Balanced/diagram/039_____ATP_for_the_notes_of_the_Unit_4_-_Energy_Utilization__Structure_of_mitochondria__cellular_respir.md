### ATP

- ATP stands for adenosine triphosphate, a molecule that stores and releases energy in cells.
- ATP consists of an adenine base, a ribose sugar, and three phosphate groups attached by high-energy bonds.
- ATP is the main energy currency of the cell, as it can be used to power many biochemical reactions.
- ATP is produced by cellular respiration, a process that converts glucose and other respiratory substrates into ATP and carbon dioxide.
- Cellular respiration can be divided into three main stages: glycolysis, the citric acid cycle, and oxidative phosphorylation .
- Glycolysis is the first stage of cellular respiration, where glucose is split into two molecules of pyruvate, generating two ATP and two NADH (a reduced form of nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide, an electron carrier) in the cytoplasm .
- Pyruvate is then transported into the mitochondria, where it is converted into acetyl coenzyme A (acetyl-CoA), a two-carbon molecule that enters the citric acid cycle .
- The citric acid cycle, also known as the Krebs cycle, is the second stage of cellular respiration, where acetyl-CoA is oxidized into carbon dioxide, generating two ATP, six NADH, and two FADH2 (a reduced form of flavin adenine dinucleotide, another electron carrier) per glucose molecule in the mitochondrial matrix .
- Oxidative phosphorylation is the third and final stage of cellular respiration, where the electrons from NADH and FADH2 are transferred to a series of proteins called the electron transport chain, creating a proton gradient across the inner mitochondrial membrane .
- The proton gradient drives the synthesis of ATP by a complex enzyme called ATP synthase, which uses the energy of the protons to join ADP and a phosphate group .
- Oxidative phosphorylation produces the majority of ATP in cellular respiration, about 26-28 ATP per glucose molecule, depending on the efficiency of the process .
- The total net yield of ATP from cellular respiration is about 30-32 ATP per glucose molecule.
- Cellular respiration can also use other compounds besides glucose as respiratory substrates, such as fats, proteins, and other carbohydrates.
- The relationship of carbohydrate metabolism to other compounds is that they can be interconverted by various pathways, such as gluconeogenesis, glycogenolysis, glycogenesis, lipogenesis, lipolysis, and amino acid catabolism.
- Factors affecting respiration include the availability of oxygen, respiratory substrates, enzymes, coenzymes, and temperature.
