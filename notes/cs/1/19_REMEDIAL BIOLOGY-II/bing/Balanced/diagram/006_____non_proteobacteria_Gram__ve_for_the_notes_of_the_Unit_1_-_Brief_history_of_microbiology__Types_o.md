### Nonproteobacteria Gram-Negative Bacteria

- Nonproteobacteria are a diverse group of bacteria that do not belong to the phylum Proteobacteria, which contains the majority of gram-negative bacteria.
- Nonproteobacteria can be either gram-negative or gram-positive, depending on their cell wall structure and staining properties.
- Nonproteobacteria can have various metabolic modes, such as photoautotrophy, chemoheterotrophy, or chemoautotrophy.
- Nonproteobacteria can be either motile or nonmotile, and some have distinctive shapes, such as spirals or filaments.
- Some examples of nonproteobacteria are:

  - Chlamydia: a group of obligate intracellular parasites that cause various diseases in humans and animals, such as chlamydia, trachoma, and psittacosis. They have a unique developmental cycle that involves two forms: elementary bodies and reticulate bodies.
  - Spirochetes: a group of motile, spiral-shaped bacteria that have a flexible cell wall and a unique flagellar arrangement called axial filaments. They can cause diseases such as syphilis, Lyme disease, and leptospirosis. They are difficult or impossible to culture in the laboratory.
  - CFB group: a group of gram-negative bacteria that includes the genera Cytophaga, Fusobacterium, and Bacteroides. They share some similarities in their DNA sequences, but are phylogenetically diverse. They are mostly found in the oral cavity, the gastrointestinal tract, and the soil. They can be involved in periodontal disease, abscesses, and anaerobic infections.
  - Planctomycetes: a group of gram-negative bacteria that have unusual features, such as a lack of peptidoglycan in their cell wall, a compartmentalized cytoplasm, and the ability to bud. They are mostly aquatic and can perform anammox, a process that converts ammonia and nitrite to nitrogen gas.
  - Phototrophic bacteria: a group of bacteria that can use light as an energy source and perform photosynthesis. They can be either oxygenic or anoxygenic, depending on whether they produce oxygen or not. They can have various pigments, such as chlorophyll, bacteriochlorophyll, or carotenoids. They include cyanobacteria, purple bacteria, green bacteria, and heliobacteria.
