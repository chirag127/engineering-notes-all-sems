### Functional Anatomy of Prokaryotic and Eukaryotic Cells

- Prokaryotic and eukaryotic cells are the two basic types of cells that make up all living organisms.
- Prokaryotic cells are simpler and smaller than eukaryotic cells. They do not have a nucleus or other membrane-bound organelles. Their DNA is circular and free-floating in the cytoplasm. They have a cell wall made of peptidoglycan (in bacteria) or other molecules (in archaea). They divide by binary fission. Examples of prokaryotes are bacteria and archaea.
- Eukaryotic cells are more complex and larger than prokaryotic cells. They have a nucleus that encloses their DNA. They have many membrane-bound organelles that perform specific functions, such as mitochondria, chloroplasts, endoplasmic reticulum, Golgi apparatus, lysosomes, etc. They have a cell wall made of cellulose (in plants) or chitin (in fungi) or no cell wall (in animals and protists). They divide by mitosis or meiosis. Examples of eukaryotes are plants, animals, fungi, and protists.
- The following table summarizes some of the differences between prokaryotic and eukaryotic cells:

| Feature | Prokaryotic cells | Eukaryotic cells |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Size | 0.1-5 micrometers | 10-100 micrometers |
| Nucleus | Absent | Present |
| DNA | Circular, single, naked | Linear, multiple, associated with histones |
| Organelles | Absent (except ribosomes) | Present (many types) |
| Cell wall | Present (peptidoglycan or other) | Present (cellulose or chitin) or absent |
| Cell division | Binary fission | Mitosis or meiosis |
| Examples | Bacteria and archaea | Plants, animals, fungi, and protists |

- The following are some of the functions of the major components of prokaryotic and eukaryotic cells:

  - Plasma membrane: The thin layer of phospholipids and proteins that surrounds the cell and regulates the movement of substances in and out of the cell.
  - Cytoplasm: The fluid-filled space inside the cell that contains the DNA, ribosomes, and other molecules and organelles.
  - Ribosomes: The small structures that synthesize proteins from amino acids using the genetic information from the DNA or RNA.
  - Nucleus: The membrane-bound organelle that contains the DNA and controls the gene expression and cell division of eukaryotic cells.
  - Mitochondria: The membrane-bound organelles that produce energy (ATP) from glucose and oxygen through cellular respiration in eukaryotic cells.
  - Chloroplasts: The membrane-bound organelles that produce glucose and oxygen from carbon dioxide and water through photosynthesis in plant and some protist cells.
  - Endoplasmic reticulum: The network of membranes that transports and modifies proteins and lipids in eukaryotic cells. It can be rough (with ribosomes) or smooth (without ribosomes).
  - Golgi apparatus: The stack of flattened membranes that sorts and packages proteins and lipids for secretion or delivery to other organelles in eukaryotic cells.
  - Lysosomes: The membrane-bound vesicles that contain digestive enzymes and break down waste and foreign materials in animal and some protist cells.
  - Cytoskeleton: The network of protein fibers that provides shape, support, and movement to the cell and its organelles.
  - Flagella: The long, whip-like appendages that propel some prokaryotic and eukaryotic cells through liquid environments.
  - Cilia: The short, hair-like projections that move fluids or particles along the surface of some eukaryotic cells.
  - Pili: The short, thin projections that help some prokaryotic cells attach to surfaces or exchange genetic material with other cells.
