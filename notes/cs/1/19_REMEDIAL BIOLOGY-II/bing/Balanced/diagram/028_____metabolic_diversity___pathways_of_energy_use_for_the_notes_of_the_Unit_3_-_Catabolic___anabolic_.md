### Metabolic diversity and pathways of energy use

- Metabolic diversity mainly refers to the different metabolic strategies that organisms have evolved to obtain energy.
- Metabolic pathways are series of connected chemical reactions that feed one another and convert starting molecules into products.
- Metabolic pathways can be broadly divided into two categories based on their effects:
  - Catabolic pathways break down complex molecules into simpler ones and release energy. Examples are glycolysis, Krebs cycle, and oxidative phosphorylation.
  - Anabolic pathways synthesize complex molecules from simpler ones and require energy. Examples are photosynthesis, gluconeogenesis, and protein synthesis.
- The energy released or required by metabolic pathways is often stored or transferred by the molecule ATP (adenosine triphosphate), which can be hydrolyzed to ADP (adenosine diphosphate) and Pi (inorganic phosphate) or phosphorylated back to ATP.
- The body uses different metabolic pathways to produce the right kind of energy to fuel different activities. The body has three different metabolic pathways:
  - Phosphagen system (ATP-PC system) for immediate energy. This system uses stored ATP and creatine phosphate (CP) in the muscles to provide energy for short bursts of activity, such as sprinting or lifting weights.
  - Glycolytic system (anaerobic system) for short-term energy. This system uses glucose from the blood or glycogen from the muscles to produce ATP through glycolysis, which does not require oxygen. This system can provide energy for up to two minutes of activity, such as running or swimming.
  - Oxidative system (aerobic system) for long-term energy. This system uses oxygen and various fuels, such as glucose, fatty acids, and amino acids, to produce ATP through the Krebs cycle and oxidative phosphorylation. This system can provide energy for hours of activity, such as jogging or cycling.
- Metabolic pathways evolved among prokaryotes before eukaryotes arose as the result of their interaction and coevolution with changing physicochemical environmental conditions.
- Metabolic diversity among prokaryotes is reflected in their ability to use different sources of carbon and energy:
  - Autotrophs can synthesize their own organic molecules from inorganic carbon sources, such as carbon dioxide or bicarbonate.
  - Heterotrophs cannot synthesize their own organic molecules and must obtain them from other organisms or organic matter.
  - Phototrophs can use light as their source of energy and perform photosynthesis, which can be oxygenic or anoxygenic depending on the type of electron donor used.
  - Chemotrophs can use chemical compounds as their source of energy and perform chemosynthesis, which can be aerobic or anaerobic depending on the type of electron acceptor used.
- Metabolic diversity among eukaryotes is less pronounced than among prokaryotes, but still exists in some groups, such as protists, fungi, and plants:
  - Protists are a diverse group of eukaryotes that can have different modes of nutrition, such as photosynthesis, ingestion, absorption, or symbiosis.
  - Fungi are heterotrophic eukaryotes that can decompose organic matter by secreting enzymes and absorbing the products, or form mutualistic or parasitic associations with other organisms.
  - Plants are autotrophic eukaryotes that can perform oxygenic photosynthesis using water as the electron donor and produce organic molecules and oxygen. Some plants can also use alternative pathways of carbon fixation, such as C4 or CAM, to adapt to different environmental conditions.
