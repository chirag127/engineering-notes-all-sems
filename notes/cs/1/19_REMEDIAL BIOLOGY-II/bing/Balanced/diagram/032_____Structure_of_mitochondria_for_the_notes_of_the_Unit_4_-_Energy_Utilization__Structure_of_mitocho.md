### Structure of mitochondria

- Mitochondria are small, rod-shaped organelles found in both plant and animal cells  that are involved in cellular respiration and energy production .
- Mitochondria have a double membrane structure, consisting of an outer membrane and an inner membrane   . The outer membrane is smooth and surrounds the organelle, while the inner membrane is folded into projections called cristae  . The cristae increase the surface area of the inner membrane and provide sites for the reactions of the electron transport chain and oxidative phosphorylation .
- The space between the outer and inner membranes is called the intermembrane space, and the space enclosed by the inner membrane is called the matrix  . The matrix contains the mitochondrial DNA, ribosomes, enzymes, and other molecules involved in the Krebs cycle and the formation of acetyl co-A  .
- Mitochondria use oxygen and organic molecules (such as glucose) to produce ATP, the main energy currency of the cell, through a process called aerobic respiration  . Aerobic respiration consists of four main stages: glycolysis, formation of acetyl co-A, Krebs cycle, and electron transport chain and oxidative phosphorylation .
- Glycolysis is the first stage of aerobic respiration and takes place in the cytoplasm of the cell. It involves the breakdown of glucose (a six-carbon molecule) into two molecules of pyruvate (a three-carbon molecule), releasing two molecules of ATP and two molecules of NADH (an electron carrier) in the process .
- Formation of acetyl co-A is the second stage of aerobic respiration and takes place in the matrix of the mitochondria. It involves the conversion of pyruvate into acetyl co-A (a two-carbon molecule), releasing one molecule of CO2 and one molecule of NADH for each pyruvate molecule .
- Krebs cycle is the third stage of aerobic respiration and takes place in the matrix of the mitochondria. It involves the oxidation of acetyl co-A into CO2, releasing two molecules of ATP, six molecules of NADH, and two molecules of FADH2 (another electron carrier) for each glucose molecule .
- Electron transport chain and oxidative phosphorylation is the fourth and final stage of aerobic respiration and takes place in the inner membrane of the mitochondria. It involves the transfer of electrons from NADH and FADH2 to a series of protein complexes and electron carriers, creating a proton gradient across the inner membrane. The protons then flow back into the matrix through a protein called ATP synthase, which uses the energy to synthesize ATP from ADP and phosphate. This stage produces about 32 molecules of ATP for each glucose molecule, as well as water as a by-product .
- ATP is the main energy source for various cellular processes, such as biosynthesis, transport, movement, and signaling . The amount of ATP produced by mitochondria depends on several factors, such as the availability of oxygen, glucose, and other substrates, the activity and efficiency of the enzymes and transporters involved, and the demand and feedback from the cell .
- Mitochondria are dynamic organelles that can change their shape, size, number, and distribution in response to the needs of the cell . They can also communicate with other organelles and the nucleus through various signaling pathways . Mitochondria play important roles in various cellular functions, such as apoptosis (programmed cell death), calcium homeostasis, oxidative stress, and aging .
