### Size, shape, and arrangement of bacterial cells

- Bacterial cells are microscopic organisms that cannot be seen by the naked eye. They are measured in micrometers (µm), which are one-thousandth of a millimeter. The average size of a bacterial cell varies between 0.2 and 2.0 µm .
- Bacterial cells have different shapes and arrangements that help them adapt to different environments and functions. The most common shapes are:
  - Cocci: spherical or oval cells that can be arranged in pairs (diplococci), chains (streptococci), clusters (staphylococci), tetrads (groups of four), sarcinae (cubical packets of eight), or other patterns .
  - Bacilli: rod-shaped or cylindrical cells that can be arranged in singles, pairs (diplobacilli), chains (streptobacilli), or palisades (side-by-side parallel rows) .
  - Spirilla: rigid, helical or spiral-shaped cells that can be arranged in singles or pairs .
  - Spirochetes: flexible, corkscrew-shaped cells that can move by rotating their axial filaments .
  - Vibrios: comma-shaped or curved cells that can be arranged in singles or pairs .
  - Coccobacilli: oval-shaped cells that are intermediate between cocci and bacilli .
  - Filamentous: long, thin, thread-like cells that can form branching networks or mycelia .
- Some bacterial cells can change their shape and arrangement depending on the environmental conditions or the stage of their life cycle. This is called pleomorphism .
- Some bacterial cells can form specialized structures such as endospores, capsules, flagella, pili, or fimbriae that enhance their survival, attachment, or motility  .
