### Functional Anatomy of Prokaryotic and Eukaryotic Cells

- Prokaryotic and eukaryotic cells are the two basic types of cells that make up all living organisms .
- Prokaryotic cells are simpler and smaller than eukaryotic cells, and lack a nucleus and other membrane-bound organelles  .
- Eukaryotic cells are more complex and larger than prokaryotic cells, and have a nucleus that contains the DNA and other membrane-bound organelles that perform specific functions  .
- The main differences between prokaryotic and eukaryotic cells are summarized in the table below:

| Feature | Prokaryotic cells | Eukaryotic cells |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Size | 0.1-5 micrometers | 10-100 micrometers |
| Nucleus | Absent | Present |
| DNA | Circular, in cytoplasm | Linear, in nucleus |
| Histones | Absent | Present |
| Organelles | Absent (except ribosomes) | Present (e.g. mitochondria, chloroplasts, endoplasmic reticulum, Golgi apparatus, etc.) |
| Cell wall | Present (made of peptidoglycan in bacteria, and other polysaccharides in archaea) | Absent in animals, present in plants (made of cellulose), fungi (made of chitin), and some protists (made of various materials) |
| Cell division | Binary fission | Mitosis or meiosis |
| Ribosomes | 70S | 80S |

- The size, shape, and arrangement of bacterial cells vary widely, and can be used to identify and classify different types of bacteria .
- Some common shapes of bacterial cells are:

  - Coccus (spherical)
  - Bacillus (rod-shaped)
  - Coccobacillus (short and plump)
  - Vibrio (curved rod)
  - Spirillum (rigid spiral)
  - Spirochete (flexible spiral)
  - Pleomorphic (variable shape)

- Some common arrangements of bacterial cells are:

  - Single (individual cells)
  - Pairs (two cells attached)
  - Chains (multiple cells attached end to end)
  - Tetrads (groups of four cells in a square)
  - Sarcinae (groups of eight cells in a cube)
  - Staphylococci (clusters of cells in a grape-like structure)
  - Palisades (cells arranged side by side in a picket fence-like structure)

- The structure and function of prokaryotic and eukaryotic cells are determined by the interactions of their components, such as the plasma membrane, cytoplasm, ribosomes, DNA, and organelles .
- The plasma membrane is a thin layer of phospholipids and proteins that surrounds the cell and regulates the movement of substances in and out of the cell .
- The cytoplasm is the fluid-filled space inside the cell that contains the DNA, ribosomes, and other molecules and structures .
- The ribosomes are the sites of protein synthesis, where the genetic information in the DNA is translated into amino acid sequences .
- The DNA is the genetic material that carries the instructions for the cell's structure and function .
- The organelles are specialized structures that perform specific functions in the cell, such as energy production, photosynthesis, protein modification, transport, and degradation .
- The cell wall is a rigid layer that surrounds the plasma membrane and provides support and protection to the cell .
