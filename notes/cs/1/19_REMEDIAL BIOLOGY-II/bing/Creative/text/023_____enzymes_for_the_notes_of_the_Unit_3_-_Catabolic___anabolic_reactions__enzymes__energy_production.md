### Enzymes

- Enzymes are biological catalysts that speed up the rate of biochemical reactions by lowering the activation energy.
- Enzymes are highly specific for their substrates, which are the molecules that bind to the enzyme's active site and undergo a chemical change.
- Enzymes can be classified into two main types based on the direction of the biochemical reaction they catalyze: anabolic and catabolic enzymes.

### Anabolic Enzymes

- Anabolic enzymes catalyze the biochemical reactions that synthesize larger complex molecules from smaller units.
- Anabolic reactions consume energy and are also called biosynthetic reactions.
- An example of an anabolic reaction is photosynthesis, where plants make glucose molecules from different raw materials such as carbon dioxide and water.
- Anabolic enzymes are involved in the synthesis of macromolecules such as carbohydrates, lipids, proteins and nucleic acids.

### Catabolic Enzymes

- Catabolic enzymes catalyze the biochemical reactions that break down larger complex molecules into smaller units.
- Catabolic reactions release energy and are also called degradative reactions.
- An example of a catabolic reaction is the process of food digestion, where different enzymes break down food particles so they can be absorbed by the small intestine.
- Catabolic enzymes are involved in the breakdown of macromolecules such as carbohydrates, lipids, proteins and nucleic acids.

### Metabolic Pathways

- Metabolic pathways are sequences of biochemical reactions that are facilitated by enzymes.
- Metabolic pathways can be either anabolic or catabolic, depending on whether they synthesize or degrade molecules.
- Metabolic pathways can be reversible or irreversible, depending on whether the reactions can proceed in both directions or only in one direction.
- Metabolic pathways can be regulated by various factors such as the availability of substrates, the concentration of products, the presence of inhibitors or activators, and the feedback mechanisms.
