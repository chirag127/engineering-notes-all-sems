### Kreb cycle

- The Kreb cycle, also known as the citric acid cycle or the tricarboxylic acid cycle, is a series of chemical reactions that occur in the mitochondria of aerobic organisms  .
- The Kreb cycle is the second stage of cellular respiration, following glycolysis and preceding the electron transport chain and oxidative phosphorylation  .
- The Kreb cycle produces energy by oxidizing acetyl-CoA, which is derived from the breakdown of carbohydrates, fats, and proteins   .
- The Kreb cycle consists of eight steps, each catalyzed by a specific enzyme  :
  - Step 1: Acetyl-CoA combines with oxaloacetate to form citrate, releasing coenzyme A.
  - Step 2: Citrate is converted to isocitrate by an isomerase enzyme.
  - Step 3: Isocitrate is oxidized to alpha-ketoglutarate, releasing a molecule of carbon dioxide and a molecule of NADH.
  - Step 4: Alpha-ketoglutarate is oxidized to succinyl-CoA, releasing another molecule of carbon dioxide and NADH.
  - Step 5: Succinyl-CoA is converted to succinate, releasing a molecule of GTP (or ATP in some organisms).
  - Step 6: Succinate is oxidized to fumarate, reducing a molecule of FAD to FADH2.
  - Step 7: Fumarate is hydrated to malate by an enzyme called fumarase.
  - Step 8: Malate is oxidized to oxaloacetate, regenerating NADH and completing the cycle.
- The Kreb cycle produces two molecules of carbon dioxide, three molecules of NADH, one molecule of FADH2, and one molecule of GTP (or ATP) per acetyl-CoA molecule that enters the cycle   .
- The Kreb cycle also provides intermediates for the synthesis of certain amino acids, such as aspartate, glutamate, and alanine .
- The Kreb cycle is regulated by the availability of substrates (such as acetyl-CoA and oxaloacetate) and the feedback inhibition of enzymes (such as citrate synthase and isocitrate dehydrogenase) by the products of the cycle (such as ATP, NADH, and citrate)   .
- The Kreb cycle is one of the oldest metabolic pathways, as it is found in almost all living organisms that use oxygen for respiration . It is thought to have evolved from an ancient anaerobic pathway that used sulfur instead of oxygen as the final electron acceptor.
