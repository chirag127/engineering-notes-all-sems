# Functional Anatomy of Prokaryotic and Eukaryotic Cells

- Prokaryotic and eukaryotic cells are the two basic types of cells that make up all living organisms.
- Prokaryotic cells are simpler and smaller than eukaryotic cells, and lack a nucleus and other membrane-bound organelles.
- Eukaryotic cells are more complex and larger than prokaryotic cells, and have a nucleus and other membrane-bound organelles that perform specific functions.
- The main differences between prokaryotic and eukaryotic cells are summarized in the table below:

| Feature | Prokaryotic cells | Eukaryotic cells |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Size | 0.1-5 micrometers | 10-100 micrometers |
| Shape | Mostly spherical, rod-shaped, or spiral | Various shapes, including spherical, rod-shaped, spiral, and irregular |
| Arrangement | Single-celled or in chains, clusters, or pairs | Single-celled or multicellular |
| Nucleus | Absent | Present |
| DNA | Circular, in the cytoplasm | Linear, in the nucleus |
| Histones | Absent | Present |
| Ribosomes | 70S | 80S |
| Cell division | Binary fission | Mitosis or meiosis |
| Chromosomes | One, usually | Two or more, usually |
| Organelles | None, except for ribosomes | Many, such as mitochondria, chloroplasts, endoplasmic reticulum, Golgi apparatus, lysosomes, etc. |
| Cell wall | Present, made of peptidoglycan (in bacteria) or pseudopeptidoglycan (in archaea) | Absent in animals, present in plants, fungi, and some protists, made of cellulose, chitin, or other polysaccharides |
| Plasma membrane | Present, with no sterols | Present, with sterols |
| Flagella | Present, simple and rigid | Present, complex and flexible |
| Cilia | Absent | Present, in some eukaryotes |
| Pili | Present, in some prokaryotes | Absent |

- Prokaryotic and eukaryotic cells have some common features, such as a plasma membrane, cytoplasm, ribosomes, and genetic material (DNA or RNA).
- Prokaryotic and eukaryotic cells also have different functions and roles in living organisms. Prokaryotic cells are the main agents of biogeochemical cycles, decomposition, fermentation, nitrogen fixation, and pathogenesis. Eukaryotic cells are the main components of multicellular organisms, such as animals, plants, fungi, and protists, and are involved in various processes, such as photosynthesis, respiration, reproduction, differentiation, and communication.
