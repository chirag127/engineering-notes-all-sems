# Arrangement of Bacterial Cells

- Bacteria are microscopic, single-celled organisms that can have different shapes and arrangements.
- The shape of a bacterial cell is determined by its cell wall, which is a rigid layer of peptidoglycan that surrounds the cell membrane.
- The arrangement of a bacterial cell is determined by how it divides and how the daughter cells remain attached to each other.
- The most common shapes of bacterial cells are:
  - Cocci: spherical or oval cells
  - Bacilli: rod-shaped or cylindrical cells
  - Spirilla: spiral or curved cells
- The most common arrangements of bacterial cells are:
  - Diplo: pairs of cells
  - Staphylo: grape-like clusters of cells
  - Strepto: chains of cells
  - Tetrad: groups of four cells in a square
  - Sarcina: groups of eight cells in a cube
- These arrangements can be applied to the different shapes of bacterial cells, for example:
  - Diplococci: pairs of cocci
  - Streptobacilli: chains of bacilli
  - Vibrio: comma-shaped spirilla
- The shape and arrangement of bacterial cells can help identify and classify them.
