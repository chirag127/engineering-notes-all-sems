# Helminthes

- Helminthes are **worm-like parasites** that can infect humans and other animals .
- They are **invertebrates** characterized by elongated, flat or round bodies .
- They are classified into three major groups based on their general external shape and the host organ they inhabit:
  - **Flukes (trematodes)**: flat, leaf-shaped worms that live in the liver, lungs, blood vessels, or intestines of their hosts .
  - **Tapeworms (cestodes)**: flat, ribbon-like worms that live in the intestines of their hosts and have a scolex (head) with hooks and suckers for attachment .
  - **Roundworms (nematodes)**: cylindrical, thread-like worms that live in the intestines, blood, lymphatic system, or tissues of their hosts and have a complete digestive system .
- Helminthes have complex life cycles that involve one or more intermediate hosts and a definitive host .
- Helminthes can cause various diseases in humans, such as **schistosomiasis**, **taeniasis**, **ascariasis**, **filariasis**, and **trichinosis**  .
- Helminthes can be diagnosed by microscopic examination of stool, urine, blood, or tissue samples, or by serological tests .
- Helminthes can be treated by anthelmintic drugs that target specific biochemical pathways or structures of the worms .
- Helminthes can be prevented by improving sanitation, hygiene, and vector control, and by avoiding raw or undercooked meat or fish that may contain larvae .
