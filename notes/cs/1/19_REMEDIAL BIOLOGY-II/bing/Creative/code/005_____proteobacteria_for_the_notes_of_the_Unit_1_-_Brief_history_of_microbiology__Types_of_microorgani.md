### Proteobacteria

- Proteobacteria is a phylum of gram-negative bacteria discovered by Carl Woese in the 1980s based on nucleotide sequence homology .
- Proteobacteria are further classified into the classes alpha-, beta-, gamma-, delta- and epsilonproteobacteria, each class having separate orders, families, genera, and species.
- Proteobacteria include a wide variety of pathogenic and free-living bacteria, such as Escherichia, Salmonella, Vibrio, Yersinia, Legionella, and many others.
- Proteobacteria are also involved in nitrogen fixation, carbon monoxide oxidation, and sulfur cycling .
- Proteobacteria have diverse shapes, metabolisms, and habitats, ranging from soil and water to animal and plant hosts  .

Some examples of proteobacteria are:

- Alphaproteobacteria: oligotrophs, organisms capable of living in low-nutrient environments such as deep oceanic sediments, glacial ice, or deep undersurface soil. Some examples are Rhizobium (nitrogen-fixing symbionts of legumes), Rickettsia (intracellular parasites of animals), and Caulobacter (stalked bacteria that attach to surfaces).
- Betaproteobacteria: mostly aerobic or facultative anaerobic bacteria that can use various organic compounds as electron donors or acceptors. Some examples are Neisseria (pathogens of the human mucous membranes), Nitrosomonas (nitrifying bacteria that oxidize ammonia to nitrite), and Burkholderia (opportunistic pathogens and bioremediators).
- Gammaproteobacteria: the largest and most diverse class of proteobacteria, comprising bacteria that can use a variety of substrates and energy sources. Some examples are Pseudomonas (versatile and ubiquitous bacteria that can degrade various pollutants), Escherichia (intestinal bacteria that can cause diarrhea and urinary tract infections), and Vibrio (marine bacteria that can cause cholera and food poisoning).
- Deltaproteobacteria: mostly anaerobic bacteria that are involved in sulfur and iron cycling. Some examples are Desulfovibrio (sulfate-reducing bacteria that can corrode metal pipes), Bdellovibrio (predatory bacteria that invade and consume other gram-negative bacteria), and Myxococcus (social bacteria that form fruiting bodies when starved).
- Epsilonproteobacteria: mostly microaerophilic or anaerobic bacteria that inhabit extreme environments such as hot springs, hydrothermal vents, and animal guts. Some examples are Helicobacter (gastric pathogens that can cause ulcers and cancer), Campylobacter (intestinal pathogens that can cause diarrhea and dysentery), and Wolinella (hydrogen-oxidizing bacteria that live in the rumen of cows).
