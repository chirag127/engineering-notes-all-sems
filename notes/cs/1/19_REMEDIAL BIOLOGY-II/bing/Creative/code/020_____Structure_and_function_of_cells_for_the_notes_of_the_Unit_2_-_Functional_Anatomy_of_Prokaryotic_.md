### Structure and function of cells

- Cells are the basic functional units that make up all living organisms. They have different shapes, sizes, and arrangements depending on their functions and types .
- Cells are enclosed by a cell membrane that separates the inside and the outside of the cell, and controls what goes in and comes out .
- Cells contain different functional structures which are collectively called organelles, and they are involved in various cellular functions .
- Cells can be divided into two major types: prokaryotic and eukaryotic. Prokaryotic cells are simpler and smaller than eukaryotic cells, and do not have a nucleus or other membrane-bound organelles. Eukaryotic cells are more complex and larger than prokaryotic cells, and have a nucleus and other membrane-bound organelles .
- Some of the common organelles found in eukaryotic cells are:
  - Nucleus: It contains the genetic material (DNA) of the cell and controls its activities .
  - Mitochondria: They are the sites of cellular respiration, where energy is produced from glucose and oxygen .
  - Ribosomes: They are the sites of protein synthesis, where amino acids are joined together to form proteins .
  - Endoplasmic reticulum: It is a network of membranes that transports materials within the cell and helps in the synthesis of lipids and proteins .
  - Golgi apparatus: It is a stack of flattened membranes that modifies, sorts, and packages proteins and other molecules for export or storage .
  - Lysosomes: They are vesicles that contain digestive enzymes that break down waste materials and foreign substances in the cell .
  - Cytoskeleton: It is a network of protein filaments that provides shape, support, and movement to the cell .
- Some of the common organelles found in prokaryotic cells are:
  - Cell wall: It is a rigid layer that surrounds the cell membrane and provides protection and support to the cell .
  - Cytoplasm: It is the fluid-filled space inside the cell that contains the genetic material, ribosomes, and other molecules .
  - Plasmids: They are small circular pieces of DNA that can replicate independently and carry genes for antibiotic resistance or other traits .
  - Flagella: They are long whip-like structures that help the cell to move .
  - Pili: They are short hair-like structures that help the cell to attach to other cells or surfaces .
