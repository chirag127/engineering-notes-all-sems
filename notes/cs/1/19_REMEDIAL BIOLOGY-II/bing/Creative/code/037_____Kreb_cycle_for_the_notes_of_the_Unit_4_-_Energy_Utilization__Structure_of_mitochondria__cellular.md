### Kreb cycle

- The Kreb cycle, also known as the citric acid cycle, is a series of chemical reactions that occur in the mitochondria of aerobic organisms  .
- The Kreb cycle is part of the cellular respiration process, which converts glucose and oxygen into carbon dioxide, water, and energy (ATP)     .
- The Kreb cycle requires pyruvate, which is the product of glycolysis, the first stage of cellular respiration   .
- Pyruvate is converted into acetyl-CoA, a two-carbon molecule, by the enzyme pyruvate dehydrogenase complex   .
- Acetyl-CoA enters the Kreb cycle by combining with oxaloacetate, a four-carbon molecule, to form citrate, a six-carbon molecule    .
- Citrate undergoes a series of eight steps, involving various enzymes and coenzymes, to regenerate oxaloacetate and produce two molecules of carbon dioxide, three molecules of NADH, one molecule of FADH2, and one molecule of ATP per cycle    .
- NADH and FADH2 are electron carriers that transfer their electrons to the electron transport chain, the final stage of cellular respiration, where most of the ATP is generated by oxidative phosphorylation    .
- The Kreb cycle also provides precursors for the synthesis of certain amino acids, as well as other biomolecules   .
- The Kreb cycle is regulated by feedback inhibition, where the products of the cycle inhibit the enzymes that catalyze the cycle    .
- The Kreb cycle is one of the oldest metabolic pathways, and is conserved across many different organisms   .
