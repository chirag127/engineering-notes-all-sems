### Role of microorganisms in the production of industrial chemicals and pharmaceuticals

- Microorganisms are used in industry to produce a variety of organic compounds including acids, growth stimulants, and enzymes.
- Microorganisms can be genetically modified or engineered to aid in large-scale production of desired products.
- Some examples of industrial chemicals and pharmaceuticals produced by microorganisms are:

  - Amino acids: Microbes make only the L-isomer of amino acids, which are essential for protein synthesis and human nutrition. Some amino acids, such as lysine and glutamic acid, are produced by fermentation of sugars or molasses using bacteria such as Corynebacterium or Brevibacterium .
  - Citric acid: Citric acid is widely used as a flavoring agent, preservative, and antioxidant in food and beverages. It is produced by the mold Aspergillus niger, which converts glucose or sucrose into citric acid in a submerged aerobic fermentation process .
  - Enzymes: Enzymes are biological catalysts that speed up chemical reactions. They have many applications in food, detergent, textile, paper, leather, and pharmaceutical industries. Some examples of enzymes produced by microorganisms are amylases, proteases, lipases, cellulases, and pectinases. These enzymes are usually secreted by fungi or bacteria, such as Aspergillus, Bacillus, or Streptomyces .
  - Antibiotics: Antibiotics are substances that inhibit the growth or kill other microorganisms. They are used to treat bacterial infections and prevent diseases. Most antibiotics are produced by fungi or bacteria, such as Penicillium, Streptomyces, or Bacillus. Some examples of antibiotics are penicillin, streptomycin, tetracycline, and erythromycin .
  - Hormones: Hormones are chemical messengers that regulate various physiological processes in animals and plants. They are used to treat hormonal disorders, enhance growth, and improve reproduction. Some examples of hormones produced by microorganisms are insulin, human growth hormone, and interferon. These hormones are usually produced by recombinant DNA technology, which involves inserting the gene for the hormone into a suitable host cell, such as Escherichia coli or Saccharomyces cerevisiae .
  - Biofuels: Biofuels are renewable sources of energy that can be derived from biomass, such as plants or microorganisms. They are used to reduce greenhouse gas emissions and dependence on fossil fuels. Some examples of biofuels produced by microorganisms are ethanol, biodiesel, biogas, and hydrogen. These biofuels are usually produced by fermentation, transesterification, or gasification of organic substrates, such as sugars, starches, oils, or wastes, using yeasts, bacteria, or algae .

- In the industrial process, microorganisms act like chemical factories. To be effective, they should liberate a large amount of a single product that can be efficiently isolated and purified. The microorganisms should be easy to maintain and cultivate, and should have genetic stability with infrequent mutations.
- The industrial process involves several steps, such as strain selection, media optimization, fermentation, downstream processing, and quality control. These steps aim to maximize the yield, quality, and safety of the product .
