### Energy Utilization

- Energy utilization is the process of converting the chemical energy stored in organic molecules into other forms of energy that can be used by living organisms for various functions.
- Energy utilization involves two main steps: catabolism and anabolism.
  - Catabolism is the breakdown of complex molecules into simpler ones, releasing energy and reducing power (electrons).
  - Anabolism is the synthesis of complex molecules from simpler ones, consuming energy and reducing power.
- The main source of energy for most living organisms is glucose, a six-carbon sugar that can be obtained from food or synthesized from other molecules.
- Glucose can be catabolized through different pathways, depending on the availability of oxygen and the type of organism.
  - Aerobic respiration is the complete oxidation of glucose to carbon dioxide and water, using oxygen as the final electron acceptor. This process yields the maximum amount of energy (about 36 ATP molecules per glucose molecule) and occurs in the mitochondria of eukaryotic cells.
  - Anaerobic respiration is the partial oxidation of glucose to other organic compounds, using an inorganic molecule other than oxygen as the final electron acceptor. This process yields less energy (about 2 ATP molecules per glucose molecule) and occurs in some prokaryotic cells.
  - Fermentation is the partial oxidation of glucose to organic acids or alcohols, using an organic molecule as the final electron acceptor. This process yields the least energy (about 2 ATP molecules per glucose molecule) and occurs in the cytoplasm of both prokaryotic and eukaryotic cells.

### Structure of mitochondria

- Mitochondria are the organelles where aerobic respiration takes place in eukaryotic cells.
- Mitochondria have a double membrane structure: an outer membrane and an inner membrane.
  - The outer membrane is smooth and permeable to small molecules.
  - The inner membrane is folded into cristae, which increase the surface area for the electron transport chain and oxidative phosphorylation. The inner membrane also contains transport proteins that regulate the movement of molecules across the membrane.
- The space between the two membranes is called the intermembrane space, which has a low pH due to the accumulation of protons from the electron transport chain.
- The space enclosed by the inner membrane is called the matrix, which contains the enzymes and coenzymes of the Krebs cycle, as well as mitochondrial DNA and ribosomes.

### Cellular respiration

- Cellular respiration is the process of releasing energy from glucose and transferring it to ATP, the main energy currency of the cell.
- Cellular respiration consists of four stages: glycolysis, formation of acetyl co-A, Krebs cycle, and electron transport system and oxidative phosphorylation.
  - Glycolysis is the splitting of glucose into two molecules of pyruvate, generating 2 ATP and 2 NADH (reduced form of nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide) molecules. Glycolysis occurs in the cytoplasm and does not require oxygen.
  - Formation of acetyl co-A is the conversion of pyruvate into acetyl coenzyme A (acetyl co-A), releasing carbon dioxide and generating 2 NADH molecules. This step occurs in the mitochondrial matrix and requires oxygen.
  - Krebs cycle is the oxidation of acetyl co-A into carbon dioxide, generating 2 ATP, 6 NADH, and 2 FADH2 (reduced form of flavin adenine dinucleotide) molecules per glucose molecule. The Krebs cycle occurs in the mitochondrial matrix and requires oxygen.
