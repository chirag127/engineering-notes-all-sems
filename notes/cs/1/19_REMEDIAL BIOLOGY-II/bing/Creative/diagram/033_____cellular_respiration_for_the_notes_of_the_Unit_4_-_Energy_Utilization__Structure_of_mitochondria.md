### Cellular respiration

- Cellular respiration is the process by which biological fuels are oxidised in the presence of an inorganic electron acceptor such as oxygen to produce large amounts of energy, to drive the bulk production of adenosine triphosphate (ATP).
- ATP is the main energy currency of the cell, and it is used to power various cellular reactions and functions.
- Cellular respiration can be divided into four main stages: glycolysis, pyruvate oxidation, the citric acid or Krebs cycle, and oxidative phosphorylation .

#### Structure of mitochondria

- Mitochondria are the organelles where most of the cellular respiration takes place in eukaryotic cells.
- Mitochondria have a double membrane structure: an outer membrane that surrounds the whole organelle, and an inner membrane that folds into cristae, creating a large surface area for the electron transport chain and ATP synthesis.
- The space between the two membranes is called the intermembrane space, and the space enclosed by the inner membrane is called the matrix, where the Krebs cycle occurs.

#### Glycolysis

- Glycolysis is the first stage of cellular respiration, and it takes place in the cytoplasm of the cell.
- Glycolysis is the breakdown of glucose, a six-carbon sugar, into two molecules of pyruvate, a three-carbon compound, with the net production of two ATP and two NADH (a reduced form of nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide, an electron carrier) .
- Glycolysis does not require oxygen, and it can occur in both aerobic and anaerobic conditions.

#### Pyruvate oxidation

- Pyruvate oxidation is the second stage of cellular respiration, and it takes place in the mitochondrial matrix.
- Pyruvate oxidation is the conversion of pyruvate into acetyl coenzyme A (acetyl-CoA), a two-carbon molecule that enters the Krebs cycle, with the release of one carbon dioxide and one NADH per pyruvate .
- Pyruvate oxidation requires oxygen, and it is the link between glycolysis and the Krebs cycle.

#### Krebs cycle

- Krebs cycle, also known as the citric acid cycle, is the third stage of cellular respiration, and it takes place in the mitochondrial matrix.
- Krebs cycle is a series of reactions that oxidise acetyl-CoA into two carbon dioxide molecules, with the production of one ATP, three NADH, and one FADH2 (a reduced form of flavin adenine dinucleotide, another electron carrier) per acetyl-CoA .
- Krebs cycle completes the oxidation of glucose, and it also provides intermediates for other metabolic pathways, such as amino acid and lipid synthesis.

#### Electron transport chain and oxidative phosphorylation

- Electron transport chain and oxidative phosphorylation are the fourth and final stage of cellular respiration, and they take place in the inner mitochondrial membrane.
- Electron transport chain is a series of protein complexes and electron carriers that transfer the electrons from NADH and FADH2 to oxygen, the final electron acceptor, forming water as a by-product .
- Electron transport chain creates a proton gradient across the inner mitochondrial membrane, which drives the synthesis of ATP by a protein called ATP synthase, in a process called oxidative phosphorylation .
- Electron transport chain and oxidative phosphorylation are the most efficient and productive stage of cellular respiration, generating about 28 to 32 ATP per glucose molecule .
