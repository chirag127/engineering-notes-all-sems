### Protozoa

- Protozoa are **unicellular, microscopic, and primitive types of animals**  that belong to the kingdom Protista .
- Protozoa are **heterotrophic**, meaning they use organic carbon as a source of energy. They also have a **membrane-bound nucleus** and other organelles.
- Protozoa can **move** in water by various means, such as flagella, cilia, pseudopodia, or pellicles . They can also **reproduce** by asexual or sexual methods, such as binary fission, multiple fission, conjugation, or syngamy .
- Protozoa are **diverse** in their morphology, physiology, ecology, and evolution . They can be **free-living** or **parasitic**, and can cause diseases in humans and other animals  .
- Some common examples of protozoa are **Amoeba, Paramecium, Plasmodium, Monocystis, Opalina, Euglena, etc.**  .
- Protozoa are **classified** into four major groups based on their mode of locomotion :
  - **Sarcodina**: These are protozoa that move by **pseudopodia**, which are temporary extensions of the cytoplasm. Examples are Amoeba, Entamoeba, etc.
  - **Mastigophora**: These are protozoa that move by **flagella**, which are whip-like structures attached to the cell membrane. Examples are Euglena, Trypanosoma, etc.
  - **Ciliophora**: These are protozoa that move by **cilia**, which are short hair-like structures covering the cell surface. Examples are Paramecium, Vorticella, etc.
  - **Sporozoa**: These are protozoa that **do not move** by themselves, but rely on other organisms or vectors for transmission. Examples are Plasmodium, Monocystis, etc.
