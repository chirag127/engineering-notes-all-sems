### Carbohydrate metabolism

Carbohydrate metabolism is the set of biochemical processes that involve the formation, breakdown, and interconversion of carbohydrates in living organisms. Carbohydrates are organic molecules that consist of carbon, hydrogen, and oxygen atoms. They can be classified into simple sugars (monosaccharides) and complex sugars (polysaccharides).

Some of the main functions of carbohydrates are:

- To provide energy for cellular activities through the process of cellular respiration.
- To store energy in the form of glycogen in animals and starch in plants.
- To serve as structural components of cell walls (cellulose) and exoskeletons (chitin).
- To act as signaling molecules and receptors on cell membranes.

The main steps of carbohydrate metabolism are:

- Digestion: The breakdown of complex carbohydrates into simple sugars by enzymes in the mouth, stomach, and small intestine. The simple sugars are then absorbed into the bloodstream by the enterocytes.
- Glycolysis: The oxidation of glucose into pyruvate in the cytoplasm of the cell, producing two molecules of ATP and two molecules of NADH.
- Pyruvate oxidation: The conversion of pyruvate into acetyl-CoA in the mitochondria, producing one molecule of CO2 and one molecule of NADH per pyruvate.
- Citric acid cycle: The oxidation of acetyl-CoA into CO2 and H2O in the mitochondrial matrix, producing two molecules of ATP, six molecules of NADH, and two molecules of FADH2 per acetyl-CoA.
- Oxidative phosphorylation: The transfer of electrons from NADH and FADH2 to oxygen in the inner mitochondrial membrane, producing a proton gradient that drives the synthesis of ATP by ATP synthase.
- Gluconeogenesis: The synthesis of glucose from non-carbohydrate precursors such as lactate, glycerol, and amino acids in the liver and kidneys, mainly during fasting or starvation.
- Glycogenesis: The synthesis of glycogen from glucose in the liver and muscles, mainly during fed or resting state.
- Glycogenolysis: The breakdown of glycogen into glucose in the liver and muscles, mainly during exercise or stress.
- Pentose phosphate pathway: The generation of pentose sugars (such as ribose) and NADPH from glucose in the cytoplasm of the cell, mainly for nucleotide and fatty acid synthesis.

Carbohydrate metabolism is regulated by various hormones, enzymes, and feedback mechanisms that ensure the balance between glucose supply and demand in the body. Some of the key regulators are:

- Insulin: A hormone secreted by the pancreas that stimulates glucose uptake by the cells, glycolysis, glycogenesis, and lipogenesis, and inhibits gluconeogenesis and glycogenolysis, mainly during high blood glucose levels.
- Glucagon: A hormone secreted by the pancreas that stimulates gluconeogenesis, glycogenolysis, and lipolysis, and inhibits glycolysis and glycogenesis, mainly during low blood glucose levels.
- Epinephrine: A hormone secreted by the adrenal glands that stimulates glycogenolysis, lipolysis, and gluconeogenesis, and inhibits glycolysis and glycogenesis, mainly during stress or exercise.
- Pyruvate kinase: An enzyme that catalyzes the final step of glycolysis, producing pyruvate and ATP. It is inhibited by ATP, acetyl-CoA, and alanine, and activated by fructose-1,6-bisphosphate and insulin, reflecting the energy and glucose status of the cell.
