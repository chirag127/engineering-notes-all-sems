### Size, shape, and arrangement of bacterial cells

- Bacterial cells are microscopic organisms that cannot be seen by the naked eye. They are measured in micrometers (µm) or nanometers (nm), where 1 µm = 0.001 mm and 1 nm = 0.001 µm.
- Bacterial cells have different shapes and arrangements that help them adapt to different environments and functions. The most common shapes are:
  - Cocci: spherical or oval cells that can be arranged in pairs (diplococci), chains (streptococci), clusters (staphylococci), or other patterns.
  - Bacilli: rod-shaped or cylindrical cells that can be arranged in singles, pairs (diplobacilli), chains (streptobacilli), or other patterns. Some bacilli have curved or helical shapes, such as vibrios, spirilla, or spirochetes.
  - Coccobacilli: short and oval cells that are intermediate between cocci and bacilli.
- The average size of bacterial cells varies depending on the shape and species. Some examples are:
  - The spherical shaped bacteria have an average diameter ranging from 0.5-2.0 µm.
  - The rod shaped bacteria have an average diameter of 0.25-1.0 µm and a length of 1-10 µm. For example, E. coli, a common bacillus, is 1.1 to 1.5 µm wide by 2.0 to 6.0 µm long.
  - The spirochetes, a group of spiral bacteria, can reach up to 500 µm in length.
  - The smallest known bacteria, from the genus Nanobacterium, have an average length of only 323 nm and an average width of 242 nm.
- The shape and arrangement of bacterial cells are determined by the cell wall, which is a rigid structure that surrounds the cell membrane and provides protection and support. The cell wall is composed of peptidoglycan, a polymer of sugars and amino acids, that forms a mesh-like layer. The cell wall can be classified into two types based on the amount and structure of peptidoglycan:
  - Gram-positive: the cell wall has a thick layer of peptidoglycan that retains a purple dye called crystal violet during a staining procedure called Gram stain. Gram-positive bacteria tend to have simpler cell walls and more regular shapes and arrangements.
  - Gram-negative: the cell wall has a thin layer of peptidoglycan that is surrounded by an outer membrane that contains lipopolysaccharides (LPS), which are complex molecules that act as endotoxins. Gram-negative bacteria tend to have more complex cell walls and more diverse shapes and arrangements.
