### Metabolic diversity and pathways of energy use

- Metabolic diversity mainly refers to the different metabolic strategies that organisms have evolved to obtain energy.
- Metabolic pathways are series of connected chemical reactions that feed one another and convert starting molecules into products.
- Metabolic pathways can be broadly divided into two categories based on their effects:
  - Catabolic pathways break down complex molecules into simpler ones and release energy. Examples are glycolysis, Krebs cycle, and oxidative phosphorylation.
  - Anabolic pathways build up complex molecules from simpler ones and consume energy. Examples are photosynthesis, gluconeogenesis, and protein synthesis.
- Metabolic pathways are regulated by enzymes, which catalyze the chemical reactions and control the rate and direction of the pathway.
- Metabolic pathways are also influenced by the availability of substrates, products, coenzymes, and environmental factors such as temperature and pH.
- Metabolic pathways evolved among prokaryotes before eukaryotes arose as the result of their interaction and coevolution with changing physicochemical environmental conditions.
- Metabolic diversity is reflected in the different types of energy sources and electron acceptors that organisms can use for cellular respiration.
  - Energy sources can be organic molecules (such as glucose, fatty acids, or amino acids) or inorganic molecules (such as hydrogen, ammonia, or sulfur). Organisms that use organic molecules are called organotrophs, while those that use inorganic molecules are called lithotrophs.
  - Electron acceptors can be oxygen (aerobic respiration), nitrate, sulfate, carbon dioxide, or other inorganic molecules (anaerobic respiration). Organisms that use oxygen are called aerobes, while those that use other electron acceptors are called anaerobes.
- Metabolic diversity is also reflected in the different types of energy production mechanisms that organisms can use to generate ATP.
  - The phosphagen system (ATP-PC system) uses creatine phosphate to quickly regenerate ATP from ADP. This system provides immediate energy for short bursts of activity, such as sprinting or lifting weights.
  - The glycolytic system (anaerobic glycolysis) uses glucose to produce ATP and lactate. This system provides energy for moderate to high intensity activities, such as running or cycling, that last for a few minutes.
  - The oxidative system (aerobic respiration) uses glucose, fatty acids, or amino acids to produce ATP, carbon dioxide, and water. This system provides energy for low to moderate intensity activities, such as walking or jogging, that last for longer periods of time.
- Metabolic diversity is important for the survival and adaptation of organisms to different environmental conditions and ecological niches.
- Metabolic diversity is also important for the biogeochemical cycles of elements such as carbon, nitrogen, sulfur, and iron, which are mediated by the metabolic activities of different organisms.
- Metabolic diversity is a result of the evolutionary history and genetic diversity of life on Earth.
