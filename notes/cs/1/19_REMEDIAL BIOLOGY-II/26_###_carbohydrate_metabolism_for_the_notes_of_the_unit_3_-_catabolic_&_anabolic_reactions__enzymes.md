### carbohydrate metabolism for the notes of the Unit 3 - Catabolic & anabolic reactions: enzymes, energy production and carbohydrate metabolism. Carbohydrate metabolism refers to the various biochemical processes that occur in cells to break down carbohydrates and convert them into energy.

1. Glucose Metabolism: Glucose is the primary source of energy for cells and is metabolized through a series of reactions known as glycolysis. During glycolysis, glucose is converted into pyruvate, which can then be used to generate ATP, the molecule that provides energy for cellular processes.

2. Aerobic Respiration: In the presence of oxygen, pyruvate can be further metabolized through the citric acid cycle and the electron transport chain, resulting in the production of large amounts of ATP. This process is known as aerobic respiration.

3. Anaerobic Respiration: In the absence of oxygen, pyruvate can be converted into lactate through anaerobic respiration. This process generates a smaller amount of ATP compared to aerobic respiration.

4. Regulation of Carbohydrate Metabolism: Carbohydrate metabolism is regulated by hormones such as insulin and glucagon, which help to control blood glucose levels. In addition, enzymes play a key role in the regulation of carbohydrate metabolism by controlling the rate of reactions.

5. Integration of Metabolism: Carbohydrate metabolism is integrated with other metabolic pathways, including lipid and protein metabolism, to ensure that cells have a constant source of energy. This integration allows cells to respond to changes in energy demand and helps to maintain homeostasis.

In conclusion, carbohydrate metabolism is the process by which carbohydrates are broken down and converted into energy. This process is regulated by hormones and enzymes and is integrated with other metabolic pathways to ensure a constant supply of energy for cells.
