## Unit 3 - Catabolic & anabolic reactions: enzymes, energy production and carbohydrate metabolism. Lipid & protein catabolism, Energy production mechanism, metabolic diversity & pathways of energy use. Integration of metabolism

Unit 3 of the subject of Remedial Biology-II KCS focuses on catabolic and anabolic reactions, including enzymes, energy production, and carbohydrate, lipid, and protein metabolism.

1. Catabolic and Anabolic Reactions: Catabolic reactions are reactions that break down molecules to release energy, while anabolic reactions are reactions that build molecules and require energy.

2. Enzymes: Enzymes are proteins that act as catalysts in chemical reactions. They increase the rate of reactions by lowering the activation energy required to initiate the reaction.

3. Energy Production: Energy is produced in cells through a series of metabolic reactions, including the breakdown of glucose and other energy-rich molecules. This energy is stored in the form of ATP (adenosine triphosphate), which is used by cells to power metabolic processes.

4. Carbohydrate Metabolism: Carbohydrates are broken down in cells to release energy. This process involves the breakdown of glucose to pyruvate through glycolysis and the citric acid cycle.

5. Lipid and Protein Catabolism: Lipids and proteins can also be broken down to release energy. Lipids are broken down to fatty acids and glycerol, while proteins are broken down to amino acids.

6. Energy Production Mechanism: Energy production in cells is a complex process that involves several metabolic pathways, including glycolysis, the citric acid cycle, and oxidative phosphorylation.

7. Metabolic Diversity: Different organisms have unique metabolic pathways that allow them to adapt to their environment and meet their energy needs.

8. Pathways of Energy Use: Energy produced in cells can be used for a variety of processes, including growth, reproduction, and movement.

In conclusion, Unit 3 of the subject of Remedial Biology-II KCS focuses on the catabolic and anabolic reactions that are essential for energy production and the maintenance of cellular function. Understanding the metabolic pathways and mechanisms of energy use is important for understanding the functioning of cells and organisms.
