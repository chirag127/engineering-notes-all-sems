Structure of Mitochondria:
- Mitochondria are membrane-bound organelles found in eukaryotic cells that are responsible for producing energy in the form of ATP.
- The structure of mitochondria is complex and includes an outer membrane and an inner membrane.
- The outer membrane is permeable to small molecules, while the inner membrane is more selective and is the site of the majority of energy production.
- The inner membrane is folded into cristae, which increase the surface area available for energy production.
- The matrix is the space within the inner membrane and contains enzymes involved in the production of ATP.
- The structure of mitochondria is essential for their function, as the inner membrane and cristae are the site of the electron transport chain and oxidative phosphorylation, the processes by which ATP is produced. 
- Understanding the structure of mitochondria is important for understanding their role in cellular respiration and energy production, and for the development of treatments for diseases associated with mitochondrial dysfunction.
