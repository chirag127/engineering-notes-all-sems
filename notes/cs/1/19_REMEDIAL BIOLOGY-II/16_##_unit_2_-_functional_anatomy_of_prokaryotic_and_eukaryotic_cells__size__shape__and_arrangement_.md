## Unit 2 - Functional Anatomy of Prokaryotic and Eukaryotic Cells: Size, shape, and arrangement of bacterial cells. Structure and function of cells

Functional Anatomy of Prokaryotic and Eukaryotic Cells:
- Prokaryotic and eukaryotic cells are the two basic types of cells found in living organisms.
- Prokaryotic cells are smaller and simpler in structure, lacking a nucleus and other membrane-bound organelles.
- Eukaryotic cells are larger and more complex, with a nucleus and other membrane-bound organelles.
- The size, shape, and arrangement of bacterial cells can vary, with some being spherical, rod-shaped, or spiral-shaped.
- The structure and function of cells are closely related, with each cell component having a specific function that contributes to the overall function of the cell.
- The cell membrane is a selectively permeable barrier that regulates the exchange of materials between the cell and its environment.
- The cytoplasm is the gel-like substance that fills the cell and contains the cell's metabolic machinery.
- The nucleus is the control center of the cell, containing the genetic material and controlling the cell's activities.
- Other membrane-bound organelles, such as mitochondria, ribosomes, and the endoplasmic reticulum, also play important roles in the function of the cell.
- Understanding the anatomy and function of cells is essential for understanding the biology of living organisms and the processes that occur within cells.
