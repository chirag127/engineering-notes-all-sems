
### Lipid & Protein Catabolism

- Lipid and protein catabolism are processes that involve the breakdown of larger molecules into smaller ones. 
- Lipids are broken down into fatty acids and glycerol, which are then further metabolized into energy-rich molecules such as acetyl-CoA and NADH. 
- Proteins are broken down into amino acids, which can be used for energy production or for the synthesis of new proteins. 
- The energy produced from the breakdown of lipids and proteins is used to fuel other metabolic processes. 
- Enzymes are important for catabolism, as they facilitate the chemical reactions that break down the molecules into smaller components. 
- The energy produced from catabolism is stored in the form of ATP, which can then be used for anabolic reactions, such as the synthesis of new proteins and lipids. 
- Metabolic pathways are important for the integration of metabolism, as they allow for the efficient use of energy and the conversion of molecules from one form to another. 
- Metabolic diversity is important for the survival of organisms in different environments, as different organisms have evolved to use different metabolic pathways.
