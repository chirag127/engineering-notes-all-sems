
### Kreb Cycle

Kreb Cycle is a part of energy utilization in the subject of REMEDIAL BIOLOGY-II KCS. It is a series of biochemical reactions that occur in the mitochondria of cells, and is essential for the production of energy. 

The structure of mitochondria, cellular respiration, and the relationship of carbohydrate metabolism to other compounds are all important for understanding the Kreb Cycle.

1. Glycolysis: Glycolysis is the process of breaking down glucose molecules into pyruvate molecules, which then enter the Kreb Cycle.
2. Formation of Acetyl Co-A: Acetyl Co-A is formed from the pyruvate molecules produced by glycolysis.
3. Kreb Cycle: The Kreb Cycle is a series of biochemical reactions that occur in the mitochondria of cells. It is essential for the production of energy.
4. Electron Transport System and Oxidative Phosphorylation: The Electron Transport System and Oxidative Phosphorylation are processes that occur in the mitochondria of cells. They are responsible for the production of energy from the Kreb Cycle.
5. ATP: ATP, or adenosine triphosphate, is the energy currency of the cell. It is produced from the Electron Transport System and Oxidative Phosphorylation.
6. Factors Affecting Respiration: Factors such as temperature, oxygen availability, and pH can affect the rate of respiration and the production of energy in the Kreb Cycle.
