
### Viral Structures

* Viruses are not considered living organisms, as they lack the ability to reproduce on their own and depend on their host to survive. 
* Viruses are composed of genetic material (DNA or RNA) surrounded by a protective protein coat. 
* Depending on the virus, the protein coat may be helical or icosahedral in shape. 
* The protein coat is also known as a capsid and is made up of many repeating protein subunits. 
* The capsid is responsible for the virus's shape, size, and ability to attach to host cells. 
* Viruses also contain an envelope, which is composed of lipids and proteins. 
* The envelope helps the virus attach to host cells and protects the viral genetic material. 
* Viruses are classified into families based on their genetic material, shape, and envelope. 
* Viruses can cause a variety of diseases in humans, ranging from the common cold to more serious illnesses such as HIV/AIDS. 
* Viruses can also cause diseases in animals, plants, and bacteria. 
* In addition, some viruses can be beneficial, such as those that are used in gene therapy and vaccines.
