### Cellular Respiration

Cellular respiration is the process by which cells break down organic compounds to release energy in the form of ATP.

#### Structure of Mitochondria

- Mitochondria are the organelles responsible for cellular respiration.
- They have an outer membrane and an inner membrane with folds called cristae.
- The space inside the inner membrane is called the matrix.

#### Relationship of Carbohydrate Metabolism to Other Compounds

- Carbohydrates are the primary fuel source for cellular respiration.
- Lipids and proteins can also be broken down for energy through different pathways.

#### Glycolysis

- Glycolysis is the first step in cellular respiration.
- It occurs in the cytoplasm and breaks down glucose into two molecules of pyruvate.
- ATP and NADH are produced during glycolysis.

#### Formation of Acetyl Co-A

- Pyruvate is transported into the mitochondria where it is converted to acetyl Co-A.
- This step generates NADH and CO2.

#### Krebs Cycle

- The Krebs cycle occurs in the matrix of the mitochondria.
- Acetyl Co-A enters the cycle and is broken down into CO2, NADH, FADH2, and ATP.
- The Krebs cycle is also known as the citric acid cycle.

#### Electron Transport System and Oxidative Phosphorylation

- The electron transport system is a series of proteins in the inner membrane of the mitochondria.
- NADH and FADH2 donate electrons to the system, which generates a proton gradient.
- The energy from the gradient is used to make ATP through oxidative phosphorylation.

#### ATP

- ATP is the energy currency of the cell.
- It is produced during cellular respiration and used for many cellular processes.

#### Factors Affecting Respiration

- Temperature, pH, and the availability of oxygen can affect the rate of cellular respiration.
- Some drugs and poisons can also interfere with cellular respiration.
