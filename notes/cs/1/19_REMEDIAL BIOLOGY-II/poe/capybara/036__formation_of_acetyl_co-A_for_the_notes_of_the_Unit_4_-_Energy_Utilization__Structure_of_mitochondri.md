- Pyruvate is transported from the cytoplasm into the mitochondria, where it undergoes a series of enzymatic reactions to form acetyl Co-A.
- The enzyme complex responsible for this reaction is called the pyruvate dehydrogenase complex (PDC).

- The formation of acetyl Co-A is an irreversible reaction, meaning that the molecule cannot be converted back into pyruvate.
- Acetyl Co-A is a key molecule in the Krebs cycle, where it is used to generate energy in the form of ATP.
- In addition to its role in energy production, acetyl Co-A is also used in the synthesis of fatty acids and cholesterol.
