### Proteobacteria

Proteobacteria is a major phylum of bacteria and includes a wide range of species that are of great importance to humans and the environment. Here are some key points about proteobacteria:

- Proteobacteria is one of the largest phyla of bacteria, with over 5,400 known species.
- They are gram-negative bacteria and are characterized by their outer membrane, which contains lipopolysaccharides (LPS).
- Proteobacteria include many well-known species, such as Escherichia coli, Salmonella, and Vibrio cholerae.
- They are found in a wide range of environments, including soil, water, and the digestive tracts of animals.
- Many proteobacteria are capable of causing disease in humans, such as Helicobacter pylori, which causes stomach ulcers, and Neisseria gonorrhoeae, which causes gonorrhea.
- Proteobacteria also play important roles in the environment, such as in the nitrogen cycle, where they convert nitrogen gas into forms that can be used by plants and other organisms.
- Some proteobacteria are also used in biotechnology, such as in the production of insulin and other pharmaceuticals.

Overall, proteobacteria are an incredibly diverse and important group of bacteria that have significant impacts on human health and the environment.
