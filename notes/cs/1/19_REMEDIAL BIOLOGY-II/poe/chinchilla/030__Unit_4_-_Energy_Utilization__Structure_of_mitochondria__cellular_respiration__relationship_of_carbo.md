## Unit 4 - Energy Utilization

In this unit, we will explore the process of energy utilization in living organisms. We will discuss the various components involved in the process, including the structure of mitochondria, cellular respiration, and the relationship of carbohydrate metabolism to other compounds. Additionally, we will cover Glycolysis, the formation of acetyl co-A, the Kreb cycle, the Electron Transport System and Oxidative Phosphorylation, and ATP. Finally, we will examine the factors that affect respiration.

### Mitochondria

- Mitochondria are the organelles responsible for producing energy in eukaryotic cells.
- They are surrounded by a double membrane, with the inner membrane being highly folded to form cristae.
- The space between the inner and outer membranes is called the intermembrane space.
- The matrix, which is found inside the inner membrane, contains enzymes and substrates needed for the Kreb cycle.

### Cellular Respiration

- Cellular respiration is the process by which cells convert glucose into ATP, the main energy currency of the cell.
- The process can be divided into three stages: glycolysis, the Kreb cycle, and oxidative phosphorylation.
- Glycolysis occurs in the cytoplasm and converts glucose into pyruvate, producing a small amount of ATP in the process.
- The pyruvate is then transported into the mitochondria, where it is converted into acetyl co-A, which enters the Kreb cycle.
- The Kreb cycle produces more ATP and carbon dioxide, which is released as a waste product.
- The final stage, oxidative phosphorylation, occurs in the inner membrane of the mitochondria and is responsible for producing the majority of ATP.

### Carbohydrate Metabolism

- Carbohydrates are the primary source of energy for most organisms.
- Other compounds, such as fats and proteins, can also be used for energy, but they must first be converted into carbohydrates or related compounds.
- Carbohydrate metabolism involves the conversion of carbohydrates into glucose, which can then be used for energy production.
- The process can be regulated by hormones such as insulin and glucagon.

### Glycolysis

- Glycolysis is the first stage of cellular respiration and occurs in the cytoplasm.
- It involves the conversion of glucose into pyruvate, producing a small amount of ATP and NADH in the process.
- The process can be regulated by enzymes such as hexokinase and phosphofructokinase.

### Formation of Acetyl co-A

- Acetyl co-A is formed from pyruvate in a process that occurs in the mitochondria.
- The process is catalyzed by the enzyme pyruvate dehydrogenase.
- Acetyl co-A is a key molecule in the Kreb cycle, as it is the precursor to citrate.

### Kreb Cycle

- The Kreb cycle, also known as the citric acid cycle, occurs in the matrix of the mitochondria.
- It involves a series of reactions that produce ATP, NADH, and FADH2.
- The Kreb cycle can be regulated by enzymes such as isocitrate dehydrogenase and alpha-ketoglutarate dehydrogenase.

### Electron Transport System and Oxidative Phosphorylation

- The Electron Transport System (ETS) is a series of proteins and enzymes located in the inner membrane of the mitochondria.
- It is responsible for transferring electrons from NADH and FADH2 to oxygen, producing a proton gradient in the process.
- The proton gradient is used to produce ATP through a process called oxidative phosphorylation.
- The ETS and oxidative phosphorylation are tightly coupled and can be regulated by a variety of factors, including oxygen availability and electron carrier concentration.

### ATP

- ATP is the main energy currency of the cell.
- It is produced by the process of cellular respiration and is used for a variety of cellular processes, including muscle contraction, protein synthesis, and active transport.
- ATP can be broken down into ADP and phosphate, releasing energy that can be used by the cell.

### Factors Affecting Respiration

- Respiration can be affected by a variety of factors, including temperature, pH, and substrate availability.
- Enzymes involved in cellular respiration are often sensitive to changes in these factors, which can lead to changes in the rate of respiration.
- Additionally, factors such as oxygen availability and electron carrier concentration can also affect the rate of respiration.
