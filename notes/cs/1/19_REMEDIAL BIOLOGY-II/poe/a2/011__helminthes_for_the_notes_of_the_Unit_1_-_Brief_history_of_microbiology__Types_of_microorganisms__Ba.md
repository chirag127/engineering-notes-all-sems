### Helminthes

- Helminthes are parasitic worms that live in and feed on a living host to gain nourishment and protection, while disrupting their hosts' nutrient absorption, causing weakness and disease.
- They are also known as parasitic worms.
- The major groups of helminthes are:
** Platyhelminthes: Flatworms like tapeworms and flukes
** Nemathelminthes: Roundworms like ascaris, hookworms, trichinella
** Acanthocephala: Thorny-headed worms

- Life cycle: Helminthes have complex life cycles involving one or more intermediate hosts. Eg: Tapeworms require pigs and cattle (intermediate hosts) to complete their life cycle. Flukes require snails or insects as intermediate hosts.
- Pathology: Helminthes cause diseases by depriving the host of nutrients, damaging tissues, and secreting toxins. This results in symptoms like diarrhoea, anaemia, swelling of organs, etc.
- Treatment: Anthelmintic drugs like albendazole, mebendazole, piperazine, etc. are used to treat helminth infections. Prevention includes improved sanitation and hygiene and use of drugs to eliminate infections in intermediate hosts.
