### Energy production mechanism for the notes of the Unit 3 - Catabolic & anabolic reactions: enzymes, energy production and carbohydrate metabolism. Lipid & protein catabolism, Energy production mechanism, metabolic diversity & pathways of energy use. Integration of metabolism in the subject of REMEDIAL BIOLOGY-II KCS.

- Cellular respiration is the process of converting nutrients into ATP and waste products. It involves 3 main stages:
- Glycolysis - glucose is broken down into pyruvate and produces a small amount of ATP and NADH.
- Citric acid cycle (Krebs cycle) - Pyruvate is converted into acetyl-CoA which enters the citric acid cycle and produces more NADH and FADH2.
- Electron transport chain - The high energy electrons from NADH and FADH2 are transferred through a chain of electron carriers which pumps protons across a membrane to generate a proton gradient. The flow of protons back into the matrix through ATP synthase produces the majority of ATP.
- The rate of cellular respiration is controlled by the supply of oxygen and ADP, and the rate of glycolysis. It is regulated to meet the energy demands of the cell.
- The products of cellular respiration are ATP, carbon dioxide and water. The ATP is used to power cellular processes, while the carbon dioxide and water are waste products excreted from the cell.
- The process of cellular respiration is essential for the production of the energy that all cells need to carry out their functions. It unlocks the energy stored in the chemical bonds of nutrients and converts it into a usable form of chemical energy in the form of ATP.
