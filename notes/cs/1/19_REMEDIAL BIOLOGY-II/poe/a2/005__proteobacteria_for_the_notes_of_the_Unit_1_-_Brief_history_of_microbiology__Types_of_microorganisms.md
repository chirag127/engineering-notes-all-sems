### Proteobacteria

- Proteobacteria is a major phylum of Gram-negative bacteria. They are distinguished by the presence of several modifications to proteins, such as the addition of phosphate groups (phosphorylation) or the addition of acetyl groups (acetylation).
- Proteobacteria are further classified into 5 classes based on their morphology, metabolism, and other characteristics:
	- Alphaproteobacteria: Includes bacteria that are usually rods, curved rods or spiral in shape. Example: Rhizobium, Brucella, etc.
	- Betaproteobacteria: Includes bacteria that are usually rods in shape. Example: Neisseria, Bordetella, etc.
	- Gammaproteobacteria: Includes bacteria that are usually rods in shape. Example: Escherichia, Salmonella, Pseudomonas, etc.
	- Deltaproteobacteria: Includes bacteria that are usually curved rods or vibrios in shape. Example: Bdellovibrio, etc.
	- Epsilonproteobacteria: Includes bacteria that are helical or curved in shape. Example: Campylobacter, Helicobacter, etc.

- Other facts:
	- Proteobacteria are found in a wide variety of environments like soil, water, plants, animals, etc.
	- Many proteobacteria are pathogens and can cause diseases in plants, animals, and humans. Example: Salmonella, Pseudomonas, Escherichia, etc.
	- Some proteobacteria are nitrogen-fixing bacteria that convert atmospheric nitrogen into ammonia, which can be used by plants. Example: Rhizobium, Bradyrhizobium, etc.
	- Proteobacteria play an important role in biogeochemical cycling and degradation of organic compounds.
