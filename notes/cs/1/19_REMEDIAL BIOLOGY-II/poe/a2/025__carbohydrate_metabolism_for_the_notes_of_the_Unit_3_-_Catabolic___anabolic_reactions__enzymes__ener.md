### Carbohydrate Metabolism

- Carbohydrates are organic compounds that contain carbon, hydrogen and oxygen atoms.
- The major carbohydrates in the human diet are sugars, starches and fiber.
- Carbohydrate catabolism refers to the breakdown of carbohydrates into simpler forms that can be utilized by the body.
- The breakdown of carbohydrates begins in the mouth through salivary amylase. The end products of carbohydrate digestion are monosaccharides glucose, fructose and galactose which are absorbed into the bloodstream.
- Glucose is the primary energy source for the body's cells. It is transported via the bloodstream to cells throughout the body.
- Excess glucose is stored in the liver and muscles as glycogen. When glucose levels decline, glycogen is converted back to glucose to maintain blood glucose homeostasis.
- If glycogen stores are full, excess glucose is converted to fat for long-term energy storage. This process is known as de novo lipogenesis.
- The rate of carbohydrate catabolism depends on factors such as hormone levels, exercise and the body's energy requirements.
- The metabolic pathways of carbohydrate catabolism are glycolysis, glycogenolysis and the pentose phosphate pathway. These pathways produce energy, NADH, FADH2 and other intermediates required for cell growth and function.
