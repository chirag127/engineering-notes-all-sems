### Kreb cycle

- Kreb cycle occurs in the mitochondrial matrix.
- It is also known as citric acid cycle or tricarboxylic acid (TCA) cycle.
- It is the second stage of cellular respiration that oxidizes the two carbon acetyl CoA into carbon dioxide with the release of energy (NADH and FADH2).
- The cycle consists of 8 steps catalyzed by 8 enzymes.
- The acetyl CoA combines with oxaloacetic acid to form citrate, which is catalyzed by citrate synthase.
- Citrate is converted to isocitrate, which is decarboxylated to form alpha-ketoglutarate.
- Succinyl CoA and succinate are produced. Succinate is oxidized to fumarate.
- Fumarate is hydrated to form malate.
- Malate is converted to oxaloacetate to complete one cycle.
- For each turn of the cycle, one molecule of glucose produces 2NADH, 1FADH2 and 1 GTP (or ATP).
- The high energy NADH and FADH2 transfer electrons to the electron transport chain to generate more ATP.
- The rate of Kreb cycle depends on the availability of oxygen, ADP, inorganic phosphate and acetyl CoA.
