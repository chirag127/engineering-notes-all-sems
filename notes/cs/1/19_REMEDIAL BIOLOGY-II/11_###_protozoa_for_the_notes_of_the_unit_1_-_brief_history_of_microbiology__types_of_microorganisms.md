Protozoa are single-celled organisms that belong to the kingdom Protista. They are characterized by their ability to move independently and their diverse range of habitats, which include freshwater, saltwater, and soil.

Protozoa can be classified into several groups based on their mode of movement and feeding, including flagellates, ciliates, amoebae, and sporozoans. Flagellates are organisms that use flagella for movement, ciliates use cilia, amoebae move by changing shape, and sporozoans are parasitic organisms that reproduce by forming spores or spores.

Protozoa play an important role in the food chain as predators and prey, and some species are also important pathogens that can cause diseases in humans and animals.
