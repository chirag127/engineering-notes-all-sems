Cellular Respiration:
- Cellular respiration is the process by which cells convert the energy stored in organic molecules into a form that can be used by the cell.
- Cellular respiration occurs in the mitochondria, the powerhouses of the cell.
- The process of cellular respiration can be divided into several stages, including glycolysis, the formation of acetyl co-A, the Krebs cycle, and the electron transport system and oxidative phosphorylation.
- During glycolysis, glucose is broken down into two molecules of pyruvate, releasing a small amount of energy.
- The formation of acetyl co-A involves the conversion of pyruvate into acetyl co-A, which can enter the Krebs cycle.
- The Krebs cycle is a series of reactions that further break down acetyl co-A, releasing energy and generating the molecule NADH.
- The electron transport system and oxidative phosphorylation use the energy from NADH to generate ATP, the energy currency of the cell.
- Factors that can affect cellular respiration include the availability of oxygen, the presence of inhibitors, and changes in the metabolic needs of the cell.
- Understanding cellular respiration is essential for understanding the production and utilization of energy in cells and the metabolic processes that support life.
