### Functional Anatomy of Prokaryotic and Eukaryotic Cells for the notes of the Unit 2 - Functional Anatomy of Prokaryotic and Eukaryotic Cells: Size, shape, and arrangement of bacterial cells. Structure and function of cells in the subject of REMEDIAL BIOLOGY-II KCS

Prokaryotic and Eukaryotic cells are the two main types of cells that make up all living organisms. They differ in their size, structure, and function.

1. Prokaryotic Cells: Prokaryotic cells are small, simple cells that lack a nucleus and other membrane-bound organelles. They are typically found in bacteria and archaea.

2. Size and Shape: Prokaryotic cells are usually much smaller than eukaryotic cells, with a diameter of 0.5-5 µm. They can have a variety of shapes, including spherical (cocci), rod-shaped (bacilli), and spiral-shaped (spirilla).

3. Structure: Prokaryotic cells have a simple structure, consisting of a plasma membrane, cytoplasm, and DNA. They also have a cell wall that provides support and protection.

4. Function: Prokaryotic cells are capable of carrying out all of the functions necessary for life, including metabolism, growth, and reproduction. They can also exchange genetic material with other prokaryotic cells through processes such as conjugation, transduction, and transformation.

5. Eukaryotic Cells: Eukaryotic cells are larger and more complex than prokaryotic cells, with a nucleus and other membrane-bound organelles. They are found in all multicellular organisms, as well as some single-celled organisms such as yeasts and protozoa.

6. Size and Shape: Eukaryotic cells are typically much larger than prokaryotic cells, with a diameter of 10-100 µm. They can have a variety of shapes, including spherical, elliptical, and elongated.

7. Structure: Eukaryotic cells have a complex structure, with a plasma membrane, cytoplasm, nucleus, and various organelles such as mitochondria, ribosomes, and the endoplasmic reticulum.

8. Function: Eukaryotic cells are capable of carrying out a wide range of functions, including energy production, protein synthesis, and cell division. They also have specialized structures such as cilia and flagella that allow them to move.

In conclusion, prokaryotic and eukaryotic cells are the two main types of cells that make up all living organisms. They differ in their size, structure, and function, with prokaryotic cells being smaller and simpler and eukaryotic cells being larger and more complex.
