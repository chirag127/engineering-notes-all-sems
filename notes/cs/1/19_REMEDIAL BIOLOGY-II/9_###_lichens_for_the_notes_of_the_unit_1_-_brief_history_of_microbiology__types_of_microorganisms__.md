Lichens:
- Lichens are a type of symbiotic organism made up of a fungus and an alga or cyanobacterium.
- The fungus provides structure and protection to the lichen, while the alga or cyanobacterium photosynthesizes and produces food for both partners.
- Lichens can be found in a variety of environments, including on rocks, trees, and soil.
- They are important indicators of air quality and can be used to monitor changes in the environment.
- Lichens are also used as a food source by some animals, and some species have medicinal properties.
- Lichens are unique in that they are not a single organism, but rather a symbiotic relationship between two different organisms.
- This relationship is an example of mutualism, in which both partners benefit from the relationship.
- The study of lichens provides insight into the evolution of symbiotic relationships and the adaptation of organisms to changing environments.
