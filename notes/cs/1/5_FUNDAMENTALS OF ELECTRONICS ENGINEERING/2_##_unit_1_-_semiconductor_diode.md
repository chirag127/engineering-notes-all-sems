## Unit 1 - Semiconductor Diode

Unit 1 - Semiconductor Diode:
A semiconductor diode is a two-terminal electronic component that acts as a one-way valve for electric current. It allows current to flow in one direction but blocks it in the opposite direction. Diodes are made of semiconductor materials, such as silicon or germanium, and can be used for a variety of purposes, including rectification, voltage regulation, and signal detection.

Working Principle:
A p-n junction is formed by doping a semiconductor material with impurities to create two regions with different electrical conductivity. When a voltage is applied across the p-n junction, the majority carriers in the p-type material are attracted to the n-type material and flow across the junction, creating a flow of current in one direction. When the voltage is reversed, the majority carriers are repelled and the flow of current is blocked.

Applications:
- Rectification: Used to convert AC voltage to DC voltage.
- Voltage regulation: Used to maintain a constant voltage output.
- Signal detection: Used to detect and amplify signals in electronic circuits.
