### Depletion layer for the notes of the Unit 1 - Semiconductor Diode in the subject of FUNDAMENTALS OF ELECTRONICS ENGINEERING

Depletion Layer:
The depletion layer is a region in a p-n junction where the majority carriers have been depleted, creating a region with a high resistance to the flow of current. The width of the depletion layer is proportional to the doping concentration and the applied voltage across the p-n junction.

Working Principle:
In a p-n junction, the majority carriers are attracted to the depletion layer and form a potential barrier that prevents the flow of current in the reverse direction. The width of the depletion layer is determined by the balance between the diffusion of majority carriers from the p-type material to the n-type material and the drift of majority carriers due to the electric field.

Applications:
- The depletion layer is important for the functioning of semiconductor diodes, as it creates the one-way valve for electric current.
- The depletion layer can also be used to control the flow of current in other semiconductor devices, such as bipolar junction transistors and metal-oxide-semiconductor field-effect transistors.
