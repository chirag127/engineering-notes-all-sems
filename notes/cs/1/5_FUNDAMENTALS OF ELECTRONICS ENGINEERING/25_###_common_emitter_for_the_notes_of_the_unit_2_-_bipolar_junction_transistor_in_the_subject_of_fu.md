### Common Emitter for the notes of the Unit 2 - Bipolar Junction Transistor in the subject of FUNDAMENTALS OF ELECTRONICS ENGINEERING

Common Emitter (CE) configuration is a bipolar junction transistor (BJT) circuit arrangement where the emitter terminal of the transistor is a common terminal to both the input and output signals. It is commonly used as an amplifier circuit. 

CE configuration offers high voltage gain and current gain, making it ideal for use in amplifiers. In this configuration, the base-emitter junction is forward-biased, while the base-collector junction is reverse-biased. This allows for the flow of current from the emitter to the collector, creating an amplified output signal.

The input signal is applied to the base terminal, and the output signal is taken from the collector terminal. The emitter terminal is connected to the ground, allowing the flow of current through the transistor. The voltage gain of a CE configuration can be calculated using the formula: Av = -hfe * (Rc/Re), where hfe is the current gain of the transistor, Rc is the collector resistance, and Re is the emitter resistance.

In conclusion, the Common Emitter configuration is a versatile and widely used arrangement in BJT circuits, providing high voltage and current gain, making it suitable for use in amplifier circuits.
