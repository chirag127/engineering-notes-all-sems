### Cellular Communication

1. Cellular networks are made up of cells, each with a low-power radio transmitter/receiver base station.
2. Cells are typically 0.5 to 5 miles in radius and overlap to provide coverage over a large geographic area.
3. As users move from cell to cell, their call or data session is handed off between base stations to maintain connectivity.
4. Mobiles phones and devices continuously scan for the strongest signal from nearby base stations and connect to the strongest available signal.
5. Cells use different radio frequencies to avoid interference, and frequency reuse patterns are employed to maximize spectrum efficiency.
6. Base stations are connected to a mobile switching center (MSC) or gateway, which manages connectivity between cells, switches calls and data to the PSTN or Internet, and tracks user location to enable call routing and handoffs.
7. 2G/3G/4G cellular networks use a variety of multiple access technologies (FDMA, TDMA, CDMA, OFDMA) to enable multiple users and devices to share the radio spectrum.
8. LTE is the 4G cellular technology that uses OFDMA for downlink and SC-FDMA for uplink and delivers significantly higher peak data rates than previous technologies.
