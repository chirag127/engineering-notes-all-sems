### Unit Follower for the notes of the Unit 4 - Operational Amplifiers

- An operational amplifier (op-amp) is a direct-coupled high gain amplifier usually consisting of differential input stages and a single-ended output stage.
- A unit follower or buffer amplifier is a op-amp circuit which has a voltage gain of 1. It is used to isolate circuits from the loading effects of other circuits.
- The input signal voltage is equal to the output signal voltage in a unit follower. It draws a negligible amount of current from the driving circuit and provides high input impedance.
- The circuit diagram consists of a op-amp with its output connected to the inverting input. The non-inverting input is grounded.
- Since there is a negative feedback, the differential input voltage is zero. This makes the inverting input voltage equal to the non-inverting input voltage. Hence, the output voltage follows the input voltage.
- The applications of unit follower include isolation, matching impedances, driving low impedance loads, etc. It prevents the loading of voltage sources by providing high input impedance.
- The bandwidth and slew rate are the important characteristics to consider while choosing an op-amp for a unit follower. A high bandwidth leads to accurate reproduction of the input waveform.
