### Transistor Construction

- A transistor consists of three layers of semiconductor material.
- The middle layer is thin and is known as the base. The top layer is the emitter and the bottom layer is the collector.
- The emitter and collector are connected to a voltage source. The base is connected to a variable voltage source to control the transistor.
- The emitter-base junction is forward biased and the collector-base junction is reverse biased.
- The emitter injects electrons into the base. The electrons move from the emitter to the collector, flowing through the base. This flow of electrons through the base is controlled by the voltage applied to the base.
- A small change in base voltage can produce a large change in emitter current. This forms the basis for the amplification function of the transistor.
- The transistor has three terminals – emitter (E), base (B) and collector (C). The input is applied between the base and emitter and the output is taken from the collector and emitter.
