## Unit 2 - Bipolar Junction Transistor

1. Bipolar Junction Transistor (BJT) is a three terminal semiconductor device consisting of two p-n junctions connected back to back.
2. The three terminals are named as Emitter (E), Base (B) and Collector (C).
3. The emitter injects electrons into the base. The base controls the number of electrons entering the collector region. The collector collects the electrons injected from the emitter.
4. The two p-n junctions are - Emitter-base junction and Collector-base junction.
5. BJT has two modes of operation - Forward active / Forward biased mode and Reverse active / Reverse biased mode.
6. In forward active mode, the emitter-base junction is forward biased and collector-base junction is reverse biased. Majority carriers (electrons) flow from emitter to collector.
7. In reverse active mode, the emitter-base junction is reverse biased and collector-base junction is forward biased. Minority carriers (holes) flow from collector to emitter.
8. BJT can act as an amplifier or a switch. The magnitude of current amplification depends on the ratio of emitter and collector doping concentrations.
9. The three regions of BJT are Emitter, Base and Collector. The Base region controls the flow of electrons from Emitter to Collector.
10. BJT has higher input impedance than MOSFET and can handle higher power but has lower switching speed than MOSFET.
