### Differentiator for the notes of the Unit 4 - Operational Amplifiers in the subject of FUNDAMENTALS OF ELECTRONICS ENGINEERING

- A differentiator is a circuit that converts a constant input voltage into a constant output voltage and converts a changing input voltage into an output voltage that is proportional to the rate of change of the input voltage.
- An op amp differentiator circuit produces an output voltage that is proportional to the differentiation of the input voltage.
- The basic op amp differentiator circuit consists of an op amp with a resistor R connected between the inverting input and ground, and a capacitor C connected between the inverting input and the output.
- The output voltage Vout is proportional to the derivative of the input voltage Vin. As Vin changes, Vout produces a change that is proportional to the rate of change of Vin.
- The gain of the differentiator is equal to -RC. The magnitude of the gain can be adjusted by varying the values of R and C.
- A differentiator amplifies high-frequency signals and attenuates low-frequency signals. This can be useful for signal processing applications such as producing steep edges in a waveform.
- The differentiator has the disadvantage of amplifying noise and high-frequency interference or jitter in the input signal. Additional filtering may be required to reduce these effects.
