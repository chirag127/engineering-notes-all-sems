## Unit 3 - Field Effect Transistor

1. Field Effect Transistor (FET) is a semiconductor device which relies on an electric field to control the conductivity of a "channel" in a semiconductor material.
2. There are two types of FETs:
- N-type FET (NFET): It uses an n-type semiconductor as the channel. The current flow is due to the movement of electrons.
- P-type FET (PFET): It uses a p-type semiconductor as the channel. The current flow is due to the movement of holes.
3. The FET has a source, drain and a gate terminal. The source and drain terminals are connected to the semiconductor channel. The gate terminal is insulated from the channel.
4. When a voltage is applied to the gate terminal, it produces an electric field which controls the conductivity of the channel. This is known as the field effect.
5. If the electric field increases the conductivity of the channel, it is known as enhancement mode. If it decreases the conductivity, it is known as depletion mode.
6. The FET is a voltage controlled device as the current flow is controlled by the gate-source voltage. It provides high input impedance, low output impedance and fast switching speeds. It has applications in amplifiers, oscillators and logic gates.
