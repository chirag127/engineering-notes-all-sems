### Transfer Characteristic for the notes of the Unit 3 - Field Effect Transistor

- The transfer characteristic of an FET shows the variation of drain current (ID) with gate-source voltage (VGS) for a constant drain-source voltage (VDS).
- For an n-channel enhancement MOSFET, when VGS=0, ID=0. As VGS is increased, ID also increases. This region is known as the ohmic or linear region.
- At a sufficiently high VGS, ID saturates and does not increase with further increase in VGS. This is known as the saturation region.
- The value of VGS at which ID starts saturating is called the pinch-off voltage (VP) and the corresponding ID is called the pinch-off current (IP).
- The FET operates in the ohmic region for VGS slightly greater than VP and in the saturation region for VGS >> VP.
- The transfer characteristic is similar to that of a diode and shows that an FET can act as a voltage controlled device.
- The shape of the transfer characteristic can be modified by varying parameters like channel length and width, doping concentration, oxide thickness etc.
