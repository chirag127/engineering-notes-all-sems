### Operation for the notes of the Unit 2 - Bipolar Junction Transistor

1. BJT has three regions of operations:
- Active region: Both junctions are forward biased. Significant current flows. Useful for amplification.
- Saturation region: Collector-base junction is reverse biased. Collector current is maximum. Used as a switch.
- Cut-off region: Base-emitter junction is reverse biased. No current flows. Used as an open switch.

2. Modes of operations:
- Forward active mode: Base-emitter junction forward biased, collector-base junction reverse biased.Output current is in phase with input current. Used for amplification.
- Reverse active mode: Base-emitter junction reverse biased, collector-base junction forward biased.Output current is out of phase with input current. Not practically used.
- Saturation mode: Base-emitter junction forward biased, collector-base junction slightly forward biased. Collector current is maximum. Used as an on switch.
- Cut-off mode: Base-emitter junction reverse biased. No current flows. Used as an off switch.

3. BJT as an Amplifier:
- Common emitter: High input impedance, low output impedance. Voltage gain high, current gain medium. Mostly used configuration.
- Common collector: Low input impedance, high output impedance. Voltage gain low, current gain high. Buffering applications.
- Common base: Medium input impedance, low output impedance. Voltage gain medium, current gain low. Wide bandwidth.
