### Op-Amp basic for the notes of the Unit 4 - Operational Amplifiers in the subject of FUNDAMENTALS OF ELECTRONICS ENGINEERING

1. An operational amplifier (op-amp) is a DC-coupled high-gain electronic voltage amplifier with a differential input and, usually, a single-ended output.
2. Op-amps are among the most widely used electronic devices today, being used in a vast array of consumer, industrial, and scientific devices.
3. The principal characteristics of an op-amp are high gain, high input impedance, low output impedance, and voltage-follower output.
4. The output voltage is dependent on the difference between the two input voltages, multiplied by the gain of the op-amp.
5. The inverting input is connected to ground through a resistor, and the non-inverting input is connected to ground through another resistor. The difference in voltage at the inputs multiplied by the gain yields the output voltage.
6. The output voltage is equal to the input voltage difference multiplied by the open-loop gain of the op-amp. An op-amp's stable and predictable operation depends on negative feedback that reduces the gain to a finite value.
7. The value of feedback components sets the resulting closed-loop gain of the op-amp circuit. Op-amps are designed to operate from dual supplies or single supplies, depending on the design.
