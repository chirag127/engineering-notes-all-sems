### Introduction for the notes of the Unit 4 - Operational Amplifiers

- Operational Amplifiers (Op-Amps) are integrated circuits which are designed to be used as voltage amplifiers. They have high gain, high input impedance and low output impedance.
- Op-Amps have two input terminals - inverting (-) and non-inverting (+) and one output terminal.
- Op-Amps are direct coupled high gain amplifier circuits usually consisting of solid state components.
- The configuration of Op-Amps can be used to perform mathematical operations like addition, subtraction, integration, differentiation, etc. on the input signals.
- Some important characteristics of Op-Amps are:
- Infinite input impedance - The input impedance of Op-Amps is very high (of the order of mega ohms) so that they do not load the input circuit.
- Zero input current - No current flows into the input terminals. All the current flows through the feedback components.
- Infinite voltage gain - The voltage gain of ideal Op-Amps is infinite but in real Op-Amps it is very high (of the order of 100000).
- Zero output impedance - The output impedance of Op-Amps is very low (of the order of 100 ohms). So, they can drive low impedance loads.
- Bandwidth - The bandwidth of Op-Amps ranges from 100 kHz to 1 MHz based on the applications.
