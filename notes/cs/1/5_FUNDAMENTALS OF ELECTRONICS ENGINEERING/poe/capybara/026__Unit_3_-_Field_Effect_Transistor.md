## Unit 3 - Field Effect Transistor

A Field Effect Transistor (FET) is a semiconductor device that uses an electric field to control the flow of current. It is widely used in electronic circuits for amplification, switching, and signal processing. Here are some important points to understand about FET:

- FET is a three-terminal device that consists of a source, a drain, and a gate.

- It operates on the principle of the electric field effect, which means that the voltage applied to the gate terminal controls the current flowing through the device.

- There are two main types of FET: Junction Field Effect Transistor (JFET) and Metal Oxide Semiconductor Field Effect Transistor (MOSFET).

- JFET is a depletion mode device that operates by varying the width of a conducting channel between the source and the drain. It is suitable for low power applications due to its low noise and high input impedance.

- MOSFET is an enhancement mode device that operates by controlling the flow of current through a conducting channel between the source and the drain. It is widely used in digital circuits due to its high switching speed and low power consumption.

- FET has several advantages over bipolar junction transistor (BJT), such as high input impedance, low noise, and low power consumption. However, it has a lower gain and a limited voltage range.

- FET can be used in various applications, such as amplifiers, oscillators, filters, and switches. It is also used in integrated circuits (ICs) for digital logic and memory.

- FET requires careful handling and protection against electrostatic discharge (ESD) due to its high sensitivity to static electricity.

In conclusion, FET is a crucial component in modern electronic circuits, and it offers several advantages over BJT. Understanding the working principle and characteristics of FET is essential for designing and analyzing electronic circuits.
