### Introduction

Operational amplifiers, or op-amps, are integrated circuits that have become an essential element of modern electronic systems. They are widely used in various applications, such as signal processing, power amplification, and control systems. In this unit, we will study the characteristics, configurations, and applications of operational amplifiers.

Here are the key concepts that you need to understand in this unit:

- The basic properties of ideal and real op-amps, including input and output impedance, gain, bandwidth, and slew rate.
- The common op-amp circuits, such as inverting, non-inverting, summing, difference, integrator, and differentiator circuits, and their analysis and design.
- The op-amp limitations and their effects on the circuit performance, such as saturation, offset voltage, noise, and frequency response.
- The applications of op-amps in various circuits, such as active filters, oscillators, comparators, voltage regulators, and instrumentation amplifiers.
- The practical considerations in op-amp design, such as power supply, stability, temperature, and layout.

By the end of this unit, you should be able to:

- Analyze and design basic op-amp circuits using the ideal op-amp model and the non-ideal op-amp model.
- Evaluate the performance of op-amp circuits in terms of gain, bandwidth, noise, and distortion.
- Select and apply appropriate op-amp configurations and components for specific applications.
- Understand the limitations and challenges of op-amp circuits and how to mitigate them.
- Design and simulate op-amp circuits using computer-aided design tools.

To achieve these learning outcomes, you will need to study the textbook chapters, attend the lectures, and participate in the laboratory sessions. You will also need to practice solving problems and analyzing circuits on your own.
