### Clampers

Clampers are circuits that are used to shift the DC level of an input signal. They are also known as DC restorers because they restore the DC level of the input signal.

Clampers are used in electronic circuits to remove the DC component of a signal. They are also used to shift the DC level of a signal to a desired level.

Clampers are used in many applications, including television and radio receivers, power supplies, and audio circuits.

There are two types of clampers: positive clampers and negative clampers.

Positive clampers shift the DC level of an input signal so that it is greater than zero volts. They are also known as peak clippers because they clip the peak of the input signal.

Negative clampers shift the DC level of an input signal so that it is less than zero volts. They are also known as bottom clippers because they clip the bottom of the input signal.

The basic circuit for a positive clamper consists of a diode, a resistor, and a capacitor. The diode is connected in series with the input signal, and the resistor and capacitor are connected in parallel across the diode.

The basic circuit for a negative clamper is the same as that for a positive clamper, except that the diode is connected in the opposite direction.

Clampers are useful in circuits where the DC level of an input signal needs to be shifted or removed. They are widely used in electronic circuits and are an important part of the fundamentals of electronics engineering.
