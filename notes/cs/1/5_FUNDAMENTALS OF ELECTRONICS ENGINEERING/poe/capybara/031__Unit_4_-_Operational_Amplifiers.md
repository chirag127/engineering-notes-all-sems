## Unit 4 - Operational Amplifiers

Operational Amplifiers, or Op-Amps, are an essential component in electronic circuits. They are used for amplification, filtering, and signal conditioning. Here are some key points to keep in mind when studying operational amplifiers:

- An Op-Amp is a differential amplifier that amplifies the difference between two input voltages. The output is proportional to the difference and is amplified by a gain factor.
- The typical Op-Amp has two inputs, one inverting and one non-inverting. The inverting input is usually marked with a negative sign, while the non-inverting input is marked with a positive sign.
- The most common Op-Amp configuration is the non-inverting amplifier, which amplifies the input signal without changing its phase. The output voltage is proportional to the input voltage and the gain factor.
- The inverting amplifier is another common configuration, which amplifies the input signal and changes its phase by 180 degrees. The output voltage is proportional to the input voltage and the gain factor, but with an inverted polarity.
- The gain of an Op-Amp is determined by the ratio of the feedback resistor to the input resistor, in the case of the non-inverting amplifier. In the case of the inverting amplifier, the gain is determined by the ratio of the feedback resistor to the input resistor, multiplied by -1.
- Op-Amps are sensitive to noise and interference, so it is important to use proper grounding and shielding techniques to reduce unwanted signals.
- Op-Amps have a limited bandwidth, which means that they can only amplify signals within a certain frequency range. This is why they are often used in combination with filters to eliminate unwanted frequencies.
- Op-Amps have a high input impedance, which means that they draw very little current from the input signal. This makes them ideal for use with sensors and other low-power devices.
- Op-Amps have a limited output voltage swing, which means that they cannot amplify signals beyond a certain voltage. This is why they are often used in combination with voltage regulators to provide a stable power supply.
- Op-Amps can be used in a variety of applications, including audio amplifiers, active filters, voltage regulators, and many more.
