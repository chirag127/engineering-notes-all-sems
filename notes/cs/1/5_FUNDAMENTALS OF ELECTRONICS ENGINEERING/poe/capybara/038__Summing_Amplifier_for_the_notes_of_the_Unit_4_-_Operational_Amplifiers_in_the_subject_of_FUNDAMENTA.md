### Summing Amplifier

A summing amplifier is an operational amplifier circuit that is used to add multiple input voltages together. It is also known as an adder or a mixer circuit. The summing amplifier is a basic building block in analog circuits and is widely used in signal processing applications.

#### Circuit Design

The summing amplifier circuit consists of an inverting operational amplifier with multiple input resistors. The input resistors are connected in series to the inverting input of the operational amplifier. The non-inverting input of the operational amplifier is grounded. The output of the operational amplifier is connected to a feedback resistor that is connected back to the inverting input of the operational amplifier.

#### Circuit Operation

The summing amplifier circuit works by summing the input voltages that are connected to the input resistors. The input resistors convert the input voltages into currents that are summed at the inverting input of the operational amplifier. The input currents are amplified by the operational amplifier and converted back into output voltage at the output of the operational amplifier.

#### Formula

The output voltage of the summing amplifier can be calculated using the following formula:

Vout = -(Rf/R1 × Vin1) - (Rf/R2 × Vin2) - (Rf/R3 × Vin3) - ... - (Rf/Rn × Vinn)

Where Vout is the output voltage, Rf is the feedback resistor, R1, R2, R3, ..., Rn are the input resistors, Vin1, Vin2, Vin3, ..., Vinn are the input voltages.

#### Applications

The summing amplifier circuit is widely used in signal processing applications such as audio mixers, synthesizers, and digital signal processing. It is also used in instrumentation and control systems to add multiple sensor signals together. The summing amplifier is a basic building block in analog circuits and is an essential circuit for any electronics engineer to learn.
