

### Basics of Signal Representation and Analysis

1. Signals can be represented in a variety of ways, such as discrete-time and continuous-time signals, analog and digital signals, and periodic and aperiodic signals.

2. Discrete-time signals are signals that are sampled at regular intervals, while continuous-time signals are signals that are continuous over time.

3. Analog signals are signals that are represented by a continuous range of values, while digital signals are signals that are represented by a discrete set of values.

4. Periodic signals are signals that repeat over time, while aperiodic signals are signals that do not repeat over time.

5. Signal analysis is the process of analyzing signals in order to determine their characteristics, such as their frequency, amplitude, phase, and more.

6. Fourier analysis is a type of signal analysis that is used to decompose a signal into its component frequencies.

7. The Nyquist-Shannon sampling theorem states that a signal can be reconstructed from its samples if the sampling rate is at least twice the highest frequency of the signal.

8. The Fourier transform is a mathematical tool used to transform a signal from the time domain to the frequency domain, and vice versa.
