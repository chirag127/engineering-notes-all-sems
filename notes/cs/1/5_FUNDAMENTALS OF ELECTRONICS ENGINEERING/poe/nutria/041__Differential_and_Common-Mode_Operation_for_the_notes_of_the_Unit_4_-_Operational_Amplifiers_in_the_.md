
### Differential and Common-Mode Operation for the notes of the Unit 4 - Operational Amplifiers in the subject of FUNDAMENTALS OF ELECTRONICS ENGINEERING

* Differential operation is the most important feature of an operational amplifier (op-amp). It enables the op-amp to amplify the difference between two input signals. 
* Common-mode operation is when both input signals are the same. In this case, the output of the op-amp is zero. 
* Differential mode gain is the ratio of the output voltage to the difference in input voltage. The gain of an op-amp is typically very high, making it ideal for amplifying small signals.
* Common-mode gain is the ratio of the output voltage to the common-mode input voltage. This is typically much lower than the differential mode gain, and is used to reject noise and other common-mode signals.
* An op-amp can also be used as a comparator. In this case, the input signals are compared, and the output is either high or low depending on the comparison. 
* Op-amps can also be used to build active filters, which are used to filter out unwanted frequencies from a signal. 
* Op-amps can also be used to build oscillators, which are used to generate a periodic signal. 
* Op-amps are widely used in many electronic systems such as power supplies, audio systems, and medical instruments.
