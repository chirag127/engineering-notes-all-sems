
### V-I Characteristics for the Notes of the Unit 1 - Semiconductor Diode in the Subject of Fundamentals of Electronics Engineering

1. A semiconductor diode is a two-terminal electronic device that is used to control the flow of current.
2. It has two terminals, an anode and a cathode, which are connected to a semiconductor material.
3. The diode is a unidirectional device that allows current to flow in one direction only.
4. The current-voltage (I-V) characteristics of a diode is a graph that shows the relationship between the current and the voltage of the diode.
5. The I-V characteristics of a diode can be used to determine the forward and reverse bias of the diode.
6. The forward bias of a diode is when the anode is connected to the positive terminal and the cathode is connected to the negative terminal.
7. The reverse bias of a diode is when the anode is connected to the negative terminal and the cathode is connected to the positive terminal.
8. The forward bias of a diode results in a decrease in the voltage across the diode, while the reverse bias results in an increase in the voltage across the diode.
9. The I-V characteristics of a diode can also be used to determine the breakdown voltage of the diode, which is the voltage at which the diode begins to conduct current.
10. The I-V characteristics of a diode can also be used to determine the maximum current that the diode can handle before it is damaged.
