
### Light-Emitting Diodes

Light-emitting diodes (LEDs) are a type of semiconductor diode that emits light when a current passes through it. LEDs are used in a variety of applications, including automotive lighting, home lighting, and electronics.

* LEDs are made from semiconductor materials, such as gallium arsenide and gallium phosphide. 
* LEDs are more energy efficient than traditional incandescent light bulbs. 
* LEDs have a longer lifespan than traditional light bulbs. 
* LEDs are available in a variety of colors and brightness levels. 
* LEDs are used in a variety of applications, such as automotive lighting, home lighting, and electronics. 
* LEDs require a current-limiting resistor to prevent damage to the diode. 
* LEDs can be damaged by excessive current, voltage, or temperature. 
* LEDs are available in a variety of packages, including surface-mount and through-hole.
