## Unit 2 - Bipolar Junction Transistor

Bipolar Junction Transistor (BJT):

Bipolar Junction Transistor (BJT) is a type of semiconductor device that is used for amplification and switching applications in electronics. In Unit 2 of Fundamentals of Electrical Engineering, it is important to understand the basic principles and operation of BJT.

1. Structure: BJT consists of three regions of semiconductor material: emitter, base, and collector. The emitter-base junction is forward-biased, while the collector-base junction is reverse-biased.

2. Operation: BJT operates based on the flow of majority carriers (electrons or holes) from the emitter to the collector, through the base. The base-emitter junction controls the flow of carriers, which results in amplification of the input signal.

3. Types of BJT: There are two types of BJT: NPN and PNP. NPN BJT has an N-type semiconductor material for the emitter and collector regions, while PNP BJT has a P-type semiconductor material for the emitter and collector regions.

4. Modes of Operation: BJT has three modes of operation: active mode, saturation mode, and cutoff mode. In the active mode, BJT operates as an amplifier, while in the saturation mode, BJT operates as a switch. In the cutoff mode, BJT is turned off and does not conduct current.

Applications of BJT:

1. Amplifiers: BJT is widely used as an amplifier in a variety of electronic circuits, such as audio amplifiers, radio frequency (RF) amplifiers, and operational amplifiers (Op-Amps).

2. Switches: BJT is also used as a switch in electronic circuits, such as digital circuits and power electronics.

3. Power Control: BJT is used for power control in a variety of applications, such as motor control and lighting control.
