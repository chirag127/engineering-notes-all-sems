### Zener Diodes breakdown mechanism (Zener and avalanche) for the notes of the Unit 1 - Semiconductor Diode in the subject of FUNDAMENTALS OF ELECTRONICS ENGINEERING

Zener Diodes and Breakdown Mechanisms:

Zener diodes are a type of semiconductor diode that are designed to operate in the reverse-bias breakdown region. In Fundamentals of Electronics Engineering, Unit 1 on Semiconductor Diodes, it is important to understand the breakdown mechanism of Zener diodes.

1. Zener Breakdown: Zener breakdown occurs in a Zener diode when the reverse-bias voltage exceeds a certain value, known as the Zener voltage. At this voltage, the electric field in the depletion region becomes strong enough to cause electrons to be injected into the conduction band, creating a large number of electron-hole pairs.

2. Avalanche Breakdown: Avalanche breakdown occurs in a Zener diode when the reverse-bias voltage exceeds a certain value, known as the avalanche voltage. At this voltage, the electric field in the depletion region becomes strong enough to cause electrons to be accelerated to high energies, creating a large number of electron-hole pairs.

Applications of Zener Diodes:

1. Voltage Regulation: Zener diodes are used in voltage regulation circuits to maintain a constant voltage across a load, even when the input voltage changes.

2. Clamping Circuits: Zener diodes are used in clamping circuits to limit the voltage across a load to a certain value.

3. Power Supplies: Zener diodes are used in power supplies to regulate the output voltage and to protect the power supply from overvoltage.

In conclusion, Zener diodes are a type of semiconductor diode that are designed to operate in the reverse-bias breakdown region. Zener breakdown and avalanche breakdown are the two types of breakdown mechanisms that occur in Zener diodes. Zener diodes are used in voltage regulation circuits, clamping circuits, and power supplies.
