# Special Purpose Two Terminal Devices

Two terminal devices are electronic components that have only two terminals, such as anode and cathode, and allow current to flow only in one direction. The most common two terminal device is the diode, which is a semiconductor device that has a p-n junction and a non-linear current-voltage characteristic. Diodes are widely used in various applications, such as rectification, switching, voltage regulation, signal modulation, etc.

However, there are some special types of diodes that have different structures and functions than the ordinary diodes. These are called special purpose two terminal devices, and they are designed to perform specific tasks in electronic circuits. Some of the examples of special purpose two terminal devices are:

- **Tunnel diode**: This is a diode that has a very thin p-n junction and a negative resistance region in its current-voltage characteristic. This means that the current decreases as the voltage increases in a certain range. Tunnel diodes are used for high-speed switching and oscillation, as they can operate at very high frequencies (up to several GHz).
- **Photo diode**: This is a diode that generates a current when exposed to light. The current is proportional to the intensity of the light. Photo diodes are used for light detection, optical communication, solar cells, etc.
- **Varactor diode**: This is a diode that has a variable capacitance depending on the applied voltage. The capacitance decreases as the voltage increases. Varactor diodes are used for tuning, frequency modulation, voltage-controlled oscillators, etc.
- **Schottky diode**: This is a diode that has a metal-semiconductor junction instead of a p-n junction. The metal-semiconductor junction has a lower voltage drop and a faster switching speed than the p-n junction. Schottky diodes are used for rectification, logic circuits, power converters, etc.
- **Light emitting diode (LED)**: This is a diode that emits light when a current flows through it. The color of the light depends on the band gap of the semiconductor material. LEDs are used for illumination, display, indicators, optical communication, etc.
- **Silicon controlled rectifier (SCR)**: This is a diode that has a third terminal called the gate. The gate controls the current flow between the anode and the cathode. The SCR can be turned on by applying a positive voltage to the gate, but it cannot be turned off by removing the gate voltage. The SCR can only be turned off by reducing the current below a certain level. SCRs are used for power control, switching, protection, etc.
