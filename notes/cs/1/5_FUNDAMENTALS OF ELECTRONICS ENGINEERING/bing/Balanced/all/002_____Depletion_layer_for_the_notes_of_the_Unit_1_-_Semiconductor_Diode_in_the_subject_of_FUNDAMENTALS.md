# Depletion layer

- A depletion layer is a region in a semiconductor diode where the mobile charge carriers are depleted, or reduced in number, due to diffusion or an electric field.
- A depletion layer acts as a barrier that opposes the flow of electrons from the n-side to the p-side of the semiconductor diode.
- A depletion layer is formed when a p-type and an n-type semiconductor material are joined together, forming a p-n junction.
- At the p-n junction, the electrons from the n-side diffuse into the p-side and recombine with the holes, leaving behind positively charged donor ions.
- Similarly, the holes from the p-side diffuse into the n-side and recombine with the electrons, leaving behind negatively charged acceptor ions.
- This process creates a region of immobile ions on both sides of the junction, which is the depletion layer.
- The depletion layer has a width that depends on the doping concentration and the applied voltage across the diode.
- The depletion layer also has a potential difference, or a voltage, that is called the built-in potential or the barrier potential.
- The built-in potential prevents further diffusion of charge carriers across the junction, unless an external voltage is applied.
- The depletion layer is important for the operation of the diode, as it determines the current-voltage characteristics and the switching behavior of the device.
