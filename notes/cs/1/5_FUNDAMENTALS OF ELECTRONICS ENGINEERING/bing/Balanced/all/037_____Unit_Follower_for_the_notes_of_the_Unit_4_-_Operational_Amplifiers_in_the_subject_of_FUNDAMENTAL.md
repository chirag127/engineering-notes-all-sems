# Unit Follower

- A unit follower, also called a voltage follower, buffer, or unity-gain amplifier, is a simple operational amplifier (op-amp) circuit that produces an output voltage equal to the input voltage.
- A unit follower is created by directly connecting the output of the op-amp to the inverting (-) input, as shown in the figure below .

![Unit follower circuit diagram](https://electronicsreference.com/sites/default/files/images/voltage-follower-circuit-diagram.png)

- A unit follower has the following characteristics  :
  - It has a voltage gain of 1, meaning that the output voltage is the same as the input voltage.
  - It has a very high input impedance and a very low output impedance, meaning that it can isolate the input source from the load and prevent loading effects.
  - It has a very high bandwidth and a very low distortion, meaning that it can faithfully reproduce the input signal without altering its frequency or shape.
  - It has a very low power consumption and a very high slew rate, meaning that it can operate at high speeds and respond quickly to changes in the input signal.
- A unit follower is useful for the following applications  :
  - It can be used to buffer a signal from a high impedance source, such as a sensor, to a low impedance load, such as an analog-to-digital converter (ADC), without affecting the signal quality or level.
  - It can be used to isolate different stages of a circuit, such as filters, amplifiers, or oscillators, to prevent interaction or interference between them.
  - It can be used to drive a capacitive load, such as a cable, a speaker, or a piezoelectric transducer, without causing instability or oscillation in the op-amp.
  - It can be used to implement a negative feedback loop, such as in a voltage regulator, a current source, or a temperature controller, to improve the performance and stability of the circuit.
