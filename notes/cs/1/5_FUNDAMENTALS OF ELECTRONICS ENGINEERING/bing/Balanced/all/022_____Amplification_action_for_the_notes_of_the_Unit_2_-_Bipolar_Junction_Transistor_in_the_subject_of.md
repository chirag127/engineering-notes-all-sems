# Amplification action of Bipolar Junction Transistor (BJT)

- A bipolar junction transistor (BJT) is a type of transistor that uses both electrons and holes as charge carriers in different regions of the device.
- A BJT has three terminals: the base (B), the collector (C), and the emitter (E). The base is the input terminal, the collector is the output terminal, and the emitter is the common terminal for both input and output currents.
- A BJT can operate in three modes: cut-off, active, and saturation. In the cut-off mode, the base-emitter junction is reverse biased and the base-collector junction is either reverse or forward biased, resulting in no current flow in the device. In the active mode, the base-emitter junction is forward biased and the base-collector junction is reverse biased, resulting in a small base current controlling a large collector current. In the saturation mode, both the base-emitter and the base-collector junctions are forward biased, resulting in a maximum collector current that is independent of the base current .
- A BJT can be used as an amplifier when it is operated in the active mode. An amplifier is a device that increases the strength of an input signal and produces an amplified output signal. Depending on the quantity amplified by the circuit, a BJT amplifier can be a voltage, current, or power amplifier .
- The basic principle of BJT amplification is that a small change in the base current causes a large change in the collector current, which is proportional to the current gain of the transistor. The current gain, also known as the beta (β) or the hFE, is the ratio of the collector current to the base current. The current gain of a BJT depends on the type and configuration of the transistor, as well as the operating conditions .
- The voltage gain of a BJT amplifier is the ratio of the output voltage to the input voltage. The voltage gain depends on the current gain, the load resistance, and the internal resistance of the transistor. The voltage gain can be increased by increasing the load resistance or decreasing the internal resistance .
- The power gain of a BJT amplifier is the ratio of the output power to the input power. The power gain depends on the voltage gain, the current gain, and the efficiency of the amplifier. The power gain can be increased by increasing the voltage gain or the current gain, or by improving the efficiency of the amplifier .
- The common emitter (CE) configuration is the most widely used BJT amplifier configuration, as it produces the highest current and power gain of all the three BJT configurations. The common emitter configuration has the emitter as the common terminal, the base as the input terminal, and the collector as the output terminal. The common emitter amplifier has a high voltage gain, a high current gain, a high power gain, a low input impedance, and a high output impedance.
- The common base (CB) configuration is the least used BJT amplifier configuration, as it produces the lowest current and power gain of all the three BJT configurations. The common base configuration has the base as the common terminal, the emitter as the input terminal, and the collector as the output terminal. The common base amplifier has a low voltage gain, a low current gain, a low power gain, a high input impedance, and a low output impedance.
- The common collector (CC) configuration, also known as the emitter follower, is the BJT amplifier configuration that produces the highest voltage gain of all the three BJT configurations. The common collector configuration has the collector as the common terminal, the base as the input terminal, and the emitter as the output terminal. The common collector amplifier has a high voltage gain, a low current gain, a low power gain, a high input impedance, and a low output impedance.
