# Fundamentals of Satellite & Radar Communication

## Satellite Communication

- Satellite communication is the use of artificial satellites to transmit and receive signals for voice, video and data communications.
- Satellites are relay stations in space that receive signals from an uplink Earth station, amplify them, change their frequency and retransmit them to a downlink Earth station.
- Satellites can cover large geographic areas and reach multiple sites simultaneously, making them economical, scalable and reliable for global communications.
- Satellites operate in different orbits, such as geostationary orbit (GEO), medium Earth orbit (MEO) and low Earth orbit (LEO), depending on their altitude, speed and inclination.
- Satellites use different frequency bands, such as C-band, Ku-band and Ka-band, to communicate with Earth stations, depending on their power, bandwidth and propagation characteristics.
- Satellite communication systems involve various components, such as antennas, transponders, modulators, demodulators, amplifiers, filters, converters and encoders.
- Satellite communication systems are subject to various challenges, such as signal attenuation, interference, noise, multipath, fading, rain, scintillation and jamming.
- Satellite communication systems are used for various applications, such as direct broadcast satellites (DBS), high throughput satellites (HTS), very small aperture terminals (VSAT), mobile satellite services (MSS), satellite navigation systems (GPS, GLONASS, Galileo, etc.), Earth observation satellites (remote sensing, weather, etc.) and deep space probes (Mars, Jupiter, etc.) .

## Radar Communication

- Radar communication is the use of radio waves to detect and locate objects by measuring their reflection and doppler shift.
- Radar stands for Radio Detection and Ranging System. It works by radiating energy into space and monitoring the echo or reflected signal from the objects.
- Radar communication involves four steps: a transmitter that produces an electromagnetic signal, an antenna that radiates the signal into space, a receiver that detects the reflected signal from the objects, and a processor that analyzes the signal characteristics.
- Radar communication uses the radar equation to calculate the received power, which depends on various factors, such as transmitted power, antenna gain, wavelength, target cross-section, distance, and losses.
- Radar communication uses different types of modulation, such as pulse modulation, frequency modulation, phase modulation and continuous wave modulation, to encode information on the transmitted signal.
- Radar communication uses different types of antennas, such as parabolic reflectors, horn antennas, slot antennas and phased arrays, to direct and focus the transmitted and received signals.
- Radar communication uses different types of scanning, such as mechanical scanning, electronic scanning and conical scanning, to change the direction of the antenna beam and cover a desired area.
- Radar communication systems are subject to various challenges, such as clutter, noise, interference, jamming, stealth, multipath, doppler, range ambiguity and resolution.
- Radar communication systems are used for various applications, such as air traffic control, navigation, surveillance, weather forecasting, missile guidance, collision avoidance, remote sensing and imaging.
