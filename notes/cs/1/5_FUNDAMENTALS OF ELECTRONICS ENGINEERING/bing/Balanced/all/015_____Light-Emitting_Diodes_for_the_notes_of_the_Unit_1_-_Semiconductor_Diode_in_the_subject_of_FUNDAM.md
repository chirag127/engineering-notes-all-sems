# Light-Emitting Diodes

- A light-emitting diode (LED) is a type of semiconductor diode that emits light when an electric current passes through it.
- The color of the light depends on the energy gap of the semiconductor material used in the LED. The energy gap is the difference between the highest occupied energy level and the lowest unoccupied energy level of the electrons in the semiconductor.
- The energy gap determines the wavelength and frequency of the photons emitted by the LED. The wavelength and frequency are inversely proportional to the energy gap. The higher the energy gap, the shorter the wavelength and the higher the frequency of the emitted light.
- The common semiconductor materials used in LEDs are gallium arsenide (GaAs), gallium phosphide (GaP), gallium arsenide phosphide (GaAsP), and gallium nitride (GaN). These materials can produce different colors of light, such as red, green, yellow, blue, and white.
- The structure of an LED consists of a p-type and an n-type semiconductor layer sandwiched between two electrodes, called the anode and the cathode. The p-type layer has excess holes, while the n-type layer has excess electrons. The junction between the two layers is called the p-n junction.
- When a forward bias voltage is applied to the LED, the electrons from the n-type layer and the holes from the p-type layer are pushed towards the p-n junction. When they recombine at the junction, they release energy in the form of photons, which are the particles of light.
- The intensity of the light emitted by the LED depends on the current flowing through it. The higher the current, the brighter the light. However, the current should not exceed a certain limit, otherwise the LED may be damaged by overheating.
- The advantages of LEDs are that they are small, durable, energy-efficient, and have a long lifespan. They can also be switched on and off very quickly, and can produce different colors of light without using filters.
- The applications of LEDs are very diverse, such as in indicators, displays, lighting, communication, sensors, and optical devices.
