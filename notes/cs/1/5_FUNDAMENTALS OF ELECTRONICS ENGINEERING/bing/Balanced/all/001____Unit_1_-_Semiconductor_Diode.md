## Unit 1 - Semiconductor Diode

- A semiconductor diode is a device that allows current to flow in one direction, but blocks it in the opposite direction  .
- A semiconductor diode is made of two types of semiconductor materials: p-type and n-type. The p-type has more holes (positive charge carriers) than electrons, while the n-type has more electrons (negative charge carriers) than holes .
- The junction of the p-type and n-type materials is called the pn junction. The anode is the p-side of the junction, and the cathode is the n-side of the junction .
- When the diode is connected to a voltage source, the polarity of the voltage determines the mode of operation of the diode: forward bias or reverse bias  .
- In forward bias, the positive terminal of the voltage source is connected to the anode, and the negative terminal is connected to the cathode. This causes the holes and electrons to move towards the junction, creating a current flow across the diode  .
- In reverse bias, the positive terminal of the voltage source is connected to the cathode, and the negative terminal is connected to the anode. This causes the holes and electrons to move away from the junction, creating a depletion region that blocks the current flow across the diode  .
- The diode has a characteristic curve that shows the relationship between the voltage across the diode and the current through the diode. The curve has two regions: the forward region and the reverse region  .
- In the forward region, the current increases exponentially with the voltage, until it reaches a saturation point. The voltage required to start the current flow is called the forward voltage or the cut-in voltage  .
- In the reverse region, the current is negligible until the voltage reaches a critical value called the breakdown voltage. At this point, the diode conducts a large current in the reverse direction, which can damage the diode  .
- The diode has many applications, such as rectification, switching, clamping, clipping, voltage regulation, and signal modulation  .
- There are different types of diodes, such as Zener diodes, light-emitting diodes, Schottky diodes, tunnel diodes, and photodiodes, each with its own characteristics and functions.
