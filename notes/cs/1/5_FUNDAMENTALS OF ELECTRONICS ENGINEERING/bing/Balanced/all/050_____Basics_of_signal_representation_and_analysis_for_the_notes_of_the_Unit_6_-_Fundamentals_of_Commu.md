# Basics of signal representation and analysis

- A signal is a physical quantity that varies with time, space, or any other independent variable. It can carry information about the state or behavior of a system or phenomenon. Examples of signals are voltage, current, sound, light, temperature, etc.
- Signal representation is the process of describing a signal using mathematical expressions or graphical plots. Signal representation can help in understanding the properties and characteristics of a signal, such as its amplitude, frequency, phase, energy, power, etc.
- Signal analysis is the process of extracting useful information from a signal, such as its spectrum, bandwidth, modulation, noise, distortion, etc. Signal analysis can help in designing, testing, and optimizing communication systems and devices that use signals to transmit or receive data.
- There are two main domains for signal representation and analysis: time domain and frequency domain. In the time domain, the signal is represented as a function of time, showing how the signal varies over time. In the frequency domain, the signal is represented as a function of frequency, showing how the signal is composed of different frequency components.
- The time domain representation of a signal is useful for observing the temporal features of a signal, such as its periodicity, duration, rise time, fall time, etc. The frequency domain representation of a signal is useful for observing the spectral features of a signal, such as its harmonics, bandwidth, power spectrum, etc.
- To convert a signal from the time domain to the frequency domain, or vice versa, mathematical tools such as Fourier transform, Laplace transform, Z-transform, etc. are used. These tools decompose a signal into its frequency components, or synthesize a signal from its frequency components, respectively.
- Some examples of signal representation and analysis are:

  - Plotting a signal in the time domain to observe its waveform and amplitude variations.
  - Plotting a signal in the frequency domain to observe its spectrum and frequency components.
  - Calculating the energy or power of a signal using the time domain or frequency domain representation.
  - Calculating the bandwidth of a signal using the frequency domain representation.
  - Filtering a signal to remove unwanted frequency components using the frequency domain representation.
  - Modulating or demodulating a signal to change its frequency or phase using the frequency domain representation.
  - Sampling or reconstructing a signal to convert it from analog to digital, or vice versa, using the time domain representation.
