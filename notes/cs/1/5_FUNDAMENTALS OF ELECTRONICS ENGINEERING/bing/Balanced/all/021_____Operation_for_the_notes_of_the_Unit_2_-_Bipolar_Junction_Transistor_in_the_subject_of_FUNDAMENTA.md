# Operation of Bipolar Junction Transistor

- A bipolar junction transistor (BJT) is a type of transistor that uses both electron and hole charge carriers.
- A BJT has three terminals: emitter, base and collector. The emitter and collector are made of the same type of semiconductor (either n-type or p-type), while the base is made of the opposite type. The emitter-base junction is forward biased, while the collector-base junction is reverse biased .
- A BJT can operate in three regions: active, saturation and cutoff.
  - Active region: The region in which the transistor operates as an amplifier. The base current controls the collector current, which is proportional to the base-emitter voltage . The collector-emitter voltage is positive for npn transistor and negative for pnp transistor.
  - Saturation region: The region in which the transistor is fully on and operates as a switch. The collector current is equal to the saturation current, which is the maximum current that can flow through the collector. The base current is large enough to saturate the transistor. The collector-emitter voltage is almost zero .
  - Cutoff region: The region in which the transistor is fully off and operates as an open circuit. The base current is zero or negligible, and the collector current is also zero. The collector-emitter voltage is equal to the supply voltage .
- The operation of a BJT can be explained by the movement of charge carriers across the two junctions. In an npn transistor, the emitter injects electrons into the base, which are the majority carriers in the emitter. Some of these electrons recombine with the holes in the base, which are the minority carriers in the base. The remaining electrons cross the reverse-biased collector-base junction and reach the collector, forming the collector current. The base current is the sum of the recombination current and the leakage current, which is the small current that flows through the reverse-biased junction. The ratio of the collector current to the base current is called the current gain, which is a measure of the amplification of the transistor . In a pnp transistor, the operation is similar, but the roles of electrons and holes are reversed.
