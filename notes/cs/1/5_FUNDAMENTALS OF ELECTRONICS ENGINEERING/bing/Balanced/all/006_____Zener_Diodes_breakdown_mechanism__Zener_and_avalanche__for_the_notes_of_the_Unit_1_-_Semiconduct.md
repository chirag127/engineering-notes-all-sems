# Zener Diodes breakdown mechanism (Zener and avalanche)

- A Zener diode is a special type of diode that can operate in the reverse breakdown region without being damaged.
- The reverse breakdown region is the region where the reverse voltage across the diode exceeds a certain threshold, called the breakdown voltage or Zener voltage.
- The breakdown voltage of a Zener diode depends on the doping concentration and the thickness of the depletion region.
- There are two main mechanisms that cause the reverse breakdown in a Zener diode: Zener breakdown and avalanche breakdown.
- Zener breakdown occurs when the electric field in the depletion region is so high that it can break the covalent bonds of the semiconductor atoms and generate electron-hole pairs. This increases the reverse current exponentially.
- Zener breakdown typically occurs in Zener diodes with low breakdown voltages (below 5 V) and high doping concentrations.
- Avalanche breakdown occurs when the electrons in the depletion region gain enough kinetic energy from the electric field to collide with the semiconductor atoms and knock out more electrons. This creates a chain reaction of electron multiplication that also increases the reverse current exponentially.
- Avalanche breakdown typically occurs in Zener diodes with high breakdown voltages (above 5 V) and low doping concentrations.
- Both Zener and avalanche breakdown are non-destructive phenomena, as long as the power dissipation in the diode is kept within the specified limits.
- Zener diodes are widely used as voltage regulators, voltage references, and protection devices in electronic circuits.
