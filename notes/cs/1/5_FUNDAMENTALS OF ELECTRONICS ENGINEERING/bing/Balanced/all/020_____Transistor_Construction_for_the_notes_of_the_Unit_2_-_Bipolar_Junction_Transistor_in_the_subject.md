# Transistor Construction

- A transistor is a three-layer semiconductor device that can amplify or switch electric currents.
- A semiconductor is a material that can conduct electricity under certain conditions, but not under others. Examples of semiconductors are silicon and germanium.
- A transistor is made by sandwiching one type of semiconductor (either P-type or N-type) between two similar other types of semiconductor. The process of sandwiching is called fabrication of transistor.
- P-type semiconductor is a material that has extra holes (the absence of electrons) in its structure. N-type semiconductor is a material that has extra electrons in its structure. These extra holes or electrons are created by adding impurities (a process called doping) to the pure semiconductor.
- The three layers of a transistor are called the emitter, the base, and the collector. The emitter and the collector are the outer layers, and the base is the middle layer. The base is usually very thin compared to the emitter and the collector.
- The emitter and the collector have the same type of semiconductor (either P-type or N-type), and the base has the opposite type of semiconductor. For example, a PNP transistor has a P-type emitter, a N-type base, and a P-type collector. An NPN transistor has a N-type emitter, a P-type base, and a N-type collector.
- The emitter is the layer that supplies the input current to the transistor. The collector is the layer that receives the output current from the transistor. The base is the layer that controls the flow of current between the emitter and the collector.
- The transistor has three terminals, one for each layer. The terminal connected to the emitter is called the emitter terminal (E). The terminal connected to the base is called the base terminal (B). The terminal connected to the collector is called the collector terminal (C).
