### Integrator

- An integrator is an electronic circuit that performs the mathematical operation of integration with respect to time. It is based on an operational amplifier (op-amp) and a feedback capacitor.   
- The basic configuration of an op-amp integrator is shown below:

![Op-amp integrator circuit](https://www.electronics-tutorials.ws/wp-content/uploads/2018/05/opamp-opamp6.gif)

- The input voltage V<sub>in</sub> is applied to the inverting terminal of the op-amp through a resistor R. The output voltage V<sub>out</sub> is fed back to the same terminal through a capacitor C. The non-inverting terminal is grounded.
- The output voltage V<sub>out</sub> is given by the following equation:

![Op-amp integrator output equation](https://www.electronics-tutorials.ws/wp-content/uploads/2018/05/opamp-opamp6a.gif)

- The output voltage is proportional to the integral of the input voltage with respect to time. The constant of integration is determined by the initial condition of the capacitor.
- The integrator can be used to perform various functions, such as generating waveforms, filtering signals, measuring areas, and converting signals from analog to digital.
