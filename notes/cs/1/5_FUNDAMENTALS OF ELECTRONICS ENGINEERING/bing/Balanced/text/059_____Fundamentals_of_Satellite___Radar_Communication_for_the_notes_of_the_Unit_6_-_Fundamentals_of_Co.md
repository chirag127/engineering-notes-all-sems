### Fundamentals of Satellite & Radar Communication

Satellite and radar communication are two types of wireless communication systems that use electromagnetic waves to transmit and receive information. They have different applications, advantages and disadvantages, and design principles.

#### Satellite Communication

- Satellite communication involves four steps:
  - An uplink Earth station or other ground equipment transmits the desired signal to the satellite
  - The satellite amplifies the incoming signal and changes the frequency
  - The satellite transmits the signal back to Earth
  - The ground equipment receives the signal
- Satellites are relay stations in space for the transmission of voice, video and data communications.
- Satellites are ideally suited to meet the global communications requirements of military, government and commercial organizations because they provide economical, scalable and highly reliable transmission services that easily reach multiple sites over large geographic areas.
- Satellites can be classified by their orbit, frequency band, function, and generation.
  - Orbit: geostationary (GEO), medium Earth orbit (MEO), low Earth orbit (LEO), etc.
  - Frequency band: C-band, Ku-band, Ka-band, etc.
  - Function: direct broadcast satellites (DBS), high throughput satellites (HTS), very small aperture terminals (VSAT), etc.
  - Generation: first generation (passive reflectors), second generation (active repeaters), third generation (on-board processing), etc.
- Satellite communication systems have some advantages and disadvantages:
  - Advantages: wide coverage, flexibility, mobility, reliability, security, etc.
  - Disadvantages: propagation delay, attenuation, interference, noise, orbital congestion, etc.

#### Radar Communication

- Radar communication involves three steps:
  - A transmitter that produces an electromagnetic signal which is radiated into space by an antenna
  - When this signal strikes an object, it gets reflected or reradiated in many directions
  - A receiver that detects the echo or reflected signal from the objects
- Radar stands for Radio Detection and Ranging System.
- Radar is basically an electromagnetic system used to detect the location and distance of an object from the point where the radar is placed.
- Radar can be classified by their frequency, waveform, modulation, polarization, and function.
  - Frequency: microwave, millimeter wave, etc.
  - Waveform: continuous wave (CW), pulsed, frequency modulated (FM), etc.
  - Modulation: amplitude, frequency, phase, etc.
  - Polarization: linear, circular, elliptical, etc.
  - Function: surveillance, tracking, navigation, mapping, etc.
- Radar communication systems have some advantages and disadvantages:
  - Advantages: all-weather, long-range, high-resolution, high-accuracy, etc.
  - Disadvantages: high-power, high-cost, complex, vulnerable to jamming, clutter, etc.
