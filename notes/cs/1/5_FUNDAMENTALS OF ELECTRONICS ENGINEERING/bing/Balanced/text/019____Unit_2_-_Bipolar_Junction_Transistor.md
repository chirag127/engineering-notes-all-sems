## Unit 2 - Bipolar Junction Transistor

- A bipolar junction transistor (BJT) is a type of transistor that uses both electrons and holes as charge carriers.
- A transistor is a semiconductor device that can amplify or switch electrical signals.
- A BJT has three terminals: the emitter (E), the base (B), and the collector (C) .
- The emitter is the terminal that supplies the majority charge carriers (electrons for npn and holes for pnp) to the base.
- The base is the terminal that controls the flow of charge carriers from the emitter to the collector.
- The collector is the terminal that collects the charge carriers from the base and delivers them to the external circuit.
- A BJT can operate in three modes: cut-off, active, and saturation.
- In the cut-off mode, the base-emitter junction and the base-collector junction are both reverse biased, and no current flows through the transistor.
- In the active mode, the base-emitter junction is forward biased and the base-collector junction is reverse biased, and a small base current controls a large collector current.
- In the saturation mode, the base-emitter junction and the base-collector junction are both forward biased, and the collector current reaches its maximum value.
- A BJT can be used as a switch, an amplifier, or an oscillator, depending on the biasing and the circuit configuration.
