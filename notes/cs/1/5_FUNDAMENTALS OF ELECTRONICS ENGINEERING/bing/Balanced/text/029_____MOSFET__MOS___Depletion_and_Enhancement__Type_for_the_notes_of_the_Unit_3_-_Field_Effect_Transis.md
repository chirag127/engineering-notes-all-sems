### MOSFET (MOS) (Depletion and Enhancement) Type

- MOSFET stands for Metal-Oxide-Semiconductor Field-Effect Transistor. It is a type of FET that uses an electric field to control the conductivity of a channel between the source and drain terminals.
- MOSFETs can be classified into two major types: depletion mode and enhancement mode, depending on whether the channel is present or absent at zero gate-source voltage.
- Depletion mode MOSFETs have a channel that is fabricated during manufacturing. They conduct current between the source and drain terminals even when the gate-source voltage is zero. Applying a reverse voltage to the gate reduces the channel width and the current flow. Applying a forward voltage increases the channel width and the current flow.
- Enhancement mode MOSFETs have no channel during manufacturing. They do not conduct current when the gate-source voltage is zero. Applying a forward voltage to the gate creates a channel and allows current to flow. Applying a reverse voltage does not affect the channel or the current flow.
- Both depletion and enhancement mode MOSFETs can be further divided into n-channel and p-channel types, depending on whether the channel is made of n-type or p-type semiconductor material. For n-channel MOSFETs, the substrate is p-type and the gate voltage is positive with respect to the source. For p-channel MOSFETs, the substrate is n-type and the gate voltage is negative with respect to the source.
- MOSFETs have many applications in digital and analog circuits, such as switches, amplifiers, converters, inverters, oscillators, and logic gates. They have advantages over other types of transistors, such as high input impedance, low power consumption, high switching speed, and compatibility with integrated circuits.
