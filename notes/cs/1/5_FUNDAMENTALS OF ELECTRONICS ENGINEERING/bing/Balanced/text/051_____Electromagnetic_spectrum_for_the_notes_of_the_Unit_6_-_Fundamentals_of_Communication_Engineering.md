### Electromagnetic spectrum

- The electromagnetic spectrum is the range of all types of electromagnetic radiation, which are energy waves that travel and spread out as they go  .
- Electromagnetic radiation can be classified by its frequency, wavelength, or photon energy, which are inversely proportional to each other .
- The electromagnetic spectrum covers electromagnetic waves with frequencies ranging from below one hertz to above 10^25^ hertz, corresponding to wavelengths from thousands of kilometers down to a fraction of the size of an atomic nucleus.
- The electromagnetic spectrum is divided into different regions based on the properties and applications of the electromagnetic waves. The regions are, from lowest to highest frequency: radio waves, microwaves, infrared, visible light, ultraviolet, X-rays, and gamma rays  .
- Radio waves have the lowest frequency and longest wavelength, and are used for communication, broadcasting, and navigation  .
- Microwaves have higher frequency and shorter wavelength than radio waves, and are used for heating, radar, and satellite communication  .
- Infrared waves have higher frequency and shorter wavelength than microwaves, and are emitted by warm objects. They are used for thermal imaging, remote sensing, and night vision  .
- Visible light waves have higher frequency and shorter wavelength than infrared waves, and are the only electromagnetic waves that humans can see. They are used for vision, photography, and illumination  .
- Ultraviolet waves have higher frequency and shorter wavelength than visible light waves, and are emitted by the sun and other hot objects. They are used for sterilization, fluorescence, and sun tanning, but can also cause skin damage and cancer  .
- X-rays have higher frequency and shorter wavelength than ultraviolet waves, and are produced by high-energy processes such as atomic collisions and electron acceleration. They are used for medical imaging, security scanning, and crystallography, but can also cause tissue damage and mutations  .
- Gamma rays have the highest frequency and shortest wavelength of all electromagnetic waves, and are generated by nuclear reactions and cosmic events. They are used for nuclear medicine, astronomy, and radiation therapy, but can also be lethal to living organisms  .
