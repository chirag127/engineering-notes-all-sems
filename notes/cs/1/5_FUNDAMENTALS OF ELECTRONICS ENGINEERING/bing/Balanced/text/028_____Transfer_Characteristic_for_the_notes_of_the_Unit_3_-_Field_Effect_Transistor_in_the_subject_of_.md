### Transfer Characteristic of FET

- The transfer characteristic of a field-effect transistor (FET) is a curve that shows the relationship between the input voltage (V<sub>GS</sub>) and the output current (I<sub>D</sub>) for a given value of the drain-source voltage (V<sub>DS</sub>).
- The transfer characteristic can be derived from the drain characteristic, which is a set of curves that show the variation of I<sub>D</sub> with V<sub>DS</sub> for different values of V<sub>GS</sub>.
- The transfer characteristic can be used to determine the operating point, the transconductance, and the amplification factor of the FET.
- The transfer characteristic can be expressed by a mathematical equation, such as the Shockley equation for the junction FET (JFET) or the square-law equation for the metal-oxide-semiconductor FET (MOSFET).
- The transfer characteristic can be plotted on a linear or a logarithmic scale, depending on the type and the region of operation of the FET.
- The transfer characteristic can be normalized by using the ratios I<sub>D</sub>/I<sub>DSS</sub> and V<sub>GS</sub>/V<sub>P</sub>, where I<sub>DSS</sub> is the saturation current and V<sub>P</sub> is the pinch-off voltage of the FET. This results in a universal transfer characteristic that can be applied to any FET with the same geometry and material.
- The transfer characteristic can be affected by the temperature, the channel length modulation, and the body effect of the FET.
