### Need of modulation and typical applications

Modulation is the process of changing the characteristics of a carrier wave (such as amplitude, frequency or phase) according to the information contained in a message signal. Modulation is required for various reasons, such as:

- To increase the range and quality of communication: Modulation enables the transmission of low-frequency message signals over long distances without significant loss or distortion. Modulation also improves the signal-to-noise ratio at the receiver, which enhances the quality of communication.
- To reduce the size and cost of the antenna: Modulation allows the use of high-frequency carrier waves, which have shorter wavelengths and require smaller antennas. A smaller antenna reduces the cost and complexity of the transmitter and receiver.
- To avoid interference and multiplexing: Modulation prevents the mixing of message signals from different sources by assigning different carrier frequencies to each source. This enables the simultaneous transmission and reception of multiple signals over a common channel, which is known as frequency division multiplexing (FDM).
- To facilitate the conversion of signals: Modulation enables the inter-conversion of signals from one form to another, such as digital to analog or vice versa. This is useful for transmitting digital signals over analog baseband or for processing analog signals using digital techniques.

Some of the typical applications of modulation are:

- Radio and television broadcasting: Modulation is used to transmit audio and video signals over radio waves. Different types of modulation, such as amplitude modulation (AM), frequency modulation (FM) or phase modulation (PM), are used for different purposes and quality requirements.
- Mobile and wireless communication: Modulation is used to transmit voice and data signals over cellular networks, Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, satellite communication, etc. Different types of modulation, such as quadrature amplitude modulation (QAM), phase shift keying (PSK) or frequency shift keying (FSK), are used for different data rates and reliability requirements.
- Optical communication: Modulation is used to transmit information over optical fibers or free space using light waves. Different types of modulation, such as intensity modulation, phase modulation or polarization modulation, are used for different bandwidth and distance requirements.
