### Introduction for the notes of the Unit 4 - Operational Amplifiers in the subject of FUNDAMENTALS OF ELECTRONICS ENGINEERING

- An operational amplifier (op-amp) is a high-gain, direct-coupled, differential-input amplifier that can perform various linear and nonlinear operations on input signals.
- An op-amp has two input terminals: the inverting input (-) and the non-inverting input (+), and one output terminal. It also has two power supply terminals: the positive supply (V+) and the negative supply (V-).
- An op-amp can be modeled as a voltage-controlled voltage source (VCVS) with a very large open-loop voltage gain (A), a very large input resistance (Ri), and a very small output resistance (Ro).
- The ideal op-amp has the following characteristics:
  - Infinite open-loop voltage gain (A = ∞)
  - Infinite input resistance (Ri = ∞)
  - Zero output resistance (Ro = 0)
  - Zero input offset voltage (Vos = 0)
  - Infinite bandwidth (BW = ∞)
  - Zero noise
- The practical op-amp has some limitations and deviations from the ideal op-amp, such as finite gain, finite input and output resistances, input bias currents, input offset voltage and current, finite bandwidth, slew rate, noise, etc.
- An op-amp can be used in various configurations to perform different functions, such as amplification, buffering, summing, subtracting, integrating, differentiating, filtering, etc. These configurations are called op-amp circuits or op-amp applications.
- An op-amp circuit consists of an op-amp and some external components, such as resistors, capacitors, diodes, etc., connected to the input and output terminals of the op-amp. The external components determine the closed-loop gain and the frequency response of the op-amp circuit.
- The most common op-amp circuits are the inverting amplifier, the non-inverting amplifier, the voltage follower, the summing amplifier, the difference amplifier, the integrator, and the differentiator. These circuits can be analyzed using the ideal op-amp assumptions and the basic circuit laws, such as Kirchhoff's current and voltage laws, Ohm's law, etc.
