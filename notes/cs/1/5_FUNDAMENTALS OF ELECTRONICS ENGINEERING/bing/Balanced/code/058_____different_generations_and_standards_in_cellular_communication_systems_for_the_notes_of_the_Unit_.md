### Different Generations and Standards in Cellular Communication Systems

- Cellular communication systems are wireless networks that use radio waves to transmit voice and data signals over a geographic area.
- Cellular communication systems have evolved through several generations, each with different features, capabilities, and standards.
- The main characteristics of each generation are summarized below:

#### 1G (First Generation Technology)

- 1G refers to the first generation of wireless cellular technology, which was introduced in the late 1970s and early 1980s .
- 1G systems were voice-oriented analog cellular and cordless telephones.
- 1G systems used frequency division multiple access (FDMA) to allocate radio channels to users.
- 1G systems had low capacity, poor voice quality, and no security or encryption.
- Some of the 1G standards were Advanced Mobile Phone System (AMPS), Nordic Mobile Telephone (NMT), and Total Access Communication System (TACS) .

#### 2G (Second Generation Technology)

- 2G refers to the second generation of wireless cellular technology, which was launched in the early 1990s .
- 2G systems were voice-oriented digital cellular and personal communication service (PCS) systems and data-oriented wireless wide area networks (WANs) and local area networks (LANs).
- 2G systems used time division multiple access (TDMA) or code division multiple access (CDMA) to multiplex users on radio channels.
- 2G systems had higher capacity, better voice quality, and more security and encryption than 1G systems.
- Some of the 2G standards were Global System for Mobile Communications (GSM), Interim Standard 95 (IS-95), and Personal Digital Cellular (PDC) .

#### 3G (Third Generation Technology)

- 3G refers to the third generation of wireless cellular technology, which was launched in the early 2000s .
- 3G systems were multimedia-oriented cellular and PCS systems that supported voice, data, and video services.
- 3G systems used wideband CDMA (WCDMA) or CDMA2000 to provide higher data rates and more efficient spectrum utilization than 2G systems.
- 3G systems had improved capacity, quality, and security, and enabled applications such as mobile internet, video streaming, and video conferencing.
- Some of the 3G standards were Universal Mobile Telecommunications System (UMTS), CDMA2000, and Enhanced Data Rates for GSM Evolution (EDGE) .

#### 4G (Fourth Generation Technology)

- 4G refers to the fourth generation of wireless cellular technology, which was launched in the late 2000s and early 2010s .
- 4G systems were broadband cellular and PCS systems that supported voice, data, and multimedia services over IP-based networks.
- 4G systems used orthogonal frequency division multiple access (OFDMA) or single carrier FDMA (SC-FDMA) to achieve higher data rates and lower latency than 3G systems.
- 4G systems had enhanced capacity, quality, and security, and enabled applications such as mobile web, online gaming, and high-definition video.
- Some of the 4G standards were Long Term Evolution (LTE), WiMAX, and LTE Advanced .

#### 5G (Fifth Generation Technology)

- 5G refers to the fifth generation of wireless cellular technology, which was launched in the late 2010s and early 2020s .
- 5G systems are ultra-broadband cellular and PCS systems that support voice, data, and multimedia services over heterogeneous networks.
- 5G systems use new radio (NR) technology to achieve higher data rates, lower latency, and more reliability than 4G systems.
