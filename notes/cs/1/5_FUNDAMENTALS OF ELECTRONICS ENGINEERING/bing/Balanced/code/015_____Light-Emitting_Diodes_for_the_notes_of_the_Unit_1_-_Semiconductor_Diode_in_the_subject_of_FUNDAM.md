### Light-Emitting Diodes

- A light-emitting diode (LED) is a type of semiconductor diode that emits light when a current passes through it.
- The color of the light depends on the energy gap of the semiconductor material used in the LED. The energy gap is the difference between the highest occupied energy level and the lowest unoccupied energy level of the electrons in the semiconductor.
- When an electron jumps from a higher energy level to a lower energy level, it releases energy in the form of a photon, which is a particle of light. The energy of the photon is equal to the energy gap of the semiconductor.
- The wavelength of the photon, and hence the color of the light, is inversely proportional to the energy of the photon. Therefore, a larger energy gap corresponds to a shorter wavelength and a higher frequency of light, and vice versa.
- Some common semiconductor materials used in LEDs and their corresponding colors are:

| Gallium arsenide phosphide (GaAsP) | 1.9 | 650 | Red |
| Aluminum gallium arsenide (AlGaAs) | 1.8 | 660 | Red |
| Gallium phosphide (GaP) | 2.26 | 550 | Green |
| Gallium nitride (GaN) | 3.4 | 365 | Ultraviolet |
| Indium gallium nitride (InGaN) | 2.9 | 430 | Blue |
| Aluminum gallium indium phosphide (AlGaInP) | 2.1 | 590 | Yellow |

- LEDs have many advantages over conventional light sources, such as:

  - They are more energy-efficient, as they convert most of the electrical energy into light and produce less heat.
  - They have a longer lifespan, as they are more resistant to shock, vibration, and temperature changes.
  - They have a faster response time, as they can be switched on and off in nanoseconds.
  - They have a smaller size and weight, as they do not require a filament, a glass envelope, or a gas filling.
  - They have a wider range of colors and brightness, as they can be combined and controlled in different ways.

- LEDs have many applications in various fields, such as:

  - Indicators and displays, such as traffic lights, digital clocks, calculators, and television screens.
  - Lighting, such as flashlights, lamps, street lights, and decorative lights.
  - Communication, such as remote controls, fiber optics, and optical data storage.
  - Medicine, such as phototherapy, endoscopy, and pulse oximetry.
  - Agriculture, such as plant growth, pest control, and food preservation.
