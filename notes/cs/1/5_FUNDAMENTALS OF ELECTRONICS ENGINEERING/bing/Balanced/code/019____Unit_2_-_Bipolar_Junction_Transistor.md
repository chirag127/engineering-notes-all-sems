## Unit 2 - Bipolar Junction Transistor

A bipolar junction transistor (BJT) is a type of semiconductor device that can amplify or switch electrical signals. It consists of three regions of doped silicon: the emitter, the base, and the collector. The emitter and the collector are of the same type (either n-type or p-type), while the base is of the opposite type. The base region is very thin and lightly doped compared to the emitter and the collector.

The main characteristics of a BJT are:

- It has three terminals: the emitter (E), the base (B), and the collector (C).
- It has two types: npn and pnp, depending on the arrangement of the n-type and p-type regions.
- It has two modes of operation: active and saturation, depending on the biasing of the base-emitter and base-collector junctions.
- It has three regions of operation: cut-off, linear, and saturation, depending on the input and output voltages and currents.
- It has a current gain factor, called beta (β), which is the ratio of the collector current to the base current.
- It has a voltage gain factor, called alpha (α), which is the ratio of the collector-emitter voltage to the base-emitter voltage.

Some of the applications of a BJT are:

- As an amplifier, it can increase the strength of a weak signal by using a small base current to control a large collector current.
- As a switch, it can turn on or off a circuit by using a base voltage to control the collector-emitter voltage.
- As an oscillator, it can generate a periodic signal by using a feedback loop to create a positive or negative resistance.
- As a logic gate, it can perform basic operations such as AND, OR, and NOT by using different combinations of input and output voltages.
