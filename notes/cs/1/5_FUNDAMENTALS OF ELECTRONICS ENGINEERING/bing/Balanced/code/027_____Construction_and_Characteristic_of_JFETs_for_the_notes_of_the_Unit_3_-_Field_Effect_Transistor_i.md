### Construction and Characteristic of JFETs

- A JFET (Junction Field Effect Transistor) is a type of field effect transistor that uses a reverse-biased p-n junction as the gate.
- A JFET has three terminals: source, drain and gate. The source and drain are connected to the two ends of a channel of either n-type or p-type semiconductor material. The gate is a p-n junction that surrounds the channel and is reverse-biased by an external voltage.
- The channel is the main current-carrying path of the JFET. The gate voltage controls the width of the channel and thus the current flow between the source and drain.
- A JFET can be either n-channel or p-channel, depending on the type of the channel and the gate. An n-channel JFET has an n-type channel and a p-type gate, while a p-channel JFET has a p-type channel and an n-type gate.

- The characteristic curves of a JFET are the plots of the drain current (I_D) versus the drain-source voltage (V_DS) for different values of the gate-source voltage (V_GS).
- The characteristic curves of a JFET show the following regions of operation: ohmic, saturation and breakdown.
- In the ohmic region, the JFET behaves like a linear resistor and the drain current is proportional to the drain-source voltage. The ohmic region is also called the triode region or the linear region.
- In the saturation region, the JFET behaves like a constant current source and the drain current is independent of the drain-source voltage. The saturation region is also called the pinch-off region or the active region.
- In the breakdown region, the JFET is damaged by a high drain-source voltage and the drain current increases rapidly. The breakdown region is also called the avalanche region or the zener region.

- The characteristic curves of a p-channel JFET are similar to those of an n-channel JFET, except that the polarities of the voltages and currents are reversed.
- The characteristic parameters of a JFET are the transconductance (g_m), the drain resistance (r_D), the pinch-off voltage (V_P) and the cut-off voltage (V_GS(off)).
- The transconductance (g_m) is a measure of the sensitivity of the drain current to the gate-source voltage. It is defined as the ratio of the change in the drain current to the change in the gate-source voltage at a constant drain-source voltage. The unit of transconductance is siemens (S) or mho (℧).
- The drain resistance (r_D) is a measure of the resistance of the channel at a constant gate-source voltage. It is defined as the ratio of the change in the drain-source voltage to the change in the drain current at a constant gate-source voltage. The unit of drain resistance is ohm (Ω).
- The pinch-off voltage (V_P) is the minimum drain-source voltage required to reduce the channel width to zero and cut off the drain current. It is also the maximum gate-source voltage that can be applied without causing breakdown. The pinch-off voltage is negative for n-channel JFETs and positive for p-channel JFETs. The unit of pinch-off voltage is volt (V).
- The cut-off voltage (V_GS(off)) is the gate-source voltage at which the drain current becomes zero. It is also the minimum gate-source voltage required to turn on the JFET. The cut-off voltage is negative for n-channel JFETs and positive for p-channel JFETs. The unit of cut-off voltage is volt (V).
