## Unit 5 - Digital Electronics

Digital electronics is the branch of electronics that deals with digital signals, logic gates, binary numbers, and circuits that perform arithmetic and logic operations.

Some of the topics covered in this unit are:

- **Digital signals**: These are signals that have only two discrete values, such as 0 and 1, or low and high. Digital signals are used to represent binary data and to communicate between digital devices.
- **Logic gates**: These are electronic devices that perform basic logic operations, such as AND, OR, NOT, NAND, NOR, XOR, and XNOR. Logic gates have one or more inputs and one output, and they produce a binary output based on the binary inputs.
- **Binary numbers**: These are numbers that use only two digits, 0 and 1, to represent any value. Binary numbers are used to store and manipulate data in digital systems. Binary numbers can be converted to and from other number systems, such as decimal, octal, and hexadecimal.
- **Binary arithmetic**: This is the arithmetic that is performed on binary numbers, such as addition, subtraction, multiplication, and division. Binary arithmetic follows some rules and methods that are different from the arithmetic on decimal numbers.
- **Binary codes**: These are codes that use binary digits to represent information, such as characters, symbols, or instructions. Some common binary codes are ASCII, BCD, Gray code, and Hamming code.
- **Logic circuits**: These are circuits that are composed of logic gates and other components, such as switches, LEDs, and registers. Logic circuits can perform various functions, such as counting, encoding, decoding, multiplexing, demultiplexing, and memory.
- **Boolean algebra**: This is the algebra that is used to simplify and analyze logic expressions and logic circuits. Boolean algebra follows some laws and rules that are different from the algebra on real numbers. Boolean algebra can be used to find the equivalent, canonical, and minimal forms of logic expressions and circuits.
