### Basics of signal representation and analysis

- A signal is a physical variable that carries information, such as voltage, current, position, velocity, etc. 
- A signal can be represented in two domains: time domain and frequency domain. 
- In the time domain, the signal is represented as a function of time, showing how the signal varies over time. 
- In the frequency domain, the signal is represented as a function of frequency, showing how the signal is composed of different frequency components. 
- The frequency domain representation is useful for signal processing tasks such as filtering, amplifying, and mixing, as well as for analyzing the spectral properties of the signal. 
- To convert a signal from the time domain to the frequency domain, or vice versa, mathematical tools such as Fourier transform, Laplace transform, or Z-transform are used. 
- A signal can also be represented as a superposition of positive and negative frequency components, which is called the analytic signal representation. 
- The analytic signal representation can be used to extract the amplitude and phase information of the signal, as well as to perform operations such as modulation and demodulation. 
- Some examples of signals that are commonly used in communication engineering are sinusoidal signals, periodic signals, aperiodic signals, deterministic signals, random signals, analog signals, and digital signals.
