### Special Purpose Two Terminal Devices

Two terminal devices are electronic components that have only two terminals, such as anode and cathode, and allow current to flow only in one direction. The most common two terminal device is the diode, which is a semiconductor device that has a p-n junction and a non-linear current-voltage characteristic.

Some diodes are designed for special purposes, such as:

- **Tunnel diode**: A diode that has a very thin p-n junction and exhibits negative resistance at certain voltage ranges. This means that the current decreases as the voltage increases, which allows the diode to operate as an oscillator or amplifier at high frequencies.
- **Photo diode**: A diode that generates a current when exposed to light. The current is proportional to the intensity of the light, which makes the diode useful for sensing or converting light signals.
- **Varactor diode**: A diode that has a variable capacitance depending on the applied voltage. The capacitance is inversely proportional to the width of the depletion region in the p-n junction, which can be controlled by the voltage. This diode can be used as a voltage-controlled capacitor or a frequency tuner in radio circuits.
- **Schottky diode**: A diode that has a metal-semiconductor junction instead of a p-n junction. This diode has a lower forward voltage drop and a faster switching speed than a normal diode, which makes it suitable for high-speed or low-power applications.
- **Light emitting diode (LED)**: A diode that emits light when a forward current passes through it. The color of the light depends on the band gap of the semiconductor material, which can be varied by doping or using different materials. LEDs are widely used as indicators, displays, or sources of illumination.
