### Different generations and standards in cellular communication systems

- Cellular communication systems are wireless networks that use radio waves to transmit voice and data signals over a large area.
- Cellular communication systems have evolved through different generations, each with its own characteristics, advantages, and limitations.
- The following are the main features of each generation of cellular communication systems:

#### 1G (First Generation Technology)

- 1G refers to the first generation of wireless cellular technology, which was introduced in the late 1970s and early 1980s.
- 1G systems were voice-oriented analog cellular and cordless telephones.
- 1G systems used frequency division multiple access (FDMA) to allocate different frequency channels to different users.
- 1G systems had low capacity, poor voice quality, and no security.
- 1G systems used different standards in different regions, such as AMPS (Advanced Mobile Phone System) in North America, TACS (Total Access Communication System) in Europe, and NMT (Nordic Mobile Telephone) in Scandinavia.

#### 2G (Second Generation Cellular Network)

- 2G refers to the second generation of wireless cellular technology, which was launched in the early 1990s.
- 2G systems were voice-oriented digital cellular and PCS (Personal Communication Service) systems and data-oriented wireless WANs (Wide Area Networks) and LANs (Local Area Networks).
- 2G systems used time division multiple access (TDMA) or code division multiple access (CDMA) to multiplex multiple users on the same frequency channel.
- 2G systems had higher capacity, better voice quality, and more security than 1G systems.
- 2G systems used different standards in different regions, such as GSM (Global System for Mobile Communications) in Europe, IS-95 (Interim Standard 95) in North America, and PDC (Personal Digital Cellular) in Japan .

#### 3G (Third Generation Technology)

- 3G refers to the third generation of wireless cellular technology, which was launched in the early 2000s.
- 3G systems were designed to provide high-speed data and multimedia services, such as video calling, mobile internet, and online gaming, in addition to voice services.
- 3G systems used wideband CDMA (W-CDMA) or CDMA2000 to achieve higher data rates and spectral efficiency than 2G systems.
- 3G systems had higher capacity, better voice quality, and more security than 2G systems.
- 3G systems used a common standard, called IMT-2000 (International Mobile Telecommunications-2000), which was based on the ITU (International Telecommunication Union) recommendations .

#### 4G (Fourth Generation Technology)

- 4G refers to the fourth generation of wireless cellular technology, which was launched in the late 2000s and early 2010s.
- 4G systems were designed to provide ultra-high-speed data and multimedia services, such as video streaming, online gaming, and cloud computing, in addition to voice services.
- 4G systems used orthogonal frequency division multiple access (OFDMA) or single carrier FDMA (SC-FDMA) to achieve higher data rates and spectral efficiency than 3G systems.
- 4G systems had higher capacity, better voice quality, and more security than 3G systems.
- 4G systems used a common standard, called LTE (Long Term Evolution), which was based on the 3GPP (Third Generation Partnership Project) specifications .
