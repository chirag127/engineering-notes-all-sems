## Unit 4 - Operational Amplifiers

Unit 4 - Operational Amplifiers:
An operational amplifier (op-amp) is a high-gain, direct-coupled, differential-input, voltage-output amplifier that can be used to perform a variety of linear and non-linear operations on electrical signals. It is a type of integrated circuit that consists of multiple transistors, resistors, and capacitors.

Working Principle:
An op-amp amplifies the difference between its two inputs, providing a high voltage gain and a low output impedance. The inputs are differential, meaning that the op-amp amplifies the difference between the two inputs, rather than the absolute voltage levels. The output of the op-amp is connected to a feedback loop that controls the gain and stability of the amplifier.

Applications:
- Amplifiers: Used to amplify weak signals.
- Filters: Used to remove unwanted frequencies from signals.
- Comparators: Used to compare two signals and generate an output based on the comparison.
- Integrators and Differentiators: Used to perform mathematical operations on signals, such as integration and differentiation.
