### Half and Full Wave rectification for the notes of the Unit 1 - Semiconductor Diode in the subject of FUNDAMENTALS OF ELECTRONICS ENGINEERING

Half-Wave Rectification: 
- A half-wave rectifier is a type of rectifier that only allows one half of the input waveform to pass, blocking the other half. 
- It is achieved by using a p-n junction diode, which acts as a switch that only conducts current in one direction. 
- The diode is connected in series with the load and the AC input is applied to the diode-load combination. 
- During the positive half cycle, the diode is forward-biased and conducts, allowing current to flow through the load. 
- During the negative half cycle, the diode is reverse-biased and blocks, preventing current from flowing through the load. 

Full-Wave Rectification: 
- A full-wave rectifier is a type of rectifier that allows both half cycles of the input waveform to pass, but in opposite directions. 
- It is achieved by using two diodes, one for each half cycle of the input waveform. 
- The diodes are connected in a bridge configuration and the AC input is applied to the diode bridge. 
- During the positive half cycle, one diode is forward-biased and conducts, while the other is reverse-biased and blocks. 
- During the negative half cycle, the conducting diode switches and the other diode conducts. 
- The result is a pulsed DC output that is more efficient than the output of a half-wave rectifier.
