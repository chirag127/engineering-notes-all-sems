V-I Characteristics of Semiconductor Diodes:

In Fundamentals of Electronics Engineering, Unit 1 on Semiconductor Diodes, the V-I characteristics of diodes are an important concept to understand. The V-I characteristics of a diode describe the relationship between the voltage across the diode and the current flowing through it.

1. Forward-Bias Characteristics: When a diode is forward-biased, the voltage across the diode is positive with respect to the cathode. The forward-bias V-I characteristics of a diode show the relationship between the forward voltage and the forward current. The forward voltage is typically in the range of 0.7V to 0.8V for silicon diodes.

2. Reverse-Bias Characteristics: When a diode is reverse-biased, the voltage across the diode is negative with respect to the cathode. The reverse-bias V-I characteristics of a diode show the relationship between the reverse voltage and the reverse current. The reverse current is typically very small, in the range of microamperes, and is due to the minority carriers in the diode.

Applications of V-I Characteristics:

1. Rectification: Diodes are commonly used in rectification circuits to convert AC signals into DC signals. The V-I characteristics of diodes are used to determine the voltage and current waveforms in rectification circuits.

2. Clipping and Clamping Circuits: Diodes are also used in clipping and clamping circuits to limit the voltage or current in a circuit. The V-I characteristics of diodes are used to determine the voltage and current waveforms in clipping and clamping circuits.

3. Switching Circuits: Diodes are used in switching circuits as electronic switches. The V-I characteristics of diodes are used to determine the voltage and current waveforms in switching circuits.

In conclusion, the V-I characteristics of semiconductor diodes describe the relationship between the voltage across the diode and the current flowing through it. The V-I characteristics are used to determine the voltage and current waveforms in rectification circuits, clipping and clamping circuits, and switching circuits.
