#### Course Outcome (CO) Bloom’s Level

Course Outcome (CO) refers to the expected knowledge, skills, and attitudes that students should possess upon completion of a specific course. These outcomes are used to assess the effectiveness of the course in achieving its intended goals.

Bloom’s Taxonomy is a framework for categorizing educational goals and objectives into different levels of complexity and specificity. It is commonly used to design assessments and learning activities that align with the desired course outcomes.

The levels of Bloom’s Taxonomy, in ascending order of complexity, are:

1. Remembering: recalling previously learned information.
2. Understanding: comprehending the meaning of the information.
3. Applying: using the information in a new situation.
4. Analyzing: breaking down the information into parts to understand its structure.
5. Evaluating: making judgments about the value of the information.
6. Creating: combining information to form a new whole.

By mapping Course Outcomes to the levels of Bloom’s Taxonomy, educators can ensure that their assessments and learning activities are appropriately challenging and aligned with the desired learning outcomes. This can help students achieve a deeper understanding of the course material and develop the skills and knowledge necessary for success in their field of study.
