#### Course Outcome (CO) Bloom’s Level

Course outcomes (COs) are statements that describe the knowledge, skills, and abilities that students should possess and can demonstrate upon completion of a course. COs are typically written at the course level and are used to guide the development of the course and its assessments.

Bloom’s Taxonomy is a framework for categorizing educational goals and objectives into different levels of complexity and specificity. The levels of Bloom’s Taxonomy, from lowest to highest, are: Remembering, Understanding, Applying, Analyzing, Evaluating, and Creating.

The Bloom’s level of a CO refers to the level of cognitive complexity that the CO is intended to address. For example, a CO that requires students to “recall the steps of the scientific method” would be at the Remembering level of Bloom’s Taxonomy, while a CO that requires students to “design an experiment to test a hypothesis” would be at the Creating level.

In summary:
- Course outcomes (COs) describe the knowledge, skills, and abilities that students should possess upon completion of a course.
- Bloom’s Taxonomy is a framework for categorizing educational goals and objectives.
- The Bloom’s level of a CO refers to the level of cognitive complexity that the CO is intended to address.
