##### 9. To study running and speed reversal of a three phase induction motor and record speed in both directions.

A three-phase induction motor is an AC motor that operates on the principle of electromagnetic induction. The motor consists of a stator, which is a stationary part, and a rotor, which is a rotating part. The stator has a three-phase winding, and when a three-phase AC supply is given to it, a rotating magnetic field is produced. This rotating magnetic field induces an electromotive force in the rotor, which causes the rotor to rotate.

The speed of a three-phase induction motor can be controlled by varying the frequency of the AC supply given to the stator. The speed of the motor is directly proportional to the frequency of the AC supply. Therefore, by increasing the frequency of the AC supply, the speed of the motor can be increased, and by decreasing the frequency of the AC supply, the speed of the motor can be decreased.

The direction of rotation of a three-phase induction motor can be reversed by interchanging any two of the three-phase supply connections to the stator. This changes the direction of the rotating magnetic field, which in turn changes the direction of rotation of the rotor.

To study the running and speed reversal of a three-phase induction motor, the following steps can be followed:

1. Connect the three-phase induction motor to a variable frequency drive (VFD).
2. Set the frequency of the VFD to a desired value and observe the speed of the motor.
3. Increase or decrease the frequency of the VFD to change the speed of the motor and record the speed at different frequencies.
4. Interchange any two of the three-phase supply connections to the stator to reverse the direction of rotation of the motor.
5. Observe and record the speed of the motor in the reverse direction at different frequencies.

By following these steps, the running and speed reversal of a three-phase induction motor can be studied and the speed of the motor can be recorded in both directions.
