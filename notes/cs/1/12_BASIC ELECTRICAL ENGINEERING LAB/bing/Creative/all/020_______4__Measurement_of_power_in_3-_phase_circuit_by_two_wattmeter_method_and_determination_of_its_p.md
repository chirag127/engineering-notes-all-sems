# 4. Measurement of power in 3- phase circuit by two wattmeter method and determination of its power factor for star as well as delta connected load.

- The two wattmeter method is a technique for measuring the total power in a three-phase circuit using two wattmeters.
- The current coils of the two wattmeters are connected in series with any two line conductors, and the potential coils of each wattmeter are connected to the third line conductor .
- The total power is given by the sum of the two wattmeter readings: P = P1 + P2
- The power factor is given by the ratio of the total power to the apparent power: PF = P / (sqrt(3) * V * I), where V is the line voltage and I is the line current.
- For a balanced three-phase load, the power factor can also be calculated from the difference of the two wattmeter readings: PF = cos(phi) = (P1 + P2) / (sqrt(3) * (P1 - P2))
- For a star-connected load, the line voltage is equal to the phase voltage times sqrt(3), and the line current is equal to the phase current: V = sqrt(3) * Vp, I = Ip
- For a delta-connected load, the line voltage is equal to the phase voltage, and the line current is equal to the phase current times sqrt(3): V = Vp, I = sqrt(3) * Ip
- The two wattmeter method can be used for both balanced and unbalanced loads, but the power factor formula is only valid for balanced loads  .
