- The efficiency of a single phase transformer is defined as the ratio of output power to input power, expressed as a percentage .
- The input and output power are measured in the same unit, either in kW or W.
- The efficiency of a single phase transformer is given by the formula :

  $$\eta = \frac{P_o}{P_i} \times 100 \%$$

  where $\eta$ is the efficiency, $P_o$ is the output power, and $P_i$ is the input power.

- The output power of a single phase transformer is equal to the product of the secondary voltage, the secondary current, and the power factor of the load :

  $$P_o = V_s I_s \cos \phi$$

  where $V_s$ is the secondary voltage, $I_s$ is the secondary current, and $\cos \phi$ is the power factor of the load.

- The input power of a single phase transformer is equal to the sum of the output power and the losses in the transformer :

  $$P_i = P_o + P_L$$

  where $P_L$ is the total losses in the transformer.

- The losses in a single phase transformer consist of two types: core losses and copper losses   .

- Core losses are caused by the alternating magnetic flux in the core, which induces eddy currents and hysteresis in the core material   .

  - Eddy currents are circular currents that flow in the core due to the changing magnetic flux. They produce heat and reduce the efficiency of the transformer   .
  - Hysteresis is the phenomenon of the magnetic domains in the core material aligning and realigning with the magnetic flux. This process also produces heat and reduces the efficiency of the transformer   .

- Core losses are constant and independent of the load. They can be reduced by using a laminated core, which reduces the eddy currents, and by using a high permeability and low hysteresis material, such as silicon steel, for the core   .

- Copper losses are caused by the resistance of the primary and secondary windings, which produce heat when current flows through them   .

  - Copper losses are proportional to the square of the current and depend on the load. They can be reduced by using thicker wires, which have lower resistance, for the windings   .

- Copper losses are given by the formula   :

  $$P_C = I_p^2 R_p + I_s^2 R_s$$

  where $P_C$ is the copper losses, $I_p$ is the primary current, $R_p$ is the primary resistance, $I_s$ is the secondary current, and $R_s$ is the secondary resistance.

- The equivalent resistance of the transformer, as seen from the primary side, is given by the formula   :

  $$R_e = R_p + \left(\frac{N_p}{N_s}\right)^2 R_s$$

  where $R_e$ is the equivalent resistance, $N_p$ is the number of turns in the primary winding, and $N_s$ is the number of turns in the secondary winding.
