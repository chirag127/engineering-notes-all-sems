# 5. Measurement of power in 3- phase circuit by two-wattmeter method and determination of its power factor for star as well as delta connected load.

- The two-wattmeter method is a technique for measuring the total power in a balanced three-phase circuit using two wattmeters .
- A wattmeter is an instrument that measures the power in a single-phase or balanced three-phase circuit by multiplying the voltage and current signals and averaging them over time .
- The two-wattmeter method involves connecting the current coils of the two wattmeters in series with any two line conductors, and the potential coils of each wattmeter to the third line conductor  .

- The total power in the three-phase circuit is given by the sum of the readings of the two wattmeters, i.e., P = P1 + P2   .
- The power factor of the load is given by the ratio of the total power to the product of the line voltage and the total line current, i.e., pf = P / (V L I L)   .
- Alternatively, the power factor of the load can be determined by using the following formula, which involves the readings of the two wattmeters and the phase angle between the line voltage and the line current, i.e., pf = cos(φ) = (P1 + P2) / (√3 V L I L)   .
- The phase angle between the line voltage and the line current can also be calculated by using the following formula, which involves the readings of the two wattmeters and the power factor of the load, i.e., φ = cos−1(pf) = tan−1(√3 (P2 − P1) / (P1 + P2))   .
- The two-wattmeter method can be used for both balanced and unbalanced loads, but the power factor formula is valid only for balanced loads   .
- The two-wattmeter method has the advantage of being simple, accurate, and economical, as it requires only two wattmeters instead of three   .
- The two-wattmeter method has the limitation of being unable to measure the power in a three-phase circuit when the load is purely inductive or capacitive, as one of the wattmeters will read zero or negative   .
