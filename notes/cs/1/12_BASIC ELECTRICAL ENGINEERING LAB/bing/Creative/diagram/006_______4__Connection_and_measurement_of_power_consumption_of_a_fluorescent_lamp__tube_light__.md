##### 4. Connection and measurement of power consumption of a fluorescent lamp (tube light).

- A fluorescent lamp (or tube light) is a type of electric light that uses low-pressure mercury vapor and phosphor coating to produce visible light by fluorescence.
- A fluorescent lamp consists of a glass tube filled with inert gas (usually argon) and a small amount of mercury, with electrodes at both ends. The tube is coated with a phosphor material that emits light when excited by ultraviolet (UV) radiation.
- A fluorescent lamp requires a ballast to provide the necessary voltage and current to start and operate the lamp. The ballast can be either magnetic or electronic, depending on the type and design of the lamp.
- A fluorescent lamp can be connected to an AC power supply in two ways: series connection or parallel connection.
  - In series connection, the lamp and the ballast are connected in series with the power supply. The ballast limits the current and provides the required voltage to start and run the lamp. The power consumption of the lamp is equal to the product of the voltage across the lamp and the current through the lamp.
  - In parallel connection, the lamp and the ballast are connected in parallel with the power supply. The ballast provides the necessary voltage and current to start and run the lamp, while the power supply provides the power to the ballast. The power consumption of the lamp is equal to the product of the voltage across the ballast and the current through the ballast.
- The power consumption of a fluorescent lamp can be measured by using a wattmeter, an ammeter, and a voltmeter. The wattmeter measures the power input to the lamp or the ballast, depending on the connection. The ammeter measures the current through the lamp or the ballast, depending on the connection. The voltmeter measures the voltage across the lamp or the ballast, depending on the connection.
- The power factor of a fluorescent lamp is the ratio of the real power (watts) to the apparent power (volt-amperes). The power factor indicates how efficiently the lamp uses the power supply. A low power factor means that the lamp draws more current than necessary, which can cause losses and heating in the power system. A high power factor means that the lamp draws less current and uses the power supply more efficiently. The power factor of a fluorescent lamp can be improved by using a capacitor in parallel with the lamp or the ballast, depending on the connection. The capacitor provides a leading reactive power that cancels out the lagging reactive power of the lamp or the ballast, resulting in a higher power factor.
