##### 3. Study of phenomenon of resonance in RLC series circuit and obtain resonant frequency.

- An RLC series circuit consists of a resistor (R), an inductor (L) and a capacitor (C) connected in series with an alternating voltage source (V).
- The current (I) in the circuit is the same for all the elements and is given by Ohm's law: I = V/Z, where Z is the total impedance of the circuit.
- The impedance Z is a complex quantity that depends on the frequency (f) of the applied voltage and the values of R, L and C. It is given by: Z = R + j(XL - XC), where j is the imaginary unit, XL = 2πfL is the inductive reactance and XC = 1/(2πfC) is the capacitive reactance.
- The phase angle (φ) between the voltage and the current is given by: tan φ = (XL - XC)/R. When φ = 0, the voltage and the current are in phase and the power factor is 1. When φ ≠ 0, the voltage and the current are out of phase and the power factor is less than 1.
- Resonance is a condition in which the circuit exhibits a maximum current or a minimum impedance at a certain frequency. This frequency is called the resonant frequency (fr) and is given by: fr = 1/(2π√LC). At resonance, the inductive reactance and the capacitive reactance are equal and cancel each other, so Z = R and φ = 0.
- The effects of resonance in a series RLC circuit are:
  - The current reaches a maximum value of Imax = V/R at the resonant frequency.
  - The voltage across the inductor and the capacitor are equal in magnitude and opposite in phase, so they cancel each other. The voltage across the resistor is equal to the applied voltage.
  - The power transferred to the circuit is maximum and equal to Pmax = V^2/R at the resonant frequency.
  - The circuit behaves like a pure resistive circuit at the resonant frequency.
