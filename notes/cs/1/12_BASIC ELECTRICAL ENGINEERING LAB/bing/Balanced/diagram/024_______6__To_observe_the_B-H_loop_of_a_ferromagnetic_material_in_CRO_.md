##### 6. To observe the B-H loop of a ferromagnetic material in CRO.

- The B-H loop of a ferromagnetic material is a graphical representation of the relationship between the magnetic flux density (B) and the magnetic field intensity (H) of the material when it is subjected to a cyclic magnetic field.
- The B-H loop shows the hysteresis behavior of the ferromagnetic material, which means that the magnetization of the material depends not only on the current value of the magnetic field, but also on the previous history of the magnetic field.
- The B-H loop can be observed in a cathode ray oscilloscope (CRO) by using a setup that consists of the following components:
  - A ferromagnetic core with a primary winding and a secondary winding.
  - A sinusoidal voltage source that is connected to the primary winding and provides the alternating magnetic field to the core.
  - A resistor that is connected in series with the secondary winding and acts as a load.
  - A CRO that is connected across the resistor and displays the voltage across the resistor (V) on the vertical axis and the current through the primary winding (I) on the horizontal axis.

  - The B-H loop has the following features:
    - The loop is closed and has a rectangular shape, indicating that the ferromagnetic material has a high coercivity (the ability to retain magnetization) and a high remanence (the residual magnetization after the magnetic field is removed).
    - The loop has a large area, indicating that the ferromagnetic material has a high hysteresis loss (the energy dissipated as heat due to the reversal of magnetization).
    - The loop shifts to the right or left depending on the direction of the initial magnetization of the material, indicating that the ferromagnetic material has a magnetic memory (the tendency to retain the previous direction of magnetization).
    - The loop becomes narrower and smaller as the amplitude of the sinusoidal voltage source decreases, indicating that the ferromagnetic material approaches saturation (the state where the magnetization does not change significantly with the magnetic field).
