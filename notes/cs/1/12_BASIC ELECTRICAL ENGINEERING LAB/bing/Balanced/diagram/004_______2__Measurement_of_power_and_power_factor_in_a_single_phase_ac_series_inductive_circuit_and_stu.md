##### 2. Measurement of power and power factor in a single phase ac series inductive circuit and study improvement of power factor using capacitor

- Power factor is a measure of energy efficiency in an ac circuit. It is the ratio of real power (W) to apparent power (VA) or the cosine of the phase angle between voltage and current   .
- Real power is the power that is actually consumed by the load and does useful work. Apparent power is the product of the rms values of voltage and current. Phase angle is the angle by which the current lags or leads the voltage in an ac circuit.
- In a purely resistive circuit, the current and voltage are in phase and the power factor is 1. In a purely inductive circuit, the current lags the voltage by 90 degrees and the power factor is 0. In a series RL circuit, the current lags the voltage by some angle less than 90 degrees and the power factor is between 0 and 1.
- The power factor can be calculated by using the formula: p.f. = W/VA = R/Z = cos φ, where W is the real power, VA is the apparent power, R is the resistance, Z is the impedance and φ is the phase angle  .
- The power factor can also be represented by a power triangle, which is a right-angled triangle with the real power as the adjacent side, the apparent power as the hypotenuse and the reactive power as the opposite side. The reactive power is the power that is stored and released by the inductor and does not do any useful work. The power triangle can be used to find the power factor by using the Pythagorean theorem and trigonometric ratios.
- The power factor can be measured by using a power factor meter, which is an instrument that measures the phase angle between voltage and current in an ac circuit. A single phase power factor meter consists of two pressure coils and a current coil. The pressure coils are connected across the supply voltage and the current coil is connected in series with the load. The pressure coils produce a torque proportional to the voltage and the current coil produces a torque proportional to the current. The net torque causes the pointer to deflect and indicate the power factor on a scale.
- The power factor can be improved by using a capacitor in parallel with the load. A capacitor is a device that stores electric charge and produces a current that leads the voltage by 90 degrees in an ac circuit. By connecting a capacitor in parallel with the load, the current drawn from the source is reduced and the phase angle between voltage and current is decreased. This increases the power factor and reduces the power losses in the circuit.
- The improvement of power factor can be verified by measuring the power and power factor before and after connecting the capacitor. The power can be measured by using a wattmeter, which is an instrument that measures the product of voltage and current in an ac circuit. A single phase wattmeter consists of two coils: a current coil and a potential coil. The current coil is connected in series with the load and the potential coil is connected across the supply voltage. The coils produce a torque proportional to the power and cause the pointer to deflect and indicate the power on a scale.
