#### Course Outcome (CO) Bloom's Level

- A course outcome (CO) is a statement that describes what a student should be able to do or demonstrate after completing a course.
- Bloom's level refers to the level of cognitive complexity or difficulty of a learning task, based on Bloom's taxonomy of educational objectives.
- Bloom's taxonomy consists of six levels: remember, understand, apply, analyze, evaluate, and create. Each level requires a higher degree of cognitive skill and knowledge than the previous one.
- A CO should be aligned with the appropriate Bloom's level, depending on the intended learning outcome and the nature of the course content.
- For example, a CO for a basic mathematics course might be: "Apply the rules of arithmetic operations to solve simple problems." This CO is aligned with the apply level of Bloom's taxonomy, as it requires the student to use the learned rules in a new situation.
- A CO for an advanced literature course might be: "Create an original interpretation of a literary text, supported by textual evidence and critical theory." This CO is aligned with the create level of Bloom's taxonomy, as it requires the student to synthesize and generate new ideas from multiple sources.
- Aligning COs with Bloom's levels helps to ensure that the course is designed to promote higher-order thinking skills and to assess the student's learning effectively.
