#### (A) Hardware based experiments

- Hardware based experiments are experiments that involve the use of physical devices, components, or systems to test a hypothesis, measure a phenomenon, or demonstrate a concept.
- Hardware based experiments can be classified into different types, such as:
  - **Simulation experiments**: These are experiments that use software or hardware models to mimic the behavior of a real system or environment. For example, using a flight simulator to test the performance of an aircraft or a pilot.
  - **Benchtop experiments**: These are experiments that use small-scale or simplified versions of a system or device to test its functionality or properties. For example, using a breadboard to test an electronic circuit or a wind tunnel to test an aerodynamic shape.
  - **Field experiments**: These are experiments that use real-world settings or conditions to test a system or device. For example, using a drone to collect data from a remote location or a solar panel to generate electricity from sunlight.
- Hardware based experiments have some advantages and disadvantages, such as:
  - **Advantages**:
    - They can provide realistic and accurate data that reflects the actual performance or behavior of a system or device.
    - They can allow for direct observation and manipulation of the system or device and its variables.
    - They can facilitate the discovery of unexpected or emergent phenomena or effects that may not be captured by theoretical or mathematical models.
  - **Disadvantages**:
    - They can be costly, time-consuming, or risky to conduct, especially if they involve large-scale, complex, or hazardous systems or devices.
    - They can be affected by external factors or noise that may interfere with the results or validity of the experiment.
    - They can be difficult to replicate or reproduce, especially if they depend on specific conditions or settings that are hard to control or standardize.
