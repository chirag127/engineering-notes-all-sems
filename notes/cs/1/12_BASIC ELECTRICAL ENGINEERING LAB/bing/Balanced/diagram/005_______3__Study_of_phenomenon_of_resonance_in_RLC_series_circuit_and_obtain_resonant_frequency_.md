##### 3. Study of phenomenon of resonance in RLC series circuit and obtain resonant frequency.

- An RLC series circuit consists of a resistor (R), an inductor (L) and a capacitor (C) connected in series to an alternating voltage source.
- The current (I) in the circuit is the same for all the elements and is given by Ohm's law: I = V/Z, where V is the source voltage and Z is the impedance of the circuit.
- The impedance (Z) of the circuit is the total opposition to the current flow and is given by Z = R + j(XL - XC), where XL is the inductive reactance, XC is the capacitive reactance and j is the imaginary unit.
- The inductive reactance (XL) is proportional to the frequency (f) of the source and the inductance (L) of the coil: XL = 2πfL.
- The capacitive reactance (XC) is inversely proportional to the frequency (f) of the source and the capacitance (C) of the capacitor: XC = 1/(2πfC).
- The phase difference (φ) between the current and the voltage is given by tanφ = (XL - XC)/R.
- Resonance is a condition in which the current in the circuit is maximum and the phase difference between the current and the voltage is zero.
- Resonance occurs when the inductive reactance (XL) is equal to the capacitive reactance (XC), or XL - XC = 0. This implies that the frequency (f) of the source is equal to the resonant frequency (fr) of the circuit, which is given by fr = 1/(2π√LC).
- At resonance, the impedance (Z) of the circuit is equal to the resistance (R) of the circuit, and the current (I) is given by I = V/R.
- The voltage across the inductor (VL) and the capacitor (VC) are equal in magnitude and opposite in phase at resonance, and are given by VL = VC = IXL = IXC.
- The voltage across the resistor (VR) is in phase with the current and the source voltage, and is given by VR = IR.
- The power (P) dissipated in the circuit at resonance is given by P = I^2R = V^2/R.
