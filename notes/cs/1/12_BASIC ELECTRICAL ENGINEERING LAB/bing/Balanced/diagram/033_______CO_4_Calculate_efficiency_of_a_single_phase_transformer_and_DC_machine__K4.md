##### CO 4 Calculate efficiency of a single phase transformer and DC machine. K4

- The efficiency of a single phase transformer is defined as the ratio of useful output power to the input power. It is expressed as a percentage or a decimal fraction. The efficiency of a transformer depends on the load and the power factor of the load.
- The input power of a transformer consists of the output power plus the losses. The losses in a transformer are mainly of two types: core losses and copper losses. Core losses are due to the alternating magnetic flux in the core, which causes hysteresis and eddy currents. Copper losses are due to the resistance of the windings, which causes heating and power dissipation.
- The core losses are constant and independent of the load, while the copper losses vary with the load current. Therefore, the efficiency of a transformer varies with the load. The maximum efficiency occurs when the core losses are equal to the copper losses. This is called the condition of maximum efficiency.
- The efficiency of a transformer can be calculated by using the following formula:

  `η = (Pout / Pin) * 100%`

  where η is the efficiency, Pout is the output power, and Pin is the input power.

- Alternatively, the efficiency can be calculated by using the following formula:

  `η = (Pout / (Pout + Pcore + Pcopper)) * 100%`

  where Pcore is the core loss and Pcopper is the copper loss.

- The core loss can be measured by performing an open circuit test, in which the secondary winding is left open and the primary winding is connected to a rated voltage. The input power in this case is equal to the core loss. The copper loss can be measured by performing a short circuit test, in which the secondary winding is shorted and the primary winding is connected to a reduced voltage. The input power in this case is equal to the copper loss.
- The efficiency of a DC machine is defined as the ratio of useful output power to the input power. It is expressed as a percentage or a decimal fraction. The efficiency of a DC machine depends on the load and the type of the machine. The input power of a DC machine consists of the output power plus the losses. The losses in a DC machine are mainly of three types: copper losses, iron losses, and mechanical losses.
- Copper losses are due to the resistance of the armature and the field windings, which causes heating and power dissipation. Iron losses are due to the alternating magnetic flux in the core, which causes hysteresis and eddy currents. Mechanical losses are due to the friction and windage of the rotating parts, such as the bearings and the brushes.
- The copper losses vary with the load current, while the iron losses and the mechanical losses are constant and independent of the load. Therefore, the efficiency of a DC machine varies with the load. The maximum efficiency occurs when the variable losses are equal to the constant losses. This is called the condition of maximum efficiency.
- The efficiency of a DC machine can be calculated by using the following formula:

  `η = (Pout / Pin) * 100%`

  where η is the efficiency, Pout is the output power, and Pin is the input power.

- Alternatively, the efficiency can be calculated by using the following formula:

  `η = (Pout / (Pout + Pcopper + Piron + Pmech)) * 100%`

  where Pcopper is the copper loss, Piron is the iron loss, and Pmech is the mechanical loss.

- The copper loss can be measured by performing a load test, in which the output power and the input current are measured at different load conditions. The iron loss and the mechanical loss can be measured by performing a no-load test, in which the output power is zero and the input current is measured at rated voltage and speed. The input power in this case is equal to the sum of the iron loss and the mechanical loss.
