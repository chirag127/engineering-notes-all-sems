##### CO 1 Conduct experiments illustrating the application of KVL/KCL and network theorems to DC electrical circuits. K3

KVL (Kirchhoff's Voltage Law) and KCL (Kirchhoff's Current Law) are fundamental principles in DC electrical circuits. KVL states that the sum of voltage drops around a closed loop in a circuit is equal to the total voltage applied to the loop. KCL states that the sum of currents entering and leaving a node in a circuit is equal to zero.

Network theorems such as Ohm's Law, Thevenin's Theorem, and Norton's Theorem can be applied to simplify complex circuits and solve for unknown voltages and currents.

A simple experiment to illustrate KVL could involve connecting resistors in a series circuit and measuring the voltage across each resistor using a multimeter. The sum of the voltage drops across each resistor should equal the total applied voltage.

KCL can be demonstrated by connecting resistors in a parallel circuit and measuring the current through each resistor using an ammeter. The sum of the currents flowing into each node should equal zero.

To illustrate network theorems, a complex circuit could be simplified by using Ohm's Law to find equivalent resistances, Thevenin's Theorem to find the equivalent voltage source and resistance seen from the load, or Norton's Theorem to find the equivalent current source and resistance seen from the load.
