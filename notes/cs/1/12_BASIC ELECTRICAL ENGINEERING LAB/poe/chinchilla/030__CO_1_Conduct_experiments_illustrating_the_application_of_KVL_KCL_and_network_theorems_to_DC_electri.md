##### CO 1 Conduct experiments illustrating the application of KVL/KCL and network theorems to DC electrical circuits. K3

In this topic, we will learn about the application of Kirchhoff's Voltage Law (KVL), Kirchhoff's Current Law (KCL), and network theorems in DC electrical circuits. These concepts are essential to understand the behavior of DC circuits and analyze their performance. Here are some experiments that will help us understand the application of KVL/KCL and network theorems in DC electrical circuits:

1. Experiment on Kirchhoff's Voltage Law (KVL):
   * Set up a simple DC circuit with a voltage source and multiple resistors connected in series or parallel.
   * Measure the voltage drops across each resistor using a voltmeter and record the values.
   * Apply KVL to the circuit and compare the calculated voltage drops with the measured values.
   * Repeat the experiment with different configurations of resistors and analyze the results.

2. Experiment on Kirchhoff's Current Law (KCL):
   * Set up a DC circuit with multiple current sources and resistors connected in series or parallel.
   * Measure the current flowing through each resistor using an ammeter and record the values.
   * Apply KCL to the circuit and compare the calculated currents with the measured values.
   * Repeat the experiment with different configurations of current sources and resistors and analyze the results.

3. Experiment on Network Theorems:
   * Set up a DC circuit with multiple resistors and voltage sources connected in a complex configuration.
   * Apply network theorems such as Thevenin's theorem or Norton's theorem to simplify the circuit and calculate the equivalent resistance and voltage/current sources.
   * Verify the results by measuring the voltage/current values and comparing them with the calculated values.
   * Repeat the experiment with different configurations of resistors and voltage sources and analyze the results.

By conducting these experiments, we can gain practical knowledge and hands-on experience in applying KVL/KCL and network theorems in DC electrical circuits. This knowledge will be useful in designing, analyzing, and troubleshooting DC circuits in various applications.
