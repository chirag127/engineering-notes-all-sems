1. The given virtual lab link is for the experiment on Half wave rectifier.
2. A half wave rectifier is a circuit that converts alternating current (AC) to direct current (DC) by allowing one half of the AC wave to pass through unchanged while reversing the other half.
3. It uses a single diode to remove either the negative or positive portion of the AC voltage waveform.
4. The output of half wave rectifier has AC components which are not desirable in most power supplies. A filter circuit is required to produce smooth DC.
5. Key components used are:
- AC source
- Diode
- Load resistor
6. Working: During positive half cycle, diode is forward biased and it conducts current. The output voltage is equal to the input voltage. During negative half cycle, diode is reverse biased and it does not conduct. The output voltage is zero. Thus we get half wave rectified output across the load.
7. Applications: Used in power supplies for conversion from AC to DC. Also used to detect the amplitude of signals in radio receivers.
