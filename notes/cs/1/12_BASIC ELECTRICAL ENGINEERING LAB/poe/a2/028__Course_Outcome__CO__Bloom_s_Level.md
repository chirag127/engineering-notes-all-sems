#### Course Outcome (CO) Bloom's Level

1. Remembering: Recalling facts, terms, basic concepts and answers. Example: List the steps involved in photosynthesis.

2. Understanding: Explaining ideas and concepts, translating one form to another. Example: Explain how water moves through the water cycle.

3. Applying: Using information in new contexts and situations. Example: Predict how evaporation will increase with rising temperature.

4. Analyzing: Breaking down information into parts and determining how parts relate, make inferences and find evidence to support generalizations. Example: Compare the structures and functions of monocot and dicot seeds.

5. Evaluating: Making judgments based on criteria and determining value or worth, detect bias and errors in logic or evidence. Example: Evaluate issues surrounding the use of genetically modified crops.

6. Creating: Putting parts together in new ways or applying new ideas to generate something new and meaningful. Example: Propose a model for improving water use efficiency in crop irrigation.

The Bloom's taxonomy organizes learning objectives into hierarchical levels: Remembering, Understanding, Applying, Analyzing, Evaluating and Creating that promote higher levels of thinking. COs are measurable learning expectations expressed using active verbs aligned with the appropriate Bloom's level.
