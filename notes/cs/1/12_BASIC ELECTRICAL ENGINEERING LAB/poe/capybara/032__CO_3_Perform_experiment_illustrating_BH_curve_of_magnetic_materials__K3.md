##### CO 3 Perform experiment illustrating BH curve of magnetic materials. K3

In order to understand the behavior of magnetic materials, it is important to study their B-H or magnetization characteristics. The B-H curve is a graphical representation of the relationship between magnetic field strength (H) and magnetic flux density (B) in a magnetic material. The curve has two parts: the initial magnetization curve (IMC) and the demagnetization curve (DC).

To perform an experiment illustrating the B-H curve of magnetic materials, follow these steps:

1. Obtain a sample of the magnetic material under study. This could be a piece of iron, steel, or any other ferromagnetic material.

2. Set up the experimental apparatus. This will typically involve a magnetizing coil, an ammeter, a voltmeter, and a sample holder.

3. Place the sample in the sample holder and connect the magnetizing coil to a power source.

4. Gradually increase the current in the coil while measuring the magnetic field strength (H) and the magnetic flux density (B) using the ammeter and voltmeter respectively.

5. Plot the values of H and B on a graph to obtain the B-H curve.

6. Determine the values of saturation magnetization (Ms) and coercive force (Hc) from the B-H curve. Ms represents the maximum magnetic flux density that can be induced in the material, while Hc represents the minimum magnetic field strength required to reduce the magnetic flux density to zero.

7. Repeat the experiment for different samples of the same material to obtain an average B-H curve and improve the accuracy of the results.

By performing this experiment, one can gain a better understanding of the magnetic properties of different materials and how they can be used in various applications such as transformers, motors, and generators. It is important to note that the B-H curve may vary depending on factors such as temperature, stress, and magnetic history, and so the experiment should be performed under controlled conditions to obtain accurate results.
