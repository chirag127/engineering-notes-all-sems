## Measurement of Power and Power Factor in a Single Phase AC Series Inductive Circuit and Study Improvement of Power Factor Using Capacitor

In the study of electrical circuits, the measurement of power and power factor is crucial. In a single phase AC circuit, the power factor is defined as the ratio of the real power, which is the power that is actually used in the circuit, to the apparent power, which is the product of the voltage and current. In this topic, we will learn about the measurement of power and power factor in a single phase AC series inductive circuit, and how the power factor can be improved using a capacitor.

### Measurement of Power

The power in a single phase AC circuit can be measured using a wattmeter. The wattmeter consists of two coils, one of which is connected in series with the load, and the other is connected in parallel with the load. The voltage coil is connected in parallel with the load, while the current coil is connected in series with the load. The wattmeter measures the product of the voltage and current, which gives the power consumed by the load.

### Measurement of Power Factor

The power factor of a single phase AC circuit can be measured using a power factor meter. The power factor meter consists of a fixed coil and a moving coil. The fixed coil is connected in series with the load, while the moving coil is connected across the load. The power factor meter measures the angle between the voltage and current, which gives the power factor of the circuit.

### Single Phase AC Series Inductive Circuit

In a single phase AC series inductive circuit, the power factor is usually low due to the presence of inductance. Inductance causes the current to lag behind the voltage, which results in a low power factor. The power factor can be improved by adding a capacitor in parallel with the load. The capacitor supplies reactive power to the circuit, which helps to reduce the lagging current and improve the power factor.

### Improvement of Power Factor Using Capacitor

The power factor of a single phase AC series inductive circuit can be improved by adding a capacitor in parallel with the load. The value of the capacitor should be chosen so that it supplies the reactive power required to compensate for the lagging current. The reactive power can be calculated using the formula:

    Q = V^2 / (ωL)

where Q is the reactive power, V is the voltage, ω is the angular frequency of the circuit, and L is the inductance.

By adding a capacitor of the appropriate value in parallel with the load, the power factor can be improved, which results in a more efficient use of the electrical energy. The improvement in power factor can be measured using a power factor meter, which will show a higher power factor after the capacitor is added.

To summarize, the measurement of power and power factor in a single phase AC series inductive circuit is important for the efficient use of electrical energy. The power factor can be improved by adding a capacitor in parallel with the load, which supplies reactive power to the circuit and reduces the lagging current.
