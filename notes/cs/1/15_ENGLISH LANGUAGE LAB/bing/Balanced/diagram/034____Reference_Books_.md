## Reference Books:

- Reference books are books that contain factual information on various topics, such as dictionaries, encyclopedias, atlases, almanacs, directories, handbooks, manuals, etc.
- Reference books are usually consulted for specific information, rather than read cover to cover.
- Reference books are often arranged alphabetically, chronologically, geographically, or by subject, depending on the type and purpose of the book.
- Reference books are usually kept in a separate section of a library, called the reference collection, and are not available for borrowing.
- Reference books can be useful for finding definitions, facts, statistics, background information, overviews, summaries, bibliographies, and more.
- Reference books can be general or specialized, depending on the scope and depth of the information they cover.
- Some examples of general reference books are:
  - The World Book Encyclopedia: a comprehensive encyclopedia that covers a wide range of topics in an accessible and concise manner.
  - The Oxford English Dictionary: a historical dictionary that traces the origin and development of the English language and its words.
  - The World Almanac and Book of Facts: a yearly publication that provides current and historical information on various subjects, such as politics, sports, science, culture, etc.
- Some examples of specialized reference books are:
  - The Merck Manual of Diagnosis and Therapy: a medical reference book that provides information on diseases, symptoms, treatments, drugs, etc.
  - The MLA Handbook for Writers of Research Papers: a style guide that provides rules and guidelines for writing and formatting academic papers in the humanities.
  - The Atlas of World History: a historical atlas that provides maps and timelines of the major events and civilizations in world history.
