### 2-Computer assisted software based Language Learning:

- Computer assisted software based language learning (CASBLL) is a type of language learning that uses software applications to facilitate the acquisition of a second or foreign language.
- CASBLL can be classified into two main categories: computer assisted language learning (CALL) and computer assisted language testing (CALT).
- CALL is the use of software to provide interactive and adaptive instruction, feedback, and practice in various language skills, such as listening, speaking, reading, writing, grammar, and vocabulary.
- CALT is the use of software to assess the language proficiency of learners, either for diagnostic, formative, or summative purposes.
- CASBLL can offer several advantages for language learners, such as:
  - Increased motivation and engagement, as the software can provide multimedia, gamification, and personalization features.
  - Increased autonomy and flexibility, as the software can allow learners to work at their own pace, level, and preferred style.
  - Increased exposure and input, as the software can provide authentic and varied materials and activities in the target language.
  - Increased feedback and correction, as the software can provide immediate and individualized responses and hints to learners' errors and queries.
- CASBLL can also pose some challenges and limitations for language learners, such as:
  - Technical issues and accessibility, as the software may require specific devices, platforms, or internet connections that are not available or affordable for all learners.
  - Pedagogical quality and validity, as the software may not be aligned with the learning objectives, curriculum, or standards of the learners' context or level.
  - Social interaction and communication, as the software may not be able to replicate or replace the human element and the sociocultural aspects of language learning.
