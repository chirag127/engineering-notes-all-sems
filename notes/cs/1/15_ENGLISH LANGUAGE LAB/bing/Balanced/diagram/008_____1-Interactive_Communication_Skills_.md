### 1-Interactive Communication Skills:

- Interactive communication skills are the abilities that enable a person to effectively exchange information, ideas, feelings, and feedback with others in various contexts, such as face-to-face, online, or in groups.
- Interactive communication skills include verbal, non-verbal, written, and visual communication, as well as listening, questioning, clarifying, summarizing, and empathizing.
- Interactive communication skills are essential for building rapport, trust, and understanding with others, as well as for resolving conflicts, negotiating, persuading, and influencing others.
- Interactive communication skills can be improved by practicing the following strategies:
  - Be clear and concise in your messages, and avoid jargon, slang, or ambiguous words.
  - Use appropriate tone, volume, and pitch in your voice, and match your body language and facial expressions to your words.
  - Adapt your communication style and content to suit your audience, purpose, and situation, and use feedback to check for understanding and adjust accordingly.
  - Listen actively and attentively to others, and show interest and respect by using eye contact, nodding, and paraphrasing.
  - Ask open-ended, probing, and clarifying questions to elicit more information, opinions, and feelings from others, and avoid interrupting or judging them.
  - Summarize and reflect back what you have heard or understood from others, and confirm or correct any misunderstandings or misinterpretations.
  - Express your own thoughts, feelings, and needs in a respectful and assertive way, and use "I" statements to avoid blaming or accusing others.
  - Show empathy and compassion to others, and try to understand their perspectives, emotions, and motivations, without agreeing or disagreeing with them.
  - Acknowledge and validate the feelings and concerns of others, and offer support, encouragement, or assistance if appropriate.
  - Manage your own emotions and reactions, and avoid getting defensive, angry, or aggressive when communicating with others, especially in difficult or stressful situations.
  - Seek to understand the root causes of any conflicts or disagreements, and use constructive and collaborative approaches to resolve them, such as compromising, brainstorming, or problem-solving.
  - Use positive and persuasive language and techniques to influence others, such as providing facts, examples, benefits, or testimonials, and appealing to their values, interests, or emotions.
  - Recognize and respect the diversity and differences among people, such as their cultures, beliefs, values, or preferences, and avoid making assumptions, stereotypes, or judgments about them.
  - Seek feedback on your own communication skills, and identify your strengths and areas for improvement, and set goals and action plans to enhance them.
