### 10. Startup presentations, Video portfolio, Extempore, Role play, Just a Minute (JAM) etc.

These are some of the common types of tasks or activities that students or professionals may encounter in their academic or career pursuits. They are designed to test various skills such as communication, creativity, critical thinking, teamwork, and problem-solving. Here is a brief overview of each type of task and some tips on how to perform well in them.

- **Startup presentations**: These are presentations that showcase a new business idea or venture to potential investors, customers, or partners. The goal is to persuade the audience that the idea is viable, innovative, and profitable. Some tips for a successful startup presentation are:

  - Identify the problem that your idea solves and the value proposition that it offers.
  - Research your target market, competitors, and industry trends.
  - Create a clear and concise pitch deck that covers the key aspects of your idea, such as the problem, solution, market, product, business model, traction, team, and financials.
  - Practice your presentation and get feedback from others.
  - Use storytelling, visuals, and data to engage the audience and demonstrate your credibility.

- **Video portfolio**: This is a collection of videos that showcase your skills, achievements, and personality. It can be used for various purposes, such as applying for jobs, scholarships, or internships, or promoting your personal brand. Some tips for creating a video portfolio are:

  - Choose a suitable platform or tool to create and host your videos, such as YouTube, Vimeo, or Wix.
  - Select the videos that best represent your work, interests, and goals. They can be projects, testimonials, interviews, or demonstrations of your skills.
  - Organize your videos into categories or themes, and add titles, descriptions, and tags to make them easy to find and navigate.
  - Edit your videos to make them clear, concise, and professional. Use transitions, effects, and music to enhance the quality and appeal of your videos.
  - Share your video portfolio with your target audience and ask for feedback.

- **Extempore**: This is a type of speech or presentation that is delivered without any prior preparation or notes. It is often used as a test of one's ability to think on one's feet, express oneself clearly, and handle unexpected situations. Some tips for doing well in an extempore are:

  - Listen carefully to the topic or question that is given to you and take a few seconds to brainstorm some ideas or points.
  - Structure your speech or presentation into a clear introduction, body, and conclusion. Use transitions and signposts to connect your ideas and guide the audience.
  - Speak confidently, fluently, and coherently. Avoid filler words, pauses, and repetitions. Use appropriate language, tone, and gestures to convey your message and emotions.
  - Support your arguments or opinions with facts, examples, or anecdotes. Address any counterarguments or objections that may arise.
  - Summarize your main points and end with a strong closing statement or call to action.

- **Role play**: This is a type of activity that involves acting out a scenario or situation with one or more partners. It is often used to simulate real-life situations, such as job interviews, customer service, or conflict resolution. The goal is to demonstrate one's skills, knowledge, and behavior in a given context. Some tips for performing well in a role play are:

  - Understand the objective, scenario, and role that you are assigned or choose. Research the background, expectations, and challenges that you may face in the situation.
  - Prepare a script or outline of the dialogue or actions that you will perform. Practice your role with your partner or by yourself.
  - Be realistic, flexible, and creative. Adapt to the situation and the responses of your partner. Use props, costumes, or accessories to enhance the realism and immersion of the role play.
  - Communicate effectively, respectfully, and empathetically with your partner. Listen actively, ask questions, and provide feedback. Use verbal and non-verbal cues to express your emotions and intentions.
  - Reflect on your performance and the feedback that you receive. Identify your strengths and areas for improvement.

  - Choose a topic that you are familiar with, interested in, and have something to say about. Avoid topics that are too broad, complex, or controversial.
