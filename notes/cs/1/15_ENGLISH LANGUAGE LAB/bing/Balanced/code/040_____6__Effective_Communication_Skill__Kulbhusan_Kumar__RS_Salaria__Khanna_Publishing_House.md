### 6. Effective Communication Skill, Kulbhusan Kumar, RS Salaria, Khanna Publishing House

- Effective communication skill is the ability to convey information and ideas clearly, accurately, and persuasively to others, using verbal and non-verbal means.
- Effective communication skill is essential for personal and professional success, as it helps to build trust, rapport, and understanding with others, and to achieve desired outcomes and goals.
- Effective communication skill involves four main components: sender, message, channel, and receiver. The sender is the person who initiates the communication, the message is the content and meaning of the communication, the channel is the medium or mode of communication, and the receiver is the person who receives and interprets the communication.
- Effective communication skill also depends on various factors, such as context, purpose, audience, tone, style, feedback, and barriers. The context is the situation or environment in which the communication takes place, the purpose is the reason or objective of the communication, the audience is the intended or actual recipient of the communication, the tone is the attitude or emotion conveyed by the communication, the style is the way of expression or presentation of the communication, the feedback is the response or reaction of the receiver to the communication, and the barriers are the obstacles or challenges that hinder or distort the communication.
- Effective communication skill can be improved by following some general principles and guidelines, such as:
  - Know your purpose and audience: Before communicating, you should have a clear idea of what you want to achieve and who you are communicating with, and tailor your message accordingly.
  - Be clear and concise: You should use simple, precise, and relevant language, and avoid jargon, ambiguity, and unnecessary details, to convey your message effectively and avoid confusion or misunderstanding.
  - Be respectful and courteous: You should show respect and courtesy to your audience, and avoid offensive, rude, or aggressive language, to build rapport and trust, and to avoid conflict or resentment.
  - Be attentive and responsive: You should listen actively and attentively to your audience, and respond appropriately and timely, to show interest and engagement, and to ensure mutual understanding and feedback.
  - Be confident and assertive: You should express your message confidently and assertively, and avoid hesitation, doubt, or apology, to demonstrate your credibility and authority, and to persuade or influence your audience.
  - Be adaptable and flexible: You should adapt and adjust your message and style according to the context, channel, and feedback, and be open to different perspectives and opinions, to cope with changing situations and expectations, and to overcome barriers and challenges.
