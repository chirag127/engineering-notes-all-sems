### 2. Conversational Skills for Interviews under suitable Professional Communication Lab conditions with emphasis on Kinesics.

- Conversational skills are the abilities to communicate effectively and appropriately with others in various situations, such as interviews.
- Interviews are formal interactions between an interviewer and an interviewee, where the interviewer evaluates the interviewee's suitability for a job, a project, or a scholarship.
- Professional communication lab conditions are the settings and equipment that simulate a real-life interview scenario, such as a video conference, a microphone, a camera, and a screen.
- Kinesics is the study of body language, such as gestures, facial expressions, eye contact, and posture, that convey meaning and emotion in communication.
- Some of the conversational skills for interviews under suitable professional communication lab conditions with emphasis on kinesics are:

  - Prepare well for the interview by researching the organization, the role, and the interviewer, and by rehearsing possible questions and answers.
  - Dress appropriately and professionally for the interview, according to the culture and norms of the organization and the role.
  - Arrive on time and check the technical settings and equipment before the interview starts.
  - Greet the interviewer politely and introduce yourself briefly and confidently.
  - Maintain a positive and respectful attitude throughout the interview, and avoid interrupting, arguing, or criticizing the interviewer or the organization.
  - Use clear and concise language, and avoid jargon, slang, or filler words, such as "um", "like", or "you know".
  - Listen actively and attentively to the interviewer's questions and comments, and respond relevantly and appropriately, using examples and evidence to support your points.
  - Use appropriate body language, such as nodding, smiling, leaning forward, and gesturing, to show interest, enthusiasm, and confidence.
  - Maintain eye contact with the interviewer, but do not stare or look away too often, as this may indicate dishonesty, boredom, or nervousness.
  - Adjust your posture and position according to the situation, and avoid slouching, fidgeting, crossing your arms, or touching your face, as this may indicate lack of confidence, discomfort, or defensiveness.
  - Ask relevant and insightful questions at the end of the interview, to show your curiosity and interest in the organization and the role.
  - Thank the interviewer for their time and consideration, and follow up with a polite and professional email or message, expressing your appreciation and interest.
