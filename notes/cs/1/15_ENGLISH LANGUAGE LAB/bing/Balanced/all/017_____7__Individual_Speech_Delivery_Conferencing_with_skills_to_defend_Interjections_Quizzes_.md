# 7. Individual Speech Delivery/Conferencing with skills to defend Interjections/Quizzes

- Individual speech delivery is a form of oral communication where a speaker presents a prepared speech to an audience, either in person or online.
- Conferencing is a form of oral communication where a speaker engages in a dialogue with one or more listeners, either in person or online, to exchange information, opinions, or feedback.
- Interjections are spontaneous interruptions or comments from the audience or other speakers during a speech or a conference.
- Quizzes are short tests or questions that assess the speaker's knowledge, understanding, or performance on a topic or skill.
- Skills to defend interjections/quizzes are the abilities and strategies that a speaker uses to respond to interjections/quizzes effectively and appropriately, without losing focus, confidence, or credibility.

Some of the skills to defend interjections/quizzes are:

- Anticipating possible interjections/quizzes and preparing responses in advance.
- Listening attentively and respectfully to the interjections/quizzes and acknowledging them.
- Clarifying the interjections/quizzes if they are unclear, vague, or ambiguous.
- Providing relevant, accurate, and concise answers or explanations to the interjections/quizzes, using evidence, examples, or logic when necessary.
- Refuting or challenging the interjections/quizzes if they are inaccurate, irrelevant, or biased, using evidence, examples, or logic when necessary.
- Inviting further questions or feedback from the audience or other speakers after answering or addressing the interjections/quizzes.
- Transitioning smoothly back to the main topic or point of the speech or conference after answering or addressing the interjections/quizzes.
- Maintaining a positive, confident, and respectful tone and body language throughout the speech or conference, especially when dealing with interjections/quizzes.
