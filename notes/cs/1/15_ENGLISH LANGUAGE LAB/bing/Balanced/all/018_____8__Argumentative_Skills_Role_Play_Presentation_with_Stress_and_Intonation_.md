# 8. Argumentative Skills/Role Play Presentation with Stress and Intonation

- Argumentative skills are the ability to present and defend a position or opinion using logical reasoning and evidence.
- Role play presentation is a type of oral communication where the speaker assumes a specific role or character and interacts with other speakers or an audience.
- Stress and intonation are the features of speech that convey emphasis, emotion, and attitude through variations in loudness, pitch, and duration.

## How to develop argumentative skills:

- Identify the main claim or thesis of your argument and state it clearly and concisely.
- Provide supporting reasons and evidence for your claim, such as facts, statistics, examples, anecdotes, quotations, etc.
- Anticipate and address possible counterarguments or objections from the opposing side, and explain why they are weak or irrelevant.
- Use appropriate language and tone for your audience and purpose, such as formal or informal, persuasive or informative, respectful or assertive, etc.
- Organize your argument in a logical and coherent way, using transitions and signposts to guide the listener.
- Use rhetorical devices and strategies to enhance your argument, such as repetition, parallelism, analogy, comparison, contrast, etc.

## How to perform a role play presentation:

- Choose a topic and a scenario that are relevant and interesting for your audience and purpose, such as a debate, a negotiation, a dialogue, a simulation, etc.
- Define the roles and characters of the speakers and their goals and motivations, such as a proponent and an opponent, a buyer and a seller, a teacher and a student, etc.
- Prepare a script or an outline of the main points and interactions of the role play, and practice it with your partner or group.
- Use appropriate body language and gestures to convey your role and character, such as eye contact, facial expressions, posture, movement, etc.
- Use appropriate stress and intonation to convey your meaning and emotion, such as rising or falling intonation, loud or soft stress, long or short pauses, etc.
- Engage with your partner or group and the audience, and respond to their feedback and questions.
