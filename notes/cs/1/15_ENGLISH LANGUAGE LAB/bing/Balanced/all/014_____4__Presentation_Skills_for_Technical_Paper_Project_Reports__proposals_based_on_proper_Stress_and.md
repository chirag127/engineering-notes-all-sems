# 4. Presentation Skills for Technical Paper/Project Reports/ proposals based on proper Stress and Intonation Mechanics

- Presentation skills are the abilities to communicate effectively and confidently with an audience, using appropriate verbal and non-verbal cues, visual aids, and structure.
- Technical paper/project reports/proposals are types of academic or professional documents that present the results of research, development, or design work in a specific field or discipline.
- Stress and intonation mechanics are the aspects of speech that convey emphasis, emotion, and attitude through variations in loudness, pitch, and duration of syllables and words.
- To improve your presentation skills for technical paper/project reports/proposals based on proper stress and intonation mechanics, you should:

  - Know your purpose, audience, and context. Define the main goal, message, and scope of your presentation. Identify the needs, expectations, and background knowledge of your audience. Consider the time, place, and format of your presentation.
  - Plan and prepare your content. Outline the main points, subpoints, and supporting details of your presentation. Use clear, concise, and accurate language. Avoid jargon, slang, and unnecessary words. Use examples, statistics, graphs, tables, diagrams, or images to illustrate your points. Cite your sources and provide references.
  - Organize and structure your presentation. Follow a logical and coherent sequence of introduction, body, and conclusion. Use transitions, signposts, and summaries to connect and highlight your points. Provide an overview, a roadmap, and a recap of your presentation. Use headings, subheadings, bullet points, or numbers to organize your content visually.
  - Practice and rehearse your presentation. Review your content and check for errors, inconsistencies, or gaps. Practice your presentation aloud, using a timer, a recorder, or a mirror. Adjust your speed, volume, tone, and pronunciation. Use stress and intonation to emphasize key words, phrases, or sentences. Use pauses, pitch changes, and intonation patterns to indicate questions, statements, contrasts, or emotions. Use gestures, eye contact, facial expressions, and body language to support your verbal communication. Seek feedback from others and improve your presentation accordingly.
  - Deliver and evaluate your presentation. Arrive early and check the equipment, the room, and the audience. Dress appropriately and professionally. Be confident, enthusiastic, and respectful. Engage your audience with questions, comments, or activities. Handle questions or challenges calmly and politely. Thank your audience and invite feedback. Reflect on your strengths and weaknesses and learn from your experience.
