# 2-Computer assisted software based Language Learning:

- Computer assisted software based language learning (CASBLL) is a subset of computer assisted language learning (CALL), which is an approach to teaching and learning languages that uses computers and other technologies to present, reinforce, and assess material to be learned, or to create environments where teachers and learners can interact with one another and the outside world.
- CASBLL focuses on the use of software applications or programs that are designed specifically for language learning purposes, such as vocabulary, grammar, pronunciation, reading, writing, listening, and speaking.
- CASBLL can be classified into different types according to the role of the computer, the mode of interaction, the type of feedback, and the pedagogical approach. Some examples of CASBLL types are:
  - Drill and practice: The computer provides exercises that require learners to produce or recognize correct forms or structures, and gives immediate feedback on their performance.
  - Tutorial: The computer provides explanations, examples, and practice activities on a specific language topic, and adapts to the learners' needs and progress.
  - Simulation: The computer creates a realistic or imaginary scenario that requires learners to use language skills to complete tasks or solve problems.
  - Game: The computer provides a fun and engaging activity that involves language use and challenges learners to achieve a goal or reward.
  - Communication: The computer facilitates interaction between learners and other speakers of the language, either synchronously or asynchronously, through text, audio, or video.
  - Authoring: The computer allows learners to create their own language learning materials, such as stories, quizzes, podcasts, or blogs.
- CASBLL can have various benefits for language learners, such as:
  - Motivation: CASBLL can increase learners' interest, engagement, and enjoyment in language learning, especially if the software is interactive, adaptive, and appealing.
  - Autonomy: CASBLL can enable learners to take control of their own learning, by allowing them to choose the content, pace, and level of difficulty of the software, and to monitor their progress and performance.
  - Feedback: CASBLL can provide learners with immediate, consistent, and personalized feedback on their language use, which can help them identify and correct their errors, and improve their accuracy and fluency.
  - Accessibility: CASBLL can make language learning more accessible and convenient, by allowing learners to use the software anytime and anywhere, with or without a teacher or a classroom.
  - Diversity: CASBLL can expose learners to a variety of language inputs, outputs, and contexts, by offering different types of software, activities, and modes of interaction, and by connecting learners with other speakers and cultures .
- CASBLL can also have some challenges and limitations, such as:
  - Quality: CASBLL can vary in the quality and suitability of the software, depending on the design, development, and evaluation of the software, and the alignment of the software with the learners' needs, goals, and preferences.
  - Cost: CASBLL can entail high costs for the development, maintenance, and updating of the software, as well as for the provision of the hardware, software, and internet access for the learners and teachers.
  - Support: CASBLL can require adequate support and guidance for the learners and teachers, in terms of the technical, pedagogical, and affective aspects of using the software, and the integration of the software with other language learning resources and activities.
  - Ethics: CASBLL can raise ethical issues, such as the privacy, security, and ownership of the data and content generated by the learners and teachers, and the potential misuse or abuse of the software for harmful or illegal purposes.
