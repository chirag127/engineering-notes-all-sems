### 4. To cultivate relevant technical style of communication and presentation at their work place and also for academic uses.

- Technical style of communication and presentation refers to the use of clear, concise, accurate and objective language to convey information or ideas in a specific field or context.
- Technical style of communication and presentation is important for both work place and academic uses, as it helps to:
  - Establish credibility and authority as a professional or a scholar.
  - Communicate effectively with different audiences, such as colleagues, clients, supervisors, instructors, peers, etc.
  - Achieve specific purposes, such as informing, persuading, instructing, proposing, etc.
  - Follow the norms and conventions of the discipline or the industry.
  - Avoid ambiguity, confusion, misunderstanding or misinterpretation.
- To cultivate relevant technical style of communication and presentation, one should:
  - Identify the purpose, audience, context and format of the communication or presentation.
  - Conduct adequate research and analysis to gather relevant and reliable information or data.
  - Organize the information or data in a logical and coherent manner, using appropriate headings, subheadings, tables, graphs, charts, etc.
  - Use precise and consistent terminology, definitions, abbreviations, symbols, units, etc.
  - Use simple and direct sentences, avoiding unnecessary words, jargon, slang, idioms, etc.
  - Use active voice, present tense, and impersonal tone, avoiding personal pronouns, opinions, emotions, etc.
  - Use correct grammar, spelling, punctuation, and citation style, following the guidelines of the discipline or the industry.
  - Revise and edit the communication or presentation, checking for clarity, accuracy, completeness, and coherence.
  - Seek feedback and suggestions from others, such as peers, mentors, instructors, etc.
  - Practice and rehearse the communication or presentation, if applicable, using appropriate voice, body language, gestures, eye contact, etc.
