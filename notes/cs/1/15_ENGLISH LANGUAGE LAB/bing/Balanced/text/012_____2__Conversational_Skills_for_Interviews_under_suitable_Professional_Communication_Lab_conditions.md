### 2. Conversational Skills for Interviews under suitable Professional Communication Lab conditions with emphasis on Kinesics.

- Conversational skills are the ability to communicate effectively and appropriately with others in various situations, such as interviews, meetings, presentations, etc.
- Interviews are formal interactions between an interviewer and an interviewee, where the interviewer evaluates the interviewee's suitability for a job, a project, a scholarship, etc.
- Professional communication lab conditions are the settings and equipment that simulate a real-life interview scenario, such as a video conference, a microphone, a camera, a screen, etc.
- Kinesics is the study of body language, gestures, facial expressions, eye contact, posture, and movement, and how they convey meaning and emotion in communication.
- Some of the conversational skills for interviews under suitable professional communication lab conditions with emphasis on kinesics are:

  - Prepare well for the interview by researching the organization, the role, the interviewer, and the common questions and answers.
  - Dress appropriately and professionally for the interview, according to the culture and norms of the organization and the industry.
  - Arrive on time and check the technical aspects of the communication lab, such as the sound, the video, the internet connection, etc.
  - Greet the interviewer politely and introduce yourself briefly and confidently, stating your name, your background, and your purpose.
  - Maintain a positive and respectful attitude throughout the interview, showing interest, enthusiasm, and gratitude for the opportunity.
  - Use clear and concise language, avoiding jargon, slang, filler words, and grammatical errors.
  - Listen actively and attentively to the interviewer, nodding, smiling, and acknowledging their points and questions.
  - Answer the questions honestly and relevantly, providing examples and evidence to support your claims and skills.
  - Ask relevant and insightful questions to the interviewer, showing your curiosity and knowledge about the organization and the role.
  - Use appropriate and effective body language, such as:

    - Eye contact: Maintain eye contact with the interviewer, but do not stare or look away too often. Eye contact shows confidence, interest, and honesty.
    - Gestures: Use gestures to emphasize your points, but do not overdo them or use them inappropriately. Gestures can show enthusiasm, passion, and clarity.
    - Facial expressions: Use facial expressions to convey your emotions, but do not exaggerate them or make them inconsistent with your words. Facial expressions can show happiness, sadness, surprise, anger, etc.
    - Posture: Use a good posture, sitting upright, leaning slightly forward, and keeping your shoulders relaxed. Posture can show confidence, alertness, and professionalism.
    - Movement: Use movement to adjust your position, but do not fidget, sway, or move too much. Movement can show nervousness, boredom, or discomfort.

  - End the interview gracefully, thanking the interviewer for their time and consideration, and expressing your interest and enthusiasm for the role.
