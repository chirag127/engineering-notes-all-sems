#### Students should practice the language with variety of activities and exercises based on employability skills. Interactive and Communicative Practical with emphasis on Oral Presentation/Spoken Communication, based on International Phonetic Alphabets (I.P.A.)

- Language learning is a skill that can enhance one's employability in the global market. It can also improve one's communication, critical thinking, and intercultural competence.
- To practice the language effectively, students should engage in a variety of activities and exercises that are relevant to their career goals and interests. These activities and exercises should focus on developing the four language skills: listening, speaking, reading, and writing.
- Some examples of activities and exercises based on employability skills are:
  - Role-playing different scenarios that may occur in the workplace, such as interviews, meetings, negotiations, presentations, etc.
  - Writing resumes, cover letters, emails, reports, proposals, etc. for different purposes and audiences.
  - Reading and analyzing authentic texts from various sources, such as newspapers, magazines, websites, blogs, etc. that are related to the students' field of study or profession.
  - Listening to and summarizing podcasts, videos, lectures, speeches, etc. that are relevant to the students' interests or career aspirations.
- Interactive and communicative practical is a method of language teaching that emphasizes the use of the language in real-life situations and contexts. It encourages students to interact with each other and the teacher, and to express their opinions, ideas, and feelings in the target language.
- Oral presentation or spoken communication is a skill that involves delivering a message to an audience in a clear, coherent, and confident manner. It requires the use of appropriate language, tone, body language, and visual aids.
- International Phonetic Alphabets (I.P.A.) are a set of symbols that represent the sounds of any language in the world. They can help students to learn the correct pronunciation and intonation of the words and sentences in the target language.
- Some examples of activities and exercises based on I.P.A. are:
  - Learning the symbols and sounds of the I.P.A. and practicing them with minimal pairs, tongue twisters, rhymes, etc.
  - Transcribing words and sentences from the target language into I.P.A. and vice versa.
  - Comparing and contrasting the sounds of the target language and the students' native language, and identifying the common and different features.
  - Listening to and imitating the speech of native speakers or models, and correcting the errors in pronunciation and intonation.
