### 3. Communication Skills for Seminars/Conferences/Workshops with emphasis on Paralinguistic/Kinesics

- Communication skills are essential for effective participation in seminars, conferences and workshops, where one has to present, discuss and interact with others on a specific topic or theme.
- Paralinguistic and kinesic features are aspects of nonverbal communication that complement and enhance the verbal message, such as voice quality, pitch, volume, rate, pauses, facial expressions, eye contact, gestures, posture and movement.
- Paralinguistic and kinesic features can convey various meanings and emotions, such as interest, enthusiasm, confidence, boredom, confusion, anger, surprise, etc. They can also signal the speaker's attitude, intention, relationship and status towards the listener and the topic.
- Paralinguistic and kinesic features can vary across cultures, contexts and situations, so one has to be aware of the norms and expectations of the audience and the setting, and adapt accordingly.
- Some tips for using paralinguistic and kinesic features effectively in seminars, conferences and workshops are:

  - Use a clear, loud and expressive voice that matches the tone and purpose of your speech. Avoid monotone, mumbling, stuttering, filler words and vocalized pauses.
  - Vary your pitch, volume and rate to emphasize key points, create contrast, show emotion and maintain attention. Avoid speaking too high, too low, too loud, too soft, too fast or too slow.
  - Use pauses strategically to signal transitions, create suspense, allow processing time and invite feedback. Avoid long, frequent or awkward pauses that disrupt the flow and coherence of your speech.
  - Use facial expressions that match and reinforce your verbal message and show your engagement and interest. Avoid expressions that contradict or undermine your message or show boredom, disinterest or disrespect.
  - Use eye contact to establish rapport, show confidence, monitor feedback and regulate turn-taking. Avoid staring, looking away, looking down or looking at your notes too often.
  - Use gestures to illustrate, emphasize and clarify your verbal message and show your enthusiasm and involvement. Avoid gestures that are distracting, inappropriate, ambiguous or overused.
  - Use posture and movement to show your confidence, openness and readiness. Avoid posture and movement that show nervousness, tension, defensiveness or aggression.
