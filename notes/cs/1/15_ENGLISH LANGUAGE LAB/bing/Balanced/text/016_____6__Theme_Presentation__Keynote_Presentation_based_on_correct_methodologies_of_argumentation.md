### 6. Theme Presentation/ Keynote Presentation based on correct methodologies of argumentation

- A theme presentation or a keynote presentation is a type of speech that introduces, develops, and summarizes a central idea or message for an audience.
- A theme presentation or a keynote presentation should have a clear and specific purpose, a well-defined audience, a coherent structure, and a persuasive style.
- A theme presentation or a keynote presentation should also follow the correct methodologies of argumentation, which are the principles and techniques of reasoning and communicating effectively.
- Some of the correct methodologies of argumentation are:

  - **Claim**: A claim is a statement that expresses the main point or conclusion of an argument. A claim should be debatable, relevant, and supported by evidence.
  - **Evidence**: Evidence is the information that supports a claim. Evidence can be factual, statistical, anecdotal, testimonial, or logical. Evidence should be reliable, sufficient, and appropriate for the claim and the audience.
  - **Warrant**: A warrant is the connection or link between the claim and the evidence. A warrant explains how and why the evidence supports the claim. A warrant can be based on common sense, values, principles, or rules.
  - **Counterargument**: A counterargument is an opposing or alternative claim that challenges the original claim. A counterargument should be acknowledged, refuted, or conceded by the speaker.
  - **Rebuttal**: A rebuttal is a response to a counterargument that defends the original claim. A rebuttal should address the weaknesses or flaws of the counterargument and provide additional evidence or reasoning to support the original claim.

- A theme presentation or a keynote presentation should use these methodologies of argumentation to construct a clear, logical, and convincing argument that appeals to the audience's emotions, values, and interests.
- A theme presentation or a keynote presentation should also use effective delivery skills, such as eye contact, gestures, voice, and language, to engage the audience and enhance the message.
