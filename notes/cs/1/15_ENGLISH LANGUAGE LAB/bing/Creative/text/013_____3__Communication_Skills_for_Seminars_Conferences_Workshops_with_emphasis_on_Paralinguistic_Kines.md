### 3. Communication Skills for Seminars/Conferences/Workshops with emphasis on Paralinguistic/Kinesics

- Paralinguistic communication refers to verbal communications that have meaning but are not part of the system of words and grammatical rules we call language.
- Paralinguistic communication includes such elements as pitch, amplitude, rate, and voice quality. Laughter, imitative speech, and prosody are also forms of paralanguage.
- Paralinguistic communication is closely related to kinesic communication, which includes gesture, body posture, and other nonverbal forms of communication. Often, particular gestures are routinely accompanied by particular sounds; such pairings may not have the same meanings when separated.
- Kinesic communication refers to the study of hand, arm, body, and face movements. Specifically, kinesic communication includes the use of gestures, head movements and posture, eye contact, and facial expressions as nonverbal communication.
- Paralinguistic and kinesic communication skills are important for seminars, conferences, and workshops because they can enhance the effectiveness and clarity of the verbal message, convey emotions and attitudes, and create rapport and trust with the audience.
- Some examples of paralinguistic and kinesic communication skills are:

  - Using appropriate pitch, volume, and rate to emphasize key points, avoid monotony, and match the mood and purpose of the speech.
  - Modulating the voice quality to avoid vocal fillers, hesitations, and distractions.
  - Using laughter, imitative speech, and prosody to add humor, interest, and personality to the speech.
  - Using gestures, head movements, and posture to complement, reinforce, or substitute the verbal message, and to show confidence, openness, and engagement.
  - Using eye contact to establish and maintain connection with the audience, to show interest and respect, and to monitor feedback and reactions.
  - Using facial expressions to express emotions, attitudes, and reactions, and to match the tone and content of the speech.

- Paralinguistic and kinesic communication skills should be used appropriately and consistently with the verbal message, the context, and the culture of the audience. Overusing or misusing these skills can lead to confusion, misunderstanding, or offense .
- Paralinguistic and kinesic communication skills can be improved by practicing, observing, and getting feedback from others. Recording and reviewing one's own speech, watching and analyzing effective speakers, and asking for constructive criticism from peers or mentors can help to develop and refine these skills .
