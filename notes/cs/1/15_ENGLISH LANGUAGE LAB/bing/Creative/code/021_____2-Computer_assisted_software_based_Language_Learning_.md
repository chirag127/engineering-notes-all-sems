### 2-Computer assisted software based Language Learning:

Computer assisted software based language learning (CASBLL) is a subset of computer assisted language learning (CALL) that focuses on the use of software applications and programs to facilitate and enhance the learning of languages. CASBLL can be used for various purposes, such as:

- Presenting, reinforcing, and assessing language material to be learned 
- Creating interactive and engaging learning environments and activities 
- Providing feedback and guidance to learners and teachers 
- Developing and practicing language skills and competencies 
- Accessing and exploring authentic and diverse language resources and contexts 

Some examples of CASBLL are:

- Language learning apps, such as Rosetta Stone, Duolingo, Lingoda, Fluenz, etc. that offer structured and personalized courses, exercises, games, and quizzes for various languages
- Language learning software, such as Sign It ASL, that teaches sign language through videos, animations, and interactive features
- Language learning platforms, such as Edmodo, Moodle, Google Classroom, etc. that allow teachers and learners to create and share online courses, assignments, discussions, and resources
- Language learning tools, such as Google Translate, Linguee, Reverso, etc. that provide translation, dictionary, and grammar services for various languages
- Language learning games, such as Influent, MindSnacks, Drops, etc. that use gamification and immersion techniques to teach vocabulary, grammar, and culture for various languages

Some benefits of CASBLL are:

- It can increase learners' motivation, interest, and engagement in language learning 
- It can provide learners with more flexibility, autonomy, and individualization in language learning 
- It can offer learners more opportunities to practice and improve their language skills and competencies 
- It can expose learners to more authentic and diverse language resources and contexts 
- It can enhance learners' intercultural awareness and competence 

Some challenges of CASBLL are:

- It can require learners and teachers to have adequate access to technology, internet, and software 
- It can pose technical, pedagogical, and ethical issues, such as compatibility, reliability, security, privacy, etc. for learners and teachers 
- It can create a gap between learners' and teachers' expectations, preferences, and needs in language learning 
- It can reduce learners' and teachers' interaction and communication in language learning 
- It can affect learners' and teachers' attitudes and beliefs towards language learning
