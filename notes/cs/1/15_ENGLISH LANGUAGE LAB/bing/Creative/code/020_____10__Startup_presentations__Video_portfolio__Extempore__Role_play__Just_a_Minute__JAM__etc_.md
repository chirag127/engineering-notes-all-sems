### 10. Startup presentations, Video portfolio, Extempore, Role play, Just a Minute (JAM) etc.

These are some of the common types of activities that are used to assess the communication skills, creativity, and personality of candidates in various fields. Here are some brief descriptions and tips for each of them:

- **Startup presentations**: These are short pitches that showcase an idea for a new business or product. They usually include the problem, the solution, the market, the revenue model, the team, and the competitive advantage. The goal is to persuade the audience to invest in or support the idea. Some tips for startup presentations are:

  - Use a clear and catchy title that summarizes the main idea.
  - Use visuals, such as graphs, charts, images, or videos, to illustrate the key points and make the presentation more engaging.
  - Use simple and concise language that avoids jargon and technical terms.
  - Highlight the benefits and value proposition of the idea, not just the features and functions.
  - Anticipate and address potential questions or objections from the audience.
  - End with a strong call to action that invites the audience to take the next step.

- **Video portfolio**: This is a collection of short videos that showcase the skills, achievements, and personality of a candidate. It can be used for various purposes, such as applying for a job, a scholarship, or a college admission. The goal is to demonstrate the suitability and uniqueness of the candidate for the desired opportunity. Some tips for video portfolio are:

  - Choose a suitable format, such as a documentary, a montage, a testimonial, or a narrative, that matches the purpose and audience of the portfolio.
  - Plan and script the content of each video, focusing on the most relevant and impressive aspects of the candidate's profile.
  - Use good quality equipment, such as a camera, a microphone, and a tripod, to ensure clear audio and video.
  - Use appropriate lighting, background, and attire to create a professional and appealing appearance.
  - Edit and polish the videos, using transitions, effects, music, and captions, to enhance the clarity and impact of the message.
  - Organize and label the videos, using a portfolio website, a YouTube channel, or a USB drive, to make it easy for the audience to access and navigate the portfolio.

- **Extempore**: This is a spontaneous speech that is given on a topic that is assigned on the spot. It usually lasts for a few minutes and requires the speaker to think and speak quickly and coherently. The goal is to showcase the speaker's knowledge, fluency, and confidence on the topic. Some tips for extempore are:

  - Listen carefully to the topic and note down the key words or ideas.
  - Use a simple and logical structure, such as introduction, body, and conclusion, to organize the speech.
  - Use examples, facts, statistics, or anecdotes to support the main points and make the speech more interesting.
  - Use transitions, such as firstly, secondly, moreover, however, etc., to connect the points and maintain the flow of the speech.
  - Use appropriate tone, volume, pace, and gestures to convey the message and engage the audience.
  - Avoid filler words, such as um, uh, like, etc., and pauses that indicate hesitation or uncertainty.

- **Role play**: This is a simulated situation that involves two or more participants who act out different roles or characters. It can be used for various purposes, such as training, problem-solving, or entertainment. The goal is to demonstrate the ability to perform the assigned role and interact with others in a realistic and effective way. Some tips for role play are:

  - Understand the objective, scenario, and role of the role play and prepare accordingly.
  - Use relevant and accurate information, such as names, dates, facts, etc., to create a credible and consistent role.
  - Use appropriate language, tone, and body language that match the role and the situation.
  - Listen and respond to the other participants, using open-ended questions, feedback, and empathy, to create a dialogue and a rapport.
  - Be flexible and adaptable to the changing circumstances and challenges of the role play.
  - Reflect and evaluate the performance and outcome of the role play and identify the strengths and areas for improvement.
