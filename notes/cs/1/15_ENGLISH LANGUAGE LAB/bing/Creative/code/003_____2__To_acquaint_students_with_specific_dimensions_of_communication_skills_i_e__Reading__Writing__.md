### 2. To acquaint students with specific dimensions of communication skills i.e. Reading, Writing, Listening, Thinking and Speaking.

Communication skills are essential for academic and professional success. They enable students to express themselves clearly, confidently, and persuasively in various situations and contexts. Communication skills can be divided into five specific dimensions: reading, writing, listening, thinking, and speaking. Each dimension has its own purpose, process, and challenges. Here are some brief descriptions of each dimension and some tips on how to improve them.

- Reading: Reading is the process of comprehending and interpreting written texts. It involves decoding symbols, recognizing words, understanding sentences, and grasping the main ideas and details of a text. Reading also requires critical thinking skills, such as evaluating the credibility, relevance, and accuracy of the information, comparing and contrasting different perspectives, and drawing inferences and conclusions. To improve your reading skills, you should:

  - Read a variety of texts, such as books, articles, reports, essays, and fiction, on topics that interest you or relate to your field of study.
  - Use different reading strategies, such as skimming, scanning, previewing, summarizing, and annotating, depending on your purpose and the type of text.
  - Expand your vocabulary by learning new words and their meanings, synonyms, antonyms, and collocations.
  - Ask questions before, during, and after reading to clarify your understanding and stimulate your curiosity.
  - Discuss what you read with others to share your opinions, insights, and questions.

- Writing: Writing is the process of producing and organizing written texts. It involves generating ideas, planning, drafting, revising, editing, and proofreading. Writing also requires rhetorical skills, such as identifying the purpose, audience, and genre of the text, developing a clear and coherent thesis statement, supporting your arguments with evidence and examples, and using appropriate language, tone, and style. To improve your writing skills, you should:

  - Write regularly and practice different types of writing, such as academic, creative, professional, and personal writing.
  - Follow the steps of the writing process and use feedback from yourself, your peers, and your instructors to improve your drafts.
  - Use various writing tools, such as outlines, graphic organizers, dictionaries, thesauruses, and grammar checkers, to help you structure, enrich, and polish your texts.
  - Study the conventions and formats of different genres, such as essays, reports, letters, emails, and blogs, and follow them in your own writing.
  - Read and analyze good examples of writing and learn from their strengths and weaknesses.

- Listening: Listening is the process of receiving and interpreting spoken messages. It involves paying attention, understanding the speaker's words, tone, and body language, and responding appropriately. Listening also requires cognitive skills, such as remembering, analyzing, synthesizing, and evaluating the information, as well as social skills, such as showing interest, respect, and empathy to the speaker. To improve your listening skills, you should:

  - Listen actively and attentively to a variety of speakers, such as teachers, classmates, friends, and media personalities, on topics that interest you or relate to your field of study.
  - Use different listening strategies, such as predicting, questioning, paraphrasing, and summarizing, to enhance your comprehension and retention of the information.
  - Expand your listening vocabulary by learning new words and their pronunciations, meanings, and usage.
  - Ask for clarification or repetition when you do not understand something or miss some information.
  - Give feedback to the speaker by nodding, smiling, making eye contact, and using verbal and non-verbal signals.

- Thinking: Thinking is the process of using your mind to create, explore, and evaluate ideas. It involves applying logic, reasoning, creativity, and imagination to various problems and situations. Thinking also requires metacognitive skills, such as planning, monitoring, and reflecting on your own thinking processes and outcomes. To improve your thinking skills, you should:

  - Think critically and creatively about different topics, issues, and questions, and challenge your own and others' assumptions, beliefs, and opinions.
  - Use different thinking techniques, such as brainstorming, mind mapping, problem-solving, and decision-making, to generate, organize, and select ideas.
  - Develop your analytical and evaluative skills by comparing and contrasting, classifying and categorizing, identifying causes and effects, and making judgments and recommendations.
  - Develop your synthetic and innovative skills by combining and integrating, modifying and adapting, and inventing and designing ideas.
  - Think reflectively and learn from your own and others' experiences, successes, and failures.
