### 1-Interactive Communication Skills:

- Interactive communication skills are the abilities that enable a person to communicate effectively and appropriately with others in various situations, such as conversations, presentations, interviews, meetings, etc.
- Interactive communication skills involve both verbal and non-verbal elements, such as words, tone, body language, facial expressions, gestures, eye contact, etc.
- Interactive communication skills can be improved by practicing the following aspects:

  - Listening: Listening is the process of paying attention to and understanding what the other person is saying. Listening skills can be enhanced by:
    - Showing interest and curiosity in the topic and the speaker.
    - Asking relevant and open-ended questions to clarify or expand the information.
    - Providing feedback and paraphrasing to confirm or summarize the main points.
    - Avoiding distractions and interruptions that can disrupt the flow of communication.
    - Being empathetic and respectful of the speaker's feelings and opinions.

  - Speaking: Speaking is the process of expressing one's thoughts, ideas, opinions, or emotions verbally. Speaking skills can be improved by:
    - Planning and organizing the content and structure of the message.
    - Choosing appropriate words, phrases, and tone that suit the purpose, audience, and context of the communication.
    - Using clear and concise language that avoids ambiguity, jargon, or slang.
    - Modulating the voice, volume, pitch, and pace to convey emphasis, emotion, and interest.
    - Using non-verbal cues, such as eye contact, gestures, facial expressions, and posture to reinforce the message and establish rapport.

  - Interacting: Interacting is the process of engaging in a dialogue or exchange with one or more people. Interacting skills can be developed by:
    - Initiating and maintaining the conversation by using appropriate greetings, introductions, topics, and closures.
    - Adapting the communication style and content to the situation, purpose, and audience.
    - Being aware of and respecting the cultural, social, and personal differences and preferences of the interlocutors.
    - Using positive and constructive language that avoids criticism, blame, or conflict.
    - Giving and receiving feedback, compliments, and suggestions in a polite and constructive manner.
