### 2. Conversational Skills for Interviews under suitable Professional Communication Lab conditions with emphasis on Kinesics

- Conversational skills are the ability to communicate effectively and appropriately with others in a variety of situations, such as interviews.
- Interviews are formal interactions between an interviewer and an interviewee, where the interviewer evaluates the interviewee's suitability for a job, a project, a scholarship, or any other opportunity.
- Professional communication lab conditions are the settings and equipment that simulate a real-life interview scenario, such as a video conference, a phone call, or a face-to-face meeting.
- Kinesics is the study of body language, gestures, facial expressions, eye contact, and posture, and how they convey meaning and emotion in communication.
- Some of the conversational skills for interviews under suitable professional communication lab conditions with emphasis on kinesics are:

  - Prepare well for the interview by researching the organization, the role, and the interviewer, and by rehearsing your answers to common questions.
  - Dress appropriately for the interview, according to the culture and norms of the organization and the role.
  - Arrive on time for the interview, and greet the interviewer with a smile and a firm handshake.
  - Maintain a confident and respectful posture, and avoid slouching, fidgeting, or crossing your arms or legs.
  - Use appropriate gestures to emphasize your points, but avoid excessive or distracting movements, such as pointing, waving, or touching your face or hair.
  - Maintain eye contact with the interviewer, but do not stare or look away too often, as this can indicate nervousness, boredom, or dishonesty.
  - Use facial expressions that match your tone and message, and show interest, enthusiasm, and sincerity.
  - Listen actively to the interviewer, and show that you are paying attention by nodding, smiling, or making verbal feedback, such as "I see", "I understand", or "That's interesting".
  - Speak clearly and confidently, and use a polite and professional language, avoiding slang, jargon, or filler words, such as "um", "like", or "you know".
  - Answer the questions directly and concisely, and provide relevant examples or evidence to support your claims.
  - Ask relevant and insightful questions to the interviewer, and show that you are interested in the organization and the role.
  - Thank the interviewer for their time and consideration, and follow up with a thank-you email or letter.
