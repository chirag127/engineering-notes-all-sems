- Chapter 1: Parts of Speech
  - A part of speech is a category of words that have similar grammatical functions and forms in a sentence.
  - There are eight parts of speech in English: nouns, pronouns, verbs, adjectives, adverbs, prepositions, conjunctions, and interjections.
  - Each part of speech has its own subcategories, such as common and proper nouns, personal and demonstrative pronouns, transitive and intransitive verbs, etc.
  - Each part of speech can also have different forms, such as singular and plural nouns, present and past tense verbs, comparative and superlative adjectives, etc.

- Chapter 2: Sentence Structure
  - A sentence is a group of words that expresses a complete thought and has a subject and a predicate.
  - A subject is the person, place, thing, or idea that the sentence is about.
  - A predicate is the part of the sentence that tells something about the subject, such as an action, a state, or an attribute.
  - A sentence can be classified into four types: declarative, interrogative, imperative, and exclamatory.
  - A sentence can also be classified into two structures: simple and complex.
  - A simple sentence has only one independent clause, which is a group of words that can stand alone as a sentence.
  - A complex sentence has one independent clause and one or more dependent clauses, which are groups of words that cannot stand alone as a sentence and depend on the independent clause for meaning.

- Chapter 3: Tenses
  - A tense is a form of a verb that shows the time of an action or a state in relation to the present, past, or future.
  - There are three main tenses in English: present, past, and future.
  - Each tense has four aspects: simple, continuous, perfect, and perfect continuous.
  - The simple aspect shows a general or habitual action or state, such as "I read books."
  - The continuous aspect shows an ongoing or incomplete action or state, such as "I am reading a book."
  - The perfect aspect shows a completed or past action or state that has a connection to the present, such as "I have read this book."
  - The perfect continuous aspect shows a continuous or repeated action or state that started in the past and has a connection to the present, such as "I have been reading this book for two hours."
  - The tense and aspect of a verb can be changed by adding auxiliary verbs, such as "do", "be", "have", "will", "can", etc.

- Chapter 4: Voice
  - Voice is a grammatical feature that shows the relationship between the subject and the object of a verb in a sentence.
  - There are two voices in English: active and passive.
  - In the active voice, the subject performs the action of the verb, and the object receives the action, such as "She wrote a letter."
  - In the passive voice, the subject receives the action of the verb, and the object performs the action, such as "A letter was written by her."
  - The passive voice is formed by using the verb "be" and the past participle of the main verb, such as "was written".
  - The passive voice is used when the doer of the action is unknown, unimportant, or obvious, or when the focus is on the action or the receiver of the action.

- Chapter 5: Mood
  - Mood is a grammatical feature that shows the attitude or mode of the speaker or writer towards the action or state of the verb in a sentence.
  - There are three moods in English: indicative, subjunctive, and imperative.
