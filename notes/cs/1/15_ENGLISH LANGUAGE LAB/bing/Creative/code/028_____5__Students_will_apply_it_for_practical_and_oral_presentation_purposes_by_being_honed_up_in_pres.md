### 5. Students will apply it for practical and oral presentation purposes by being honed up in presentation skills and voice-dynamics. They will apply techniques for developing interpersonal communication skills and positive attitude leading to their professional competence.

- Presentation skills are the abilities to plan, prepare, deliver, and evaluate effective oral presentations using appropriate language, visuals, and technology.
- Voice-dynamics are the aspects of vocal delivery that affect the audience's attention, interest, and understanding, such as volume, pitch, tone, pace, and pauses.
- Interpersonal communication skills are the abilities to interact effectively with others in various contexts and situations, such as listening, speaking, questioning, clarifying, empathizing, and negotiating.
- Positive attitude is the tendency to have a favorable outlook and approach towards oneself, others, and situations, such as being optimistic, confident, respectful, and cooperative.

Some of the benefits of applying these skills and attitude for practical and oral presentation purposes are:

- They can help students to convey their ideas clearly, persuasively, and confidently to various audiences, such as peers, teachers, employers, and clients.
- They can help students to engage and motivate their listeners, and to adapt to their feedback and expectations.
- They can help students to build rapport and trust with their interlocutors, and to resolve conflicts and misunderstandings constructively.
- They can help students to enhance their self-esteem and self-efficacy, and to overcome their fear and anxiety of public speaking.
- They can help students to develop their professional competence and career prospects, as these skills and attitude are highly valued and sought after in the workplace.

Some of the techniques for developing these skills and attitude are:

- Practicing and rehearsing the presentation beforehand, and seeking feedback from others.
- Using a clear and concise structure, and an engaging and appropriate style for the presentation.
- Using visual aids, such as slides, charts, graphs, and images, to support and illustrate the main points.
- Using voice-dynamics, such as varying the volume, pitch, tone, and pace, and using pauses and emphasis, to convey the message effectively and expressively.
- Using eye contact, facial expressions, gestures, and body language, to establish rapport and credibility with the audience.
- Listening actively and attentively to the audience, and responding to their questions and comments politely and constructively.
- Asking open-ended and probing questions, and clarifying and summarizing the information, to ensure mutual understanding and avoid confusion.
- Showing empathy and respect for the feelings, opinions, and perspectives of others, and acknowledging and appreciating their contributions.
- Being optimistic and confident about the outcome of the presentation, and focusing on the positive aspects and opportunities of the situation.
- Being respectful and cooperative with the co-presenters, and supporting and complementing each other's strengths and weaknesses.
