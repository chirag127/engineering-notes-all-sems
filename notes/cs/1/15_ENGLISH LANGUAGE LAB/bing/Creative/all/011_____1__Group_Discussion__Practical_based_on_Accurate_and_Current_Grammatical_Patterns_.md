# 1. Group Discussion: Practical based on Accurate and Current Grammatical Patterns.

- A group discussion is a form of oral communication where a group of participants exchange their views on a given topic in a structured and interactive manner.
- A group discussion can have various purposes, such as to assess the participants' knowledge, skills, attitudes, opinions, or personality traits; to generate ideas, solutions, or recommendations; to stimulate critical thinking, creativity, or collaboration; or to enhance learning, understanding, or awareness of a subject matter.
- A group discussion requires the participants to use accurate and current grammatical patterns in their speech, as well as appropriate vocabulary, pronunciation, intonation, and non-verbal cues.
- Accurate and current grammatical patterns are the rules and conventions that govern the structure, form, and meaning of sentences and phrases in a language. They include the use of nouns, verbs, adjectives, adverbs, pronouns, articles, prepositions, conjunctions, and other word classes; the formation of tenses, aspects, moods, voices, and other verb forms; the agreement of number, gender, case, and person between words; the word order and punctuation of sentences; and the use of clauses, phrases, modifiers, and other sentence elements.
- Current grammatical patterns are those that reflect the contemporary usage and norms of a language, as opposed to outdated, obsolete, or archaic forms. They may vary depending on the context, purpose, audience, and style of communication, as well as the regional, social, or professional variations of a language.
- To use accurate and current grammatical patterns in a group discussion, the participants should:

  - Be familiar with the topic and the relevant terminology, concepts, facts, and arguments.
  - Plan and organize their thoughts and points in a logical and coherent manner.
  - Use clear, concise, and precise language that conveys their meaning and intention effectively.
  - Avoid grammatical errors, such as incorrect verb forms, subject-verb agreement, pronoun reference, word order, punctuation, etc.
  - Avoid informal, slang, or colloquial expressions that may be inappropriate, ambiguous, or offensive in a formal or academic setting.
  - Use a variety of grammatical structures and devices, such as active and passive voice, simple and complex sentences, parallelism, coordination, subordination, etc., to create interest, emphasis, contrast, or connection between ideas.
  - Use appropriate modal verbs, conditionals, and other forms to express possibility, probability, necessity, obligation, permission, advice, suggestion, request, opinion, preference, etc.
  - Use appropriate tense and aspect to indicate the time and duration of actions, events, or states, as well as their relation to other actions, events, or states in the past, present, or future.
  - Use appropriate mood and voice to indicate the attitude, emotion, or tone of the speaker or the writer, such as indicative, imperative, interrogative, exclamatory, declarative, etc.
  - Use appropriate intonation, stress, and rhythm to convey the meaning, emphasis, or attitude of the speaker, as well as to mark the boundaries, segments, and patterns of sentences and phrases.
  - Use appropriate non-verbal cues, such as eye contact, facial expressions, gestures, posture, and body language, to complement, reinforce, or modify the verbal message, as well as to show interest, attention, respect, or agreement with the other participants.
