# 4. Presentation Skills for Technical Paper/Project Reports/ proposals based on proper Stress and Intonation Mechanics

- Presentation skills are the abilities to communicate effectively and confidently with an audience, using appropriate verbal and non-verbal cues, visual aids, and structure.
- Technical paper/project reports/proposals are types of academic or professional documents that present the results of research, analysis, design, or implementation of a specific topic or problem.
- Stress and intonation mechanics are the aspects of speech that convey the emphasis, tone, and mood of the speaker, using variations in loudness, pitch, and duration of syllables and words.
- Proper stress and intonation mechanics can enhance the clarity, credibility, and persuasiveness of a presentation, as well as the engagement and interest of the audience.
- Some tips for using proper stress and intonation mechanics in a presentation are:

  - Identify the main points, keywords, and transitions in your presentation, and mark them with symbols or colors to indicate the level of stress and intonation you want to use.
  - Use primary stress (the strongest emphasis) for the most important information, such as the main idea, the conclusion, or the recommendation. Use secondary stress (a weaker emphasis) for supporting details, examples, or explanations. Use no stress or reduced stress for less important information, such as articles, prepositions, or conjunctions.
  - Use rising intonation (a higher pitch) for questions, incomplete sentences, or lists. Use falling intonation (a lower pitch) for statements, complete sentences, or final items in a list. Use rising-falling intonation (a combination of both) for contrast, surprise, or emphasis.
  - Use pauses (a brief silence) to separate different parts of your presentation, such as the introduction, the body, and the conclusion. Use pauses also to signal a change of topic, a transition, or a summary. Use pauses to create suspense, to emphasize a point, or to allow the audience to process the information.
  - Practice your presentation aloud, using a recorder or a mirror, and pay attention to your stress and intonation mechanics. Adjust them as needed to improve your clarity, credibility, and persuasiveness. Ask for feedback from a friend, a colleague, or a teacher, and incorporate their suggestions.
