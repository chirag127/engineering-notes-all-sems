### 1. Group Discussion: Practical based on Accurate and Current Grammatical Patterns.

- A group discussion is a form of oral communication where a group of people exchange their views and opinions on a given topic.
- A group discussion can have various purposes, such as problem-solving, decision-making, brainstorming, information-sharing, or learning.
- A group discussion can also be used to assess the communication skills, interpersonal skills, critical thinking skills, and leadership skills of the participants.
- To participate effectively in a group discussion, one should follow some basic rules and etiquette, such as:
  - Be prepared with the topic and do some research beforehand.
  - Listen actively and respectfully to others and do not interrupt or dominate the conversation.
  - Speak clearly, confidently, and politely and use appropriate language and tone.
  - Express your views and opinions with relevant facts, examples, and evidence and avoid vague or ambiguous statements.
  - Respect the views and opinions of others and do not criticize or ridicule them.
  - Ask questions and seek clarifications when needed and do not make assumptions or jump to conclusions.
  - Agree or disagree with others based on logical reasoning and not on personal bias or emotions.
  - Summarize the main points and conclusions of the discussion and acknowledge the contributions of others.
- To participate effectively in a group discussion, one should also use accurate and current grammatical patterns, such as:
  - Use the correct tense, voice, mood, and aspect to express the time, manner, and attitude of the action or state.
  - Use the correct subject-verb agreement, pronoun-antecedent agreement, and noun-number agreement to avoid confusion and inconsistency.
  - Use the correct word order, punctuation, and capitalization to ensure clarity and coherence.
  - Use the correct spelling, vocabulary, and idioms to convey the intended meaning and avoid errors and misunderstandings.
  - Use the correct sentence structure, modifiers, and connectors to form complex and compound sentences and avoid fragments and run-ons.
  - Use the correct parallelism, comparison, and contrast to show the relationship and difference between ideas and avoid illogical and faulty constructions.
  - Use the correct style, tone, and register to suit the purpose, audience, and context of the discussion and avoid informal, slang, or offensive language.
