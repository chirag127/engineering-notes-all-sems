### 4. Students will be made to evaluate the correct and error-free writing by being well-versed in rules of English grammar and cultivate relevant technical style of communication &presentation at their work place and also for academic uses.

- Correct and error-free writing is essential for effective communication and presentation in any context, whether it is academic, professional, or personal.
- To achieve correct and error-free writing, students need to be well-versed in the rules of English grammar, such as syntax, punctuation, spelling, vocabulary, and usage.
- Students also need to cultivate a relevant technical style of communication and presentation, which means adapting their language, tone, format, and structure to suit the purpose, audience, and context of their writing.
- Some examples of technical style of communication and presentation are:
  - Writing a formal report or proposal for a project or research, using headings, subheadings, bullet points, tables, graphs, and references.
  - Writing an email or a letter to a client, colleague, or supervisor, using a polite and professional tone, clear and concise language, and appropriate salutations and closings.
  - Writing a blog post or a social media post for a specific topic or niche, using an engaging and informative tone, catchy and relevant titles, and multimedia elements such as images, videos, or links.
  - Writing a resume or a cover letter for a job application, using a persuasive and confident tone, highlighting the skills, achievements, and qualifications that match the job requirements, and using action verbs and keywords.
- To evaluate their own and others' writing, students need to apply the rules of English grammar and the relevant technical style of communication and presentation, and check for the following aspects:
  - Accuracy: The writing should be free of grammatical, spelling, punctuation, and vocabulary errors, and should convey the intended meaning and message clearly and correctly.
  - Coherence: The writing should have a logical and consistent flow of ideas, arguments, and information, and should use transitions, connectors, and signposts to guide the reader.
  - Relevance: The writing should address the topic, purpose, and audience of the writing task, and should include only the necessary and appropriate details and evidence to support the main points.
  - Organization: The writing should have a clear and effective structure, with an introduction, a body, and a conclusion, and should use headings, subheadings, and paragraphs to organize the content.
  - Style: The writing should use a suitable tone, language, format, and structure for the type and genre of the writing task, and should avoid informal, vague, or inappropriate expressions.
