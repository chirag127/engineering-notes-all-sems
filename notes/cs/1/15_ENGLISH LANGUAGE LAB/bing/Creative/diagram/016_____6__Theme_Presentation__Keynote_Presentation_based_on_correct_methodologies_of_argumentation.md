### 6. Theme Presentation/ Keynote Presentation based on correct methodologies of argumentation

- A theme presentation or keynote presentation is a type of speech that aims to convey a central message or idea to the audience, usually in a persuasive or inspirational manner.
- A theme presentation or keynote presentation should have a clear and specific purpose, a well-defined audience, a coherent structure, and a strong delivery.
- A theme presentation or keynote presentation should also follow the correct methodologies of argumentation, which are the principles and techniques of reasoning and persuading effectively.
- Some of the correct methodologies of argumentation are:

  - **Claim**: A claim is a statement that expresses the main point or position of the speaker. A claim should be debatable, relevant, and supported by evidence.
  - **Evidence**: Evidence is the information or data that supports the claim. Evidence can be factual, statistical, anecdotal, testimonial, or analogical. Evidence should be reliable, sufficient, and appropriate for the audience and the topic.
  - **Warrant**: A warrant is the logical connection or explanation that shows how the evidence supports the claim. A warrant can be based on common sense, values, principles, or rules. A warrant should be valid, sound, and consistent with the claim and the evidence.
  - **Counterargument**: A counterargument is a statement that challenges or opposes the claim or a part of the argument. A counterargument should be acknowledged, refuted, or conceded by the speaker, depending on its strength and relevance.
  - **Rebuttal**: A rebuttal is a response to the counterargument that defends or restates the claim or the argument. A rebuttal should address the specific points of the counterargument and provide counter-evidence or counter-warrants to weaken or refute it.

- A theme presentation or keynote presentation should use these methodologies of argumentation to construct a clear, logical, and persuasive argument that supports the central message or idea of the speech.
- A theme presentation or keynote presentation should also use rhetorical devices, such as ethos, pathos, and logos, to appeal to the audience's emotions, values, and logic, and to enhance the credibility, clarity, and impact of the speech.
