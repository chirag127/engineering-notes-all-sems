### 7. Individual Speech Delivery/Conferencing with skills to defend Interjections/Quizzes.

- Individual speech delivery is the process of presenting a speech to an audience, either in person or online, using verbal and nonverbal communication skills.
- Conferencing is the process of engaging in a dialogue with an audience, either in person or online, using verbal and nonverbal communication skills, to clarify, elaborate, or respond to questions or feedback.
- Skills to defend interjections/quizzes are the abilities to handle interruptions, challenges, or inquiries from the audience during or after a speech or a conference, using verbal and nonverbal communication skills, to maintain credibility, rapport, and coherence.

Some tips for individual speech delivery/conferencing with skills to defend interjections/quizzes are:

- Prepare and practice your speech or conference beforehand, anticipating possible questions or objections from the audience and preparing appropriate responses.
- Use a clear, confident, and expressive voice, adjusting your volume, pitch, rate, and tone according to the context and the audience.
- Use appropriate body language, such as eye contact, gestures, posture, and facial expressions, to convey your message and connect with the audience.
- Use visual aids, such as slides, charts, graphs, or images, to support your speech or conference, making sure they are relevant, clear, and easy to follow.
- Use transitions, signposts, and summaries to organize your speech or conference and guide the audience through your main points.
- Use rhetorical devices, such as repetition, parallelism, contrast, or analogy, to emphasize your key ideas and persuade the audience.
- Use evidence, such as facts, statistics, examples, or testimonials, to support your claims and arguments, citing your sources and avoiding plagiarism.
- Use ethical principles, such as honesty, respect, and responsibility, to communicate your message and avoid offensive or misleading language.
- Be respectful and attentive to the audience, listening to their questions or feedback and responding politely and constructively.
- Be flexible and adaptable, adjusting your speech or conference according to the audience's reactions, interests, or needs, and handling unexpected situations or technical difficulties with grace and professionalism.
