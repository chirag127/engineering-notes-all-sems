### 8. Argumentative Skills/Role Play Presentation with Stress and Intonation.

Argumentative skills are essential in both personal and professional settings. Whether you are debating a topic with friends or presenting a proposal to your boss, the way you present your argument can make all the difference in whether or not you are successful.

One key aspect of argumentative skills is the ability to use stress and intonation effectively. This can help you emphasize important points in your argument, convey confidence and conviction, and engage your audience.

Here are some tips for using stress and intonation in your argumentative presentations:

1. Vary your pitch: Use a range of high and low pitches to keep your audience engaged and interested. Avoid speaking in a monotone voice, as this can come across as boring and unconvincing.

2. Emphasize important words: Use stress to highlight key points in your argument. This can help your audience remember your main ideas and understand the significance of your arguments.

3. Use pauses: Pausing between sentences or ideas can help you create suspense and drama in your argument. It can also give your audience time to process what you are saying and consider your points.

4. Speak clearly and confidently: Use a clear and confident tone of voice to convey your expertise and authority on the topic. This can help you persuade your audience and gain their trust and respect.

5. Practice, practice, practice: The more you practice your argumentative presentation, the more comfortable you will become with using stress and intonation effectively. Record yourself and listen back to identify areas where you can improve.

In addition to these tips, role-playing can be a useful tool for developing your argumentative skills. By practicing arguments with a partner or in a group, you can get feedback on your presentation style and learn to adapt to different types of audiences.

Remember that argumentative skills are not just about winning a debate; they are about presenting your ideas in a clear and compelling way. By using stress and intonation effectively, you can make your arguments more persuasive and engaging, and increase your chances of success.
