### 11. Business Correspondence and Report Writing by Prof. R.C. Sharma & Krishna Mohan, Tata McGraw Hill & Co. Ltd., 2001, New Delhi.

Business correspondence and report writing are important aspects of communication in the corporate world. They are used to convey information, ideas, and opinions among individuals and organizations. Here are some key points to keep in mind when writing business correspondence and reports:

#### Business Correspondence:
- Business correspondence should be formal and professional in tone, and should avoid the use of slang or informal language.
- The purpose of the correspondence should be clearly stated in the opening paragraph.
- The main body of the correspondence should provide details about the purpose, including any relevant background information, facts, or figures.
- The conclusion should summarize the main points and include any necessary action items or next steps.
- Correspondence should be proofread carefully for errors in spelling, grammar, and punctuation before sending.

#### Types of Business Correspondence:
- Letters: Formal letters are used to convey important information or requests to individuals or organizations. They should follow a standard format, including a letterhead, date, address, salutation, body, and closing.
- Memos: Memos are internal communications within an organization, and are typically used to convey information or requests to colleagues or superiors. They should follow a standard format, including a heading, body, and signature.
- Emails: Emails are a common form of business correspondence and should be professional in tone. They should include a clear subject line, salutation, body, and closing.

#### Report Writing:
- Reports should be well-structured and organized, with a clear introduction, body, and conclusion.
- The purpose of the report should be clearly stated in the introduction, along with any background information or context.
- The body of the report should provide details about the subject, including any relevant data, analysis, or conclusions.
- The conclusion should summarize the main points of the report and provide any necessary recommendations or next steps.
- Reports should be proofread carefully for errors in spelling, grammar, and punctuation before submitting.

#### Types of Reports:
- Formal Reports: Formal reports are used to convey important information or analysis to senior management or external stakeholders. They should follow a standard format, including a title page, table of contents, executive summary, introduction, main body, conclusion, and recommendations.
- Informal Reports: Informal reports are used for internal communication within an organization and are typically shorter and less formal than formal reports. They may include a brief introduction, body, and conclusion, and should still be well-organized and clearly structured.
