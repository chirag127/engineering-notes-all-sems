### 1. Group Discussion: Practical based on Accurate and Current Grammatical Patterns.

1. Analyze sample conversations and identify grammatical errors or inaccuracies. Discuss ways to correct them.
- Incorrect: Me and John went to the movies. (Should be: John and I went to the movies.)
- Incorrect: I was sat in the front row. (Should be: I sat in the front row.)

2. Evaluate common grammatical issues like subject-verb agreement, pronoun references, misplaced modifiers, etc. and provide solutions.
- Incorrect: The team have finished their project. (Should be: The team has finished their project.)
- Incorrect: Everyone brought their lunch to the office. (Should be: Everyone brought his or her lunch to the office.)

3. Discuss the importance of using proper grammar in various contexts like workplace, academics, publications, etc. Explain how correct grammar affects clarity and credibility.
- Correct grammar portrays a professional image, conveys credibility, and ensures clear communication.
- In formal contexts, grammatical errors can be seen as a reflection of poor skills or work quality.

4. Stay up-to-date with the changes and evolution of grammar rules. For example, certain nouns can be used as verbs now or collective nouns can take either singular or plural verbs. Discuss such adaptations.
- Examples: to medal (win a medal), to podium (finish on the podium), team wins vs. team win.
- Grammar rules evolve with usage so it's important to be aware of such accepted changes. However, inconsistencies or illogical uses should still be avoided.
