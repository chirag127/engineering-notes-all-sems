### 11. Business Correspondence and Report Writing by Prof. R.C. Sharma & Krishna Mohan, Tata McGraw Hill & Co. Ltd., 2001, New Delhi.

- Business correspondence refers to the exchange of letters and messages for business purposes. It is a vital part of business communication and helps in conducting business transactions and building business relationships.
- The key aspects of effective business correspondence are:
    - Clear and concise language: Use simple and straightforward language to convey the message. Avoid verbosity and complex sentences.
    - Appropriate style: Choose an appropriate style based on the recipient and purpose, e.g. formal style for official letters or informal style for letters to colleagues.
    - Correct structure: Structure the letter correctly with essential sections like subject line, greeting, body, and closing. Follow standard formats for different types of letters.
    - Polite and respectful tone: Maintain a polite, courteous, and respectful tone to appear professional and build goodwill.
    - Accuracy: Ensure that the letter is error-free and factually accurate with correct names, dates, numbers, etc. to avoid misunderstandings or legal issues.
- Business reports are formal documents that provide information and present analysis and recommendations to decision-makers. Key tips for writing effective business reports are:
    - Focus on the readers' needs: Identify the readers' requirements and tailor the content and language to meet their needs.
    - Structured layout: Use a standard structure with sections for executive summary, introduction, methods, results, discussion, and conclusion. This makes the key insights easy to understand and navigate.
    - Concise writing: Keep the writing tight and avoid unnecessary elaborations. Use simple and clear language to get the points across.
    - Data-driven: Base the analysis and recommendations on factual data and evidence. Provide data, statistics, examples, quotes, citations, etc. to support the key points.
    - Specific and action-oriented: Give specific and practical recommendations that can be acted upon. Mention what needs to be done, by whom, and by when.
