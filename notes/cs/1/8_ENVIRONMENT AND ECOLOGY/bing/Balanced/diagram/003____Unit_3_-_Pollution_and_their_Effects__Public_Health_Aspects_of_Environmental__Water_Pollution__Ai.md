## Unit 3 - Pollution and their Effects; Public Health Aspects of Environmental; Water Pollution, Air Pollution, Soil Pollution, Noise Pollution, Solid Waste Management

- Pollution is the introduction of harmful substances or energy into the environment that cause adverse effects on living organisms and natural resources.
- Pollution can be classified into different types based on the source, the medium, and the effect of the pollutants. Some of the common types are water pollution, air pollution, soil pollution, noise pollution, and solid waste management.
- Water pollution is the contamination of water bodies such as rivers, lakes, oceans, and groundwater by human activities or natural processes. Water pollution can affect the quality and availability of water for drinking, irrigation, recreation, and ecosystem services. Some of the causes of water pollution are industrial effluents, agricultural runoff, sewage disposal, oil spills, mining, and thermal pollution.
- Air pollution is the presence of harmful substances or particles in the atmosphere that can affect human health and the environment. Air pollution can be caused by natural sources such as volcanic eruptions, dust storms, and wildfires, or by anthropogenic sources such as fossil fuel combustion, vehicle emissions, industrial activities, and biomass burning. Some of the effects of air pollution are respiratory diseases, cardiovascular diseases, asthma, cancer, and climate change.
- Soil pollution is the degradation of soil quality due to the presence of harmful substances or agents. Soil pollution can result from direct contact with polluted soil or from indirect contact with other media, such as water or food, that are affected by the soil. Some of the causes of soil pollution are over-irrigation, pesticide use, sewage dumping, garbage disposal, deforestation, and mining. Some of the effects of soil pollution are reduced soil fertility, loss of biodiversity, soil erosion, and contamination of crops and groundwater.
- Noise pollution is the excessive or unwanted sound that can cause annoyance, stress, hearing loss, and other health problems. Noise pollution can be generated by various sources, such as traffic, construction, aircraft, machinery, and entertainment. Some of the effects of noise pollution are sleep disturbance, hypertension, cardiovascular diseases, cognitive impairment, and psychological disorders.
- Solid waste management is the collection, transportation, treatment, and disposal of solid wastes that are generated by human activities or natural processes. Solid waste management is essential to prevent the spread of diseases, reduce environmental pollution, conserve resources, and improve the quality of life. Some of the methods of solid waste management are recycling, composting, incineration, landfilling, and bioremediation.
