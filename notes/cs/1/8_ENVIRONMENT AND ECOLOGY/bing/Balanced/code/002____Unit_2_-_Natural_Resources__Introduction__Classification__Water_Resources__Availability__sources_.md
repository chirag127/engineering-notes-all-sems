- Introduction: Natural resources are substances or materials from nature that humans use to sustain life. These resources are naturally available on Earth without any human action. Natural resources have economic, ecological, scientific, aesthetic, and cultural values in human life.
- Classification: Natural resources can be classified into different categories based on their origin, level of development and uses, stock or deposits, and their distribution. On the basis of their origin, natural resources can be classified into living or biotic and non-living or abiotic resources. Living or biotic resources are those that come from living things or organic materials, such as plants, animals, and microorganisms. Non-living or abiotic resources are those that come from nonliving or inorganic materials, such as minerals, metals, water, and air. On the basis of their level of development and uses, natural resources can be classified into renewable and non-renewable resources. Renewable resources are those that can be regenerated or replenished by natural processes within a reasonable time, such as sunlight, wind, water, and biomass. Non-renewable resources are those that cannot be regenerated or replenished by natural processes within a reasonable time, such as fossil fuels, coal, oil, natural gas, and nuclear energy. On the basis of their stock or deposits, natural resources can be classified into actual and potential resources. Actual resources are those that have been surveyed, quantified, and are being used in the present, such as coal mines, oil wells, and hydroelectric dams. Potential resources are those that have not been surveyed, quantified, or used in the present, but are expected to be available and usable in the future, such as geothermal energy, tidal energy, and solar energy. On the basis of their distribution, natural resources can be classified into ubiquitous and localized resources. Ubiquitous resources are those that are found everywhere or in many places on Earth, such as air, sunlight, and water. Localized resources are those that are found only in certain regions or places on Earth, such as petroleum, iron ore, and diamonds.
- Water Resources: Water is one of the most essential and abundant natural resources on Earth. It covers about 71% of the Earth's surface and is vital for all forms of life. Water resources refer to the sources and quality of water that are available for human use and consumption. Water resources can be classified into surface water and groundwater. Surface water is the water that is found on the Earth's surface, such as rivers, lakes, streams, ponds, and oceans. Groundwater is the water that is stored beneath the Earth's surface, such as aquifers, wells, and springs. The availability of water resources depends on various factors, such as precipitation, evaporation, runoff, infiltration, and human activities. The quality of water resources depends on various factors, such as pollution, contamination, salinity, hardness, and pH. Water resources are essential for various purposes, such as drinking, irrigation, sanitation, hygiene, industry, transportation, recreation, and power generation.
- Water Borne and Water Induced Diseases: Water borne diseases are those that are caused by the ingestion of contaminated water or food that has been washed or prepared with contaminated water. Some of the common water borne diseases are cholera, typhoid, dysentery, hepatitis, and giardiasis. Water induced diseases are those that are caused by the exposure to contaminated water or vectors that breed in water. Some of the common water induced diseases are malaria, dengue, filariasis, and schistosomiasis. Water borne and water induced diseases can be prevented by improving the quality and quantity of water resources, ensuring safe water supply and sanitation, practicing good hygiene and personal care, and controlling the vectors and reservoirs of infection.
