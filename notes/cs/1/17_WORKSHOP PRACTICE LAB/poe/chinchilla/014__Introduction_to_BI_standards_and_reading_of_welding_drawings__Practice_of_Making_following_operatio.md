#### Introduction to BI Standards and Reading of Welding Drawings. Practice of Making following Operations: Butt Joint, Lap Joint, TIG Welding, MIG Welding

Welding is a process of joining two or more metals together by heating them to a high temperature and applying pressure. The process involves the use of various welding techniques, which are widely used in the manufacturing and construction industries. In order to ensure that welding is done correctly and safely, it is important to follow the relevant standards and guidelines. In this study material, we will discuss BI standards and reading of welding drawings, as well as practice making the following operations: butt joint, lap joint, TIG welding, and MIG welding.

#### BI Standards

BI (British Institute) standards are a set of guidelines that provide recommendations for the welding of different types of metals. These standards are important because they ensure that welding is done safely, efficiently, and according to industry best practices. Some of the key BI standards that are relevant to welding include:

- BS EN ISO 15614-1: Specification and qualification of welding procedures for metallic materials - Welding procedure test - Part 1: Arc and gas welding of steels and arc welding of nickel and nickel alloys
- BS EN ISO 9606-1: Qualification testing of welders - Fusion welding - Part 1: Steels
- BS EN 1090-2: Execution of steel structures and aluminium structures - Part 2: Technical requirements for steel structures

#### Reading Welding Drawings

Welding drawings are used to communicate important information about the welding process, including the type of weld, the size of the weld, and the location of the weld. In order to read welding drawings effectively, it is important to understand the following:

- Weld symbols: These symbols are used to indicate the type of weld that is required (e.g. fillet weld, butt weld, etc.).
- Dimensions: These indicate the size of the weld, as well as the distance between the welds and other features on the drawing.
- Material requirements: These indicate the type of metal that is being welded, as well as any other materials that are required (e.g. flux, filler wire, etc.).
- Welding process: This indicates the type of welding process that is required (e.g. TIG welding, MIG welding, etc.).

#### Butt Joint

A butt joint is a type of joint where two pieces of metal are joined together by welding the edges together. This type of joint is commonly used in the manufacturing of pipes, tanks, and other structures. To make a butt joint, the following steps must be followed:

- Prepare the edges of the metal so that they are clean and free from any contaminants.
- Position the two pieces of metal so that the edges are touching.
- Apply heat to the edges of the metal using a welding torch.
- Add filler metal to the joint as required.
- Allow the joint to cool and solidify.

#### Lap Joint

A lap joint is a type of joint where two pieces of metal are overlapped and welded together. This type of joint is commonly used in the manufacturing of sheet metal products, such as car bodies and airplane wings. To make a lap joint, the following steps must be followed:

- Prepare the edges of the metal so that they are clean and free from any contaminants.
- Position the two pieces of metal so that they are overlapping.
- Apply heat to the edges of the metal using a welding torch.
- Add filler metal to the joint as required.
- Allow the joint to cool and solidify.

#### TIG Welding

TIG (Tungsten Inert Gas) welding is a type of welding that uses a non-consumable tungsten electrode to produce the weld. This type of welding is commonly used in the manufacturing of high-quality products, such as aircraft parts and medical equipment. To perform TIG welding, the following steps must be followed:

- Prepare the metal so that it is clean and free from any contaminants.
- Position the metal so that it is in the correct location for the weld.
- Begin the arc by striking the tungsten electrode against the metal.
- Add filler metal to the weld as required.
- Control the heat and welding speed to produce a high-quality weld.

#### MIG Welding

MIG (Metal Inert Gas) welding is a type of welding that uses a consumable wire electrode to produce the weld. This type of welding is commonly used in the manufacturing of automotive parts and other metal products. To perform MIG welding, the following steps must be followed:

- Prepare the metal so that it is clean and free from any contaminants.
- Position the metal so that it is in the correct location for the weld.
- Begin the arc by feeding the wire electrode through the welding torch.
- Control the heat and welding speed to produce a high-quality weld.
- Add shielding gas to protect the weld from contamination.
