### 6 Moulding and Casting Shop 6 Hrs

Moulding and casting are two processes that are often used in the manufacturing of products. Both processes involve creating a negative space in order to create a positive product. However, there are some key differences between moulding and casting that you should be aware of.

- Moulding is the process of shaping a material by using a rigid frame or model called a mould. The mould can be made of various materials such as wood, metal, plastic, or clay. The material to be moulded is usually a liquid or a soft solid that can flow into the mould cavity. Examples of moulding materials are plaster, clay, wax, resin, and rubber. Moulding can be used to create decorative and architectural elements, such as trim and moulding , or functional parts, such as gears and valves.
- Casting is the process of pouring a liquid material into a mould and letting it harden. The liquid material is usually a metal, such as bronze, iron, or aluminum, or a polymer, such as epoxy, polyester, or silicone. Casting can be used to create complex shapes and details that are difficult to achieve by moulding, such as sculptures, jewelry, tools, and more . Casting can also be used to replicate existing objects by making a mould of them first.

Some of the advantages of moulding and casting are:

- They can produce multiple copies of the same product with high accuracy and consistency.
- They can reduce the waste of material and energy by using only the required amount of material and reusing the moulds.
- They can create products with intricate and detailed designs that are difficult to make by other methods.

Some of the disadvantages of moulding and casting are:

- They require a lot of skill and experience to design and make the moulds and cast the products.
- They can be affected by environmental factors such as temperature, humidity, and air bubbles that can cause defects and flaws in the products.
- They can be limited by the size and shape of the moulds and the properties of the materials.
