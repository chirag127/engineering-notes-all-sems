#### Introduction to BI standards and reading of welding drawings. Practice of Making following operations Butt Joint Lap Joint TIG Welding MIG Welding

- BI standards are the British standards for welding and fabrication. They provide specifications, guidelines, and best practices for various welding processes, materials, and applications.
- Reading of welding drawings is the skill of interpreting the symbols, dimensions, and annotations on a welding drawing. A welding drawing shows the design, location, orientation, and size of the welds, as well as the type of welding process, filler material, and quality requirements.
- A butt joint is a type of joint where two pieces of metal are joined along their edges by welding. The edges can be square, beveled, or grooved, depending on the thickness and angle of the joint. A butt joint can be welded from one or both sides, depending on the access and penetration required.
- A lap joint is a type of joint where two pieces of metal are overlapped and welded together. The overlap can be partial or full, depending on the strength and appearance of the joint. A lap joint can be welded from one or both sides, depending on the access and penetration required.
- TIG welding, or tungsten inert gas welding, is a type of welding process that uses a non-consumable tungsten electrode and an inert gas (usually argon) to create an arc between the electrode and the workpiece. The arc melts the base metal and the filler metal (if used) and forms a weld pool. TIG welding is suitable for thin and delicate materials, such as stainless steel, aluminum, and copper. TIG welding requires high skill and precision, as the welder has to control the arc, the filler metal, and the weld pool simultaneously.
- MIG welding, or metal inert gas welding, is a type of welding process that uses a consumable wire electrode and an inert gas (usually argon) to create an arc between the electrode and the workpiece. The arc melts the wire electrode and the base metal and forms a weld pool. MIG welding is suitable for thick and heavy materials, such as carbon steel, low-alloy steel, and cast iron. MIG welding is faster and easier than TIG welding, as the welder only has to control the wire feed and the arc.
