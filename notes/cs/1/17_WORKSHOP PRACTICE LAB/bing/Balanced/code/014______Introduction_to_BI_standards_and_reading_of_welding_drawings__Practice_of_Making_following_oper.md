#### Introduction to BI standards and reading of welding drawings. Practice of Making following operations Butt Joint Lap Joint TIG Welding MIG Welding

- BI stands for Bureau of Indian Standards, which is the national standards body of India. It is responsible for setting and maintaining standards for various products, processes and services in the country.
- Welding drawings are technical drawings that show the details of welding joints, symbols, dimensions, materials, procedures and quality requirements for a specific welding project.
- Reading of welding drawings involves interpreting the information given on the drawing, such as the type and location of welds, the size and shape of weld beads, the welding position, the welding process, the filler metal, the preheat and postheat treatment, the inspection and testing methods, and the acceptance criteria.
- A butt joint is a type of joint where two pieces of metal are joined along a single edge or plane. The joint can be welded from one or both sides, depending on the thickness and accessibility of the metal pieces. A butt joint can be square, single-V, double-V, single-U, double-U, single-J, double-J, or single-bevel, depending on the shape of the groove prepared for welding.
- A lap joint is a type of joint where two pieces of metal are overlapped and welded together. The joint can be welded from one or both sides, depending on the thickness and accessibility of the metal pieces. A lap joint can be fillet, plug, slot, or spot, depending on the shape and location of the welds.
- TIG welding stands for tungsten inert gas welding, which is a welding process that uses a non-consumable tungsten electrode to create an arc between the electrode and the workpiece. The arc melts the metal and forms a weld pool, which is protected from the atmosphere by an inert gas, such as argon or helium. TIG welding can be used to weld a variety of metals, such as steel, stainless steel, aluminum, copper, titanium, and nickel alloys. TIG welding requires a high level of skill and precision, as the welder has to control the arc, the filler metal, and the gas flow simultaneously.
- MIG welding stands for metal inert gas welding, which is a welding process that uses a consumable wire electrode to create an arc between the electrode and the workpiece. The arc melts the metal and forms a weld pool, which is protected from the atmosphere by an inert gas, such as argon, carbon dioxide, or a mixture of both. MIG welding can be used to weld a variety of metals, such as steel, stainless steel, aluminum, copper, and magnesium. MIG welding is faster and easier than TIG welding, as the welder only has to control the wire feed and the gas flow.
