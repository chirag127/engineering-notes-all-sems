### Unit 2 - Introduction to IC Engines and Electric Vehicles
#### Construction and Working of Two stroke and four stroke SI & CI engine

- An internal combustion engine (ICE) is a heat engine where the combustion of a fuel occurs with an oxidizer (usually air) in a combustion chamber that is an integral part of the working fluid flow circuit.
- There are two main types of internal combustion engines: spark ignition (SI) and compression ignition (CI).
- SI engines use a spark plug to ignite the fuel-air mixture, while CI engines rely on the heat generated by the compression of the air in the cylinder to ignite the fuel.
- Both SI and CI engines can be designed to operate on either a two-stroke or a four-stroke cycle.
- In a two-stroke engine, the four events of intake, compression, power, and exhaust occur in two strokes of the piston (one up and one down) for each cycle of the engine.
- In a four-stroke engine, these four events occur in four strokes of the piston (two up and two down) for each cycle of the engine.
- The main components of an internal combustion engine include the cylinder, piston, connecting rod, crankshaft, and valves.
- The construction and operation of a two-stroke and four-stroke engine differ in several ways.
- In a two-stroke engine, the intake and exhaust events occur simultaneously as the piston moves towards the bottom of the cylinder, while in a four-stroke engine, these events occur separately during the intake and exhaust strokes.
- Additionally, two-stroke engines require a method of lubrication, such as oil mixed with the fuel, while four-stroke engines have a separate lubrication system.
- The choice between a two-stroke and four-stroke engine depends on the specific application and the desired balance between power, efficiency, and emissions.
