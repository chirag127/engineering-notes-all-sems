### Specific Gravity

Specific gravity is a dimensionless quantity that is defined as the ratio of the density of a substance to the density of a reference substance, typically water at 4°C. It is commonly used to compare the densities of different substances.

Some key points to remember about specific gravity are:

1. Specific gravity is a ratio and therefore has no units.
2. The reference substance for liquids and solids is typically water at 4°C, while for gases it is air at room temperature.
3. Specific gravity is temperature-dependent, as the density of both the substance and the reference substance can change with temperature.
4. Specific gravity can be used to determine the relative density of a substance, which is the ratio of its density to the density of a standard substance, such as water or air.
5. Specific gravity can also be used to determine the buoyancy of an object in a fluid, as an object will float if its specific gravity is less than that of the fluid, and sink if its specific gravity is greater than that of the fluid.
