### Strain
Strain is a measure of deformation representing the displacement between particles in the body relative to a reference length. It is defined as the change in length divided by the original length. Strain is a dimensionless quantity and is usually expressed as a decimal fraction, a percentage, or in parts-per notation.

There are two types of strain: normal strain and shear strain.

- Normal strain: Normal strain occurs when the deformation of a body is caused by an external force that is perpendicular to the surface of the body. It is defined as the change in length divided by the original length.

- Shear strain: Shear strain occurs when the deformation of a body is caused by an external force that is parallel to the surface of the body. It is defined as the change in the angle between two lines that were originally perpendicular.

Strain can be either positive or negative. Positive strain indicates that the body is elongated, while negative strain indicates that the body is compressed.

Strain is an important concept in mechanics, as it is used to determine the amount of deformation a body undergoes when subjected to external forces. It is also used to calculate the stress in a body, which is the force per unit area that arises from the deformation of the body.
