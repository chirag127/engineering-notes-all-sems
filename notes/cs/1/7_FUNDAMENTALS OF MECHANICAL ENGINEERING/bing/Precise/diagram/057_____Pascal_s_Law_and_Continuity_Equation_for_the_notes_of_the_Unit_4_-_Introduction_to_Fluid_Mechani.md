### Pascal’s Law and Continuity Equation

#### Pascal’s Law
Pascal’s Law states that pressure exerted anywhere in a confined incompressible fluid is transmitted equally in all directions throughout the fluid such that the pressure variations (initial differences) remain the same. This principle is stated mathematically as:

`P = F/A`

Where:
- `P` is the pressure
- `F` is the force applied
- `A` is the area over which the force is applied

#### Continuity Equation
The continuity equation is a statement of the conservation of mass. It states that the mass flow rate into a control volume must equal the mass flow rate out of the control volume plus the rate of change of mass within the control volume. Mathematically, this is expressed as:

`ρ1A1V1 = ρ2A2V2`

Where:
- `ρ` is the density of the fluid
- `A` is the cross-sectional area of the flow
- `V` is the flow velocity

The continuity equation is used to analyze fluid flow in pipes and other systems where the fluid is confined.
