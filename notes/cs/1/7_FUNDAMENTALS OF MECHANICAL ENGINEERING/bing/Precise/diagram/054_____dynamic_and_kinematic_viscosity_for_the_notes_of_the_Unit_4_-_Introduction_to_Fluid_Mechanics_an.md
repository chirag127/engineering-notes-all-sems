### Unit 4 - Introduction to Fluid Mechanics and Applications
#### FUNDAMENTALS OF MECHANICAL ENGINEERING

**Dynamic and Kinematic Viscosity**

1. Viscosity is a measure of a fluid's resistance to flow. It describes the internal friction of a moving fluid. A fluid with high viscosity resists motion because its molecular makeup gives it a lot of internal friction. A fluid with low viscosity flows easily because its molecular makeup results in very little friction when it is in motion.
2. Dynamic viscosity, also known as absolute viscosity, is the measure of a fluid's resistance to flow under an applied force. It is commonly expressed in units of Pascal-seconds (Pa·s) or poise (P).
3. Kinematic viscosity is the ratio of dynamic viscosity to density. It is a measure of a fluid's resistance to flow due to gravity. It is commonly expressed in units of square meters per second (m²/s) or stokes (St).
4. The viscosity of a fluid can be affected by factors such as temperature, pressure, and the presence of impurities. As temperature increases, the viscosity of a fluid generally decreases. As pressure increases, the viscosity of a fluid generally increases.
5. Viscosity is an important property of fluids in engineering applications. It affects the flow of fluids in pipes, the operation of pumps and other fluid machinery, and the heat transfer characteristics of fluids.
