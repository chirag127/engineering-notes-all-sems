### Poisson’s ratio

Poisson’s ratio is a measure of the deformation of a material when subjected to an applied stress. It is defined as the ratio of the lateral strain to the axial strain in the direction of the applied stress.

- Poisson’s ratio is denoted by the Greek letter nu (ν).
- It is a dimensionless quantity, meaning it has no units.
- The value of Poisson’s ratio typically ranges between -1 and 0.5 for most materials.
- A value of 0.5 indicates that the material is incompressible, meaning that when it is subjected to an applied stress, its volume remains constant.
- A value of 0 indicates that the material is perfectly compressible, meaning that when it is subjected to an applied stress, its volume changes but its shape remains unchanged.
- A negative value of Poisson’s ratio indicates that the material exhibits auxetic behavior, meaning that when it is subjected to an applied stress, it expands in the direction perpendicular to the applied stress.

Poisson’s ratio is an important property of materials as it is used in the calculation of stress and strain in various engineering applications. It is also used in the design of structures and components to ensure that they can withstand the applied loads without failure.
