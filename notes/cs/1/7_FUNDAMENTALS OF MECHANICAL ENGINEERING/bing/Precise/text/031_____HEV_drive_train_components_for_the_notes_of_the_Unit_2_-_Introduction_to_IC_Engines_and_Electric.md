### HEV Drive Train Components

A hybrid electric vehicle (HEV) combines a conventional internal combustion engine (ICE) with an electric propulsion system. The presence of the electric powertrain is intended to achieve either better fuel economy or better performance compared to a conventional vehicle. The drive train components of an HEV include:

1. **Internal Combustion Engine (ICE):** The ICE in an HEV is typically smaller and more efficient than in a conventional vehicle. It is used to provide power to the wheels and to charge the battery.

2. **Electric Motor:** The electric motor in an HEV is used to provide additional power to the wheels, either alone or in combination with the ICE. It can also be used to generate electricity to charge the battery.

3. **Battery:** The battery in an HEV is used to store electrical energy that can be used to power the electric motor. It is typically charged by the ICE and by regenerative braking.

4. **Transmission:** The transmission in an HEV is used to transfer power from the ICE and/or electric motor to the wheels. It can be either a conventional transmission or a continuously variable transmission (CVT).

5. **Power Electronics:** The power electronics in an HEV are used to control the flow of electrical energy between the battery, electric motor, and other components.

6. **Control System:** The control system in an HEV is used to manage the operation of the ICE, electric motor, and other components to optimize performance and fuel economy.

These are the main components of an HEV drive train. Each component plays a crucial role in the overall operation of the vehicle. Understanding these components and how they work together is essential for understanding the operation of an HEV.
