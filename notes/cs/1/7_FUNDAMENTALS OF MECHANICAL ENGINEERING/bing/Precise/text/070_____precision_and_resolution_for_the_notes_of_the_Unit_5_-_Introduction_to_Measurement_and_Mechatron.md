### Precision and Resolution

Precision and resolution are two important concepts in the field of measurement and mechatronics, particularly in the subject of Fundamentals of Mechanical Engineering.

1. **Precision** refers to the degree of consistency or repeatability of measurements. In other words, it is a measure of how close repeated measurements of the same quantity are to each other. A high degree of precision indicates that the measurements are consistent and reliable.

2. **Resolution** refers to the smallest change in the quantity being measured that can be detected by the measuring instrument. It is determined by the smallest unit of measurement that the instrument is capable of detecting. A high resolution indicates that the instrument is capable of detecting very small changes in the quantity being measured.

It is important to note that precision and resolution are not the same thing. An instrument can have high resolution but low precision, or vice versa. For example, a digital scale that can detect changes in weight as small as 0.1 grams has high resolution, but if it consistently gives different readings for the same weight, it has low precision.

In summary, precision and resolution are important concepts in measurement and mechatronics, and they play a crucial role in ensuring the accuracy and reliability of measurements. Understanding these concepts is essential for students studying the subject of Fundamentals of Mechanical Engineering.
