### Specific Gravity

Specific gravity is a dimensionless quantity that represents the ratio of the density of a substance to the density of a reference substance, usually water at 4°C. It is commonly used in fluid mechanics to compare the densities of different substances.

Some key points to remember about specific gravity are:

1. Specific gravity is a ratio and therefore has no units.
2. The reference substance for liquids and solids is usually water at 4°C, while for gases it is air at room temperature.
3. Specific gravity can be used to determine the relative density of a substance, which is the ratio of its density to the density of a reference substance at the same temperature and pressure.
4. Specific gravity can also be used to determine the buoyancy of an object in a fluid. An object will float in a fluid if its specific gravity is less than that of the fluid, and will sink if its specific gravity is greater than that of the fluid.
5. Specific gravity can be measured using a hydrometer, which is a device that measures the density of a liquid by comparing its weight to the weight of an equal volume of water.
