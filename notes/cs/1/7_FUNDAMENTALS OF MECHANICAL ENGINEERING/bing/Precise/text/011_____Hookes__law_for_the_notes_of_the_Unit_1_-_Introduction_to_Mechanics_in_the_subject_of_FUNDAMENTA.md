### Hookes’ law for the notes of the Unit 1 - Introduction to Mechanics in the subject of FUNDAMENTALS OF MECHANICAL ENGINEERING

- Hooke's law is a principle of physics that states that the force needed to extend or compress a spring by some distance is proportional to that distance.
- The law is named after 17th-century British physicist Robert Hooke.
- Mathematically, Hooke's law can be expressed as F = kx, where F is the force applied to the spring, k is the spring constant, and x is the displacement of the spring from its equilibrium position.
- The spring constant, k, is a measure of the stiffness of the spring. A larger value of k indicates a stiffer spring that requires more force to stretch or compress it.
- Hooke's law is an example of a linear relationship between two quantities, in this case, force and displacement.
- Hooke's law is only valid within the elastic limit of the material. Beyond this limit, the material will deform permanently and will not return to its original shape when the force is removed.
- Hooke's law is widely used in many areas of engineering, including the design of springs, the analysis of structures, and the study of materials.
