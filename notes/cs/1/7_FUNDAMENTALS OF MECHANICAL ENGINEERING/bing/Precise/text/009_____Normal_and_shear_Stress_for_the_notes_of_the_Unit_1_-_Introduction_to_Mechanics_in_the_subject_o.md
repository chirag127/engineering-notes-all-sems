### Normal and Shear Stress

Normal stress is the stress component perpendicular to a given plane. It is denoted by the Greek letter sigma (σ). Normal stress can be either tensile or compressive. Tensile stress is when the material is being pulled apart, while compressive stress is when the material is being pushed together.

Shear stress is the stress component parallel to a given plane. It is denoted by the Greek letter tau (τ). Shear stress is the result of forces acting parallel to the plane, causing deformation in the material.

Some key points to remember about normal and shear stress are:
- Normal stress is perpendicular to the plane, while shear stress is parallel to the plane.
- Normal stress can be either tensile or compressive, while shear stress is always parallel to the plane.
- The units of stress are typically Pascals (Pa) or pounds per square inch (psi).
- Stress is a measure of the internal forces within a material, while strain is a measure of the deformation of the material.
