## Unit 3 - Introduction to Refrigeration and Air-Conditioning

1. **Refrigeration** is the process of removing heat from a space or substance and transferring it to another location where it is not objectionable.
2. **Air conditioning** is the process of treating air to control its temperature, humidity, cleanliness, and distribution to meet the requirements of the conditioned space.
3. The basic components of a refrigeration system are the compressor, condenser, expansion valve, and evaporator.
4. The refrigeration cycle involves the compression of a refrigerant gas, which is then cooled and condensed into a liquid. The liquid refrigerant is then expanded and evaporated, absorbing heat from the space being cooled.
5. Air conditioning systems can be classified into two main categories: central air conditioning systems and room air conditioners.
6. Central air conditioning systems use a network of ducts to distribute cooled air throughout a building, while room air conditioners are self-contained units that cool a single room or space.
7. The efficiency of an air conditioning system is measured by its Seasonal Energy Efficiency Ratio (SEER), which is the ratio of the cooling output to the energy input over a typical cooling season.
8. Proper maintenance of air conditioning systems is important to ensure their efficient operation and to prolong their lifespan.
9. Common problems with air conditioning systems include refrigerant leaks, dirty filters, and blocked condensate drains.
10. Advances in technology have led to the development of more energy-efficient air conditioning systems, as well as the use of alternative refrigerants that have a lower impact on the environment.
