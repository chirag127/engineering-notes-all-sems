### Wet Bulb Temperature

- Wet bulb temperature is the lowest temperature to which air can be cooled by the evaporation of water into the air at a constant pressure.
- It is measured by wrapping a wet wick around the bulb of a thermometer and the measured temperature corresponds to the wet bulb temperature.
- By combining the dry bulb and wet bulb temperature in a psychrometric chart or Mollier diagram the state of the humid air can be determined.
- A wet-bulb temperature of 35°C marks our upper physiological limit, and much lower values have serious health and productivity impacts.
- Even heat-adapted people cannot carry out normal outdoor activities past a wet-bulb temperature of 32 °C (90 °F), equivalent to a heat index of 55 °C (130 °F).
- Quite comfortable wet-bulb temperature is around 70 °F (22 °C), but no more than 86 °F (30 °C). Above this temperature, physical labor becomes impossible.
- Wet-bulb temperature is not the same as Heat Index.
