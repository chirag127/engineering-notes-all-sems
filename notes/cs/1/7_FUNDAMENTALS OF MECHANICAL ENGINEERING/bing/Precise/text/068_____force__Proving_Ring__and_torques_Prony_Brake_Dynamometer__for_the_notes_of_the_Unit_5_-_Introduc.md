### Force (Proving Ring) and Torques (Prony Brake Dynamometer)

#### Proving Ring
- A proving ring is a device used to measure force.
- It consists of an elastic ring with a known relationship between applied force and deformation.
- The deformation of the ring is measured using a micrometer or dial gauge, and the corresponding force is calculated using the known relationship.
- Proving rings are commonly used in materials testing, such as tensile and compression testing.

#### Prony Brake Dynamometer
- A Prony brake dynamometer is a device used to measure torque.
- It consists of a brake drum attached to the output shaft of the engine or motor being tested, and a brake band that wraps around the drum.
- The brake band is tightened to apply a load to the drum, and the torque is calculated by measuring the force required to keep the brake band stationary.
- Prony brake dynamometers are commonly used to measure the torque output of engines and motors.

These are the basic concepts of force and torque measurement using a proving ring and a Prony brake dynamometer, respectively, in the context of Unit 5 - Introduction to Measurement and Mechatronics in the subject of FUNDAMENTALS OF MECHANICAL ENGINEERING.
