### Strain

Strain is a measure of deformation representing the displacement between particles in the body relative to a reference length. It is defined as the change in length divided by the original length.

There are two types of strain: normal strain and shear strain.

1. Normal strain: It is the ratio of the change in length to the original length. It is a measure of the elongation or compression of a line segment. The normal strain in the axial direction is called axial strain.

2. Shear strain: It is the change in angle between two line segments originally perpendicular to each other. It is a measure of the deformation of the angle between two originally perpendicular lines.

Strain is a dimensionless quantity and is usually expressed as a decimal fraction, a percentage, or in parts-per notation.

Strain can be positive (tensile) or negative (compressive). In tensile strain, the material elongates, while in compressive strain, the material shortens.

Strain can be caused by external forces (stress) or by changes in temperature.

Hooke's Law states that the strain of a material is proportional to the applied stress within the elastic limit of that material. The constant of proportionality is called the modulus of elasticity or Young's modulus.

Strain can be measured using various techniques, including strain gauges, extensometers, and optical methods.

Strain is an important concept in the study of mechanics of materials, as it is used to determine the deformation and failure of materials under load. It is also used in the design of structures and machines, to ensure that they can withstand the expected loads without excessive deformation or failure.
