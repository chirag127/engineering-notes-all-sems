### Principle of Transmissibility

The principle of transmissibility states that the external effect of a force on a rigid body is the same, regardless of the point of application, provided the line of action of the force remains unchanged.

This principle is fundamental to the study of mechanics and is used in the analysis of forces acting on rigid bodies. It allows us to replace a force acting at a given point with an equivalent force acting at a different point, as long as the line of action of the force remains the same.

Some key points to remember about the principle of transmissibility are:
- The external effect of a force on a rigid body is the same, regardless of the point of application.
- The line of action of the force must remain unchanged.
- This principle is used in the analysis of forces acting on rigid bodies.
- It allows us to replace a force acting at a given point with an equivalent force acting at a different point.

This principle is an important concept in the subject of Fundamentals of Mechanical Engineering, specifically in Unit 1 - Introduction to Mechanics. It is essential to understand this principle in order to effectively analyze and solve problems involving forces acting on rigid bodies.
