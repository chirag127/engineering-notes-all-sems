### Strain (Bonded and Unbonded Strain Gauge)

Strain is defined as the deformation of a body due to an applied force. It is a measure of the change in length of a material per unit length. Strain gauges are devices used to measure strain. There are two types of strain gauges: bonded and unbonded.

1. Bonded Strain Gauge: A bonded strain gauge is attached directly to the surface of the material being measured. The gauge consists of a wire or foil grid that is bonded to a thin backing. As the material deforms, the grid deforms with it, causing a change in the electrical resistance of the grid. This change in resistance is measured and used to calculate the strain.

2. Unbonded Strain Gauge: An unbonded strain gauge consists of a wire or foil grid that is suspended between two supports. The gauge is not attached directly to the material being measured. Instead, the material deforms the supports, which in turn causes the grid to deform. This deformation causes a change in the electrical resistance of the grid, which is measured and used to calculate the strain.

Both types of strain gauges are commonly used in the field of measurement and mechatronics. They are an important tool in the study of the mechanical behavior of materials and structures.
