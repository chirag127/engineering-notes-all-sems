### Unit 4 - Introduction to Fluid Mechanics and Applications

#### Dynamic and Kinematic Viscosity

1. **Viscosity** is a measure of a fluid's resistance to flow. It describes the internal friction of a moving fluid. A fluid with high viscosity resists motion because its molecular makeup gives it a lot of internal friction. A fluid with low viscosity flows easily because its molecular makeup results in very little friction when it is in motion.
2. **Dynamic viscosity**, also known as absolute viscosity, is the measure of a fluid's resistance to flow under an applied force. It is commonly denoted by the Greek letter mu (μ) and is defined as the ratio of the shear stress to the shear rate in a fluid.
3. **Kinematic viscosity** is the ratio of dynamic viscosity to density. It is a measure of a fluid's resistance to flow under the influence of gravity. It is commonly denoted by the Greek letter nu (ν) and is defined as the ratio of the dynamic viscosity to the density of a fluid.
4. The units of dynamic viscosity are typically expressed in pascal-seconds (Pa·s) or poise (P), while the units of kinematic viscosity are typically expressed in square meters per second (m²/s) or stokes (St).
5. The viscosity of a fluid can be affected by several factors, including temperature, pressure, and the presence of impurities. In general, the viscosity of a fluid decreases as its temperature increases and increases as its pressure increases.
6. Viscosity is an important property of fluids in many applications, including lubrication, hydraulic systems, and fluid transport. Understanding the viscosity of a fluid is essential for the proper design and operation of many mechanical systems.
