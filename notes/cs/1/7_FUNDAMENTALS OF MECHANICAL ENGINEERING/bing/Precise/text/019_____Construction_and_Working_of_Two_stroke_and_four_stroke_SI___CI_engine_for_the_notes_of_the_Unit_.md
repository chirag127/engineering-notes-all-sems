### Construction and Working of Two stroke and four stroke SI & CI engine

Unit 2 - Introduction to IC Engines and Electric Vehicles in the subject of FUNDAMENTALS OF MECHANICAL ENGINEERING

1. **Two-stroke engine**: A two-stroke engine is an internal combustion engine that completes the thermodynamic cycle in two movements of the piston, compared to twice that number for a four-stroke engine. This is accomplished by the end of the combustion stroke and the beginning of the compression stroke happening simultaneously and performing the intake and exhaust functions at the same time.

2. **Construction of Two-stroke engine**: Two-stroke engines have ports rather than valves, and the crankcase is used as part of the induction tract. The piston has a deflector head to direct the incoming fuel-air mixture towards the spark plug and to improve scavenging.

3. **Working of Two-stroke engine**: The working of a two-stroke engine can be divided into two cycles: the power cycle and the scavenging cycle. During the power cycle, the piston moves upwards, compressing the fuel-air mixture and igniting it with the spark plug. The resulting explosion forces the piston downwards, producing power. During the scavenging cycle, the piston moves upwards again, expelling the exhaust gases and drawing in a fresh fuel-air mixture through the transfer port.

4. **Four-stroke engine**: A four-stroke engine is an internal combustion engine that completes the thermodynamic cycle in four movements of the piston, compared to twice that number for a two-stroke engine. The four strokes are intake, compression, power, and exhaust.

5. **Construction of Four-stroke engine**: Four-stroke engines have valves rather than ports, and the crankcase is not used as part of the induction tract. The piston moves up and down in the cylinder, and the valves open and close to allow the fuel-air mixture to enter the combustion chamber and the exhaust gases to exit.

6. **Working of Four-stroke engine**: The working of a four-stroke engine can be divided into four cycles: the intake stroke, the compression stroke, the power stroke, and the exhaust stroke. During the intake stroke, the piston moves downwards, drawing in the fuel-air mixture through the intake valve. During the compression stroke, the piston moves upwards, compressing the fuel-air mixture and igniting it with the spark plug. The resulting explosion forces the piston downwards, producing power. During the exhaust stroke, the piston moves upwards again, expelling the exhaust gases through the exhaust valve.

7. **SI engine**: An SI (Spark Ignition) engine is an internal combustion engine that uses a spark plug to ignite the fuel-air mixture in the combustion chamber.

8. **CI engine**: A CI (Compression Ignition) engine is an internal combustion engine that uses the heat generated by the compression of the air in the cylinder to ignite the fuel, rather than using a spark plug.
