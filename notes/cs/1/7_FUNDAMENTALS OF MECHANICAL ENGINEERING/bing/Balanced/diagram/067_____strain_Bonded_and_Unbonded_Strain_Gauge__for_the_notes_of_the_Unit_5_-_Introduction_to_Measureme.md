### Strain Bonded and Unbonded Strain Gauge

- Strain is the measure of the deformation or change in dimensions of a body due to an applied force or stress.
- Strain gauge is a device that converts strain into a measurable electrical signal.
- There are two types of strain gauges: bonded and unbonded.

#### Bonded Strain Gauge

- A bonded strain gauge is a thin metallic foil or wire that is attached or glued to the surface of the specimen whose strain is to be measured.
- When the specimen is subjected to stress, the strain gauge also deforms and changes its resistance proportionally to the strain.
- The change in resistance can be measured by a Wheatstone bridge circuit or a similar device.
- Bonded strain gauges are widely used for static and dynamic measurements of stress, force, torque, pressure, etc.
- Bonded strain gauges have the advantages of high sensitivity, accuracy, stability, and durability.
- Bonded strain gauges have the disadvantages of being affected by temperature changes, surface preparation, and bonding errors.

#### Unbonded Strain Gauge

- An unbonded strain gauge is a resistance wire or foil that is stretched between two frames or supports, but not attached to the specimen whose strain is to be measured.
- The specimen is placed between the frames and exerts a force on the strain gauge, causing it to elongate or compress and change its resistance.
- The change in resistance can be measured by a Wheatstone bridge circuit or a similar device.
- Unbonded strain gauges are used for measuring very small strains and forces, such as in precision instruments and transducers.
- Unbonded strain gauges have the advantages of being less affected by temperature changes, surface conditions, and bonding errors.
- Unbonded strain gauges have the disadvantages of being less sensitive, accurate, stable, and durable than bonded strain gauges.
