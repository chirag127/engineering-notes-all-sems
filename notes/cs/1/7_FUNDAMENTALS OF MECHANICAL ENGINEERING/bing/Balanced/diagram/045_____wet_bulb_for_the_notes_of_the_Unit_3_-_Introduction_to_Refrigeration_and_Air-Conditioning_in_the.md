### Wet Bulb

- Wet bulb is a term used to describe the temperature of a thermometer whose bulb is covered with a wet cloth and exposed to air flow.
- Wet bulb temperature is the lowest temperature that can be reached by evaporating water into the air at constant pressure.
- Wet bulb temperature is a measure of how humid the air is and how the human body would feel in direct sunlight.
- Wet bulb temperature is lower than dry bulb temperature (the temperature of a thermometer without a wet cloth) unless the air is saturated (100% relative humidity).
- Wet bulb temperature can be calculated from dry bulb temperature, relative humidity, and atmospheric pressure using formulas or tables.
- Wet bulb temperature is used in psychrometry, the study of the properties of moist air and its interactions with various materials.
- Wet bulb temperature is also used in meteorology, climatology, and engineering to assess heat stress, cooling tower performance, air conditioning efficiency, and other applications.
