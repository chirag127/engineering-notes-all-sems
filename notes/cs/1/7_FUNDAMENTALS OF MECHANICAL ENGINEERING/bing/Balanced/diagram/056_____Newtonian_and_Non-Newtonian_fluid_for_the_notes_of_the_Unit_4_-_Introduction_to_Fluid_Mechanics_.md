### Newtonian and Non-Newtonian fluids

- Fluids can be divided into two major types: **Newtonian** and **non-Newtonian** .
- **Newtonian fluids** are the ones that obey **Newton’s law of constant viscosity** . These fluids have a **constant viscosity** and **zero shear rate** and **shear stress** . These fluids have a **linear relationship** between viscosity and shear stress.
- Examples of Newtonian fluids are water, mineral oil, gasoline, alcohol, etc.
- **Non-Newtonian fluids** are those that do not have a constant viscosity and have a **variable relationship** with shear stress . In non-Newtonian fluids, **viscosity can change** when under force to either more liquid or more solid. These fluids have a **non-linear relationship** between viscosity and shear stress.
- Examples of non-Newtonian fluids are ketchup, blood, yogurt, toothpaste, etc .
- Non-Newtonian fluids can be further classified into different types based on their response to shear stress, such as **dilatant**, **pseudoplastic**, **Bingham plastic**, **thixotropic**, **rheopectic**, etc.
- The importance of knowing the difference between Newtonian and non-Newtonian fluids is that it affects the **processing**, **handling**, **pumping**, and **mixing** of fluids in various applications.
