## Unit 4 - Introduction to Fluid Mechanics and Applications

- Fluid mechanics is the branch of physics that deals with the behavior of fluids (liquids, gases, and plasmas) and the forces acting on them.
- Fluid mechanics has many applications in engineering, science, and biology, such as hydraulic and aeronautical engineering, chemical engineering, meteorology, zoology, astrophysics, and blood circulation .
- Fluid mechanics can be divided into two main subfields: fluid statics and fluid dynamics.
  - Fluid statics is the study of fluids at rest and the forces and pressures they exert on their surroundings.
  - Fluid dynamics is the study of fluids in motion and the effects of viscosity, turbulence, and compressibility on their flow.
- Some of the fundamental concepts and principles of fluid mechanics are:
  - Density: the mass per unit volume of a fluid.
  - Pressure: the force per unit area exerted by a fluid on a surface.
  - Buoyancy: the upward force exerted by a fluid on a submerged or floating object.
  - Pascal's principle: the pressure at any point in a static fluid is the same in all directions and is transmitted equally to all parts of the fluid and the walls of the container.
  - Bernoulli's principle: the sum of the pressure, the kinetic energy per unit volume, and the potential energy per unit volume of a fluid is constant along a streamline.
  - Continuity equation: the product of the cross-sectional area and the velocity of a fluid is constant along a pipe or a tube.
  - Reynolds number: a dimensionless number that characterizes the type of flow of a fluid, whether laminar (smooth and orderly) or turbulent (chaotic and irregular).
  - Navier-Stokes equations: a set of partial differential equations that describe the motion of a viscous, compressible fluid.
