### Equilibrium equations and support reactions

- Equilibrium equations are the conditions that must be satisfied for a body or a system of bodies to be in static equilibrium, i.e., to have zero net force and zero net moment acting on it.
- The equilibrium equations can be written as:

  - Sum of forces in x-direction = 0
  - Sum of forces in y-direction = 0
  - Sum of moments about any point = 0

- Support reactions are the forces and moments that are exerted by the supports on a structure to keep it in equilibrium.
- The types of support reactions depend on the type of support, such as:

  - A pin support can resist two forces in x and y directions, but not a moment.
  - A roller support can resist one force in the direction perpendicular to the surface, but not a force parallel to the surface or a moment.
  - A fixed support can resist two forces in x and y directions, and a moment.

- To determine the support reactions, the following steps can be followed:

  - Draw a free body diagram of the structure, showing all the applied loads and the unknown support reactions.
  - Choose a convenient point to take moments about, and write the moment equilibrium equation. Solve for one of the unknown reactions.
  - Write the force equilibrium equations in x and y directions, and solve for the remaining unknown reactions.
  - Check the results by substituting them back into the equilibrium equations.
