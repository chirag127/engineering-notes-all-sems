### Refrigerating effect

- Refrigerating effect is the amount of heat transferred by one kg of refrigerant as it circulates in the refrigeration system.
- Refrigerating effect is also the heat absorbed in the evaporator per lb of refrigerant. It is determined by the difference in enthalpy of a lb of refrigerant vapor leaving the evaporator and that of a lb of liquid just upstream of the expansion valve at the evaporator.
- Refrigerating effect is the evaporator load in kJ/kg, which is the product of the mass flow rate of the refrigerant, the specific heat of the refrigerant, and the temperature difference between the raw and chilled fluid in the evaporator.
- Refrigerating effect is the measure of the cooling capacity of a refrigeration system. It is expressed in tons of refrigeration (TR), where one ton of refrigeration is equivalent to the heat removal rate of 211 kJ/min or 3.517 kW.
- Refrigerating effect is the opposite of the heat engine effect, where heat is converted into work. In a refrigeration system, work is used to create a temperature difference and transfer heat from a lower temperature reservoir to a higher temperature reservoir.
