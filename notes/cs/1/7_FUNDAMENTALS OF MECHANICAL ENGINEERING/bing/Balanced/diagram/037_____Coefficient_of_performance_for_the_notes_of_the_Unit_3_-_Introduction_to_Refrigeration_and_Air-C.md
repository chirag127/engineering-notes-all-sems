### Coefficient of performance

- The coefficient of performance (COP) is a measure of the efficiency of a refrigeration or air-conditioning system. It is defined as the ratio of the useful cooling or heating effect to the work input.  
- The COP can be expressed as:

  COP = Q/W

  where Q is the heat removed from the cold reservoir or delivered to the hot reservoir, and W is the work done by the compressor.  
- The COP depends on the temperatures of the reservoirs and the type of refrigeration cycle. For a given refrigeration cycle, the COP increases as the temperature difference between the reservoirs decreases.  
- The COP can also be expressed in terms of the refrigeration capacity (RC) and the power input (P):

  COP = RC/P

  where RC is the rate of heat removal from the cold reservoir or delivery to the hot reservoir, and P is the rate of work input by the compressor.  
- The refrigeration capacity can be expressed in units of ton of refrigeration (TR), which is equal to the rate of energy removal needed to freeze one ton of water in one day. One TR is defined as 12,000 Btu/hr, or 3.517 kW. 
- The COP can vary depending on the application and the operating conditions. For example, the typical COP of a refrigeration system for cutting and preparation rooms is 2.6-3.0, while the typical COP of a refrigeration system for ice making is 1.6-2.0.  
- The COP can be used to compare the performance of different refrigeration or air-conditioning systems, and to evaluate the potential for energy savings and cost reduction.
