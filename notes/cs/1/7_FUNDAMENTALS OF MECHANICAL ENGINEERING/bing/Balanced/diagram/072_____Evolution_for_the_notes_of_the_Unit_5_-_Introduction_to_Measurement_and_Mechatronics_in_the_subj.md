### Evolution of measurement and mechatronics

- Measurement is the process of quantifying physical phenomena, such as length, mass, temperature, pressure, etc. Measurement instruments are devices that perform measurement tasks, such as rulers, scales, thermometers, barometers, etc.
- Mechatronics is the integration of mechanical, electrical, and computer engineering disciplines to design and control complex systems, such as robots, vehicles, machines, etc. Mechatronics systems combine sensors, actuators, controllers, and software to achieve desired functionality and performance.
- The evolution of measurement and mechatronics can be traced back to the following historical stages   :

  - Industrial Revolution (18th-19th centuries): The development of steam engines, railways, factories, and machines led to the need for accurate and reliable measurement of physical quantities, such as speed, force, power, etc. Measurement instruments such as chronometers, dynamometers, gauges, etc. were invented and improved to meet the industrial demands.
  - Semiconductor Revolution (20th century): The invention of transistors, integrated circuits, microprocessors, and microcontrollers enabled the miniaturization and digitization of electronic components and systems. This allowed for the development of more sophisticated and versatile measurement instruments, such as oscilloscopes, multimeters, logic analyzers, etc. that could process and display digital signals and data.
  - Information Revolution (21st century): The advancement of information and communication technologies, such as the internet, wireless networks, cloud computing, artificial intelligence, etc. enabled the interconnection and integration of measurement instruments and mechatronics systems. This allowed for the creation of smart and autonomous systems, such as drones, self-driving cars, robots, etc. that could communicate, sense, actuate, and control themselves and their environment.

- The evolution of measurement and mechatronics is driven by the following factors   :

  - Innovation: The invention and improvement of new technologies, methods, and concepts that enhance the functionality and performance of measurement instruments and mechatronics systems.
  - Application: The identification and exploitation of new domains and problems that require the use of measurement instruments and mechatronics systems.
  - Integration: The combination and coordination of different disciplines, components, and systems that enable the realization of measurement instruments and mechatronics systems.
  - Optimization: The refinement and adjustment of the design and operation of measurement instruments and mechatronics systems to achieve the best possible outcomes and efficiency.
