## Unit 2 - Introduction to IC Engines and Electric Vehicles

- An **IC engine** or **internal combustion engine** is a type of engine that converts the chemical energy of a fuel into mechanical energy by burning it inside a combustion chamber.
- An **electric vehicle** or **EV** is a type of vehicle that uses an electric motor and a battery instead of an IC engine and a fuel tank to propel itself.
- The main differences between IC engines and electric vehicles are:

  - IC engines use fossil fuels such as gasoline or diesel, which emit harmful gases and contribute to global warming and air pollution. Electric vehicles use electricity, which can be generated from renewable sources such as solar or wind, and have zero tailpipe emissions.
  - IC engines have lower efficiency and higher maintenance costs than electric motors, which have fewer moving parts and can recover energy through regenerative braking. Electric vehicles also have lower operating costs due to cheaper electricity and less frequent servicing.
  - IC engines have higher power and torque output than electric motors, which have limited range and performance depending on the battery capacity and state of charge. Electric vehicles also face challenges such as long charging time, limited charging infrastructure, and battery degradation.

- There are different types of electric vehicles, such as:

  - **Battery electric vehicles (BEV)**, which run solely on electricity stored in a battery pack and need to be plugged in to recharge.
  - **Hybrid electric vehicles (HEV)**, which use both an electric motor and an IC engine. The IC engine generates the required power to drive the motor or charge the battery, and can also directly drive the wheels. HEVs have lower emissions and better fuel economy than conventional IC vehicles, but still rely on fossil fuels.
  - **Plug-in hybrid electric vehicles (PHEV)**, which are similar to HEVs, but have a larger battery that can be charged from an external source. PHEVs can run on electricity for a longer distance before switching to the IC engine, and have lower emissions and fuel consumption than HEVs.
  - **Fuel cell electric vehicles (FCEV)**, which use a fuel cell to convert hydrogen gas into electricity, which then powers an electric motor. FCEVs have zero tailpipe emissions, except for water vapor, and have a longer range and faster refueling time than BEVs. However, FCEVs are expensive and require a hydrogen infrastructure, which is not widely available.

- The conversion of an IC engine vehicle into an electric vehicle is a process of replacing the IC engine and its associated components with an electric motor and a battery pack, along with other modifications such as wiring, controller, charger, etc. The conversion can be done by professionals or by DIY enthusiasts, and can offer benefits such as lower emissions, lower operating costs, and improved performance. However, the conversion also has drawbacks such as high initial cost, reduced cargo space, reduced range, and safety issues .
