### Density

- Density is the measurement of how tightly a material is packed together.
- It is an intensive property, which means it does not depend on the amount of substance.
- It is defined as the mass per unit volume of a material substance.
- The formula for density is:

$$\rho = \frac{m}{V}$$

where $\rho$ is the density, $m$ is the mass, and $V$ is the volume of the substance  .

- The SI unit of density is kilogram per cubic meter (kg/m$^3$) . It is also frequently represented in the cgs unit of grams per cubic centimeter (g/cm$^3$) .
- Density can be used to identify a substance, compare the compactness of different materials, or calculate the mass or volume of an object .
- Density is affected by temperature and pressure. As temperature increases, the volume of a substance usually expands, and as pressure increases, the volume of a substance usually decreases. Therefore, density is inversely proportional to temperature and directly proportional to pressure .
