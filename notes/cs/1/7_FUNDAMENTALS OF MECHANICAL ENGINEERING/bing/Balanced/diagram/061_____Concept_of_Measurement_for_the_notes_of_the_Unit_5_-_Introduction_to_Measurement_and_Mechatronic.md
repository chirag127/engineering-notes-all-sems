### Concept of Measurement

- Measurement is the process of comparing an unknown quantity with a known standard or unit.
- Measurement is essential for verifying the conformity of a manufactured component with its specifications, as well as for controlling and improving the quality of products and processes.
- Measurement is also important for scientific research, engineering design, and technological development.
- The science of measurement is called metrology, which includes the study of measurement standards, methods, instruments, and errors.
- Measurement can be classified into different types based on the nature of the quantity being measured, such as length, mass, time, angle, temperature, etc.
- Measurement can also be classified into direct and indirect measurement, based on whether the quantity is measured directly or by using a mathematical relation with other quantities.
- Measurement instruments are devices that are used to perform measurements, such as rulers, scales, clocks, thermometers, etc.
- Measurement instruments can be analog or digital, based on whether they display the measurement result in a continuous or discrete form.
- Measurement instruments can be classified into three categories based on their accuracy and precision: absolute, secondary, and working instruments.
- Absolute instruments are the most accurate and precise instruments, which are used to define and calibrate the standards of measurement.
- Secondary instruments are less accurate and precise than absolute instruments, but more than working instruments. They are used to compare and transfer the standards of measurement to working instruments.
- Working instruments are the least accurate and precise instruments, which are used for routine measurements in industries and laboratories.
- Measurement errors are the differences between the true value and the measured value of a quantity, which can arise due to various sources, such as human, instrument, method, or environmental factors.
- Measurement errors can be classified into two types: systematic and random errors.
- Systematic errors are the errors that are consistent and predictable, and can be corrected by applying suitable corrections or calibrations to the measurement result.
- Random errors are the errors that are unpredictable and vary randomly, and can be reduced by increasing the number of measurements and applying statistical methods to the measurement result.
