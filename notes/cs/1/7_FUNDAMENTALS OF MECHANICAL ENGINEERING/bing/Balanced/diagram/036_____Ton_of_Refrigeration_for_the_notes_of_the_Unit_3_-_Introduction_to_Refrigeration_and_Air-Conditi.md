### Ton of Refrigeration

- A ton of refrigeration (TR or TOR), also called a refrigeration ton (RT), is a unit of power used in some countries (especially in North America) to describe the heat-extraction capacity of refrigeration and air conditioning equipment .
- One ton of refrigeration is equal to the amount of heat required to melt one ton (2000 pounds) of ice in 24 hours. This is equivalent to 12,000 British thermal units per hour (BTU/h) or 3.517 kilowatts (kW) of power .
- The origin of the term ton of refrigeration dates back to the 19th century, when ice was the main source of cooling. The amount of ice needed to cool a given space was estimated by the weight of ice that melted in that space in one day. Thus, a ton of refrigeration represented the cooling effect of one ton of ice .
- The ton of refrigeration is not a standard unit in the International System of Units (SI). The SI unit of power is the watt (W), which is equal to one joule of energy per second. The SI unit of heat is the joule (J), which is equal to the amount of energy needed to raise the temperature of one gram of water by one degree Celsius. The SI unit of time is the second (s). Therefore, one ton of refrigeration can be converted to SI units as follows:

  - 1 TR = 12,000 BTU/h x 1.055056 J/BTU x 1/3600 h/s = 3.516853 W
  - 1 TR = 3.516853 W x 3600 s/h x 24 h/day = 302,526.9 J/day
  - 1 TR = 302,526.9 J/day x 1/334 J/g°C x 1/1000 g/kg x 1/1000 kg/ton = 0.9072 ton/day

- The ton of refrigeration is still widely used in the refrigeration and air conditioning industry, especially in North America. However, some other units of power are also used, such as the kilowatt (kW), the horsepower (hp), and the calorie per second (cal/s). The following table shows the approximate conversion factors between these units:

  | Unit | Symbol | Conversion Factor |
  |------|--------|-------------------|
  | Ton of refrigeration | TR or TOR | 1 TR = 1 TR |
  | Refrigeration ton | RT | 1 RT = 1 TR |
  | Kilowatt | kW | 1 kW = 0.2843 TR |
  | Horsepower | hp | 1 hp = 0.212 TR |
  | Calorie per second | cal/s | 1 cal/s = 0.0016 TR |

- To calculate the cooling capacity of a refrigeration or air conditioning system, the following formula can be used:

  - Q = m x Cp x ΔT

  - Where Q is the cooling capacity in TR, m is the mass flow rate of the refrigerant in kg/s, Cp is the specific heat capacity of the refrigerant in kJ/kg°C, and ΔT is the temperature difference between the inlet and outlet of the evaporator in °C.

  - For example, if a refrigeration system uses R-134a as the refrigerant, and the mass flow rate is 0.1 kg/s, the specific heat capacity is 1.51 kJ/kg°C, and the temperature difference is 10 °C, then the cooling capacity is:

    - Q = 0.1 x 1.51 x 10
    - Q = 1.51 TR
