### Resultant of force system- concurrent and non-concurrent coplanar forces

- A force system is a set of forces acting on a rigid body.
- A resultant force is a single force that has the same effect as the original force system.
- A coplanar force system is a force system in which all the forces lie in the same plane.
- A concurrent force system is a force system in which all the lines of action of the forces intersect at a common point.
- A non-concurrent force system is a force system in which the lines of action of the forces do not intersect at a common point.
- A parallel force system is a force system in which all the forces are parallel to each other.
- A non-parallel force system is a force system in which the forces are not parallel to each other.

- The resultant of a concurrent coplanar force system can be found by using the parallelogram law of forces, the triangle law of forces, or the polygon law of forces. These laws state that the resultant force is the diagonal of the parallelogram, the third side of the triangle, or the closing side of the polygon formed by the given forces, respectively.
- The resultant of a non-concurrent coplanar force system can be found by using the method of moments, the method of components, or the method of equilibrium. These methods involve finding the resultant force and the resultant moment of the force system, and then locating the point of application of the resultant force such that the resultant moment is zero.
- The resultant of a parallel coplanar force system can be found by adding the forces algebraically, and then finding the resultant moment about any point. The point of application of the resultant force is then located such that the resultant moment is zero.
- The resultant of a non-parallel non-concurrent coplanar force system can be found by using the method of components, or the method of equilibrium. These methods involve resolving the forces into horizontal and vertical components, and then finding the resultant force and the resultant moment of the components. The point of application of the resultant force is then located such that the resultant moment is zero.
