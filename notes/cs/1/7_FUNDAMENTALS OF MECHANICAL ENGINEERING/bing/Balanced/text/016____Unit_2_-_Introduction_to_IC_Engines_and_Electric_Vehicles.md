## Unit 2 - Introduction to IC Engines and Electric Vehicles

- An **IC engine** or **internal combustion engine** is a type of engine that converts chemical energy stored in fuel into mechanical energy by burning the fuel inside a combustion chamber .
- An **electric vehicle** or **EV** is a type of vehicle that uses an electric motor and a battery instead of an IC engine and a fuel tank to propel the vehicle .
- IC engines and electric vehicles have different advantages and disadvantages in terms of performance, efficiency, emissions, cost, maintenance, and environmental impact.
- Some of the main differences between IC engines and electric vehicles are:

| IC engines | Electric vehicles |
| --- | --- |
| - Use gasoline or diesel as fuel | - Use electricity as fuel |
| - Produce harmful emissions such as carbon dioxide, nitrogen oxides, and particulate matter | - Produce zero tailpipe emissions, but may have indirect emissions from electricity generation |
| - Have lower efficiency due to energy losses in the combustion process and friction | - Have higher efficiency due to direct conversion of electric energy into mechanical energy and regenerative braking |
| - Have higher maintenance costs due to more moving parts and wear and tear | - Have lower maintenance costs due to fewer moving parts and less wear and tear |
| - Have longer range and shorter refueling time | - Have shorter range and longer recharging time |
| - Have more variety and availability in the market | - Have less variety and availability in the market |
| - Have more noise and vibration | - Have less noise and vibration |

- There are also different types of electric vehicles, such as:

  - **Battery electric vehicles (BEVs)**, which run solely on electricity stored in batteries and have no IC engine or fuel tank.
  - **Hybrid electric vehicles (HEVs)**, which use both an IC engine and an electric motor to power the vehicle and can switch between them depending on the driving conditions .
  - **Plug-in hybrid electric vehicles (PHEVs)**, which are similar to HEVs but have larger batteries that can be recharged from an external source and can run longer on electric mode.
  - **Fuel cell electric vehicles (FCEVs)**, which use hydrogen as fuel and generate electricity through a chemical reaction in a fuel cell, which powers an electric motor.

- Electric vehicles are expected to become more popular and competitive in the future due to the following reasons:

  - Increasing environmental awareness and regulations to reduce greenhouse gas emissions and air pollution .
  - Improving battery technology and infrastructure to increase the range, performance, and affordability of electric vehicles .
  - Growing consumer demand and preference for cleaner, quieter, and more efficient vehicles  .
  - Innovating new designs and features to enhance the appeal and functionality of electric vehicles  .
