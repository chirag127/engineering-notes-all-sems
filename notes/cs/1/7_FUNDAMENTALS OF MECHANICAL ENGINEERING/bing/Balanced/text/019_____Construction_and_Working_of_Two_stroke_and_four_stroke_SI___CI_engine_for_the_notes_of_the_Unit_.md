### Construction and Working of Two stroke and four stroke SI & CI engine

- SI engine stands for spark ignition engine, which uses a spark plug to ignite the air-fuel mixture in the combustion chamber.
- CI engine stands for compression ignition engine, which uses high pressure and temperature to ignite the fuel injected into the air in the combustion chamber.
- Both SI and CI engines can be classified into two types based on the number of strokes required to complete one cycle: two stroke and four stroke engines.

#### Two stroke engine
- A two stroke engine is an engine that completes one cycle in one revolution of the crankshaft, which means it has one power stroke for every revolution.
- A two stroke engine has only two strokes: suction-compression stroke and expansion-exhaust stroke.
- A two stroke engine uses piston-controlled ports instead of valves to control the intake and exhaust of gases.
- A two stroke engine uses the total loss lubrication method, which means the oil is mixed with the fuel and enters the engine along with it.
- A two stroke engine has a simple and compact construction, but it also has some disadvantages such as low efficiency, high emissions, and poor durability.

#### Four stroke engine
- A four stroke engine is an engine that completes one cycle in two revolutions of the crankshaft, which means it has one power stroke for every two revolutions.
- A four stroke engine has four strokes: suction stroke, compression stroke, power stroke, and exhaust stroke.
- A four stroke engine uses valves to control the intake and exhaust of gases, which are operated by a camshaft and a timing belt or chain.
- A four stroke engine uses a separate lubrication system, which means the oil is circulated by a pump and does not enter the combustion chamber.
- A four stroke engine has a complex and bulky construction, but it also has some advantages such as high efficiency, low emissions, and long life.

#### Working of two stroke SI engine
- Suction-compression stroke: The piston moves from top dead center (TDC) to bottom dead center (BDC), creating a vacuum in the crankcase. The inlet port opens and the air-fuel mixture enters the crankcase. The piston also compresses the air-fuel mixture in the cylinder. The spark plug ignites the mixture near the end of the stroke.
- Expansion-exhaust stroke: The piston moves from BDC to TDC, expanding the burned gases and producing power. The exhaust port opens and the exhaust gases leave the cylinder. The transfer port also opens and the fresh air-fuel mixture from the crankcase enters the cylinder, pushing out the remaining exhaust gases.

#### Working of two stroke CI engine
- Suction-compression stroke: The piston moves from TDC to BDC, creating a vacuum in the crankcase. The inlet port opens and the air enters the crankcase. The piston also compresses the air in the cylinder to a high pressure and temperature. The fuel injector sprays the fuel into the cylinder near the end of the stroke.
- Expansion-exhaust stroke: The piston moves from BDC to TDC, expanding the burned gases and producing power. The exhaust port opens and the exhaust gases leave the cylinder. The transfer port also opens and the fresh air from the crankcase enters the cylinder, pushing out the remaining exhaust gases.

#### Working of four stroke SI engine
- Suction stroke: The piston moves from TDC to BDC, creating a vacuum in the cylinder. The inlet valve opens and the air-fuel mixture enters the cylinder.
- Compression stroke: The piston moves from BDC to TDC, compressing the air-fuel mixture in the cylinder. The spark plug ignites the mixture near the end of the stroke.
- Power stroke: The piston moves from TDC to BDC, expanding the burned gases and producing power. The inlet and exhaust valves are closed.
- Exhaust stroke: The piston moves from BDC to TDC, pushing out the exhaust gases from the cylinder. The exhaust valve opens and the exhaust gases leave the cylinder.
