### Ton of Refrigeration

- A ton of refrigeration (TR or TOR), also called a refrigeration ton (RT), is a unit of power used in some countries (especially in North America) to describe the heat-extraction capacity of refrigeration and air conditioning equipment .
- One ton of refrigeration is equal to the amount of heat required to melt one ton (2000 pounds) of ice in 24 hours. This is equivalent to 12,000 British thermal units per hour (BTU/h) or 3.517 kilowatts (kW) of power .
- The origin of the term ton of refrigeration dates back to the 19th century, when ice was the main source of cooling. The amount of ice needed to cool a given space was estimated by the weight of ice that melted in a day. Thus, a refrigeration machine that could produce the same cooling effect as one ton of ice was said to have a capacity of one ton of refrigeration .
- The ton of refrigeration is commonly used, and mostly used, in North America. In other parts of the world, the cooling capacity of refrigeration and air conditioning equipment is usually expressed in kilowatts or kilocalories per hour .
- The ton of refrigeration is not a standard unit in the International System of Units (SI). However, it can be converted to other units using the following formulas :

  - 1 TR = 12,000 BTU/h = 3.517 kW = 3,024 kcal/h
  - 1 kW = 0.2843 TR = 3,412 BTU/h = 860 kcal/h
  - 1 BTU/h = 0.000293 TR = 0.000293 kW = 0.252 kcal/h
  - 1 kcal/h = 0.001163 TR = 0.001163 kW = 3.968 BTU/h
