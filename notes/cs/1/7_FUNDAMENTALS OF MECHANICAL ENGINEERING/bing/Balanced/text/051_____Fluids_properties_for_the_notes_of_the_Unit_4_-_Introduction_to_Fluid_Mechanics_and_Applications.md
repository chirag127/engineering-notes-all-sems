### Fluid Properties for the Notes of the Unit 4 - Introduction to Fluid Mechanics and Applications in the Subject of Fundamentals of Mechanical Engineering

Fluid mechanics is the science concerned with the response of fluids to forces exerted upon them. Fluids are substances that can flow and deform under applied shear stress, such as liquids and gases. The study of fluids is important for many applications in engineering, such as hydraulic systems, aerodynamics, fluid power, heat transfer, and lubrication.

To understand the behavior of fluids, we need to know some of their basic properties, such as density, specific weight, specific volume, specific gravity, and viscosity. These properties are defined and calculated as follows:

- **Density or mass density** is the ratio of the mass of a fluid to the volume of the fluid. It is denoted by the Greek letter rho ($\rho$) and is expressed in units of kg/m$^3$ or lb/ft$^3$. Density is a measure of how much mass is contained in a given volume of fluid .

- **Specific weight or weight density** is the ratio of the weight of a fluid to the volume of the fluid. It is denoted by the Greek letter gamma ($\gamma$) and is expressed in units of N/m$^3$ or lb/ft$^3$. Specific weight is a measure of how much gravitational force is acting on a given volume of fluid .

- **Specific volume** is the reciprocal of density, that is, the ratio of the volume of a fluid to the mass of the fluid. It is denoted by the letter v and is expressed in units of m$^3$/kg or ft$^3$/lb. Specific volume is a measure of how much space is occupied by a given mass of fluid .

- **Specific gravity** is the ratio of the density of a fluid to the density of a standard fluid, usually water at 4°C or air at standard atmospheric conditions. It is denoted by the letter S and is a dimensionless quantity. Specific gravity is a measure of how heavy a fluid is compared to water or air .

- **Viscosity** is the measure of the internal friction or resistance of a fluid to flow. It is denoted by the Greek letter mu ($\mu$) for dynamic viscosity or nu ($\nu$) for kinematic viscosity and is expressed in units of N.s/m$^2$ or ft.lb/s/ft$^2$ for dynamic viscosity or m$^2$/s or ft$^2$/s for kinematic viscosity. Viscosity is a measure of how easily a fluid can be deformed or sheared by an applied force .

These are some of the basic properties of fluids that are useful for the analysis of fluid mechanics problems. Other properties, such as compressibility, surface tension, and capillarity, will be discussed in later units.
