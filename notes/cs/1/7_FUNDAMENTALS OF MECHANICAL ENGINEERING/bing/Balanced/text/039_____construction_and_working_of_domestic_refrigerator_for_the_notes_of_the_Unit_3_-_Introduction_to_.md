### Construction and Working of Domestic Refrigerator

- A domestic refrigerator is a device that uses the vapor compression cycle to maintain a low temperature inside a closed cabinet for storing food and other items.
- The main components of a domestic refrigerator are  :
  - **Compressor**: A hermetically sealed pump that compresses the refrigerant gas from low pressure to high pressure.
  - **Condenser**: A coil of metal pipe located at the back of the refrigerator that dissipates the heat from the hot refrigerant gas to the surrounding air, causing it to condense into a liquid.
  - **Expansion valve**: A device that reduces the pressure and temperature of the high-pressure liquid refrigerant by allowing it to flow through a narrow opening.
  - **Evaporator**: A coil of metal pipe located inside the freezer box that absorbs heat from the contents of the refrigerator, causing the liquid refrigerant to evaporate into a gas.
  - **Capillary tube**: A thin tube that connects the condenser and the evaporator, acting as a metering device for the refrigerant flow.
  - **Thermostat**: A device that controls the temperature inside the refrigerator by switching the compressor on and off according to the set point.
  - **Accumulator**: A device that collects any liquid refrigerant that may have entered the compressor, preventing damage to the compressor.
  - **Drier and strainer**: A device that removes any moisture and impurities from the refrigerant, preventing corrosion and clogging of the system.

- The working principle of a domestic refrigerator is as follows  :
  - The compressor compresses the refrigerant gas from low pressure to high pressure, increasing its temperature.
  - The hot refrigerant gas flows through the condenser, where it releases its heat to the surrounding air and condenses into a liquid.
  - The high-pressure liquid refrigerant passes through the expansion valve, where it undergoes a sudden drop in pressure and temperature, becoming a cold liquid-vapor mixture.
  - The cold refrigerant flows through the evaporator, where it absorbs heat from the contents of the refrigerator, evaporating into a gas.
  - The low-pressure refrigerant gas returns to the compressor, completing the cycle.
  - The thermostat monitors the temperature inside the refrigerator and switches the compressor on and off to maintain the desired level of cooling.
  - The accumulator collects any liquid refrigerant that may have entered the compressor, protecting it from damage.
  - The drier and strainer removes any moisture and impurities from the refrigerant, ensuring the efficiency and reliability of the system.
