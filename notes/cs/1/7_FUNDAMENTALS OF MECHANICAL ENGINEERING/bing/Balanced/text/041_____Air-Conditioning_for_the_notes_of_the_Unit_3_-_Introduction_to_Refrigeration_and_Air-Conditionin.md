### Air-Conditioning

- Air-conditioning is the process of treating air so as to control simultaneously its temperature, humidity, purity and distribution to meet the requirements of the conditioned space, such as comfort and health of human beings and other needs of the situation.
- Air-conditioning can also be defined as the science which deals with supply and maintaining desirable internal atmospheric condition irrespective of external condition.
- Air-conditioning is based on the principles of thermodynamics, which state that heat flows from a higher temperature region to a lower temperature region, and that work is required to transfer heat against the natural direction of flow .
- Air-conditioning operates using the refrigeration cycle, which consists of four processes: compression, condensation, expansion, and evaporation .
- Air-conditioning requires a specific refrigerant as the working fluid in the refrigeration cycle. A refrigerant is a substance that can change its phase from liquid to vapor and vice versa at low temperatures and pressures .
- Air-conditioning can be classified into different types based on the purpose, the system, and the refrigerant used. Some common types are:
  - Comfort air-conditioning: This type of air-conditioning is used to provide comfortable and healthy conditions for human occupants in residential, commercial, and public buildings .
  - Industrial air-conditioning: This type of air-conditioning is used to provide suitable conditions for industrial processes, such as manufacturing, storage, testing, etc .
  - Central air-conditioning: This type of air-conditioning is used to cool and dehumidify large areas or multiple rooms through a network of ducts and vents. A central unit, usually located outdoors, contains the compressor, condenser, and expansion valve, while the indoor units, usually located on the ceiling or wall, contain the evaporator and fan .
  - Split air-conditioning: This type of air-conditioning is used to cool and dehumidify a single room or a small area. A split unit consists of two parts: an outdoor unit, which contains the compressor, condenser, and expansion valve, and an indoor unit, which contains the evaporator and fan. The two units are connected by pipes that carry the refrigerant .
  - Window air-conditioning: This type of air-conditioning is used to cool and dehumidify a single room or a small area. A window unit is a compact device that fits into a window or a wall opening. It contains all the components of the refrigeration cycle in one unit .
  - Vapor compression system: This type of air-conditioning is the most common and widely used system. It uses a mechanical compressor to compress the refrigerant vapor, which then condenses into liquid at a higher pressure and temperature. The liquid refrigerant then passes through an expansion valve, which reduces its pressure and temperature, and enters the evaporator, where it absorbs heat from the air and evaporates into vapor again. The cycle repeats continuously  .
  - Vapor absorption system: This type of air-conditioning is an alternative system that does not use a mechanical compressor, but instead uses a heat source, such as gas, steam, or solar energy, to drive the refrigeration cycle. It uses a solution of refrigerant and absorbent, which are two substances that can dissolve each other. The solution is heated in a generator, where the refrigerant vaporizes and separates from the absorbent. The refrigerant vapor then follows the same steps as in the vapor compression system, while the absorbent solution is cooled and pumped back to the generator. The cycle repeats continuously  .
