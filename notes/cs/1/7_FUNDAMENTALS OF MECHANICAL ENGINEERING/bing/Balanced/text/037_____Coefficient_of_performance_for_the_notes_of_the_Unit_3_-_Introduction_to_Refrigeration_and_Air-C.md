### Coefficient of performance for the notes of the Unit 3 - Introduction to Refrigeration and Air-Conditioning in the subject of FUNDAMENTALS OF MECHANICAL ENGINEERING

- The coefficient of performance (COP) is a measure of the efficiency of a refrigeration or air-conditioning system. It is defined as the ratio of the useful cooling or heating effect to the work input required by the system .
- The COP can be expressed as:

  COP = Q/W

  where Q is the heat removed from the cold reservoir (for refrigeration) or the heat delivered to the hot reservoir (for heating), and W is the work done by the compressor.
- The COP depends on the temperatures of the reservoirs and the type of refrigerant used. Higher COP values indicate higher efficiency, lower energy consumption and lower operating costs .
- The COP of a refrigeration system can vary from 2 to 6, depending on the temperature difference between the cold and hot reservoirs. For example, a refrigerator that maintains an inside temperature of -18°C and operates in an environment of 25°C has a COP of about 2.6.
- The COP of an air-conditioning system can vary from 1 to 4, depending on the temperature difference between the indoor and outdoor air. For example, an air-conditioner that maintains an indoor temperature of 20°C and operates in an environment of 35°C has a COP of about 2.5.
- The COP of a heat pump system can vary from 2 to 10, depending on the temperature difference between the source and the sink of heat. For example, a heat pump that extracts heat from the ground at 10°C and delivers it to a house at 20°C has a COP of about 6.
- The COP of a refrigeration or air-conditioning system can be improved by using more efficient compressors, heat exchangers and refrigerants, reducing heat losses and leakage, and maintaining proper operation and maintenance .
