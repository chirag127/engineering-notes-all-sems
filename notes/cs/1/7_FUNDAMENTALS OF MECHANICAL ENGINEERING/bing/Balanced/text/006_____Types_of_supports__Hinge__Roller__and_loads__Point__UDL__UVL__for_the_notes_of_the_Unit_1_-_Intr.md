### Types of supports (Hinge, Roller) and loads (Point, UDL, UVL) for the notes of the Unit 1 - Introduction to Mechanics in the subject of FUNDAMENTALS OF MECHANICAL ENGINEERING

- Supports are devices that connect a structural member to the ground or to another part of the structure and provide resistance to forces and moments.
- Supports can be classified into four types: simple, roller, hinge, and fixed.
- Simple support is a support that allows free rotation and horizontal translation, but prevents vertical translation. It can resist only a vertical force.
- Roller support is a support that allows free rotation and horizontal translation along the surface on which it rests, but prevents vertical translation. It can resist only a vertical force.
- Hinge support is a support that allows free rotation about any axis, but prevents horizontal and vertical translation. It can resist both horizontal and vertical forces, but not moments.
- Fixed support is a support that prevents rotation and translation in any direction. It can resist horizontal and vertical forces as well as moments.
- The type of support affects the stability and determinacy of a structure, as well as the number and direction of the reactions.
- Loads are external forces or moments that act on a structural member and cause deformation, stress, or displacement.
- Loads can be classified into three types: point, uniformly distributed (UDL), and uniformly varying (UVL).
- Point load is a load that acts at a single point on a structural member. It can be represented by a single force or moment vector.
- Uniformly distributed load (UDL) is a load that acts with constant intensity along a length or area of a structural member. It can be represented by a force or moment per unit length or area.
- Uniformly varying load (UVL) is a load that acts with varying intensity along a length or area of a structural member. It can be represented by a function of force or moment per unit length or area.
