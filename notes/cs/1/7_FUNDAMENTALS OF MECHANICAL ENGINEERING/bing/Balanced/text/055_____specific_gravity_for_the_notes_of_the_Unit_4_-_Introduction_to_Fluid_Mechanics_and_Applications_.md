### Specific Gravity

- Specific gravity is a dimensionless quantity that compares the density of a substance with the density of a reference substance, usually water at 4°C .
- Specific gravity can be calculated using the following formula  :

  `Specific Gravity = Density of substance / Density of water`

- Specific gravity is also known as relative density.
- Specific gravity has no units, but it is often expressed in terms of grams per cubic centimeter (g/cm³) or kilograms per cubic meter (kg/m³) .
- Specific gravity can be used to determine the purity of a substance, the concentration of a solution, or the buoyancy of an object  .
- Specific gravity can be affected by temperature, pressure, and solutes .
- Some examples of specific gravity values for common substances are:

  - Gold: 19.3
  - Mercury: 13.6
  - Lead: 11.3
  - Iron: 7.9
  - Aluminum: 2.7
  - Ice: 0.92
  - Ethanol: 0.79
  - Air: 0.0012
