### Free Body Diagram

A free body diagram is a graphical tool that helps us analyze the forces acting on an object in a given situation. It shows the object as a point or a simple shape, and the forces as arrows pointing away from the object. A free body diagram can help us find the net force, the equilibrium condition, and the motion of the object.

Some steps to draw a free body diagram are:

- Identify the object of interest and isolate it from its surroundings. Draw a point or a simple shape to represent the object.
- Identify all the forces acting on the object and their directions. Draw arrows extending from the object to show the forces. Label the arrows with the type and magnitude of the force, if known.
- If the object is in contact with another object or a surface, include the normal force and the friction force, if any. The normal force is perpendicular to the surface, and the friction force is parallel to the surface and opposite to the motion or tendency of motion.
- If the object is under the influence of gravity, include the weight force, which is equal to the mass of the object times the acceleration due to gravity. The weight force is always directed downward.
- If the object is attached to a string, rope, or cable, include the tension force, which is equal to the pull exerted by the string, rope, or cable. The tension force is always directed along the string, rope, or cable.
- If the object is subject to a spring force, include the spring force, which is proportional to the displacement of the spring from its equilibrium position. The spring force is always directed toward or away from the equilibrium position, depending on whether the spring is compressed or stretched.
- If the object is subject to any other external force, such as a push, a pull, an electric force, a magnetic force, etc., include that force and its direction.

Some examples of free body diagrams are:

- A book resting on a table. The forces acting on the book are the weight force downward, the normal force upward from the table, and the friction force to the left or right from the table, depending on whether the book is sliding or not.

- A car moving on a flat road. The forces acting on the car are the weight force downward, the normal force upward from the road, the friction force backward from the road, and the driving force forward from the engine.

![car on road](https://www.physicsclassroom.com/Class/newtlaws/u2l2a3.gif)

- A pendulum swinging from a fixed point. The forces acting on the pendulum are the weight force downward, and the tension force upward and to the left or right from the string.
