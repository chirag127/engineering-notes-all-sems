### Density

- Density is the measurement of how tightly a material is packed together. It is defined as the mass per unit volume.
- Density is a physical property that depends on the temperature and pressure of the substance. It can be used to identify or compare different materials.
- The symbol for density is D or ρ (the Greek letter rho). The SI unit of density is kilogram per cubic metre (kg/m^3^). Other common units of density are gram per cubic centimetre (g/cm^3^), gram per millilitre (g/mL), and kilogram per litre (kg/L).
- The formula for density is d = M / V, where d is density, M is mass, and V is volume. To calculate the density of an object, we need to measure its mass and volume and then divide them.
- Density can be measured by various methods, such as using a balance and a graduated cylinder, using a hydrometer, or using Archimedes' principle.
- Density is an intensive property, which means it does not depend on the amount of the substance. For example, a small piece of iron and a large piece of iron have the same density.
- Density can vary with temperature and pressure. Generally, density decreases when temperature increases, and density increases when pressure increases. This is because the molecules of the substance move faster and farther apart when heated, and move slower and closer together when compressed.
- Density can be used to determine whether an object will float or sink in a fluid. An object will float if its density is less than the density of the fluid, and sink if its density is greater than the density of the fluid.
- Density can also be used to calculate the specific gravity of a substance, which is the ratio of its density to the density of water at a given temperature. Specific gravity is a dimensionless quantity that can be used to compare the densities of different substances.
- Some examples of density are:

  - The density of water is 1 g/cm^3^ or 1000 kg/m^3^ at 4°C and 1 atm pressure.
  - The density of air is 1.29 kg/m^3^ at 0°C and 1 atm pressure.
  - The density of iron is 7.87 g/cm^3^ or 7870 kg/m^3^ at 20°C and 1 atm pressure.
  - The density of gold is 19.3 g/cm^3^ or 19300 kg/m^3^ at 20°C and 1 atm pressure.
  - The density of diamond is 3.51 g/cm^3^ or 3510 kg/m^3^ at 20°C and 1 atm pressure.
  - The density of cork is 0.24 g/cm^3^ or 240 kg/m^3^ at 20°C and 1 atm pressure.
