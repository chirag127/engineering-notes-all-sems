### Equilibrium Equations and Support Reactions

- Equilibrium equations are the conditions that must be satisfied for a structure or a body to be in a state of static equilibrium, meaning that there is no net force or moment acting on it.
- The equilibrium equations can be derived from Newton's laws of motion, and they are:

  - Sum of forces in x-direction = 0
  - Sum of forces in y-direction = 0
  - Sum of moments about any point = 0

- Support reactions are the forces and moments that are exerted by the supports on the structure or the body to keep it in equilibrium.
- The types of support reactions depend on the types of supports, such as:

  - A pin or a hinge support can provide two reaction forces in x and y directions, but no moment.
  - A roller support can provide one reaction force perpendicular to the surface, but no moment.
  - A fixed or a built-in support can provide two reaction forces in x and y directions, and one reaction moment.

- To determine the support reactions, the following steps are usually followed:

  - Draw a free body diagram of the structure or the body, showing all the applied loads and the support reactions as unknowns.
  - Apply the equilibrium equations to the free body diagram, and solve for the unknowns.
  - If the number of unknowns is equal to the number of equilibrium equations, the structure or the body is determinate, meaning that the support reactions can be found by statics alone.
  - If the number of unknowns is greater than the number of equilibrium equations, the structure or the body is indeterminate, meaning that the support reactions cannot be found by statics alone, and additional methods such as compatibility equations or deformation analysis are needed.
