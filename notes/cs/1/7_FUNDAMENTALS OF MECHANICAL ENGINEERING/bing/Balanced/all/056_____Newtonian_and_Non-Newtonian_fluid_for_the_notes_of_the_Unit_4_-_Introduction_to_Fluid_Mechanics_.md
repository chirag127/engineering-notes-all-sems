# Newtonian and Non-Newtonian Fluids

- Fluids can be divided into two major types: **Newtonian** and **non-Newtonian** .
- **Newtonian fluids** are the ones that obey **Newton’s law of constant viscosity** . These fluids have a **constant viscosity** and **zero shear rate and shear stress** . Examples of Newtonian fluids are water, mineral oil, gasoline, and alcohol.
- **Non-Newtonian fluids** are those that do not have a constant viscosity and have a **variable relationship with shear stress** . These fluids can change their viscosity when under force to either more liquid or more solid. Examples of non-Newtonian fluids are ketchup, blood, yogurt, and toothpaste .
- The importance of knowing the type of fluid is that it affects the **processing** and **handling** of the fluid. For example, non-Newtonian fluids may require different types of pumps, pipes, valves, and mixers than Newtonian fluids. Non-Newtonian fluids may also exhibit different flow behaviors such as **thixotropy**, **rheopecty**, **dilatancy**, and **Bingham plasticity** .
- Thixotropy is the property of some non-Newtonian fluids to become less viscous over time when shaken, agitated, or otherwise stressed. Rheopecty is the opposite of thixotropy, where some non-Newtonian fluids become more viscous over time when subjected to stress. Dilatancy is the property of some non-Newtonian fluids to become more viscous when subjected to shear stress. Bingham plasticity is the property of some non-Newtonian fluids to behave like a solid until a certain amount of stress is applied, then flow like a liquid.
- To summarize, Newtonian fluids have a constant viscosity and zero shear rate and shear stress, while non-Newtonian fluids have a variable viscosity and a complex relationship with shear stress. Non-Newtonian fluids can also exhibit different flow behaviors depending on the type and amount of stress applied. Knowing the type of fluid is important for processing and handling purposes.
