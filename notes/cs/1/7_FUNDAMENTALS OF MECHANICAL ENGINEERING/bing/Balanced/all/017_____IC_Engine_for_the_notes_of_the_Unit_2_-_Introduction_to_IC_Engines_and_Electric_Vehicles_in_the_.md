# IC Engine

An IC engine is a machine that transforms the energy released from the combustion of fuel into mechanical energy. The motion of the piston is then converted into rotary motion by a crankshaft, which can be used to drive a wide range of machines and vehicles.

## Classification of IC Engines

IC engines can be classified according to different criteria, such as:

- The type of fuel used: gas, petrol, kerosene, diesel, etc.
- The working cycle: Otto, Diesel, Dual, etc.
- The speed of the engine: low, medium, high, etc.
- The number of cylinders: single, twin, multi, etc.
- The arrangement of cylinders: inline, V, W, radial, etc.
- The valve arrangement: overhead, side, sleeve, etc.
- The cooling system: air, water, oil, etc.
- The ignition system: spark, compression, etc.
- The fuel injection system: carburetor, direct, indirect, etc.

## Components of IC Engines

The main components of an IC engine are:

- Cylinder: It is the cylindrical chamber in which the piston moves and the combustion takes place.
- Piston: It is a cylindrical part that fits inside the cylinder and transmits the force of the combustion to the crankshaft.
- Connecting rod: It is a rod that connects the piston to the crankshaft and converts the reciprocating motion of the piston into rotary motion of the crankshaft.
- Crankshaft: It is a shaft that rotates with the help of the connecting rod and delivers the power to the load.
- Cylinder head: It is the part that covers the top of the cylinder and contains the valves, spark plug, injector, etc.
- Valves: They are the devices that control the flow of air and fuel into the cylinder and the flow of exhaust gases out of the cylinder.
- Spark plug: It is a device that produces a spark to ignite the air-fuel mixture in the cylinder of a spark ignition engine.
- Injector: It is a device that injects the fuel into the cylinder of a compression ignition engine.
- Carburetor: It is a device that mixes the air and fuel in a proper ratio before sending it to the cylinder of a spark ignition engine.
- Air filter: It is a device that filters the air before it enters the engine to remove dust and dirt particles.
- Fuel filter: It is a device that filters the fuel before it enters the engine to remove impurities and water.
- Exhaust manifold: It is a pipe that collects the exhaust gases from the cylinders and sends them to the muffler and the tail pipe.
- Muffler: It is a device that reduces the noise of the exhaust gases by absorbing the sound waves.
- Tail pipe: It is a pipe that releases the exhaust gases into the atmosphere.

## Working Principle of IC Engines

The working principle of an IC engine is based on the conversion of chemical energy of the fuel into mechanical energy by the process of combustion. The combustion takes place inside the cylinder at a high temperature and pressure, which pushes the piston downwards. The piston then moves the connecting rod, which rotates the crankshaft. The crankshaft then delivers the power to the load.

The working cycle of an IC engine consists of four strokes: intake, compression, power, and exhaust. These strokes are repeated in a cyclic manner to produce continuous power output. The following diagram shows the four strokes of an IC engine:

- Intake stroke: The piston moves from the top dead center (TDC) to the bottom dead center (BDC), creating a vacuum in the cylinder. The intake valve opens and the air-fuel mixture enters the cylinder through the intake manifold.
- Compression stroke: The piston moves from the BDC to the TDC, compressing the air-fuel mixture in the cylinder. The intake valve closes and the compression ratio increases. The compression ratio is the ratio of the volume of the cylinder when the piston is at the BDC to the volume of the cylinder when the piston is at the TDC.
- Power stroke: The piston is at the TDC and the air-fuel mixture is ignited by the spark plug (in a spark ignition engine) or by the high temperature and pressure (in a compression ignition engine).
