# Equilibrium Equations and Support Reactions

- Equilibrium equations are the conditions that must be satisfied for a structure or a body to be in static equilibrium, i.e., to have no net force or moment acting on it.
- The equilibrium equations for a two-dimensional system are:

  - Sum of forces in the x-direction is zero: $\sum F_x = 0$
  - Sum of forces in the y-direction is zero: $\sum F_y = 0$
  - Sum of moments about any point is zero: $\sum M = 0$

- The equilibrium equations for a three-dimensional system are:

  - Sum of forces in the x-direction is zero: $\sum F_x = 0$
  - Sum of forces in the y-direction is zero: $\sum F_y = 0$
  - Sum of forces in the z-direction is zero: $\sum F_z = 0$
  - Sum of moments about the x-axis is zero: $\sum M_x = 0$
  - Sum of moments about the y-axis is zero: $\sum M_y = 0$
  - Sum of moments about the z-axis is zero: $\sum M_z = 0$

- Support reactions are the forces and moments that are applied by the supports or constraints on a structure or a body to keep it in equilibrium.
- The types of support reactions depend on the types of supports or constraints, such as:

  - A pin or a hinge support can provide two reaction forces in the x- and y-directions, but no moment.
  - A roller or a rocker support can provide one reaction force in the direction perpendicular to the surface, but no moment.
  - A fixed or a built-in support can provide two reaction forces in the x- and y-directions, and one reaction moment.
  - A cable or a rope support can provide one reaction force in the direction of the cable or rope, but no moment.

- To compute the support reactions for a planar structure, the following steps can be followed:

  - Draw a free-body diagram of the structure, showing all the applied loads and the support reactions as unknowns.
  - Apply the equilibrium equations to the free-body diagram, and solve for the unknown support reactions.
  - If the number of unknowns is equal to the number of equilibrium equations, the structure is statically determinate, and the support reactions can be found uniquely.
  - If the number of unknowns is more than the number of equilibrium equations, the structure is statically indeterminate, and the support reactions cannot be found by equilibrium equations alone. Additional methods, such as compatibility equations, are needed.
  - If the number of unknowns is less than the number of equilibrium equations, the structure is unstable, and the support reactions cannot be found. The structure will collapse or move under any load.
