# Construction and Working of Two Stroke and Four Stroke SI and CI Engines

## Two Stroke Engine

- A two stroke engine is an internal combustion engine that completes one power cycle in two piston strokes or one crankshaft revolution.
- A two stroke engine uses piston-controlled ports instead of valves which makes its construction less complex than the four stroke engine.
- A two stroke engine uses the 'total loss lubrication' method to lubricate the engine components in which the oil is previously added to the fuel. Thus the oil directly enters the engine along with the fuel.
- A two stroke engine can be classified into two types: petrol engine and diesel engine.

### Two Stroke Petrol Engine

- A two stroke petrol engine works on the principle of spark ignition. It uses a mixture of air and fuel as the working medium.
- A two stroke petrol engine has the following main components: cylinder, piston, spark plug, inlet port, transfer port, exhaust port, crankcase, and crankshaft.
- A two stroke petrol engine completes one power cycle in the following two strokes:

#### Suction-Compression Stroke

- In this stroke, the piston moves from the bottom dead center (BDC) to the top dead center (TDC), compressing the fresh charge in the crankcase and the residual gases in the cylinder.
- As the piston moves up, it uncovers the inlet port and allows the fresh charge to enter the crankcase through the carburetor.
- At the same time, the piston covers the transfer port and the exhaust port, sealing the cylinder from the crankcase.
- Near the end of this stroke, the spark plug ignites the compressed charge in the cylinder, causing a rapid increase in pressure and temperature.

#### Power-Exhaust Stroke

- In this stroke, the piston moves from the TDC to the BDC, expanding the burnt gases and producing useful work on the piston.
- As the piston moves down, it uncovers the exhaust port and allows the burnt gases to escape through the exhaust manifold.
- At the same time, the piston also uncovers the transfer port and allows the fresh charge from the crankcase to enter the cylinder through the deflector on the piston crown.
- The fresh charge helps to scavenge the remaining burnt gases from the cylinder and prepares it for the next cycle.

### Two Stroke Diesel Engine

- A two stroke diesel engine works on the principle of compression ignition. It uses only air as the working medium and injects fuel at the end of the compression stroke.
- A two stroke diesel engine has the following main components: cylinder, piston, fuel injector, inlet port, transfer port, exhaust port, crankcase, and crankshaft.
- A two stroke diesel engine completes one power cycle in the following two strokes:

#### Suction-Compression Stroke

- In this stroke, the piston moves from the BDC to the TDC, compressing the air in the crankcase and the cylinder.
- As the piston moves up, it uncovers the inlet port and allows the fresh air to enter the crankcase through the air filter.
- At the same time, the piston covers the transfer port and the exhaust port, sealing the cylinder from the crankcase.
- Near the end of this stroke, the fuel injector sprays the fuel into the cylinder, where it mixes with the hot compressed air and ignites spontaneously, causing a rapid increase in pressure and temperature.

#### Power-Exhaust Stroke

- In this stroke, the piston moves from the TDC to the BDC, expanding the burnt gases and producing useful work on the piston.
- As the piston moves down, it uncovers the exhaust port and allows the burnt gases to escape through the exhaust manifold.
- At the same time, the piston also uncovers the transfer port and allows the fresh air from the crankcase to enter the cylinder through the deflector on the piston crown.
- The fresh air helps to scavenge the remaining burnt gases from the cylinder and prepares it for the next cycle.

## Four Stroke Engine

- A four stroke engine is an internal combustion engine that completes one power cycle in four piston strokes or two crankshaft revolutions.
- A four stroke engine uses valves to control the flow of the working medium into and out of the cylinder. It also uses a separate lubrication system to lubricate the engine components.
- A four stroke engine can be classified into two types: petrol engine and diesel engine.
