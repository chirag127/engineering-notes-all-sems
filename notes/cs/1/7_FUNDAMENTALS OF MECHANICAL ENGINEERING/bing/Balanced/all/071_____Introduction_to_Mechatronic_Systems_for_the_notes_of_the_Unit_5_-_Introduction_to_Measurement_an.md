# Introduction to Mechatronic Systems

- A mechatronic system is a system that integrates mechanical, electrical, and computer engineering components to perform a desired function.
- Mechatronic systems can be found in various applications, such as robotics, automotive, aerospace, biomedical, manufacturing, and consumer products.
- Mechatronic systems are designed using a multidisciplinary approach that considers the interactions and trade-offs among different subsystems and components.
- Mechatronic systems typically consist of four main elements: sensors, actuators, controllers, and interfaces.
  - Sensors are devices that measure physical quantities, such as position, velocity, force, temperature, pressure, etc., and convert them into electrical signals.
  - Actuators are devices that convert electrical signals into physical actions, such as motion, force, torque, etc., to control the output of the system.
  - Controllers are devices that process the sensor signals and generate the actuator signals, using algorithms, logic, or feedback, to achieve the desired system performance and behavior.
  - Interfaces are devices that enable the communication and interaction between the system and the user, the environment, or other systems, using displays, keyboards, switches, wireless networks, etc.
- Mechatronic systems are characterized by some key features, such as:
  - Synergy: the system performance is greater than the sum of the individual component performances, due to the integration and coordination of different domains and disciplines.
  - Adaptability: the system can adjust its behavior and parameters according to the changes in the operating conditions, the user preferences, or the external disturbances, using feedback, learning, or optimization techniques.
  - Intelligence: the system can perform complex tasks, such as perception, decision making, planning, or learning, using artificial intelligence, machine learning, or computer vision methods.
  - Autonomy: the system can operate independently, without human intervention or supervision, using self-diagnosis, self-repair, or self-organization capabilities.
