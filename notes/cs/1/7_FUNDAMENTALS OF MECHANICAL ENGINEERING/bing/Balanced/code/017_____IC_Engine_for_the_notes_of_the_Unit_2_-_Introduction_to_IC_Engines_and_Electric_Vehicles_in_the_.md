### IC Engine

- An IC engine (internal combustion engine) is a type of heat engine that converts the heat energy released during combustion of fuel into mechanical work  .
- The combustion takes place within the engine cylinder, which is an integral part of the working fluid flow circuit.
- The working fluid is usually a mixture of air and fuel, which is compressed, ignited, and expanded in the cylinder, pushing the piston and producing power.
- The IC engine can use liquid and gaseous fuels for combustion, such as gasoline, diesel, natural gas, propane, biodiesel, or ethanol .
- The IC engine can be classified into two main types based on the cycle of operation: spark ignition (SI) engine and compression ignition (CI) engine .
  - In an SI engine, the fuel-air mixture is ignited by a spark plug at the end of the compression stroke. The SI engine is also known as a gasoline or petrol engine.
  - In a CI engine, the air is compressed to a high pressure and temperature, and then the fuel is injected directly into the cylinder. The fuel-air mixture ignites spontaneously due to the high temperature. The CI engine is also known as a diesel engine.
- The IC engine can also be classified into two main types based on the number of strokes: four-stroke engine and two-stroke engine .
  - In a four-stroke engine, the piston completes four strokes (intake, compression, power, and exhaust) in two revolutions of the crankshaft. The four-stroke engine is more efficient and cleaner than the two-stroke engine, but it is also more complex and heavier.
  - In a two-stroke engine, the piston completes two strokes (compression and power) in one revolution of the crankshaft. The two-stroke engine is simpler and lighter than the four-stroke engine, but it is also less efficient and more polluting.
- The IC engine can also be classified into different types based on the arrangement of cylinders: inline, V, W, radial, opposed, or rotary .
  - In an inline engine, the cylinders are arranged in a single row along the crankshaft. The inline engine is simple and compact, but it has a limit on the number of cylinders.
  - In a V engine, the cylinders are arranged in two rows at an angle to the crankshaft. The V engine can accommodate more cylinders than the inline engine, but it is also more complex and bulky.
  - In a W engine, the cylinders are arranged in three rows at an angle to the crankshaft. The W engine can accommodate even more cylinders than the V engine, but it is also very complex and rare.
  - In a radial engine, the cylinders are arranged in a circle around the crankshaft. The radial engine is compact and powerful, but it has a poor cooling and balance.
  - In an opposed engine, the cylinders are arranged in two rows opposite to each other along the crankshaft. The opposed engine has a good balance and cooling, but it is also wide and flat.
  - In a rotary engine, the cylinders are arranged in a ring around a triangular rotor that rotates inside a housing. The rotary engine is also known as a Wankel engine. The rotary engine has a high power-to-weight ratio and smooth operation, but it also has a low efficiency and high emissions.
