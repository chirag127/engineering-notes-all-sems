### Difference between two-stroke and four stroke IC engines and SI and CI Engines

- Two-stroke and four-stroke engines are types of internal combustion engines that convert chemical energy into mechanical energy by burning fuel.
- SI and CI engines are types of internal combustion engines that differ in the way they ignite the fuel-air mixture inside the cylinder.

#### Two-stroke vs four-stroke engines

- A two-stroke engine completes one power cycle in two strokes of the piston, while a four-stroke engine completes one power cycle in four strokes of the piston.
- A stroke is the movement of the piston from one end of the cylinder to the other.
- A power cycle consists of four phases: intake, compression, power, and exhaust.
- In a two-stroke engine, the intake and exhaust phases occur simultaneously during the first stroke, while the compression and power phases occur simultaneously during the second stroke.
- In a four-stroke engine, each phase occurs separately during each stroke.
- A two-stroke engine has one revolution of the crankshaft per power cycle, while a four-stroke engine has two revolutions of the crankshaft per power cycle.
- A two-stroke engine has a simpler design, lighter weight, higher power-to-weight ratio, and easier starting than a four-stroke engine.
- A four-stroke engine has better fuel efficiency, lower emissions, more torque at lower rpm, and longer durability than a two-stroke engine.

#### SI vs CI engines

- SI stands for spark ignition, while CI stands for compression ignition.
- In an SI engine, the fuel-air mixture is ignited by a spark plug at the end of the compression stroke, creating a rapid expansion of the gas that pushes the piston down and produces power.
- In a CI engine, the air is compressed to a high pressure and temperature, and then the fuel is injected directly into the cylinder, where it ignites spontaneously due to the heat of the compressed air, creating a gradual expansion of the gas that pushes the piston down and produces power.
- An SI engine can use gasoline, ethanol, or natural gas as fuel, while a CI engine can use diesel, biodiesel, or vegetable oil as fuel.
- An SI engine has a lower compression ratio, higher speed, lower thermal efficiency, and higher noise than a CI engine.
- A CI engine has a higher compression ratio, lower speed, higher thermal efficiency, and lower noise than an SI engine.
