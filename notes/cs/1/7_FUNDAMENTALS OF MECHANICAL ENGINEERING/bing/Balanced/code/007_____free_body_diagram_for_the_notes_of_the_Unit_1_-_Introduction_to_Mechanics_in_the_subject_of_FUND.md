# Free Body Diagram

- A free body diagram is a graphical illustration used to visualize the applied forces, moments, and resulting reactions on a body in a given condition.
- A free body diagram allows us to keep track of all of the forces acting on an object.
- A free body diagram is a special example of the vector diagrams that were discussed in an earlier unit.
- A free body diagram is a representation of an object with all the forces that act on it.
- The external environment (other objects, the floor on which the object sits, etc.), as well as the forces that the object exerts on other objects, are omitted in a free body diagram.

## Types of Free Body Diagrams

- There are two types of free body diagrams: isolated and non-isolated.
- An isolated free body diagram shows only the object of interest and the forces that act on it.
- A non-isolated free body diagram shows the object of interest and other objects that interact with it, as well as the forces that act on each object.

## How to Draw a Free Body Diagram

- To draw a free body diagram, follow these steps  :
  - Identify the object of interest and draw a dot or a simple shape to represent it.
  - Draw arrows extending from the dot or shape to show the forces that act on the object. The arrows should point in the direction of the force and have a length proportional to the magnitude of the force. Label each arrow with the name or symbol of the force.
  - If the object is in contact with another object or a surface, draw a normal force perpendicular to the contact surface and a friction force parallel to the contact surface. The normal force is the force that prevents the object from penetrating the surface, and the friction force is the force that opposes the relative motion of the object and the surface.
  - If the object is under the influence of gravity, draw a weight force pointing downward from the center of mass of the object. The weight force is the force that the earth exerts on the object, and it is equal to the mass of the object times the acceleration due to gravity.
  - If the object is attached to another object by a string, rope, cable, or spring, draw a tension force along the direction of the attachment. The tension force is the force that the attachment exerts on the object, and it is equal to the force that the object exerts on the attachment.
  - If the object is subjected to any other external forces, such as applied forces, electric forces, magnetic forces, or air resistance, draw them accordingly and label them with their names or symbols.
