### Strain and Strain Gauges

- Strain is the measure of the deformation or change in dimensions of a body due to an applied force or stress.
- Strain is defined as the ratio of the change in length to the original length of the body.
- Strain can be positive (tensile) or negative (compressive) depending on the direction of the applied force.
- Strain gauges are devices that can measure strain by converting it into a change in electrical resistance.
- Strain gauges are made of thin metallic wires or foils that are attached to the surface of the body under stress.
- When the body deforms, the strain gauge also deforms and changes its resistance according to the strain.
- The change in resistance can be measured by a Wheatstone bridge circuit and calibrated to give the value of strain.
- Strain gauges can be classified into two types: bonded and unbonded strain gauges.

### Bonded Strain Gauges

- Bonded strain gauges are strain gauges that are glued or cemented to the surface of the body whose strain is being measured.
- Bonded strain gauges are in direct contact with the body and transfer the entire strain to the strain gauge.
- Bonded strain gauges are more sensitive, accurate, and reliable than unbonded strain gauges.
- Bonded strain gauges can be further classified into two types: metallic and semiconductor strain gauges.
- Metallic strain gauges are made of metals such as constantan, nichrome, platinum, or copper-nickel alloys that have a high gauge factor (ratio of relative change in resistance to relative change in length).
- Semiconductor strain gauges are made of materials such as silicon or germanium that have a very high gauge factor (up to 100 times higher than metallic strain gauges) and can measure very small strains.
- However, semiconductor strain gauges are also more sensitive to temperature changes and require temperature compensation.

### Unbonded Strain Gauges

- Unbonded strain gauges are strain gauges that are not attached to the surface of the body whose strain is being measured.
- Unbonded strain gauges are suspended between two frames that are connected to the body by flexible wires or rods.
- When the body deforms, the frames also deform and change the tension in the strain gauge wire.
- Unbonded strain gauges are less sensitive, accurate, and reliable than bonded strain gauges.
- Unbonded strain gauges are also more prone to errors due to friction, hysteresis, and creep.
- Unbonded strain gauges are mainly used for measuring large strains or dynamic strains that vary rapidly with time.
