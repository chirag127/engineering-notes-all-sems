### Types of supports (Hinge, Roller) and loads (Point, UDL, UVL) for the notes of the Unit 1 - Introduction to Mechanics in the subject of FUNDAMENTALS OF MECHANICAL ENGINEERING

- Supports are devices that connect a structural member to the ground or to another part of the structure and provide resistance to forces and moments.
- Supports can be classified into four types: simple, roller, hinge, and fixed.
- Simple support is a support that allows free rotation and horizontal translation, but prevents vertical translation. It can resist only a vertical force. An example of a simple support is a beam resting on a wall.
- Roller support is a support that allows free rotation and horizontal translation, but prevents vertical translation. It can resist only a vertical force. An example of a roller support is a beam resting on a roller.
- Hinge support is a support that allows free rotation, but prevents horizontal and vertical translation. It can resist both vertical and horizontal forces, but not moments. An example of a hinge support is a beam connected to a wall by a hinge.
- Fixed support is a support that prevents rotation and translation in any direction. It can resist vertical and horizontal forces and moments. An example of a fixed support is a beam embedded in a wall.
- Loads are forces or moments that act on a structural member and cause deformation, stress, or displacement.
- Loads can be classified into three types: point, uniformly distributed (UDL), and uniformly varying (UVL).
- Point load is a load that acts at a single point on a structural member. It can be represented by a single force or moment vector. An example of a point load is a weight hanging from a beam.
- Uniformly distributed load (UDL) is a load that acts with a constant intensity along a length of a structural member. It can be represented by a series of parallel force vectors or a single equivalent force vector. An example of a UDL is the weight of a beam itself.
- Uniformly varying load (UVL) is a load that acts with a varying intensity along a length of a structural member. It can be represented by a series of non-parallel force vectors or a single equivalent force vector. An example of a UVL is the weight of a triangular roof on a beam.
