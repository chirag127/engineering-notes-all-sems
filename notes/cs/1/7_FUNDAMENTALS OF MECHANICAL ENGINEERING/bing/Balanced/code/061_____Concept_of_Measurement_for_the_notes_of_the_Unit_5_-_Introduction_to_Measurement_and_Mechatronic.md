### Concept of Measurement

- Measurement is the process of assigning numbers or symbols to physical quantities or attributes of objects or phenomena, according to certain rules or standards.
- Measurement is essential for engineering design, analysis, testing, quality control, and communication.
- The science of measurement is known as metrology, which includes the study of measurement systems, units, standards, errors, and uncertainties.
- Measurement can be classified into two types: direct and indirect. Direct measurement involves comparing the quantity to be measured with a standard or a known value of the same kind. Indirect measurement involves using a mathematical relationship between the quantity to be measured and one or more other quantities that can be directly measured.
- Measurement can also be classified into three categories: nominal, ordinal, and interval/ratio. Nominal measurement assigns names or labels to objects or phenomena, without implying any order or magnitude. Ordinal measurement assigns ranks or positions to objects or phenomena, based on some criterion or preference. Interval/ratio measurement assigns numbers to objects or phenomena, based on a fixed unit and a zero point, allowing for comparison of magnitudes and ratios.
- Measurement instruments are devices or tools that are used to perform measurements. They can be mechanical, electrical, optical, thermal, acoustic, or chemical in nature. They can be analog or digital, depending on whether they display continuous or discrete values. They can be classified into four types: indicating, recording, integrating, and controlling instruments.
- Measurement instruments have certain characteristics that affect their performance and accuracy, such as sensitivity, resolution, range, linearity, hysteresis, repeatability, reproducibility, and stability. These characteristics are influenced by various factors, such as calibration, environmental conditions, human errors, and instrument errors.
- Measurement instruments need to be selected, used, and maintained properly, following the principles and procedures of measurement practice. Some of the aspects of measurement practice are: choosing the appropriate instrument, preparing the instrument and the object to be measured, performing the measurement, recording and reporting the measurement results, and estimating and reducing the measurement uncertainties.
