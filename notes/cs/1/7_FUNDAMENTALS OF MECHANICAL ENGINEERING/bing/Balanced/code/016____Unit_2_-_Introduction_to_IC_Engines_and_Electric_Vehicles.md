# Unit 2 - Introduction to IC Engines and Electric Vehicles

- An IC engine (internal combustion engine) is a type of engine that converts the chemical energy of a fuel into mechanical energy by burning it inside a combustion chamber. The mechanical energy is then used to power a vehicle or a machine. Examples of IC engines are gasoline engines, diesel engines, and gas turbines .
- An electric vehicle (EV) is a type of vehicle that uses an electric motor and a battery instead of an IC engine and a fuel tank. The electric motor converts the electrical energy stored in the battery into mechanical energy to drive the vehicle. Examples of EVs are battery electric vehicles (BEVs), plug-in hybrid electric vehicles (PHEVs), and fuel cell electric vehicles (FCEVs) .
- The main differences between IC engines and electric vehicles are:
  - IC engines use fossil fuels (gasoline, diesel, etc.) as their energy source, while electric vehicles use electricity from the grid or renewable sources (solar, wind, etc.) as their energy source.
  - IC engines emit harmful pollutants (carbon dioxide, nitrogen oxides, particulate matter, etc.) that contribute to global warming and air pollution, while electric vehicles emit zero or very low tailpipe emissions depending on the source of electricity.
  - IC engines have lower efficiency (around 20-40%) than electric motors (around 80-90%), meaning that they waste more energy as heat and noise, while electric motors use most of the energy to power the vehicle.
  - IC engines have more moving parts (pistons, valves, crankshaft, etc.) than electric motors (rotor, stator, etc.), meaning that they require more maintenance and repairs, while electric motors have fewer parts and are more reliable.
  - IC engines have a limited range (around 300-600 miles) depending on the size of the fuel tank and the fuel economy, while electric vehicles have a variable range (around 100-400 miles) depending on the size of the battery and the driving conditions  .
- The main advantages of IC engines over electric vehicles are:
  - IC engines have a longer history and a wider availability than electric vehicles, meaning that they are more familiar and accessible to consumers and manufacturers.
  - IC engines have a faster refueling time (around 5 minutes) than electric vehicles, which have a longer charging time (around 30 minutes to several hours) depending on the type of charger and the battery capacity.
  - IC engines have a better performance and a more diverse range of options than electric vehicles, meaning that they can offer more power, speed, and variety to drivers .
- The main advantages of electric vehicles over IC engines are:
  - Electric vehicles have a lower operating cost and a higher environmental benefit than IC engines, meaning that they can save money on fuel and maintenance and reduce greenhouse gas emissions and air pollution.
  - Electric vehicles have a simpler and quieter operation and a smoother ride than IC engines, meaning that they can offer more comfort and convenience to drivers and passengers.
  - Electric vehicles have a higher potential for innovation and integration than IC engines, meaning that they can incorporate more advanced technologies and features such as regenerative braking, smart charging, vehicle-to-grid, and autonomous driving .
