### Electric vehicles and hybrid vehicles

- Electric vehicles (EVs) are vehicles that use one or more electric motors as their primary source of propulsion. EVs have a battery instead of a gasoline tank, and an electric motor instead of an internal combustion engine (ICE) .
- Hybrid vehicles are vehicles that use a combination of two or more power sources, such as an ICE and an electric motor, to provide propulsion. Hybrid vehicles have a battery, an electric motor, a gasoline tank, and an ICE .
- There are different types of hybrid vehicles, depending on how the power sources are connected and controlled. The main types are:
  - Parallel hybrids: The ICE and the electric motor are both connected to the mechanical transmission and can simultaneously transmit power to drive the wheels, usually through a conventional transmission .
  - Series hybrids: The ICE is not connected to the wheels, but only to a generator that produces electricity for the battery and the electric motor. The electric motor is the only source of power for the wheels .
  - Plug-in hybrids (PHEVs): These are hybrid vehicles that can be recharged from an external power source, such as a wall outlet, in addition to the onboard generator. PHEVs can run on electricity alone for a certain distance, depending on the battery capacity, before switching to the hybrid mode .
- The main advantages of EVs and hybrid vehicles are:
  - They are more energy-efficient and environmentally friendly than conventional vehicles, as they use less or no fossil fuels and emit less or no greenhouse gases and air pollutants  .
  - They can reduce the dependence on oil and enhance the energy security of the country, as they can use electricity from various sources, such as renewable energy, nuclear power, or natural gas  .
  - They can offer better performance and driving experience, as they have smoother acceleration, lower noise, and higher torque than conventional vehicles  .
- The main challenges of EVs and hybrid vehicles are:
  - They are more expensive than conventional vehicles, as they require more complex components, such as batteries, electric motors, and power electronics  .
  - They have limited range and availability of charging infrastructure, as the battery capacity and the charging speed are still limited, and the number and location of charging stations are still insufficient  .
  - They have potential safety and environmental issues, such as the risk of fire, explosion, or leakage of the battery, and the disposal and recycling of the battery materials  .
