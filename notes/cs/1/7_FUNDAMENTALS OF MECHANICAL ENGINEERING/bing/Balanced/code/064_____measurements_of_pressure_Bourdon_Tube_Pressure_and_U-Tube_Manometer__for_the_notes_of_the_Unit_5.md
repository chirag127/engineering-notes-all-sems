### Measurements of Pressure (Bourdon Tube Pressure and U-Tube Manometer)

- Pressure is the force exerted by a fluid per unit area on a surface.
- Pressure can be measured by various devices that use the principle of balancing the fluid force with a known force, such as gravity, spring, or air.
- Two common devices for measuring pressure are the Bourdon tube pressure gauge and the U-tube manometer.

#### Bourdon Tube Pressure Gauge

- A Bourdon tube pressure gauge consists of a hollow metal tube that is bent into a circular or elliptical shape and has a flattened cross-section.
- One end of the tube is fixed and connected to the pressure source, while the other end is free and attached to a pointer that moves over a calibrated scale.
- When the pressure inside the tube increases, the tube tends to straighten due to the internal force, causing the free end to move and rotate the pointer.
- The pointer indicates the pressure difference between the inside and the outside of the tube, which is proportional to the amount of deflection of the tube.
- The Bourdon tube pressure gauge can measure both positive and negative pressures, and can be calibrated for different units of pressure, such as psi, bar, kPa, etc.
- The Bourdon tube pressure gauge is widely used in various applications because of its high precision, easy reading, and durability.

#### U-Tube Manometer

- A U-tube manometer consists of a U-shaped tube that is partially filled with a liquid, such as mercury, water, or oil.
- One end of the tube is open to the atmosphere, while the other end is connected to the pressure source.
- When the pressure inside the tube is different from the atmospheric pressure, the liquid level in the tube will rise or fall, creating a height difference between the two arms of the tube.
- The height difference, or the pressure head, is proportional to the pressure difference between the inside and the outside of the tube.
- The U-tube manometer can measure both positive and negative pressures, and can be calibrated for different units of pressure, such as mmHg, cmH2O, inH2O, etc.
- The U-tube manometer is simple, inexpensive, and accurate, but it requires a large vertical space and a stable liquid level.
