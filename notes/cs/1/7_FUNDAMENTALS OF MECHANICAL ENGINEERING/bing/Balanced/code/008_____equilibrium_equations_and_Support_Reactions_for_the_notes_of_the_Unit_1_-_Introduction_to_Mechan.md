### Equilibrium equations and support reactions

- Equilibrium equations are the conditions that must be satisfied for a structure or a body to be in a state of static equilibrium, meaning that there is no net force or moment acting on it.
- The equilibrium equations can be derived from Newton's second law of motion, which states that the sum of all external forces and moments acting on a body is equal to its mass times its acceleration. If the body is at rest or moving at a constant velocity, then its acceleration is zero, and the equilibrium equations reduce to:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sum F_{x} &=0 \\
\sum F_{y} &=0 \\
\sum M_{z} &=0
\end{aligned}
$$

- These equations are also known as the scalar equations of equilibrium, because they involve the scalar components of the forces and moments in the x, y, and z directions. Alternatively, the equilibrium equations can be written in vector form as:

$$
\sum \mathbf{F}=\mathbf{0} \\
\sum \mathbf{M}=\mathbf{0}
$$

- These equations are also known as the vector equations of equilibrium, because they involve the vector sums of the forces and moments. The vector equations of equilibrium are equivalent to the scalar equations of equilibrium, but they are more compact and convenient for some problems.

- Support reactions are the forces and moments that are exerted by the supports or constraints on a structure or a body. They are also known as the unknown forces and moments, because they are usually not given in the problem statement, but have to be determined by applying the equilibrium equations.
- The types and number of support reactions depend on the types and number of supports or constraints that are applied to the structure or body. Some common types of supports or constraints are:

  - Fixed support: A fixed support prevents all translations and rotations of the body at the point of contact. It has three support reactions: a horizontal force, a vertical force, and a moment.
  - Pin support: A pin support prevents all translations of the body at the point of contact, but allows rotation. It has two support reactions: a horizontal force and a vertical force.
  - Roller support: A roller support prevents translation of the body in one direction, but allows translation in the other direction and rotation. It has one support reaction: a force perpendicular to the direction of motion.
  - Cable support: A cable support exerts a tension force on the body along the direction of the cable. It has one support reaction: a force along the cable.
  - Spring support: A spring support exerts a force on the body proportional to the displacement of the spring from its equilibrium position. It has one support reaction: a force along the spring.

- To determine the support reactions, the following steps are usually followed:

  - Draw a free-body diagram of the structure or body, showing all the external forces and moments, including the support reactions, acting on it.
  - Choose a convenient coordinate system and label the components of the forces and moments in the x, y, and z directions.
  - Apply the equilibrium equations to the free-body diagram and solve for the unknown support reactions. If there are more unknowns than equations, then the structure or body is statically indeterminate, and additional methods are needed to solve the problem. If there are fewer unknowns than equations, then the structure or body is statically unstable, and the equilibrium equations are not sufficient to describe its behavior.
