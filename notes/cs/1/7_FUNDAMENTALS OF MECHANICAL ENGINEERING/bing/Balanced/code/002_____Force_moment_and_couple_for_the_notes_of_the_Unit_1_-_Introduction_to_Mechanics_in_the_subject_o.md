### Force moment and couple

- A force is a push or pull that acts on a body and causes a change in its state of rest or motion.
- A moment of a force is the tendency of the force to rotate the body about a point or an axis. It is also called torque. It is a vector quantity and has a direction and magnitude.
- A moment of a force is defined as the product of the force and the perpendicular distance from the point or axis to the line of action of the force. Mathematically, M = F x d, where M is the moment, F is the force, and d is the perpendicular distance.
- The moment of a force depends on the choice of the point or axis of rotation. It is said to be the moment about that point or axis. Different points or axes may give different moments for the same force.
- A couple is a system of two equal and opposite parallel forces that act on a body. A couple has no resultant force, but it has a resultant moment. It is also called a pure moment or a force couple.
- A couple can cause or prevent the rotation of a body, but it cannot cause any translation or linear motion of the body.
- The moment of a couple is independent of the choice of the point or axis of rotation. It is equal to the product of one of the forces and the perpendicular distance between the lines of action of the two forces. Mathematically, M = F x d, where M is the moment, F is one of the forces, and d is the perpendicular distance between the forces. This distance is also called the arm of the couple.
- A couple can be resolved into a single force by applying an equal and opposite force at any point on the line of action of one of the forces. This will create a force and a moment that are statically equivalent to the original couple. This process is called resolving a couple into a force and a moment.
