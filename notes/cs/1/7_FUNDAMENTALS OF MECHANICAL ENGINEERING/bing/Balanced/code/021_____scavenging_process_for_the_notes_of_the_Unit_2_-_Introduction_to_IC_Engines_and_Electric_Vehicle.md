### Scavenging Process

Scavenging is the process of replacing the exhaust gas in a cylinder of an internal combustion engine with the fresh air/fuel mixture (or fresh air, in the case of direct-injection engines) for the next cycle. Scavenging is necessary for the engine's proper fuel combustion and better power output.

Some of the points to note about scavenging are:

- Scavenging occurs in the ic engine during the overlapping of valves (Opens at the same time) in which burnt gases are released out of the cylinder with the entering of fresh charge inside the engine at the same time.
- Scavenging is more important for two-stroke engines than for four-stroke engines, because in two-stroke engines, the intake and exhaust ports are open simultaneously, and there is no dedicated exhaust stroke.
- The efficiency of scavenging depends on the design of the cylinder, the piston, the ports, the valves, and the exhaust system.
- The three types of scavenging used in two-stroke ic engines are as follows:
  - Uniflow scavenging: The fresh charge enters from one end of the cylinder and the exhaust gas exits from the other end. This is the most efficient type of scavenging, but it requires a poppet valve or a piston valve for the exhaust port.
  - Cross-flow scavenging: The fresh charge enters from one side of the cylinder and the exhaust gas exits from the opposite side. This is the simplest type of scavenging, but it causes a lot of mixing of the fresh charge and the exhaust gas, leading to poor performance and emissions.
  - Loop or reverse scavenging: The fresh charge enters from the bottom of the cylinder and the exhaust gas exits from the top. This type of scavenging creates a loop of flow inside the cylinder, which helps to push out the exhaust gas, but it also wastes some of the fresh charge.
