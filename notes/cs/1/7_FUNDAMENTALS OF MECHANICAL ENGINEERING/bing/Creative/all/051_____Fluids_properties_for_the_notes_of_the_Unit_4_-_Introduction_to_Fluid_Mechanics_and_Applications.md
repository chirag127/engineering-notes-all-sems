# Fluids Properties

Fluids are substances that can flow and change their shape according to the shape of the container they are in. Fluids include liquids and gases, and also some solids that can behave like fluids under certain conditions, such as sand or plastic. Fluids are important in many fields of engineering, such as fluid mechanics, hydraulics, aerodynamics, and thermodynamics.

Some of the physical properties of fluids that are relevant for engineering applications are:

- **Density**: It is the measure of the amount of mass per unit volume of a fluid. It is usually represented by the symbol $\rho$ and expressed in kg/m$^3$. Density can vary with temperature, pressure, and composition of the fluid. For example, water has a density of about 1000 kg/m$^3$ at room temperature and atmospheric pressure, but it decreases slightly when heated and increases slightly when compressed. Density is an intensive property, meaning that it does not depend on the size or shape of the fluid sample.

- **Specific weight**: It is the measure of the weight per unit volume of a fluid. It is usually represented by the symbol $\gamma$ and expressed in N/m$^3$. Specific weight is related to density by the equation $\gamma = \rho g$, where $g$ is the acceleration due to gravity. Specific weight is also an intensive property, and it can vary with the location and altitude of the fluid sample due to the variation of gravity.

- **Specific volume**: It is the measure of the volume per unit mass of a fluid. It is usually represented by the symbol $v$ and expressed in m$^3$/kg. Specific volume is the inverse of density, that is, $v = 1/\rho$. Specific volume is also an intensive property, and it can vary with the same factors that affect density.

- **Specific gravity**: It is the measure of the ratio of the density of a fluid to the density of a reference fluid, usually water at 4°C. It is usually represented by the symbol $SG$ and expressed as a dimensionless number. Specific gravity is also an intensive property, and it can be used to compare the densities of different fluids. For example, mercury has a specific gravity of about 13.6, meaning that it is 13.6 times denser than water.

- **Viscosity**: It is the measure of the resistance of a fluid to deformation or flow. It is usually represented by the symbol $\mu$ and expressed in Pa$\cdot$s or N$\cdot$s/m$^2$. Viscosity can be thought of as the internal friction of the fluid, which opposes the relative motion of its layers. Viscosity can vary with temperature, pressure, and composition of the fluid. For example, honey has a high viscosity, meaning that it flows slowly and requires a large force to move it, while air has a low viscosity, meaning that it flows easily and requires a small force to move it. Viscosity is an extensive property, meaning that it depends on the size and shape of the fluid sample.

- **Kinematic viscosity**: It is the measure of the ratio of the dynamic viscosity to the density of a fluid. It is usually represented by the symbol $\nu$ and expressed in m$^2$/s. Kinematic viscosity can be thought of as the diffusion of momentum in the fluid, which affects the rate of flow and the formation of eddies or vortices. Kinematic viscosity can vary with the same factors that affect dynamic viscosity and density. For example, water has a kinematic viscosity of about 1$\times$10$^{-6}$ m$^2$/s at room temperature and atmospheric pressure, while air has a kinematic viscosity of about 1.5$\times$10$^{-5}$ m$^2$/s. Kinematic viscosity is also an extensive property.

- **Surface tension**: It is the measure of the force per unit length that acts on the surface of a fluid. It is usually represented by the symbol $\sigma$ and expressed in N/m. Surface tension can be thought of as the tendency of the fluid to minimize its surface area, which results from the cohesive forces between the molecules of the fluid. Surface tension can vary with temperature, pressure, and composition of the fluid. For example, water has a high surface tension, meaning that it forms droplets and can support small objects on its surface, while alcohol has a low surface tension, meaning that it spreads easily and cannot support objects on its surface. Surface tension is an intensive property, and it can affect the shape and stability of fluid interfaces.
