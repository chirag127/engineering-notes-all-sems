### Temperature Measurement Using Thermocouple and Optical Pyrometer

- A **thermocouple** is a device that consists of two dissimilar metals joined together at one end, forming a junction. When the junction is heated or cooled, a voltage is generated across the open ends of the wires, proportional to the temperature difference between the junction and the reference point. The voltage can be measured and converted to temperature using a calibration table or equation.
- A **pyrometer** is a device that measures the temperature of a hot body by detecting the intensity of the infrared radiation emitted by the body. Pyrometers can be classified into two types: contact and non-contact. Contact pyrometers are in direct contact with the hot body, while non-contact pyrometers measure the temperature from a distance.
- An **optical pyrometer** is a type of non-contact pyrometer that uses a lens or a mirror to focus the radiation from the hot body onto a thermocouple or a photodetector. The thermocouple or the photodetector generates a voltage or a current that is proportional to the intensity of the radiation, which can be calibrated to temperature using the Planck's law or the Stefan-Boltzmann law .
- Some advantages of using a thermocouple for temperature measurement are:
  - It can measure a wide range of temperatures, from -200°C to 2300°C.
  - It is simple, rugged, and inexpensive.
  - It has a fast response time and a high sensitivity.
- Some disadvantages of using a thermocouple for temperature measurement are:
  - It requires a reference junction at a known temperature for accurate measurement.
  - It is susceptible to errors due to corrosion, oxidation, and thermal EMF.
  - It has a non-linear voltage-temperature relationship and a low output voltage.
- Some advantages of using an optical pyrometer for temperature measurement are:
  - It can measure very high temperatures, up to 6000°C.
  - It does not require physical contact with the hot body, avoiding damage and interference.
  - It has a high accuracy and a fast response time.
- Some disadvantages of using an optical pyrometer for temperature measurement are:
  - It requires a clear line of sight and a known emissivity of the hot body.
  - It is affected by ambient radiation and atmospheric absorption.
  - It is more complex and expensive than a thermocouple.
