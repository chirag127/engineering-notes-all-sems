### HEV drive train components

- A hybrid electric vehicle (HEV) is a type of hybrid vehicle that combines a conventional internal combustion engine (ICE) system with an electric propulsion system (hybrid vehicle drivetrain).
- The key components in an HEV drivetrain are:
  - Electric traction motors/controllers: These are devices that convert electrical energy into mechanical energy to drive the wheels of the vehicle. They also control the speed and torque of the motors.
  - Electric energy storage systems, such as batteries and ultracapacitors: These are devices that store electrical energy and provide it to the motors when needed. They also capture the energy from regenerative braking and reuse it for acceleration.
  - Internal combustion engine (ICE): This is a device that burns fuel and air to produce mechanical power. It can be used to drive the wheels directly or to charge the batteries.
  - Fuel tank: This is a device that stores the fuel for the ICE.
  - Control board: This is a device that coordinates the operation of the ICE and the electric drive system. It determines the optimal power split between the two sources and manages the energy flow and efficiency of the vehicle.
- There are different types of HEV drivetrain configurations, such as:
  - Series hybrid: In this configuration, the ICE is only used to generate electricity for the batteries and the motors. The ICE does not drive the wheels directly. This allows for a smaller and more efficient ICE, but requires a larger and more expensive electric drive system.
  - Parallel hybrid: In this configuration, the ICE and the electric drive system can both drive the wheels directly. The ICE can also charge the batteries. This allows for a simpler and cheaper electric drive system, but requires a larger and less efficient ICE.
  - Series-parallel hybrid: In this configuration, the ICE and the electric drive system can both drive the wheels directly or work together. The ICE can also charge the batteries. This allows for a more flexible and optimal power split between the two sources, but requires a more complex and costly control system.
