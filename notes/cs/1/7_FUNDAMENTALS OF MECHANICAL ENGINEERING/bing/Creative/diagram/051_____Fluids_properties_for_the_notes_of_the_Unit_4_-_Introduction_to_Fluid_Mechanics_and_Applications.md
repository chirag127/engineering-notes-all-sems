### Fluids properties for the notes of the Unit 4 - Introduction to Fluid Mechanics and Applications in the subject of FUNDAMENTALS OF MECHANICAL ENGINEERING

- A fluid is a substance that deforms continuously (change in shape due to relative motion) under the action of shear force.
- Fluids can be classified as Newtonian or Non-Newtonian based on the viscosity which changes with respect to shear stress.
- Some of the physical properties of fluids are as follows:
  - Viscosity: It is the resistance offered by fluids to deformations, and which tends to impede fluidity. It is usually represented in Pa.s or N.s/m^2^.
  - Density: It is the measure of the amount of matter in a given volume, it is usually represented in kg/m^3^.
  - Specific Weight: It is the weight of a unit volume of fluid, it is usually represented in N/m^3^.
  - Specific Gravity: It is the ratio of the density of a fluid to the density of a standard fluid (usually water or air), it is a dimensionless quantity.
  - Bulk Modulus: It is the ratio of the change in pressure to the fractional change in volume, it is a measure of the compressibility of a fluid, it is usually represented in Pa.
  - Kinematic Viscosity: It is the ratio of the dynamic viscosity to the density, it is a measure of the fluidity of a fluid, it is usually represented in m^2^/s.
  - Surface Tension: It is the force per unit length acting along the interface between a fluid and another fluid or a solid, it is a result of the cohesive forces between the molecules of the fluid, it is usually represented in N/m.
  - Capillarity: It is the phenomenon of rise or fall of a liquid in a narrow tube due to the combined effects of surface tension and adhesion, it is usually represented in m.
- Fluids can be modeled as ideal or real, depending on the assumptions made about their behavior. Ideal fluids are incompressible, inviscid, and irrotational, while real fluids have finite compressibility, viscosity, and vorticity.
- Fluids can be analyzed using the principles of conservation of mass, momentum, and energy, as well as the equations of state, continuity, Bernoulli, and Navier-Stokes.
