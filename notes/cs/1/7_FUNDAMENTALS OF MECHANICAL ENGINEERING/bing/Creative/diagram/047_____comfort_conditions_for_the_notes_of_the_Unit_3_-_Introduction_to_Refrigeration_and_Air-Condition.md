### Comfort Conditions

- Comfort conditions are the environmental parameters that affect the thermal comfort of human beings.
- Thermal comfort is the state of mind that expresses satisfaction with the thermal environment.
- The main factors that influence thermal comfort are:
  - Air temperature: the average temperature of the air surrounding the body.
  - Air velocity: the speed of the air movement relative to the body.
  - Relative humidity: the ratio of the actual water vapour pressure in the air to the saturation water vapour pressure at the same temperature.
  - Mean radiant temperature: the average temperature of the surfaces surrounding the body, weighted by their view factors.
  - Metabolic rate: the rate of energy production by the body due to physical activity.
  - Clothing insulation: the resistance to heat transfer offered by the clothing worn by the person.
- The comfort zone is the range of values of these factors that provide thermal comfort for most people under normal conditions.
- The comfort zone can be represented by a psychrometric chart, which shows the relationship between air temperature, relative humidity, and specific humidity (mass of water vapour per unit mass of dry air).
- The comfort zone varies depending on the season, the location, the type of activity, and the personal preference of the individual.
- The typical comfort zone for summer is between 23°C and 26°C of air temperature, and between 40% and 60% of relative humidity.
- The typical comfort zone for winter is between 20°C and 23°C of air temperature, and between 30% and 50% of relative humidity.
- The aim of refrigeration and air-conditioning systems is to maintain the comfort conditions within the desired range by controlling the temperature, humidity, and quality of the air in the occupied spaces.
