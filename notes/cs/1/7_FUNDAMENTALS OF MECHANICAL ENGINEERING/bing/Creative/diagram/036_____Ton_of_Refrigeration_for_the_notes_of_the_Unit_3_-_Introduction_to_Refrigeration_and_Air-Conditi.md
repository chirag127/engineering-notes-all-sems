### Ton of Refrigeration

- A ton of refrigeration (TR or TOR), also called a refrigeration ton (RT), is a unit of power used in some countries (especially in North America) to describe the heat-extraction capacity of refrigeration and air conditioning equipment .
- One ton of refrigeration is equal to the amount of heat required to melt one ton (2000 pounds) of ice in 24 hours. This is equivalent to 12,000 British thermal units per hour (BTU/h) or 3.517 kilowatts (kW) of power .
- The origin of the term ton of refrigeration dates back to the 19th century, when ice was the main source of cooling. The amount of ice needed to cool a given space was measured in tons, and the rate of ice consumption was used as an indicator of the cooling performance of a refrigeration system .
- The ton of refrigeration is still widely used in the HVAC industry, especially for commercial and industrial applications. However, it is not a standard unit in the International System of Units (SI) and should be converted to watts or kilowatts for scientific calculations .
- Some other units of cooling capacity are:

  - The kilocalorie per hour (kcal/h), which is the amount of heat required to raise the temperature of one kilogram of water by one degree Celsius. One kcal/h is equal to 1.163 watts or 0.0003968 TR.
  - The horsepower (hp), which is a unit of mechanical power. One hp is equal to 745.7 watts or 0.212 TR.
  - The coefficient of performance (COP), which is the ratio of the cooling effect to the input power of a refrigeration system. The higher the COP, the more efficient the system is. The COP is dimensionless and depends on the operating conditions of the system.
