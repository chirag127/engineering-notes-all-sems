### Construction and Working of Two stroke and four stroke SI & CI engine

- SI engine stands for spark ignition engine, which uses a spark plug to ignite the air-fuel mixture in the combustion chamber. CI engine stands for compression ignition engine, which uses high pressure and temperature to ignite the fuel injected into the air in the combustion chamber.
- Both SI and CI engines can operate on either two-stroke or four-stroke cycle. A stroke is the movement of the piston from one end of the cylinder to the other. A cycle is the sequence of events that occur in one complete operation of the engine.
- In a two-stroke engine, the cycle is completed in two strokes of the piston or one revolution of the crankshaft. In a four-stroke engine, the cycle is completed in four strokes of the piston or two revolutions of the crankshaft.
- The main components of a two-stroke or four-stroke engine are the cylinder, piston, connecting rod, crankshaft, valves, spark plug (for SI engine), fuel injector (for CI engine), and exhaust system.

#### Two-stroke SI engine

- A two-stroke SI engine consists of a cylinder with one end fitted with a cover for the placement of spark plug and the other fitted with a sealed crank case which will act as a leak-proof pump during operation. The cylinder is provided with inlet port, transfer port, and exhaust port.
- The working of a two-stroke SI engine can be explained as follows:
  - **Suction and compression stroke**: When the piston moves from the top dead center (TDC) to the bottom dead center (BDC), it creates a partial vacuum in the crank case. This causes the fresh air-fuel mixture to enter the crank case through the inlet port. At the same time, the piston compresses the air-fuel mixture in the cylinder above it. When the piston reaches the BDC, the spark plug ignites the compressed mixture, causing a rapid expansion of gases and pushing the piston upwards.
  - **Power and exhaust stroke**: When the piston moves from the BDC to the TDC, it transfers the fresh air-fuel mixture from the crank case to the cylinder through the transfer port. At the same time, the exhaust gases are expelled from the cylinder through the exhaust port. When the piston reaches the TDC, the cycle is repeated.
