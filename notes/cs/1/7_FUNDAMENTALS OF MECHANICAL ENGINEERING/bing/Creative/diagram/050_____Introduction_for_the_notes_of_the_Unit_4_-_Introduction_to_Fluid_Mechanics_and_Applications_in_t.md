### Introduction

- Fluid mechanics is the branch of science that deals with the behavior of fluids (liquids and gases) at rest and in motion.
- Fluid mechanics has many applications in engineering, such as aerodynamics, hydraulics, fluid power, heat transfer, lubrication, etc.
- Fluid mechanics can be divided into two main subfields: fluid statics and fluid dynamics.
- Fluid statics is the study of fluids at rest or in equilibrium. It deals with the concepts of pressure, buoyancy, hydrostatic forces, etc.
- Fluid dynamics is the study of fluids in motion. It deals with the concepts of velocity, acceleration, flow rate, continuity, Bernoulli's principle, etc.
- Fluid dynamics can be further classified into two types: laminar flow and turbulent flow.
- Laminar flow is the smooth and orderly flow of fluids in which the fluid particles move in parallel layers or streamlines. It occurs at low velocities and low Reynolds numbers.
- Turbulent flow is the chaotic and irregular flow of fluids in which the fluid particles move in random directions and cross the streamlines. It occurs at high velocities and high Reynolds numbers.
- Fluid mechanics is governed by three fundamental laws: conservation of mass, conservation of momentum, and conservation of energy.
- These laws can be expressed in different forms, such as integral, differential, or dimensional analysis, depending on the problem and the assumptions made.
- Fluid mechanics also involves the study of various fluid properties, such as density, viscosity, surface tension, compressibility, etc., and how they affect the fluid behavior and performance.
