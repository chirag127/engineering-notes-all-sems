### Strain Gauge

- A strain gauge is a device that converts a mechanical displacement (such as deformation, stress, or strain) into a change of electrical resistance  .
- A strain gauge consists of a thin metallic foil pattern that is attached to an insulating flexible backing by a suitable adhesive .
- The foil pattern has a nominal resistance that changes when the foil is stretched or compressed along its length .
- The change of resistance is proportional to the applied strain, which can be measured by a Wheatstone bridge circuit or a strain indicator .
- Strain gauges are widely used in various fields, such as mechanical engineering, civil engineering, aerospace, biomedical, and industrial applications .
- Strain gauges can measure static or dynamic strain, and can be classified into two types: bonded and unbonded .

#### Bonded Strain Gauge

- A bonded strain gauge is directly attached to the surface of the test object by a thin layer of adhesive .
- The strain experienced by the test object is transferred to the strain gauge, which changes its resistance accordingly .
- Bonded strain gauges are more sensitive, accurate, and stable than unbonded strain gauges, but they are also more susceptible to temperature effects and surface irregularities .
- Bonded strain gauges can be further divided into two subtypes: foil strain gauges and wire strain gauges .

##### Foil Strain Gauge

- A foil strain gauge is made of a thin metal foil (usually constantan, an alloy of copper and nickel) that is etched into a grid pattern on a plastic backing .
- The grid pattern has many parallel lines that increase the effective length and resistance of the foil .
- The foil strain gauge is the most common type of strain gauge, and it can be made into various shapes and sizes to suit different applications .
- The foil strain gauge has a high gauge factor (the ratio of relative change in resistance to relative change in strain), which means it can detect small strains with high sensitivity .

##### Wire Strain Gauge

- A wire strain gauge is made of a fine metal wire (usually constantan or platinum-tungsten) that is wound around a frame of thin paper or plastic .
- The wire strain gauge has a lower gauge factor than the foil strain gauge, which means it is less sensitive and requires a higher strain to produce a measurable change in resistance .
- The wire strain gauge is more durable and resistant to fatigue than the foil strain gauge, but it is also more difficult to manufacture and install .
- The wire strain gauge is mainly used for high-temperature applications or for measuring large strains .

#### Unbonded Strain Gauge

- An unbonded strain gauge is not attached to the surface of the test object, but rather suspended in a frame or a capsule that is connected to the test object by thin wires or rods .
- The strain experienced by the test object is transmitted to the strain gauge by the wires or rods, which cause the strain gauge to move slightly within the frame or capsule .
- The movement of the strain gauge changes its resistance, which can be measured by a Wheatstone bridge circuit or a strain indicator .
- Unbonded strain gauges are less sensitive, accurate, and stable than bonded strain gauges, but they are also less affected by temperature variations and surface conditions .
- Unbonded strain gauges are mainly used for measuring large strains or for applications where the test object is inaccessible or has a complex shape .
