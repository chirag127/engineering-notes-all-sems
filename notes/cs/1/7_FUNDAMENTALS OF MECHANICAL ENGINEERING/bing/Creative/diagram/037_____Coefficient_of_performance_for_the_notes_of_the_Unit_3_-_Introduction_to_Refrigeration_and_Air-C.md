### Coefficient of performance for refrigeration and air-conditioning

- The coefficient of performance (COP) of a refrigeration or air-conditioning system is a ratio of useful cooling or heating provided to work (energy) required.
- Higher COPs equate to higher efficiency, lower energy (power) consumption and thus lower operating costs.
- The COP of a refrigeration system is defined as the heat removed from the cold reservoir Q_cold (i.e., inside a refrigerator) divided by the work W done to remove the heat (i.e., the work done by the compressor).
- The COP of a refrigeration system can be expressed as:

  COP = Q_cold / W

- The COP of a refrigeration system depends on the temperature requirements and the operating conditions of the system .
- The typical COP of a refrigeration system varies from 2.6 to 3.0 for cutting and preparation rooms, 3.0 to 4.0 for cold storage rooms, and 4.0 to 6.0 for ice making machines.
- The COP of an air-conditioning system is defined as the heat removed from the conditioned space Q_cool (i.e., inside a room) plus the heat rejected to the hot reservoir Q_hot (i.e., outside the room) divided by the work W done to remove the heat (i.e., the work done by the compressor).
- The COP of an air-conditioning system can be expressed as:

  COP = (Q_cool + Q_hot) / W

- The COP of an air-conditioning system depends on the temperature difference between the conditioned space and the outside environment, the humidity level, and the air flow rate.
- The typical COP of an air-conditioning system varies from 2.0 to 4.0 for window units, 3.0 to 5.0 for split systems, and 4.0 to 6.0 for central systems.
