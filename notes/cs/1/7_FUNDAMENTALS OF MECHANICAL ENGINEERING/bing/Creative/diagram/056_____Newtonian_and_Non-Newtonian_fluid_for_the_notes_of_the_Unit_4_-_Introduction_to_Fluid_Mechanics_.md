### Newtonian and Non-Newtonian Fluids

- Fluids are substances that can flow and deform under external forces.
- Fluids can be divided into two major types: Newtonian and non-Newtonian.
- Newtonian fluids are the ones that obey Newton's law of viscosity, which states that the shear stress is proportional to the shear rate and the viscosity is constant.
- Examples of Newtonian fluids are water, mineral oil, gasoline, alcohol, etc.
- Non-Newtonian fluids are those that do not obey Newton's law of viscosity and have a variable relationship with shear stress.
- Examples of non-Newtonian fluids are ketchup, blood, yogurt, toothpaste, etc.
- Non-Newtonian fluids can be further classified into different types based on their behavior under shear forces, such as:
  - Dilatant fluids: These fluids increase in viscosity with an increase in shear rate. They become thicker when stirred or shaken. Examples are cornstarch and water mixture, sand and water mixture, etc.
  - Pseudoplastic fluids: These fluids decrease in viscosity with an increase in shear rate. They become thinner when stirred or shaken. Examples are ketchup, honey, paint, etc.
  - Bingham plastic fluids: These fluids behave like solids until a certain threshold of shear stress is reached, and then they flow like liquids. Examples are toothpaste, mayonnaise, mud, etc.
  - Thixotropic fluids: These fluids decrease in viscosity with time under constant shear stress. They become thinner when left undisturbed. Examples are yogurt, jelly, blood, etc.
  - Rheopectic fluids: These fluids increase in viscosity with time under constant shear stress. They become thicker when left undisturbed. Examples are cream, starch solutions, etc.
- The viscosity of non-Newtonian fluids depends on various factors, such as temperature, pressure, composition, history, etc.
- The study of non-Newtonian fluids is important for various applications in engineering, medicine, food, cosmetics, etc.
