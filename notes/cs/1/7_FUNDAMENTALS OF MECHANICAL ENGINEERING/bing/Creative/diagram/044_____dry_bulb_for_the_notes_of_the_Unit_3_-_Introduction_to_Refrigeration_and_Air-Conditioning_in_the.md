### Dry Bulb Temperature

- Dry bulb temperature (DBT) is the temperature of air measured by a thermometer freely exposed to the air, but shielded from radiation and moisture.
- DBT is the temperature that is usually thought of as air temperature, and it is the true thermodynamic temperature. It is directly proportional to the mean kinetic energy of the air molecules.
- DBT is also called ambient air temperature or simply air temperature. It is the most common and basic air property that is used in refrigeration and air-conditioning.
- DBT can be measured using a normal thermometer or a dry-bulb thermometer. A dry-bulb thermometer is a thermometer that has a dry bulb (hence the name) and is not affected by the moisture of the air.
- DBT is shown along the bottom axis of the psychrometric chart or along the left side of the Mollier diagram. Constant DBT lines appear as vertical lines in the psychrometric chart or horizontal lines in the Mollier diagram.
- DBT is an important parameter for determining the heat load and the cooling capacity of a refrigeration or air-conditioning system. It is also used to calculate other air properties, such as relative humidity, wet bulb temperature, and dew point temperature.
