### Mass Flow Rate (Venturi Meter and Orifice Meter)

Mass flow rate is the amount of mass of a fluid passing through a cross-sectional area per unit time. It is usually denoted by m-dot and has the SI unit of kg/s.

Venturi meter and orifice meter are devices that are used to measure the mass flow rate of a fluid by reducing the pressure of the fluid and applying the continuity equation and the Bernoulli's equation.

#### Venturi Meter

A venturi meter is a device that consists of a converging section, a throat section, and a diverging section. The fluid enters the converging section with a higher pressure and a lower velocity, and then accelerates and reaches the minimum pressure and the maximum velocity at the throat section. The fluid then decelerates and recovers some of the pressure in the diverging section.

The mass flow rate of the fluid can be calculated by measuring the pressure difference between the inlet and the throat sections, and applying the continuity equation and the Bernoulli's equation as follows:

m-dot = A1 * sqrt(2 * rho * (P1 - P2) / (1 - (A2 / A1)^2))

where m-dot is the mass flow rate, A1 and A2 are the cross-sectional areas of the inlet and the throat sections, rho is the density of the fluid, and P1 and P2 are the pressures at the inlet and the throat sections.

A venturi meter is more accurate than an orifice meter because it has a smoother and gradual contraction and expansion, which reduces the energy losses and the coefficient of discharge. The coefficient of discharge is the ratio of the actual flow rate to the theoretical flow rate, and it accounts for the friction, turbulence, and viscosity effects. A venturi meter has a coefficient of discharge of about 0.96 to 0.99, while an orifice meter has a coefficient of discharge of about 0.6 to 0.62 .

#### Orifice Meter

An orifice meter is a device that consists of a thin plate with a small hole or an orifice in the middle. The fluid flows through the orifice and experiences a pressure drop due to the constriction. The mass flow rate of the fluid can be calculated by measuring the pressure difference across the orifice and applying the continuity equation and the Bernoulli's equation as follows:

m-dot = Cd * A2 * sqrt(2 * rho * (P1 - P2) / (1 - (A2 / A1)^2))

where m-dot is the mass flow rate, Cd is the coefficient of discharge, A1 and A2 are the cross-sectional areas of the pipe and the orifice, rho is the density of the fluid, and P1 and P2 are the pressures before and after the orifice.

An orifice meter is less accurate than a venturi meter because it has a sudden and sharp contraction and expansion, which causes more energy losses and a lower coefficient of discharge. An orifice meter also has a higher pressure drop and a lower rangeability than a venturi meter.
