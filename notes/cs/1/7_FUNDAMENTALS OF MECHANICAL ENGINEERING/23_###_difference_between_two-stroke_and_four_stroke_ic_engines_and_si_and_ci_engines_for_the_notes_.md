### difference between two-stroke and four stroke IC engines and SI and CI Engines for the notes of the Unit 2 - Introduction to IC Engines and Electric Vehicles in the subject of FUNDAMENTALS OF MECHANICAL ENGINEERING

Two-Stroke and Four-Stroke IC Engines:

Unit 2 of Fundamentals of Mechanical Engineering covers Introduction to IC Engines and Electric Vehicles. Two-stroke and four-stroke internal combustion (IC) engines are two types of engines used in vehicles.

1. Two-Stroke Engines: A two-stroke engine completes a power cycle in two strokes of the piston, or one crankshaft revolution. They are lighter, simpler, and less expensive than four-stroke engines, but are less efficient and produce more pollution.

2. Four-Stroke Engines: A four-stroke engine completes a power cycle in four strokes of the piston, or two crankshaft revolutions. They are more efficient and produce less pollution than two-stroke engines, but are heavier, more complex, and more expensive.

SI and CI Engines:

1. SI Engines: SI engines are spark-ignition engines that use a spark plug to ignite a fuel-air mixture. They are commonly used in passenger cars and light-duty vehicles.

2. CI Engines: CI engines are compression-ignition engines that use high compression to ignite the fuel-air mixture. They are commonly used in heavy-duty vehicles, such as trucks and buses.
