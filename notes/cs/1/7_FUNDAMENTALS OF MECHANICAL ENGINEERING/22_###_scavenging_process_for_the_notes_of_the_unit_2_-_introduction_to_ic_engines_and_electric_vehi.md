Scavenging Process in IC Engines and Electric Vehicles:

Unit 2 of Fundamentals of Mechanical Engineering covers Introduction to IC Engines and Electric Vehicles. Scavenging is an important process in the operation of internal combustion engines.

1. Introduction: Scavenging is the process of removing the spent gases from the combustion chamber and replacing them with fresh air. This is necessary to maintain the proper air-fuel mixture in the engine and to increase engine efficiency.

2. IC Engines: In internal combustion engines, scavenging is achieved by using a system of intake and exhaust valves. The intake valves admit fresh air into the engine, while the exhaust valves remove the spent gases.

3. Electric Vehicles: Electric vehicles do not have internal combustion engines and therefore do not require scavenging. Instead, they use electric motors to produce power, which is stored in batteries.

4. Scavenging Process: The scavenging process in internal combustion engines is critical to the operation of the engine. The process involves the removal of the spent gases from the combustion chamber and the admission of fresh air into the engine. This helps to maintain the proper air-fuel mixture and to increase engine efficiency.

5. Importance of Scavenging: Scavenging is important for several reasons. It helps to maintain the proper air-fuel mixture in the engine, which is necessary for efficient combustion. It also helps to reduce emissions and to increase engine efficiency.

In conclusion, scavenging is an important process in the operation of internal combustion engines. It involves the removal of the spent gases from the combustion chamber and the admission of fresh air into the engine. Scavenging is critical to the operation of the engine and helps to maintain the proper air-fuel mixture, reduce emissions, and increase engine efficiency. Electric vehicles do not have internal combustion engines and therefore do not require scavenging.
