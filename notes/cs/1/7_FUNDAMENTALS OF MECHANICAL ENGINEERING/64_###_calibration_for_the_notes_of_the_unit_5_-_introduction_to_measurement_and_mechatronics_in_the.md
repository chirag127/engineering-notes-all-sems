### Calibration for the notes of the Unit 5 - Introduction to Measurement and Mechatronics in the subject of FUNDAMENTALS OF MECHANICAL ENGINEERING

Calibration is the process of comparing the readings of a measuring instrument to a known standard and adjusting the instrument to match the standard. In Unit 5 - Introduction to Measurement and Mechatronics of Fundamentals of Mechanical Engineering, students learn about the basic principles of calibration, including the types of calibration, the importance of calibration, and the methods used for calibration.

There are several types of calibration, including dimensional calibration, electrical calibration, and temperature calibration. Dimensional calibration involves comparing the readings of a measuring instrument to a known standard, such as a ruler or a micrometer. Electrical calibration involves comparing the readings of an electrical instrument, such as a voltmeter or an ammeter, to a known standard. Temperature calibration involves comparing the readings of a temperature instrument, such as a thermometer or a thermocouple, to a known standard.

Calibration is important because it ensures that measuring instruments provide accurate and reliable readings. This is essential for many applications, including quality control, product development, and scientific research.

In this unit, students learn about the different methods used for calibration, including direct comparison, traceability, and statistical methods. They also learn about the importance of maintaining a calibration program and the documentation required for calibration.

The study of calibration is important for students who plan to pursue careers in mechanical engineering, as calibration is widely used in many industries, including manufacturing, aerospace, and energy. Understanding the principles and applications of calibration is essential for engineers to design and implement accurate and reliable measurement systems.
