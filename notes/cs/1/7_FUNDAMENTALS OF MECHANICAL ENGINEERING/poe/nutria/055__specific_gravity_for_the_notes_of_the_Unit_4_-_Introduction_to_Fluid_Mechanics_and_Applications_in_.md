
### Specific Gravity

Specific gravity is a measure of the relative density of a substance compared to water. It is defined as the ratio of the density of a substance to the density of water at a given temperature.

The specific gravity of a substance can be used to determine the composition of a mixture, as different substances have different specific gravities. For example, the specific gravity of a mixture of oil and water can be used to determine the percentage of oil in the mixture.

Specific gravity is also used to calculate the buoyancy of a substance. The greater the specific gravity, the greater the buoyancy. This is because higher specific gravity substances are denser and therefore more likely to sink.

Specific gravity is also used in the field of mechanical engineering to calculate the pressure at a certain depth in a fluid. This is because the pressure at a certain depth is proportional to the density of the fluid at that depth. Therefore, the pressure at a certain depth can be calculated by multiplying the specific gravity of the fluid by the depth.
