
### Newtonian and Non-Newtonian Fluids

1. Newtonian fluids are fluids that obey the linear relationship between shear stress and shear rate. This means that the shear rate of the fluid is directly proportional to the shear stress applied to it. Examples of Newtonian fluids include water, air, and most common liquids.

2. Non-Newtonian fluids are fluids that do not obey the linear relationship between shear stress and shear rate. This means that the shear rate of the fluid is not directly proportional to the shear stress applied to it. Examples of Non-Newtonian fluids include ketchup, toothpaste, and paint.

3. Newtonian fluids are used in a variety of applications, such as lubrication, hydraulics, and cooling systems. Non-Newtonian fluids are used in applications such as adhesives and paints.

4. The viscosity of a Newtonian fluid is constant and independent of the shear rate. The viscosity of a Non-Newtonian fluid is not constant and can vary greatly depending on the shear rate.

5. The flow of a Newtonian fluid is laminar, meaning that the flow is smooth and the particles move in a straight line. The flow of a Non-Newtonian fluid is turbulent, meaning that the flow is chaotic and the particles move in a random pattern.
