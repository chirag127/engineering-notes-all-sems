
### IC Engines 

* Internal combustion engines (IC engines) are heat engines in which combustion of a fuel occurs with an oxidizer (usually air) in a combustion chamber that is an integral part of the working fluid flow circuit. 
* IC engines are the most commonly used form of heat engine and are found in a wide variety of applications, including automobiles, motorcycles, aircraft, ships, power plants, and stationary engines. 
* IC engines are classified based on the type of fuel used, the number of cylinders, the type of ignition system, and the number of strokes per cycle. 
* The four main types of IC engines are spark-ignition (SI) engines, compression-ignition (CI) engines, two-stroke engines, and four-stroke engines. 
* SI engines use spark plugs to ignite the fuel-air mixture, while CI engines use compression of the air-fuel mixture to ignite the fuel. 
* Two-stroke engines have two strokes per cycle and four-stroke engines have four strokes per cycle. 
* The four strokes of a four-stroke engine are intake, compression, power, and exhaust. 
* The main components of an IC engine include the engine block, cylinder head, crankshaft, connecting rods, pistons, valves, fuel injectors, and spark plugs. 
* The most common fuels used in IC engines are gasoline, diesel, and natural gas. 
* The efficiency of an IC engine is affected by the compression ratio, the engine speed, the air-fuel ratio, and the exhaust gas temperature.
