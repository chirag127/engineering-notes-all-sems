### Construction and Working of Two stroke and four stroke SI & CI engine for the notes of the Unit 2 - Introduction to IC Engines and Electric Vehicles in the subject of FUNDAMENTALS OF MECHANICAL ENGINEERING

- Two stroke engine:
  - Only two strokes of piston (up and down) complete one power cycle.
  - Has an inlet and outlet port on the cylinder wall.
  - Mixing of fuel and air and scavenging (removing exhaust gases) happens simultaneously.
  - Parts include piston, connecting rod, crankshaft, cylinder, cylinder head, fuel inlet and outlet ports.
  - Working: When inlet port is uncovered, air-fuel mixture enters. When piston moves up, exhaust port is closed first then inlet port is closed. This traps charge which is ignited causing explosion. Exhaust gases are pushed out when exhaust port is opened.
- Four stroke engine:
  - Four strokes (intake, compression, power, exhaust) complete one cycle.
  - Separate intake and exhaust valves and a inlet manifold and exhaust manifold.
  - Working: Intake - air-fuel mixture enters when inlet valve opens. Compression - inlet valve closes and charge is compressed. Ignition - spark from spark plug ignites charge. Exhaust - exhaust valve opens and byproducts are expelled.
- Spark ignition (SI) and compression ignition (CI) engines:
  - SI engine uses spark plug to ignite air-fuel mixture which is premixed. Usually gasoline engine.
  - CI engine uses high compression to ignite fuel which is injected as per requirement. Usually diesel engine.
  - Differences in components and working cycle.
  - Applications and advantages/disadvantages of both types.
