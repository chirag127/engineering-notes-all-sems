### Dry Bulb

- The temperature measured by a thermometer exposed to the air in a place is known as the dry bulb temperature.
- It is the temperature of the air itself and does not take into account the humidity.
- The dry bulb temperature is lower than the wet bulb temperature in the presence of moisture or humidity.
- The difference between the dry bulb and wet bulb temperatures is used to determine the relative humidity and moisture content of air.
- The dry bulb temperature is used to determine the cooling/heating load and in selecting refrigeration/air conditioning equipment.
- A higher dry bulb temperature implies a higher cooling load and a lower temperature implies a lower cooling load.
