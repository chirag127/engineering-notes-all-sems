### Precision and Resolution

- Precision refers to the closeness of agreement between independent measurements of the same quantity. It is a measure of the reproducibility or repeatability of measurements.
- High precision means that multiple measurements are very close to each other while low precision means that the measurements vary widely.
- Resolution refers to the smallest change in the quantity being measured that leads to a perceptible change in the measurement readings. It is the smallest division of the scale which can be detected.
- High resolution means that even small changes in the quantity can be detected while low resolution means only large changes are detectable.
- For example, a ruler with markings in millimeters has lower resolution but higher precision than a ruler with markings in centimeters. A very precise but low resolution weighing scale may show the same reading for objects ranging over a few grams.
- Improving precision decreases the error or uncertainty in measurements while improving resolution allows perception of smaller changes. Both are required for accurate measurements and precise control systems.
