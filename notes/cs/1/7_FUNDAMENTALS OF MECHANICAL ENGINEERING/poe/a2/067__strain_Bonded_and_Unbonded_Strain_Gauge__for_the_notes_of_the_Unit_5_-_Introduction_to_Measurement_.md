### Strain (Bonded and Unbonded Strain Gauge)

- **Bonded Strain Gauge**: A strain gauge is bonded directly to the surface of the specimen using a adhesive. The strain experienced by the specimen is transferred to the strain gauge, which results in the change of resistance. This type is more accurate but it is not reusable.
- **Unbonded Strain Gauge**: The strain gauge is not bonded to the specimen. It is just kept in contact with the specimen surface. This type is reusable but less accurate as there may be slip between the gauge and specimen.

Points to note:

- The change in resistance is very small (Fraction of ohm). Hence, strain gauges are connected into a Wheatstone bridge circuit to get measurable voltage change.
- The gauge factor indicates the sensitivity of the strain gauge and it depends on the material of the strain gauge.
- The strain gauge can measure either tensile or compressive strain based on how it is mounted on the specimen.
- The outputs of strain gauges are temperature dependent. Hence, temperature compensation is required for high precision measurements.
