### Force, Moment and Couple

- **Force:** Force is a push or pull that tends to accelerate an object. It is a vector quantity that has both magnitude and direction.
- **Moment:** Moment is the tendency of a force to rotate an object about a point or axis. It is calculated as force multiplied by the perpendicular distance from the point to the line of action of the force.
- **Couple:** A couple is a pair of equal and opposite parallel forces that do not share a line of action. A couple produces rotation without translation. The magnitude of the couple is the product of one of the forces and the perpendicular distance between them.

Couples provide a means of creating rotation in an object without creating translational motion. They are useful for analyzing situations where pure rotation occurs without translation.

The concepts of force, moment and couple are fundamental to the analysis of mechanisms and structures in mechanical engineering. They provide a means to quantitatively understand how systems react to applied loads.
