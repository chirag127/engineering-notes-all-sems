### Specific Gravity

- Specific gravity is the ratio of the density of a substance to the density of water at 4°C. It is a dimensionless quantity and has no units.
- It is used to compare the densities of substances to the density of water.
- A substance with a specific gravity greater than 1 will sink in water while a substance with a specific gravity less than 1 will float in water.
- For liquids, specific gravity is measured at 20°C. For solids, specific gravity is measured at room temperature.
- Specific gravity can be used to identify an unknown substance by comparing its density to that of water.
- Some examples of specific gravity:
	- Gold: 19.3
	- Mercury: 13.6
	- Water: 1
	- Ice: 0.92
	- Wood: 0.5-1.0

- To calculate specific gravity (SPGR), use the formula:
SPGR = Density of substance / Density of water

- The density of water at 4°C is 1 g/cm3. So for liquids, specific gravity equals the density in g/cm3.
- Specific gravity is an important property used in hydrostatics to calculate buoyancy and flotation.
