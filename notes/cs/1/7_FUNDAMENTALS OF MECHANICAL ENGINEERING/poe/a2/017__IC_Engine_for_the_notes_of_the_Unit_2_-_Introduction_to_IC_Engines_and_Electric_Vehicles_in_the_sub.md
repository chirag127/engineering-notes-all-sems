### IC Engine

- An internal combustion engine (ICE) is a heat engine in which the combustion of a fuel occurs with an oxidizer (usually air) in a combustion chamber.
- The reciprocating type IC engine uses pistons that move in cylinders containing the combustion gases.
- The four-stroke cycle includes the intake, compression, power, and exhaust strokes. The piston moves downwards in the intake stroke, allowing the air-fuel mixture to enter the cylinder. The piston then moves up and compresses the mixture in the compression stroke. The compressed mixture is ignited, driving the piston downwards in the power stroke. The byproducts of combustion are exhausted in the exhaust stroke.
- The two-stroke cycle has charging and power strokes, with overlap that allows some of the intake charge to be exhausted and replaced by a fresh charge. Two-stroke engines are simpler but less efficient than four-stroke engines.
- Common fuels for IC engines include gasoline, diesel, and propane. The air-fuel mixture is ignited in gasoline engines using a spark from a spark plug. In diesel engines, the high compression of the air-fuel mixture causes it to ignite.
- IC engines have high power density but produce air pollution. They are widely used in vehicles and equipment but are being increasingly replaced by electric vehicles to reduce emissions.
