### Air-Conditioning

- Air-conditioning is the process of removing heat and controlling the humidity of the air in an enclosed space by cooling and dehumidification.
- The major components of an air-conditioning system are: compressor, condenser, expansion valve, evaporator and fan.
- The basic refrigeration cycle involves four processes: vapor-compression, condensation, expansion and evaporation.
- In vapor-compression, the refrigerant is evaporated by absorbing heat. In condensation, the vapor is compressed and condensed into liquid by releasing heat. In expansion, the liquid refrigerant expands and evaporates. In evaporation, the refrigerant absorbs heat and evaporates.
- The cooling effect is produced due to the evaporation of the refrigerant in the evaporator. The heat from the air in the enclosed space is absorbed, thereby cooling the air.
- The dehumidification is achieved by condensing the water vapor present in the air into liquid in the cooling coil and collecting it. The dry air is blown into the room.
- The controls for air-conditioning include thermostats to set the required temperature and humidity; on/off switches to control the equipment; and servomotors to control the dampers for regulating air flow.
- The applications of air-conditioning include residential buildings, offices, hospitals, industries, aircraft, trains, automobiles, etc. Air-conditioning provides comfort and suitable working conditions.
