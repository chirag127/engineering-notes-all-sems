### Force (Proving Ring) and Torques(Prony Brake Dynamometer)

**Proving Ring:**

- It is used to calibrate force transducers like load cells.
- It consists of an elastic ring with an initial compression or tension.
- When an unknown force is applied, the ring gets further compressed or stretched and the amount of deformation is measured to determine the magnitude of the applied force.
- The ring has a known spring constant so the deformation can be related to the applied force by Hooke's law.
- Advantages: Simple design, wide range of forces can be measured.
- Disadvantages: Friction and hysteresis errors. Periodic recalibration required.

**Prony Brake Dynamometer:**

- It is used to measure torque and power.
- It consists of a brake drum attached to the rotating shaft and friction pads pressed against the drum.
- The torque is varied by changing the pressure on the friction pads.
- The braking torque is determined from the force on the brake pads and the radius of the drum. The power is calculated from the torque and rotation speed.
- Advantages: Simple and inexpensive. Wide range of torques and power can be measured.
- Disadvantages: Friction losses and wear of brake pads affects accuracy. Periodic recalibration required.
