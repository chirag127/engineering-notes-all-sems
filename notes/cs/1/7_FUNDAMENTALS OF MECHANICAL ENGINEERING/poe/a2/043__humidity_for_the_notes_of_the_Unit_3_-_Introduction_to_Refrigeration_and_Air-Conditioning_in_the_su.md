### Humidity

- Humidity refers to the amount of water vapor present in air. The higher the humidity, the more uncomfortable it feels.
- Absolute humidity refers to the actual mass of water vapor per unit volume of air. It is measured in grams per cubic meter (g/m3).
- Relative humidity refers to the ratio of the partial pressure of water vapor to the saturated vapor pressure of water at the same temperature. It is expressed in percentage.
- The relative humidity of air depends on both its temperature and absolute humidity. As the temperature increases, the air can hold more moisture. So, for the same absolute humidity, the relative humidity decreases with increase in temperature.
- High relative humidity is undesirable in cooling applications as it reduces the effectiveness of cooling and dehumidification. It can lead to issues like mold growth.
- In air conditioning applications, the humidity of air is controlled using dehumidifiers. The dehumidifiers remove moisture from air to control its humidity.
- Proper control of humidity is essential for human comfort in indoor environments. The ideal relative humidity range is from 40% to 60%.
