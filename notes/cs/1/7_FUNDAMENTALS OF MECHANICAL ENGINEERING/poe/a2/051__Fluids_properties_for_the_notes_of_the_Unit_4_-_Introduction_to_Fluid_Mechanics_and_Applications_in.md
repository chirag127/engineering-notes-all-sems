### Fluids properties for the notes of the Unit 4 - Introduction to Fluid Mechanics and Applications in the subject of FUNDAMENTALS OF MECHANICAL ENGINEERING

1. Density: Mass per unit volume of a fluid. It is a measure of how tightly the molecules are packed in a fluid. Denser fluids have molecules packed more tightly than less dense fluids.

2. Specific Gravity: Ratio of the density of a fluid to the density of water at 4°C. It is a dimensionless quantity used to compare densities of fluids.

3. Viscosity: Resistance of a fluid to shear stress. It is a measure of the internal friction of a moving fluid. More viscous fluids offer more resistance to shear than less viscous fluids.

4. Compressibility: Ability of a fluid to change its volume when pressure is applied. Gases are compressible while liquids are nearly incompressible.

5. Surface Tension: Tendency of the surface of a liquid to contract and possess elastic properties. It arises due to cohesive forces between liquid molecules. Liquids with high surface tension form drops while low surface tension leads to spreading.

6. Buoyancy: Upward force exerted by a fluid on an object immersed in it. It equals the weight of the fluid displaced by the object. Buoyancy causes objects to float or rise in fluids.

The above points cover the key properties of fluids to understand fluid flow and applications. Thorough understanding of these properties is essential to study fluid mechanics.
