### Density

Density is defined as the mass per unit volume of a substance. It is represented by the Greek letter ρ (rho). The SI unit of density is kilograms per cubic meter (kg/m³).

Some important points to remember about density are:

- Density is an intensive property, which means it does not depend on the amount of substance present. It is a characteristic property of a substance.
- Density is affected by temperature and pressure. As temperature increases, the density of a substance decreases. As pressure increases, the density of a substance also increases.
- Density can be used to identify a substance. Each substance has a unique density, which can be used to distinguish it from other substances.
- The density of a substance can be used to determine its buoyancy in a fluid. If the density of an object is less than the density of the fluid, it will float. If the density of an object is greater than the density of the fluid, it will sink.
- The density of a mixture is a weighted average of the densities of the individual components. The weight is determined by the mass or volume of each component in the mixture.

In fluid mechanics, density plays a crucial role in understanding the behavior of fluids. Some important applications of density in fluid mechanics are:

- Calculation of pressure: The pressure of a fluid depends on its density. For incompressible fluids, the pressure can be calculated using the hydrostatic equation, which relates pressure to density, gravity, and height.
- Calculation of flow rate: The flow rate of a fluid can be calculated using the continuity equation, which relates the density, velocity, and cross-sectional area of the fluid.
- Calculation of forces: The forces acting on a fluid can be calculated using the Navier-Stokes equations, which take into account the density, viscosity, and velocity of the fluid.

In conclusion, density is a fundamental property of matter that plays a crucial role in fluid mechanics. Understanding density is essential for engineers working in fields such as fluid dynamics, thermodynamics, and materials science.
