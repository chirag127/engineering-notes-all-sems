## Unit 1 - Introduction to Mechanics

### Definition

- Mechanics is the branch of physics that deals with the study of motion, forces, and energy.
- It is concerned with describing how objects move and the forces that cause the motion.

### Kinematics

- Kinematics is the study of motion without considering the forces that cause it.
- It involves studying the position, velocity, and acceleration of an object.

### Dynamics

- Dynamics is the study of motion that takes into account the forces that cause it.
- It involves studying how forces affect the motion of an object.

### Newton's Laws of Motion

- Newton's first law of motion states that an object at rest will remain at rest, and an object in motion will remain in motion at a constant velocity, unless acted upon by a net external force.
- Newton's second law of motion states that the acceleration of an object is directly proportional to the net external force acting on it and inversely proportional to its mass.
- Newton's third law of motion states that for every action, there is an equal and opposite reaction.

### Work and Energy

- Work is done when a force acts on an object and causes it to move a certain distance.
- Energy is the ability to do work.
- The total amount of energy in a closed system remains constant, according to the law of conservation of energy.

### Conservation of Momentum

- Momentum is the product of an object's mass and velocity.
- The law of conservation of momentum states that the total momentum of a closed system remains constant, provided that no external forces act on it.

### Applications of Mechanics

- Mechanics is used in many areas of science and engineering, including the design of machines and structures, the study of the behavior of fluids, and the understanding of the motion of planets and other celestial bodies.
- It has also led to the development of new technologies, such as the automobile and the airplane.
