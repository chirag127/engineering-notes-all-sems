### Hybrid Electric Vehicles

Hybrid Electric Vehicles (HEVs) are a combination of an internal combustion engine and an electric motor. Here are some important points about HEVs:

- HEVs have a smaller engine compared to traditional gasoline-powered vehicles. The electric motor provides additional power and assists the engine to improve fuel efficiency.
- HEVs use regenerative braking, which captures the kinetic energy lost during braking and converts it into electrical energy to recharge the battery.
- HEVs can operate in three modes: electric-only, gasoline-only, and hybrid mode. The mode selection depends on the driving conditions and battery charge level.
- HEVs have a higher initial cost compared to traditional vehicles due to the additional components, but the fuel savings over time can offset the cost.
- HEVs emit fewer pollutants and greenhouse gases compared to traditional vehicles, making them more environmentally friendly.
- HEVs require less maintenance compared to traditional vehicles since the electric motor is used to assist the engine, reducing wear and tear on the engine.
- HEVs have a limited all-electric range, and the battery needs to be recharged through regenerative braking or by plugging it into an electrical outlet.

In summary, HEVs are a hybrid technology that combines the benefits of an internal combustion engine and an electric motor to improve fuel efficiency and reduce emissions. They are a promising technology for the future of transportation as they continue to improve in efficiency and cost-effectiveness.
