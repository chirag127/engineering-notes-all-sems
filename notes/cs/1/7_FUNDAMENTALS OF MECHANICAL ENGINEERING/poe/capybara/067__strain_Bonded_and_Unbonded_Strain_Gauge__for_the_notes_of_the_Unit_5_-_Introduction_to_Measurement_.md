### Strain (Bonded and Unbonded Strain Gauge)

In the field of mechanical engineering, strain is defined as the change in length or shape of a material when subjected to an external force. Strain gauges are used to measure this strain and are commonly used in various applications such as load cells, pressure transducers, and torque measurement devices. There are two types of strain gauges: bonded and unbonded.

#### Bonded Strain Gauge

A bonded strain gauge is a device that is directly attached to the surface of the material being tested. The gauge consists of a thin wire or foil that is glued to the surface of the material. When an external force is applied to the material, the strain gauge experiences a change in resistance, which can be measured using a Wheatstone bridge circuit. The amount of resistance change is directly proportional to the strain experienced by the material.

Bonded strain gauges are commonly used in applications where high accuracy is required, such as in the aerospace and automotive industries. They are also used in materials testing laboratories to perform tensile, compression, and bending tests.

#### Unbonded Strain Gauge

An unbonded strain gauge is a device that is not directly attached to the surface of the material being tested. Instead, it is installed in a separate fixture that is attached to the material. The fixture is designed to transfer the strain experienced by the material to the strain gauge. Unbonded strain gauges typically use a wire or foil that is suspended between two supports. When the material is subjected to an external force, the strain gauge experiences a change in resistance, which can be measured using a Wheatstone bridge circuit.

Unbonded strain gauges are commonly used in applications where the material being tested is too large or too hot to attach a bonded strain gauge directly. They are also used in materials testing laboratories to perform strain measurements on large structures such as bridges and buildings.

Both types of strain gauges have their advantages and disadvantages, and the choice of which type to use depends on the specific application. Bonded strain gauges are generally more accurate but require direct attachment to the material being tested. Unbonded strain gauges are more versatile but may be less accurate due to the potential for errors introduced by the fixture. Proper installation and calibration of both types of strain gauges are essential to ensure accurate and reliable measurements.
