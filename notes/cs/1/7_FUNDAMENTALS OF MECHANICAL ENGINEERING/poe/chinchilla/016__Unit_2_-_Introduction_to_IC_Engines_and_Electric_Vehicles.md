## Unit 2 - Introduction to IC Engines and Electric Vehicles

In this unit, we will learn about the fundamental concepts of Internal Combustion (IC) Engines and Electric Vehicles (EVs). We will understand the working principles, components, and applications of both IC engines and EVs.

### Internal Combustion Engines
- An IC engine is a heat engine that converts chemical energy stored in fuel into mechanical energy through combustion.
- IC engines operate on the principle of the four-stroke cycle: intake, compression, power, and exhaust.
- The main components of an IC engine are the cylinder, piston, connecting rod, crankshaft, valves, spark plug, and fuel injector.
- IC engines can be classified into two types: spark-ignition (SI) engines and compression-ignition (CI) engines.
- SI engines use spark plugs to ignite the fuel-air mixture, while CI engines rely on compression to ignite the fuel.
- IC engines have various applications, including automotive, marine, aviation, and power generation.

### Electric Vehicles
- EVs are vehicles that run on electric motors powered by rechargeable batteries instead of IC engines.
- EVs use regenerative braking to convert kinetic energy into electrical energy, which is stored in the battery.
- The main components of an EV are the battery, electric motor, power electronics, and charging system.
- EVs can be classified into three types: battery electric vehicles (BEVs), plug-in hybrid electric vehicles (PHEVs), and hybrid electric vehicles (HEVs).
- BEVs rely solely on batteries for power, while PHEVs and HEVs have both batteries and IC engines.
- EVs have several advantages over IC engines, including lower emissions, reduced noise pollution, and lower operating costs.
- EVs are being increasingly adopted in various industries, including automotive, transportation, and logistics.

In conclusion, understanding the principles and applications of IC engines and EVs is essential in the current scenario of increasing environmental concerns and the need for sustainable transportation. This knowledge will not only help us make informed decisions as consumers but also contribute to the development of cleaner and more efficient technologies in the future.
