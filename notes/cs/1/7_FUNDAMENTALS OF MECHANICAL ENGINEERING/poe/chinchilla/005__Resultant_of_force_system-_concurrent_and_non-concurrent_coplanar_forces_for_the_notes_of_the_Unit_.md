### Resultant of force system- concurrent and non-concurrent coplanar forces

In mechanics, the term "resultant" refers to the single force that has the same effect on a body as a system of forces acting on it. The resultant force of a system of forces acting on a body can be found by adding all the forces together. This resultant force can be used to represent the net effect of all the forces on the body.

#### Concurrent forces

Concurrent forces are forces that act on a common point and have a common line of action. In other words, the forces meet at a single point and act in the same direction or opposite directions. 

The resultant of concurrent forces can be found by drawing a diagram of the forces and using vector addition. To find the magnitude and direction of the resultant force, we can use the Pythagorean theorem and trigonometry. 

#### Non-concurrent forces

Non-concurrent forces are forces that do not meet at a single point and do not have a common line of action. 

To find the resultant force of non-concurrent forces, we need to use the concept of moments. The moment of a force about a point is the product of the force and the perpendicular distance from the point to the line of action of the force. The moment of a force can be positive or negative depending on the direction of the force and the direction of rotation.

The resultant force of non-concurrent forces can be found by resolving the forces into their components and finding the horizontal and vertical components of the forces. The horizontal and vertical components can be added separately to find the resultant force. 

#### Coplanar forces

Coplanar forces are forces that act in the same plane. This means that the forces are parallel or intersect at a single point. 

The resultant force of coplanar forces can be found by adding the forces together using vector addition. If the forces are parallel, we can use the principle of moments to find the resultant force. 

In conclusion, the resultant force of a system of forces can be found by adding all the forces together. For concurrent forces, we can use vector addition and trigonometry to find the magnitude and direction of the resultant force. For non-concurrent forces, we need to use the concept of moments and resolve the forces into their components.
