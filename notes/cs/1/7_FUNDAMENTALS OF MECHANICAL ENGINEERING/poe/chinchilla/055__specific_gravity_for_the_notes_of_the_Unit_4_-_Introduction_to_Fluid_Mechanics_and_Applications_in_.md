### Specific Gravity

Specific gravity is a dimensionless value that represents the ratio of the density of a fluid to the density of a reference substance. The reference substance is usually water at a specified temperature and pressure. Specific gravity is an important property of fluids and is widely used in various applications in fluid mechanics. Some key points about specific gravity are:

- Specific gravity is also known as relative density or density ratio.
- It is defined as the ratio of the density of a fluid to the density of water at a specified temperature and pressure.
- It is a unitless value, as it is a ratio of two densities with the same units.
- The symbol for specific gravity is usually "SG" or "ρ".
- The specific gravity of water at room temperature (20°C) and atmospheric pressure (1 atm) is 1.000.
- The specific gravity of a fluid can be determined by measuring its density and comparing it to the density of water at the same temperature and pressure.
- Specific gravity is used to classify fluids as lighter or heavier than water.
- If the specific gravity of a fluid is less than 1, it is lighter than water and will float on water.
- If the specific gravity of a fluid is greater than 1, it is heavier than water and will sink in water.
- Specific gravity is important in many applications, such as determining the buoyancy of objects in fluids, calculating the pressure differences in fluid flow, and in the design of hydraulic systems.
- The specific gravity of a fluid can also be used to determine the concentration of a solute in a solution, such as in the case of measuring the sugar content of a solution in the food industry.
