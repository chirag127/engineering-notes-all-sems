### Construction and Working of Two stroke and four stroke SI & CI engine for the notes of the Unit 2 - Introduction to IC Engines and Electric Vehicles in the subject of FUNDAMENTALS OF MECHANICAL ENGINEERING

Unit 2 - Introduction to IC Engines and Electric Vehicles:

Construction and working of two-stroke and four-stroke engines are important concepts in the study of internal combustion engines. In Unit 2 of Fundamentals of Mechanical Engineering, it is important to understand the basic principles and differences between two-stroke and four-stroke engines.

1. Two-Stroke Engine: A two-stroke engine is a type of internal combustion engine that completes a power cycle in just two strokes of the piston. It is a simpler and lighter engine than a four-stroke engine.

2. Construction: A two-stroke engine has a simpler construction compared to a four-stroke engine. It has a single crankcase that houses the crankshaft, connecting rod, and piston. The fuel mixture is drawn into the crankcase and compressed by the rising piston.

3. Working: In a two-stroke engine, the fuel mixture is drawn into the crankcase during the downward stroke of the piston. The mixture is then compressed during the upward stroke of the piston and is ignited, causing the explosion that drives the piston back down. The exhaust gases are expelled during the downward stroke of the piston.

4. Four-Stroke Engine: A four-stroke engine is a type of internal combustion engine that completes a power cycle in four strokes of the piston. It is a more complex and heavier engine than a two-stroke engine.

5. Construction: A four-stroke engine has a more complex construction compared to a two-stroke engine. It has a separate cylinder head, camshaft, and valve train. The fuel mixture is drawn into the cylinder during the intake stroke of the piston, compressed during the compression stroke, ignited during the power stroke, and expelled during the exhaust stroke.

6. Working: In a four-stroke engine, the fuel mixture is drawn into the cylinder during the intake stroke of the piston. The mixture is then compressed during the compression stroke, causing the mixture to become highly pressurized and volatile. The mixture is then ignited during the power stroke, causing the explosion that drives the piston back down. The exhaust gases are expelled during the exhaust stroke.

7. SI and CI Engines: There are two main types of internal combustion engines: Spark Ignition (SI) engines and Compression Ignition (CI) engines. SI engines use a spark plug to ignite the fuel mixture, while CI engines rely on the high compression ratio to ignite the fuel mixture.

In conclusion, two-stroke and four-stroke engines are important concepts in the study of internal combustion engines. A two-stroke engine is a simpler and lighter engine that completes a power cycle in just two strokes of the piston, while a four-stroke engine is a more complex and heavier engine that completes a power cycle in four strokes of the piston. There are two main types of internal combustion engines: Spark Ignition (SI) engines and Compression Ignition (CI) engines.
