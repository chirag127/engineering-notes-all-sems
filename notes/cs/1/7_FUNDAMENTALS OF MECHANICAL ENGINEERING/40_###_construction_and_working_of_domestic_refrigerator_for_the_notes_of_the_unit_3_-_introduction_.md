Construction and Working of Domestic Refrigerator:
A domestic refrigerator is a household appliance that uses refrigeration to preserve food and other perishable items. It consists of several components, including a refrigeration system, a compressor, a condenser, an evaporator, a refrigerant, and a control system.

Refrigeration System:
The refrigeration system is the core component of the refrigerator that removes heat from the interior of the refrigerator and transfers it to the exterior. It consists of a compressor, a condenser, an evaporator, and a refrigerant.

Compressor:
The compressor is the heart of the refrigeration system and is responsible for compressing the refrigerant and circulating it through the system. The compressed refrigerant is then passed through the condenser, where it releases heat to the exterior of the refrigerator.

Condenser:
The condenser is a heat exchanger that removes heat from the refrigerant as it flows from the compressor. The heat is transferred to the exterior of the refrigerator, where it is dissipated to the surrounding air.

Evaporator:
The evaporator is a heat exchanger that removes heat from the interior of the refrigerator and transfers it to the refrigerant. The refrigerant evaporates as it absorbs heat, and the resulting low-pressure refrigerant is then returned to the compressor to start the cycle again.

Refrigerant:
The refrigerant is a fluid that is used to transfer heat from the interior of the refrigerator to the exterior. It is a substance that evaporates and condenses easily, and it is chosen for its ability to absorb and release heat efficiently.

Control System:
The control system is responsible for regulating the operation of the refrigerator. It includes a thermostat that controls the temperature inside the refrigerator, and a control valve that regulates the flow of refrigerant through the system.

Working Principle:
The working principle of a domestic refrigerator is based on the refrigeration cycle, which involves the transfer of heat from the interior of the refrigerator to the exterior. The refrigerant is compressed by the compressor, releasing heat to the exterior of the refrigerator. The refrigerant then flows to the evaporator, where it absorbs heat from the interior of the refrigerator. The refrigerant then returns to the compressor, where the cycle starts again. The control system regulates the temperature inside the refrigerator by controlling the flow of refrigerant through the system.
