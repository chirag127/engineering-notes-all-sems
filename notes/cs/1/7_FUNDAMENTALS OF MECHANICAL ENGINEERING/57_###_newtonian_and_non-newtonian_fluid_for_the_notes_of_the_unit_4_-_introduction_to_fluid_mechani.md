### Newtonian and Non-Newtonian fluid for the notes of the Unit 4 - Introduction to Fluid Mechanics and Applications in the subject of FUNDAMENTALS OF MECHANICAL ENGINEERING

Newtonian and Non-Newtonian fluids are two types of fluids that exhibit different fluid behavior when subjected to stress. In the subject of Fundamentals of Mechanical Engineering, Unit 4 - Introduction to Fluid Mechanics and Applications, students learn about the characteristics and applications of these two types of fluids.

Newtonian fluids are fluids that obey Newton's law of viscosity, which states that the shear stress is proportional to the shear rate. Newtonian fluids include liquids such as water, oil, and alcohol. They have constant viscosity and behave in a predictable manner when subjected to stress.

Non-Newtonian fluids are fluids that do not obey Newton's law of viscosity. These fluids have varying viscosity and can exhibit different fluid behavior when subjected to stress. Examples of non-Newtonian fluids include ketchup, paint, and blood. Non-Newtonian fluids can exhibit shear-thickening or shear-thinning behavior, which can make them difficult to predict and model.

Understanding the characteristics and applications of Newtonian and Non-Newtonian fluids is important for students pursuing mechanical engineering degrees, as it forms the basis for many advanced topics in fluid mechanics, heat transfer, and thermodynamics.
