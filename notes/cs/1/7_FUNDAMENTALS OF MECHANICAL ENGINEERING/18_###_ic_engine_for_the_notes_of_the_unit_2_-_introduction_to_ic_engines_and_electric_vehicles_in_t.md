Unit 2 - Introduction to IC Engines and Electric Vehicles:

The internal combustion engine (IC engine) is a type of heat engine that uses the combustion of fuel to convert heat energy into mechanical energy. In Unit 2 of Fundamentals of Mechanical Engineering, it is important to understand the basics of IC engines and electric vehicles.

1. Internal Combustion Engine (IC Engine): An IC engine is a type of heat engine that uses the combustion of fuel to convert heat energy into mechanical energy. The heat energy is generated by the combustion of fuel in the engine's cylinders. The mechanical energy is used to drive a vehicle or to perform other tasks.

2. Types of IC Engines: There are several types of IC engines, including the spark-ignition engine and the compression-ignition engine. Spark-ignition engines use a spark plug to ignite the fuel-air mixture, while compression-ignition engines use the high compression of the air-fuel mixture to ignite the fuel.

3. Electric Vehicles: An electric vehicle (EV) is a vehicle that is powered by an electric motor. EVs use energy stored in batteries to drive the vehicle. Unlike IC engines, EVs do not produce emissions and are more efficient in converting energy into motion.

4. Advantages of Electric Vehicles: The main advantages of EVs are their low emissions, high efficiency, and low operating costs. EVs are also quiet, smooth, and easy to drive.

5. Challenges of Electric Vehicles: Despite their advantages, EVs face several challenges, including limited range, high upfront cost, and the need for charging infrastructure.

In conclusion, the internal combustion engine (IC engine) is a type of heat engine that uses the combustion of fuel to convert heat energy into mechanical energy. There are several types of IC engines, including the spark-ignition engine and the compression-ignition engine. Electric vehicles (EVs) are vehicles that are powered by an electric motor and have low emissions, high efficiency, and low operating costs. However, EVs face several challenges, including limited range, high upfront cost, and the need for charging infrastructure.
