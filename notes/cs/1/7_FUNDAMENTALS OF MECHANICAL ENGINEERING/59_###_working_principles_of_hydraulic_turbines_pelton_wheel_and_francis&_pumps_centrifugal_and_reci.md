### Working principles of hydraulic turbines (Pelton Wheel and Francis)& pumps (Centrifugal and Reciprocating) and their classifications and hydraulic lift for the notes of the Unit 4 - Introduction to Fluid Mechanics and Applications in the subject of FUNDAMENTALS OF MECHANICAL ENGINEERING

Hydraulic turbines and pumps are used to convert hydraulic energy into mechanical energy and vice versa. In the subject of Fundamentals of Mechanical Engineering, Unit 4 - Introduction to Fluid Mechanics and Applications, the working principles of hydraulic turbines (Pelton Wheel and Francis) and pumps (Centrifugal and Reciprocating) are studied in detail.

Hydraulic Turbines:
1. Pelton Wheel: Pelton wheel is a type of impulse turbine that uses high-speed water jets to strike the blades of the turbine wheel. The kinetic energy of the water is converted into mechanical energy by the turbine wheel.

2. Francis: Francis is a type of reaction turbine that uses the pressure difference between the inlet and outlet of the turbine to generate mechanical energy.

Hydraulic Pumps:
1. Centrifugal: Centrifugal pumps use a rotating impeller to generate a flow of fluid by converting rotational energy into fluid energy.

2. Reciprocating: Reciprocating pumps use a reciprocating motion to generate a flow of fluid by converting mechanical energy into fluid energy.

Classification of hydraulic turbines and pumps is based on the type of fluid flow, the direction of fluid flow, and the type of energy conversion. Understanding the working principles and classifications of hydraulic turbines and pumps is essential for students pursuing mechanical engineering degrees, as it forms the basis for many advanced topics in fluid mechanics and hydraulic systems.

Hydraulic lift:
Hydraulic lift is a type of machine that uses hydraulic power to lift heavy loads. It consists of a hydraulic cylinder, a pump, and a fluid reservoir. The hydraulic cylinder is filled with fluid, and the pump is used to generate pressure in the fluid, which is then used to lift the load. Understanding the working principles of hydraulic lifts is important for students pursuing mechanical engineering degrees, as it forms the basis for many advanced topics in hydraulic systems and materials handling.
