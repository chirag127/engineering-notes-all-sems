### Electric vehicles and hybrid vehicles for the notes of the Unit 2 - Introduction to IC Engines and Electric Vehicles in the subject of FUNDAMENTALS OF MECHANICAL ENGINEERING

Electric Vehicles and Hybrid Vehicles:

Unit 2 of Fundamentals of Mechanical Engineering covers Introduction to IC Engines and Electric Vehicles. In this unit, it is important to understand the basics of electric vehicles (EVs) and hybrid vehicles (HVs).

1. Electric Vehicles: Electric vehicles are powered by an electric motor and a battery. They produce zero emissions and are considered environmentally friendly. EVs have a limited range, but can be recharged using charging stations.

2. Hybrid Vehicles: Hybrid vehicles are powered by a combination of an internal combustion engine and an electric motor. HVs use the electric motor for low-speed driving and the internal combustion engine for high-speed driving. HVs have improved fuel efficiency compared to traditional internal combustion engine vehicles.

3. Advantages of Electric Vehicles: The main advantages of EVs are their zero emissions, quiet operation, and improved fuel efficiency compared to traditional internal combustion engine vehicles.

4. Advantages of Hybrid Vehicles: The main advantages of HVs are their improved fuel efficiency compared to traditional internal combustion engine vehicles and their ability to use the electric motor for low-speed driving, which results in lower emissions.

5. Limitations of Electric Vehicles: The main limitations of EVs are their limited range and the need for charging stations. Additionally, EVs are more expensive compared to traditional internal combustion engine vehicles.

6. Limitations of Hybrid Vehicles: The main limitations of HVs are their higher cost compared to traditional internal combustion engine vehicles and their dependence on the internal combustion engine for high-speed driving.

In conclusion, electric vehicles and hybrid vehicles are alternative forms of transportation that are becoming increasingly popular. EVs are powered by an electric motor and a battery and produce zero emissions. HVs are powered by a combination of an internal combustion engine and an electric motor and have improved fuel efficiency compared to traditional internal combustion engine vehicles. Both EVs and HVs have advantages and limitations, and it is important to understand these in order to make informed decisions about transportation.
