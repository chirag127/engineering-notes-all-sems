### Pyramids

- A pyramid is a solid figure with a polygonal base and triangular faces that meet at a common vertex.
- The base can be any polygon, such as a square, a triangle, a pentagon, etc.
- The height of a pyramid is the perpendicular distance from the vertex to the base plane.
- The slant height of a pyramid is the distance from the vertex to the midpoint of any edge of the base.
- The axis of a pyramid is the line joining the vertex and the center of the base.
- The projection of a pyramid is the representation of the pyramid on a plane, such as the horizontal plane (HP) or the vertical plane (VP).
- The projection of a pyramid can show different views of the pyramid, such as the front view, the top view, the side view, etc.
- The projection of a pyramid can also show the true shape and size of the base or any face of the pyramid, if the plane of projection is parallel to the base or the face.
- The projection of a pyramid can be done by using the following steps:
  - Draw the base of the pyramid on the plane of projection, such as HP or VP.
  - Draw the axis of the pyramid from the center of the base to the vertex, making an angle with the plane of projection, if given.
  - Draw the projectors from the vertex to the corners of the base, intersecting the plane of projection.
  - Join the points of intersection to form the outline of the pyramid.
  - If the pyramid is cut by another plane, such as a cutting plane (CP), draw the intersection curve of the pyramid and the plane on the projection.
  - If the pyramid is truncated by another plane, such as a truncating plane (TP), draw the outline of the truncated pyramid on the projection.
  - Label the points, lines, and angles on the projection as per the given notation.
- The projection of a pyramid can be used to visualize the shape and dimensions of the pyramid, and to calculate the surface area and volume of the pyramid.
