### Cylinder and cone when the axis is inclined to one reference plane by change of position method

- A cylinder is a solid generated by the revolution of a rectangle about one of its sides, which remains fixed.
- A cone is a solid generated by the revolution of a right-angled triangle about one of its sides, which remains fixed.
- The axis of a cylinder or a cone is the line joining the centers of the two bases.
- When the axis of a cylinder or a cone is inclined to one reference plane (either HP or VP) and parallel to the other, the projections of the solid can be obtained by the change of position method.
- The change of position method involves rotating the solid about an axis perpendicular to the reference plane until the axis becomes perpendicular to the same plane.
- The steps for drawing the projections of a cylinder or a cone by the change of position method are :
  - Draw the projections of the solid in the simple position, i.e., when the axis is perpendicular to HP and parallel to VP, or vice versa.
  - Mark the center of the base and the vertex of the solid on the reference plane.
  - Draw a circle with the center as the center of the base and the radius as the length of the axis.
  - Mark the angle of inclination of the axis with the reference plane on the circle.
  - Draw a line from the center of the base to the point of intersection of the circle and the angle.
  - This line is the true length and inclination of the axis in the reference plane.
  - Rotate the solid about this line until the axis becomes perpendicular to the reference plane.
  - Draw the projections of the solid in the new position, keeping the base and the vertex on the reference plane.
  - Join the corresponding points of the projections in the simple and the inclined positions by dashed lines.
  - Erase the unwanted lines and darken the visible edges.
