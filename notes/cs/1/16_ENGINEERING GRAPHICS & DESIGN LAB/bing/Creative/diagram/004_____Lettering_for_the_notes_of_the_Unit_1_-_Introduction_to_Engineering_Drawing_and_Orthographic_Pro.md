### Lettering for the notes of the Unit 1 - Introduction to Engineering Drawing and Orthographic Projections

- Lettering is the process of writing or drawing letters, symbols, or numbers on a drawing or a diagram.
- Lettering is used to convey information, such as dimensions, specifications, instructions, titles, etc.
- Lettering should be clear, legible, uniform, and consistent throughout the drawing or the diagram.
- Lettering should follow some standard rules and guidelines, such as:

  - The size and style of the lettering should be appropriate for the scale and purpose of the drawing or the diagram.
  - The lettering should be aligned horizontally or vertically, unless otherwise specified.
  - The lettering should be spaced evenly and proportionally, with adequate margins and gaps between words and lines.
  - The lettering should be written or drawn with a sharp pencil, pen, or marker, using straight or curved strokes.
  - The lettering should be written or drawn in capital letters, unless otherwise specified.
  - The lettering should be written or drawn in a single stroke, without overwriting or erasing.
  - The lettering should be written or drawn in a plain and simple font, such as Gothic, Roman, or Italic, avoiding fancy or decorative fonts.
  - The lettering should be written or drawn in black or dark color, contrasting with the background of the drawing or the diagram.

- Lettering can be classified into two types, based on the shape and orientation of the letters, symbols, or numbers:

  - Vertical lettering: The letters, symbols, or numbers are written or drawn with their vertical axis perpendicular to the horizontal line of the drawing or the diagram. Vertical lettering is more commonly used in engineering drawings and diagrams, as it is easier to read and write.
  - Inclined lettering: The letters, symbols, or numbers are written or drawn with their vertical axis inclined at an angle to the horizontal line of the drawing or the diagram. Inclined lettering is less commonly used in engineering drawings and diagrams, as it requires more skill and precision to write and read.

- Lettering can also be classified into two types, based on the height and width of the letters, symbols, or numbers:

  - Single stroke lettering: The letters, symbols, or numbers are written or drawn with a constant height and width, using a single stroke for each character. Single stroke lettering is more suitable for small and medium-sized lettering, as it is faster and simpler to write and draw.
  - Double stroke lettering: The letters, symbols, or numbers are written or drawn with a variable height and width, using two strokes for each character. Double stroke lettering is more suitable for large and bold lettering, as it is more visible and attractive to write and draw.

- Lettering can also be classified into two types, based on the thickness and shape of the strokes of the letters, symbols, or numbers:

  - Thick and thin lettering: The letters, symbols, or numbers are written or drawn with a combination of thick and thin strokes, creating a contrast and emphasis on the characters. Thick and thin lettering is more suitable for titles, headings, and labels, as it is more eye-catching and expressive to write and draw.
  - Uniform lettering: The letters, symbols, or numbers are written or drawn with a uniform thickness and shape of the strokes, creating a consistency and harmony on the characters. Uniform lettering is more suitable for dimensions, specifications, and instructions, as it is more clear and precise to write and draw.
