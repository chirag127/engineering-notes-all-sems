### Fillet

- A fillet is a curved surface that connects two adjacent faces or edges of a solid object, such as a mechanical part or a 3D model.
- Fillets are used to smooth sharp corners, reduce stress concentration, improve appearance, and facilitate manufacturing processes such as casting, molding, or machining.
- Fillets can be classified into two types: internal and external. Internal fillets are concave curves that connect two interior faces or edges, while external fillets are convex curves that connect two exterior faces or edges.
- Fillets can be dimensioned using the radius (R) of the curve, or the diameter (D) of the circle that defines the curve. The radius is preferred for internal fillets, while the diameter is preferred for external fillets .
- Fillets can be shown in technical drawings using different methods, depending on the complexity and symmetry of the object. Some common methods are :
  - Showing the fillet with an arrow and a dimension, such as R5 or D10, indicating the radius or diameter of the curve.
  - Showing the fillet with a leader line and a note, such as FILLET R5 or FILLET D10, indicating the radius or diameter of the curve.
  - Showing the fillet with a symbol, such as ⌒ or ⌒⌒, indicating the presence of a fillet without specifying the size.
  - Showing the fillet with a general note, such as FILLETS R5 UNLESS OTHERWISE INDICATED, indicating the default size of the fillets in the drawing.
  - Showing the fillet with a multiple dimension, such as 4XR5 or 4XD10, indicating the number and size of the symmetrical fillets in the drawing.
- Fillets can be quickly designed onto parts using 3D solid modeling engineering CAD software by invoking the function and picking edges of interest. Smooth edges connecting two simple flat features are generally simple for a computer to create and fast for a human user to specify.
