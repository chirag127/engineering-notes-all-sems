### Isometric projections of simple and combination of solids

- Isometric projection is a method of graphic representation of three-dimensional objects, used by engineers, technical illustrators, and architects.
- Isometric projection shows the object as it appears to the eye from a particular direction, with all the dimensions preserved at the same scale.
- Isometric projection is obtained by rotating the object 45 degrees about a vertical axis, then tilting the object forward until the body diagonal appears as a point in the front view .
- Isometric projection can be used to show simple geometrical solids, such as prisms, pyramids, cylinders, and cones, as well as combinations of any two or more of these solids  .
- Isometric projection can also be used to show the truncation of a solid when it is in a simple vertical position, by a cutting plane inclined to the horizontal plane (HP).
- To draw an isometric projection of a simple solid, the following steps are followed :
  - Draw the isometric axes, which are three mutually perpendicular lines that represent the length, width, and height of the object.
  - Draw the isometric scale, which is a scale that converts the true dimensions of the object to the isometric dimensions, using the ratio of 1:√2.
  - Draw the isometric outline of the object, using the isometric scale and the isometric axes as guides.
  - Draw the hidden edges of the object, using dashed lines.
  - Draw the details of the object, such as curves, holes, or chamfers, using the isometric scale and the isometric axes as guides.
  - Shade the object, if required, to show the light and shadow effects.
- To draw an isometric projection of a combination of solids, the following steps are followed :
  - Draw the isometric projection of the larger solid, following the steps for a simple solid.
  - Draw the isometric projection of the smaller solid, following the steps for a simple solid, and aligning it with the larger solid according to the given position.
  - Erase the overlapping or hidden edges of the solids, using dashed lines or eraser.
  - Draw the details of the combination, such as curves, holes, or chamfers, using the isometric scale and the isometric axes as guides.
  - Shade the combination, if required, to show the light and shadow effects.
- To draw an isometric projection of a truncated solid, the following steps are followed:
  - Draw the isometric projection of the solid before truncation, following the steps for a simple solid.
  - Draw the isometric projection of the cutting plane, using the given inclination angle and the isometric scale and axes as guides.
  - Mark the points of intersection of the cutting plane and the solid, using the isometric scale and axes as guides.
  - Draw the isometric projection of the truncated solid, by joining the points of intersection and erasing the unwanted edges of the solid.
  - Draw the details of the truncated solid, such as curves, holes, or chamfers, using the isometric scale and the isometric axes as guides.
  - Shade the truncated solid, if required, to show the light and shadow effects.
