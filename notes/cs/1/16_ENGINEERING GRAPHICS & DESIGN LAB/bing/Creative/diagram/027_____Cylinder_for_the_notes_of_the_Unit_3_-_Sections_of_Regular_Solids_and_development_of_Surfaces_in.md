### Cylinder

A cylinder is a three-dimensional solid that has two circular bases and a curved surface. The bases are parallel and congruent, and the axis of the cylinder is the line segment that joins the centers of the bases. The height of the cylinder is the distance between the bases. A cylinder can be either right or oblique, depending on whether the axis is perpendicular or slanted to the bases.

Some examples of cylinders are pipes, cans, barrels, and tubes.

#### Sections of a Cylinder

A section of a cylinder is the intersection of a plane and the cylinder. Depending on the position and orientation of the plane, the section can have different shapes. Some common types of sections are:

- A circular section, when the plane is parallel to the bases and passes through the curved surface. The section is a circle whose radius is equal to the radius of the cylinder.
- An elliptical section, when the plane is oblique to the bases and passes through the curved surface. The section is an ellipse whose major axis is equal to the diameter of the cylinder and whose minor axis depends on the angle of the plane.
- A rectangular section, when the plane is perpendicular to the axis and passes through the curved surface. The section is a rectangle whose length is equal to the circumference of the cylinder and whose width is equal to the height of the cylinder.
- A triangular section, when the plane is perpendicular to the axis and passes through one of the bases and the curved surface. The section is a triangle whose base is equal to the diameter of the cylinder and whose height is equal to the height of the cylinder.
