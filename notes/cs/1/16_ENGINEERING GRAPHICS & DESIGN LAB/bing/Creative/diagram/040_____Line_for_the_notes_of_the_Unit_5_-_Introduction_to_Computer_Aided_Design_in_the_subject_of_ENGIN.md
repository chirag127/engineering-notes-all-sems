### Line

- A line is the basic element of any geometrical drawing. It can be defined as the path traced by a moving point in a plane or space.
- A line can be classified into different types based on its direction, position, length, and thickness.
- Some of the common types of lines are:
  - Horizontal line: A line that is parallel to the horizon or the x-axis of a coordinate system.
  - Vertical line: A line that is perpendicular to the horizon or the x-axis of a coordinate system.
  - Inclined line: A line that makes an angle with the horizon or the x-axis of a coordinate system.
  - Perpendicular line: A line that makes a right angle with another line.
  - Parallel line: A line that maintains a constant distance from another line.
  - Intersecting line: A line that crosses another line at a point.
  - Coincident line: A line that coincides with another line.
  - Tangent line: A line that touches a curve or a surface at a point without crossing it.
  - Secant line: A line that cuts a curve or a surface at two points.
  - Transversal line: A line that intersects two or more lines at different points.
- A line can also be classified into different types based on its thickness and style. Some of the common types of lines are:
  - Continuous line: A line that is drawn without any breaks or gaps.
  - Dashed line: A line that is drawn with short dashes or gaps.
  - Dotted line: A line that is drawn with small dots or gaps.
  - Chain line: A line that is drawn with alternating long and short dashes.
  - Center line: A line that is drawn with long dashes and short gaps to indicate the center of a symmetrical object or a circular feature.
  - Dimension line: A line that is drawn with short dashes and arrowheads to indicate the size and direction of a measurement.
  - Extension line: A line that is drawn with short dashes to extend the outline of an object or a feature to the dimension line.
  - Leader line: A line that is drawn with an arrowhead to connect a note or a symbol to an object or a feature.
  - Construction line: A line that is drawn with thin and light strokes to assist in the drawing of an object or a feature.
  - Hidden line: A line that is drawn with short dashes to indicate the edges or surfaces of an object or a feature that are not visible from the given view.
  - Phantom line: A line that is drawn with long dashes and two short dashes to indicate the alternate position or motion of an object or a feature.
  - Cutting plane line: A line that is drawn with thick and long dashes and two short dashes to indicate the location and direction of a sectional view.
  - Break line: A line that is drawn with zigzag or wavy strokes to indicate the omission of a part of an object or a feature.

  - Pencil and ruler: A simple and traditional way of drawing a line by using a pencil and a ruler to guide the direction and length of the line.
  - Compass and straightedge: A classical way of drawing a line by using a compass and a straightedge to construct the line based on geometric principles and properties.
  - Mouse and keyboard: A common way of drawing a line by using a mouse and a keyboard to input the coordinates or the parameters of the line in a computer-aided design (CAD) software.
  - Touchscreen and stylus: A modern way of drawing a line by using a touchscreen and a stylus to trace the line on a digital device or a tablet.
