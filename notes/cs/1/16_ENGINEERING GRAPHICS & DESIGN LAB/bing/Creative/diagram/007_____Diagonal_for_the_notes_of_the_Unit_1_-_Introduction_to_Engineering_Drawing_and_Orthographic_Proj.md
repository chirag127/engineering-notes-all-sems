### Diagonal

- A diagonal is a straight line that joins two opposite corners of a plane figure, such as a rectangle, a square, or a parallelogram.
- A diagonal can be used to divide a plane figure into two congruent triangles, or to find the length of a side or the area of a plane figure using the Pythagorean theorem or trigonometry.
- A diagonal can also be used to represent the direction or orientation of an object in an orthographic projection, which is a method of representing a three-dimensional object on a two-dimensional plane using parallel lines.
- An orthographic projection consists of three principal views: the front view, the top view, and the right side view. Each view shows the object as seen from a different direction, perpendicular to one of the principal planes: the frontal plane, the horizontal plane, and the profile plane.
- A diagonal can help to identify the true shape and size of a plane surface of an object in an orthographic projection, by showing the angle between the surface and the principal planes. For example, if a surface is parallel to the frontal plane, it will appear as a true shape and size in the front view, but as a line in the top and right side views. If a surface is inclined to the frontal plane, it will appear as a foreshortened shape in the front view, and as a true shape and size in either the top or the right side view, depending on the angle of inclination.
- A diagonal can also help to locate the position of a point or a feature of an object in an orthographic projection, by showing the distance from the point or the feature to the principal planes. For example, if a point is located on the diagonal of a rectangular prism, it will have the same distance from the frontal plane and the horizontal plane, and it will appear at the intersection of the diagonal lines in the front and top views. If a feature, such as a hole or a notch, is located on the diagonal of a rectangular prism, it will have the same distance from the frontal plane and the horizontal plane, and it will appear as a circle or a rectangle in the front and top views, with the center or the corner at the intersection of the diagonal lines.
