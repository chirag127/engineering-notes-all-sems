## Unit 1 - Introduction to Engineering Drawing and Orthographic Projections

- Engineering drawing is a graphical language used by engineers and other technical personnel to communicate design ideas, specifications, and dimensions of physical objects and systems.
- Engineering drawing can be classified into two types: artistic drawing and technical drawing. Artistic drawing is used to express aesthetic or creative ideas, while technical drawing is used to convey accurate and precise information about the shape, size, and features of an object or system.
- Technical drawing can be further divided into two categories: pictorial drawing and orthographic drawing. Pictorial drawing is a three-dimensional representation of an object or system, such as isometric, oblique, or perspective drawing. Orthographic drawing is a two-dimensional representation of an object or system, such as front, top, and side views.
- Orthographic projection is a method of representing a three-dimensional object or system on a two-dimensional plane by projecting its features perpendicular to the plane. Orthographic projection is also known as orthogonal projection or multiview projection.
- Orthographic projection follows two main principles: the principle of projection and the principle of alignment. The principle of projection states that each point on the object or system is projected perpendicular to the plane of projection. The principle of alignment states that the corresponding points on different views are aligned along a common line perpendicular to the plane of projection.
- Orthographic projection can be done using two methods: first-angle projection and third-angle projection. First-angle projection is used in Europe and Asia, while third-angle projection is used in North America and Australia. The difference between the two methods is the orientation of the object or system relative to the planes of projection.
- In first-angle projection, the object or system is placed in the first quadrant, between the horizontal and vertical planes of projection. The front view is projected on the vertical plane, the top view is projected on the horizontal plane, and the side view is projected on the plane perpendicular to the horizontal and vertical planes. The views are then unfolded to form a two-dimensional drawing, with the front view on the right, the top view on the bottom, and the side view on the left.
- In third-angle projection, the object or system is placed in the third quadrant, behind the horizontal and vertical planes of projection. The front view is projected on the vertical plane, the top view is projected on the horizontal plane, and the side view is projected on the plane perpendicular to the horizontal and vertical planes. The views are then unfolded to form a two-dimensional drawing, with the front view on the left, the top view on the top, and the side view on the right.
- To distinguish between the two methods of orthographic projection, a symbol called the projection symbol is used. The projection symbol consists of a cone and a plane, representing the object or system and the plane of projection, respectively. The projection symbol is placed in the bottom right corner of the drawing, with the cone pointing to the quadrant where the object or system is located. For first-angle projection, the cone points to the first quadrant, while for third-angle projection, the cone points to the third quadrant.
