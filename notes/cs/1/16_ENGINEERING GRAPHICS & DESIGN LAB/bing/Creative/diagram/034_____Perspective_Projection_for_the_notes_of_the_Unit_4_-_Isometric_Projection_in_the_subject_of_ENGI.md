### Perspective Projection

- Perspective projection is a type of projection that shows a three-dimensional object on a two-dimensional plane as it appears to the human eye from a certain point of view.
- Perspective projection is used to create realistic and artistic drawings of objects and scenes, but it is not suitable for engineering drawings that require accurate dimensions and shapes.
- Perspective projection is based on the principle that the lines of projection from the object to the center of projection (or station point) are not parallel, but converge at a single point.
- The center of projection is the position of the observer's eye, and the distance from the center of projection to the plane of projection (or picture plane) is called the perspective distance.
- The perspective distance affects the degree of distortion and foreshortening of the object. The smaller the perspective distance, the more distorted and foreshortened the object appears.
- There are three types of perspective projection, depending on the number of principal axes of the object that are parallel to the plane of projection: one-point perspective, two-point perspective, and three-point perspective.
- In one-point perspective, only one principal axis of the object is parallel to the plane of projection, and the other two axes converge at a single vanishing point on the horizon line.
- In two-point perspective, no principal axis of the object is parallel to the plane of projection, and the three axes converge at two different vanishing points on the horizon line.
- In three-point perspective, no principal axis of the object is parallel to the plane of projection, and the three axes converge at three different vanishing points, one of which is above or below the horizon line.
- To draw a perspective projection of an object, the following steps are followed:
  - Choose a suitable center of projection, plane of projection, and perspective distance.
  - Draw the horizon line and the vanishing points on the plane of projection.
  - Draw the outline of the object on the plane of projection, using the vanishing points as guides for the converging lines.
  - Draw the lines of projection from the corners and edges of the object to the center of projection.
  - Mark the points of intersection of the lines of projection with the plane of projection, and connect them to form the perspective view of the object.
  - Erase the lines of projection and the parts of the object that are hidden behind the plane of projection.
