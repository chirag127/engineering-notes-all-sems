## Unit 3 - Sections of Regular Solids and Development of Surfaces

- A section of a solid is the shape obtained by cutting the solid with a plane.
- A development of a surface of a solid is the unfolded shape of the surface when it is laid out flat.
- Sections and developments are useful for engineering drawing, fabrication, and analysis of solids.

### Sections of Regular Solids

- A regular solid is a solid whose faces are congruent regular polygons.
- Examples of regular solids are prisms, pyramids, cylinders, and cones.
- To draw the section of a regular solid, follow these steps:
  - Draw the front view and the top view of the solid in orthographic projection.
  - Draw the cutting plane as a line on the front view and a polygon on the top view.
  - Mark the points where the cutting plane intersects the edges of the solid.
  - Join the points with straight lines or curves to form the sectional shape.
  - Shade the sectional shape to indicate the cut portion of the solid.
  - Label the sectional shape with letters and indicate the direction of viewing with arrows.

### Development of Surfaces of Regular Solids

- To draw the development of a surface of a regular solid, follow these steps:
  - Draw the front view and the top view of the solid in orthographic projection.
  - Divide the surface into simple shapes, such as rectangles, triangles, or sectors.
  - Mark the points where the edges of the solid intersect the surface.
  - Draw the true lengths of the edges on a horizontal line, called the stretch-out line.
  - Draw the shapes of the surface elements using the true lengths and the angles between the edges.
  - Join the shapes of the surface elements to form the development of the surface.
  - Label the development with letters and indicate the fold lines with dashed lines.
