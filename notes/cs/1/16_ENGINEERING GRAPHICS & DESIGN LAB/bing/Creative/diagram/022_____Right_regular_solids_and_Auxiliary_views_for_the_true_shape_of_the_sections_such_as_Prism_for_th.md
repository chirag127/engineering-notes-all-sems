### Right regular solids and Auxiliary views for the true shape of the sections such as Prism

- A right regular solid is a three-dimensional geometric figure that has congruent and regular faces, and whose axis is perpendicular to its base. Examples of right regular solids are cube, tetrahedron, octahedron, dodecahedron, icosahedron, right circular cylinder, right circular cone, and right regular prism.
- A prism is a right regular solid that has two parallel and congruent polygonal bases, and rectangular lateral faces. The bases can be triangles, squares, pentagons, hexagons, etc. The height of the prism is the perpendicular distance between the bases.
- An auxiliary view is a view that shows the true shape and size of a surface that is oblique or inclined to the principal planes of projection. An auxiliary view is projected from an existing view, usually the front, top, or side view, along a reference line that is perpendicular to the inclined surface   .
- To draw an auxiliary view for the true shape of the section of a prism, follow these steps:
  - Draw the front, top, and side views of the prism with the cutting plane line.
  - Draw a reference line perpendicular to the cutting plane line on the front view.
  - Project the points of intersection of the cutting plane line and the edges of the prism from the front view to the top view.
  - Draw the true shape of the section on the top view by connecting the projected points with straight lines.
  - Draw an auxiliary reference line perpendicular to the reference line on the front view.
  - Project the points of the true shape of the section from the top view to the auxiliary reference line.
  - Draw the auxiliary view by connecting the projected points with straight lines parallel to the reference line.
  - Label the auxiliary view and the reference line.
