- Orthographic projection is a form of parallel projection in which all the projection lines are orthogonal to the projection plane, resulting in every plane of the scene appearing in affine transformation on the viewing surface.
- Perspective projection is a form of projection in which the projection lines are not parallel, but converge at a single point called the center of projection or the vanishing point. This gives the impression of depth and distance, as objects that are farther away appear smaller and closer to the vanishing point.
- Plane figures are two-dimensional shapes that can be drawn on a flat surface, such as circles, squares, triangles, etc. Simple solids are three-dimensional shapes that have flat faces, such as cubes, prisms, pyramids, etc.
- Visual ray method is a technique for drawing perspective views of plane figures and simple solids, in which the edges and vertices of the object are projected along visual rays from the center of projection to the picture plane.

Here is an example of how to use the visual ray method to draw a perspective view of a cube:

1. Draw a horizontal line to represent the ground line (GL) and a vertical line to represent the picture plane (PP).
2. Choose a point on the GL to be the center of projection (CP) and a point on the PP to be the station point (SP). The SP is the position of the observer's eye.
3. Draw a line from the CP to the SP and extend it beyond the PP. This is the principal visual ray (PVR).
4. Draw a square on the GL to represent the base of the cube. Label the vertices as A, B, C, and D.
5. Draw visual rays from the CP to the vertices of the base and extend them to the PP. Label the points where they intersect the PP as A', B', C', and D'.
6. Draw a line parallel to the GL from the SP to the PP. This is the horizon line (HL).
7. Choose a point on the HL to be the vanishing point (VP). The VP is where all the parallel lines in the scene converge in the perspective view.
8. Draw a line from the VP to the PVR and extend it beyond the PP. This is the measuring line (ML).
9. Choose a distance on the ML to represent the height of the cube. Mark this distance from the PVR as E.
10. Draw visual rays from the CP to E and extend them to the PP. Label the point where it intersects the PP as E'.
11. Draw lines parallel to the ML from A', B', C', and D' to the PVR. Label the points where they intersect the PVR as F, G, H, and I.
12. Draw visual rays from the CP to F, G, H, and I and extend them to the PP. Label the points where they intersect the PP as F', G', H', and I'.
13. Draw lines parallel to the edges of the base from A' to F', from B' to G', from C' to H', and from D' to I'. These are the edges of the cube in the perspective view.
14. Erase the visual rays and the ML. The perspective view of the cube is complete.
