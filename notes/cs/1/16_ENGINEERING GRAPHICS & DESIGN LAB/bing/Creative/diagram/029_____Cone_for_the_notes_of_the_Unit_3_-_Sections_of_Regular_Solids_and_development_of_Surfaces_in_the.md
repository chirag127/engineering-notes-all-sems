### Cone

- A cone is a solid generated by revolving a right-angled triangle about one of its sides, called the axis of the cone.
- The base of the cone is a circle, whose radius is equal to the length of the other side of the triangle, called the slant height of the cone.
- The vertex of the cone is the point where the axis intersects the plane of the base.
- The angle between the slant height and the axis is called the semi-vertical angle of the cone.

![Cone](https://i.imgur.com/1c7Z4fH.png)

- A cone can be divided into two parts by a plane parallel to the base, called the frustum of the cone. The smaller part is called the apex of the cone.
- The height of the frustum is the distance between the two parallel planes. The slant height of the frustum is the length of the slant edge of the frustum.

![Frustum](https://i.imgur.com/8wZ6q0o.png)

### Sections of Cone

- A section of a cone is the curve obtained by cutting the cone by a plane.
- The shape of the section depends on the position and orientation of the cutting plane.
- There are three types of sections of a cone: ellipse, parabola, and hyperbola.

![Sections](https://i.imgur.com/8X9XZaO.png)

- An ellipse is the section of a cone by a plane that cuts the cone at an angle less than the semi-vertical angle of the cone.
- A parabola is the section of a cone by a plane that cuts the cone at an angle equal to the semi-vertical angle of the cone.
- A hyperbola is the section of a cone by a plane that cuts the cone at an angle greater than the semi-vertical angle of the cone.

### Development of Cone

- The development of a cone is the unfolded or flattened surface of the cone, obtained by cutting the cone along a generator and spreading it on a plane.
- The development of a cone is a sector of a circle, whose radius is equal to the slant height of the cone, and whose angle is equal to the angle subtended by the base of the cone at the vertex.

![Development](https://i.imgur.com/0x9X9lO.png)

- The development of a frustum of a cone is also a sector of a circle, whose radius is equal to the slant height of the frustum, and whose angle is equal to the angle subtended by the base of the frustum at the vertex.
- The development of a frustum of a cone can be obtained by cutting the frustum along a slant edge and spreading it on a plane.
