### Creation of engineering models and their presentation in standard 2D blueprint form

- Engineering models are representations of physical objects or systems that capture their geometry, topology, and properties.
- Engineering models can be created using various methods, such as sketching, drafting, solid modeling, surface modeling, or mesh modeling.
- Engineering models can be presented in different forms, such as 2D blueprints, 3D wire-frames, shaded solids, or rendered images.
- 2D blueprints are drawings that show the dimensions, tolerances, and annotations of an engineering model using standard symbols and conventions.
- 2D blueprints are used to communicate the design specifications and requirements of an engineering model to other engineers, manufacturers, or customers.
- 2D blueprints can be generated automatically from 3D models using modeling software that supports associative modeling, interference checking, and bill of materials.
- 2D blueprints follow the ISO and ANSI standards for coordinate dimensioning and tolerancing, and geometric dimensioning and tolerancing.
- Coordinate dimensioning and tolerancing specify the size, location, and orientation of features using linear and angular measurements, and the allowable variations using limits or modifiers.
- Geometric dimensioning and tolerancing specify the shape, form, profile, orientation, and position of features using geometric symbols, datums, and modifiers, and the allowable variations using tolerance zones.
- 2D blueprints consist of multiple views, such as front, top, side, isometric, section, detail, or auxiliary views, that show different aspects of the engineering model.
- 2D blueprints also include title blocks, borders, scales, notes, legends, and other information that provide additional details about the engineering model.
