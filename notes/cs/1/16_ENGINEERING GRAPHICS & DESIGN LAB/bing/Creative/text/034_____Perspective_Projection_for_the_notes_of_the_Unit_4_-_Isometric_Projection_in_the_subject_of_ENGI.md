### Perspective Projection

- Perspective projection is a type of projection that shows a three-dimensional object as it appears to the human eye from a certain point of view.
- Perspective projection is used to create realistic and artistic drawings of objects and scenes, but it is not suitable for engineering drawings that require accurate dimensions and shapes.
- Perspective projection is based on the principle that the lines of projection from the object to the center of projection (or station point) are not parallel, but converge at a single point.
- The center of projection can be located at a finite or infinite distance from the object, depending on the type of perspective projection.
- There are three main types of perspective projection: one-point, two-point, and three-point perspective, depending on the number of principal axes of the object that are parallel to the picture plane.
- One-point perspective is used when one of the principal axes of the object is parallel to the picture plane, and the other two axes converge at a single vanishing point on the horizon line.
- Two-point perspective is used when two of the principal axes of the object are parallel to the picture plane, and the third axis converges at two vanishing points on the horizon line.
- Three-point perspective is used when none of the principal axes of the object are parallel to the picture plane, and all three axes converge at three vanishing points on or off the horizon line.
- The perspective projection of an object can be constructed by drawing the station point, the picture plane, the horizon line, the vanishing points, and the projectors from the object to the center of projection.
- The perspective projection of an object can also be obtained by using a perspective grid, which is a network of lines that represent the projectors from the object to the center of projection. The perspective grid can be drawn by using a ruler and a protractor, or by using a software tool.
