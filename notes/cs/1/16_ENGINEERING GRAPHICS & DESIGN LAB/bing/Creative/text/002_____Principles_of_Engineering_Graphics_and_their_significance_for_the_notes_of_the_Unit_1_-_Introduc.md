### Principles of Engineering Graphics and their significance

- Engineering graphics is the discipline of creating and interpreting graphical representations of physical objects, concepts, and processes using various techniques and tools.
- Engineering graphics is essential for communicating design ideas, visualizing spatial relationships, and documenting engineering specifications.
- Engineering graphics follows certain principles and standards to ensure clarity, accuracy, and consistency of the graphical information.
- Some of the principles of engineering graphics are    :
  - Use of appropriate projections, views, and scales to show the shape, size, and orientation of the object.
  - Use of proper symbols, notations, and conventions to indicate dimensions, tolerances, materials, and surface conditions of the object.
  - Use of correct line types, thicknesses, and colors to distinguish different features and elements of the object.
  - Use of auxiliary lines, center lines, break lines, and section lines to aid the visualization and understanding of the object.
  - Use of orthographic, isometric, and perspective projections to represent the object in different ways and perspectives.
  - Use of freehand sketching, manual drafting, and computer-aided drafting and design (CAD/CAM) tools to create and modify the graphical representations of the object.
- The significance of engineering graphics is that it enables engineers to:
  - Express their ideas and concepts in a clear and concise manner.
  - Communicate and collaborate with other engineers, designers, manufacturers, and customers.
  - Analyze and evaluate the feasibility, functionality, and aesthetics of the design.
  - Test and verify the performance and quality of the design.
  - Document and present the design for fabrication, assembly, and maintenance.
