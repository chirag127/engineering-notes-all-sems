## Unit 4 - Isometric Projection

- Isometric projection is a method for visually representing three-dimensional objects in two dimensions in technical and engineering drawings.
- It is an axonometric projection in which the three coordinate axes appear equally foreshortened and the angle between any two of them is 120 degrees .
- Isometric projection is a form of orthographic projection, which means that the object is mapped onto the drawing plane by dropping perpendiculars from each point to the plane.
- Isometric projection preserves the proportions and dimensions of the object, but distorts the angles and shapes.
- Isometric projection is useful for showing the overall shape and size of an object, but not the details or hidden features.
- Isometric projection can be drawn using a 30-degree set square, a compass, and a ruler, or using a computer-aided design (CAD) software.
- Isometric projection can be classified into two types: isometric drawing and isometric pictorial.
  - Isometric drawing is a precise and accurate representation of the object, following the rules of isometric projection.
  - Isometric pictorial is a more informal and artistic representation of the object, using some approximations and simplifications to enhance the appearance.
- Isometric projection can be used for various purposes, such as engineering design, architecture, video games, and art .
