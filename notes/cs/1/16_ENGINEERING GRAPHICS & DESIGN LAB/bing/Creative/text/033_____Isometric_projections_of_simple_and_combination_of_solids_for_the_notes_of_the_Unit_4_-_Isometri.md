### Isometric projections of simple and combination of solids

- Isometric projection is a method for visually representing three-dimensional objects in two dimensions in technical and engineering drawings.
- It is an axonometric projection in which the three coordinate axes appear equally foreshortened and the angle between any two of them is 120 degrees .
- Isometric projection is useful for showing the overall shape and dimensions of an object, but it does not preserve the true lengths or angles of the object.
- To draw an isometric projection of a simple solid, such as a cube, cylinder, cone, or prism, the following steps are followed:
  - Choose a suitable scale and orientation for the isometric axes.
  - Draw the isometric axes as three equally inclined lines that meet at a point, called the isometric center.
  - Project the vertices of the solid onto the isometric axes by dropping perpendiculars from the vertices to the axes.
  - Connect the projected vertices with straight lines to form the isometric outline of the solid.
  - Shade or hatch the hidden faces of the solid to indicate depth and perspective.
- To draw an isometric projection of a combination of solids, such as a cylinder on a cube, a cone on a prism, or a sphere on a cylinder, the following steps are followed:
  - Draw the isometric projection of the base solid, such as the cube, prism, or cylinder, following the steps above.
  - Locate the center of the top face of the base solid and draw a vertical line from it to the isometric center.
  - Draw the isometric projection of the top solid, such as the cylinder, cone, or sphere, using the vertical line as the axis of symmetry.
  - Erase the overlapping lines and faces of the base and top solids that are hidden from view.
  - Shade or hatch the hidden faces of the combination of solids to indicate depth and perspective.
