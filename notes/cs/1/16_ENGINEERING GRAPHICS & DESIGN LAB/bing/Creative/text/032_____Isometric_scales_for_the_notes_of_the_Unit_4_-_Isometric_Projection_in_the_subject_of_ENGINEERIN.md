### Isometric scales for the notes of the Unit 4 - Isometric Projection in the subject of ENGINEERING GRAPHICS & DESIGN LAB

- Isometric projection is a method for visually representing three-dimensional objects in two dimensions in technical and engineering drawings.
- It is an axonometric projection in which the three coordinate axes appear equally foreshortened and the angle between any two of them is 120 degrees.
- Isometric projection is useful for showing the general shape and dimensions of an object without distortion.
- Isometric scale is a tool for constructing isometric projections of objects by reducing the actual lengths of the edges by a factor of 0.816.
- The isometric scale is constructed as follows:
  - Draw a horizontal line OA.
  - Through O draw OP and OQ at 30º and 45º to the horizontal respectively.
  - On OQ mark the actual scale in mm.
  - Draw verticals from each of the division points on the actual scale to cut OP at the corresponding divisions.
  - The points on OP form the isometric scale.
- The isometric scale can be used to measure the isometric lengths of the edges of an object by placing it parallel to the isometric axes.
- The isometric scale can also be used to draw circles and arcs in isometric projection by using a compass and a protractor.
- Isometric projection is different from isometric view, which is a pictorial representation of an object without using an isometric scale.
- Isometric view is more convenient and practical for showing the appearance of an object, while isometric projection is more accurate and precise for showing the dimensions of an object.
- Isometric design is a facet of graphic design that uses isometric projection to create visually appealing and realistic illustrations of objects and scenes.
- Isometric design can be used for various purposes, such as web design, game design, logo design, infographic design, and animation.
