### Introduction to Engineering Drawing and Orthographic Projections

- Engineering drawing is a graphical language used by engineers and other technical personnel to communicate technical information, such as design specifications, dimensions, tolerances, materials, and manufacturing processes.
- Engineering drawing follows a set of standards and conventions to ensure clarity, accuracy, and uniformity of the drawings.
- Engineering drawing can be classified into two types: pictorial and orthographic.
- Pictorial drawing is a drawing that shows the object as it appears to the eye, with three dimensions (length, width, and height) visible. Pictorial drawing can be further divided into isometric, oblique, and perspective drawing, depending on the angle and position of the object relative to the viewer.
- Orthographic drawing is a drawing that shows the object as it is projected onto two or more planes perpendicular to each other, with only two dimensions (length and width) visible on each plane. Orthographic drawing can be further divided into multiview and axonometric drawing, depending on the number and orientation of the planes.
- Orthographic projection is a method of representing a three-dimensional object on a two-dimensional plane by projecting the object along parallel lines perpendicular to the plane. Orthographic projection can be classified into two types: first-angle and third-angle projection, depending on the position of the object relative to the planes.
- First-angle projection is a method of orthographic projection in which the object is placed in the first quadrant, between the horizontal and vertical planes. The front view is projected onto the vertical plane, the top view is projected onto the horizontal plane, and the right side view is projected onto the plane perpendicular to both. The planes are then unfolded to form a single plane, with the front view on the right, the top view on the bottom, and the right side view on the left.
- Third-angle projection is a method of orthographic projection in which the object is placed in the third quadrant, behind the horizontal and vertical planes. The front view is projected onto the vertical plane, the top view is projected onto the horizontal plane, and the right side view is projected onto the plane perpendicular to both. The planes are then unfolded to form a single plane, with the front view on the left, the top view on the top, and the right side view on the right.
- The choice of projection method depends on the convention followed by the country or region. In general, first-angle projection is used in Europe, Asia, and Australia, while third-angle projection is used in North America.
- To indicate the projection method used in a drawing, a symbol called the projection symbol is placed in the bottom right corner of the drawing. The projection symbol consists of a truncated cone with two circles representing the horizontal and vertical planes. The position of the cone relative to the circles indicates the projection method. For first-angle projection, the cone is placed above and behind the circles, while for third-angle projection, the cone is placed below and in front of the circles.
