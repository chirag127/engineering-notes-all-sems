## Unit 3 - Sections of Regular Solids and development of Surfaces

- A **regular solid** is a three-dimensional shape whose faces are congruent regular polygons, and whose vertices are all the same.
- Examples of regular solids are cubes, tetrahedrons, octahedrons, dodecahedrons, and icosahedrons.
- A **section** of a solid is the shape obtained by cutting the solid with a plane.
- A **development** of a solid is a flat pattern that can be folded to form the solid.
- To find the section of a regular solid, we need to determine the points of intersection of the cutting plane and the edges of the solid, and then connect them with straight lines.
- To find the development of a regular solid, we need to unfold the faces of the solid along the edges, and then draw them on a flat surface without overlapping.
- The development of a regular solid depends on how the solid is cut and unfolded.
- Some properties of the development of a regular solid are:
  - The area of the development is equal to the surface area of the solid.
  - The shape and size of each face in the development are the same as in the solid.
  - The angles between adjacent faces in the development are the same as in the solid.
