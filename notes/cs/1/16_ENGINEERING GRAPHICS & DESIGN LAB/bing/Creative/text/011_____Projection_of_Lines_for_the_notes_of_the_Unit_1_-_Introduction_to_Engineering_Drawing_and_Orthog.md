### Projection of Lines

- A projection of a line is a geometrically represented image of the line obtained on a plane or surface.
- A line can be projected on different planes, such as the horizontal plane (HP), the vertical plane (VP), the profile plane (PP), or the auxiliary plane (AP).
- The projection of a line on a plane depends on the position and orientation of the line with respect to the plane.
- The projection of a line can be classified into four types based on the angle it makes with the plane of projection:
  - Parallel: The line is parallel to the plane of projection and makes zero angle with it. The projection of the line is equal to the true length of the line.
  - Perpendicular: The line is perpendicular to the plane of projection and makes a right angle with it. The projection of the line is a point.
  - Inclined: The line is inclined to the plane of projection and makes an acute or obtuse angle with it. The projection of the line is shorter than the true length of the line.
  - Oblique: The line is oblique to the plane of projection and makes different angles with the two reference planes (HP and VP). The projection of the line on each plane is shorter than the true length of the line.
- The projection of a line can be determined by using the following steps:
  - Draw the reference planes (HP and VP) and the line of intersection (XY) between them.
  - Locate the end points of the line on the reference planes by using the given dimensions or coordinates.
  - Project the end points of the line on the other plane by drawing perpendiculars from them to the line of intersection (XY).
  - Join the projected end points to get the projection of the line on the other plane.
  - Label the end points and the projections of the line with suitable notations.
  - Draw the dimension lines and the projection symbols to indicate the plane of projection and the angle of inclination of the line.
