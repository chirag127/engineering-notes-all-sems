### Projection of lines inclined to one plane and both planes

- A line is a geometrical object that has length but no width or thickness.
- A line can be represented by two points, A and B, that are its extremities.
- A line can be positioned in space in different ways, depending on its orientation with respect to the reference planes, namely the horizontal plane (HP) and the vertical plane (VP).
- The projection of a line is the image of the line on a plane of projection, which is perpendicular to the line of sight of an observer.
- The projection of a line on the HP is called the top view or plan, and the projection of a line on the VP is called the front view or elevation.
- The projection of a line can be classified into three types, depending on its inclination to the reference planes:
  - Parallel to both planes: The line is parallel to both the HP and the VP, and its projections are equal and parallel to the line itself.
  - Inclined to one plane and parallel to the other: The line is inclined to either the HP or the VP, and its projection on the inclined plane is shorter and perpendicular to the projection on the parallel plane.
  - Inclined to both planes: The line is inclined to both the HP and the VP, and its projections are shorter and inclined to each other.
- To draw the projections of a line, the following steps are followed:
  - Assume a suitable position of the line in space, and mark the points A and B on the line.
  - Draw the projections of the points A and B on the HP and the VP, and join them to get the projections of the line.
  - If the line is inclined to one or both planes, find the traces of the line on the planes. A trace is the point where the line intersects the plane.
  - Use the traces and the projections of the points to determine the true length and inclination of the line. The true length of the line is the distance between its traces, and the inclination of the line to a plane is the angle between its projection and its trace on that plane.
