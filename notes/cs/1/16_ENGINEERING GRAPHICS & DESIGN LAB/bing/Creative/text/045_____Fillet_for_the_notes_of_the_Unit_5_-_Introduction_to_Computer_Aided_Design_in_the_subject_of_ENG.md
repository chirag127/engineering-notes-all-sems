### Fillet

- A fillet is a curved surface that connects two adjacent faces or edges of a solid object, such as a mechanical part or a 3D model.
- Fillets are used to smooth sharp corners, reduce stress concentrations, improve aesthetics, and facilitate manufacturing processes such as casting, molding, and machining.
- Fillets can be classified into two types: internal and external. Internal fillets are concave curves that connect two interior faces or edges, while external fillets are convex curves that connect two exterior faces or edges.
- Fillets can be dimensioned in technical drawings using the radius (R) of the curve and the number of fillets (if they are symmetrical). For example, 4XR10 means four fillets with a radius of 10 units.
- Fillets can be created using computer-aided design (CAD) software by selecting the edges or faces to be filleted and specifying the radius value. Some CAD software also allows the user to adjust the shape and orientation of the fillet curve.
- Fillets are commonly used in engineering graphics and design to represent realistic and functional parts and models. They can also be used to create complex and organic shapes by combining multiple fillets of different sizes and directions.
