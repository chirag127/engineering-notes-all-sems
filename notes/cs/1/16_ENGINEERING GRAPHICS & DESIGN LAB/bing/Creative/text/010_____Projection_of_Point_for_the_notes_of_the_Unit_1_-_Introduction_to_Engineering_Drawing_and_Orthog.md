### Projection of Point

- A projection of a point is the image of the point on a plane or surface, obtained by connecting the point with the eye of the observer or the focus of projection, and extending the line until it intersects the plane or surface.
- A point can be projected on different planes or surfaces, depending on the orientation and position of the point, the plane or surface, and the eye or focus of projection.
- In engineering graphics, two types of projections are commonly used: parallel projection and perspective projection.
- In parallel projection, the lines of sight from the point to the plane or surface are parallel to each other, and the projection is independent of the distance between the point and the plane or surface. Parallel projection is used to represent objects in their true shape and size, and to preserve the geometric properties of the object, such as angles, lengths, and ratios.
- In perspective projection, the lines of sight from the point to the plane or surface converge at a single point, called the focus of projection or the vanishing point. Perspective projection is used to represent objects as they appear to the human eye, and to create a realistic and three-dimensional impression of the object. Perspective projection distorts the shape and size of the object, depending on the distance and angle of the point, the plane or surface, and the focus of projection.
- In engineering graphics, parallel projection is further classified into two types: orthographic projection and oblique projection.
- In orthographic projection, the plane or surface is perpendicular to the lines of sight from the point, and the projection is called a normal projection. Orthographic projection is used to represent the object in its true shape and size, and to show the details of the object from different views, such as front view, top view, and side view. Orthographic projection is also known as orthogonal projection or multiview projection.
- In oblique projection, the plane or surface is inclined to the lines of sight from the point, and the projection is called an oblique projection. Oblique projection is used to represent the object in its true shape and size, but with a slight distortion in the depth dimension, and to show the three-dimensional appearance of the object from a single view. Oblique projection is also known as axonometric projection or pictorial projection.
- In engineering graphics, oblique projection is further classified into three types: isometric projection, dimetric projection, and trimetric projection.
- In isometric projection, the plane or surface is inclined to the lines of sight from the point at equal angles of 120 degrees, and the projection is called an isometric projection. Isometric projection is used to represent the object in its true shape and size, but with a uniform reduction in the scale of the object, and to show the three-dimensional appearance of the object from a single view. Isometric projection is also known as isometric drawing or isometric sketching.
- In dimetric projection, the plane or surface is inclined to the lines of sight from the point at two different angles, and the projection is called a dimetric projection. Dimetric projection is used to represent the object in its true shape and size, but with a different reduction in the scale of the object along two axes, and to show the three-dimensional appearance of the object from a single view. Dimetric projection is also known as dimetric drawing or dimetric sketching.
- In trimetric projection, the plane or surface is inclined to the lines of sight from the point at three different angles, and the projection is called a trimetric projection. Trimetric projection is used to represent the object in its true shape and size, but with a different reduction in the scale of the object along three axes, and to show the three-dimensional appearance of the object from a single view. Trimetric projection is also known as trimetric drawing or trimetric sketching.
