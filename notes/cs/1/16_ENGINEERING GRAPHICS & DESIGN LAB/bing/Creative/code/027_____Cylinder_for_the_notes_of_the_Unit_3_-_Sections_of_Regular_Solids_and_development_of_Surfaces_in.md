### Cylinder

A cylinder is a three-dimensional solid that has two circular bases and a curved surface that connects them. The bases are parallel and congruent, and the axis of the cylinder is the line segment that joins the centers of the bases. The height of the cylinder is the distance between the bases. A cylinder can be classified as a right cylinder or an oblique cylinder, depending on whether the axis is perpendicular or slanted to the bases.

Some important terms related to a cylinder are:

- Radius: The distance from the center of the base to the edge of the base.
- Diameter: The distance across the base, passing through the center. It is twice the radius.
- Circumference: The distance around the edge of the base. It is equal to pi times the diameter.
- Lateral surface: The curved surface that connects the bases.
- Lateral area: The area of the lateral surface. It is equal to the product of the circumference and the height.
- Base area: The area of one of the circular bases. It is equal to pi times the square of the radius.
- Total surface area: The sum of the lateral area and the base areas. It is equal to the product of the circumference and the height plus twice the base area.

Some applications of cylinders in engineering graphics are:

- Drawing isometric views of cylindrical objects, such as pipes, rods, cans, etc. Isometric views are three-dimensional views that show the length, width, and height of an object on a 2D plane. To draw an isometric view of a cylinder, one can use the following steps:

  - Draw a vertical line for the axis of the cylinder.
  - Draw two ellipses for the top and bottom bases of the cylinder. The ellipses should be symmetrical and have the same major and minor axes. The major axis should be parallel to the isometric axes, and the minor axis should be perpendicular to the isometric axes.
  - Draw four vertical lines to connect the corresponding points of the ellipses.
  - Erase the hidden lines and add dimensions and labels as needed.

- Developing the surface of a cylinder, which is the process of unfolding the lateral surface into a flat plane. The surface development of a cylinder is a rectangle whose length is equal to the circumference of the base and whose width is equal to the height of the cylinder. To develop the surface of a cylinder, one can use the following steps:

  - Draw a circle for the base of the cylinder and divide it into equal parts, such as 12 or 16. Label the points as A, B, C, etc.
  - Draw a horizontal line for the axis of the cylinder and mark the height of the cylinder on it. Label the points as P and Q.
  - Draw a vertical line perpendicular to the axis and mark the radius of the cylinder on it. Label the point as R.
  - Draw a rectangle whose length is equal to the arc length of the circle and whose width is equal to the height of the cylinder. Label the corners as P, Q, S, and T.
  - Divide the top and bottom edges of the rectangle into the same number of parts as the circle and label them as A, B, C, etc.
  - Draw slant lines to connect the corresponding points of the circle and the rectangle. These lines represent the generators of the cylinder.
  - Cut along the generators and unfold the rectangle to get the surface development of the cylinder.

- Using centerlines to show the axis and the circular features of a cylinder on a 2D drawing. Centerlines are dashed lines that indicate the center of symmetry or rotation of an object. They are used to show the location and size of cylindrical features, such as holes, pins, discs, etc. To use centerlines correctly, one should follow these rules:

  - Draw centerlines as thin lines with long and short dashes alternately.
  - Extend centerlines beyond the outline of the object by a small distance.
  - Do not cross centerlines with dimension lines or extension lines.
  - Do not use centerlines as object lines or hidden lines.
  - Do not use centerlines to show the axis of an oblique cylinder. Use a chain line instead.
