# Orthographic Projection

Orthographic projection is a method of representing a three-dimensional object in two dimensions by projecting its surfaces on a plane perpendicular to the line of sight. Orthographic projection is also known as orthogonal projection or perpendicular projection.

## Principles of Orthographic Projection

The principles of orthographic projection are based on the following assumptions:

- The object is viewed from a fixed position and direction, called the **station point** or **eye point**.
- The object is placed in a rectangular box, called the **glass box** or **projection box**, that encloses the object completely.
- The object is projected onto six planes, called the **principal planes** or **projection planes**, that coincide with the faces of the glass box. The principal planes are named as **frontal plane**, **horizontal plane**, **profile plane**, **top plane**, **bottom plane**, and **back plane**.
- The projection of the object on each plane is obtained by drawing straight lines, called **projectors**, from the station point to the points on the object that are perpendicular to the plane. The projectors are assumed to be parallel to each other and to the line of sight.
- The projection of the object on each plane is scaled down by a factor, called the **scale factor**, that is equal to the ratio of the distance from the station point to the plane and the distance from the station point to the object.
- The projection of the object on each plane is shown in its true shape and size, without any distortion or perspective.

## Types of Orthographic Projection

There are two main types of orthographic projection, depending on the orientation of the object and the principal planes:

- **First-angle projection**: In this type, the object is placed in the first quadrant of the glass box, i.e., between the station point and the frontal plane. The projection of the object on the frontal plane is called the **front view**, and the projection of the object on the horizontal plane is called the **top view**.
