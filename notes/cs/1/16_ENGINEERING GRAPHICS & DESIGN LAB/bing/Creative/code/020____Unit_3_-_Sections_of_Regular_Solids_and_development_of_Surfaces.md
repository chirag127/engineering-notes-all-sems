## Unit 3 - Sections of Regular Solids and development of Surfaces

- A **regular solid** is a three-dimensional shape that has all its faces congruent and all its angles equal. Examples of regular solids are cubes, tetrahedrons, octahedrons, dodecahedrons, and icosahedrons.
- A **section** of a regular solid is the shape that is formed when a plane cuts through the solid. The section can be a polygon, a circle, an ellipse, or a combination of these shapes. The section can also be parallel, perpendicular, or oblique to the base of the solid.
- The **development** of a surface of a regular solid is the process of unfolding the surface into a flat plane. The development can be used to find the area, the net, or the pattern of the surface. The development can also be used to construct the solid from a flat sheet of material.
- To draw the section of a regular solid, the following steps are followed:
  - Draw the front view and the top view of the solid in orthographic projection.
  - Draw the cutting plane in both views and mark the points where it intersects the edges of the solid.
  - Join the points in the front view to form the outline of the section. Shade the section to show that it is cut.
  - Project the points from the front view to the top view and join them to form the true shape of the section.
- To draw the development of a surface of a regular solid, the following steps are followed:
  - Draw the front view and the top view of the solid in orthographic projection.
  - Draw the cutting plane in both views and mark the points where it intersects the edges of the solid.
  - Choose a point on the surface as the starting point and draw a line perpendicular to the edge that it meets. This line is called the **generator**.
  - Draw an arc with the generator as the radius and the point of intersection as the center. This arc is called the **element**.
  - Repeat the above steps for all the points on the surface and draw the elements in a clockwise or anticlockwise direction. The elements should form a closed curve. This curve is called the **contour**.
  - Cut along the contour and unfold the surface into a flat plane. The shape that is obtained is the development of the surface.
