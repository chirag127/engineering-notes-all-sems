### Principles of Engineering Graphics and their significance

- Engineering graphics is the graphical representation of engineering concepts, designs, and specifications using standard symbols, conventions, and techniques.
- Engineering graphics is essential for communicating engineering ideas and information effectively and accurately among engineers, technicians, manufacturers, and customers.
- Engineering graphics follows certain principles and rules that ensure clarity, consistency, and completeness of the drawings and reduce the chances of errors and ambiguities.
- Some of the principles of engineering graphics are:

  - Relationship to other technical drawing types: Engineering graphics is a subset of technical drawing that deals with engineering-related topics, such as mechanical, electrical, civil, and architectural drawings. Engineering graphics can also be classified into different types based on the purpose, such as design, production, assembly, installation, and maintenance drawings.
  - Cascading of conventions by specialty: Engineering graphics uses various conventions and standards that are specific to different engineering disciplines, such as symbols, units, dimensions, tolerances, and projections. These conventions and standards are often derived from international or national standards organizations, such as ISO, ANSI, ASME, and BIS.
  - Legal instruments: Engineering graphics can serve as legal documents that define the rights and obligations of the parties involved in the engineering project, such as contracts, patents, specifications, and codes. Engineering graphics must comply with the relevant laws and regulations and must be clear, accurate, and unambiguous to avoid disputes and litigation.
  - Standardization and disambiguation: Engineering graphics must use standardized and consistent terminology, notation, and format to avoid confusion and misinterpretation. Engineering graphics must also avoid unnecessary details, duplication, and contradiction that may obscure the main information and intent of the drawing.
  - Media: Engineering graphics can be created and presented using various media, such as paper, computer, and digital devices. Engineering graphics must consider the advantages and limitations of each medium and choose the appropriate one for the intended audience and purpose. Engineering graphics must also ensure the quality, durability, and accessibility of the media used.
  - Relationship to model-based definition (MBD/DPD): Engineering graphics can be integrated with model-based definition (MBD) or digital product definition (DPD), which is a method of defining and documenting engineering products using three-dimensional (3D) models and associated data. Engineering graphics can complement MBD/DPD by providing additional information, such as views, sections, dimensions, and annotations, that may not be easily represented or extracted from the 3D models.
  - Systems of dimensioning and tolerancing: Engineering graphics must use appropriate systems of dimensioning and tolerancing to specify the size, shape, location, and variation of the features and components of the engineering product. Engineering graphics must follow the rules and principles of the chosen system, such as linear, angular, geometric, and coordinate dimensioning and tolerancing, to ensure the functionality, interchangeability, and manufacturability of the product.
