### Cylinder

A cylinder is a three-dimensional solid that has two circular bases and a curved surface that connects them. The bases are parallel and congruent, and the axis of the cylinder is the line segment that joins the centers of the bases. The height of the cylinder is the distance between the bases.

Some examples of cylinders are pipes, cans, barrels, and tubes.

The following are some important terms related to cylinders:

- Radius: The distance from the center of the base to the edge of the base. It is the same for both bases.
- Diameter: The distance across the base. It is twice the radius.
- Circumference: The distance around the edge of the base. It is given by the formula C = 2πr, where r is the radius.
- Lateral surface: The curved surface that connects the bases. It is also called the side surface or the mantle.
- Lateral edge: The line segment that joins the edges of the bases. It is also the height of the cylinder.
- Base area: The area of one of the bases. It is given by the formula A = πr^2, where r is the radius.
- Lateral area: The area of the lateral surface. It is given by the formula L = 2πrh, where r is the radius and h is the height.
- Total surface area: The sum of the base area and the lateral area. It is given by the formula T = 2πr(r + h), where r is the radius and h is the height.
- Volume: The amount of space occupied by the cylinder. It is given by the formula V = πr^2h, where r is the radius and h is the height.
