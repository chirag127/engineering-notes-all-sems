### Right regular solids and Auxiliary views for the true shape of the sections such as Prism for the notes of the Unit 3 - Sections of Regular Solids and development of Surfaces in the subject of ENGINEERING GRAPHICS & DESIGN LAB

- A right regular solid is a three-dimensional geometric figure that has congruent and regular faces, and whose axis is perpendicular to its base. Examples of right regular solids are cube, tetrahedron, octahedron, dodecahedron, icosahedron, right circular cylinder, right circular cone, and right regular prism.
- A prism is a right regular solid that has two parallel and congruent polygonal bases, and rectangular lateral faces. The number of lateral faces is equal to the number of sides of the base. The height of the prism is the perpendicular distance between the bases.
- A section of a solid is the shape obtained by cutting the solid with a plane. The plane is called the cutting plane or the section plane. The section plane can be parallel, perpendicular, or inclined to the axis of the solid.
- A sectional view of a solid is the view obtained by removing the portion of the solid in front of the section plane. The sectional view shows the internal details of the solid, such as holes, slots, ribs, etc. The sectional view is drawn in the same position and orientation as the original view of the solid.
- An auxiliary view of a solid is a view that shows the true shape and size of a surface that is not parallel to any of the principal planes of projection. The auxiliary view is obtained by projecting the solid onto an auxiliary plane that is perpendicular to the surface of interest. The auxiliary plane is then unfolded to align with one of the principal planes, and the auxiliary view is drawn in the same position and orientation as the original view of the solid .
- An auxiliary view can be symmetrical or unilateral, depending on whether the auxiliary plane lies on both sides or one side of the reference line. The reference line is the line where the auxiliary plane intersects the principal plane. The reference line is also called the hinge line or the folding line .
- To draw an auxiliary view of a prism, the following steps can be followed  :
  - Identify the surface of the prism that needs to be shown in true shape and size. This surface should be inclined to the principal planes of projection.
  - Draw the front, top, and side views of the prism using orthographic projection. Label the vertices and edges of the prism in each view.
  - Select a reference line that is perpendicular to the inclined surface and lies on one of the principal planes. Draw the reference line in the corresponding view of the prism.
  - Draw an auxiliary plane that is perpendicular to the reference line and the inclined surface. The auxiliary plane should pass through the vertices and edges of the inclined surface. Draw the auxiliary plane in the corresponding view of the prism.
  - Project the vertices and edges of the inclined surface onto the auxiliary plane. Label the projected points in the corresponding view of the prism.
  - Unfold the auxiliary plane to align with the principal plane that contains the reference line. The reference line should act as the hinge or the folding line. Draw the unfolded auxiliary plane in the corresponding view of the prism.
  - Transfer the projected points from the folded auxiliary plane to the unfolded auxiliary plane. Label the transferred points in the corresponding view of the prism.
  - Connect the transferred points to form the outline of the inclined surface. This is the auxiliary view of the prism that shows the true shape and size of the inclined surface. Label the auxiliary view in the corresponding view of the prism.
