# Conversion of pictorial view into orthographic projection

- A pictorial view is a 3-D view of an object that shows how it looks in reality.
- An orthographic projection is a collection of 2-D views of different sides of an object that shows its shape and dimensions.
- To convert a pictorial view into an orthographic projection, the following steps are required:
  - Identify the principal view of the object, which is the view that shows the most features and details of the object.
  - Draw the principal view on a plane of projection, which is a flat surface that is perpendicular to the line of sight.
  - Draw the other views on adjacent planes of projection, which are parallel to the principal plane of projection and perpendicular to each other.
  - Align the views according to the projection method, which can be either first-angle or third-angle projection.
  - Add dimensions, labels, and symbols to the views as per the drawing standards and conventions.
  - Erase the projection lines and planes that are not needed for clarity.

- An example of converting an isometric pictorial view of a cube into an orthographic projection is shown below:

![Isometric view of a cube](https://www.theengineerspost.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/04/isometric-view-of-a-cube.png)



- The principal view of the cube is the front view, which shows the front face of the cube.
- The front view is drawn on the vertical plane of projection, which is perpendicular to the line of sight.
- The top view is drawn on the horizontal plane of projection, which is parallel to the vertical plane and perpendicular to the ground.
- The right side view is drawn on the profile plane of projection, which is parallel to the vertical plane and perpendicular to the horizontal plane.
- The views are aligned according to the first-angle projection method, which means that the object is placed in the first quadrant and the planes are unfolded clockwise.
- The dimensions, labels, and symbols are added to the views as per the drawing standards and conventions.
- The projection lines and planes that are not needed for clarity are erased.
