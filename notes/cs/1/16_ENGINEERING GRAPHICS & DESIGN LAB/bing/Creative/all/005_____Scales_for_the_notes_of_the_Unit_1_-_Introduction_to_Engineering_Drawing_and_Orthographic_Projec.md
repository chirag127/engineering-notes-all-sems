# Scales for the notes of the Unit 1 - Introduction to Engineering Drawing and Orthographic Projections

- Scales are used to measure and represent the actual dimensions of an object or a part of it on a drawing.
- Scales can be classified into three types: plain scales, diagonal scales, and vernier scales.
- Plain scales are used to show the measurements in one or two units, such as meters and centimeters, or feet and inches. They are divided into equal parts and marked with the corresponding values. For example, a plain scale of 1:100 means that 1 cm on the scale represents 100 cm on the actual object.
- Diagonal scales are used to show the measurements in three units, such as meters, decimeters, and centimeters, or feet, inches, and fractions of an inch. They are constructed by drawing a rectangle and dividing it into a number of equal parts, then drawing diagonal lines to subdivide each part into smaller units. For example, a diagonal scale of 1:50 means that 1 cm on the scale represents 50 cm on the actual object, and each part of the scale is divided into 10 parts, each representing 5 cm, and each part is further divided by a diagonal line into 10 parts, each representing 0.5 cm.
- Vernier scales are used to show the measurements in two units with a high degree of accuracy, such as millimeters and tenths of a millimeter, or inches and thousandths of an inch. They are constructed by using two scales, one fixed and one movable, that slide over each other. The fixed scale is divided into equal parts and marked with the larger unit, while the movable scale is divided into a slightly smaller number of equal parts and marked with the smaller unit. The difference between the two scales is called the least count, which is the smallest unit that can be measured by the vernier scale. For example, a vernier scale of 1:10 means that 1 cm on the fixed scale represents 10 cm on the actual object, and the fixed scale is divided into 10 parts, each representing 1 cm, while the movable scale is divided into 9 parts, each representing 0.9 cm. The least count is 0.1 cm, which is the difference between 1 cm and 0.9 cm.

The scale ratios are typically 10, 20, 30, 40, 50, or 60, which mean that 1 inch on the scale represents 10, 20, 30, 40, 50, or 60 feet on the actual object . Engineering scales are used to draw objects that are too large to fit on a standard sheet of paper, such as buildings, bridges, roads, etc.
