# Diagonal Scale

- A diagonal scale is a type of graphical scale that is used to measure small fractions of the unit of measurement in engineering and cartographic drawings .
- It represents a unit into three different multiples, such as meters, centimeters, and millimeters, or feet, inches, and fractions of an inch .
- It consists of a main scale, a sub-scale, and a diagonal scale .
- The main scale is divided into equal parts, each representing a unit of measurement, such as a meter or a foot .
- The sub-scale is a smaller scale that is drawn below the main scale, and is divided into 10 equal parts, each representing a sub-unit of measurement, such as a centimeter or an inch .
- The diagonal scale is a series of diagonal lines that are drawn from the subdivisions of the sub-scale to the successive next subdivisions on the last vertical line of the main scale .
- The diagonal scale is divided into 10 equal parts, each representing a smaller sub-unit of measurement, such as a millimeter or a fraction of an inch .
- To measure a length using a diagonal scale, the following steps are followed :
  - Locate the nearest lower unit of measurement on the main scale, and mark it as the starting point.
  - Locate the nearest lower sub-unit of measurement on the sub-scale, and mark it as the second point.
  - Locate the exact sub-unit of measurement on the diagonal scale, and mark it as the third point.
  - Draw a line from the starting point to the third point, and measure its length using a ruler or a scale.
  - The length of the line is the required measurement.
