# Projection of Straight Lines

- A straight line is the shortest distance between two points.
- A projection of a straight line is the figure obtained by joining the points at which the straight lines drawn from various points on the line meet a plane.
- A projection of a straight line can be used to represent any object like a machine component or a structural element in engineering drawing.
- There are two types of projections of straight lines: orthographic projection and oblique projection.
- Orthographic projection is a method of representing a three-dimensional object in two dimensions by using two or more perpendicular planes.
- Oblique projection is a method of representing a three-dimensional object in two dimensions by using one plane that is not perpendicular to the object.
- The orientation or position of a straight line in space can be described by its inclination with the three principal planes: horizontal plane (HP), vertical plane (VP), and profile plane (PP) .
- The inclination of a straight line with a plane is the angle between the line and its projection on the plane.
- The true length of a straight line is the length of the line itself, not its projection on any plane.
- The true length of a straight line can be found by using the Pythagoras theorem or the cosine rule.
- The true inclination of a straight line with a plane is the angle between the line and the plane, not the angle between the projection of the line and the plane.
- The true inclination of a straight line with a plane can be found by using the sine rule or the tangent rule.
- The projections of a straight line can be drawn by using the following steps :
  - Draw the projections of the two end points of the line on the reference planes.
  - Join the projections of the end points on each plane to get the projections of the line on that plane.
  - Label the projections of the end points and the line with suitable letters and symbols.
  - Indicate the inclination of the line with the planes by using angle marks or dimensions.
  - Indicate the true length and the true inclination of the line by using a dotted line or a leader line.
