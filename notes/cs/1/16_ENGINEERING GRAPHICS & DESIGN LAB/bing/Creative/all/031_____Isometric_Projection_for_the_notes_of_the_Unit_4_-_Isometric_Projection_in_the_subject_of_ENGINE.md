# Isometric Projection

- Isometric projection is a method for visually representing three-dimensional objects in two dimensions in technical and engineering drawings.
- It is an axonometric projection in which the three coordinate axes appear equally foreshortened and the angle between any two of them is 120 degrees .
- Isometric projection is intended to combine the illusion of depth with the clarity and accuracy of a plan view.
- Isometric projection is used by engineers, technical illustrators, and, occasionally, architects to show the shape, size, and orientation of an object without distortion .
- Isometric projection can be drawn by using an isometric grid, a 30-degree set square, or a compass and ruler.
- Isometric projection has some advantages and disadvantages over other types of projections, such as:

  - Advantages:
    - It is easy to draw and understand.
    - It preserves the true dimensions and angles of the object.
    - It shows all the faces of the object in one view.
    - It does not require perspective or foreshortening techniques.
  - Disadvantages:
    - It can be confusing or misleading for complex or curved objects.
    - It can be difficult to measure or scale the object from the drawing.
    - It can be affected by optical illusions or distortions.

- Isometric projection can be classified into two methods: isometric drawing and isometric dimensioning.

  - Isometric drawing is a freehand sketching technique that uses the isometric grid or the 30-degree set square to draw the object as it appears to the eye.
  - Isometric dimensioning is a more precise and accurate technique that uses the compass and ruler to draw the object according to its true dimensions and angles.

- Isometric projection can be used to draw various shapes and solids, such as cubes, prisms, pyramids, cylinders, cones, spheres, etc. by following some basic rules and steps.

  - Rules:
    - The horizontal lines are drawn at 30 degrees to the horizontal plane.
    - The vertical lines are drawn perpendicular to the horizontal plane.
    - The parallel lines are drawn parallel to each other.
    - The hidden lines are drawn with dashed or dotted lines.
    - The dimensions are marked along the axes or the center lines.
  - Steps:
    - Draw the isometric axes (X, Y, and Z) according to the orientation of the object.
    - Draw the base of the object along the X and Y axes.
    - Draw the height of the object along the Z axis.
    - Draw the other faces of the object by connecting the corresponding vertices.
    - Draw the details and features of the object, such as holes, curves, chamfers, etc.
    - Mark the dimensions and labels of the object.
