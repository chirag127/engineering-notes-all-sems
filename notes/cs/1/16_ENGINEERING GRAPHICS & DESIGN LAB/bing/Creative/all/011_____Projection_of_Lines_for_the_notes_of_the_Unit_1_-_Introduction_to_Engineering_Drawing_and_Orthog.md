# Projection of Lines

- Projection of lines is a topic in engineering drawing that deals with the representation of lines on different planes.
- A line is a geometrical element that has only one dimension, namely length. It can be straight or curved, horizontal, vertical, inclined, or perpendicular to a plane.
- A projection of a line is the figure obtained by joining the points where the line intersects a plane or a surface. It can be a point, a line segment, or a line of the same or different length and direction as the original line.
- There are two types of projection methods used in engineering drawing: orthographic projection and oblique projection.
- Orthographic projection is a method of projecting an object on two or more perpendicular planes, such as the front, top, and side views. It is also called parallel projection because the lines of sight are parallel to each other and perpendicular to the planes of projection.
- Oblique projection is a method of projecting an object on a single plane that is not perpendicular to the object. It is also called angular projection because the lines of sight are inclined to the plane of projection at an angle. The angle can be 30°, 45°, or 60° depending on the type of oblique projection: cavalier, cabinet, or general.
- The projection of a line on a plane depends on the position and orientation of the line relative to the plane. There are four possible cases: 
  - The line is parallel to the plane. In this case, the projection is a line segment of the same length and direction as the original line.
  - The line is perpendicular to the plane. In this case, the projection is a point where the line meets the plane.
  - The line is inclined to the plane. In this case, the projection is a line segment of different length and direction than the original line. The length of the projection is proportional to the cosine of the angle between the line and the plane. The direction of the projection is parallel to the projection of the line on a plane perpendicular to the plane of projection.
  - The line is parallel to a plane perpendicular to the plane of projection. In this case, the projection is a line of the same length but opposite direction as the original line. This is also called a true length projection because it shows the actual length of the line.

- In engineering drawing, it is important to know how to determine the true length, true inclination, and apparent inclination of a line from its projections on different planes. The true length is the actual length of the line, the true inclination is the angle between the line and a reference plane, and the apparent inclination is the angle between the projection of the line and a reference line on the same plane.
- The following formulas can be used to find the true length, true inclination, and apparent inclination of a line from its projections:

  - True length = √(front view length² + top view length²)
  - True inclination with horizontal plane = tan⁻¹(front view length / true length)
  - True inclination with vertical plane = tan⁻¹(top view length / true length)
  - Apparent inclination with horizontal line = tan⁻¹(front view length / front view projection length)
  - Apparent inclination with vertical line = tan⁻¹(top view length / top view projection length)

Its projections on the front and top planes are A'B' and A"B" respectively. The following steps can be followed to find the true length, true inclination, and apparent inclination of the line:

  - Step 1: Draw the front and top views of the line AB using the given dimensions and angles. Mark the points A', B', A", and B" on the projections.
  - Step 2: Find the length of the front view projection A'B' using trigonometry. A'B' = AB cos 30° = 50 cos 30° = 43.3 mm
