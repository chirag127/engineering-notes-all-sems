# Isometric Projections of Simple and Combination of Solids

- Isometric projection is a method for visually representing three-dimensional objects in two dimensions in technical and engineering drawings.
- It is an axonometric projection in which the three coordinate axes appear equally foreshortened and the angle between any two of them is 120 degrees.
- Isometric projection is useful for showing the general shape and dimensions of an object, but it does not preserve the true lengths or angles of the object.
- To draw an isometric projection of a simple solid, such as a cube, a cylinder, a cone, or a sphere, the following steps are followed:
  - Choose an isometric plane as the drawing surface, which is a plane that makes equal angles (30 degrees) with the three principal planes of the object.
  - Draw three isometric axes, which are the projections of the three principal axes of the object on the isometric plane. The isometric axes are equally inclined to each other at 120 degrees.
  - Project the vertices of the object onto the isometric plane along lines parallel to the isometric axes. The distances along the isometric axes are scaled by a factor of 0.816, which is the cosine of 30 degrees.
  - Connect the projected vertices to form the edges and faces of the object. Use dashed lines for hidden edges and solid lines for visible edges.
  - Add dimensions, labels, and other details as needed.
- To draw an isometric projection of a combination of solids, such as a cylinder on a cube, a cone on a sphere, or a prism on a pyramid, the following steps are followed:
  - Choose an isometric plane as the drawing surface, which is a plane that makes equal angles (30 degrees) with the three principal planes of the object.
  - Draw three isometric axes, which are the projections of the three principal axes of the object on the isometric plane. The isometric axes are equally inclined to each other at 120 degrees.
  - Project the vertices of the base solid onto the isometric plane along lines parallel to the isometric axes. The distances along the isometric axes are scaled by a factor of 0.816, which is the cosine of 30 degrees.
  - Connect the projected vertices to form the edges and faces of the base solid. Use dashed lines for hidden edges and solid lines for visible edges.
  - Project the vertices of the top solid onto the isometric plane along lines parallel to the isometric axes. The distances along the isometric axes are scaled by a factor of 0.816, which is the cosine of 30 degrees.
  - Connect the projected vertices to form the edges and faces of the top solid. Use dashed lines for hidden edges and solid lines for visible edges.
  - Add dimensions, labels, and other details as needed.
