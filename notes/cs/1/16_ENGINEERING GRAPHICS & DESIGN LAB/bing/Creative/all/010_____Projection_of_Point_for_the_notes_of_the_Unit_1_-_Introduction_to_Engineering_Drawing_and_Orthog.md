# Projection of Point

- Projection in engineering drawing is a technique for representing a three-dimensional object on a two-dimensional drawing.
- A point is the simplest geometric element that has no dimensions, only a position in space.
- To project a point onto a plane, we need to draw a line of sight from the point to the plane and mark the point where the line intersects the plane. This point is called the projection of the point.
- The projection of a point depends on the position of the point relative to the plane and the orientation of the plane relative to the observer.
- There are two types of projections in engineering drawing: parallel projection and perspective projection.
- Parallel projection is a type of projection where the lines of sight are assumed to be parallel to each other and perpendicular to the plane of projection. Parallel projection is used for technical drawings where accuracy and measurements are important.
- Perspective projection is a type of projection where the lines of sight converge at a single point called the vanishing point. Perspective projection is used for artistic drawings where realism and depth are important.

- In parallel projection, the projection of a point is the same regardless of the distance from the observer to the point. In perspective projection, the projection of a point becomes smaller as the distance from the observer to the point increases.
- There are different methods of parallel projection, such as orthographic projection, isometric projection, oblique projection, etc. Each method has its own rules and conventions for projecting a point onto a plane.
