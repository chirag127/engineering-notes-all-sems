# Orthographic representation of perspective views — Plane figures and simple solids — Visual Ray Method

- Orthographic projection is a method of representing three-dimensional objects in two dimensions by projecting them onto a plane.
- Orthographic projection is a form of parallel projection, in which the projection lines are perpendicular to the projection plane.
- Orthographic projection is also a perspective projection, in which the point of perspective is at infinite distance from the object.
- Orthographic projection preserves the true shape and size of the object, but distorts the depth and angle information.
- Orthographic projection can be used to show different views of the same object, such as front, top, and side views.
- Orthographic projection can be used to represent plane figures and simple solids, such as circles, squares, triangles, cubes, cylinders, cones, and spheres.
- Visual Ray Method is a technique of drawing orthographic views of an object by using imaginary rays from the observer's eye to the object.
- Visual Ray Method involves the following steps:
  - Draw a pictorial view of the object, such as an isometric view, and mark the dimensions and features of the object.
  - Draw a horizontal line to represent the ground line (GL), and a vertical line to represent the center line (CL).
  - Draw two projection planes, one perpendicular to the GL and one parallel to the GL, to represent the front view (FV) and the top view (TV) respectively.
  - Draw rays from the observer's eye to the corners and edges of the object, and extend them until they intersect the projection planes.
  - Mark the points of intersection on the projection planes, and connect them with straight lines to form the outlines of the orthographic views.
  - Add the dimensions and features of the object to the orthographic views, and label them accordingly.
