# Creation of engineering models and their presentation in standard 2D blueprint form

- Engineering models are representations of physical objects or systems that capture their essential features and behavior. They can be used for analysis, design, simulation, testing, or communication purposes.
- Engineering models can be created using various methods, such as sketching, drafting, CAD (computer-aided design), or CAE (computer-aided engineering) software. The choice of method depends on the complexity, accuracy, and purpose of the model.
- Engineering models can be presented in different forms, such as 2D blueprints, 3D wireframes, 3D shaded solids, or 3D meshes. The choice of form depends on the level of detail, clarity, and realism required for the presentation.
- 2D blueprints are drawings that show the shape, size, and dimensions of an object or system in two dimensions. They are often used for manufacturing, assembly, or inspection purposes. They can also show the relationships and constraints between different parts or components of a system.
- 2D blueprints follow standard conventions and rules for layout, projection, scaling, dimensioning, tolerancing, and annotation. They use symbols, lines, and text to convey information. They can be created manually or automatically using CAD software.
- 2D blueprints can be classified into different types, such as orthographic, isometric, oblique, or perspective drawings. Orthographic drawings show the object or system from different views, such as front, top, or side. Isometric drawings show the object or system from a 3D angle, but with equal scaling along all axes. Oblique drawings show the object or system from a 3D angle, but with different scaling along different axes. Perspective drawings show the object or system from a 3D angle, but with realistic depth and distortion effects.
- 2D blueprints can also include different types of views, such as sectional, auxiliary, or detail views. Sectional views show the internal features or cross-sections of an object or system by cutting through it. Auxiliary views show the features or dimensions of an object or system that are not visible or clear from the main views. Detail views show the enlarged or magnified features or dimensions of an object or system that are too small or complex to be shown in the main views.
