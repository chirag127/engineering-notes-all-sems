# Engineering Scales

- Engineering scales are rulers that produce ratio drawings, which are drawings that show the actual dimensions of an object in a reduced or enlarged proportion .
- Engineering scales are commonly used for drafting and blueprint creation, especially in civil engineering, mechanical engineering, and architecture  .
- Engineering scales are usually 300 mm long, which is a little more than 12 inches. The extra space on the ends is meant to provide a safety margin. If the scale is exposed to wear and tear, its end notches will still be visible.
- Engineering scales are typically shaped like a long triangular prism, with each side of each point marked with a different scale for measuring. A total of six scales are used.
- The scales are divided into decimalized fractions of an inch, such as 1:10, 1:20, 1:30, 1:40, 1:50, and 1:60, unless otherwise stated. The first graduation on an engineering scale is the zero mark.
- To use an engineering scale, one must first select the appropriate scale for the drawing, then align the zero mark with the starting point of the measurement, and then read the value at the end point of the measurement.
- Engineering scales are different from architect scales, which use fractional divisions of an inch, such as 1/4, 1/8, 1/16, etc. Architect scales are more suitable for building design and construction, while engineering scales are more suitable for technical and mechanical drawings.
