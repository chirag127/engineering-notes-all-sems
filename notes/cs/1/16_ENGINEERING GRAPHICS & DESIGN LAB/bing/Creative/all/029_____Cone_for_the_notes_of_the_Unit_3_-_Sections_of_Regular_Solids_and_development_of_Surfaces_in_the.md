# Cone for the notes of the Unit 3 - Sections of Regular Solids and development of Surfaces in the subject of ENGINEERING GRAPHICS & DESIGN LAB

- A cone is a three-dimensional solid that has a circular base and a curved surface that tapers to a point called the vertex or apex.
- A cone can be cut by a plane in different ways to produce different conic sections, such as circles, ellipses, parabolas and hyperbolas.
- The conic section depends on the angle and position of the cutting plane relative to the axis of the cone.
- The axis of the cone is the line joining the vertex and the center of the base.
- The slant height of the cone is the distance from the vertex to any point on the base edge.
- The height of the cone is the perpendicular distance from the vertex to the base.
- The base radius of the cone is the radius of the circular base.
- The semi-vertical angle of the cone is the angle between the axis and the slant height.

## Sections of a cone

- A section of a cone is the intersection of the cone and a plane.
- The shape of the section depends on the angle and position of the plane.
- If the plane is perpendicular to the axis of the cone, the section is a circle.
- If the plane is parallel to the base of the cone, the section is also a circle.
- If the plane is parallel to the slant height of the cone, the section is an isosceles triangle.
- If the plane is inclined to the axis of the cone and cuts through both nappes (the upper and lower parts of the cone), the section is a hyperbola.
- If the plane is inclined to the axis of the cone and cuts through only one nappe, the section is either an ellipse or a parabola, depending on the eccentricity of the section.
- The eccentricity of a conic section is a measure of how much it deviates from being circular. It is defined as the ratio of the distance from any point on the curve to the focus and the distance from the same point to the directrix.
- The focus of a conic section is a fixed point on the axis of the cone that determines the shape of the curve.
- The directrix of a conic section is a fixed line perpendicular to the axis of the cone that determines the shape of the curve.
- If the eccentricity is less than 1, the conic section is an ellipse.
- If the eccentricity is equal to 1, the conic section is a parabola.
- If the eccentricity is greater than 1, the conic section is a hyperbola.

## Development of a cone

- The development of a cone is the process of unfolding the curved surface of the cone into a flat plane.
- The development of a cone is useful for making patterns for cutting and bending sheet metal or paper into conical shapes.
- The development of a cone is obtained by cutting the cone along a slant height and spreading it out on a plane.
- The development of a cone is a sector of a circle with the same radius as the slant height of the cone and the same angle as the semi-vertical angle of the cone.
- The length of the arc of the sector is equal to the circumference of the base of the cone.
- The development of a cone can be drawn by following these steps:

  - Draw a circle with the radius equal to the slant height of the cone.
  - Draw a line from the center of the circle to any point on the circle. This is the radius of the sector.
  - Measure the semi-vertical angle of the cone and mark it on the circle using a protractor.
  - Draw another line from the center of the circle to the marked point. This is the other radius of the sector.
  - Cut out the sector and join the two radii to form the development of the cone.
