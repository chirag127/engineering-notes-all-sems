# Orthographic Projection

Orthographic projection is a method of representing three-dimensional objects in two dimensions. It is commonly used in engineering graphics to communicate the shape and dimensions of an object to the viewers. Orthographic projection is also known as orthogonal projection.

Some points to note about orthographic projection are:

- Orthographic projection is a form of parallel projection, in which the projection lines are perpendicular to the projection plane. This means that the angles and lengths are preserved in the projection, but the depth is distorted.
- Orthographic projection usually consists of three views of the object: the front view, the top view, and the right view. These views are aligned with the principal axes of the object, and show the object as if it is viewed from the front, the top, and the right side, respectively.
- The three views are arranged in a standard layout, called the first-angle projection or the third-angle projection, depending on the convention used. In the first-angle projection, the object is placed in the first quadrant, and the views are projected onto the planes that form the other three quadrants. In the third-angle projection, the object is placed in the third quadrant, and the views are projected onto the planes that form the first quadrant. - The views are drawn with hidden lines, center lines, dimension lines, and other symbols to indicate the features and measurements of the object. The views are also labeled with letters and numbers to identify them. The figure below shows an example of an orthographic projection of a hexagonal prism.

![Orthographic projection of a hexagonal prism](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/7a/Hexagonal_prism_orthographic_projection.svg/1200px-Hexagonal_prism_orthographic_projection.svg.png)

- Orthographic projection is useful for showing the shape and size of an object, but it does not convey the depth or perspective of the object. To overcome this limitation, other methods of projection, such as isometric projection, oblique projection, or perspective projection, can be used to show the object in three dimensions.
