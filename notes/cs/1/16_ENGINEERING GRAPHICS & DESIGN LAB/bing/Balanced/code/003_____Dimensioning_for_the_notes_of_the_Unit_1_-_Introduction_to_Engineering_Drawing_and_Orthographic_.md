### Dimensioning for the notes of the Unit 1 - Introduction to Engineering Drawing and Orthographic Projections in the subject of ENGINEERING GRAPHICS & DESIGN LAB

- Dimensioning is the process of defining the size, form and location of geometric features and components on an engineering drawing.
- Dimensioning is essential for the fabrication and assembly of parts, as it provides the necessary information for the accuracy and quality of the product.
- There are two main types of dimensions in engineering drawings: linear and angular.
  - Linear dimensions measure the length, width, height, or diameter of a part or a feature, while angular dimensions measure the angle between two lines, planes, or surfaces.
  - Linear dimensions are indicated by extension lines, dimension lines, arrowheads, and numerical values, while angular dimensions are indicated by arc symbols, dimension lines, arrowheads, and numerical values.
- There are different methods of dimensioning in engineering drawings, such as aligned, unidirectional, chain, parallel, baseline, and ordinate.
  - Aligned dimensioning places the numerical values parallel to the dimension lines, while unidirectional dimensioning places them horizontally.
  - Chain dimensioning places the dimensions one after another along a single direction, while parallel dimensioning places them parallel to each other along different directions.
  - Baseline dimensioning places the dimensions from a common reference line or point, while ordinate dimensioning places them from a common origin and axes.
- There are different principles of dimensioning in engineering drawings, such as clarity, completeness, consistency, and economy.
  - Clarity means that the dimensions should be legible, unambiguous, and free from errors or contradictions.
  - Completeness means that the dimensions should provide all the information necessary for the fabrication and assembly of the part, without any assumptions or calculations.
  - Consistency means that the dimensions should follow the same standards, conventions, and rules throughout the drawing, such as units, symbols, and formats.
  - Economy means that the dimensions should be as few as possible, but as many as necessary, to avoid redundancy, confusion, or clutter.
- There are different standards and codes for dimensioning in engineering drawings, such as ASME Y14 series, ISO 129 series, and BS 8888 series .
  - These standards and codes specify the rules and guidelines for the dimensioning of different types of engineering drawings, such as mechanical, electrical, civil, and architectural.
  - These standards and codes also define the terms and symbols for the geometrical dimensioning and tolerancing (GD&T) of engineering drawings, which is a system of specifying the allowable variations in the shape, size, orientation, and location of the features and components on an engineering drawing.
