### Diagonal

- A diagonal is a straight line that connects two opposite corners of a polygon or a plane figure.
- In engineering drawing, a diagonal is often used to measure small fractions of the unit of measurement with higher accuracy .
- A diagonal scale is a type of graphical scale that represents a unit into three different multiples, such as meters, centimeters, and millimeters .
- A diagonal scale can be drawn by following these steps:
  - Finding the length of the scale based on the given data and the required accuracy.
  - Drawing a horizontal line equal to the length of the scale and dividing it into equal parts according to the main unit and the first sub-unit.
  - Drawing a vertical line at the right end of the horizontal line and dividing it into equal parts according to the second sub-unit.
  - Joining the points from the subdivisions on the horizontal line to the successive next subdivisions on the vertical line by diagonal lines.
  - Marking the values of the units and sub-units on the scale and labeling it.
- A diagonal scale can be used to measure the length of a line or an object by placing it parallel to the scale and reading the values at the points of intersection with the diagonal lines .
- A diagonal line can also be used to indicate the area that has been theoretically cut in a section view of an object .
- A section view is a special type of multi-view drawing that shows the interior features of an object by cutting it along a plane.
- A section line or a cross-hatching is a thin line drawn at a 45-degree angle to the major outline of the object to show the cut area.
- A section line can be used to mark the centers of circles and the axes of symmetrical parts, such as cylinders and bolts.
- A break line is another type of diagonal line that is used to show where an object is broken to save drawing space or reveal interior features.
- A break line can be either a freehand thick line or a long, ruled thin line with zigzags.
