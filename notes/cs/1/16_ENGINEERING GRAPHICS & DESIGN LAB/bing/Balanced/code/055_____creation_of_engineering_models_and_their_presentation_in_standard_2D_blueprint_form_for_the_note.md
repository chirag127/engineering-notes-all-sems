### Creation of engineering models and their presentation in standard 2D blueprint form

- Engineering models are representations of physical objects or systems that capture their geometry, topology, and properties.
- Engineering models can be created using various methods, such as sketching, drafting, solid modeling, surface modeling, or mesh modeling.
- Engineering models can be presented in different forms, such as 2D blueprints, 3D wire-frames, 3D shaded solids, or 3D rendered images.
- 2D blueprints are the most common and traditional form of presenting engineering models. They consist of orthographic projections, dimensions, tolerances, annotations, and symbols that describe the shape, size, and specifications of the model.
- 2D blueprints follow certain standards and conventions, such as ISO and ANSI, to ensure clarity, accuracy, and consistency in communication and interpretation.
- 2D blueprints can be generated manually or automatically from 3D models using computer-aided design (CAD) software. CAD software allows creating associative models that link the 2D blueprints to the 3D models, so that any changes in one are reflected in the other.
- 2D blueprints are used for various purposes, such as engineering analysis, manufacturing, inspection, assembly, and documentation.
