### Scales

- A scale is a ratio that compares the dimensions of a drawing to the actual dimensions of the object.
- Scales are used to reduce or enlarge the size of an object while maintaining its proportions and accuracy.
- Scales are expressed as fractions or ratios, such as 1:10, 1/10, or 10%.
- There are different types of scales, such as:

  - Representative fraction (RF): This is the ratio of the length of a line on the drawing to the corresponding length of the line on the object. For example, if a line on the drawing is 2 cm long and the corresponding line on the object is 20 cm long, then the RF is 2/20 or 1/10 or 0.1.
  - Plain scale: This is a scale that shows the measurements of one unit, such as meters, centimeters, or millimeters, on a straight line. For example, a plain scale of 1:10 can show the measurements of 10 cm, 20 cm, 30 cm, etc. on a line that is 10 cm long.
  - Diagonal scale: This is a scale that shows the measurements of two units, such as meters and centimeters, or feet and inches, on a straight line. For example, a diagonal scale of 1:10 can show the measurements of 10 cm, 11 cm, 12 cm, etc. on a line that is 10 cm long, by using diagonal lines to divide the line into 10 equal parts.
  - Vernier scale: This is a scale that shows the measurements of three units, such as meters, centimeters, and millimeters, on a straight line. For example, a vernier scale of 1:10 can show the measurements of 10 cm, 10.1 cm, 10.2 cm, etc. on a line that is 10 cm long, by using a sliding scale that has 10 divisions that correspond to 9 divisions on the main scale.
  - Comparative scale: This is a scale that shows the comparison of two different units, such as kilometers and miles, or meters and feet, on a straight line. For example, a comparative scale of 1 km = 0.6 miles can show the measurements of 1 km, 2 km, 3 km, etc. and 0.6 miles, 1.2 miles, 1.8 miles, etc. on a line that is 10 cm long, by using two parallel lines with different divisions.

- Scales are important for engineering drawing and orthographic projections because they allow the representation of objects with different sizes and shapes on a standard paper size, and they enable the calculation of dimensions, distances, areas, and volumes of the objects.
