## Unit 5 - Introduction to Computer Aided Design

- Computer aided design (CAD) is the use of computers to aid in the creation, modification, analysis, or optimization of a design.
- CAD software is used to increase the productivity of the designer, improve the quality of design, improve communications through documentation, and to create a database for manufacturing.
- CAD involves using computers to help with engineering and design for a wide range of projects in various industries, such as metal fabrication, carpentry, and 3D printing.
- CAD allows designers and engineers to visualize a product’s construction, before fabricating it, and to share, review, simulate, and modify designs easily .
- CAD can create 2D drawings or 3D models of real-world products, depending on the needs and preferences of the user.
- CAD can be classified into different types, such as 2D CAD, 3D CAD, 3D wireframe and surface modeling, solid modeling, parametric modeling, and direct modeling.
- CAD can be integrated with other computer-aided technologies, such as computer-aided engineering (CAE), computer-aided manufacturing (CAM), computer-aided process planning (CAPP), and computer-aided quality assurance (CAQ).
- CAD can be used for various purposes, such as conceptual design, product design, engineering analysis, simulation, testing, prototyping, documentation, and presentation.
- CAD can benefit the users in various ways, such as reducing errors, saving time and cost, enhancing creativity, improving accuracy, facilitating collaboration, and increasing customer satisfaction .
