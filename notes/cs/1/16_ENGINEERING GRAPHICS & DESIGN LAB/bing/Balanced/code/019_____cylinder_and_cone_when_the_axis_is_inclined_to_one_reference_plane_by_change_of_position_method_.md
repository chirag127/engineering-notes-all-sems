### Cylinder and cone when the axis is inclined to one reference plane by change of position method

- A cylinder or a cone is said to have its axis inclined to one reference plane (either HP or VP) when it is not perpendicular to that plane.
- To draw the projections of such a solid, the change of position method can be used. This method involves rotating the solid about an axis parallel to the reference plane until the axis becomes perpendicular to that plane. Then, the projections of the solid in the new position are drawn and the true shape of the base is obtained. Finally, the solid is rotated back to its original position and the projections are traced accordingly.
- The steps for drawing the projections of a cylinder or a cone when its axis is inclined to HP and parallel to VP are as follows:

  1. Draw the front view of the solid in the original position, showing the axis and the base circle.
  2. Draw the top view of the solid in the original position, showing the axis and the base ellipse.
  3. Rotate the solid about an axis parallel to VP until the axis becomes perpendicular to HP. Mark the new position of the axis and the base circle in the top view. The front view remains unchanged.
  4. Draw the true shape of the base circle by projecting it on a plane perpendicular to the axis. This can be done by dividing the base circle into equal parts and projecting them on the plane.
  5. Rotate the solid back to its original position and trace the projections of the solid in the original position. The base ellipse in the top view can be drawn by using the points obtained from the true shape of the base circle.
  6. Mark the dimensions and the inclination angle of the axis.

- The steps for drawing the projections of a cylinder or a cone when its axis is inclined to VP and parallel to HP are similar, except that the solid is rotated about an axis parallel to HP instead of VP.
