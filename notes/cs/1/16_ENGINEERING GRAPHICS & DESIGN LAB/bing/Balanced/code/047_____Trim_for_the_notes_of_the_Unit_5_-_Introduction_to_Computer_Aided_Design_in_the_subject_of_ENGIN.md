### Trim for the notes of the Unit 5 - Introduction to Computer Aided Design in the subject of ENGINEERING GRAPHICS & DESIGN LAB

- Trim is a command or a function in computer aided design (CAD) software that allows the user to cut or remove unwanted parts of an object or a surface.
- Trim can be used to create complex shapes, modify existing designs, or optimize the geometry of a model for analysis or fabrication.
- Trim can be applied to different types of objects or surfaces, such as curves, solids, meshes, or NURBS (non-uniform rational B-splines).
- Trim can be performed by using different methods or tools, such as:
  - Selecting the objects or surfaces to be trimmed and the cutting elements, such as another object, a plane, or a sketch.
  - Specifying the boundary or the region to be trimmed, such as inside, outside, or both.
  - Choosing the trim option, such as extend, keep, or delete the trimmed parts.
- Trim can have different effects or outcomes, depending on the type and the option of the trim, such as:
  - Creating gaps or holes in the object or the surface.
  - Joining or merging the trimmed parts into a single object or surface.
  - Changing the topology or the shape of the object or the surface.
  - Reducing the complexity or the size of the object or the surface.
- Trim can be useful for various purposes or applications, such as:
  - Creating precise and accurate designs that meet the specifications and requirements of the project.
  - Improving the aesthetics and the functionality of the designs by removing unnecessary or unwanted parts.
  - Simplifying the analysis and the fabrication of the designs by reducing the number of elements or features.
  - Modifying or editing the designs by adding or subtracting parts or surfaces.
