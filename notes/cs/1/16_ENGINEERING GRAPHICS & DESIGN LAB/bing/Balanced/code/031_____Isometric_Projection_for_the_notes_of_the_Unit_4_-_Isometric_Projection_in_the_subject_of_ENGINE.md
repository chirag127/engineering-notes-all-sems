# Isometric Projection

- Isometric projection is a method for visually representing three-dimensional objects in two dimensions in technical and engineering drawings.
- It is an axonometric projection in which the three coordinate axes appear equally foreshortened and the angle between any two of them is 120 degrees .
- Isometric projection is intended to combine the illusion of depth with the clarity and accuracy of orthogonal views.
- Isometric projection can be used to draw simple or complex shapes, such as cubes, prisms, pyramids, cylinders, spheres, etc.
- Isometric projection has some advantages and disadvantages over other types of projections, such as:

  - Advantages:
    - It preserves the true proportions and angles of the object.
    - It is easy to measure and dimension the object.
    - It is suitable for showing the general shape and appearance of the object.
    - It does not require special drawing instruments or skills.
  - Disadvantages:
    - It does not show the true lengths of the object's edges.
    - It may cause distortion or confusion in some cases, such as when the object has curved or inclined surfaces.
    - It may not reveal the hidden details or features of the object.
