# Projection of Straight Lines

- A straight line is the shortest distance between two points  .
- The projection of a straight line is obtained by joining the projections of its end points .
- The actual length of the straight line is known as the true length (TL).
- The projections of a straight line on the horizontal and vertical planes are called the top view and the front view respectively .
- The top view and the front view of a straight line may have different lengths depending on the orientation of the line with respect to the planes of projection .
- The angle between the line and the horizontal plane is called the inclination with HP (α) and the angle between the line and the vertical plane is called the inclination with VP (β) .
- The projections of a straight line can be classified into three types based on the inclinations with HP and VP :
  - Parallel to both planes: α = 0° and β = 0°
  - Parallel to one plane and perpendicular to the other: α = 0° or 90° and β = 0° or 90°
  - Inclined to both planes: α ≠ 0° and β ≠ 0°
- The projections of a straight line can be drawn using the following steps  :
  - Draw the reference line XY and mark the distance between the end points of the line on it.
  - Draw the projections of the end points on the horizontal and vertical planes using the given inclinations with HP and VP.
  - Join the projections of the end points to get the top view and the front view of the line.
  - Measure the lengths of the top view and the front view and compare them with the true length of the line.
  - Draw the auxiliary views of the line if required to show the true length and the true inclinations.
- The projections of a straight line can be drawn using the first angle or the third angle of projection as per the ISO convention . In the first angle of projection, the object is placed in the first quadrant and the observer looks from the right side. In the third angle of projection, the object is placed in the third quadrant and the observer looks from the left side.
- The projections of a straight line can be used to represent various objects such as edges, axes, diagonals, etc. in engineering drawing . They can also be used to find the true length, true inclinations, and true shape of the objects .
