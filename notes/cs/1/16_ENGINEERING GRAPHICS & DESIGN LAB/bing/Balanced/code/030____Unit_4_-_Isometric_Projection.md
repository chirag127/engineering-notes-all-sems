## Unit 4 - Isometric Projection

- Isometric projection is a method of representing three-dimensional objects in two dimensions.
- It is a type of axonometric projection, which means that the three principal axes are equally inclined to the plane of projection, and the scale along each axis is the same.
- Isometric projection preserves the angles and proportions of the object, but distorts the shape and size.
- To draw an isometric projection, the following steps are followed:
  - Choose a suitable scale and orientation for the object.
  - Draw three axes, called isometric axes, at 120 degrees to each other.
  - Project the vertices of the object along the isometric axes, using the same scale for each axis.
  - Connect the projected vertices to form the edges and faces of the object.
  - Add details, such as hidden lines, dimensions, and labels, as needed.
- Isometric projection is useful for engineering and technical drawings, as it shows the overall shape and structure of the object without perspective distortion.
- It is also used for artistic and gaming purposes, as it creates a sense of depth and realism.
