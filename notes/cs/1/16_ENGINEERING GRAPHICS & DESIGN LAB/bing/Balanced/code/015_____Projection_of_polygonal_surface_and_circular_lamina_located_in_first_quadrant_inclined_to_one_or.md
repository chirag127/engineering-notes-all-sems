### Projection of polygonal surface and circular lamina located in first quadrant inclined to one or both reference planes

- A polygonal surface is a plane figure bounded by straight lines, such as a triangle, square, pentagon, etc.
- A circular lamina is a plane figure bounded by a circle, such as a disc, coin, etc.
- Projection of a plane surface is the outline of the surface as seen from a given direction.
- To project a plane surface, we need to know its position and orientation with respect to the reference planes (HP and VP).
- The position of a plane surface can be described by the distances of its corners or center from the reference planes.
- The orientation of a plane surface can be described by the angles it makes with the reference planes.
- A plane surface is said to be inclined to a reference plane if it makes an angle other than 0° or 90° with it.
- A plane surface is said to be perpendicular to a reference plane if it makes an angle of 90° with it.
- A plane surface is said to be parallel to a reference plane if it makes an angle of 0° with it.
- A plane surface located in the first quadrant is one that is above HP and in front of VP.
- To project a plane surface located in the first quadrant inclined to one or both reference planes, we can follow these steps:
  - Draw the top view and the front view of the plane surface in its true shape and size, as if it is parallel to both HP and VP.
  - Mark the distances of the corners or center of the plane surface from the reference planes.
  - Rotate the plane surface about one of its edges or axes until it is inclined to the reference plane(s) as given.
  - Draw the new top view and the front view of the plane surface after the rotation, showing the true inclination and the apparent shape and size.
  - Mark the traces of the plane surface on the reference plane(s) if any.
  - Label the points and dimensions clearly.
