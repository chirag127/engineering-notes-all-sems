### Right regular solids and Auxiliary views for the true shape of the sections such as Prism

- A right regular solid is a three-dimensional figure that has congruent and regular polygonal faces, with all the angles and sides equal. Examples of right regular solids are cubes, tetrahedrons, octahedrons, dodecahedrons, and icosahedrons.
- A prism is a right regular solid that has two parallel and congruent polygonal bases, and rectangular lateral faces. The bases can be any regular polygon, such as triangles, squares, pentagons, hexagons, etc. The height of the prism is the perpendicular distance between the bases.
- An auxiliary view is a view that is projected from an existing view, usually a principal view, to show the true shape and size of a feature that is not parallel to any of the principal planes. Auxiliary views are useful for showing the true shape of sections, inclined surfaces, holes, slots, etc.
- To draw an auxiliary view for the true shape of the section of a prism, follow these steps  :
  - Draw the front, top, and right side views of the prism, showing the cutting plane that defines the section.
  - Draw a reference line perpendicular to the cutting plane from the front view, and project it to the top and right side views.
  - Draw an auxiliary plane perpendicular to the reference line, and label it as the auxiliary view.
  - Project the vertices of the section from the front view to the auxiliary plane, and connect them with straight lines to form the true shape of the section.
  - Add dimensions, labels, and notes as needed.
