## Unit 5 - Introduction to Computer Aided Design

- Computer Aided Design (CAD) is the use of computer software to create, modify, analyze, and optimize the design of a product or a system.
- CAD software can help designers to improve the quality, efficiency, and productivity of their work, as well as to reduce errors, costs, and time.
- CAD software can also enable designers to perform simulations, testing, and evaluation of their designs before manufacturing or implementation.
- CAD software can support various types of design, such as mechanical, electrical, architectural, civil, and industrial design.
- CAD software can also be integrated with other computer applications, such as Computer Aided Manufacturing (CAM), Computer Aided Engineering (CAE), and Product Lifecycle Management (PLM).
- CAD software can be classified into two main categories: 2D CAD and 3D CAD.
  - 2D CAD software allows designers to create and manipulate two-dimensional drawings, such as sketches, blueprints, and schematics.
  - 3D CAD software allows designers to create and manipulate three-dimensional models, such as solids, surfaces, and meshes.
  - 3D CAD software can also provide features such as rendering, animation, and virtual reality, to enhance the visualization and presentation of the design.
- Some examples of popular CAD software are AutoCAD, SolidWorks, CATIA, SketchUp, and Revit.
- CAD software can be operated on various platforms, such as desktop computers, laptops, tablets, and smartphones.
- CAD software can also be accessed through web browsers, cloud services, or online platforms, such as Onshape, Tinkercad, and Fusion 360.
