### Creation of engineering models and their presentation in standard 2D blueprint form

- Engineering models are representations of physical objects or systems that capture their geometry, topology, and properties.
- Engineering models can be created using various methods, such as sketching, drafting, solid modeling, surface modeling, and mesh modeling.
- Engineering models can be presented in different forms, such as 2D blueprints, 3D wire-frames, 3D shaded solids, and 3D rendered images.
- 2D blueprints are drawings that show the dimensions, tolerances, and annotations of an engineering model using standard symbols and conventions.
- 2D blueprints are useful for communicating the design specifications, manufacturing instructions, and quality control requirements of an engineering model.
- 2D blueprints can be generated automatically from 3D models using modeling software that supports associative modeling, interference checking, and bill of materials.
- 2D blueprints must follow the ISO and ANSI standards for coordinate dimensioning and tolerancing, and geometric dimensioning and tolerancing, to ensure clarity, accuracy, and consistency.
- Coordinate dimensioning and tolerancing specifies the location, size, and shape of features using linear and angular measurements, and defines the allowable variations using limits or modifiers.
- Geometric dimensioning and tolerancing specifies the form, orientation, position, and runout of features using geometric symbols, datums, and feature control frames, and defines the allowable variations using tolerance zones.
