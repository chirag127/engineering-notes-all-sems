### Perspective Projection

- Perspective projection is a type of projection that shows a three-dimensional object as it appears to the human eye from a certain viewpoint.
- Perspective projection is used to create realistic and artistic drawings of objects and scenes, but it is not suitable for engineering drawings that require accurate dimensions and shapes.
- Perspective projection is based on the principle that parallel lines converge at a single point called the **vanishing point** or the **center of projection** as they recede into the distance.
- Perspective projection can be classified into three types: **one-point perspective**, **two-point perspective**, and **three-point perspective**, depending on the number of vanishing points used.
- One-point perspective is used when the object is facing the viewer directly, and only one set of parallel lines (usually the depth) converges at the vanishing point on the horizon line.
- Two-point perspective is used when the object is rotated at an angle to the viewer, and two sets of parallel lines (usually the width and the depth) converge at two vanishing points on the horizon line.
- Three-point perspective is used when the object is tilted up or down, and three sets of parallel lines (the width, the depth, and the height) converge at three vanishing points, two on the horizon line and one above or below it.
- To draw a perspective projection of an object, the following steps are followed:
  - Draw a horizontal line to represent the **horizon line**, which is the eye level of the viewer.
  - Locate the **station point**, which is the position of the viewer's eye, and draw a line perpendicular to the horizon line to represent the **picture plane**, which is the imaginary plane where the object is projected.
  - Determine the number and position of the **vanishing points** on the horizon line according to the type of perspective projection.
  - Draw the **projectors**, which are the lines that connect the corners and edges of the object to the vanishing points or the station point.
  - Mark the intersections of the projectors and the picture plane, and connect them to form the **perspective view** of the object.
