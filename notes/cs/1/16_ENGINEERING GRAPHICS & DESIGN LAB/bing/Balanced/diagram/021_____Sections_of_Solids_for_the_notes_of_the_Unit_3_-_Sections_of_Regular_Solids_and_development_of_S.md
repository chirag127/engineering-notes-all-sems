### Sections of Solids

- A section of a solid is the shape obtained by cutting the solid with a plane, called the section plane or cutting plane.
- The purpose of drawing sections of solids is to show the hidden or internal features of the object more clearly and accurately.
- The section plane can be perpendicular or inclined to one or both of the reference planes (HP and VP).
- The true shape of the section is the shape of the cut surface as seen from a direction perpendicular to the section plane.
- The true shape of the section can be obtained by projecting the sectional points on the section plane and joining them in order.
- The common types of solids that are studied in engineering graphics are prisms, pyramids, cylinders and cones.
- The sections of these solids can be classified into three categories: right, oblique and parallel sections.
- A right section is obtained when the section plane is perpendicular to the axis of the solid.
- An oblique section is obtained when the section plane is inclined to the axis of the solid and cuts the solid obliquely.
- A parallel section is obtained when the section plane is parallel to the base of the solid.
- The method of drawing sections of solids involves the following steps:
  - Draw the top view and the front view of the solid in the given position.
  - Draw the section plane in the front view and mark the sectional points where it cuts the edges of the solid.
  - Draw projectors from the sectional points to the top view and mark the corresponding points.
  - Draw the sectional view by joining the sectional points in the top view.
  - Draw the true shape of the section by projecting the sectional points on the section plane and joining them in order.
  - Shade the sectional view and the true shape of the section with parallel lines to indicate the cut surface.
