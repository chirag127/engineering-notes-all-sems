- Engineering drawing is a graphical language used by engineers and other technical personnel to communicate design ideas, specifications, and dimensions of physical objects and systems.
- Engineering drawing can be classified into two types: artistic drawing and technical drawing. Artistic drawing is used to express aesthetic or creative ideas, while technical drawing is used to convey accurate and precise information about the shape, size, and features of an object or system.
- Engineering drawing follows a set of standards and conventions to ensure clarity, consistency, and readability. Some of the common standards are: International Organization for Standardization (ISO), American National Standards Institute (ANSI), British Standards Institution (BSI), and Indian Standards Institution (ISI).
- Engineering drawing can be done by hand or by using computer-aided design (CAD) software. CAD software allows the creation, modification, analysis, and visualization of engineering drawings in a digital format.
- Orthographic projection is a method of representing a three-dimensional object or system in two dimensions by projecting its views on parallel planes. Orthographic projection is also known as orthogonal projection or multiview projection.
- Orthographic projection consists of three principal views: front view, top view, and side view. These views show the object or system as seen from the front, top, and side directions, respectively. The views are aligned with each other along the projection lines, which are perpendicular to the planes of projection.
- Orthographic projection follows a set of rules and conventions to ensure accuracy and completeness. Some of the common rules and conventions are: first-angle projection and third-angle projection, hidden lines and center lines, dimensioning and tolerancing, and scaling and hatching.
