### Transformation of Projections

- A projection is a method of representing a three-dimensional (3D) object on a two-dimensional (2D) plane, such as a sheet of paper or a computer screen.
- A projection is produced by connecting the points where the lines of sight from an observer to the object pierce the projection plane.
- A projection can be classified into two types: parallel and perspective, depending on the distance and orientation of the observer to the object.
- A parallel projection is a projection where the lines of sight are assumed to be parallel, and the projection plane is perpendicular to a principal axis of the object. This type of projection is commonly used in engineering and technical drawings, as it preserves the true shape and size of the object.
- A perspective projection is a projection where the lines of sight converge at a single point, called the center of projection or the eye point. This type of projection is more realistic and mimics how humans perceive the world, as it shows the effects of distance and angle on the object's appearance.
- A transformation of projections is a process of changing the type, orientation, or position of a projection, by applying a mathematical operation on the coordinates of the object or the projection plane.
- A transformation of projections can be classified into three types: affine, projective, and perspective, depending on the degree of distortion or change in the object's shape and size.
- An affine transformation is a transformation that preserves the parallelism and ratio of distances between points, but not the angles or lengths. Examples of affine transformations are translation, rotation, scaling, and shearing.
- A projective transformation is a transformation that preserves the straightness of lines and the incidence of points on lines, but not the parallelism or ratio of distances. Examples of projective transformations are perspective projection and reflection.
- A perspective transformation is a transformation that preserves the incidence of points on lines and the cross-ratio of four collinear points, but not the straightness of lines or the ratio of distances. Examples of perspective transformations are oblique projection and fisheye projection.
