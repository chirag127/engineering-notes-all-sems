- Orthographic projection is a form of parallel projection in which all the projection lines are orthogonal to the projection plane, resulting in every plane of the scene appearing in affine transformation on the viewing surface.
- Perspective projection is a form of projection in which the projection lines converge at a single point of perspective, resulting in a more realistic representation of depth and distance.
- Plane figures and simple solids are geometric shapes that can be represented by orthographic or perspective projections. Plane figures are two-dimensional shapes such as circles, squares, triangles, etc. Simple solids are three-dimensional shapes such as cubes, spheres, cones, cylinders, etc.
- Visual ray method is a technique for constructing orthographic projections of plane figures and simple solids. It involves drawing visual rays from the point of perspective to the vertices or edges of the object, and then projecting them orthogonally onto the projection plane.
