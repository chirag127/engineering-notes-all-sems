- Orthographic views are 2-D views of an object that show its shape and dimensions. There are two types of orthographic views: multiview and axonometric.
- Multiview orthographic projection is a method of drawing an object using several views that show different sides of the object. Each view is drawn on a plane that is parallel to the corresponding face of the object. The views are aligned with each other using projection lines that are perpendicular to the planes.
- Axonometric orthographic projection is a method of drawing an object using a single view that shows three sides of the object. The view is drawn on a plane that is tilted at an angle to the object. The projection lines are parallel to each other and to the axes of the object.
- Isometric projection is a type of axonometric orthographic projection that shows the object with equal angles (120 degrees) between the axes. The object appears equally foreshortened along each axis.

To convert a pictorial view into an orthographic projection, you need to follow these steps:

- Identify the type of pictorial view (perspective, isometric or oblique) and the orientation of the object (front, top, right, left, etc.).
- Draw a projection plane that is parallel to the face of the object that you want to show as the principal view (usually the front view).
- Draw projection lines from the corners and edges of the object that are perpendicular to the projection plane.
- Mark the intersections of the projection lines with the projection plane as the vertices of the principal view.
- Connect the vertices with straight lines to form the outline of the principal view.
- Repeat the steps 2 to 5 for the other views (usually the top and right views) that are perpendicular to the principal view. Align the views with each other using the projection lines.
- Add dimensions, labels and symbols as needed to the orthographic projection.
