### Fillet

- A fillet is a curved surface that connects two adjacent faces or edges of a solid object, such as a mechanical part or a building component.
- A fillet has a constant radius that is specified by the designer or the engineer.
- Fillets are used to smooth sharp corners, reduce stress concentrations, improve aesthetics, and facilitate manufacturing processes such as casting, molding, and machining.
- Fillets can be classified into two types: internal and external.
  - Internal fillets are concave curves that connect two interior faces or edges of a solid object. They are also called rounds.
  - External fillets are convex curves that connect two exterior faces or edges of a solid object. They are also called chamfers.
- Fillets can be quickly designed onto parts using 3D solid modeling engineering CAD software by invoking the function and picking edges of interest.
- Fillets are commonly shown in technical drawings using circular arcs and dimensions. The dimension of a fillet is the radius of the arc.
- Fillets can be dimensioned individually or collectively, depending on the symmetry and uniformity of the part.
  - If the fillets are of the same size and symmetrical, they can be dimensioned once with a multiplier indicating the number of fillets. For example, 4XR10 means four symmetrical fillets with a radius of 10 units.
  - If the fillets are of different sizes or asymmetrical, they need to be dimensioned separately with individual arrows and labels. For example, R5 and R8 mean two fillets with radii of 5 and 8 units, respectively.
- Fillets can also be indicated on a side of the technical drawing page with a general note such as “FILLETS R4 UNLESS OTHERWISE INDICATED”. This means that all the fillets on the part have a radius of 4 units, except for those that are explicitly dimensioned differently.
- Fillets can be shown in different views of the technical drawing, such as front, top, side, isometric, or sectional views. The choice of view depends on the clarity and visibility of the fillet geometry and dimension.
