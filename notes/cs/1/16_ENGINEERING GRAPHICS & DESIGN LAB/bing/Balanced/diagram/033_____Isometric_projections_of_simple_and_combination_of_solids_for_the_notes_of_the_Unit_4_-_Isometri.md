### Isometric projections of simple and combination of solids

- Isometric projection is a method of graphic representation of three-dimensional objects, used by engineers, technical illustrators, and architects.
- Isometric projection is based on the principle that the three principal axes of an object are equally inclined to the plane of projection, and the lengths of these axes are drawn in the same scale.
- Isometric projection can be used to show the shape and size of simple geometrical solids, such as prisms, pyramids, cylinders, and cones, as well as combinations of any two or more of these solids .
- Isometric projection can also be used to show the truncation of a solid when it is cut by a plane inclined to the horizontal plane (HP).
- To draw an isometric projection of a solid, the following steps are followed:
  - Draw an isometric axis, which is a vertical line representing the height of the solid.
  - Draw two lines at 30 degrees to the isometric axis, representing the front and side edges of the solid.
  - Draw the top and bottom faces of the solid by connecting the ends of the front and side edges with parallel lines.
  - Draw the other faces of the solid by connecting the corresponding vertices with parallel lines.
  - If the solid is truncated, draw the cutting plane as a polygon on the top face of the solid, and erase the part of the solid above the cutting plane.
  - If the solid is a combination of two or more solids, draw each solid separately and then join them along the common edges or surfaces.
- To draw an isometric projection of a combination of solids, the following steps are followed:
  - Draw an isometric projection of the first solid, following the steps above.
  - Draw an isometric projection of the second solid, following the steps above, but with a different orientation or position relative to the first solid.
  - Join the two solids along the common edges or surfaces, and erase the hidden lines or faces.
  - Repeat the same process for any additional solids in the combination.
