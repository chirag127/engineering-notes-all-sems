### Projection of straight lines

- A straight line is the basic conceptual figure using which any object like a machine component or a structural element is represented.
- Projection of a straight line is the figure obtained by joining, in correct sequence, the points at which the lines drawn from various points on the contour of the line meet the plane.
- Projection of a straight line depends on the orientation and position of the line with respect to the plane of projection.
- There are two methods of projection commonly used in engineering drawing: first angle projection and third angle projection.
- In first angle projection, the object is placed in the first quadrant, between the observer and the plane of projection. The top view is projected below the front view and the right side view is projected to the left of the front view.
- In third angle projection, the object is placed in the third quadrant, behind the plane of projection. The top view is projected above the front view and the right side view is projected to the right of the front view.
- As per ISO convention, the first angle of projection is used.
- The projection of a straight line can be classified into four types based on its orientation and position with respect to the plane of projection:
  - Parallel to both the planes
  - Perpendicular to one plane and inclined to the other
  - Inclined to both the planes
  - Parallel to one plane and perpendicular to the other
- The projection of a straight line can be determined by projecting the two end points of the line and joining them in the required view.
- The projection of a straight line can be verified by using the following properties:
  - The true length of the line is equal to the length of its projection on a plane parallel to it.
  - The angle between the line and a plane is equal to the angle between its projection and the same plane.
  - The projection of the line on a plane perpendicular to it is a point.
