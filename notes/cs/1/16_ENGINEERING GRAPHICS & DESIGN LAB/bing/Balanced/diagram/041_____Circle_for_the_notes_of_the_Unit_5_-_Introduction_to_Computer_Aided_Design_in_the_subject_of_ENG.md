### Circle

- A circle is a closed curve that consists of all points that are equidistant from a fixed point called the center.
- The distance from the center to any point on the circle is called the radius. The diameter is the longest chord of the circle that passes through the center. The circumference is the length of the boundary of the circle.
- A circle can be defined by three non-collinear points on the circle, or by two points and the radius, or by the center and the radius.
- A circle can be drawn using a compass or a computer-aided design (CAD) software. In CAD, a circle can be created by specifying the center point and the radius or diameter, or by selecting three points on the circle.
- A circle can be divided into equal parts called sectors by drawing radii from the center to the points on the circumference. The angle formed by two radii is called the central angle. The area of a sector is proportional to the central angle.
- A circle can also be divided into equal parts called segments by drawing chords from one point on the circumference to another. The area of a segment is the difference between the area of the sector and the area of the triangle formed by the chord and the radii.
- A circle can be tangent to a line or another circle when they touch at one point. A circle can be secant to a line or another circle when they intersect at two points. A circle can be concentric with another circle when they have the same center but different radii.
- A circle can be inscribed in a polygon when all the vertices of the polygon lie on the circle. A circle can be circumscribed about a polygon when all the sides of the polygon are tangent to the circle. A circle can be escribed from a polygon when it is tangent to all the extensions of the sides of the polygon.
