### Cylinder

A cylinder is a three-dimensional solid that has two circular bases and a curved surface that connects them. The bases are parallel and congruent, and the axis of the cylinder is the line segment that joins the centers of the bases. The height of the cylinder is the distance between the bases.

Some important terms related to cylinders are:

- Radius: The distance from the center of the base to the edge of the base.
- Diameter: The distance across the base, passing through the center. It is twice the radius.
- Lateral surface: The curved surface that connects the bases.
- Lateral edge: The line segment that joins the points on the edges of the bases.
- Base area: The area of the circular base. It is equal to πr^2, where r is the radius.
- Lateral area: The area of the lateral surface. It is equal to 2πrh, where r is the radius and h is the height.
- Total surface area: The sum of the base area and the lateral area. It is equal to 2πr(r + h).
- Volume: The amount of space occupied by the cylinder. It is equal to πr^2h.

To draw a cylinder in engineering graphics, we can use the following steps:

- Draw a horizontal line to represent the ground line (GL).
- Draw an ellipse above the GL to represent the top base of the cylinder. The major axis of the ellipse should be parallel to the GL, and the minor axis should be perpendicular to the GL.
- Draw two vertical lines from the ends of the major axis of the ellipse to the GL. These are the lateral edges of the cylinder.
- Draw another ellipse below the GL to represent the bottom base of the cylinder. The major and minor axes of this ellipse should be equal and parallel to those of the top ellipse.
- Label the dimensions of the cylinder, such as the radius, the height, and the diameter.

To develop the surface of a cylinder, we can use the following steps:

- Draw a rectangle with length equal to the circumference of the base and width equal to the height of the cylinder. This is the development of the lateral surface.
- Divide the rectangle into equal parts along the length. The number of parts should be equal to the number of divisions on the base ellipse.
- Draw a semicircle with diameter equal to the base diameter. This is the development of the base.
- Divide the semicircle into equal parts along the arc. The number of parts should be equal to the number of divisions on the base ellipse.
- Join the corresponding points on the rectangle and the semicircle with straight lines. These are the development lines.
- Cut out the rectangle and the semicircle along the outer edges and the development lines.
- Fold the rectangle along the development lines and join the ends to form a cylinder.
- Fold the semicircle along the development lines and attach it to the base of the cylinder.
