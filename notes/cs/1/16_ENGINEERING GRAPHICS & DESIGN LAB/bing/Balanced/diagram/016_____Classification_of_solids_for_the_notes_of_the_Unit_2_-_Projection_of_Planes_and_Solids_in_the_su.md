### Classification of Solids

- A solid is an object that has three dimensions, i.e., length, breadth and height.
- In engineering graphics, solids are represented by their projections on two perpendicular planes, called the horizontal plane (HP) and the vertical plane (VP).
- The projections of a solid show its shape, size and position in space.
- Solids can be classified into two types: simple solids and complex solids.

#### Simple Solids

- A simple solid is a solid that can be generated by the revolution of a plane figure about an axis.
- Examples of simple solids are prism, pyramid, cone, cylinder and sphere.
- A simple solid has a fixed cross-section, which is the same as the plane figure that generates it.
- A simple solid has two types of elements: faces and edges.
- A face is a flat surface that bounds the solid. An edge is a line segment where two faces meet.
- A simple solid can be further classified based on the shape of its base and the inclination of its axis.

##### Classification of Simple Solids Based on the Shape of the Base

- A prism is a solid whose base is a polygon and whose lateral faces are parallelograms.
- A pyramid is a solid whose base is a polygon and whose lateral faces are triangles meeting at a common vertex called the apex.
- A cone is a solid whose base is a circle and whose lateral surface is a curved surface meeting at a common vertex called the apex.
- A cylinder is a solid whose base is a circle and whose lateral surface is a curved surface parallel to the base.
- A sphere is a solid whose surface is a curved surface equidistant from a fixed point called the center.

##### Classification of Simple Solids Based on the Inclination of the Axis

- The axis of a simple solid is the line passing through the center of the base and the apex (for pyramid and cone) or the center of the top face (for prism and cylinder).
- The axis of a simple solid can be inclined to one or both of the reference planes (HP and VP).
- A simple solid is said to be in a simple position if its axis is perpendicular to one of the reference planes and parallel to the other.
- A simple solid is said to be in an inclined position if its axis is inclined to one of the reference planes and parallel to the other.
- A simple solid is said to be in an oblique position if its axis is inclined to both of the reference planes.

#### Complex Solids

- A complex solid is a solid that cannot be generated by the revolution of a plane figure about an axis.
- Examples of complex solids are frustum, prismoid, tetrahedron, octahedron, etc.
- A complex solid has a variable cross-section, which is different from the plane figure that generates it.
- A complex solid has three types of elements: faces, edges and vertices.
- A face is a flat or curved surface that bounds the solid. An edge is a line segment or a curve where two faces meet. A vertex is a point where three or more edges meet.
- A complex solid can be further classified based on the shape and number of its faces.

##### Classification of Complex Solids Based on the Shape and Number of the Faces

- A frustum is a solid obtained by cutting a simple solid (prism, pyramid, cone or cylinder) by a plane parallel to the base, removing the smaller part.
- A prismoid is a solid whose bases are two parallel polygons of different shapes and sizes, and whose lateral faces are trapezoids or triangles.
- A tetrahedron is a solid whose faces are four equilateral triangles.
- An octahedron is a solid whose faces are eight equilateral triangles.
- A polyhedron is a solid whose faces are polygons. A polyhedron can have different types of polygons as faces, such as triangles, squares, pentagons, etc.
