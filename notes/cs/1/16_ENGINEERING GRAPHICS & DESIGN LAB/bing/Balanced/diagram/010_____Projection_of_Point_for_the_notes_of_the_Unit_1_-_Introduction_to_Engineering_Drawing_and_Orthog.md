### Projection of Point

- Projection in engineering drawing is a technique for representing a three-dimensional object on a two-dimensional drawing.
- A point is the simplest geometric element that has no dimensions, only a position in space.
- To project a point onto a plane, we need to draw a line of sight from the observer to the point and mark where it intersects the plane. This is called the projection of the point.
- The projection of a point depends on the position of the observer, the position of the point, and the orientation of the plane.
- There are two types of projections: parallel and perspective. Parallel projections assume that the observer is at an infinite distance from the object, and the lines of sight are parallel. Perspective projections assume that the observer is at a finite distance from the object, and the lines of sight converge at a point called the center of projection.
- Parallel projections are further classified into orthographic and oblique projections. Orthographic projections are parallel projections where the plane is perpendicular to the lines of sight. Oblique projections are parallel projections where the plane is inclined to the lines of sight.
- Orthographic projections are commonly used in engineering drawings to show the true shape and size of an object. Oblique projections are used to show the depth and orientation of an object.
- The projection of a point on an orthographic plane is a point. The projection of a point on an oblique plane is a point or a line, depending on the angle of inclination of the plane.
