# Lettering for the notes of the Unit 1 - Introduction to Engineering Drawing and Orthographic Projections in the subject of ENGINEERING GRAPHICS & DESIGN LAB

- Lettering is the process of writing letters, numerals, and symbols on a drawing to convey information.
- Lettering is an essential part of engineering drawing, as it helps to identify the dimensions, specifications, notes, and other details of the object or system being represented.
- Lettering should be clear, legible, uniform, and consistent throughout the drawing.
- Lettering should follow the standard rules and conventions of engineering drawing, such as the size, style, spacing, alignment, and orientation of the letters, numerals, and symbols.
- Lettering should be done with a sharp pencil or a pen of suitable thickness and color, preferably black or blue.
- Lettering should be done on a horizontal or a vertical guide line, depending on the type and location of the information.
- Lettering should be done in a single stroke, without overwriting or erasing.
- Lettering should be done in upper case, unless otherwise specified.
- Lettering should be done in one of the following styles: vertical, inclined, or curved.
- Vertical lettering is the most common and preferred style of lettering, as it is easy to read and write. It is used for general notes, titles, dimensions, and specifications on a drawing. Vertical lettering is done with the letters perpendicular to the horizontal guide line, and the numerals parallel to it.
- Inclined lettering is used for special notes, symbols, and abbreviations on a drawing. Inclined lettering is done with the letters and numerals inclined at an angle of 75 degrees to the horizontal guide line.
- Curved lettering is used for circular or curved notes, such as the names of views, sections, or details on a drawing. Curved lettering is done with the letters and numerals following the arc of the circle or curve, and the center line of the letters perpendicular to the tangent at that point.
- Lettering should be done in one of the following sizes: 2.5 mm, 3.5 mm, 5 mm, or 7 mm, depending on the scale and complexity of the drawing. The size of the lettering should be proportional to the size of the object or system being represented, and should not be too large or too small to affect the clarity and readability of the drawing.
- Lettering should be done with a uniform spacing between the letters, numerals, and symbols, as well as between the words, lines, and paragraphs. The spacing should be about one-third of the height of the lettering, unless otherwise specified.
- Lettering should be done with a proper alignment of the information, such as left, right, center, or justified, depending on the type and location of the information. The alignment should be consistent throughout the drawing, and should not create confusion or ambiguity.
- Lettering should be done with a correct orientation of the information, such as horizontal, vertical, or inclined, depending on the type and location of the information. The orientation should be consistent throughout the drawing, and should not create confusion or ambiguity.
