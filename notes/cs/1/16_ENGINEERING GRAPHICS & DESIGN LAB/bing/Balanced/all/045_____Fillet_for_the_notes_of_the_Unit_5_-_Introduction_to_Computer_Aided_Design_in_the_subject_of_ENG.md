# Fillet

- A fillet is a curved surface that connects two adjacent faces or edges of a solid object, such as a mechanical part or a 3D model.
- A fillet has a radius value that determines the size and shape of the curve.
- Fillets are commonly used in engineering graphics and design to smooth sharp corners, reduce stress concentration, improve appearance, and facilitate manufacturing processes such as casting, molding, and machining.
- Fillets can be dimensioned and shown in technical drawings using various methods, depending on the number, size, and symmetry of the fillets.
- Some common methods of showing fillets in technical drawings are :
  - Using a leader line and an arrow to point to a fillet and indicate its radius value, such as R10 for a 10-unit radius fillet.
  - Using a chain dimension to show the distance between two fillets along a common edge, such as 20 for a 20-unit distance between two fillets.
  - Using a note on the side of the drawing to specify the radius value of all the fillets in the drawing, unless otherwise indicated, such as FILLETS R4 UNLESS OTHERWISE INDICATED.
  - Using a multiplier symbol and a radius value to show the number and size of symmetrical fillets along an edge or a face, such as 4XR10 for four symmetrical fillets with a 10-unit radius.
- Fillets can be quickly designed onto parts using 3D solid modeling engineering CAD software by invoking the function and picking edges of interest.
- Fillets can also be modified, deleted, or suppressed using the CAD software features and commands.
