# Perspective Projection

- Perspective projection is a type of projection that shows a three-dimensional object as it appears to the human eye from a certain viewpoint.
- Perspective projection is used to create realistic and artistic drawings of objects and scenes.
- Perspective projection is based on the principle that the lines of projection from the object to the center of projection (or the station point) are not parallel, but converge at a single point.
- Perspective projection has the following characteristics :
  - The size of the object decreases as the distance from the center of projection increases.
  - The shape of the object changes depending on the angle of view and the position of the center of projection.
  - The parallel lines of the object appear to converge at a vanishing point on the horizon line.
  - The foreshortening effect is more pronounced for the parts of the object that are closer to the center of projection.
- Perspective projection can be classified into three types based on the number of vanishing points :
  - One-point perspective: when the object is parallel to one of the principal planes and perpendicular to the other two. There is only one vanishing point on the horizon line.
  - Two-point perspective: when the object is parallel to one of the principal planes and oblique to the other two. There are two vanishing points on the horizon line.
  - Three-point perspective: when the object is oblique to all three principal planes. There are three vanishing points, two on the horizon line and one above or below it.
- Perspective projection is rarely used for engineering drawings because it does not show the exact size and shape of the object and it is difficult to construct accurately .
- Perspective projection is more suitable for visualizing the appearance and impression of the object and for conveying the depth and distance of the scene .
