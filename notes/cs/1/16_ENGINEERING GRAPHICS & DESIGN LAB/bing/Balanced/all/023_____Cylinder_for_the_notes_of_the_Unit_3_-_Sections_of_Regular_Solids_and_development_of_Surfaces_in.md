# Cylinder

A cylinder is a three-dimensional solid that has two circular bases and a curved surface connecting them. The bases are parallel and congruent, and the axis of the cylinder is the line segment joining the centers of the bases. The height of the cylinder is the distance between the bases.

Some points to remember about cylinders are:

- The volume of a cylinder is given by the formula V = πr^2h, where r is the radius of the base and h is the height.
- The surface area of a cylinder is given by the formula S = 2πrh + 2πr^2, where r is the radius of the base and h is the height.
- The lateral surface area of a cylinder is given by the formula L = 2πrh, where r is the radius of the base and h is the height.
- A cylinder can be classified as a right cylinder or an oblique cylinder, depending on whether the axis is perpendicular or slanted to the bases.
- A cylinder can also be classified as a circular cylinder or an elliptical cylinder, depending on whether the bases are circles or ellipses.

## Sections of a Cylinder

A section of a cylinder is the intersection of a plane and the cylinder. Depending on the position and orientation of the plane, different types of sections can be obtained, such as:

- A circular section, when the plane is perpendicular to the axis of the cylinder.
- An elliptical section, when the plane is oblique to the axis of the cylinder and does not pass through it.
- A parabolic section, when the plane is parallel to the axis of the cylinder and does not pass through it.
- A hyperbolic section, when the plane is oblique to the axis of the cylinder and passes through it.

The development of a cylinder can be used to find the true shape and size of the sections of the cylinder.
