## Unit 4 - Isometric Projection

- Isometric projection is a method of representing three-dimensional objects in two dimensions.
- Isometric projection preserves the angles and proportions of the object, but distorts the lengths and shapes of the faces.
- Isometric projection is commonly used in engineering, architecture, and video games to show the overall shape and appearance of an object without perspective distortion.
- To draw an isometric projection, one needs to follow these steps:
  - Choose an isometric axis, which is a line that makes equal angles of 120 degrees with the three principal axes of the object.
  - Draw the isometric axis as a vertical line on the drawing plane.
  - Draw two more lines at 30 degrees to the left and right of the isometric axis. These are the isometric lines that represent the other two principal axes of the object.
  - Project the vertices of the object along the isometric lines to find their positions on the drawing plane.
  - Connect the projected vertices with straight lines to form the edges of the object.
  - Shade or color the faces of the object according to the light source and the material properties.

- Some advantages of isometric projection are:
  - It is easy to draw and measure using a 30-60-90 degree triangle or a compass.
  - It shows the true shape and size of the object along the isometric axis and the isometric lines.
  - It allows the viewer to see three sides of the object at once.
- Some disadvantages of isometric projection are:
  - It does not show the true shape and size of the object along the other directions.
  - It does not show the depth or distance of the object from the viewer.
  - It may cause confusion or ambiguity if the object has symmetrical or circular features.
