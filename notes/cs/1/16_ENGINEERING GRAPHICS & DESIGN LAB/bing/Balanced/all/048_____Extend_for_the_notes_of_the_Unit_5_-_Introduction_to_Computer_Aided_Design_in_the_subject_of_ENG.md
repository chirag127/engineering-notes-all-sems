# Unit 5 - Introduction to Computer Aided Design in the subject of ENGINEERING GRAPHICS & DESIGN LAB

- Computer Aided Design (CAD) is the use of computer software to create, modify, analyze, and optimize a design.
- CAD software can help engineers and designers to visualize the design in 2D or 3D, test the functionality and performance of the design, and generate the documentation and specifications for the design.
- CAD software can also provide instructions on how to manufacture, assemble, and maintain the design.
- CAD software can be used for various engineering disciplines, such as civil, mechanical, electrical, and architectural engineering.
- CAD software can also be used for artistic purposes, such as creating computer graphics, animations, and games.

## Benefits and Uses of CAD

- CAD software can improve the quality and accuracy of the design by reducing human errors, such as miscalculations, misinterpretations, and omissions.
- CAD software can increase the productivity and efficiency of the design process by automating repetitive tasks, such as drawing, dimensioning, and labeling.
- CAD software can enhance the creativity and innovation of the design by allowing the designer to explore different alternatives, scenarios, and perspectives.
- CAD software can facilitate the communication and collaboration of the design by enabling the designer to share, edit, and review the design with other stakeholders, such as clients, colleagues, and manufacturers.
- CAD software can reduce the cost and time of the design by minimizing the need for physical prototypes, materials, and labor.

## Topics Covered in Unit 5

- Geometric construction: The use of basic geometric shapes, such as points, lines, circles, and polygons, to create complex figures and patterns.
- Sketching: The use of freehand drawing to quickly and roughly represent the design idea or concept.
- Orthographic projection: The use of multiple 2D views to show the shape and size of a 3D object from different directions, such as front, top, and side views.
- Isometric view: The use of a 3D view to show the shape and size of a 3D object from a 30-degree angle, which gives a more realistic impression of the object.
- Sectional view: The use of a 2D view to show the internal features of a 3D object by cutting through the object along a plane, which reveals the cross-section of the object.
- Detailed view: The use of a 2D view to show a specific part or feature of a 3D object in more detail, such as dimensions, tolerances, and annotations.
- Geometric dimensioning and tolerancing (GD&T): The use of symbols and standards to specify the size, shape, orientation, and location of the features of a design, as well as the allowable variations and deviations from the ideal design.
- Engineering drawings: The use of a set of 2D views to document the design specifications and requirements for manufacturing, assembling, and testing the design.
- Assemblies: The use of a 3D view to show how multiple parts or components fit together to form a complex system or structure.
