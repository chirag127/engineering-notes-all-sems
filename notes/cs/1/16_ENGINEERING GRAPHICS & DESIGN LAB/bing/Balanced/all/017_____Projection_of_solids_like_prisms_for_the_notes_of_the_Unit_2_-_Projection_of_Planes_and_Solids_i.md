# Projection of Solids like Prisms

- A solid is a three-dimensional object that has length, breadth and height.
- A prism is a solid that has two congruent and parallel faces called bases and the other faces are parallelograms called lateral faces.
- The projection of a solid is the representation of its shape on a plane, such as the horizontal plane (HP) or the vertical plane (VP).
- The projection of a solid can be obtained by using orthographic projection, isometric projection or perspective projection methods.
- Orthographic projection is a method of drawing the front view, top view and side view of a solid on two mutually perpendicular planes, such as HP and VP.
- Isometric projection is a method of drawing a three-dimensional solid on a plane, such as HP, such that the three axes of the solid are equally inclined to the plane and the scale is reduced by a factor of 0.816.
- Perspective projection is a method of drawing a three-dimensional solid on a plane, such as HP, such that the solid appears as it is seen by an observer at a finite distance from the solid and the scale varies according to the distance.

## Steps to draw the orthographic projection of a prism

- Step 1: Draw the front view of the prism on the VP, such that the axis of the prism is perpendicular to the VP.
- Step 2: Draw the top view of the prism on the HP, such that the axis of the prism is perpendicular to the HP.
- Step 3: Project the vertices of the front view to the top view by drawing perpendicular lines from the VP to the HP.
- Step 4: Join the corresponding vertices of the top view to complete the shape of the prism.
- Step 5: Mark the dimensions and the names of the views.

## Steps to draw the isometric projection of a prism

- Step 1: Draw an isometric axis on the HP, such that the three axes are equally inclined to the HP and the scale is reduced by a factor of 0.816.
- Step 2: Draw the base of the prism on the HP, such that the center of the base coincides with the origin of the isometric axis and the edges of the base are parallel to the isometric axes.
- Step 3: Draw the height of the prism on the HP, such that the height is parallel to the vertical isometric axis and the length is equal to the actual height of the prism multiplied by 0.816.
- Step 4: Draw the lateral faces of the prism on the HP, such that the edges of the lateral faces are parallel to the horizontal isometric axes and the lengths are equal to the actual lengths of the lateral faces multiplied by 0.816.
- Step 5: Join the corresponding vertices of the base and the lateral faces to complete the shape of the prism.
- Step 6: Mark the dimensions and the name of the view.
