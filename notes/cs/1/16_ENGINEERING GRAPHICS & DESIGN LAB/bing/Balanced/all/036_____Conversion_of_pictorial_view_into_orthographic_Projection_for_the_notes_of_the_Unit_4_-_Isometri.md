# Conversion of pictorial view into orthographic projection

- A pictorial view is a 3-D view of an object that shows how it looks in reality. It can be perspective, isometric or oblique.
- An orthographic projection is a 2-D view of an object that shows only one side or face of the object. It can be front, top, side, etc. It is used in engineering drawings to represent the object without distortion or ambiguity.
- To convert a pictorial view into an orthographic projection, the following steps can be followed:
  - Identify the principal view of the object, which is the view that shows the most features or details of the object. Usually, it is the front view, but it can vary depending on the object.
  - Draw the principal view on a plane of projection that is parallel to the face of the object. Use a scale and a grid to maintain accuracy and proportion. Use solid lines to show visible edges and dashed lines to show hidden edges.
  - Draw the other views on adjacent planes of projection that are perpendicular to the principal plane. Use the same scale and grid as the principal view. Align the views according to the projection method, which can be first-angle or third-angle.
  - Label the views and add dimensions, symbols, notes and other information as needed. Follow the standards and conventions of engineering drawing.
