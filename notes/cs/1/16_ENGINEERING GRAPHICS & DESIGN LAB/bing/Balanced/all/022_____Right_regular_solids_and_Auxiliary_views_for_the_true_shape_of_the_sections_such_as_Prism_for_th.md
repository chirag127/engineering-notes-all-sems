# Right regular solids and Auxiliary views for the true shape of the sections such as Prism

- A right regular solid is a three-dimensional shape that has congruent and regular faces, and whose axis is perpendicular to its base. Examples of right regular solids are cubes, tetrahedrons, octahedrons, etc.
- A prism is a right regular solid that has two parallel and congruent polygonal bases, and rectangular lateral faces. The number of lateral faces is equal to the number of sides of the base. Examples of prisms are triangular prisms, square prisms, hexagonal prisms, etc.
- A section of a solid is the shape obtained by cutting the solid with a plane. The plane is called the cutting plane or the section plane. The section can be used to show the internal features of the solid, such as holes, ribs, slots, etc.
- An auxiliary view is a view that is projected from an existing view, usually at an angle, to show the true shape and size of a feature that is not parallel to any of the principal planes. Auxiliary views are used to show the true shape of sections, inclined surfaces, oblique edges, etc.
- To draw the auxiliary view for the true shape of the section of a prism, the following steps can be followed:
  - Draw the front, top and right side views of the prism, showing the cutting plane in the front view.
  - Draw a line perpendicular to the cutting plane from the front view, and mark the points where the cutting plane intersects the edges of the prism. Label the points with letters, such as A, B, C, etc.
  - Project the points from the front view to the top view, and draw the section line in the top view. Label the points with the same letters as in the front view.
  - Draw a line perpendicular to the section line from the top view, and mark the points where the section line intersects the edges of the prism. Label the points with the same letters as in the top view.
  - Project the points from the top view to the auxiliary view, and draw the true shape of the section in the auxiliary view. Label the points with the same letters as in the top view.
  - The auxiliary view should be aligned with the top view, and the distance between the points in the auxiliary view should be equal to the distance between the points in the front view.
