# Orthographic representation of perspective views — Plane figures and simple solids — Visual Ray Method

- Orthographic projection is a method of representing a three-dimensional object in two dimensions by projecting its points onto a plane or a set of planes.
- Perspective projection is a method of representing a three-dimensional object in two dimensions by simulating the effect of distance and depth on the appearance of the object.
- The difference between orthographic and perspective projections is that in orthographic projection, the lines of projection are parallel to each other and perpendicular to the plane of projection, while in perspective projection, the lines of projection converge at a single point called the center of projection or the vanishing point.
- Orthographic projection is more suitable for technical drawings, such as engineering and architecture, because it preserves the true shape and size of the object and its features. Perspective projection is more suitable for artistic drawings, such as painting and photography, because it creates a realistic impression of the object and its surroundings.
- Plane figures and simple solids are basic geometric shapes that can be represented by orthographic projection. Plane figures include circles, squares, triangles, polygons, etc. Simple solids include cubes, spheres, cylinders, cones, prisms, pyramids, etc.
- Visual ray method is a technique for constructing orthographic views of plane figures and simple solids by using imaginary rays that connect the points on the object to the corresponding points on the plane of projection. The steps of the visual ray method are as follows:
  - Draw the outline of the object in the desired view (front, top, or side).
  - Draw the plane of projection perpendicular to the view direction and parallel to the paper.
  - Draw rays from the corners and edges of the object to the plane of projection, extending them beyond the plane if necessary.
  - Mark the points where the rays intersect the plane of projection. These are the projections of the corners and edges of the object.
  - Connect the projected points with straight lines to form the orthographic view of the object. Erase the rays and the plane of projection.
  - Repeat the process for the other views as needed. Align the views according to the standard conventions of orthographic projection.
