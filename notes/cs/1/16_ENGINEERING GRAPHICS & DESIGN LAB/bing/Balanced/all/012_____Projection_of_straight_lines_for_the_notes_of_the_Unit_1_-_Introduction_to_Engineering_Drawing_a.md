# Projection of Straight Lines

- A straight line is the basic conceptual figure using which any object like a machine component or a structural element is represented.
- Projection of a straight line is the figure obtained by joining, in correct sequence, the points at which the lines drawn from various points on the contour of the line meet the plane.
- Projection of a straight line depends on the orientation and position of the line with respect to the reference planes (horizontal and vertical) and the direction of projection (frontal and profile).
- There are four possible orientations of a straight line: parallel to both planes, perpendicular to one plane and inclined to the other, inclined to both planes, and parallel to one plane and inclined to the other.
- There are six possible positions of a straight line: lying in one of the planes, parallel to one of the planes and offset from it, perpendicular to one of the planes and offset from it, inclined to one of the planes and offset from it, inclined to both planes and offset from both, and inclined to both planes and passing through one of them.
- To draw the projection of a straight line, the following steps are followed:
  - Draw the reference planes and the direction of projection.
  - Locate the end points of the line on the reference planes using given dimensions or coordinates.
  - Draw the projectors from the end points to the plane of projection perpendicular to the direction of projection.
  - Join the points where the projectors meet the plane of projection to get the projection of the line.
  - Label the end points and the projection of the line with appropriate letters and symbols.
  - Indicate the true length and inclination of the line with respect to the reference planes using dimensions or angles.
