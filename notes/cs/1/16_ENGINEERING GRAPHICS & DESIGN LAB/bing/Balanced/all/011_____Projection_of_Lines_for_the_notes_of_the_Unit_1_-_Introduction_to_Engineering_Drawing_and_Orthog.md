# Projection of Lines

- Projection of lines is a method of representing a three-dimensional object on a two-dimensional plane using straight lines that connect the points on the object to the plane .
- Projection of lines is used in engineering drawing to show the shape, size, and position of an object in different views.
- Projection of lines can be classified into two types: parallel projection and perspective projection.
- Parallel projection is a type of projection where the lines of sight are parallel to each other and perpendicular to the plane of projection. Parallel projection can be further divided into orthographic projection and oblique projection.
- Orthographic projection is a type of parallel projection where the plane of projection is parallel to one of the principal planes of the object. Orthographic projection can be used to show the front, top, and side views of an object.
- Oblique projection is a type of parallel projection where the plane of projection is inclined to one of the principal planes of the object. Oblique projection can be used to show the true shape and size of an object in one view.
- Perspective projection is a type of projection where the lines of sight converge at a single point called the vanishing point. Perspective projection can be used to show the realistic appearance of an object as seen by the human eye.
- To draw the projection of lines in engineering drawing, the following steps are required:
  - Choose a suitable scale and orientation for the object and the plane of projection.
  - Draw the reference lines (horizontal, vertical, and inclined) and the projection lines (front, top, and side) on the plane of projection.
  - Locate the end points of the line on the reference lines and the projection lines using the given dimensions and angles.
  - Join the end points of the line on each view to obtain the projection of the line.
  - Label the views and the line with appropriate symbols and notations.
