### Cylinder

A cylinder is a solid figure with two circular bases that are parallel and congruent. The distance between the bases is called the height of the cylinder. The lateral surface of the cylinder is the curved surface that connects the bases. The axis of the cylinder is the line segment that joins the centers of the bases. A cylinder can be classified as a right cylinder or an oblique cylinder depending on the angle between the axis and the bases.

- A right cylinder is a cylinder whose axis is perpendicular to the bases. The lateral surface of a right cylinder is a rectangle that wraps around the bases. The lateral area of a right cylinder is the product of the circumference of the base and the height of the cylinder. The total surface area of a right cylinder is the sum of the lateral area and the areas of the two bases. The volume of a right cylinder is the product of the area of the base and the height of the cylinder.

- An oblique cylinder is a cylinder whose axis is not perpendicular to the bases. The lateral surface of an oblique cylinder is not a rectangle, but a parallelogram that wraps around the bases. The lateral area of an oblique cylinder is the product of the perimeter of the base and the slant height of the cylinder. The slant height is the distance from any point on one base to the corresponding point on the other base along the lateral surface. The total surface area of an oblique cylinder is the sum of the lateral area and the areas of the two bases. The volume of an oblique cylinder is the same as the volume of a right cylinder with the same base and height.
