### Isometric projections of simple and combination of solids

- Isometric projection is a method of graphic representation of three-dimensional objects, used by engineers, technical illustrators, and architects.
- Isometric projection shows the object as it appears to the eye from a particular direction, with all the dimensions preserved at the same scale.
- Isometric projection is obtained by rotating the object 45° about a vertical axis, then tilting the object forward until the body diagonal appears as a point in the front view .
- Isometric projection can be used to show solids like prism, pyramid, cylinder, cone, and their combinations  .
- Isometric projection can also show the truncation of a solid when it is in a simple vertical position, by a cutting plane inclined to the horizontal plane (HP).
- To draw an isometric projection of a solid, the following steps are followed:
  - Draw the isometric axes, which are three mutually perpendicular lines meeting at a point called the origin .
  - Draw the isometric scale, which is a scale that converts the true lengths of the edges of the solid into isometric lengths .
  - Locate the isometric center, which is the point where the three principal planes of the solid intersect .
  - Draw the isometric outline of the solid, which is the projection of the edges of the solid on the isometric planes .
  - Draw the isometric details, such as curves, holes, or cut surfaces, by using appropriate methods .
- To draw an isometric projection of a combination of solids, the following steps are followed:
  - Draw the isometric projection of the larger solid first, following the steps above .
  - Draw the isometric projection of the smaller solid, following the steps above, and aligning it with the larger solid according to the given position .
  - Erase the hidden lines or edges, and show the visible lines or edges clearly .
  - Shade or color the isometric projection to enhance the appearance and clarity .
