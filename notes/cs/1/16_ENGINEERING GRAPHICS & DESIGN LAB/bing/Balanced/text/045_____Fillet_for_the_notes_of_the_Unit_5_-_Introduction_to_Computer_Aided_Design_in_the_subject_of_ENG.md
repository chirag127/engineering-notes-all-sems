### Fillet

- A fillet is a curved surface that connects two adjacent faces or edges of a solid object, such as a mechanical part or a 3D model.
- A fillet has a radius value that determines the size and shape of the curve.
- Fillets are commonly used in engineering graphics and design to:
  - Smooth sharp corners and edges that may cause stress concentration, fatigue failure, or injury.
  - Reduce manufacturing costs by simplifying machining, casting, or molding processes.
  - Improve the aesthetic appearance and functionality of the object.
- Fillets can be classified into two types: internal and external.
  - Internal fillets are concave curves that connect two interior faces or edges of an object, such as a hole or a groove.
  - External fillets are convex curves that connect two exterior faces or edges of an object, such as a corner or a boss.
- Fillets can be quickly designed onto parts using 3D solid modeling engineering CAD software by invoking the function and picking edges of interest.
- Fillets can be dimensioned and shown in technical drawings using standard symbols and conventions.
  - Fillets are usually indicated by the letter R followed by the radius value, such as R10 or R0.5.
  - Fillets are usually shown with a circular arc and a leader line pointing to the center of the arc.
  - Fillets that are symmetrical, equal, or uniform can be dimensioned once and labeled with a multiplier, such as 4XR10 or 8XR0.5.
  - Fillets that are not visible in the view can be shown with a phantom line or a dashed line.
  - Fillets that are of the same size throughout the object can be indicated on a side of the technical drawing page, such as “FILLETS R4 UNLESS OTHERWISE INDICATED”.
