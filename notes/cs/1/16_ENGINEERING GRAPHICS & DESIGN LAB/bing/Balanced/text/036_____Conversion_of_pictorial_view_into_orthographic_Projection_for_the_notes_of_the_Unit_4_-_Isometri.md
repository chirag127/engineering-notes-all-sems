### Conversion of pictorial view into orthographic projection

- Orthographic projection is a method of representing a three-dimensional object in two dimensions by using projecting lines and planes.
- A pictorial view is a three-dimensional representation of an object that shows its shape and features, but not its true dimensions or proportions.
- To convert a pictorial view into an orthographic projection, the following steps are required:
  - Identify the principal planes of projection, which are the front, top and right side views of the object.
  - Align the pictorial view with the principal planes of projection, such that the front view is parallel to the front plane, the top view is parallel to the top plane, and the right side view is parallel to the right plane.
  - Project the visible edges and features of the object from the pictorial view onto the corresponding planes of projection, using perpendicular lines.
  - Draw the outlines of the projected views on the planes of projection, using appropriate line types and symbols.
  - Label the views and dimensions according to the engineering drawing standards.
  - Arrange the views in a standard layout, such as the first-angle or third-angle projection.
