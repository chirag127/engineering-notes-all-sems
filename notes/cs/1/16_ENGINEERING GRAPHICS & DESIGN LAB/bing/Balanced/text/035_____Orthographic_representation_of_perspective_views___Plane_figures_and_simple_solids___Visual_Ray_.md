### Orthographic representation of perspective views — Plane figures and simple solids — Visual Ray Method

- Orthographic projection is a common method of representing three-dimensional objects by three two-dimensional views in each of which the object is viewed along parallel lines that are perpendicular to the plane of the drawing.
- The visual ray method is a technique of obtaining orthographic views of an object by extending the rays from an observer's eye to the contour of the object and intersecting them with the planes of projection.
- The steps of the visual ray method are as follows :
  - Place the object in front of a horizontal plane (H.P) and a vertical plane (V.P) that are perpendicular to each other.
  - Assume an observer at a finite distance from the object and draw the visual rays from the observer's eye to the points on the object.
  - Extend the visual rays to meet the H.P and the V.P.
  - Join the points of intersection on the H.P to obtain the top view of the object.
  - Join the points of intersection on the V.P to obtain the front view of the object.
  - If necessary, draw a third plane of projection perpendicular to both the H.P and the V.P and repeat the same process to obtain the side view of the object.
- The visual ray method can be applied to plane figures and simple solids such as cubes, prisms, pyramids, cylinders, cones, and spheres.
- The advantages of the visual ray method are that it helps to visualize the object in three dimensions and to check the accuracy of the orthographic views.
- The disadvantages of the visual ray method are that it is time-consuming and requires a lot of construction lines that may clutter the drawing.
- The visual ray method is mainly used for teaching and learning purposes and not for practical engineering drawings.
