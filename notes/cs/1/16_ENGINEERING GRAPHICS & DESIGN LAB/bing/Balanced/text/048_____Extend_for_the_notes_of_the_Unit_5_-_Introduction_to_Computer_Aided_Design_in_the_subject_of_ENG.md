### Unit 5 - Introduction to Computer Aided Design in the subject of ENGINEERING GRAPHICS & DESIGN LAB

- Computer Aided Design (CAD) is the use of computer software to create, modify, analyze, and optimize a design. CAD software can help engineers, architects, and other professionals to produce accurate and detailed drawings, models, and simulations of their projects.
- CAD software can also provide information on the materials, dimensions, tolerances, and manufacturing processes of the design, as well as perform various calculations and tests to ensure the quality and feasibility of the design.
- CAD software can be classified into two types: 2D and 3D. 2D CAD software allows the user to create and manipulate drawings in two dimensions, such as lines, circles, arcs, and polygons. 3D CAD software allows the user to create and manipulate solid models in three dimensions, such as cubes, spheres, cylinders, and complex shapes.
- CAD software can also be categorized based on the specific domain or industry it is designed for, such as mechanical, electrical, civil, architectural, or artistic. Some examples of CAD software are AutoCAD, SolidWorks, SketchUp, and Photoshop.
- Engineering Graphics is the discipline of creating and interpreting graphical representations of engineering concepts, such as sketches, diagrams, charts, and symbols. Engineering graphics can help engineers to communicate their ideas, design solutions, and technical information to others, as well as to visualize and analyze their own work.
- Engineering graphics can be divided into two types: manual and computer-aided. Manual engineering graphics involves the use of drawing tools, such as pencils, rulers, compasses, and protractors, to create and modify drawings on paper or other media. Computer-aided engineering graphics involves the use of CAD software to create and modify drawings on a computer screen or other devices.
- Engineering graphics can also be classified based on the projection method used to represent a 3D object in a 2D plane, such as orthographic, isometric, oblique, or perspective. Orthographic projection shows the object as it is seen from different views, such as front, top, and side. Isometric projection shows the object as it is seen from a 45-degree angle, with all the dimensions preserved. Oblique projection shows the object as it is seen from a side view, with one face parallel to the plane. Perspective projection shows the object as it is seen from a point of view, with the dimensions distorted by distance and angle.
- Engineering Graphics and Computer Aided Design Lab is a course that introduces the students to the fundamentals of engineering graphics and CAD software, using a 3D solid modeling software package. The course covers topics such as geometric construction, sketching, orthographic projection, isometric, sectional and detailed views, geometric dimensioning and tolerancing, engineering drawings and assemblies. The course also teaches the students how to use CAD software to create, edit, and analyze their own designs, as well as to export and print their drawings and models. The course aims to develop the students' skills and knowledge in engineering graphics and CAD, as well as to enhance their creativity and problem-solving abilities.
