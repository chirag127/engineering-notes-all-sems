## Unit 3 - Sections of Regular Solids and development of Surfaces

- A **regular solid** is a three-dimensional shape whose faces are congruent regular polygons, and whose vertices are all the same.
- A **section** of a solid is the shape obtained by cutting the solid with a plane.
- A **development** of a solid is the unfolding of the solid into a flat surface that can be cut and folded to form the original solid.
- Some examples of regular solids are cubes, prisms, pyramids, cylinders, cones, and spheres.
- To draw the section of a regular solid, we need to follow these steps:
  - Draw the front view and the top view of the solid in orthographic projection.
  - Draw the cutting plane as a line on the front view and the top view, and label the points where it intersects the edges of the solid.
  - Project the points from the front view to the top view, or vice versa, to find the true shape of the section.
  - Draw the sectional view by joining the points on the true shape of the section.
- To draw the development of a regular solid, we need to follow these steps:
  - Draw the front view and the top view of the solid in orthographic projection.
  - Divide the solid into parts that can be unfolded into flat surfaces, such as rectangles, triangles, circles, etc.
  - Draw the development of each part by tracing the edges of the solid on a flat sheet of paper, and adding tabs for gluing.
  - Arrange the parts in such a way that they can be folded back to form the original solid, and mark the fold lines and the tabs.
