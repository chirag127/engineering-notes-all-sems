### Notes for Unit 1 - Introduction to Engineering Drawing and Orthographic Projections

- Engineering drawing is a graphical language used by engineers and other technical personnel to communicate technical information, such as design specifications, dimensions, tolerances, materials, and manufacturing processes.
- Engineering drawing can be divided into two types: sketching and drafting. Sketching is a freehand drawing technique used to quickly record ideas and concepts. Drafting is a precise and standardized drawing technique used to create detailed and accurate drawings that follow certain rules and conventions.
- Engineering drawing can be performed using different methods, such as manual drawing, computer-aided design (CAD), and computer-aided engineering (CAE). Manual drawing involves using drawing instruments, such as pencils, pens, rulers, compasses, protractors, and scales, to create drawings on paper or other media. CAD involves using software applications, such as AutoCAD, SolidWorks, and CATIA, to create and modify drawings on a computer screen. CAE involves using software applications, such as ANSYS, MATLAB, and Simulink, to perform analysis and simulation of engineering systems and components based on the drawings.
- Engineering drawing can be classified into different types, such as pictorial drawing, orthographic projection, isometric projection, oblique projection, perspective projection, section view, auxiliary view, and dimensioning. Pictorial drawing is a drawing that shows an object as it appears to the eye, with three dimensions visible. Orthographic projection is a drawing that shows an object as it is projected onto two or more planes, such as the front, top, and right views, with only two dimensions visible in each view. Isometric projection is a type of pictorial drawing that shows an object as it is projected onto three planes that form a 120-degree angle with each other, with the same scale in all directions. Oblique projection is a type of pictorial drawing that shows an object as it is projected onto a plane that is parallel to the front view, with the other two views skewed at an angle, such as 30, 45, or 60 degrees. Perspective projection is a type of pictorial drawing that shows an object as it is projected onto a plane that is perpendicular to the line of sight, with the size and shape of the object changing according to the distance and angle from the observer. Section view is a drawing that shows an object as it is cut by a plane, with the internal features and hidden lines visible. Auxiliary view is a drawing that shows an object as it is projected onto a plane that is not parallel or perpendicular to the principal planes, such as the inclined or oblique planes. Dimensioning is a process of adding numerical values and symbols to a drawing to indicate the size, shape, location, and tolerance of the features of an object.
- Engineering drawing follows certain standards and conventions to ensure clarity, accuracy, and consistency of the drawings. Some of the common standards and conventions are:

  - Line types: Different types of lines are used to represent different types of features, such as visible edges, hidden edges, center lines, extension lines, dimension lines, cutting planes, and break lines.
  - Line weights: Different thicknesses of lines are used to indicate the relative importance and visibility of the features, such as thick lines for visible edges, thin lines for hidden edges, and dashed lines for center lines.
  - Lettering: Letters, numbers, and symbols are used to label and annotate the drawings, such as names, titles, units, dimensions, tolerances, and notes. The lettering should be clear, legible, uniform, and aligned with the drawing.
  - Symbols: Symbols are used to represent common or complex features, such as geometric tolerances, surface finish, welding, and electrical components. The symbols should follow the standard codes and formats, such as ANSI, ISO, or ASME.
  - Scales: Scales are used to indicate the ratio between the actual size of the object and the size of the drawing. The scale should be chosen to fit the drawing on the paper or screen, and to show the details of the object clearly. The scale should be stated on the drawing, such as 1:10, 2:1, or 1/4" = 1'.
  - Views: Views are used to show the different aspects and orientations of the object, such as front, top, right, left, bottom, back, isometric, section, and auxiliary views. The views should be arranged and aligned in a logical and consistent manner, such as the first-angle or third-angle projection methods. The views should also be labeled and numbered, such as A, B, C, or 1, 2, 3.
