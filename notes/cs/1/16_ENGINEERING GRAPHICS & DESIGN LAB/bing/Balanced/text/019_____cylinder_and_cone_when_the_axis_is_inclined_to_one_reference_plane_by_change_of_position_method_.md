### Cylinder and cone when the axis is inclined to one reference plane by change of position method

- A cylinder is a solid of revolution generated by rotating a rectangle about one of its sides.
- A cone is a solid of revolution generated by rotating a right triangle about one of its legs.
- The axis of a cylinder or a cone is the line joining the centers of the circular bases.
- When the axis of a cylinder or a cone is inclined to one reference plane (either HP or VP) and parallel to the other, the projections of the solid are obtained by the change of position method.
- The change of position method involves rotating the solid about an axis perpendicular to the reference plane until the axis becomes perpendicular to the reference plane.
- The projections of the solid in the rotated position are drawn first, and then the true length of the axis and the true shape of the base are obtained.
- The projections of the solid in the original position are obtained by rotating the solid back to its original position, keeping the axis and the base in the same planes as before.
- The projections of the solid in the original position are shown by solid lines, and the projections of the solid in the rotated position are shown by dotted lines.
- The following steps are followed to draw the projections of a cylinder or a cone when the axis is inclined to one reference plane by the change of position method :

  1. Draw the projections of the solid in the simple position, i.e., when the axis is perpendicular to the reference plane and parallel to the other.
  2. Mark the center of the base and the center of the top on both the projections.
  3. Draw a line joining the centers of the base and the top on both the projections. This is the axis of the solid.
  4. Mark the angle of inclination of the axis with the reference plane on the projection perpendicular to the reference plane.
  5. Rotate the solid about an axis perpendicular to the reference plane until the axis becomes perpendicular to the reference plane. The angle of rotation is equal to the angle of inclination of the axis.
  6. Draw the projections of the solid in the rotated position. The projection parallel to the reference plane remains unchanged, while the projection perpendicular to the reference plane changes.
  7. Mark the true length of the axis and the true shape of the base on the projection perpendicular to the reference plane.
  8. Rotate the solid back to its original position, keeping the axis and the base in the same planes as before.
  9. Draw the projections of the solid in the original position. The projection parallel to the reference plane changes, while the projection perpendicular to the reference plane remains unchanged.
  10. Show the projections of the solid in the original position by solid lines, and the projections of the solid in the rotated position by dotted lines.
