### Diagonal

- A diagonal is a line segment that connects two non-adjacent vertices of a polygon or polyhedron.
- In a two-dimensional figure, such as a square or rectangle, the diagonals are the line segments that connect opposite corners.
- In a three-dimensional figure, such as a cube or rectangular prism, the diagonals are the line segments that connect opposite corners of the base to the opposite corners of the top face.
- The length of the diagonal can be calculated using the Pythagorean theorem in two dimensions, or the space diagonal formula in three dimensions.
- In engineering drawing and orthographic projections, diagonals can be used to determine the true length of an inclined line or to find the true shape of an inclined surface.
- Diagonals can also be used to check the accuracy of a drawing by verifying that the length of the diagonals is equal in a square or rectangle, or by verifying that the diagonals intersect at the center of a regular polygon.
