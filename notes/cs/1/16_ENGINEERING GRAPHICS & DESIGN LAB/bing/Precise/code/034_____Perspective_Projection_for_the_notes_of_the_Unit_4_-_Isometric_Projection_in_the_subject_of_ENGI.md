### Perspective Projection

Perspective projection is a type of graphical projection used in engineering graphics and design to represent three-dimensional objects on a two-dimensional plane. It is used to create a realistic representation of an object as it would appear to an observer from a particular viewpoint.

Some key points to note about perspective projection are:

1. Perspective projection is based on the principle that objects appear smaller as they recede into the distance.
2. Parallel lines appear to converge towards a vanishing point in perspective projection.
3. The position of the vanishing point depends on the position of the observer and the direction of view.
4. Perspective projection can be achieved using one, two, or three vanishing points, depending on the complexity of the scene being represented.
5. One-point perspective is used to represent objects facing the observer, with the vanishing point located on the horizon line.
6. Two-point perspective is used to represent objects at an angle to the observer, with two vanishing points located on the horizon line.
7. Three-point perspective is used to represent objects viewed from above or below, with three vanishing points, two on the horizon line and one above or below it.
8. Perspective projection is commonly used in art, architecture, and engineering to create realistic representations of objects and scenes.
