### Hatch
Hatch is a pattern of lines or dots that is used to represent a surface or a solid in technical drawings. It is commonly used in engineering graphics and design to indicate the material of the object being represented. Hatching can also be used to represent shading and shadows in a drawing.

Some common types of hatching patterns include:
- Parallel lines: This pattern consists of a series of parallel lines that are spaced evenly apart. It is commonly used to represent uniform materials such as metals or plastics.
- Crosshatching: This pattern consists of two sets of parallel lines that intersect at right angles. It is commonly used to represent materials that have a woven or textured surface.
- Dots: This pattern consists of a series of dots that are spaced evenly apart. It is commonly used to represent porous materials such as foam or sponge.

Hatching can be applied manually using a pen or pencil, or it can be applied automatically using computer-aided design (CAD) software. In CAD software, hatching is typically applied using a fill or pattern command, and the user can select from a library of predefined hatching patterns or create their own custom patterns.

In summary, hatching is an important tool in technical drawing that is used to represent the material, texture, and shading of an object. It can be applied manually or automatically using CAD software, and there are many different hatching patterns to choose from.
