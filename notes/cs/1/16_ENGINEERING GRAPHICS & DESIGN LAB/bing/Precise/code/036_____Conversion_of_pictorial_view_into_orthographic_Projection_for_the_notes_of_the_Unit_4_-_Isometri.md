### Conversion of Pictorial View into Orthographic Projection

Orthographic projection is a method of representing a three-dimensional object in two dimensions. It is a form of parallel projection, where all the projection lines are orthogonal to the projection plane. In orthographic projection, an object is represented by its views on two or more mutually perpendicular planes.

To convert a pictorial view into an orthographic projection, the following steps can be followed:

1. Identify the principal planes of projection: The principal planes of projection are the planes onto which the views of the object are projected. These are typically the front, top, and side views.

2. Project the points of the object onto the principal planes: The points of the object are projected onto the principal planes by drawing projection lines perpendicular to the planes.

3. Draw the views: The views of the object are drawn by connecting the projected points on the principal planes.

4. Add dimensions and details: Dimensions and details such as hidden lines and center lines can be added to the views to provide more information about the object.

This is a brief overview of the process of converting a pictorial view into an orthographic projection. It is important to note that the specific steps and details may vary depending on the object being represented and the conventions used in the field of engineering graphics and design. It is recommended to consult a textbook or reference material for more detailed information on this topic.
