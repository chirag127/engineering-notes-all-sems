### Conversion of Pictorial View into Orthographic Projection

Orthographic projection is a method of representing a three-dimensional object in two dimensions. It is a form of parallel projection, where all the projection lines are orthogonal to the projection plane. In orthographic projection, the object is viewed from different angles to create multiple two-dimensional views, typically a front view, a top view, and a side view.

To convert a pictorial view into an orthographic projection, follow these steps:

1. Identify the principal planes of projection: The three principal planes of projection are the horizontal plane, the frontal plane, and the profile plane. These planes correspond to the top, front, and side views of the object, respectively.

2. Project the object onto the principal planes: Imagine lines being drawn from each point on the object perpendicular to each of the principal planes. The points where these lines intersect the planes are the projections of the object onto the planes.

3. Draw the views: Using the projections on the principal planes, draw the top, front, and side views of the object. Each view should be drawn in its corresponding plane.

4. Add dimensions and details: Add dimensions and other details to the views to fully describe the object.

This is the basic process for converting a pictorial view into an orthographic projection. It is important to note that the views in an orthographic projection are not necessarily to scale, and may need to be adjusted to accurately represent the object.
