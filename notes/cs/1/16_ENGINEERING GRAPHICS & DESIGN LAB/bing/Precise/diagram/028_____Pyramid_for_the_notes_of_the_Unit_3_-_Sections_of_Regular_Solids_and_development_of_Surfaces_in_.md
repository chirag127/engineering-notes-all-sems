### Pyramid

A pyramid is a polyhedron with a polygonal base and triangular faces that meet at a common point called the apex. The base can be any polygon, such as a square, rectangle, or hexagon. The triangular faces are called lateral faces.

- The height of a pyramid is the perpendicular distance from the base to the apex.
- The slant height of a pyramid is the distance from the center of the base to the midpoint of an edge of the base.
- The lateral area of a pyramid is the sum of the areas of its lateral faces.
- The total surface area of a pyramid is the sum of the lateral area and the area of the base.

In the context of engineering graphics and design, the development of the surface of a pyramid involves unfolding the pyramid into a flat pattern that can be cut out and folded to form the original pyramid. This is useful for designing and manufacturing objects with a pyramid shape.

To develop the surface of a pyramid, the following steps can be followed:
1. Draw the base of the pyramid in its true size and shape.
2. Draw the lateral faces of the pyramid as triangles with the base of the triangle equal to the corresponding edge of the base and the height of the triangle equal to the slant height of the pyramid.
3. Arrange the triangles around the base, making sure that the edges of the triangles coincide with the edges of the base.
4. Add tabs to the edges of the triangles to allow for gluing or fastening the pyramid together when it is folded.
