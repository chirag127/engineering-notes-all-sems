# Isometric projections of simple and combination of solids

Isometric projection is a method for visually representing three-dimensional objects in two dimensions. It is commonly used in engineering, architecture, and related fields. In isometric projection, the three coordinate axes appear equally foreshortened and the angles between them are 120 degrees.

Here are some key points to remember when creating isometric projections of simple and combination of solids:

1. Isometric projection is a type of axonometric projection, which means that the object is rotated along one or more of its axes relative to the plane of projection.
2. In isometric projection, the most common form of axonometric projection, the direction of viewing is such that the three axes of space appear equally foreshortened, and there is a common angle of 120 degrees between them.
3. Isometric projection can be used to represent simple solids such as cubes, cylinders, and prisms, as well as more complex combinations of solids.
4. To create an isometric projection, the object is first rotated so that its edges align with the isometric axes. Then, the object is projected onto the plane of projection along lines parallel to the isometric axes.
5. Isometric projection preserves the relative proportions of the object's dimensions, but not its angles or its actual size.
6. Isometric projection is commonly used in technical drawing and computer graphics to represent 3D objects in 2D.
