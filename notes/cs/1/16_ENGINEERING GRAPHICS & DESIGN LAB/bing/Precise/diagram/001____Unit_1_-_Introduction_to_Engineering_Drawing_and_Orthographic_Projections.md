## Unit 1 - Introduction to Engineering Drawing and Orthographic Projections

Engineering drawing is a graphical language used by engineers and other technical personnel to convey technical information. It is a means of communication between the design and manufacturing stages of a product. Engineering drawings are used to fully and clearly define requirements for engineered items.

Orthographic projection is a method of representing three-dimensional objects in two dimensions. It is a form of parallel projection, where the view direction is orthogonal to the projection plane, resulting in each plane of the scene appearing in affine transformation on the viewing surface.

The following are some key points to remember when creating engineering drawings using orthographic projections:

1. Orthographic projections are created using multiple views of an object, typically the front, top, and right side views.
2. Each view is a 2D representation of the object as seen from a particular direction.
3. The views are arranged in a specific layout, with the front view typically placed at the bottom center of the drawing, the top view above it, and the right side view to the right of the front view.
4. The views are aligned with each other, with corresponding points in each view connected by projection lines.
5. Hidden lines are used to represent features that are not visible in a particular view.
6. Dimensions and notes are added to the drawing to provide additional information about the object.
