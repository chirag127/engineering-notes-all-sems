### Cylinder
A cylinder is a three-dimensional geometric shape that has two parallel, congruent bases connected by a curved surface. The bases are usually circular, but can also be elliptical or polygonal. The axis of the cylinder is the line segment that joins the centers of the two bases and is perpendicular to the plane of the bases.

Here are some key points to remember about cylinders:
- The height of a cylinder is the perpendicular distance between its two bases.
- The lateral surface area of a cylinder is the area of the curved surface that connects the two bases.
- The total surface area of a cylinder is the sum of the areas of its two bases and its lateral surface area.
- The volume of a cylinder is the product of the area of its base and its height.

In the context of engineering graphics and design, cylinders are often used to represent objects such as pipes, tubes, and columns. The development of the surface of a cylinder involves "unrolling" the lateral surface to create a flat pattern that can be used to construct the cylinder. This process is known as "developing the surface" of the cylinder.
