## Unit 4 - Isometric Projection

Isometric projection is a method for visually representing three-dimensional objects in two dimensions. It is a type of axonometric projection, where the three coordinate axes appear equally foreshortened and the angles between them are 120 degrees.

Some key points to remember about isometric projection are:

1. Isometric projection is used to create a pictorial drawing of an object, where the object is rotated along one or more of its axes relative to the plane of projection.
2. Isometric projection is commonly used in technical and engineering drawings, as well as in video games and other forms of media.
3. In an isometric projection, the scale along each axis of the projection is the same, and all the edges of the object that are parallel to one of the three axes of the projection are drawn as parallel lines.
4. Isometric projection is not the same as isometric view, which is a perspective projection where the camera is placed at an infinite distance from the object, resulting in parallel lines appearing parallel in the projection.
