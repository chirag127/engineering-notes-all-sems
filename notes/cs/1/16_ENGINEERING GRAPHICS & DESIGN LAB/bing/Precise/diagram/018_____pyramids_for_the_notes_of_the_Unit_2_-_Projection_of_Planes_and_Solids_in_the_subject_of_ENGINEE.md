### Pyramids for the notes of the Unit 2 - Projection of Planes and Solids in the subject of ENGINEERING GRAPHICS & DESIGN LAB

1. A pyramid is a polyhedron with a polygonal base and triangular faces that meet at a common point called the apex.
2. The base of the pyramid can be any polygon, such as a square, rectangle, triangle, or hexagon.
3. The number of faces, edges, and vertices of a pyramid can be determined using Euler's formula: F + V = E + 2, where F is the number of faces, V is the number of vertices, and E is the number of edges.
4. The height of a pyramid is the perpendicular distance from the base to the apex.
5. The volume of a pyramid can be calculated using the formula: V = (1/3)Bh, where B is the area of the base and h is the height of the pyramid.
6. The surface area of a pyramid can be calculated by adding the area of the base to the sum of the areas of the triangular faces.
7. In engineering graphics, the projection of a pyramid can be drawn using orthographic projection techniques.
8. The true shape of the triangular faces can be determined using auxiliary views.
9. The development of a pyramid can be constructed by drawing the true shape of each face and joining them together along their common edges.
