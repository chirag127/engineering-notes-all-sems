# Cylinder

A cylinder is a three-dimensional geometric shape that consists of two parallel circular bases connected by a curved surface. It is a type of prism, where the cross-section is a circle. The axis of the cylinder is the line joining the centers of the two bases.

Some important points to note about cylinders are:
- The height of the cylinder is the perpendicular distance between the two bases.
- The volume of a cylinder is given by the formula V = πr^2h, where r is the radius of the base and h is the height of the cylinder.
- The lateral surface area of a cylinder is given by the formula A = 2πrh, where r is the radius of the base and h is the height of the cylinder.
- The total surface area of a cylinder is given by the formula A = 2πr(r+h), where r is the radius of the base and h is the height of the cylinder.

In the context of engineering graphics and design, the development of the surface of a cylinder involves unfolding the curved surface into a flat plane. This can be done by cutting the cylinder along its height and unrolling the lateral surface. The resulting shape is a rectangle with a length equal to the circumference of the base and a width equal to the height of the cylinder.
