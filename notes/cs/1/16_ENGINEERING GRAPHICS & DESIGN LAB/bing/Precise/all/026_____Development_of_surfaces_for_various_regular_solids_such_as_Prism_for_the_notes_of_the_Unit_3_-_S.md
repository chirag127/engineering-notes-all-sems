# Development of Surfaces for Various Regular Solids such as Prism

In the subject of Engineering Graphics & Design Lab, Unit 3 covers the sections of regular solids and the development of surfaces. One of the regular solids covered in this unit is the prism.

A prism is a polyhedron with two parallel and congruent polygonal faces, called bases, and a set of rectangular or parallelogram faces, called lateral faces, connecting the corresponding sides of the bases.

The development of the surface of a prism involves unfolding the prism into a flat pattern, which can then be cut out and folded to form the original prism. This process is also known as "unwrapping" the prism.

To develop the surface of a prism, the following steps can be followed:

1. Draw the base of the prism.
2. Draw the lateral faces, which are rectangles or parallelograms, connected to the sides of the base.
3. Unfold the lateral faces to form a flat pattern.
4. Add tabs to the lateral faces to allow for gluing or taping the faces together when the prism is reassembled.

The development of the surface of a prism can be used in various applications, such as creating a net for a cardboard or paper model of the prism, or for calculating the surface area of the prism.

It is important to note that the development of the surface of a prism is not unique, as the lateral faces can be unfolded in different ways to create different flat patterns. However, all of the flat patterns will have the same surface area and can be used to reassemble the original prism.
