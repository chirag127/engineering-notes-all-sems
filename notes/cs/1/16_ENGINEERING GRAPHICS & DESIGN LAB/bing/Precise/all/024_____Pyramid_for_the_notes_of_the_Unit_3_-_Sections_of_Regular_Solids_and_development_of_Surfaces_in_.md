# Pyramid

A pyramid is a polyhedron with a polygonal base and triangular faces that meet at a common point called the apex. The base can be any polygon, but it is usually a square. The triangular faces are called lateral faces.

Here are some key points to remember about pyramids:

- The number of lateral faces is equal to the number of sides of the base.
- The lateral faces meet at the apex.
- The altitude of a pyramid is the perpendicular distance from the base to the apex.
- The slant height of a pyramid is the distance from the center of the base to the midpoint of a lateral edge.
- The volume of a pyramid is equal to one-third the product of the area of the base and the altitude.

In the context of engineering graphics and design, the development of the surface of a pyramid involves unfolding the lateral faces onto a flat plane. This can be done by drawing the lateral faces as separate triangles, with their bases along the edges of the base, and their vertices meeting at a common point representing the apex. The resulting figure is called the development of the surface of the pyramid.
