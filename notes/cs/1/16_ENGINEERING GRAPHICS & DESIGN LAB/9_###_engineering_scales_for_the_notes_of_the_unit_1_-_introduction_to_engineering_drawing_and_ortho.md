### Engineering Scales for the notes of the Unit 1 - Introduction to Engineering Drawing and Orthographic Projections in the subject of ENGINEERING GRAPHICS & DESIGN LAB

Engineering Scales are used to represent real-world objects in a smaller size on a drawing. They are used to accurately represent the size and proportion of objects in a drawing. The most common scales used in engineering drawings are:

1. Architectural Scales (e.g. 1/4" = 1'-0")
2. Engineering Scales (e.g. 1:100, 1:50)
3. Metric Scales (e.g. 1:100, 1:200)

The scale chosen depends on the size of the object being drawn and the level of detail required. The scale is indicated on the drawing by a fraction or ratio, such as 1/4" = 1'-0". This means that 1/4" on the drawing represents 1'-0" in the real world.

Orthographic Projections are a type of technical drawing that uses multiple views of an object to represent its three-dimensional form. The views are typically top, front, side, and sometimes isometric views. These views are used to accurately represent the size, shape, and position of objects in a drawing.

In conclusion, Engineering Scales and Orthographic Projections are essential tools for engineers and designers to accurately represent objects in a drawing.
