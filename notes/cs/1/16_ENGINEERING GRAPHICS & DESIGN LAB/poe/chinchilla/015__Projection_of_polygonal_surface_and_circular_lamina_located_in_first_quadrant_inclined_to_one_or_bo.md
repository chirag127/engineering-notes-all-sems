### Projection of Polygonal Surface and Circular Lamina located in First Quadrant inclined to one or both Reference Planes

When it comes to projection of solids, it is important to understand how to project polygonal surfaces and circular lamina located in the first quadrant inclined to one or both reference planes. In this section, we will discuss the steps involved in projecting these types of solids accurately.

#### Projection of Polygonal Surface

Here are the steps involved in projecting a polygonal surface:

1. Determine the position of the solid in the first quadrant and the orientation of the surface with respect to the reference planes.
2. Draw the front view of the solid and locate the vertices of the polygonal surface.
3. Draw a line from each vertex perpendicular to the reference plane on which the surface is projected.
4. Determine the true lengths of these perpendiculars by projecting them onto the reference plane on which they are perpendicular.
5. Draw the true shape of the polygonal surface by connecting the true lengths of the perpendiculars.
6. Project the true shape of the polygonal surface onto the reference planes to get the top and side views of the solid.

#### Projection of Circular Lamina

Here are the steps involved in projecting a circular lamina:

4. Determine the true length of this perpendicular by projecting it onto the reference plane on which it is perpendicular.
5. Draw the true shape of the circular lamina by drawing a circle with the true length of the perpendicular as its radius.
6. Project the true shape of the circular lamina onto the reference planes to get the top and side views of the solid.

It is important to note that for solids inclined to both reference planes, the true lengths of the perpendiculars need to be projected onto both reference planes. Also, care should be taken to ensure that the projections of the solid are accurate and aligned properly.
