### Projection of lines inclined to one plane and both planes

In Engineering Drawing and Orthographic Projections, it is essential to know how to project lines that are inclined to one plane and both planes. This topic is crucial in creating accurate and detailed engineering drawings.

Here are some important points to keep in mind:

#### Projection of lines inclined to one plane

1. A line that is inclined to one plane can be projected onto that plane using the standard orthographic projection techniques.
2. The line is first projected perpendicular to the plane it intersects, and then its true length is measured and projected onto the plane.
3. The projection of the line on the plane is parallel to its true length, and its position is determined by the distance between the line and the plane.
4. The projection of the line on the plane is shown by a dotted line.

#### Projection of lines inclined to both planes

1. When a line is inclined to both planes, it is first projected onto one plane using standard orthographic projection techniques.
2. The projection is then extended until it intersects the second plane.
3. The true length of the line is then measured and projected onto the second plane.
4. The projection of the line on the second plane is shown by a dotted line, and its position is determined by the distance between the line and the second plane.
5. The projection of the line on the first plane is shown by a solid line.

It is essential to practice these techniques to master the projection of lines inclined to one plane and both planes. Accuracy and attention to detail are crucial in engineering drawing, and knowing how to project lines correctly is a fundamental skill.
