### Hatch for the notes of the Unit 5 - Introduction to Computer Aided Design in the subject of ENGINEERING GRAPHICS & DESIGN LAB

Hatching is an important aspect of computer-aided design that involves filling an enclosed area with a pattern of lines or dots that are used to represent the material or texture of an object. In the context of engineering graphics and design, hatching is used to indicate the material of which a part is made, or the surface texture of a part. Here are some important points to keep in mind when working with hatching in computer-aided design:

- Hatching patterns can be applied to a variety of objects, including lines, circles, arcs, and polygons. 
- When hatching an object, it is important to select a pattern that accurately represents the material or texture that is being depicted. Common hatching patterns include solid fill, crosshatching, and stippling. 
- The spacing and angle of the hatching lines can be adjusted to achieve the desired effect. A smaller spacing and a steeper angle will result in a denser hatching pattern, while a larger spacing and a shallower angle will result in a more open pattern. 
- In some cases, it may be necessary to use multiple hatching patterns to accurately represent the materials or textures of an object. For example, a part that is made of two different materials may require two different hatching patterns to be used. 
- Hatching can also be used to indicate the direction of a surface, such as in the case of a curved surface where the hatching lines are oriented to follow the contour of the surface. 
- In computer-aided design software, hatching is typically applied using a dedicated hatch tool that allows the user to select the hatching pattern, spacing, and angle. The user can then click on the enclosed area to apply the hatching pattern. 
- It is important to ensure that the hatching lines are clearly visible and do not overlap or intersect with other lines in the drawing. This can be achieved by adjusting the spacing and angle of the lines as necessary. 
- Hatching can be used in conjunction with other techniques, such as shading and lighting, to create a realistic representation of an object in computer-aided design. 

In conclusion, hatching is an important aspect of computer-aided design that is used to represent the material and texture of an object. By selecting the appropriate hatching pattern, spacing, and angle, and ensuring that the lines are clearly visible, the user can create an accurate and realistic representation of the object in the drawing.
