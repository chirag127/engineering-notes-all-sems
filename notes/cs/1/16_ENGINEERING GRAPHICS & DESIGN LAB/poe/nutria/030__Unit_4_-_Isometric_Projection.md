

## Unit 4 - Isometric Projection

1. Isometric projection is a type of graphical projection used for the display of three-dimensional objects.
2. It is an axonometric projection in which the three coordinate axes appear equally foreshortened and the angles between any two of them are 120 degrees.
3. The term "isometric" comes from the Greek for "equal measure," reflecting that the scale along each axis of the projection is the same.
4. Isometric projection is commonly used in engineering, architecture, and video games.
5. In isometric projection, all three axes are equally foreshortened, and the angles between any two of them are 120 degrees.
6. The lines of projection in isometric projection are not truly parallel, but are foreshortened by approximately 80%.
7. Isometric projection is not an actual representation of the object, but rather an interpretation of it.
8. The advantage of isometric projection is that it allows a three-dimensional object to be represented in two dimensions without distorting its proportions.
9. This makes it ideal for creating technical drawings and diagrams.
10. Isometric projection can also be used to create perspective drawings, which give the illusion of depth and distance.
