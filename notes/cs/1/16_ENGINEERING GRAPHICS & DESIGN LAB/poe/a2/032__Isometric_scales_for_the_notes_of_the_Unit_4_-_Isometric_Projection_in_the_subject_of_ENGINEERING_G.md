### Isometric scales for the notes of the Unit 4 - Isometric Projection in the subject of ENGINEERING GRAPHICS & DESIGN LAB.

1. Introduction
- Isometric projection is a way of representing three-dimensional objects in two dimensions. It produces three-dimensional pictorial effect.
- In isometric projection, the angles between the projections of the axes are equal to 120°. The scale along each axis is different, which are called isometric scales.

2. Isometric scales
- The isometric scales are the scale ratios in which the measurements along the three axes are drawn to represent the object in isometric projection.
- The isometric scales are obtained by dividing the actual measurement of the object along each axis by the corresponding isometric measurement.
- If a, b and c are the actual measurements along X, Y and Z axes respectively and A, B and C are the isometric measurements, then the isometric scales are:

Scale along X-axis (Sx) = A/a
Scale along Y-axis (Sy) = B/b
Scale along Z-axis (Sz) = C/c

3. Conventions and applications
- The isometric scales are generally taken as 1:2:1 or 1:√3:1. 1:2:1 isometric scale is easy to construct but 1:√3:1 provides less distortion.
- Isometric projection is useful to represent the designs of 3D objects such as machines, structures etc. as it gives an 3D effect with all the three dimensions shown in a single view.
- It is a quick way of visualizing the spatial relationships between the parts of an object. The measurements can be directly taken from the isometric view and converted to actual measurements using the isometric scales.
