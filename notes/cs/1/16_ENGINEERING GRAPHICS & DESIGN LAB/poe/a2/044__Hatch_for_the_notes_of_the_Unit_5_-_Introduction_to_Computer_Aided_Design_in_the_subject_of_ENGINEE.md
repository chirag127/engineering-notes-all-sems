### Hatch

- Hatch refers to the pattern of lines that is used to shade an enclosed area in a drawing to indicate the material or surface texture.
- Hatches make the drawing more visually appealing and help in conveying the idea more effectively.
- Different types of hatches are used for different materials e.g. brick hatch is used for brick area, diagonal hatch for wood, circular hatch for a concrete surface etc.
- While applying hatch, ensure that the enclosed area is completely bound by a visible and continuous line. The hatch lines should also not cross the boundary lines.
- Hatch lines can be at any angle but 45° and 90° angles are preferred as they produce neat patterns and are easier to create.
- The hatch pattern, scale and spacing can be modified as per requirement. Closer hatch lines indicate a smoother surface while wider spacing indicates a rougher texture.
- Hatching should not be overly complicated and should allow the details and outlines in the drawing to be clearly visible.
- In CAD, hatch patterns are available in the software and can be selected and applied with options to modify spacing, angle etc. This is more convenient than drawing the hatch lines manually.
