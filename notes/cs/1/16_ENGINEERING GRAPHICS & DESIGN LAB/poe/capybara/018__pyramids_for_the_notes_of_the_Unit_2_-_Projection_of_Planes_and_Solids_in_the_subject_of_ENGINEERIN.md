### Pyramids

A pyramid is a three-dimensional solid with a polygonal base and triangular faces that meet at a common point called the apex. The pyramid is classified according to the shape of its base such as a square pyramid, triangular pyramid, or pentagonal pyramid.

#### Types of Pyramids

1. Square Pyramid
   - A square pyramid has a square base and four triangular faces that meet at a common apex.
   - The projection of a square pyramid can be obtained by projecting the vertices of the base and the apex perpendicular to the base.

2. Triangular Pyramid
   - A triangular pyramid has a triangular base and three triangular faces that meet at a common apex.
   - The projection of a triangular pyramid can be obtained by projecting the vertices of the base and the apex perpendicular to the base.

3. Pentagonal Pyramid
   - A pentagonal pyramid has a pentagonal base and five triangular faces that meet at a common apex.
   - The projection of a pentagonal pyramid can be obtained by projecting the vertices of the base and the apex perpendicular to the base.

#### Properties of Pyramids

1. Surface area
   - The surface area of a pyramid is the sum of the areas of its faces.
   - Surface area = area of base + sum of areas of lateral faces

2. Volume
   - The volume of a pyramid is given by the formula V = (1/3) × base area × height.
   - The height of the pyramid is the perpendicular distance from the apex to the base.

3. Euler's Formula
   - Euler's formula for pyramids states that the number of vertices (V), edges (E), and faces (F) of a pyramid is related by the equation V - E + F = 2.
   - This formula applies to any pyramid, regardless of its shape or size.

In conclusion, pyramids are three-dimensional solids that are widely used in engineering and architecture. Understanding the properties of pyramids is essential for designing and projecting complex structures.
