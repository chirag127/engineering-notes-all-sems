- To develop the surface of a prism, it is necessary to "unfold" the solid into a flat two-dimensional shape.
- The development of a prism's surface is essentially a series of rectangles, each representing one of the sides of the prism.
- The length of each rectangle is equal to the perimeter of the corresponding base, while the width is equal to the height of the prism.
- The rectangles are then arranged in a specific pattern to represent the overall shape of the prism's surface.
- It is important to note that the development of a prism's surface will vary depending on the dimensions and angles of the prism itself.
