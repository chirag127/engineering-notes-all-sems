## Unit 2 - Projection of Planes and Solids

In this unit, we will be discussing the concept of projection of planes and solids. The main aim of this unit is to help students understand the different types of projections that exist and the methods of obtaining them. 

Below are the key points that will be covered in this unit:

1. Definition of Projection: Projection is the process of representing a three-dimensional object onto a two-dimensional plane. It is important to note that the projection of an object depends on the position of the observer and the direction of the projection.

2. Types of Projections: There are two types of projections; Orthographic projection and Perspective projection. Orthographic projection is a method of projection where the projectors are perpendicular to the plane of projection, while Perspective projection is a method of projection where the projectors are at a finite distance from the object.

3. Types of Orthographic Projections: Orthographic projections can be divided into three types, namely; planar, cylindrical and spherical projections. Planar projection involves projecting an object onto a single plane, while cylindrical projection involves projecting an object onto a cylinder. Spherical projection involves projecting an object onto a sphere.

4. Methods of Obtaining Orthographic Projections: There are two methods of obtaining orthographic projections; First-angle projection and Third-angle projection. First-angle projection is commonly used in Europe, while Third-angle projection is commonly used in the United States.

5. Views in Orthographic Projection: In orthographic projection, there are six standard views that are commonly used, namely; front view, top view, right-side view, left-side view, back view, and bottom view.

6. Types of Perspective Projections: Perspective projections can be divided into two types, namely; one-point perspective and two-point perspective. One-point perspective is used when the object is viewed from the front, while two-point perspective is used when the object is viewed from the side.

7. Vanishing Points: In perspective projection, vanishing points are used to obtain the projection of an object. Vanishing points are points on the horizon where parallel lines appear to converge.

In conclusion, the understanding of projection of planes and solids is crucial for anyone studying engineering, architecture, or any field that involves the designing of structures or objects. Students should take the time to master the different types of projections and the methods of obtaining them.
