Hardness in water refers to the presence of dissolved minerals, mainly calcium and magnesium ions. Alkalinity refers to the ability of water to neutralize acids. These two parameters are important to determine the quality of water for drinking, industrial, and agricultural purposes.

Numerical problems for determining hardness and alkalinity involve calculating the concentration of ions in water. The most common method for determining hardness is titration with a calcium and magnesium indicator. Alkalinity can be determined by titrating with a strong acid, such as sulfuric acid.

In both cases, the endpoint of the titration is determined by a change in the color of the indicator solution. The amount of titrant added to reach the endpoint is directly proportional to the concentration of the ions in the water sample. The results can be expressed in mg/L or ppm (parts per million).

It is important to accurately determine the hardness and alkalinity of water, as it affects the performance of boilers, cooling systems, and the growth of plants. A water sample with high hardness and alkalinity may require treatment to reduce the concentration of ions before it can be used for a specific purpose.
