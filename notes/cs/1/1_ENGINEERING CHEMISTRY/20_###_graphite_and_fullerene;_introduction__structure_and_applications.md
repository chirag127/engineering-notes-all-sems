### Graphite and Fullerene; Introduction, Structure and applications.

Graphite: 

Introduction: Graphite is a naturally occurring form of carbon, known for its soft and slippery texture. It is an allotrope of carbon, meaning it has a different arrangement of atoms than other forms of carbon, such as diamond.

Structure: Graphite consists of layers of carbon atoms arranged in a hexagonal pattern, with weak bonds between the layers and strong bonds within the layers. This unique structure gives graphite its characteristic properties, such as its ability to conduct electricity and act as a lubricant.

Applications: 

1. Lubricants: Graphite is commonly used as a lubricant due to its slippery texture.

2. Batteries: Graphite is used as the anode in lithium-ion batteries due to its high electrical conductivity and capacity to store lithium ions.

3. Refractories: Graphite is used in high-temperature applications, such as furnace linings, due to its high thermal stability.

4. Pencils: Graphite is used in the lead of pencils due to its ability to leave a mark on paper.

Fullerene:

Introduction: Fullerene is a type of carbon molecule that consists of 60 carbon atoms arranged in a spherical shape. It was first discovered in 1985 and named after the architect Buckminster Fuller, who designed geodesic domes with a similar structure.

Structure: Fullerene molecules have a unique structure, consisting of hexagons and pentagons arranged in a spherical shape. The carbon atoms in fullerenes are bonded together with strong covalent bonds, creating a stable and rigid molecule.

Applications:

1. Nanotechnology: Fullerenes are used in nanotechnology due to their unique structure and properties, such as their ability to act as a host for other atoms and molecules.

2. Catalysts: Fullerenes are used as catalysts in chemical reactions due to their ability to activate chemical bonds.

3. Solar cells: Fullerenes are used in the production of solar cells due to their ability to absorb and transfer light.

4. Medical applications: Fullerenes are being researched for their potential use in medical applications, such as drug delivery and cancer therapy.

In conclusion, Graphite is a naturally occurring form of carbon with unique properties, such as its ability to conduct electricity and act as a lubricant, and is used in applications such as batteries, refractories, and pencils. Fullerene is a type of carbon molecule with a unique spherical structure and strong covalent bonds, and is used in applications such as nanotechnology, catalysts, solar cells, and medical research.
