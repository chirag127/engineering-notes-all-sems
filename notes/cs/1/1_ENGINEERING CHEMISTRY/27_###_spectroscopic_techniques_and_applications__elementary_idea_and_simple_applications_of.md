### Spectroscopic Techniques and Applications: Elementary idea and simple applications of

Spectroscopy is the study of the interaction of electromagnetic radiation with matter. It is used to determine the properties of substances, such as their composition, structure, and molecular properties. Spectroscopic techniques are used to analyze the light absorbed, emitted, or scattered by a sample.

1. Absorption spectroscopy: Absorption spectroscopy measures the amount of light absorbed by a sample. It is used to determine the concentration of a substance in a sample, as well as its molecular properties.

2. Emission spectroscopy: Emission spectroscopy measures the light emitted by a sample. It is used to determine the electronic structure of molecules, as well as their chemical composition and molecular properties.

3. Scattering spectroscopy: Scattering spectroscopy measures the light scattered by a sample. It is used to determine the size, shape, and structure of particles, as well as their molecular properties.

Applications of spectroscopic techniques include:

1. Environmental monitoring: Spectroscopic techniques are used to monitor environmental pollutants and to determine the quality of air, water, and soil.

2. Medical diagnosis: Spectroscopic techniques are used in medical diagnosis to detect diseases and to monitor the effectiveness of treatments.

3. Food analysis: Spectroscopic techniques are used to analyze food for contaminants and to determine the nutritional content of food.

4. Material analysis: Spectroscopic techniques are used to analyze materials for their composition and properties, such as their strength and durability.

In conclusion, spectroscopy is the study of the interaction of electromagnetic radiation with matter and spectroscopic techniques are used to analyze the light absorbed, emitted, or scattered by a sample. Applications of spectroscopic techniques include environmental monitoring, medical diagnosis, food analysis, and material analysis.
