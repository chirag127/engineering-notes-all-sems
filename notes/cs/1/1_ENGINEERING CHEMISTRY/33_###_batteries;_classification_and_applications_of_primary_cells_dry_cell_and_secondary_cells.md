### Batteries; Classification and applications of Primary Cells (Dry Cell) and Secondary Cells

Batteries are devices that store electrical energy and convert it into electrical power when needed. They are classified into two types: primary cells and secondary cells. 

Primary cells, also known as dry cells, are one-time use batteries that cannot be recharged. They are commonly used in devices such as flashlights, remote controls, and toys. The most common type of dry cell is the zinc-carbon battery, which consists of a zinc anode, a carbon cathode, and an electrolyte.

Secondary cells, also known as rechargeable batteries, can be recharged and used multiple times. They are commonly used in devices such as laptops, smartphones, and electric vehicles. The most common type of secondary cell is the lithium-ion battery, which consists of a lithium-ion cathode, a carbon anode, and a liquid electrolyte. 

Applications of batteries vary widely, from powering handheld devices to providing backup power for large scale systems. In transportation, batteries are used to power electric vehicles, while in renewable energy systems, they are used to store excess energy generated from sources such as solar and wind.
