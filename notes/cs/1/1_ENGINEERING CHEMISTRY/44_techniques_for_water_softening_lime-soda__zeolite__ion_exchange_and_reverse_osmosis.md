Techniques for water softening (Lime-Soda, Zeolite, Ion Exchange and Reverse Osmosis

Water softening is the process of removing hardness-causing minerals (calcium and magnesium) from water. There are four common methods: 

1. Lime-Soda Softening: Involves adding lime and soda ash to water to precipitate hardness-causing minerals. 

2. Zeolite Softening: Uses a zeolite resin bed to exchange hardness-causing ions with sodium ions. 

3. Ion Exchange Softening: Similar to zeolite softening, but uses a synthetic resin bed to exchange ions. 

4. Reverse Osmosis (RO) Softening: Involves forcing water through a semi-permeable membrane to remove hardness-causing minerals. 

Each method has its own advantages and disadvantages, and the choice of method depends on the specific needs of the user.
