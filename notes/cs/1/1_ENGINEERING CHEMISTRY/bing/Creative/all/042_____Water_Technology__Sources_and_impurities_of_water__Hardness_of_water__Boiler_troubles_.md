# Water Technology: Sources and impurities of water, Hardness of water, Boiler troubles

## Sources and impurities of water

- Water is a vital resource for human and industrial activities. It is obtained from various sources, such as surface water (rivers, lakes, ponds, etc.), groundwater (wells, springs, etc.), rainwater, seawater, etc.
- Water from different sources may contain various impurities, such as dissolved solids (salts, minerals, metals, etc.), dissolved gases (oxygen, carbon dioxide, hydrogen sulfide, etc.), suspended solids (sand, clay, silt, etc.), colloidal particles (organic matter, bacteria, viruses, etc.), and dissolved organic compounds (pesticides, herbicides, etc.).
- The impurities in water may affect its quality, suitability, and safety for various purposes, such as drinking, domestic use, irrigation, industrial use, etc. Therefore, water quality analysis and treatment are essential to ensure the desired standards and specifications of water for different applications.

## Hardness of water

- Hardness of water is a measure of the concentration of dissolved calcium and magnesium salts in water. These salts react with soap to form insoluble precipitates, called scum or curd, which reduce the lathering and cleaning ability of soap. Hard water also causes scaling and corrosion of pipes, boilers, heat exchangers, and other equipment that use water.
- Hardness of water is classified into two types: temporary hardness and permanent hardness. Temporary hardness is caused by the presence of bicarbonates of calcium and magnesium, which can be removed by boiling the water or by adding lime. Permanent hardness is caused by the presence of sulfates, chlorides, and nitrates of calcium and magnesium, which cannot be removed by boiling or lime treatment, but require other methods, such as ion exchange, reverse osmosis, etc.
- The degree of hardness of water is expressed in terms of milligrams of calcium carbonate equivalent per liter of water (mg/L as CaCO3) or parts per million (ppm) of calcium carbonate equivalent. The following table shows the classification of water based on its hardness:

| Hard               | 150 - 300                      |
| Very hard          | Above 300                      |

## Boiler troubles

- Boiler troubles are the problems that arise due to the use of hard water in boilers, which are devices that generate steam from water by heating it. Some of the common boiler troubles are:

  - Scale formation: Scale is the hard and adherent deposit of calcium and magnesium salts, such as calcium sulfate, magnesium hydroxide, and calcium bicarbonate, on the inner walls of the boiler. Scale reduces the heat transfer efficiency of the boiler, increases the fuel consumption, and may cause overheating and bursting of the boiler tubes.
  - Sludge formation: Sludge is the soft and loose deposit of calcium and magnesium salts, such as calcium carbonate and magnesium carbonate, on the bottom of the boiler. Sludge reduces the water circulation in the boiler, increases the corrosion of the boiler parts, and may clog the pipes and valves of the boiler.
  - Corrosion: Corrosion is the deterioration of the metal surfaces of the boiler due to the chemical or electrochemical reactions with the water and its impurities. Corrosion weakens the strength and durability of the boiler, and may cause leaks and cracks in the boiler. Some of the causes of corrosion are:

    - Dissolved oxygen: Oxygen in water reacts with the iron of the boiler to form rust (iron oxide), which corrodes the boiler.
    - Dissolved carbon dioxide: Carbon dioxide in water forms carbonic acid, which lowers the pH of water and makes it acidic. Acidic water corrodes the boiler and forms hydrogen gas, which may cause pitting and embrittlement of the boiler metal.
    - Dissolved salts: Salts in water, such as magnesium chloride and magnesium sulfate, hydrolyze to form acidic products, such as hydrochloric acid and sulfuric acid, which corrode the boiler. Salts also increase the electrical conductivity of water, which enhances the electrochemical corrosion of the boiler.
