# Organometallic Compounds (RMgX and LiAlH4)

- Organometallic compounds are compounds that contain a metal-carbon bond, R-M, where R is an organic group and M is a metal.
- Organometallic compounds of Li and Mg (Grignard reagents) are among the most important and widely used organic reagents. They provide a source of nucleophilic carbon atoms that can react with electrophilic carbon to form a new carbon-carbon bond.
- Other metals, such as Na, Cu and Zn, can also form organometallic compounds with different reactivities and applications.
- Organometallic compounds of Li and Mg are prepared by the reaction of an alkyl or aryl halide with the metal in an inert solvent, such as ether or THF. The reaction is highly exothermic and requires careful control of the temperature and stirring.
- The general formula of organometallic compounds of Li and Mg is R-M, where R is an alkyl or aryl group and M is Li or MgX, where X is a halogen (Cl, Br or I). For example, CH3-MgBr is methylmagnesium bromide, a Grignard reagent.
- Organometallic compounds of Li and Mg are strong bases and nucleophiles. They can react with various electrophiles, such as carbonyl compounds, epoxides, alkyl halides, and acid derivatives, to form new carbon-carbon bonds. The products are usually alcohols, ethers, or amines after hydrolysis or protonation.

  - Addition to carbonyl compounds: R-M + R'2C=O -> R'2(R)COM + HOM, where R'2(R)COM is an alkoxide that can be hydrolyzed to an alcohol. This reaction is useful for the synthesis of primary, secondary, and tertiary alcohols.
  - Addition to epoxides: R-M + R'2C-O-CR'2 -> R'2C(R)-O-CR'2M + HOM, where R'2C(R)-O-CR'2M is an alkoxide that can be hydrolyzed to an ether. This reaction is useful for the synthesis of ethers with a new carbon-carbon bond.
  - Substitution of alkyl halides: R-M + R'X -> R-R' + MX, where X is a halogen. This reaction is useful for the synthesis of alkanes with a new carbon-carbon bond.
  - Reduction of acid derivatives: R-M + R'COX -> R-R'COH + MX, where X is a halogen, OR, or NR2. This reaction is useful for the synthesis of aldehydes, ketones, or amines from acid derivatives.
