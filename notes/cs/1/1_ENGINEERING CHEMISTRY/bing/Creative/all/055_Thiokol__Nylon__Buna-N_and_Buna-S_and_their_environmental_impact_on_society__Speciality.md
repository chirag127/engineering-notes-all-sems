# Thiokol, Nylon, Buna-N and Buna-S and their environmental impact on society, Speciality

- Thiokol is a synthetic rubber made from ethylene dichloride and sodium polysulfide. It is used as a sealant, adhesive, and binder for solid rocket propellants. It has high resistance to heat, cold, and chemicals, and low permeability to gases. It is also flexible and elastic. 
- Nylon is a synthetic polymer made from repeating units of amide linkages. It is used as a fiber for textiles, carpets, ropes, and parachutes, as well as a plastic for gears, bearings, and other parts. It has high strength, durability, and flexibility, and low moisture absorption. It is also resistant to abrasion, insects, and fungi. 
- Buna-N is a synthetic rubber made from acrylonitrile and butadiene. It is also known as nitrile rubber or NBR. It is used as a seal, hose, gasket, and glove material for various industries, such as automotive, oil, and medical. It has high resistance to oil, fuel, and other chemicals, and good abrasion and tear resistance. It is also resistant to heat, ozone, and weathering. 
- Buna-S is a synthetic rubber made from styrene and butadiene. It is also known as styrene butadiene rubber or SBR. It is used as a tire, conveyor belt, and shoe sole material, as well as a component of adhesives, coatings, and latex. It has good abrasion and wear resistance, and low rolling resistance. It is also resistant to water, acids, and alkalis. 

- The environmental impact of these synthetic materials on society is complex and multifaceted. Some of the positive impacts include:
  - Providing alternatives to natural materials that may be scarce, expensive, or inferior in performance.
  - Enhancing the functionality, safety, and efficiency of various products and applications.
  - Reducing the dependence on fossil fuels and other non-renewable resources.
  - Supporting the development of new technologies and innovations.
- Some of the negative impacts include:
  - Consuming large amounts of energy and water during production and processing.
  - Emitting greenhouse gases and other pollutants during manufacturing and disposal.
  - Generating waste and litter that may not be biodegradable or recyclable.
  - Contributing to the depletion of natural resources and the degradation of ecosystems.
  - Posing potential health and environmental risks due to the leaching of toxic substances or the accumulation of microplastics.
