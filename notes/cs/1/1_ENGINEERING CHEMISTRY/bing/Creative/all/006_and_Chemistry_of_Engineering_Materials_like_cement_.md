# Chemistry of Engineering Materials: Cement

Cement is a binding material that is widely used in construction and civil engineering. It is made by heating a mixture of raw materials, mainly limestone and clay, in a kiln at high temperatures. The resulting product, called clinker, is then ground with a small amount of gypsum to form a fine powder called cement.

## Composition of Cement

The main chemical compounds in cement are:

- **Tricalcium silicate (3CaO · SiO2)**: This is the most abundant and important compound in cement. It is responsible for the initial setting and early strength development of cement. It reacts with water to form calcium silicate hydrate (C-S-H) and calcium hydroxide (Ca(OH)2).
- **Dicalcium silicate (2CaO · SiO2)**: This is the second most abundant compound in cement. It reacts with water more slowly than tricalcium silicate, and contributes to the long-term strength and durability of cement. It also forms C-S-H and Ca(OH)2.
- **Tricalcium aluminate (3CaO · Al2O3)**: This is the third most abundant compound in cement. It reacts with water very rapidly, and can cause flash setting and cracking of cement. It forms calcium aluminate hydrate (C-A-H) and Ca(OH)2. To control its reactivity, a small amount of gypsum is added to the cement.
- **Tetracalcium aluminoferrite (4CaO · Al2O3Fe2O3)**: This is the fourth most abundant compound in cement. It reacts with water moderately, and forms complex hydrates with iron, aluminum, and sulfate ions. It influences the color and heat of hydration of cement.

## Types of Cement

There are different types of cement based on their composition, properties, and applications. Some of the common types are:

- **Portland cement**: This is the most widely used type of cement. It is composed of tricalcium silicate, dicalcium silicate, tricalcium aluminate, and tetracalcium aluminoferrite, with a small amount of gypsum. It has a high compressive strength and a moderate setting time. It is suitable for general purpose concrete and mortar.
- **Hydraulic cement**: This is a type of cement that can set and harden in the presence of water. It is composed of hydraulic compounds, such as calcium silicates, aluminates, and ferrites, that react with water to form insoluble and stable hydrates. It is resistant to water and chemical attack. It is used for underwater and marine structures, dams, and foundations.
- **Non-hydraulic cement**: This is a type of cement that can set and harden only by carbonation with the carbon dioxide in the air. It is composed of non-hydraulic compounds, such as lime, magnesia, and clay, that react with carbon dioxide to form carbonates. It has a low compressive strength and a long setting time. It is used for plastering and decorative purposes.
- **Blended cement**: This is a type of cement that is made by mixing Portland cement with one or more supplementary cementitious materials, such as fly ash, slag, silica fume, or natural pozzolans. These materials enhance the properties and performance of cement, such as reducing the heat of hydration, improving the workability, and increasing the durability. They also reduce the cost and environmental impact of cement production.
