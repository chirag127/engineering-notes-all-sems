# Graphite and Fullerene; Introduction, Structure and Applications

- Graphite and fullerene are two forms of carbon that have different structures and properties.
- Graphite is a soft, black, metallic material that consists of layers of hexagonal rings of carbon atoms. Graphite is a good conductor of electricity and heat, and has a high melting point. Graphite is used in pencils, lubricants, batteries, electrodes, and as a moderator in nuclear reactors.
- Fullerene is a family of carbon molecules that have hollow, cage-like shapes. The most common and stable fullerene is C60, also known as buckminsterfullerene or buckyball, which has 60 carbon atoms arranged in a spherical structure. Other fullerenes include C70, C76, C84, and nanotubes, which are cylindrical fullerenes.
- Fullerene has a similar structure to graphite, but it also has pentagonal or heptagonal rings that prevent the carbon sheet from being flat. Fullerene has a high symmetry and stability, and can form complexes with other molecules. Fullerene is a semiconductor and has unique optical, magnetic, and chemical properties .
- Fullerene has potential applications in various fields, such as electronics, medicine, nanotechnology, and materials science. Some examples of fullerene applications are:

  - Photovoltaic cells: Fullerene can be used as an electron acceptor in organic solar cells, which can convert sunlight into electricity.
  - Drug delivery: Fullerene can be functionalized with different groups to carry drugs or genes into cells, or to target specific diseases or organs.
  - Sensors: Fullerene can be used as a sensor for detecting various gases, molecules, or ions, due to its high sensitivity and selectivity.
  - Nanocomposites: Fullerene can be mixed with other materials, such as polymers, metals, or ceramics, to enhance their mechanical, electrical, or thermal properties.

- Graphene is a single layer of graphite, which is one atom thick. Graphene has a honeycomb structure of carbon atoms, and has extraordinary properties, such as high strength, flexibility, transparency, conductivity, and thermal stability. Graphene is considered as a wonder material that can revolutionize many fields and industries .
- Graphene has many possible applications, such as:

  - Transistors: Graphene can be used as a channel material in field-effect transistors, which can switch faster and consume less power than silicon-based transistors .
  - Solar cells: Graphene can be used as a transparent electrode in solar cells, which can reduce the cost and increase the efficiency of solar energy conversion .
  - Water purification: Graphene can be used as a membrane for water filtration, which can remove impurities, bacteria, and salt from water .
  - Sensors: Graphene can be used as a sensor for detecting various biomolecules, gases, or chemicals, due to its high surface area and sensitivity .
  - Batteries and supercapacitors: Graphene can be used as an electrode material in batteries and supercapacitors, which can store more energy and charge faster than conventional materials .
