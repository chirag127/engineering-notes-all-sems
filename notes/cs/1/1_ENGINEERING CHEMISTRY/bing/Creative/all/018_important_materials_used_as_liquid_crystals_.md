# Important Materials Used as Liquid Crystals

- Liquid crystals are substances that exhibit properties of both liquids and crystalline solids, such as fluidity, anisotropy, and birefringence.
- Liquid crystals can be classified into two main types: thermotropic and lyotropic, depending on whether their phase transitions depend on temperature or concentration, respectively.
- Thermotropic liquid crystals are composed of low molar mass or high molar mass organic molecules that have a rod-like or disc-like shape and can align themselves in different ways under external stimuli, such as electric fields or light.
- Some examples of thermotropic liquid crystals are:
  - Nematic liquid crystals, which have no positional order but a long-range orientational order of their molecules. They are used in liquid crystal displays (LCDs), optical switches, and sensors.
  - Smectic liquid crystals, which have a layered structure with a short-range positional order and a long-range orientational order of their molecules. They are used in ferroelectric displays, memory devices, and thermochromic materials.
  - Cholesteric liquid crystals, which have a helical arrangement of their molecules in each layer. They are used in reflective displays, color filters, and biosensors.
  - Columnar liquid crystals, which have a hexagonal or rectangular lattice of disc-like molecules. They are used in organic light-emitting diodes (OLEDs), photovoltaic cells, and nonlinear optics.
- Lyotropic liquid crystals are formed by amphiphilic molecules, such as surfactants, lipids, proteins, or DNA, that self-assemble into ordered structures in the presence of a solvent, usually water.
- Some examples of lyotropic liquid crystals are:
  - Micellar liquid crystals, which have spherical or cylindrical aggregates of surfactant molecules that are dispersed in a solvent. They are used in drug delivery, cosmetics, and detergents.
  - Lamellar liquid crystals, which have alternating layers of amphiphilic molecules and solvent. They are used in biological membranes, moisturizers, and lubricants.
  - Hexagonal liquid crystals, which have a honeycomb-like structure of cylindrical aggregates of amphiphilic molecules. They are used in nanomaterials, catalysts, and emulsions.
  - Cubic liquid crystals, which have a three-dimensional lattice of spherical or polyhedral aggregates of amphiphilic molecules. They are used in pharmaceuticals, food, and cosmetics.
- Some other materials that can form liquid crystals are:
  - Biological materials, such as myelin, DNA, gelatin, and tobacco mosaic virus, which have a rod-like or helical shape and can form nematic or cholesteric phases.
  - Metallotropic liquid crystals, which are composed of metal-containing organic or inorganic molecules that can form columnar or lamellar phases.
