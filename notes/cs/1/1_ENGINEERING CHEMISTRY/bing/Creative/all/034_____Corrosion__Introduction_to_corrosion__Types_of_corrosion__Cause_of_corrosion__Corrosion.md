# Corrosion: Introduction to corrosion, Types of corrosion, Cause of corrosion, Corrosion

## Introduction to corrosion
- Corrosion is the deterioration of a material, usually a metal, due to a chemical or electrochemical reaction with its environment.
- Corrosion can cause loss of material, mechanical strength, functionality, and aesthetic appeal of the metal.
- Corrosion can also pose safety, health, and environmental hazards if not prevented or controlled.
- Corrosion is a natural process that can be influenced by many factors, such as the type and composition of the metal, the nature and concentration of the corrosive agent, the temperature, the humidity, the presence of oxygen, the pH, the stress, and the time of exposure.

## Types of corrosion
- There are many types of corrosion, depending on the mechanism and morphology of the corrosion process. Some common types are:
  - Uniform corrosion: The metal surface corrodes at a uniform rate, resulting in a thin and even layer of corrosion products. This type of corrosion is easy to predict and measure, but it can cause significant material loss over time.
  - Galvanic corrosion: The metal surface corrodes at different rates due to the presence of a galvanic cell, which is formed when two dissimilar metals or metal alloys are in contact with each other and an electrolyte. The metal that acts as the anode corrodes faster than the metal that acts as the cathode. This type of corrosion can cause localized damage and failure of the metal structure.
  - Pitting corrosion: The metal surface corrodes at discrete points, resulting in small holes or pits that penetrate deep into the metal. This type of corrosion is difficult to detect and measure, but it can cause severe weakening and perforation of the metal structure.
  - Crevice corrosion: The metal surface corrodes at narrow gaps or crevices, where the access of oxygen and the removal of corrosion products are restricted. This type of corrosion can cause accelerated and localized damage to the metal structure.
  - Intergranular corrosion: The metal surface corrodes along the grain boundaries, where the microstructure and composition of the metal may differ from the bulk. This type of corrosion can cause embrittlement and cracking of the metal structure.
  - Stress corrosion cracking: The metal surface corrodes and cracks under the combined effect of tensile stress and a corrosive environment. This type of corrosion can cause sudden and catastrophic failure of the metal structure.
  - Corrosion fatigue: The metal surface corrodes and cracks under the combined effect of cyclic stress and a corrosive environment. This type of corrosion can cause reduced fatigue life and failure of the metal structure.
  - Erosion corrosion: The metal surface corrodes and wears away due to the mechanical action of a fluid or a solid particle that impinges on the metal. This type of corrosion can cause increased material loss and roughening of the metal surface.

## Cause of corrosion
- The cause of corrosion is the thermodynamic tendency of metals to revert to their more stable forms, such as oxides, hydroxides, sulfides, or carbonates, in the presence of a corrosive agent.
- The corrosive agent can be a chemical substance, such as an acid, a base, a salt, or a gas, that reacts with the metal and forms corrosion products.
- The corrosive agent can also be an electric current, which drives the oxidation and reduction reactions that occur at the metal surface and form corrosion products.
- The corrosion process can be represented by the following general equation:

  - Metal + Corrosive agent -> Corrosion products + Energy

- The energy released by the corrosion process can be in the form of heat, light, or electricity, depending on the type and rate of the corrosion process.

## Corrosion prevention and control
- Corrosion prevention and control are the methods and techniques used to reduce or eliminate the corrosion of metals and their structures.
- Corrosion prevention and control can be classified into four categories: material selection, design, protection, and maintenance.
  - Material selection: The choice of the most suitable metal or metal alloy for a given application, based on its corrosion resistance, mechanical properties, cost, and availability.
  - Design: The optimization of the shape, size, arrangement, and connection of the metal components, to minimize the exposure to corrosive agents, the formation of galvanic cells, the accumulation of stress, and the occurrence of erosion.
  - Protection: The application of a barrier, a coating, a cathodic protection, or an inhibitor, to isolate the metal from the corrosive environment, to modify the surface properties of the metal, to reduce the corrosion potential of the metal, or to interfere with the corrosion reaction.
