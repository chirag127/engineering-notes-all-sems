# Theoretical calculation of calorific value by Dulong’s method, Ranking of Coal, Analysis of coal

- Calorific value of coal is the amount of heat released by the complete combustion of a unit mass of coal. It is an important parameter to evaluate the quality and efficiency of coal.
- Dulong's method is a simple empirical formula that relates the calorific value of coal to its ultimate analysis, which is the percentage of carbon, hydrogen, oxygen, nitrogen and sulfur in coal.
- Dulong's formula is given by:

$$
CV = 338C + 1442(H - O/8) + 93S
$$

where CV is the calorific value in kJ/kg, and C, H, O, S are the percentages of carbon, hydrogen, oxygen and sulfur in coal, respectively .

- Dulong's formula assumes that the heat of combustion of an organic compound is nearly equal to the heats of combustion of the elements in it, multiplied by their percentage content in the compound.
- Dulong's formula is simple and easy to use, but it has some limitations and sources of error, such as:
  - It does not account for the moisture and ash content of coal, which affect the calorific value.
  - It does not account for the variation of the heats of combustion of different forms of carbon, such as graphite, coke and coal.
  - It does not account for the variation of the heats of combustion of different forms of hydrogen, such as free hydrogen, water vapor and hydrocarbons.
  - It does not account for the variation of the heats of combustion of different forms of sulfur, such as free sulfur, sulfides and sulfates.
  - It does not account for the variation of the heats of combustion of different forms of oxygen, such as free oxygen, carbon monoxide, carbon dioxide and water.
  - It does not account for the variation of the heats of combustion of different forms of nitrogen, such as free nitrogen, ammonia and nitric oxide.
- Therefore, Dulong's formula may not be accurate for all types of coals, especially those with high moisture, ash, volatile matter or fixed carbon content. Some modifications and corrections have been proposed to improve the accuracy of Dulong's formula, such as the modified Dulong formula and the Vandralek formula.

- Ranking of coal is the classification of coal according to its degree of metamorphism, or the change in its physical and chemical properties due to the action of heat and pressure over time. The rank of coal reflects its maturity, quality and potential uses.
- The main parameters used to rank coal are:
  - Moisture content: the percentage of water in coal. It affects the calorific value, handling and storage of coal. Higher rank coals have lower moisture content than lower rank coals.
  - Volatile matter: the percentage of coal that is converted into gas when heated in the absence of air. It affects the ignition, combustion and emission characteristics of coal. Higher rank coals have lower volatile matter than lower rank coals.
  - Fixed carbon: the percentage of coal that remains as solid residue after heating in the absence of air. It is the main source of heat in coal. Higher rank coals have higher fixed carbon than lower rank coals.
  - Ash content: the percentage of inorganic impurities in coal, such as minerals, clay and rock. It affects the calorific value, handling and disposal of coal. Higher rank coals have lower ash content than lower rank coals.
  - Carbon content: the percentage of carbon in coal. It is the main element that determines the calorific value of coal. Higher rank coals have higher carbon content than lower rank coals.
  - Hydrogen content: the percentage of hydrogen in coal. It is the second most important element that determines the calorific value of coal. Higher rank coals have lower hydrogen content than lower rank coals.
  - Oxygen content: the percentage of oxygen in coal. It is the third most important element that determines the calorific value of coal. Higher rank coals have lower oxygen content than lower rank coals.
