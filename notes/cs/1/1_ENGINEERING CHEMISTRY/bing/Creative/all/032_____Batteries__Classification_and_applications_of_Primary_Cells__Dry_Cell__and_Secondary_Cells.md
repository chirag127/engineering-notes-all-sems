# Batteries; Classification and Applications of Primary Cells (Dry Cell) and Secondary Cells

Batteries are devices that store chemical energy and convert it into electrical energy. They consist of one or more electrochemical cells that are connected in series or parallel. Each cell has a positive electrode (cathode), a negative electrode (anode), and an electrolyte that allows the flow of ions between the electrodes.

Batteries can be classified into two main types based on their ability to be recharged:

- Primary cells (or primary batteries) are non-rechargeable batteries that can only be used once. They have a limited amount of chemical reactants that are consumed during the discharge process. When the reactants are exhausted, the battery stops producing electricity and must be replaced or disposed of. Primary cells are usually cheaper, lighter, and have a higher energy density than secondary cells. However, they also have a shorter shelf life and a lower power output than secondary cells. Some examples of primary cells are zinc-carbon (dry cell), alkaline, lithium, and silver oxide batteries.

- Secondary cells (or secondary batteries) are rechargeable batteries that can be used multiple times. They have chemical reactants that can be restored by applying an external electric current. When the battery is fully charged, the reactants are in their original state and ready to be discharged again. Secondary cells are usually more expensive, heavier, and have a lower energy density than primary cells. However, they also have a longer shelf life and a higher power output than primary cells. Some examples of secondary cells are lead-acid, nickel-cadmium, nickel-metal hydride, and lithium-ion batteries.

Batteries can also be classified based on their application or use. Some common types of batteries and their uses are:

- Household batteries: These are the types of batteries that are more likely to be known to the common man. They find uses in a wide range of household appliances (such as torches, clocks, and cameras). These batteries can be further classified into two subcategories:

  - Dry cell batteries: These are primary cells that have a paste-like electrolyte that prevents leakage. They are also called zinc-carbon batteries because they use zinc as the anode and carbon as the cathode. They have a low voltage (1.5 V) and a low capacity. They are suitable for low-power devices that are used intermittently, such as remote controls, toys, and flashlights.

  - Alkaline batteries: These are primary cells that have an alkaline electrolyte (usually potassium hydroxide) that enhances the performance and shelf life of the battery. They also use zinc as the anode and manganese dioxide as the cathode. They have a higher voltage (1.5 V) and a higher capacity than dry cell batteries. They are suitable for high-power devices that are used frequently, such as digital cameras, radios, and portable music players.

- Industrial batteries: These are the types of batteries that are built to serve heavy-duty requirements. They are usually large, heavy, and expensive. They have a high voltage and a high capacity. They are used for applications that require a lot of power and reliability, such as backup power systems, electric vehicles, and renewable energy storage. These batteries can be further classified into two subcategories:

  - Lead-acid batteries: These are secondary cells that have a liquid electrolyte (usually sulfuric acid) that allows the flow of ions between the lead plates. They have a low voltage (2 V) and a high capacity. They are the oldest and most widely used type of rechargeable battery. They are suitable for applications that require a lot of current and a long cycle life, such as car batteries, uninterruptible power supplies, and solar power systems.

  - Lithium-ion batteries: These are secondary cells that have a solid or gel-like electrolyte that prevents leakage. They use lithium as the anode and various materials as the cathode, such as cobalt oxide, nickel oxide, or iron phosphate. They have a high voltage (3.6 V) and a high capacity. They are the most advanced and popular type of rechargeable battery. They are suitable for applications that require a lot of energy and a long shelf life, such as laptop batteries, mobile phones, and electric cars.

- Vehicle batteries: These are the types of batteries that are used to power various modes of transportation. They are usually secondary cells that have a high power output and a high durability.
