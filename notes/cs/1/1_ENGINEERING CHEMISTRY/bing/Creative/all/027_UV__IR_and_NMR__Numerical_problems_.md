# UV, IR and NMR, Numerical problems

UV, IR and NMR are spectroscopic techniques that can be used to identify organic compounds by analyzing their molecular structure and functional groups.

## UV spectroscopy

UV spectroscopy measures the absorption of ultraviolet radiation by a molecule. It is useful for studying compounds with conjugated pi systems, such as alkenes, aromatic rings, carbonyls, and nitro groups. The extent of conjugation affects the wavelength and intensity of the absorption peak.

The Beer-Lambert law relates the absorbance (A) of a solution to the concentration (c), path length (l), and molar absorptivity (ε) of the solute:

A = εcl

Molar absorptivity is a constant that depends on the nature of the molecule and the wavelength of the radiation. It can be used to compare the relative strengths of absorption peaks.

Example problem:

A solution of 1,3-butadiene has an absorbance of 0.45 at 217 nm in a 1 cm cell. The molar absorptivity of 1,3-butadiene at this wavelength is 20,000 L mol-1 cm-1. What is the concentration of the solution?

Solution:

Using the Beer-Lambert law, we can rearrange the equation to solve for c:

c = A / (εl)

Plugging in the given values, we get:

c = 0.45 / (20,000 x 1)

c = 0.0000225 mol L-1

## IR spectroscopy

IR spectroscopy measures the absorption of infrared radiation by a molecule. It is useful for identifying the functional groups present in a molecule, such as alcohols, amines, carboxylic acids, esters, ethers, aldehydes, ketones, and halides. Different functional groups have characteristic absorption frequencies that correspond to the stretching or bending vibrations of their bonds.

The absorption peaks are reported in units of wavenumber (cm-1), which is the reciprocal of the wavelength. The higher the wavenumber, the higher the energy of the radiation. The intensity of the peaks depends on the dipole moment change of the molecule during the vibration.

Example problem:

Identify the functional groups in the following IR spectrum of an organic compound with the molecular formula C4H8O2.

![IR spectrum](https://www.masterorganicchemistry.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/11/IR-spectrum-1.png)

Solution:

The broad peak at 3400 cm-1 indicates the presence of an O-H group, which could be either an alcohol or a carboxylic acid. The sharp peak at 1730 cm-1 indicates the presence of a C=O group, which could be either an aldehyde, a ketone, an ester, or a carboxylic acid. The absence of any peaks in the 2800-3000 cm-1 region indicates the absence of any aldehyde or ketone. The absence of any peaks in the 1000-1300 cm-1 region indicates the absence of any ester or ether. Therefore, the compound must be a carboxylic acid.

## NMR spectroscopy

NMR spectroscopy measures the absorption of radiofrequency radiation by a molecule in a magnetic field. It is useful for determining the number and types of hydrogen atoms (1H NMR) and carbon atoms (13C NMR) in a molecule, as well as their relative positions and environments.

The absorption peaks are reported in units of parts per million (ppm), which is the ratio of the frequency shift of the nucleus relative to a reference compound (usually tetramethylsilane, TMS). The higher the ppm, the more shielded the nucleus is from the magnetic field. The chemical shift of a nucleus depends on the electronegativity of the neighboring atoms and the magnetic induction of the adjacent bonds.

The area under the peak (integration) is proportional to the number of equivalent nuclei in the molecule. The splitting of the peak (multiplicity) is determined by the number of neighboring nuclei that are not equivalent to the nucleus of interest (n), according to the n+1 rule.
