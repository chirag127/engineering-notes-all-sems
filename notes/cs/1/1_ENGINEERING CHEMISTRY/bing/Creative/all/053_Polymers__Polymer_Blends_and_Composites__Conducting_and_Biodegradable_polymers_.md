# Polymers, Polymer Blends and Composites, Conducting and Biodegradable polymers

- Polymers are large molecules composed of repeating units called monomers, which are linked by covalent bonds. Polymers can have various properties depending on their chemical structure, molecular weight, degree of crystallinity, and crosslinking. Polymers are widely used in various fields such as plastics, rubber, textiles, coatings, adhesives, and biomedical applications.
- Polymer blends are mixtures of two or more polymers that are physically mixed but not chemically bonded. Polymer blends can improve the properties of individual polymers by combining their advantages and minimizing their disadvantages. For example, polymer blends can enhance the mechanical strength, thermal stability, processability, and biodegradability of polymers.
- Polymer composites are materials that consist of a polymer matrix and a reinforcing filler, such as fibers, particles, or nanomaterials. Polymer composites can have superior properties than the polymer matrix alone, such as high stiffness, strength, toughness, electrical conductivity, and thermal conductivity. Polymer composites are widely used in aerospace, automotive, construction, and biomedical industries.
- Conducting polymers are a class of polymers that can conduct electricity due to the presence of conjugated double bonds or delocalized electrons along the polymer backbone. Conducting polymers have attracted much attention for their potential applications in electronics, optoelectronics, sensors, actuators, and energy storage devices. However, most conducting polymers are non-degradable and may pose environmental and health risks.
- Biodegradable polymers are polymers that can be degraded by biological agents, such as microorganisms, enzymes, or hydrolysis, into harmless products. Biodegradable polymers have many advantages over non-degradable polymers, such as reducing waste, pollution, and toxicity, and enhancing biocompatibility and bioactivity. Biodegradable polymers can be classified into natural and synthetic polymers, depending on their origin. Natural biodegradable polymers include cellulose, starch, chitin, and proteins, while synthetic biodegradable polymers include polyesters, polyurethanes, and polyanhydrides.
- Biodegradable conducting polymers are a new type of polymers that combine the electrical conductivity of conducting polymers and the biodegradability of biodegradable polymers. Biodegradable conducting polymers can be synthesized by incorporating hydrolyzable linkages, such as ester, amide, or anhydride bonds, into the polymer backbone, or by blending or copolymerizing conducting polymers with biodegradable polymers. Biodegradable conducting polymers have promising applications in biomedical fields, such as tissue engineering, drug delivery, biosensors, and antibacterial activity.
