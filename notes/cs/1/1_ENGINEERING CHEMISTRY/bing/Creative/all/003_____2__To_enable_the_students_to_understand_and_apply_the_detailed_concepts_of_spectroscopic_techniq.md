- Spectroscopy is the study of the interaction of electromagnetic radiation with matter.
- Spectroscopic techniques are methods that use different types of radiation to obtain information about the structure, composition, and properties of matter.
- Spectroscopic techniques can be classified based on the type of radiation used, such as ultraviolet (UV), visible (VIS), infrared (IR), microwave (MW), radiofrequency (RF), nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR), X-ray, etc.
- Spectroscopic techniques can also be classified based on the type of interaction between radiation and matter, such as absorption, emission, scattering, reflection, refraction, diffraction, etc.
- Spectroscopic techniques can be used to identify, quantify, and characterize different types of molecules, atoms, ions, and solids.
- Spectroscopic techniques can be applied to various fields of science and engineering, such as chemistry, physics, biology, medicine, materials, geology, astronomy, etc.
- Spectroscopic techniques require a source of radiation, a sample of matter, a detector of radiation, and a data analysis system.
- Spectroscopic techniques involve the following steps:
  - Preparation of the sample and the source of radiation
  - Exposure of the sample to the radiation and measurement of the transmitted, reflected, or emitted radiation
  - Processing and interpretation of the spectral data
  - Comparison of the spectral data with reference data or theoretical models
  - Reporting and communication of the results and conclusions
