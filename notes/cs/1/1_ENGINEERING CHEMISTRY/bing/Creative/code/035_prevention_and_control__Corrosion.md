# Prevention and Control of Corrosion

Corrosion is the deterioration of a metal or its properties due to a chemical or electrochemical reaction with its environment. Corrosion can cause loss of material, strength, functionality, and aesthetics of metal structures and components. Corrosion can also pose safety and environmental hazards, such as leaks, fires, explosions, and pollution. Therefore, it is important to prevent and control corrosion in various applications and industries.

Some of the methods to prevent and control corrosion are:

- **Material selection**: Choosing a metal or alloy that is resistant to the corrosive environment or compatible with the other metals in contact. For example, stainless steel, aluminum, and copper are more corrosion-resistant than carbon steel, iron, and zinc. Some metals can also form a protective oxide layer on their surface, such as aluminum, titanium, and chromium, which can prevent further corrosion.
- **Coating**: Applying a layer of paint, plastic, metal, or ceramic on the metal surface to isolate it from the corrosive environment. Coatings can also provide aesthetic and functional benefits, such as color, texture, lubrication, and electrical insulation. Coatings should be selected based on the type and severity of the corrosion, the service conditions, and the cost and durability of the coating. Some examples of coatings are epoxy, polyurethane, zinc, nickel, and enamel.
- **Cathodic protection**: Applying an external electric current to the metal to make it the cathode of an electrochemical cell, which prevents the metal from corroding. Cathodic protection can be achieved by using a sacrificial anode, which is a more active metal that corrodes instead of the protected metal, or by using an impressed current, which is a direct current supplied by a power source. Cathodic protection is commonly used for pipelines, storage tanks, ships, bridges, and offshore structures.
- **Anodic protection**: Applying an external electric current to the metal to make it the anode of an electrochemical cell, which forms a protective oxide layer on the metal surface. Anodic protection can only be used for metals that have a passive region in their polarization curve, such as stainless steel, nickel, and titanium. Anodic protection is mainly used for chemical and food processing equipment, where the corrosive environment is acidic and oxidizing.
- **Corrosion inhibitors**: Adding chemicals to the corrosive environment to reduce the rate of corrosion. Corrosion inhibitors can act by forming a protective film on the metal surface, by scavenging the corrosive agents, or by altering the electrochemical reactions. Corrosion inhibitors can be classified into anodic, cathodic, mixed, or organic inhibitors, depending on their mode of action and chemical nature. Corrosion inhibitors are widely used for water treatment, oil and gas production, and industrial processes.
- **Design and maintenance**: Designing and maintaining the metal structures and components to minimize the exposure to the corrosive environment and to facilitate the inspection and repair of corrosion damage. Design and maintenance can include avoiding crevices, cracks, and sharp edges, providing adequate drainage and ventilation, using proper welding and joining techniques, selecting appropriate fasteners and gaskets, and applying periodic cleaning and coating. Design and maintenance can also involve monitoring the corrosion rate and performance of the metal using various techniques, such as visual inspection, weight loss measurement, electrochemical testing, and non-destructive testing.
