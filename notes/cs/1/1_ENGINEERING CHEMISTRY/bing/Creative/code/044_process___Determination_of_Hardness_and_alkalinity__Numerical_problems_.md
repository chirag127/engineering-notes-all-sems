# Process of Determination of Hardness and Alkalinity

Hardness and alkalinity are two important parameters of water quality that affect its suitability for various purposes. Hardness is the measure of the concentration of calcium and magnesium ions in water, which can cause scaling and corrosion problems in pipes and boilers. Alkalinity is the measure of the capacity of water to neutralize acids, which can affect the pH and buffering capacity of water.

There are different methods to determine the hardness and alkalinity of water, but one of the most common and simple methods is titration. Titration is a process of adding a known amount of a solution of known concentration (called the titrant) to a solution of unknown concentration (called the analyte) until a chemical reaction is complete. The point at which the reaction is complete is called the endpoint, and it can be detected by a change in color, pH, or electrical conductivity.

## Determination of Alkalinity

Alkalinity measurements are based on titration of a water sample to a designated pH using diluted sulfuric acid (0.1 N or 0.02 N H2SO4) as a titrant and a pH meter for measuring the pH. The steps are as follows:

- Fill a 100 mL volumetric flask with the water sample and record the initial volume.
- Add a few drops of phenolphthalein indicator to the flask. If the solution turns pink, it means that the water has some free alkalinity (due to the presence of hydroxide or carbonate ions). Record the initial pH.
- Start adding the acid titrant slowly and stir the solution until the pink color disappears. This is the first endpoint, and it corresponds to the pH of 8.3. Record the volume of the acid used and the pH.
- Continue adding the acid titrant until the pH reaches 4.5. This is the second endpoint, and it corresponds to the total alkalinity (due to the presence of hydroxide, carbonate, and bicarbonate ions). Record the final volume of the acid used and the pH.

The alkalinity can be calculated from the following formula:

Alkalinity (mg/L as CaCO3) = (Volume of acid used x Normality of acid x 50,000) / Volume of water sample

The factor of 50,000 is used to convert the units of normality (equivalents/L) to milligrams of calcium carbonate per liter.

## Determination of Hardness

Hardness measurements are based on titration of a water sample with a standard solution of ethylenediaminetetraacetic acid (EDTA) at pH 10, using a metal indicator such as Eriochrome Black T or Calmagite. The steps are as follows:

- Fill a 50 mL burette with the EDTA solution of known concentration (usually 0.01 M or 0.02 M).
- Fill a 250 mL Erlenmeyer flask with 50 mL of the water sample and add a few drops of buffer solution to adjust the pH to 10.
- Add a few drops of the metal indicator to the flask. The solution will turn red or wine red, indicating the presence of calcium and magnesium ions.
- Start adding the EDTA solution slowly and stir the solution until the color changes to blue or purple. This is the endpoint, and it indicates that all the calcium and magnesium ions have been complexed by the EDTA.
- Record the volume of the EDTA solution used.

The hardness can be calculated from the following formula:

Hardness (mg/L as CaCO3) = (Volume of EDTA used x Molarity of EDTA x 1,000 x 50.04) / Volume of water sample

The factor of 50.04 is the molecular weight of calcium carbonate.
