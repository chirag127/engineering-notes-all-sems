### 3. To enable the students to understand and apply the concepts related to Electrochemistry, Batteries, Corrosion

Electrochemistry is the branch of chemistry that studies the relationship between electricity and chemical reactions. Electrochemical reactions involve the transfer of electrons between substances, either in a solution or on a solid surface. Electrochemistry has many applications in various fields, such as energy, industry, medicine, and environment.

Some of the main concepts related to electrochemistry are:

- Electrochemical cells: devices that convert chemical energy into electrical energy or vice versa. There are two types of electrochemical cells: galvanic cells and electrolytic cells. Galvanic cells produce electricity from spontaneous redox reactions, while electrolytic cells use electricity to drive non-spontaneous redox reactions.
- Electrode potentials: the measure of the tendency of an electrode to gain or lose electrons. Electrode potentials depend on the nature of the electrode material, the concentration of the solution, the temperature, and the presence of other substances. The standard electrode potential is the electrode potential of a half-cell when all the reactants and products are in their standard states. The standard hydrogen electrode (SHE) is the reference electrode with a potential of zero volts.
- Cell potential: the difference in electrode potentials between the two electrodes of an electrochemical cell. Cell potential indicates the amount of electrical energy that can be obtained from or supplied to an electrochemical cell. The standard cell potential is the cell potential when all the reactants and products are in their standard states. The sign of the cell potential determines whether the cell is galvanic or electrolytic. A positive cell potential means that the cell is galvanic, while a negative cell potential means that the cell is electrolytic.
- Nernst equation: an equation that relates the cell potential to the concentration of the reactants and products. The Nernst equation shows that the cell potential decreases as the reaction proceeds towards equilibrium. The Nernst equation can be used to calculate the cell potential at any given condition, or to determine the equilibrium constant of an electrochemical reaction.
- Faraday's laws of electrolysis: two laws that describe the quantitative relationship between the amount of electricity passed through an electrolytic cell and the amount of substance produced or consumed at the electrodes. The first law states that the mass of a substance produced or consumed at an electrode is directly proportional to the amount of electricity passed through the cell. The second law states that the mass of a substance produced or consumed at an electrode is inversely proportional to its equivalent weight, which is the ratio of its molar mass to its charge.
- Batteries: electrochemical cells or systems of cells that provide a continuous source of electrical energy. Batteries consist of one or more galvanic cells connected in series or parallel. The electrodes and electrolytes of a battery are chosen to provide a high cell potential, a large capacity, and a long shelf life. Some common types of batteries are primary batteries, secondary batteries, and fuel cells. Primary batteries are disposable batteries that cannot be recharged, such as zinc-carbon and alkaline batteries. Secondary batteries are rechargeable batteries that can be used multiple times, such as lead-acid and lithium-ion batteries. Fuel cells are batteries that use external sources of fuel and oxidant, such as hydrogen and oxygen, to produce electricity and water.
- Corrosion: the deterioration of a metal or an alloy due to its reaction with the environment. Corrosion is an electrochemical process that involves the formation of anodic and cathodic sites on the metal surface, where oxidation and reduction reactions occur, respectively. The rate and extent of corrosion depend on the nature of the metal, the composition of the environment, the temperature, and the presence of inhibitors or protectors. Some common forms of corrosion are uniform corrosion, galvanic corrosion, pitting corrosion, crevice corrosion, and stress corrosion cracking. Corrosion can be prevented or reduced by various methods, such as coating, alloying, cathodic protection, and corrosion inhibitors.
