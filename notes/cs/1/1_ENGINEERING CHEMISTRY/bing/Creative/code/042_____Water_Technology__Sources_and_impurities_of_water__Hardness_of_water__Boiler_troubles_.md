### Water Technology: Sources and impurities of water, Hardness of water, Boiler troubles

- **Sources of water**: Water is a vital resource for human and industrial activities. Water can be obtained from various sources, such as surface water (rivers, lakes, ponds, etc.), groundwater (wells, springs, etc.), rainwater, seawater, etc. Each source has different characteristics and quality of water, depending on the natural and anthropogenic factors affecting it.
- **Impurities of water**: Water is rarely pure in nature. It contains various impurities, which can be classified into physical, chemical, and biological impurities. Physical impurities are the suspended solids, turbidity, color, odor, and taste of water. Chemical impurities are the dissolved solids, gases, minerals, salts, metals, organic compounds, and pollutants of water. Biological impurities are the microorganisms, such as bacteria, viruses, algae, fungi, protozoa, etc., that can cause diseases or spoilage of water.
- **Hardness of water**: Hardness of water is the property of water that prevents the lathering of soap. Hardness is caused by the presence of dissolved calcium and magnesium salts, such as bicarbonates, sulfates, chlorides, and nitrates. Hardness of water can be classified into temporary hardness and permanent hardness. Temporary hardness is due to the bicarbonates of calcium and magnesium, which can be removed by boiling or adding lime. Permanent hardness is due to the sulfates, chlorides, and nitrates of calcium and magnesium, which can be removed by adding soda or using ion exchange resins.
- **Boiler troubles**: Boiler troubles are the problems that arise due to the use of hard water in boilers. Boilers are the devices that produce steam by heating water. Hard water can cause various boiler troubles, such as scale formation, sludge formation, corrosion, priming, and foaming. Scale formation is the deposition of hard and adherent coating of calcium and magnesium salts on the inner walls of the boiler, which reduces the heat transfer efficiency and increases the fuel consumption. Sludge formation is the accumulation of soft and loose precipitates of calcium and magnesium salts at the bottom of the boiler, which obstructs the water circulation and causes overheating. Corrosion is the deterioration of the metal parts of the boiler due to the action of dissolved gases, such as oxygen and carbon dioxide, or acidic impurities, such as sulfates and chlorides. Priming is the carryover of water droplets along with the steam, which reduces the quality and quantity of steam and causes water hammering. Foaming is the formation of bubbles on the surface of water, which reduces the steam space and causes fluctuation of water level and pressure.
