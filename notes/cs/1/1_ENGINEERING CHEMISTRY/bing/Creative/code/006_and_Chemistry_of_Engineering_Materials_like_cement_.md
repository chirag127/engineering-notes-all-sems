# Chemistry of Engineering Materials: Cement

Cement is a binding material that is widely used in construction and civil engineering. It is made by heating a mixture of raw materials, mainly limestone and clay, in a kiln at high temperatures. The resulting product, called clinker, is then ground with a small amount of gypsum to form a fine powder called cement.

## Composition of Cement

The main chemical compounds that make up cement are:

- **Tricalcium silicate (3CaO · SiO2)**: This is the most abundant and important compound in cement. It is responsible for the initial setting and early strength development of cement. It reacts with water to form calcium silicate hydrate (C-S-H) and calcium hydroxide (Ca(OH)2).
- **Dicalcium silicate (2CaO · SiO2)**: This is the second most abundant compound in cement. It reacts with water more slowly than tricalcium silicate, and contributes to the long-term strength and durability of cement. It also forms C-S-H and Ca(OH)2.
- **Tricalcium aluminate (3CaO · Al2O3)**: This is the third most abundant compound in cement. It reacts with water very rapidly, and can cause flash setting and cracking of cement if not controlled. It forms calcium aluminate hydrate (C-A-H) and Ca(OH)2.
- **Tetracalcium aluminoferrite (4CaO · Al2O3 · Fe2O3)**: This is the fourth most abundant compound in cement. It reacts with water moderately, and forms complex hydrates with iron and aluminum. It influences the color and heat of hydration of cement.

## Types of Cement

There are different types of cement that are used for different purposes and applications. Some of the common types are:

- **Portland cement**: This is the most widely used type of cement, and is suitable for most general purposes. It is made by grinding clinker with 5% gypsum. It has a moderate setting time and strength development, and can resist sulfate attack and moderate heat of hydration.
- **Hydraulic cement**: This is a type of cement that can set and harden in the presence of water. It is made by adding certain additives to portland cement, such as blast furnace slag, fly ash, silica fume, or pozzolana. It has a lower heat of hydration and higher durability than portland cement.
- **Non-hydraulic cement**: This is a type of cement that can only set and harden by carbonation with the carbon dioxide in the air. It is made by calcining natural materials, such as lime or gypsum. It has a high early strength, but a low resistance to water and weathering.
- **White cement**: This is a type of cement that has a white color, and is used for aesthetic purposes. It is made by using raw materials that are low in iron and manganese, and by controlling the temperature and cooling rate of the clinker. It has similar properties to portland cement, but a higher cost.

## Hydration of Cement

Hydration is the chemical reaction between cement and water, which results in the formation of solid products that bind the aggregates together. Hydration is a complex and dynamic process that depends on many factors, such as the composition, fineness, temperature, and curing of the cement.

The main stages of hydration are:

- **Initial stage**: This is the stage when water is first added to cement, and the dissolution of the cement compounds begins. The water-cement ratio and the mixing time affect the initial stage. The initial stage lasts for a few minutes, and is followed by a dormant period.
