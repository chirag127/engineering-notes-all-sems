## Unit-2: 8

- This unit covers the topic of **linear equations** and their applications in various contexts.
- A linear equation is an algebraic equation that can be written in the form **ax + b = c**, where **a**, **b**, and **c** are constants and **x** is a variable.
- The **solution** of a linear equation is the value of **x** that makes the equation true. For example, the solution of **2x + 3 = 11** is **x = 4**, because **2(4) + 3 = 11**.
- To **solve** a linear equation, we can use different methods, such as **addition**, **subtraction**, **multiplication**, **division**, **distributive property**, and **combining like terms**. The goal is to isolate **x** on one side of the equation and a constant on the other side.
- A **system of linear equations** is a set of two or more linear equations that have the same variables. For example, **x + y = 5** and **2x - y = 3** are a system of linear equations.
- The **solution** of a system of linear equations is the ordered pair **(x, y)** that makes both equations true. For example, the solution of the system above is **(x, y) = (2, 3)**, because **2 + 3 = 5** and **2(2) - 3 = 3**.
- To **solve** a system of linear equations, we can use different methods, such as **graphing**, **substitution**, **elimination**, and **matrix methods**. The goal is to find the point of intersection of the graphs of the equations, or the values of **x** and **y** that satisfy both equations simultaneously.
- Linear equations can be used to model various real-world situations, such as **rate of change**, **slope-intercept form**, **point-slope form**, **standard form**, **parallel and perpendicular lines**, **scatter plots**, **correlation**, **line of best fit**, and **systems of inequalities**. These topics will be discussed in more detail in the following sections.
