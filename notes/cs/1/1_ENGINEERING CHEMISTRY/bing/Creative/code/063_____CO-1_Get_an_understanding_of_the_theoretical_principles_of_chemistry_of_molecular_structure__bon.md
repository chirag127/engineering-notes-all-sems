### CO-1 Get an understanding of the theoretical principles of chemistry of molecular structure, bonding and

- Molecular structure refers to the three-dimensional arrangement of atoms in a molecule, which determines its physical and chemical properties.
- Bonding refers to the process of forming chemical bonds between atoms, which involves the sharing or transfer of valence electrons.
- There are different types of chemical bonds, such as covalent, ionic, metallic, and hydrogen bonds, each with different characteristics and effects on the molecular structure.
- Theoretical principles of chemistry of molecular structure and bonding include:
  - Lewis structures and structural formulas, which are ways of representing the valence electrons and bonds in a molecule.
  - Formal charge, which describes the changes in the number of valence electrons as an atom becomes bonded into a molecule.
  - VSEPR theory, which predicts the molecular geometry based on the repulsion between electron pairs around a central atom.
  - Molecular orbital theory, which describes the formation of molecular orbitals from the combination of atomic orbitals, and their influence on the bond order, bond length, bond energy, and magnetic properties of a molecule.
  - Hybridization, which is the mixing of atomic orbitals to form hybrid orbitals that are better suited for bonding.
  - Electronegativity, which is a measure of the tendency of an atom to attract electrons in a bond.
  - Polarity, which is the uneven distribution of charge in a molecule due to the difference in electronegativity between the bonded atoms.
  - Intermolecular forces, which are the attractive or repulsive forces between molecules, such as dipole-dipole, hydrogen bonding, and London dispersion forces .
