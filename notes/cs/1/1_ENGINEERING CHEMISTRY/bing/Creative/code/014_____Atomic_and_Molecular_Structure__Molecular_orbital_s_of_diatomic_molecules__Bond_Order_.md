### Atomic and Molecular Structure: Molecular orbitals of diatomic molecules, bond order

- Molecular orbitals (MOs) are the wave functions that describe the distribution of electrons in a molecule. They are formed by the linear combination of atomic orbitals (AOs) of the atoms that constitute the molecule.
- The number of MOs is equal to the number of AOs that are combined. The MOs can be classified into two types: bonding and antibonding. Bonding MOs have lower energy and higher electron density between the nuclei, while antibonding MOs have higher energy and lower electron density between the nuclei.
- The MOs can be arranged in order of increasing energy, forming the molecular orbital diagram. The electrons are filled in the MOs according to the Aufbau principle, Hund's rule, and Pauli exclusion principle. The number of electrons in the bonding MOs minus the number of electrons in the antibonding MOs is called the bond order. The bond order indicates the strength and stability of the bond between the atoms.
- For diatomic molecules, the MOs can be derived from the AOs of the same symmetry. For example, the s orbitals of two atoms can combine to form a sigma (σ) bonding MO and a sigma star (σ*) antibonding MO. The p orbitals of two atoms can combine to form a sigma (σ) bonding MO, a sigma star (σ*) antibonding MO, and two pi (π) bonding MOs and two pi star (π*) antibonding MOs. The relative energies of the MOs depend on the overlap and the electronegativity of the AOs.
