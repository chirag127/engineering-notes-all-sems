# Properties, Chemistry of Advanced Materials (Liquid Crystals, Nanomaterials, Graphite & Fullerene)

## Liquid Crystals

- Liquid crystals are substances that exhibit both fluidity and long-range order.
- They are classified into thermotropic (temperature-dependent) and lyotropic (concentration-dependent) liquid crystals.
- They have different phases, such as nematic, smectic, cholesteric, and discotic, depending on the orientation and arrangement of the molecules.
- They have applications in displays, sensors, optical devices, and templates for advanced materials .
- They have properties such as birefringence, optical activity, electro-optic effect, and selective reflection.

## Nanomaterials

- Nanomaterials are materials that have at least one dimension in the range of 1-100 nm.
- They have unique physical, chemical, optical, and biological properties that differ from their bulk counterparts.
- They can be synthesized by various methods, such as top-down, bottom-up, self-assembly, and template-assisted approaches.
- They have applications in electronics, catalysis, energy, medicine, biotechnology, and environmental remediation.
- They can be tuned as desired by controlling the size, shape, synthesis conditions, and functionalization.

## Graphite

- Graphite is an allotrope of carbon that has a hexagonal layered structure.
- It has a high electrical conductivity, thermal stability, lubricity, and mechanical strength.
- It can be exfoliated into graphene, a single layer of carbon atoms with exceptional properties.
- It has applications in batteries, electrodes, lubricants, pencils, and composites.
- It has properties such as anisotropy, sp2 hybridization, delocalized electrons, and van der Waals forces.

## Fullerene

- Fullerene is a nanomaterial that is made of globular hollow cages of carbon atoms.
- It has various forms, such as C60, C70, nanotubes, and buckyballs.
- It has a high electron affinity, versatility, and structural diversity.
- It has applications in drug delivery, solar cells, sensors, and nanomedicine.
- It has properties such as aromaticity, superconductivity, photoluminescence, and radical scavenging.
