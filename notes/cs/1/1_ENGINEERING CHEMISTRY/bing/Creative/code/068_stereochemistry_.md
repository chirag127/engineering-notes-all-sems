# Stereochemistry

Stereochemistry is the branch of chemistry that deals with the three-dimensional arrangement of atoms and molecules and the effect of this on chemical reactions . Stereochemistry focuses on the relationships between stereoisomers, which are molecules that have the same molecular formula and sequence of bonded atoms, but differ in the orientation of their atoms in space.

Some of the main topics in stereochemistry are:

- **Chirality**: the property of a molecule that makes it non-superimposable on its mirror image. A molecule that has chirality is called a chiral molecule, and it can exist in two forms called enantiomers, which are mirror images of each other. Enantiomers have the same physical and chemical properties, except for their interaction with plane-polarized light and other chiral molecules .
- **Optical activity**: the ability of a chiral molecule to rotate the plane of polarization of plane-polarized light. The direction and degree of rotation depend on the structure and configuration of the molecule, the wavelength of light, the temperature, and the solvent. The specific rotation is a characteristic property of a chiral molecule, and it can be used to identify and quantify it .
- **Configuration**: the spatial arrangement of the atoms or groups in a chiral molecule, which can be determined by the relative positions of four different substituents attached to a stereogenic center (a carbon atom that is bonded to four different groups). The configuration of a chiral molecule can be assigned by using the Cahn-Ingold-Prelog (CIP) priority rules and the R/S notation .
- **Conformation**: the spatial arrangement of the atoms or groups in a molecule that can be changed by rotation about single bonds. The conformation of a molecule affects its physical and chemical properties, such as stability, reactivity, and intermolecular interactions. The conformation of a molecule can be represented by using Newman projections, sawhorse projections, or Fischer projections .
- **Stereoisomerism**: the phenomenon of having different stereoisomers of the same molecule. There are two types of stereoisomerism: configurational and conformational. Configurational stereoisomerism occurs when the molecules have different configurations and cannot be interconverted by rotation about single bonds. Conformational stereoisomerism occurs when the molecules have different conformations and can be interconverted by rotation about single bonds .
- **Stereochemical reactions**: the chemical reactions that involve changes in the stereochemistry of the reactants or products. Some examples of stereochemical reactions are: inversion, retention, racemization, resolution, asymmetric synthesis, and stereoselective reactions .
