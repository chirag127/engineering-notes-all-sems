### 2. To enable the students to understand and apply the detailed concepts of spectroscopic techniques and

- Spectroscopy is the study of the interaction of electromagnetic radiation with matter. It can be used to identify the structure, composition, and properties of molecules and materials.
- Spectroscopic techniques are based on the principle that different types of atoms and bonds absorb or emit radiation at specific wavelengths or frequencies, creating characteristic spectra.
- Spectroscopic techniques can be classified into different categories based on the type of radiation used, such as ultraviolet-visible (UV-Vis), infrared (IR), nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR), mass spectrometry (MS), etc.
- Spectroscopic techniques can also be classified based on the type of information obtained, such as absorption, emission, scattering, fluorescence, etc.
- Spectroscopic techniques can be applied to various fields of science and engineering, such as chemistry, physics, biology, medicine, materials science, environmental science, etc.
- Spectroscopic techniques can be used to analyze the structure and function of molecules, such as proteins, DNA, drugs, polymers, etc.
- Spectroscopic techniques can also be used to measure the concentration, purity, and reaction kinetics of substances, such as pollutants, catalysts, enzymes, etc.
- Spectroscopic techniques can provide qualitative and quantitative information about the samples, depending on the type and accuracy of the instrument and the method of analysis.
