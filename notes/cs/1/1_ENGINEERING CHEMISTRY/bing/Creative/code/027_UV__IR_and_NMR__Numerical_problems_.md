# UV, IR and NMR, Numerical problems

UV, IR and NMR are spectroscopic techniques that can be used to identify the structure and properties of organic molecules.

## UV spectroscopy

UV spectroscopy measures the absorption of ultraviolet (UV) light by a molecule. The absorption of UV light causes electronic transitions from the ground state to the excited state of the molecule. The wavelength and intensity of the absorbed light depend on the structure and conjugation of the molecule.

### Numerical problem

A compound with molecular formula C<sub>6</sub>H<sub>6</sub>O has a UV absorption peak at 270 nm. What is the structure of the compound?

#### Solution

The compound is likely to be a phenol, since phenols have a characteristic UV absorption peak around 270 nm due to the π-π* transition of the aromatic ring. The structure of the compound is:

## IR spectroscopy

IR spectroscopy measures the absorption of infrared (IR) light by a molecule. The absorption of IR light causes vibrational transitions of the molecular bonds. The frequency and intensity of the absorbed light depend on the type, strength and environment of the bonds.

### Numerical problem

A compound with molecular formula C<sub>4</sub>H<sub>8</sub>O has an IR absorption peak at 1720 cm<sup>-1</sup>. What is the structure of the compound?

#### Solution

## NMR spectroscopy

NMR spectroscopy measures the absorption of radiofrequency (RF) light by a molecule in a magnetic field. The absorption of RF light causes nuclear spin transitions of the atoms in the molecule. The frequency and intensity of the absorbed light depend on the magnetic environment and the number of neighboring atoms of the nuclei.

### Numerical problem

A compound with molecular formula C<sub>4</sub>H<sub>8</sub>O has the following <sup>1</sup>H NMR spectrum:

![NMR spectrum](https://i.imgur.com/9yYJX6i.png)

What is the structure of the compound?

#### Solution

The compound has four different types of protons, as indicated by the four peaks in the spectrum. The peak at 3.6 ppm corresponds to the proton attached to the oxygen atom, since it is the most deshielded by the electronegative oxygen. The peak at 2.2 ppm corresponds to the protons adjacent to the carbonyl group, since they are also deshielded by the partial positive charge on the carbon. The peak at 1.2 ppm corresponds to the methyl protons at the end of the chain, since they are the most shielded by the electron-donating alkyl groups. The peak at 0.9 ppm corresponds to the other methyl protons, which are slightly less shielded than the ones at 1.2 ppm. The structure of the compound is the same as the one obtained from IR spectroscopy:
