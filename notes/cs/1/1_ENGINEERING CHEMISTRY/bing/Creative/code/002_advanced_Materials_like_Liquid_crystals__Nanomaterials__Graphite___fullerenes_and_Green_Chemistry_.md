# Advanced Materials

Advanced materials are materials that have novel properties or functions that enable new applications or improved performance. Some examples of advanced materials are liquid crystals, nanomaterials, graphite, fullerenes and green chemistry.

## Liquid Crystals

Liquid crystals are substances that have properties of both liquids and crystals. They can flow like liquids, but also have an ordered structure like crystals. Liquid crystals can be classified into different types based on their molecular orientation and alignment, such as nematic, smectic, cholesteric and discotic. Liquid crystals have many applications in displays, sensors, optical devices, thermometers and cosmetics.

## Nanomaterials

Nanomaterials are materials that have at least one dimension in the range of 1 to 100 nanometers. Nanomaterials can have unique physical, chemical, optical, electrical and magnetic properties due to their small size and high surface area. Nanomaterials can be synthesized by various methods, such as top-down, bottom-up, self-assembly, template-assisted and biological routes. Nanomaterials have many applications in electronics, medicine, energy, environment, catalysis and nanotechnology.

## Graphite

Graphite is a form of carbon that consists of layers of hexagonal rings of carbon atoms. Graphite has a high electrical conductivity, thermal conductivity, lubricity and chemical stability. Graphite can be used as an electrode, a lubricant, a moderator in nuclear reactors, a filler in composite materials and a precursor for other carbon-based materials.

## Fullerenes

Fullerenes are another form of carbon that consists of spherical, cylindrical or ellipsoidal cages of carbon atoms. The most common fullerene is C60, also known as buckminsterfullerene or buckyball, which has a shape of a soccer ball. Fullerenes have a high strength, stability, flexibility and reactivity. Fullerenes can be used as nanocarriers, antioxidants, catalysts, sensors, photovoltaics and superconductors.

## Green Chemistry

Green chemistry is the design of chemical products and processes that reduce or eliminate the use or generation of hazardous substances. Green chemistry aims to minimize the environmental impact, health risk and waste generation of chemical activities. Green chemistry can be achieved by following some principles, such as prevention, atom economy, less hazardous synthesis, safer solvents, energy efficiency, renewable feedstocks, waste reduction and green design. Green chemistry can benefit the society, the economy and the environment.
