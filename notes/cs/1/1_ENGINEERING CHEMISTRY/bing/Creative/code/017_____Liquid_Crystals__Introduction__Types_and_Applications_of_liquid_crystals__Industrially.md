### Liquid Crystals; Introduction, Types and Applications of liquid crystals, Industrially

Liquid crystals are substances that exhibit a phase of matter that is between a solid and a liquid. They have some degree of molecular order, such as orientation or alignment, but also some degree of mobility or fluidity. Liquid crystals can be manipulated by external stimuli, such as electric fields, temperature, pressure, or light, to change their optical, electrical, or mechanical properties. Liquid crystals have many applications in various fields, such as displays, sensors, lasers, medical diagnostics, and drug delivery.

#### Introduction to liquid crystals

- Liquid crystals can be classified into two main categories: thermotropic and lyotropic .
- Thermotropic liquid crystals are formed by heating a crystalline solid above its melting point. The molecules in the solid become more mobile and less ordered, but still retain some orientation or alignment. Thermotropic liquid crystals can be further divided into different types based on the degree and symmetry of their molecular order, such as nematic, smectic, cholesteric, or discotic .
- Lyotropic liquid crystals are formed by dissolving certain molecules, such as amphiphiles, polymers, or biological macromolecules, in a solvent, such as water or organic solvents. The molecules in the solution self-assemble into ordered structures, such as micelles, vesicles, or lamellae, depending on the concentration, temperature, and solvent properties. Lyotropic liquid crystals can also exhibit different types of molecular order, such as nematic, smectic, or hexagonal .
- Liquid crystals can also be classified based on their response to external stimuli, such as electric fields, magnetic fields, temperature, pressure, or light. Some liquid crystals are more sensitive or responsive than others, and can change their optical, electrical, or mechanical properties accordingly. For example, some liquid crystals can change their color, transparency, reflectivity, or birefringence when subjected to an electric field or a temperature change. These liquid crystals are called electro-optic or thermo-optic liquid crystals, respectively .

#### Types of liquid crystals

- Nematic liquid crystals are the simplest and most common type of thermotropic liquid crystals. They have no positional order, but have a preferred direction of orientation, called the director. The molecules in a nematic liquid crystal can rotate around the director, but not along it. Nematic liquid crystals can be uniaxial or biaxial, depending on whether they have one or two distinct directors .
- Smectic liquid crystals are more ordered than nematic liquid crystals. They have both orientational and positional order, meaning that the molecules are arranged in layers, with each layer having a preferred direction of orientation. The layers can slide past each other, but not within each layer. Smectic liquid crystals can be further subdivided into different types based on the arrangement and orientation of the layers and the molecules within them, such as smectic A, smectic C, smectic I, or smectic F .
- Cholesteric liquid crystals are a special type of nematic liquid crystals that have a chiral twist. The molecules in a cholesteric liquid crystal are arranged in layers, like in a smectic liquid crystal, but the director in each layer is slightly rotated with respect to the adjacent layer, forming a helical structure. The pitch of the helix determines the optical properties of the cholesteric liquid crystal, such as its selective reflection of circularly polarized light or its ability to form textures or patterns .
- Discotic liquid crystals are formed by disc-shaped molecules, such as benzene rings or phthalocyanines, that stack on top of each other, forming columns. The columns can have different types of order, such as nematic, smectic, or hexagonal, depending on the orientation and arrangement of the discs within them. Discotic liquid crystals have unique electrical and optical properties, such as high charge carrier mobility or self-assembly into nanowires or nanotubes .
