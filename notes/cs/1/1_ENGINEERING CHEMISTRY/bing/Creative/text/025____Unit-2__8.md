## Unit-2: 8

- Unit-2: 8 is a topic in mathematics that covers the concepts of **exponents** and **scientific notation**.
- An exponent is a small number written above and to the right of a base number, indicating how many times the base number is multiplied by itself.
- For example, 2^3^ means 2 multiplied by itself 3 times, which is equal to 8.
- Scientific notation is a way of writing very large or very small numbers using powers of 10.
- For example, 3.6 x 10^6^ means 3.6 multiplied by 10 6 times, which is equal to 3,600,000.
- Scientific notation is useful for expressing numbers that are too big or too small to write in standard form.
- Some rules for working with exponents and scientific notation are:
  - To multiply two numbers with the same base, add their exponents. For example, 2^3^ x 2^4^ = 2^(3+4)^ = 2^7^.
  - To divide two numbers with the same base, subtract their exponents. For example, 2^5^ / 2^2^ = 2^(5-2)^ = 2^3^.
  - To raise a power to another power, multiply their exponents. For example, (2^3^)^4^ = 2^(3x4)^ = 2^12^.
  - To write a number in scientific notation, move the decimal point so that there is only one digit before it, and multiply by a power of 10 that indicates how many places the decimal point moved. For example, 0.00045 = 4.5 x 10^-4^, and 123,000 = 1.23 x 10^5^.
  - To convert a number from scientific notation to standard form, move the decimal point according to the power of 10. For example, 4.5 x 10^-4^ = 0.00045, and 1.23 x 10^5^ = 123,000.
