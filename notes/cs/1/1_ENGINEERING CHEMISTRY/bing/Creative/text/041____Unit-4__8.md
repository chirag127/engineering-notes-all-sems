## Unit-4: 8

- This unit covers the topic of **linear equations** and their applications in various contexts.
- A linear equation is an equation of the form **ax + b = c**, where **a**, **b**, and **c** are constants and **x** is a variable.
- The solution of a linear equation is the value of **x** that makes the equation true. For example, the solution of **2x + 3 = 11** is **x = 4**.
- To solve a linear equation, we can use the following steps:
  - Simplify both sides of the equation by combining like terms and applying the distributive property if needed.
  - Isolate the variable on one side of the equation by adding, subtracting, multiplying, or dividing both sides by the same number.
  - Check the solution by substituting it back into the original equation and verifying that both sides are equal.
- A linear equation can represent a real-world situation, such as a relationship between two quantities, a cost function, a rate of change, or a balance point. For example, the equation **y = 3x + 5** can represent the cost **y** of renting a car for **x** days at $3 per day plus a $5 fee.
- To model a real-world situation with a linear equation, we can use the following steps:
  - Identify the two quantities that are related and assign them variables, such as **x** and **y**.
  - Find the slope of the line that represents the rate of change between the two quantities, such as **m**.
  - Find the y-intercept of the line that represents the initial or fixed value of one quantity, such as **b**.
  - Write the equation of the line in the form **y = mx + b** or **ax + b = c**.
  - Interpret the meaning of the slope and the y-intercept in the context of the problem.
- A linear equation can also be used to find the value of one quantity given the value of another quantity, or to compare two different situations. For example, we can use the equation **y = 3x + 5** to find the cost of renting a car for 7 days, or to compare the cost of renting a car with another company that charges $4 per day plus a $2 fee.
