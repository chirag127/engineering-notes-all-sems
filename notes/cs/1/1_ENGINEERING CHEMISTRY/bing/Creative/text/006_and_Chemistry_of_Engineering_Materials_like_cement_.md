# Chemistry of Engineering Materials: Cement

- Cement is a powdery substance that binds and hardens other materials together when mixed with water.
- Cement is mainly used to make mortar and concrete, which are essential for construction and civil engineering.
- Cement can be classified into two types based on how it sets and hardens: hydraulic cement and non-hydraulic cement.
  - Hydraulic cement hardens due to the chemical reaction of water with the cement compounds, such as silicates and aluminates. It can be used underwater or in moist conditions. Portland cement is the most common type of hydraulic cement.
  - Non-hydraulic cement hardens by absorbing carbon dioxide from the air, such as lime and gypsum. It cannot be used underwater or in moist conditions, as it will dissolve or crumble.
- The chemical composition of Portland cement, the most widely used cement, consists of four main compounds:
  - Tricalcium silicate (3CaO · SiO<sub>2</sub>), which contributes to the early strength and heat of hydration of cement.
  - Dicalcium silicate (2CaO · SiO<sub>2</sub>), which contributes to the long-term strength and durability of cement.
  - Tricalcium aluminate (3CaO · Al<sub>2</sub>O<sub>3</sub>), which reacts rapidly with water and causes flash setting and shrinkage of cement.
  - Tetracalcium aluminoferrite (4CaO · Al<sub>2</sub>O<sub>3</sub>Fe<sub>2</sub>O<sub>3</sub>), which influences the color and resistance to sulfates of cement.
- The cement clinker is produced by heating a mixture of raw materials, such as limestone, clay, iron ore, and sand, in a rotary kiln at a high temperature. The clinker is then ground with gypsum and other additives to make the final product of cement.
- The hydration of cement is the process of chemical reaction between water and cement compounds, which results in the formation of a solid gel-like material called the cement paste. The cement paste binds the aggregates together and fills the pores and voids in the concrete.
- The hydration of cement is influenced by various factors, such as the water-cement ratio, the temperature, the curing time, the type and amount of additives, and the environmental conditions. The hydration of cement is not a simple process, but involves complex molecular interactions and transformations.
