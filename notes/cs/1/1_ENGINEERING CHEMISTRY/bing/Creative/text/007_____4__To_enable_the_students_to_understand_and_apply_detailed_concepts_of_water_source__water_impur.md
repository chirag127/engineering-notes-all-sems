### 4. To enable the students to understand and apply detailed concepts of water source, water impurities, hardness

- Water source is the origin of water that is used for various purposes such as drinking, irrigation, industry, etc. Water sources can be classified into surface water and groundwater.
- Surface water is the water that collects on the land surface, such as rivers, lakes, ponds, reservoirs, etc. Surface water is influenced by factors such as precipitation, evaporation, runoff, and human activities.
- Groundwater is the water that exists below the land surface, in the pores and cracks of rocks and soil. Groundwater is replenished by infiltration of rainwater and snowmelt, and discharged by springs, wells, and seepage into surface water bodies. Groundwater is less affected by surface conditions, but more by geology and hydrology.
- Water impurities are the substances that are present in water other than water molecules. Water impurities can be classified into dissolved solids, dissolved gases, suspended solids, colloidal particles, and microorganisms.
- Dissolved solids are the salts and minerals that are dissolved in water, such as sodium, calcium, magnesium, chloride, sulfate, carbonate, etc. Dissolved solids affect the taste, hardness, and conductivity of water.
- Dissolved gases are the gases that are dissolved in water, such as oxygen, carbon dioxide, nitrogen, hydrogen sulfide, etc. Dissolved gases affect the pH, corrosion, and biological activity of water.
- Suspended solids are the particles that are not dissolved in water, but are small enough to remain suspended by the water turbulence, such as sand, silt, clay, organic matter, etc. Suspended solids affect the turbidity, color, and sedimentation of water.
- Colloidal particles are the particles that are not dissolved in water, but are too small to be seen by the naked eye or to settle by gravity, such as bacteria, viruses, algae, fungi, etc. Colloidal particles affect the stability, coagulation, and filtration of water.
- Microorganisms are the living organisms that are present in water, such as bacteria, viruses, algae, fungi, protozoa, etc. Microorganisms affect the health, quality, and treatment of water.
- Hardness is the property of water that causes it to form scales or deposits when heated or to interfere with the action of soap or detergent. Hardness is mainly caused by the presence of dissolved calcium and magnesium ions in water.
- Hardness can be classified into temporary hardness and permanent hardness. Temporary hardness is the hardness that can be removed by boiling or by adding lime. Permanent hardness is the hardness that cannot be removed by boiling or by adding lime, but by other methods such as ion exchange or reverse osmosis.
- Hardness can be measured by various methods, such as titration, soap test, conductivity, etc. Hardness can be expressed in various units, such as milligrams per liter (mg/L), grains per gallon (gpg), parts per million (ppm), etc.
