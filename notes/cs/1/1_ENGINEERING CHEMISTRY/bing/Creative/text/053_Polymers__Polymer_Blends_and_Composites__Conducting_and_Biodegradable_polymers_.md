# Polymers, Polymer Blends and Composites, Conducting and Biodegradable polymers

- Polymers are large molecules composed of repeating units called monomers, which are linked by covalent bonds. Polymers can have various properties depending on their chemical structure, molecular weight, degree of crystallinity, and crosslinking.
- Polymer blends are mixtures of two or more polymers that are physically mixed but not chemically bonded. Polymer blends can improve the properties of individual polymers by combining their advantages and reducing their disadvantages. For example, polymer blends can enhance the mechanical strength, thermal stability, biocompatibility, or processability of polymers.
- Polymer composites are materials that consist of a polymer matrix and a reinforcing phase, such as fibers, particles, or nanomaterials. Polymer composites can have superior properties than the polymer matrix alone, such as high stiffness, strength, toughness, electrical conductivity, or thermal conductivity. Polymer composites are widely used in various applications, such as aerospace, automotive, biomedical, and energy.
- Conducting polymers are a class of polymers that can conduct electricity due to the presence of conjugated double bonds or delocalized electrons along the polymer backbone. Conducting polymers can have tunable electrical, optical, magnetic, and electrochemical properties by doping, blending, or forming composites with other materials. Conducting polymers have many potential applications in biosensors, tissue engineering, drug delivery, and antibacterial activity.
- Biodegradable polymers are polymers that can be degraded by biological agents, such as microorganisms or enzymes, into harmless products, such as water, carbon dioxide, or biomass. Biodegradable polymers can reduce the environmental impact of plastic waste and can be used for biomedical purposes, such as implants, sutures, or drug delivery systems. Biodegradable polymers can have hydrolyzable linkages, such as ester, amide, or anhydride bonds, along the polymer chain that are susceptible to cleavage by water or enzymes.
