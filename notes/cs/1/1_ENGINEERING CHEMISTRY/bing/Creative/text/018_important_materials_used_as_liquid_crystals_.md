# Important Materials Used as Liquid Crystals

- Liquid crystals are substances that exhibit properties of both liquids and crystalline solids, such as fluidity, anisotropy, and birefringence.
- Liquid crystals can be classified into two main types: thermotropic and lyotropic .
- Thermotropic liquid crystals are temperature-dependent and change their phase and orientation according to the external temperature .
- Thermotropic liquid crystals can be further divided into high molar mass materials and low molar mass materials.
- High molar mass materials are suitable for polymer applications and include polyesters, polyamides, polyacrylates, and polysiloxanes.
- Low molar mass materials are used for display devices and optical switches and include aromatic compounds, such as azobenzenes, biphenyls, terphenyls, and cyanobiphenyls.
- Lyotropic liquid crystals are concentration-dependent and form different phases and orientations depending on the ratio of amphiphilic molecules and hydrophilic solvents.
- Amphiphilic molecules are molecules that have both hydrophobic and hydrophilic parts, such as long-chain alcohols, deoxyribonucleic acid (DNA), gelatin, surfactants, and membrane phospholipids .
- Hydrophilic solvents are solvents that can dissolve or mix with water, such as water itself, alcohols, and glycols .
- Lyotropic liquid crystals are found in many biological materials, such as myelin, a fatty material extracted from nerve cells, and tobacco mosaic virus, a rodlike virus that forms a nematic phase .
