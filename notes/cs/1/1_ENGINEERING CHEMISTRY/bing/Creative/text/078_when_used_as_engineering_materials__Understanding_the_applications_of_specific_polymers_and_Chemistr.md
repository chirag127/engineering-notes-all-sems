# Polymers: Engineering Materials, Applications and Chemistry

Polymers are long chain macromolecules built from repeated smaller chemical structures or ‘-mers’. They are found widely in the natural world (e.g. proteins, rubber, cellulose) and, increasingly the past century, in synthetic form (e.g. “plastics”). Polymers have many attractive properties, such as monodispersity, biocompatibility, controlled composition and chain length, and tunable chemical properties. Polymers can also be endowed with functions by using certain special preparation methods or by introducing functional groups or fillers into material.

Some of the applications of polymers as engineering materials are:

- In aircraft, aerospace, and sports equipment: Polymers can be used to make lightweight, strong, and durable materials that can withstand high stress and temperature. Examples are carbon fiber reinforced polymers (CFRP), epoxy resins, and polyimides.
- In 3D printing plastics: Polymers can be used to create complex shapes and structures with high precision and accuracy. Examples are acrylonitrile butadiene styrene (ABS), polylactic acid (PLA), and polyethylene terephthalate glycol (PETG).
- In biopolymers and biomolecules: Polymers can be used to mimic natural structures and functions in biological systems. Examples are DNA, proteins, polysaccharides, and lipids. They can also be used for molecular recognition, drug delivery, biosensor devices, tissue engineering, and cosmetics .
- In holography: Polymers can be used to create holograms that can store and display information in three dimensions. Examples are photopolymers, dichromated gelatin, and thermoplastic polymers.
- In water purification: Polymers can be used to remove contaminants and impurities from water. Examples are polyacrylamide, polyvinyl alcohol, and chitosan.
- In printed circuit board substrates: Polymers can be used to provide electrical insulation and mechanical support for electronic components. Examples are polyimide, epoxy, and polytetrafluoroethylene (PTFE).
- In green chemicals: Polymers and biopolymers can be used to replace petroleum-based chemicals and reduce environmental impact. Examples are polylactic acid (PLA), polyhydroxyalkanoates (PHA), and cellulose .

The chemistry of polymers involves the synthesis, characterization, and modification of polymer chains and structures. Some of the important concepts and methods in polymer chemistry are:

- Polymerization: The process of forming polymer chains from monomers. There are two main types of polymerization: chain-growth and step-growth. Chain-growth polymerization involves the addition of monomers to the end of a growing chain, while step-growth polymerization involves the condensation of monomers with the elimination of a small molecule.
- Copolymerization: The process of forming polymer chains from two or more different types of monomers. There are different types of copolymers, such as random, alternating, block, and graft copolymers, depending on the arrangement of the monomers along the chain.
- Crosslinking: The process of forming covalent bonds between polymer chains to create a network structure. Crosslinking can increase the strength, rigidity, and stability of polymers. Examples of crosslinked polymers are vulcanized rubber, bakelite, and epoxy.
- Polymer degradation: The process of breaking down polymer chains due to chemical, thermal, mechanical, or biological factors. Polymer degradation can affect the properties and performance of polymers. Examples of polymer degradation are oxidation, hydrolysis, photodegradation, and biodegradation.
- Polymer characterization: The process of determining the structure, composition, molecular weight, and properties of polymers. Some of the techniques used for polymer characterization are spectroscopy, chromatography, microscopy, rheology, and thermal analysis.
- Polymer modification: The process of changing the properties and functions of polymers by introducing chemical or physical changes. Some of the methods used for polymer modification are functionalization, blending, grafting, and doping.
