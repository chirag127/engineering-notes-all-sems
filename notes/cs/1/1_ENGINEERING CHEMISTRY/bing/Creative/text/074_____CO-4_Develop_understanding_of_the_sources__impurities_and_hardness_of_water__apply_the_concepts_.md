### CO-4 Develop understanding of the sources, impurities and hardness of water, apply the concepts of

- Sources of water: Water is a natural resource that is essential for life. Water can be obtained from various sources, such as surface water (rivers, lakes, ponds, etc.), groundwater (wells, springs, aquifers, etc.), rainwater (collected from roofs, tanks, etc.), seawater (desalinated by reverse osmosis, distillation, etc.), and wastewater (treated by biological, chemical, or physical methods).
- Impurities of water: Water is rarely pure in nature. It contains various impurities, such as dissolved solids (salts, minerals, metals, etc.), dissolved gases (oxygen, carbon dioxide, nitrogen, etc.), suspended solids (clay, silt, sand, etc.), colloidal particles (organic matter, bacteria, viruses, etc.), and dissolved organic compounds (pesticides, herbicides, pharmaceuticals, etc.). These impurities can affect the quality, taste, odor, color, and suitability of water for various purposes.
- Hardness of water: Hardness of water is a measure of the concentration of calcium and magnesium ions in water. These ions form insoluble compounds with soap, causing scum and reducing the lathering ability of soap. Hard water also causes scaling and corrosion of pipes, boilers, and appliances. Hardness of water can be expressed in various units, such as parts per million (ppm), milligrams per liter (mg/L), grains per gallon (gpg), or degrees of hardness (dH). Hardness of water can be classified as soft (< 60 ppm), moderately hard (60-120 ppm), hard (120-180 ppm), or very hard (> 180 ppm).
- Concepts of water treatment: Water treatment is the process of removing or reducing the impurities of water to make it suitable for various purposes, such as drinking, irrigation, industrial use, etc. Water treatment can involve various methods, such as coagulation and flocculation (adding chemicals to form larger particles that can be settled or filtered), sedimentation (allowing the heavier particles to settle at the bottom of a tank), filtration (passing water through a porous medium to remove suspended solids and colloids), disinfection (killing or inactivating microorganisms by chlorine, ozone, ultraviolet light, etc.), softening (removing or replacing calcium and magnesium ions by ion exchange, lime-soda process, etc.), and membrane processes (separating water from dissolved solids by reverse osmosis, nanofiltration, ultrafiltration, etc.).
