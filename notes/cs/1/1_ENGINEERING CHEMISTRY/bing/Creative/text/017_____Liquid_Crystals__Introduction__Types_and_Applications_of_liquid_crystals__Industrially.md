### Liquid Crystals; Introduction, Types and Applications of liquid crystals, Industrially

- Liquid crystals (LCs) are substances that exhibit a phase of matter that is between a solid and a liquid. They have some degree of molecular order and orientation, but also some degree of fluidity and mobility  .
- Liquid crystals can be classified into two main types: thermotropic and lyotropic .
  - Thermotropic LCs are formed by heating a crystalline solid and depend on temperature and pressure for their phase transitions .
  - Lyotropic LCs are formed by dissolving a solute in a solvent and depend on concentration and temperature for their phase transitions .
- Liquid crystals have various applications in different fields, such as display technology, optics, lasers, sensors, medical diagnostics, pharmaceuticals, and analytical chemistry  .
  - Display technology is the most common and widely recognized application of liquid crystals, where they are used to create flat panel displays for TVs, computers, smartphones, and other devices. Liquid crystal displays (LCDs) use electric fields to control the orientation and alignment of liquid crystal molecules, which in turn affect the polarization and transmission of light.
  - Optics and lasers are another application of liquid crystals, where they are used to create tunable lenses, filters, polarizers, modulators, and beam steering devices. Liquid crystals can change their optical properties, such as refractive index, birefringence, and optical activity, in response to external stimuli, such as electric fields, magnetic fields, temperature, and light.
  - Sensors and medical diagnostics are another application of liquid crystals, where they are used to detect and measure various physical, chemical, and biological parameters, such as temperature, pressure, pH, humidity, gas, glucose, DNA, and antibodies . Liquid crystals can change their color, shape, orientation, and alignment in response to the presence and concentration of analytes, which can be observed visually or optically .
  - Pharmaceuticals and analytical chemistry are another application of liquid crystals, where they are used to deliver drugs, enhance solubility, improve stability, and facilitate spectroscopic measurements . Liquid crystals can act as anisotropic solvents, carriers, matrices, and probes for various molecules, such as drugs, proteins, DNA, and lipids . Liquid crystals can also enhance the resolution, sensitivity, and selectivity of spectroscopic techniques, such as nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR), infrared (IR), and fluorescence.
