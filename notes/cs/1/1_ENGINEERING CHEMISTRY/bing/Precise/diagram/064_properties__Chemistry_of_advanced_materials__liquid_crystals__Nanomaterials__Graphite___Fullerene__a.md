# Properties and Chemistry of Advanced Materials

## Liquid Crystals
- Liquid crystals are a state of matter that has properties between those of a conventional liquid and those of a solid crystal.
- They can flow like a liquid, but have the long-range order of a solid.
- Liquid crystals are used in displays, such as LCD screens, because they can be manipulated to control the passage of light.
- The two main types of liquid crystals are thermotropic and lyotropic.
- Thermotropic liquid crystals change phase in response to temperature, while lyotropic liquid crystals change phase in response to the concentration of a solvent.

## Nanomaterials
- Nanomaterials are materials with at least one dimension in the nanoscale range (1-100 nanometers).
- They have unique properties due to their small size and high surface area to volume ratio.
- Nanomaterials can be used in a variety of applications, including electronics, medicine, and energy production.
- Some common types of nanomaterials include nanoparticles, nanotubes, and nanowires.

## Graphite and Fullerene
- Graphite is a form of carbon that consists of layers of carbon atoms arranged in a hexagonal lattice.
- It is a good conductor of electricity and heat, and is used in a variety of applications, including batteries and lubricants.
- Fullerene is a molecule composed entirely of carbon, in the form of a hollow sphere, ellipsoid, or tube.
- Fullerenes have unique properties and potential applications in materials science, electronics, and medicine.
- The most famous fullerene is the buckminsterfullerene, also known as the "buckyball," which is a spherical molecule composed of 60 carbon atoms.
