# Chemistry of Engineering Materials: Cement

Cement is a binding material that is used in construction to set, harden, and adhere to other materials. The chemical composition of cement depends on the raw materials used in its manufacturing, which are mainly lime, silica, alumina, and iron oxide .

One of the most commonly used types of cement is Portland cement, which is made up of four main compounds: tricalcium silicate (3CaO · SiO2), dicalcium silicate (2CaO · SiO2), tricalcium aluminate (3CaO · Al2O3), and a tetra-calcium aluminoferrite (4CaO · Al2O3Fe2O3)  .

Cement can be used alone, but it is normally mixed with inert materials known as aggregates to form mortar or concrete. Mortar is cement mixed with sand or crushed stone, while concrete is cement mixed with sand, crushed stone, and water .

With a better understanding of cement chemistry, scientists could potentially alter production or change ingredients to reduce the impact of cement production on emissions or add ingredients that are capable of actively absorbing carbon dioxide .
