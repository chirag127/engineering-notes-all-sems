# UV, IR and NMR, Numerical problems

UV, IR and NMR are three types of spectroscopy techniques used to study the structure of molecules.

- **UV spectroscopy** uses ultraviolet light to study the electronic structure of molecules. It is commonly used to determine the concentration of a substance in a solution.

- **IR spectroscopy** uses infrared light to study the vibrational modes of molecules. It is commonly used to identify functional groups in organic molecules.

- **NMR spectroscopy** uses radio waves to study the magnetic properties of atomic nuclei. It is commonly used to determine the structure of organic molecules.

Numerical problems in these techniques involve using the principles of spectroscopy to solve problems related to the structure and properties of molecules. These problems may involve calculating the concentration of a substance in a solution, identifying functional groups in a molecule, or determining the structure of a molecule.

Here are some examples of numerical problems in UV, IR and NMR spectroscopy:

1. A solution of compound X has an absorbance of 0.75 at a wavelength of 250 nm. Calculate the concentration of compound X in the solution, given that the molar absorptivity of compound X at this wavelength is 15,000 L/mol/cm.

2. An IR spectrum of an unknown compound shows a strong absorption at 1710 cm-1. What functional group is likely present in the molecule?

3. An NMR spectrum of an unknown compound shows a singlet at 3.5 ppm with an integration of 3. What is the likely structure of the molecule?

These are just a few examples of the types of numerical problems that can be solved using UV, IR and NMR spectroscopy. To solve these problems, it is important to have a strong understanding of the principles of spectroscopy and the properties of molecules.
