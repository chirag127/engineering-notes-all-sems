### Fuels and Combustion

#### Definition
- A fuel is a substance that is burned to produce heat or power.
- Combustion is the process of burning a fuel in the presence of oxygen to release energy in the form of heat and light.

#### Classification
Fuels can be classified into three main categories:
1. Solid fuels: such as coal, wood, and charcoal.
2. Liquid fuels: such as gasoline, diesel, and kerosene.
3. Gaseous fuels: such as natural gas, propane, and hydrogen.

#### Characteristics of a good fuel
A good fuel should have the following characteristics:
1. High calorific value: The fuel should release a large amount of heat per unit mass or volume when burned.
2. Easy to ignite: The fuel should ignite easily and burn steadily.
3. Clean burning: The fuel should burn cleanly, producing minimal pollutants and leaving little or no residue.
4. Safe to handle and store: The fuel should be safe to handle and store, with a low risk of accidental ignition or explosion.
5. Readily available: The fuel should be readily available and easy to obtain.
6. Economical: The fuel should be cost-effective, providing a high amount of energy for a reasonable price.

#### Calorific value
- The calorific value of a fuel is the amount of heat released per unit mass or volume of the fuel when it is completely burned.
- It is usually expressed in units of energy per unit mass, such as joules per gram (J/g) or calories per gram (cal/g).
- The higher the calorific value of a fuel, the more heat it releases when burned, and the more efficient it is as a source of energy.
