### Liquid Crystals

#### Introduction
Liquid crystals (LC) are liquids featuring a certain level of orientational order. Specifically, molecules in LCs tend to point to a certain direction, while they still have translational (positional) freedom . Liquid crystal phases represent a unique state of matter characterized by both mobility and order on a molecular and at the supramolecular levels .

#### Types
Liquid crystals can be classified into two main categories: thermotropic liquid crystals, and lyotropic liquid crystals. These two types of liquid crystals are distinguished by the mechanisms that drive their self-organization, but they are also similar in many ways . Thermotropic liquid crystals are obtained by melting a crystalline solid . Lyotropic liquid crystals are found in certain colloidal solutions, such as aqueous solutions of tobacco mosaic virus and certain polymers .

#### Applications
Liquid crystals have found a wide range of commercial applications. They are used, for example, in the liquid crystal displays (LCDs) in digital watches, calculators, and computer and video displays . Two groups of potential applications are the use of liquid crystals as anisotropic solvent for the determination of molecular properties by means of spectroscopy, and their use in analytical chemistry, particularly in gas chromatography . Liquid crystals are also an essential part of all life forms. Lyotropic liquid crystals are essential organic substances, DNA, lipids of cellular membranes and proteins are some examples of well known liquid crystals .

#### Industrial Applications
Liquid crystals have found a wide range of industrial applications, particularly in the field of display technology. Liquid crystal displays (LCDs) are widely used in various devices such as digital watches, calculators, and computer and video displays .
