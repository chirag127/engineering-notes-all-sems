# Values, Gross & Net calorific value, Determination of calorific value by Bomb Calorimeter

- **Values** refer to the principles or standards of behavior that are considered important or worthwhile by an individual or society.
- **Calorific value** is the amount of heat energy released when a substance is burned completely.
- **Gross calorific value (GCV)** is the amount of heat released by the complete combustion of a unit of fuel, including the heat of vaporization of any water produced during combustion.
- **Net calorific value (NCV)** is the amount of heat released by the complete combustion of a unit of fuel, excluding the heat of vaporization of any water produced during combustion.
- The difference between GCV and NCV is the heat of vaporization of the water produced during combustion.
- **Bomb calorimeter** is a device used to determine the calorific value of a substance. It consists of a strong container (the bomb) in which the substance is burned, surrounded by a water bath to absorb the heat released during combustion.
- To determine the calorific value of a substance using a bomb calorimeter, the substance is weighed and placed in the bomb, which is then filled with oxygen and sealed. The bomb is placed in the water bath and the substance is ignited. The heat released during combustion is absorbed by the water, causing its temperature to rise. The temperature rise is measured and used to calculate the calorific value of the substance.
