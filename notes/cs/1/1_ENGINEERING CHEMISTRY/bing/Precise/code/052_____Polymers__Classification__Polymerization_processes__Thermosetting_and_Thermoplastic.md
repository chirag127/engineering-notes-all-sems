### Polymers; Classification, Polymerization processes, Thermosetting and Thermoplastic

Polymers are large molecules made up of repeating subunits called monomers. They can be classified based on their origin, structure, and composition.

1. **Classification of Polymers:**
    - **Based on Origin:** Polymers can be classified as natural or synthetic. Natural polymers are found in nature, such as cellulose, starch, and proteins. Synthetic polymers are man-made, such as plastics, synthetic fibers, and rubber.
    - **Based on Structure:** Polymers can be classified as linear, branched, or cross-linked. Linear polymers have a simple, long-chain structure. Branched polymers have side chains attached to the main chain. Cross-linked polymers have covalent bonds between the chains.
    - **Based on Composition:** Polymers can be classified as homopolymers or copolymers. Homopolymers are made up of only one type of monomer. Copolymers are made up of two or more different types of monomers.

2. **Polymerization Processes:**
    - **Addition Polymerization:** In this process, monomers with unsaturated bonds (double or triple bonds) react to form a polymer. The unsaturated bonds break and new bonds are formed between the monomers, creating a long chain.
    - **Condensation Polymerization:** In this process, monomers react with each other to form a polymer and a small molecule, such as water, is eliminated. This process is also called step-growth polymerization.

3. **Thermosetting and Thermoplastic:**
    - **Thermosetting Polymers:** These polymers are formed by a curing process that involves the formation of cross-links between the polymer chains. Once cured, they cannot be melted or reshaped. Examples include epoxy, phenolic, and melamine.
    - **Thermoplastic Polymers:** These polymers can be melted and reshaped multiple times. They do not have cross-links between the polymer chains. Examples include polyethylene, polypropylene, and polyvinyl chloride.
