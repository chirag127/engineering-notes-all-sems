#### Hardness and Alkalinity

Hardness and alkalinity are two important parameters in water chemistry. Hardness refers to the concentration of calcium and magnesium ions in water, while alkalinity refers to the ability of water to neutralize acids.

#### Determination of Hardness

Hardness can be determined using several methods, including:

1. EDTA titration: This method involves titrating a water sample with a standard solution of ethylenediaminetetraacetic acid (EDTA) using a suitable indicator.
2. Atomic absorption spectroscopy (AAS): This method involves measuring the absorption of light by calcium and magnesium ions in a water sample.
3. Inductively coupled plasma optical emission spectroscopy (ICP-OES): This method involves measuring the emission of light by calcium and magnesium ions in a water sample.

#### Determination of Alkalinity

Alkalinity can be determined using several methods, including:

1. Titration with a standard acid: This method involves titrating a water sample with a standard solution of a strong acid, such as hydrochloric acid (HCl), using a suitable indicator.
2. Gran titration: This method involves titrating a water sample with a standard solution of a strong acid and plotting the titration curve to determine the alkalinity.
3. pH meter: This method involves measuring the pH of a water sample and using the pH value to calculate the alkalinity.

#### Numerical Problems

1. A water sample has a hardness of 150 mg/L as CaCO3. Calculate the concentration of calcium ions in the water sample in mg/L.

Solution: The molar mass of CaCO3 is 100 g/mol, and the molar mass of Ca2+ is 40 g/mol. Therefore, the concentration of Ca2+ in the water sample is (150 mg/L) * (40 g/mol) / (100 g/mol) = 60 mg/L.

2. A water sample has an alkalinity of 100 mg/L as CaCO3. Calculate the concentration of bicarbonate ions in the water sample in mg/L.

Solution: The molar mass of CaCO3 is 100 g/mol, and the molar mass of HCO3- is 61 g/mol. Therefore, the concentration of HCO3- in the water sample is (100 mg/L) * (61 g/mol) / (100 g/mol) = 61 mg/L.
