# Proximate and Ultimate Analysis Method

Proximate and ultimate analysis are two methods used to determine the chemical composition of solid fuels, such as coal and biomass.

## Proximate Analysis

Proximate analysis provides the percentage of the material that burns in a gaseous state (volatile matter), in the solid state (fixed carbon), and the percentage of inorganic waste material (ash). This analysis is of fundamental importance for biomass energy use.

## Ultimate Analysis

Ultimate analysis, on the other hand, is based on the analysis of components of the gas phase that is formed during combustion of the studied samples in oxygen. This analysis typically involves the quantification of elements such as carbon, hydrogen, nitrogen, oxygen, and sulfur.

## Numerical Problems

Numerical problems involving proximate and ultimate analysis may involve calculations based on the data obtained from these analyses. For example, a problem may involve determining the percentage of volatile matter, fixed carbon, and ash in a sample of coal based on the results of a proximate analysis.

## Chemistry of Biogas

Biogas is a renewable energy source produced from the anaerobic digestion of organic matter. The chemical composition of biogas varies depending on the feedstock used and the conditions of the digestion process. However, the main components of biogas are typically methane (CH4) and carbon dioxide (CO2), with smaller amounts of other gases such as hydrogen sulfide (H2S) and nitrogen (N2).
