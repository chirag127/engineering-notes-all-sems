# UV, IR and NMR Spectroscopy

UV, IR and NMR spectroscopy are analytical techniques used to determine the structure of molecules.

## UV Spectroscopy
- UV spectroscopy uses ultraviolet light to excite electrons in a molecule to higher energy levels.
- The absorption of UV light by a molecule is measured and used to determine the structure of the molecule.
- UV spectroscopy is commonly used to identify functional groups in organic molecules.

## IR Spectroscopy
- IR spectroscopy uses infrared light to excite vibrations in the bonds of a molecule.
- The absorption of IR light by a molecule is measured and used to determine the structure of the molecule.
- IR spectroscopy is commonly used to identify functional groups in organic molecules.

## NMR Spectroscopy
- NMR spectroscopy uses radio waves to excite the nuclei of atoms in a molecule.
- The absorption of radio waves by a molecule is measured and used to determine the structure of the molecule.
- NMR spectroscopy is commonly used to determine the connectivity of atoms in a molecule.

## Numerical Problems
- Numerical problems in UV, IR and NMR spectroscopy involve using the principles of these techniques to solve problems related to the structure of molecules.
- These problems may involve calculating the absorption of light by a molecule, predicting the spectrum of a molecule, or interpreting the spectrum of a molecule to determine its structure.
