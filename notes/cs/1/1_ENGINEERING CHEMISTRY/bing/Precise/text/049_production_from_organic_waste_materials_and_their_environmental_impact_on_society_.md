# Production from Organic Waste Materials and their Environmental Impact on Society

Organic waste materials can be used for production in various ways. One of the most common methods is composting, which is the process of decomposing organic matter to produce a nutrient-rich soil amendment. Composting can significantly reduce methane emissions, a potent greenhouse gas, that is generated when organic waste is disposed of in landfills. Compost can also reduce or eliminate the need for chemical fertilizers.

Organic waste feedstocks of aquatic, agricultural, forestry, and terrestrial animal origin are suitable for valorization. Valorization is the process of converting waste materials into valuable products, such as biofuels, fertilizers, and animal feed.

However, the disposal of organic waste in landfills can have a negative impact on the environment. Anaerobic decomposition of organic materials in landfills produces methane, a greenhouse gas with a global warming potential approximately 85 times higher than carbon dioxide over a 20-year time period. Landfills are one of the top emitters of man-made methane emissions in the United States.

In conclusion, the production from organic waste materials can have a positive impact on the environment by reducing greenhouse gas emissions and the need for chemical fertilizers. However, the disposal of organic waste in landfills can have a negative impact on the environment by producing methane, a potent greenhouse gas.
