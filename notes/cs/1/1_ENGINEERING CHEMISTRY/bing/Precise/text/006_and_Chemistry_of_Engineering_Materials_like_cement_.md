# Chemistry of Engineering Materials: Cement

Cement is a binding material that is used in construction to set, harden, and adhere to other materials. The chemical composition of cement depends on the raw materials used in its manufacturing. These raw materials include lime, silica, alumina, and iron oxide.

The cement clinker is produced by igniting a mixture of raw materials, one of which is composed mainly of calcium carbonate and the other of alumino-silicates and oxides of aluminum and iron. The following complex mineralogical compounds constitute the cement clinker.

The raw materials for cement production are limestone (calcium), sand or clay (silicon), bauxite (aluminum), and iron ore. These may include shells, chalk, marl, shale, clay, blast furnace slag, and slate. Chemical analysis of cement raw materials provides insight into the chemical properties of cement.

Cements may be used alone, but the normal use is in mortar and concrete in which the cement is mixed with an inert material known as aggregate. Mortar is cement mixed with sand or crushed stone that must be less than approximately 5 mm in size.

Researchers have developed methods to monitor the cement hydration process in real-world conditions. The concrete world that surrounds us owes its shape and durability to chemical reactions that start when ordinary Portland cement is mixed with water.
