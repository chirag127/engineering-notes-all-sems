# Proximate and Ultimate Analysis of Coal

## Proximate Analysis

- Proximate analysis is the technique used to analyze the **moisture content, ash content, volatile matter and fixed carbon** of coal.
- It is reported as **dry weight**.
- It is used on coals during **exploration, mining and preparation**.
- It is performed in a **proximate analyzer**, which is an automated instrument.
- The steps involved in proximate analysis are:
  - **Moisture content**: The coal sample is dried in an oven at 105°C for 1 hour and the weight loss is recorded as moisture content.
  - **Ash content**: The dried coal sample is burned in a furnace at 750°C for 3 hours and the remaining residue is weighed as ash content.
  - **Volatile matter**: The dried coal sample is heated in a crucible with a lid at 950°C for 7 minutes and the weight loss is recorded as volatile matter.
  - **Fixed carbon**: The fixed carbon content is calculated by subtracting the moisture, ash and volatile matter from 100.

## Ultimate Analysis

- Ultimate analysis is the technique used to analyze the **chemical composition** of coal, such as **carbon, hydrogen, nitrogen, sulfur and oxygen**.
- It is reported as **mass fraction**.
- It is used to determine the **calorific value, combustion efficiency and environmental impact** of coal.
- It is performed in an **ultimate analyzer**, which measures the weight percent of each element by combustion in oxygen.
- The steps involved in ultimate analysis are:
  - **Carbon and hydrogen**: A coal sample is burned in oxygen in a bomb calorimeter and the resulting gases are collected and analyzed by gas chromatography or infrared spectroscopy.
  - **Nitrogen**: A coal sample is burned in oxygen in a bomb calorimeter and the resulting gases are passed through a copper oxide tube to convert nitrogen oxides to nitrogen gas, which is measured by thermal conductivity or chemiluminescence.
  - **Sulfur**: A coal sample is burned in oxygen in a bomb calorimeter and the resulting gases are passed through a magnesium perchlorate tube to absorb water vapor, then through a barium chloride solution to precipitate barium sulfate, which is weighed as sulfur content.
  - **Oxygen**: The oxygen content is calculated by subtracting the carbon, hydrogen, nitrogen and sulfur from 100.

## Numerical Problems

- Example 1: A coal sample has the following proximate analysis: moisture 5%, ash 10%, volatile matter 35%, fixed carbon 50%. Calculate the dry and dry ash free basis of the coal sample.
  - Solution: 
    - Dry basis = (100 - moisture) / 100 = 0.95
    - Dry ash free basis = (100 - moisture - ash) / 100 = 0.85
- Example 2: A coal sample has the following ultimate analysis: carbon 75%, hydrogen 5%, nitrogen 1%, sulfur 2%, oxygen 17%. Calculate the calorific value of the coal sample in kJ/kg, assuming complete combustion and using the following heats of formation: C + O2 -> CO2, -393.5 kJ/mol; H2 + 0.5 O2 -> H2O, -285.8 kJ/mol; S + O2 -> SO2, -296.8 kJ/mol.
  - Solution: 
    - Calorific value = (75/12) x (-393.5) + (5/2) x (-285.8) + (2/32) x (-296.8) = -32188.8 kJ/kg
