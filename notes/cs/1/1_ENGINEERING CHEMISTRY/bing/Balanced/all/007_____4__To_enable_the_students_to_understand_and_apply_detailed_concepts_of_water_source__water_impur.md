# 4. To enable the students to understand and apply detailed concepts of water source, water impurities, hardness

- Water source is the origin of water that is used for various purposes such as drinking, irrigation, industry, etc. Water sources can be classified into surface water and groundwater.
- Surface water is the water that collects on the land surface, such as rivers, lakes, reservoirs, etc. Surface water is influenced by precipitation, evaporation, runoff, and human activities.
- Groundwater is the water that exists below the land surface, in the pores and cracks of rocks and soil. Groundwater is replenished by infiltration of rainwater and snowmelt, and discharged by springs, wells, and seepage to surface water bodies.
- Water impurities are the substances that are present in water and affect its quality, quantity, or suitability for use. Water impurities can be classified into physical, chemical, and biological impurities.
- Physical impurities are the suspended or dissolved solids, such as sand, clay, silt, organic matter, etc. that affect the appearance, taste, odor, and turbidity of water.
- Chemical impurities are the dissolved or undissolved substances, such as salts, metals, acids, bases, gases, etc. that affect the pH, hardness, conductivity, and corrosivity of water.
- Biological impurities are the living or dead organisms, such as bacteria, viruses, algae, fungi, protozoa, etc. that affect the health, safety, and aesthetic quality of water.
- Hardness is the property of water that causes it to form scales or deposits on surfaces, such as pipes, boilers, heaters, etc. Hardness is mainly caused by the presence of calcium and magnesium ions in water, which react with soap to form insoluble precipitates.
- Hardness can be classified into temporary hardness and permanent hardness.
- Temporary hardness is the hardness that can be removed by boiling or adding lime to water. Temporary hardness is caused by the presence of bicarbonates of calcium and magnesium in water, which decompose to form carbonates upon heating or reacting with lime.
- Permanent hardness is the hardness that cannot be removed by boiling or adding lime to water. Permanent hardness is caused by the presence of chlorides, sulfates, and nitrates of calcium and magnesium in water, which do not decompose upon heating or reacting with lime.
