# Important Materials Used as Liquid Crystals

- Liquid crystals are substances that exhibit properties of both liquids and crystalline solids, such as fluidity, anisotropy, and birefringence.
- Liquid crystals can be classified into two main types: thermotropic and lyotropic.
- Thermotropic liquid crystals are temperature-dependent and change their phase and orientation according to the external temperature.
- Lyotropic liquid crystals are concentration-dependent and change their phase and orientation according to the amount of solvent (usually water) present in the system.
- Some examples of thermotropic liquid crystals are:
  - High molar mass materials, such as polymers, that can form liquid crystalline phases by aligning their long and flexible chains.
  - Low molar mass materials, such as aromatic compounds, that can form liquid crystalline phases by stacking their rigid and flat molecules.
- Some examples of lyotropic liquid crystals are:
  - Amphiphilic molecules, such as long-chain alcohols, surfactants, and phospholipids, that can form liquid crystalline phases by arranging their hydrophilic and hydrophobic parts in different ways.
  - Biological materials, such as DNA, gelatin, and tobacco mosaic virus, that can form liquid crystalline phases by self-assembling their rodlike or helical structures.
- Liquid crystals have many applications in various fields, such as display technology, optical devices, sensors, and biomedicine.
