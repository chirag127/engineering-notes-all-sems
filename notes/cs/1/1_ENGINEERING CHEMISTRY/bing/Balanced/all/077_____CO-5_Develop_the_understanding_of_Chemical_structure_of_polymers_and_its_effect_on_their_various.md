# CO-5 Develop the understanding of Chemical structure of polymers and its effect on their various properties

- Polymers are very long carbon-based molecules that have a wide range of beneficial structural (and sometimes thermal) properties.
- The chemical structure of polymers refers to the arrangement of atoms and bonds in the polymer chains, as well as the degree of branching, cross-linking, and crystallinity.
- The chemical structure of polymers affects their physical properties, such as density, melting point, strength, elasticity, solubility, and reactivity.
- Some of the factors that influence the physical properties of polymers are:

  - Chain length: Longer polymer chains have more intermolecular forces, such as van der Waals forces and hydrogen bonding, which make them stronger and stiffer .
  - Side groups: The presence of different functional groups or atoms attached to the polymer chains can affect the polarity, flexibility, and reactivity of the polymers. For example, polyvinyl chloride (PVC) has chlorine atoms as side groups, which make it more polar and less flexible than polyethylene.
  - Branching: The degree of branching refers to the number of side chains that branch off from the main polymer chain. More branching reduces the ability of the polymer chains to pack closely together, which lowers the density, melting point, and crystallinity of the polymers .
  - Cross-linking: The degree of cross-linking refers to the number of covalent bonds that link different polymer chains together. More cross-linking increases the rigidity, strength, and thermal stability of the polymers, but also reduces their solubility and elasticity. For example, vulcanized rubber is more cross-linked than natural rubber, which makes it more durable and resistant to heat.
  - Crystallinity: The degree of crystallinity refers to the proportion of polymer chains that are arranged in a regular and ordered manner. More crystallinity increases the density, melting point, and strength of the polymers, but also reduces their flexibility and transparency. For example, high-density polyethylene (HDPE) is more crystalline than low-density polyethylene (LDPE), which makes it harder and stronger.

- To change the properties of polymers, different methods can be used, such as:

  - Copolymerization: This is the process of combining two or more different monomers to form a polymer with a mixed chemical structure. For example, nylon is a copolymer of adipic acid and hexamethylenediamine, which gives it both hydrophilic and hydrophobic properties.
  - Blending: This is the process of mixing two or more different polymers to form a composite material with improved properties. For example, polycarbonate is blended with acrylonitrile butadiene styrene (ABS) to increase its impact resistance and toughness.
  - Additives: These are substances that are added to polymers to modify their properties, such as plasticizers, stabilizers, fillers, colorants, and flame retardants. For example, plasticizers are added to PVC to make it more flexible and soft.
