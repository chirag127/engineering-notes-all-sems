# Chemistry of Atomic and Molecular Structure

- Atomic structure refers to the arrangement of protons, neutrons and electrons in an atom. Protons and neutrons form the nucleus, which is the central core of the atom, while electrons orbit around the nucleus in shells or orbitals.
- The number of protons in an atom determines its atomic number, which is also the number of electrons in a neutral atom. The number of neutrons in an atom determines its mass number, which is the sum of protons and neutrons. The number of electrons in an atom can vary depending on its charge or oxidation state.
- The arrangement of electrons in an atom is governed by the principles of quantum mechanics, which describe the energy levels and shapes of the orbitals. The orbitals are classified into four types: s, p, d and f, each with a different shape and number of sub-orbitals. The electrons fill the orbitals according to the Aufbau principle, which states that the electrons occupy the lowest energy orbitals first, and the Pauli exclusion principle, which states that no two electrons can have the same set of quantum numbers.
- The electronic configuration of an atom is a notation that shows the distribution of electrons in the orbitals. For example, the electronic configuration of carbon is 1s2 2s2 2p2, which means that carbon has two electrons in the 1s orbital, two electrons in the 2s orbital and two electrons in the 2p orbital. The electronic configuration can also be written using the noble gas notation, which uses the symbol of the nearest noble gas with a lower atomic number to represent the core electrons. For example, the electronic configuration of carbon can also be written as [He] 2s2 2p2, where [He] represents the two electrons in the 1s orbital of helium.
- The valence electrons are the electrons in the outermost shell or orbital of an atom, which are involved in chemical bonding and reactions. The number of valence electrons determines the chemical properties and reactivity of an atom. For example, carbon has four valence electrons, which means that it can form four covalent bonds with other atoms. The valence shell is the shell or orbital that contains the valence electrons. For example, the valence shell of carbon is the 2s and 2p orbitals.
- Molecular structure refers to the arrangement of atoms in a molecule, which is a group of two or more atoms held together by chemical bonds. The type and number of bonds between atoms depend on the valence electrons and the octet rule, which states that atoms tend to form bonds to achieve a stable configuration of eight electrons in their valence shell, except for hydrogen and helium, which only need two electrons. The types of bonds are ionic, covalent and metallic, each with different characteristics and properties.
- Ionic bonds are formed when atoms transfer electrons to achieve the octet rule, resulting in the formation of positive and negative ions, which are attracted by electrostatic forces. For example, sodium (Na) transfers one electron to chlorine (Cl) to form sodium chloride (NaCl), which is an ionic compound composed of sodium ions (Na+) and chloride ions (Cl-).
- Covalent bonds are formed when atoms share electrons to achieve the octet rule, resulting in the formation of molecules. For example, carbon (C) shares four electrons with four hydrogen (H) atoms to form methane (CH4), which is a covalent molecule composed of one carbon atom and four hydrogen atoms. Covalent bonds can be classified into single, double and triple bonds, depending on the number of electron pairs shared between atoms. For example, carbon dioxide (CO2) has two double bonds between carbon and oxygen, while nitrogen (N2) has one triple bond between two nitrogen atoms.
- Metallic bonds are formed when atoms of metals donate their valence electrons to a common pool of electrons, which are free to move throughout the metal. This results in the formation of positive metal ions, which are held together by the attraction to the negative electron cloud. For example, iron (Fe) forms metallic bonds with other iron atoms to form iron metal, which is composed of iron ions (Fe2+) and a sea of electrons.
