# Process of Determination of Hardness and Alkalinity

## Alkalinity
- Alkalinity is the measure of the ability of water to neutralize acids.
- Alkalinity is mainly due to the presence of bicarbonates, carbonates, and hydroxides of calcium, magnesium, and other metals in water.
- Alkalinity is expressed as milligrams per liter of equivalent calcium carbonate (mg/L CaCO3).
- Alkalinity can be measured by titrating a water sample with a standard acid solution, such as sulfuric acid (H2SO4), using a pH meter or an indicator to detect the endpoint.
- The endpoint of the titration depends on the type and amount of alkaline substances in water.
- The most common endpoints are pH 4.5, pH 8.3, and pH 10.0, corresponding to total, carbonate, and bicarbonate alkalinity, respectively.
- The titration curve of alkalinity shows the change of pH with the addition of acid.
- The inflection points of the curve indicate the equivalence points of the titration, where the moles of acid added are equal to the moles of alkaline substances neutralized.
- The alkalinity can be calculated from the volume and concentration of the acid used and the volume of the water sample.
- The formula for alkalinity calculation is:

    Alkalinity (mg/L CaCO3) = (Volume of acid (mL) x Normality of acid (eq/L) x 50,000) / Volume of water sample (mL)

- Some tips for alkalinity measurement are:
  - Use fresh and standardized acid solution.
  - Rinse the burette and the pH electrode with acid and water before use.
  - Stir the water sample constantly during the titration.
  - Record the initial and final readings of the burette and the pH meter.
  - Repeat the titration until consistent results are obtained.

## Hardness
- Hardness is the measure of the concentration of calcium and magnesium ions in water.
- Hardness is mainly due to the dissolution of limestone, dolomite, gypsum, and other minerals in water.
- Hardness is expressed as milligrams per liter of equivalent calcium carbonate (mg/L CaCO3).
- Hardness can be measured by titrating a water sample with a standard complexing agent, such as ethylenediaminetetraacetic acid (EDTA), using an indicator that changes color when all the calcium and magnesium ions are complexed.
- The indicator used for hardness titration is usually eriochrome black T (EBT), which forms a red complex with free calcium and magnesium ions, and turns blue when they are bound to EDTA.
- The endpoint of the titration is the color change from red to blue.
- The titration curve of hardness shows the change of EBT concentration with the addition of EDTA.
- The equivalence point of the curve is where the EBT concentration is zero, and the moles of EDTA added are equal to the moles of calcium and magnesium ions in water.
- The hardness can be calculated from the volume and concentration of the EDTA used and the volume of the water sample.
- The formula for hardness calculation is:

    Hardness (mg/L CaCO3) = (Volume of EDTA (mL) x Normality of EDTA (eq/L) x 50,000) / Volume of water sample (mL)

- Some tips for hardness measurement are:
  - Use fresh and standardized EDTA solution.
  - Rinse the burette and the flask with EDTA and water before use.
  - Add a buffer solution to adjust the pH of the water sample to 10.
  - Add a few drops of EBT indicator to the water sample and swirl to mix.
  - Titrate the water sample with EDTA until the color changes from red to blue.
  - Record the initial and final readings of the burette.
  - Repeat the titration until consistent results are obtained.
