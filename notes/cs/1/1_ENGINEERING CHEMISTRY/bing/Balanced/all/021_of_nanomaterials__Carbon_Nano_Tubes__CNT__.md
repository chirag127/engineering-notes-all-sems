# Nanomaterials, Carbon Nanotubes (CNT)

- Nanomaterials are materials that have at least one dimension in the nanometer range (1-100 nm).
- Carbon nanomaterials are a family of carbon allotropes, consisting of different forms and shapes of carbon atoms arranged in various structures.
- Carbon nanotubes (CNTs) are one of the most important types of carbon nanomaterials. They are cylindrical tubes of carbon atoms, with diameters ranging from a few nanometers to tens of nanometers, and lengths up to several micrometers or even millimeters.
- CNTs can be classified into two main categories: single-walled carbon nanotubes (SWCNTs) and multi-walled carbon nanotubes (MWCNTs).
  - SWCNTs consist of a single layer of carbon atoms arranged in a hexagonal lattice, forming a seamless cylinder of graphene.
  - MWCNTs consist of multiple concentric layers of carbon atoms, forming nested tubes of different diameters.
- CNTs have remarkable physical and chemical properties, such as high strength, stiffness, elasticity, thermal conductivity, electrical conductivity, and optical activity. They also have potential applications in various fields, such as nanoelectronics, nanomedicine, nanocomposites, energy storage, and sensors.
- CNTs are mainly synthesized by chemical vapor deposition (CVD), which involves the decomposition of a carbon-containing gas (such as methane, acetylene, or carbon monoxide) on a metal catalyst (such as iron, nickel, or cobalt) at high temperatures (600-1200 °C).
- CNTs can also be functionalized or modified by attaching different molecules or groups to their surface or ends, to enhance their solubility, dispersibility, biocompatibility, or reactivity. Some common methods of functionalization include oxidation, reduction, covalent bonding, non-covalent bonding, and polymer wrapping.
