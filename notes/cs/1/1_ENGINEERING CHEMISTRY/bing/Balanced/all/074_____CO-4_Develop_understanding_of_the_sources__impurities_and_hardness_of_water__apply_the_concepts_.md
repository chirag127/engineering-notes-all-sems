# CO-4 Develop understanding of the sources, impurities and hardness of water, apply the concepts of

- Water is a vital resource for life and various human activities. It is obtained from different sources, such as surface water (rivers, lakes, ponds, etc.), groundwater (wells, springs, etc.), rainwater, seawater, etc.
- Water from natural sources is not pure. It contains various impurities, such as dissolved gases, minerals, salts, organic matter, microorganisms, etc. These impurities may affect the quality, taste, odor, color, and suitability of water for different purposes.
- Hardness of water is a measure of its ability to form lather with soap. It is caused by the presence of dissolved calcium and magnesium salts, such as bicarbonates, sulphates, chlorides, etc. Hard water has some disadvantages, such as forming scales in boilers, pipes, and appliances, reducing the efficiency of detergents and soap, causing skin irritation, etc.
- The concepts of hardness of water include the following:

  - Types of hardness: Temporary hardness and permanent hardness. Temporary hardness is caused by the presence of bicarbonates of calcium and magnesium, which can be removed by boiling or adding lime. Permanent hardness is caused by the presence of sulphates, chlorides, and nitrates of calcium and magnesium, which cannot be removed by boiling or adding lime.
  - Units of hardness: Parts per million (ppm), grains per gallon (gpg), milligrams per liter (mg/L), degrees Clark (°Cl), degrees French (°Fr), degrees German (°dH), etc. One ppm of hardness is equivalent to one milligram of calcium carbonate (CaCO3) per liter of water. One gpg of hardness is equivalent to 17.1 ppm of hardness. One °Cl of hardness is equivalent to 14.3 ppm of hardness. One °Fr of hardness is equivalent to 10 ppm of hardness. One °dH of hardness is equivalent to 17.8 ppm of hardness.
  - Methods of removing hardness: Ion exchange, reverse osmosis, distillation, precipitation, etc. Ion exchange is a process of exchanging the calcium and magnesium ions in hard water with sodium or hydrogen ions in a resin. Reverse osmosis is a process of forcing hard water through a semipermeable membrane that allows water molecules to pass but blocks the dissolved salts. Distillation is a process of vaporizing hard water and condensing the pure water. Precipitation is a process of adding chemicals, such as lime, soda, zeolite, etc., to hard water to form insoluble precipitates of calcium and magnesium salts, which can be filtered out.
