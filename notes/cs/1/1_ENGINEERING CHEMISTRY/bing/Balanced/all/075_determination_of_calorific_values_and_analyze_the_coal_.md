# Determination of Calorific Values and Analysis of Coal

- Calorific value is a measure of the amount of energy produced from a unit weight of coal when it is combusted in oxygen.
- Calorific value can be determined by different methods, such as thermal analysis, bomb calorimetry, and laser-induced breakdown spectroscopy  .
- Thermal analysis is a technique that measures the changes in physical and chemical properties of coal as a function of temperature or time. It can be used to estimate the calorific value of coal based on the heat effects of combustion reactions.
- Bomb calorimetry is a method that involves the complete combustion of a measured sample of coal in a sealed vessel under high pressure and temperature. It can be used to measure the calorific value of coal directly by the amount of heat released in the process.
- Laser-induced breakdown spectroscopy is a method that involves the generation of a plasma by a laser pulse on the surface of coal and the detection of the emitted light spectra. It can be used to measure the calorific value of coal indirectly by the correlation between the spectral intensity and the elemental composition of coal.
- Analysis of coal is the process of determining the physical and chemical properties of coal, such as moisture, ash, volatile matter, fixed carbon, sulfur, and oxygen. It can be used to evaluate the quality and performance of coal for various applications.
- Analysis of coal can be performed by different methods, such as proximate analysis, ultimate analysis, and specific gravity analysis.
- Proximate analysis is a method that measures the moisture, ash, volatile matter, and fixed carbon content of coal. It can be used to estimate the heating value, combustion behavior, and coking potential of coal.
- Ultimate analysis is a method that measures the carbon, hydrogen, nitrogen, sulfur, and oxygen content of coal. It can be used to calculate the empirical formula, air-fuel ratio, and flue gas composition of coal.
- Specific gravity analysis is a method that measures the density of coal relative to water. It can be used to indicate the rank, maturity, and impurities of coal.
