## Unit-5: 8

- This unit covers the topic of **linear programming**, which is a method of finding the optimal solution to a problem that involves multiple constraints and objectives.
- Linear programming can be used to model and solve various real-world problems, such as resource allocation, production planning, transportation, scheduling, etc.
- The basic idea of linear programming is to **maximize** or **minimize** a linear function of decision variables, subject to a set of linear equations or inequalities that represent the constraints of the problem.
- A linear function is a function that can be written as a sum of constant multiples of the decision variables, such as `f(x,y) = 3x + 5y`.
- A linear equation is an equation that can be written as a sum of constant multiples of the decision variables equal to a constant, such as `2x + y = 10`.
- A linear inequality is an inequality that can be written as a sum of constant multiples of the decision variables less than or equal to a constant, such as `x + 2y <= 8`.
- The decision variables are the unknown quantities that need to be determined by the linear programming method, such as the amount of products to produce, the number of workers to hire, the routes to take, etc.
- The constraints are the limitations or restrictions that the decision variables must satisfy, such as the availability of resources, the demand of customers, the capacity of facilities, etc.
- The objective function is the linear function that represents the goal or the measure of performance that needs to be optimized, such as the profit, the cost, the time, etc.
- The optimal solution is the set of values of the decision variables that maximize or minimize the objective function, while satisfying all the constraints.
- The feasible region is the set of all possible values of the decision variables that satisfy the constraints. It is usually a polygonal region in the coordinate plane, bounded by the lines or half-planes that correspond to the constraints.
- The vertices or corner points of the feasible region are the points where two or more constraints intersect. They are potential candidates for the optimal solution, as they often have the highest or lowest values of the objective function among all feasible points.
- The graphical method of linear programming is a technique of finding the optimal solution by plotting the feasible region and the objective function on the same graph, and then locating the vertex or the edge of the feasible region that gives the optimal value of the objective function.
- The graphical method can only be applied to problems that have two decision variables, as they can be easily represented on a two-dimensional graph. For problems that have more than two decision variables, other methods, such as the simplex method, are needed.
- The steps of the graphical method are as follows:

  1. Identify the decision variables and assign them symbols, such as `x` and `y`.
  2. Write the objective function as a linear function of the decision variables, and specify whether it needs to be maximized or minimized.
  3. Write the constraints as linear equations or inequalities of the decision variables, and simplify them if possible.
  4. Plot the constraints on a coordinate plane, and shade the feasible region that satisfies all the constraints.
  5. Plot the objective function as a straight line on the same graph, and vary its slope or intercept until it is parallel to one of the edges of the feasible region, or passes through one of the vertices of the feasible region. This is the optimal value of the objective function.
  6. Find the coordinates of the vertex or the edge of the feasible region that corresponds to the optimal value of the objective function. This is the optimal solution of the problem.
  7. Interpret the optimal solution in the context of the problem, and check if it makes sense.
