# Polymers, Polymer Blends and Composites, Conducting and Biodegradable polymers

- Polymers are large molecules composed of repeating units called monomers, which are linked by covalent bonds. Polymers can have various properties depending on their chemical structure, molecular weight, degree of crystallinity, and crosslinking. Polymers are widely used in various fields such as plastics, rubber, textiles, coatings, adhesives, and biomedical applications.
- Polymer blends are mixtures of two or more polymers that are physically mixed but not chemically bonded. Polymer blends can improve the properties of individual polymers by combining their advantages and minimizing their disadvantages. For example, polymer blends can enhance the mechanical strength, thermal stability, processability, and compatibility of polymers.
- Polymer composites are materials that consist of a polymer matrix and a reinforcing filler, such as fibers, particles, or nanomaterials. Polymer composites can improve the properties of polymers by increasing their stiffness, strength, toughness, conductivity, and biodegradability. Polymer composites can be classified into two types: thermosets and thermoplastics. Thermosets are crosslinked polymers that cannot be melted or reshaped, while thermoplastics are polymers that can be melted and reshaped.
- Conducting polymers are polymers that can conduct electricity due to the presence of conjugated double bonds or delocalized electrons in their backbone. Conducting polymers have attracted much attention for their potential applications in biosensors, tissue engineering, drug delivery, and antibacterial activity. However, most conducting polymers are non-degradable and may cause toxicity and inflammation in the body. Therefore, researchers have developed biodegradable and electrically conductive polymers (DECPs) by incorporating degradable units or blending with biodegradable polymers.
- Biodegradable polymers are polymers that can be degraded naturally by the action of microorganisms, producing eco-friendly and useful materials such as CO2 and CH4. Biodegradable polymers can reduce the environmental pollution and waste disposal problems caused by conventional polymers. Biodegradable polymers can be derived from renewable resources, such as starch, cellulose, chitin, and natural rubber, or synthesized from synthetic monomers, such as polylactic acid (PLA), polyglycolic acid (PGA), polycaprolactone (PCL), and polybutylene adipate terephthalate (PBAT).
