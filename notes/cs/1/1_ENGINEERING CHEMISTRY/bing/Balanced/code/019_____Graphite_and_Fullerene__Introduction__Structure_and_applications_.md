### Graphite and Fullerene; Introduction, Structure and Applications

- Graphite and fullerene are two forms of carbon that have different structures and properties.
- Graphite is a three-dimensional material that consists of layers of hexagonal rings of carbon atoms, held together by weak van der Waals forces. Each carbon atom in graphite is bonded to three other carbon atoms in the same plane, forming sp2 hybridized orbitals. Graphite is a good conductor of electricity and heat, and has a high melting point and lubricity. Graphite is used in pencils, batteries, electrodes, lubricants, and as a moderator in nuclear reactors.
- Fullerene is a class of molecules that have a closed cage or a cylindrical shape, composed of carbon atoms arranged in pentagons and hexagons. The most common fullerene is C60, also known as buckminsterfullerene or buckyball, which has a spherical shape with 12 pentagons and 20 hexagons. Each carbon atom in fullerene is bonded to three other carbon atoms, forming sp2 hybridized orbitals. Fullerene is a semiconductor and has unique optical, electrical, and mechanical properties. Fullerene is used in nanotechnology, drug delivery, solar cells, and superconductors.
