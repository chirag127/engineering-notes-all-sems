# Polymers

- Polymers are any class of natural or synthetic substances composed of very large molecules, called macromolecules, that are multiples of simpler chemical units called monomers.
- Examples of polymers are rubber, plastics, and nylon.
- Polymers have different physical and chemical properties, which are affected by the structure, type of monomer units, and other factors.
- Some of the properties of polymers are:
  - Density: The mass per unit volume of a polymer. It depends on the type and arrangement of monomer units, and the degree of crystallinity.
  - Melting point: The temperature at which a polymer changes from solid to liquid state. It depends on the strength of intermolecular forces and the degree of crystallinity.
  - Glass transition temperature: The temperature at which a polymer changes from a rigid and brittle state to a soft and rubbery state. It depends on the flexibility and mobility of polymer chains.
  - Tensile strength: The maximum stress that a polymer can withstand before breaking. It depends on the orientation and alignment of polymer chains, and the presence of cross-links or branches.
  - Elasticity: The ability of a polymer to return to its original shape after being stretched or compressed. It depends on the flexibility and mobility of polymer chains, and the presence of cross-links or branches.
  - Solubility: The ability of a polymer to dissolve in a solvent. It depends on the polarity and compatibility of polymer and solvent molecules.
  - Thermal stability: The ability of a polymer to resist degradation or decomposition at high temperatures. It depends on the type and arrangement of monomer units, and the presence of stabilizers or additives.
- Polymers can be classified into different types based on the structure, composition, and synthesis of monomer units:
  - Homopolymers: Polymers that are made of only one type of monomer unit. For example, polyethylene, polypropylene, and polystyrene.
  - Copolymers: Polymers that are made of two or more different types of monomer units. For example, nylon, polyester, and rubber.
  - Linear polymers: Polymers that have long and straight chains of monomer units. For example, polyethylene, polypropylene, and nylon.
  - Branched polymers: Polymers that have long chains of monomer units with some side chains or branches. For example, low-density polyethylene, amylopectin, and glycogen.
  - Cross-linked polymers: Polymers that have long chains of monomer units with some covalent bonds between them. For example, vulcanized rubber, bakelite, and melamine.
  - Network polymers: Polymers that have long chains of monomer units with many covalent bonds between them, forming a three-dimensional network. For example, epoxy resin, silicone, and diamond.
