### Fuels and Combustion: Definition, Classification, Characteristics of a good fuel, Calorific value

- **Fuels** are substances that can be burned to produce heat or other forms of energy. They are usually derived from natural sources such as coal, petroleum, natural gas, wood, etc. Fuels can be classified into three main types: solid, liquid and gaseous fuels.
- **Combustion** is the process of rapid oxidation of a fuel with the release of heat and light. It is also called burning. Combustion can be complete or incomplete, depending on the availability of oxygen and the nature of the fuel.
- **Characteristics of a good fuel** are the properties that make a fuel suitable for a specific purpose. Some of the important characteristics are :
  - High calorific value: It is the amount of heat produced by the complete combustion of a unit mass or volume of a fuel. The higher the calorific value, the more efficient the fuel is.
  - Low ignition point: It is the minimum temperature at which a fuel catches fire spontaneously. The lower the ignition point, the easier the fuel is to ignite.
  - High combustion efficiency: It is the ratio of the actual heat produced by the combustion of a fuel to the theoretical heat that could be produced. The higher the combustion efficiency, the less the wastage of fuel and heat.
  - Low smoke and gas production: A good fuel should produce minimal amounts of smoke and harmful gases such as carbon monoxide, sulfur dioxide, nitrogen oxides, etc. These pollutants can cause environmental and health problems.
  - Low cost and availability: A good fuel should be readily available at a reasonable price and should not depend on external factors such as geopolitics, weather, etc.
  - Easy storage and transportation: A good fuel should be easy to handle, store and transport without posing any safety or environmental risks.
  - Uniform size and shape: In case of solid fuels, the size and shape should be uniform so that the combustion is regular and controllable.
  - Controllable combustion: A good fuel should allow the user to adjust the rate and intensity of combustion according to the need and preference.
- **Calorific value** is the quantity of heat produced by the complete combustion of a unit mass or volume of a fuel. It is usually expressed in kilojoules per kilogram (kJ/kg) for solid and liquid fuels, and kilojoules per cubic meter (kJ/m^3^) for gaseous fuels. It is an important measure of the quality and efficiency of a fuel. The higher the calorific value, the more heat and energy a fuel can provide .
