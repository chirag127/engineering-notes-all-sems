# UV, IR and NMR, Numerical problems

- UV, IR and NMR are spectroscopic methods that can be used to identify organic compounds by analyzing their molecular structure and functional groups.
- UV spectroscopy measures the absorption of ultraviolet light by a molecule, which depends on the presence and number of conjugated pi bonds.
- IR spectroscopy measures the absorption of infrared light by a molecule, which depends on the presence and type of functional groups that have characteristic vibrational frequencies.
- NMR spectroscopy measures the absorption of radio waves by a molecule, which depends on the magnetic environment of the nuclei, especially hydrogen and carbon, that have different chemical shifts and coupling patterns.
- Numerical problems involve using the data from these spectroscopic methods, along with the molecular formula and degree of unsaturation, to deduce the structure of an unknown compound.
- Some examples of numerical problems are:

## Problem 1
- A compound with molecular formula C<sub>5</sub>H<sub>10</sub>O has the following IR and <sup>1</sup>H NMR spectra. What is the structure of the compound?

![IR spectrum](https://www.masterorganicchemistry.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/11/ir-spectroscopy-problem-1.png)

![NMR spectrum](https://www.masterorganicchemistry.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/11/nmr-spectroscopy-problem-1.png)

- Solution:
  - The molecular formula C<sub>5</sub>H<sub>10</sub>O has a degree of unsaturation of 1, which means there is either a double bond or a ring in the structure.
  - The IR spectrum shows a strong absorption at 1720 cm<sup>-1</sup>, which indicates the presence of a carbonyl group (C=O).
  - The NMR spectrum shows four signals, which means there are four different types of protons in the molecule. The signal at 2.2 ppm is a singlet with an area of 3, which corresponds to a methyl group (CH<sub>3</sub>) next to a carbonyl group. The signal at 2.4 ppm is a quartet with an area of 2, which corresponds to a methylene group (CH<sub>2</sub>) next to a methyl group. The signal at 3.6 ppm is a triplet with an area of 2, which corresponds to another methylene group next to a methine group (CH). The signal at 4.1 ppm is a quartet with an area of 1, which corresponds to a methine group next to an oxygen atom.
