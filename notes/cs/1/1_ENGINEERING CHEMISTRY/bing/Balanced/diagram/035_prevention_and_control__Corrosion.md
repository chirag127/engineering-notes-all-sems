# Prevention and Control of Corrosion

Corrosion is the deterioration of a metal or its properties due to a reaction with its environment. Corrosion can cause loss of strength, appearance, and functionality of the metal. Corrosion can also pose safety and environmental hazards. Therefore, it is important to prevent and control corrosion by using various methods.

Some of the methods of prevention and control of corrosion are:

- **Material selection**: Choosing a metal that is resistant to corrosion in a given environment, or using a non-metallic material instead of a metal. For example, stainless steel is resistant to corrosion in many acidic and alkaline environments, while plastic or ceramic can be used instead of metal in some applications.
- **Coating**: Applying a layer of another material on the surface of the metal to protect it from the corrosive environment. The coating can be metallic or non-metallic, such as paint, enamel, rubber, or epoxy. The coating should be uniform, adherent, and resistant to the corrosive environment. For example, zinc coating (galvanizing) is used to protect iron and steel from rusting, while paint is used to protect wood and metal from moisture and oxygen.
- **Cathodic protection**: Connecting the metal to be protected to a more easily corroded metal (sacrificial anode) or an external power source (impressed current) to make it the cathode in an electrochemical cell. This way, the metal to be protected does not lose electrons and does not corrode, while the sacrificial anode or the power source supplies electrons and corrodes instead. For example, zinc or magnesium anodes are used to protect steel pipelines and ship hulls from corrosion, while impressed current is used to protect underground tanks and pipelines from corrosion.
- **Anodic protection**: Applying a small positive potential to the metal to be protected to make it the anode in an electrochemical cell. This way, the metal forms a thin oxide film on its surface that acts as a barrier to the corrosive environment. The potential should be carefully controlled to avoid excessive oxidation and pitting of the metal. For example, anodic protection is used to protect stainless steel tanks and vessels from corrosion in acidic solutions.
- **Corrosion inhibitors**: Adding chemicals to the corrosive environment to reduce the rate of corrosion of the metal. The inhibitors can act by forming a protective film on the metal surface, reducing the concentration of corrosive agents, or interfering with the electrochemical reactions of corrosion. For example, chromates, phosphates, and nitrites are used as corrosion inhibitors for cooling water systems, while amines and oxygen scavengers are used as corrosion inhibitors for boiler water systems.
