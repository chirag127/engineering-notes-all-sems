# Production from organic waste materials and their environmental impact on society

- Organic waste materials are any biodegradable substances that come from plants or animals, such as food scraps, yard trimmings, paper, wood, leather, etc.
- Organic waste materials can be converted into useful products, such as compost, biogas, biofuels, bioplastics, etc., through various processes, such as composting, anaerobic digestion, pyrolysis, gasification, fermentation, etc.
- Production from organic waste materials can have positive environmental impacts, such as:
  - Reducing the amount of waste sent to landfills or incinerators, which can save space, energy, and greenhouse gas emissions   .
  - Enhancing the quality and fertility of soil, which can improve crop yields, water retention, and carbon sequestration .
  - Providing renewable sources of energy, materials, and chemicals, which can reduce the dependence on fossil fuels and their associated environmental problems .
- Production from organic waste materials can also have negative environmental impacts, such as:
  - Generating pollutants, such as leachate, odors, pathogens, and contaminants, which can affect the quality of water, air, and soil .
  - Requiring additional resources, such as land, water, and energy, which can have trade-offs and opportunity costs .
  - Creating environmental justice issues, such as the unequal distribution of benefits and burdens among different communities and groups .
- Production from organic waste materials can be optimized and regulated to minimize the negative impacts and maximize the positive impacts, by applying the principles of circular economy, sustainable development, and environmental management .
