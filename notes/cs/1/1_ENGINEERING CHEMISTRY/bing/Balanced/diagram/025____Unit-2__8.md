## Definition and Properties of Logarithms

- A logarithm is an operation that answers the question: how many times do we need to multiply a certain number (called the base) by itself to get another number (called the argument)?
- For example, the logarithm of 8 with base 2 is 3, because 2 multiplied by itself 3 times is 8. We write this as log<sub>2</sub>(8) = 3.
- The base of a logarithm must be a positive number that is not equal to 1. The argument of a logarithm must be a positive number.
- The logarithm of 1 with any base is 0, because any number raised to the power of 0 is 1. For example, log<sub>2</sub>(1) = 0.
- The logarithm of the base with the same base is 1, because any number raised to the power of 1 is itself. For example, log<sub>2</sub>(2) = 1.
- The logarithm of a product is equal to the sum of the logarithms of the factors. For example, log<sub>2</sub>(8 * 4) = log<sub>2</sub>(8) + log<sub>2</sub>(4) = 3 + 2 = 5.
- The logarithm of a quotient is equal to the difference of the logarithms of the numerator and the denominator. For example, log<sub>2</sub>(8 / 4) = log<sub>2</sub>(8) - log<sub>2</sub>(4) = 3 - 2 = 1.
