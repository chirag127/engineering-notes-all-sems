### CO-3 Utilize the theory of construction of electrodes, batteries and fuel cells in redesigning new engineering

- Electrodes are conductors that allow electric current to flow in and out of a cell or an electrochemical device. They are usually made of metals, alloys, or carbon materials. Electrodes can be classified into two types: inert electrodes and active electrodes. Inert electrodes do not participate in the electrochemical reactions, but only transfer electrons. Active electrodes are involved in the electrochemical reactions, and their composition and properties may change during the process.
- Batteries are electrochemical devices that convert chemical energy into electrical energy. They consist of one or more cells, each containing a positive electrode (cathode), a negative electrode (anode), and an electrolyte that allows the movement of ions. Batteries can be classified into two types: primary batteries and secondary batteries. Primary batteries are disposable and cannot be recharged, as the electrode reactions are irreversible. Secondary batteries are rechargeable and can be recharged by reversing the electrode reactions.
- Fuel cells are electrochemical devices that convert chemical energy into electrical energy. They are similar to batteries, but they do not run down or need recharging, as long as fuel is supplied. They consist of two electrodes (anode and cathode) and an electrolyte, but the fuel and the oxidant are stored externally and fed continuously to the electrodes. Fuel cells can be classified into different types based on the type of electrolyte and the operating temperature, such as proton exchange membrane fuel cells, solid oxide fuel cells, and alkaline fuel cells  .
- The theory of construction of electrodes, batteries and fuel cells is based on the principles of electrochemistry, thermodynamics, kinetics, and transport phenomena. The theory helps to understand the factors that affect the performance, efficiency, and durability of these devices, such as the electrode materials, the electrolyte properties, the reaction rates, the mass transfer, the heat transfer, and the polarization losses. The theory also helps to design and optimize new engineering solutions for various applications, such as electric vehicles, portable electronics, grid storage, and renewable energy integration.
