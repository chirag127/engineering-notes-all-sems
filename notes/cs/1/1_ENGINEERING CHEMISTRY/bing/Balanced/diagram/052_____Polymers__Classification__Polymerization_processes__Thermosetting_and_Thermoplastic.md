### Polymers; Classification, Polymerization processes, Thermosetting and Thermoplastic

- Polymers are large molecules composed of repeating units called monomers, which are linked together by covalent bonds.
- Polymers can be classified based on various factors, such as their chemical structure, their thermal behavior, their molecular weight, their mode of synthesis, and their applications .
- Based on their chemical structure, polymers can be divided into two main groups: thermosets and thermoplastics .
  - Thermosets are polymers that have cross-linked molecular chains, which give them high strength and rigidity. They cannot be melted or reshaped once they are cured by heat or chemical agents. Examples of thermosets are epoxy, bakelite, and vulcanized rubber  .
  - Thermoplastics are polymers that have linear or slightly branched molecular chains, which allow them to be softened by heating and solidified by cooling. They can be repeatedly molded and recycled without losing their properties. Examples of thermoplastics are polyethylene, polyvinyl chloride, and nylon  .
- Based on their thermal behavior, polymers can be classified into two groups: crystalline and amorphous.
  - Crystalline polymers are polymers that have ordered and regular arrangements of molecular chains, which result in sharp melting points and high density. They are usually opaque and rigid. Examples of crystalline polymers are polyethylene, polypropylene, and polytetrafluoroethylene.
  - Amorphous polymers are polymers that have random and irregular arrangements of molecular chains, which result in broad melting ranges and low density. They are usually transparent and flexible. Examples of amorphous polymers are polystyrene, polyvinyl chloride, and polycarbonate.
- Polymerization is the process of forming polymers from monomers. There are two main types of polymerization processes: chain polymerization and step polymerization.
  - Chain polymerization is a process in which monomers are added one by one to the growing polymer chain, which is initiated by a free radical, a cation, or an anion. The polymerization can be controlled by the concentration, temperature, and catalysts. Examples of chain polymerization are addition polymerization and coordination polymerization.
  - Step polymerization is a process in which monomers react with each other to form dimers, trimers, and so on, until a high molecular weight polymer is formed. The polymerization is driven by the elimination of a small molecule, such as water or ammonia. Examples of step polymerization are condensation polymerization and polyaddition.
