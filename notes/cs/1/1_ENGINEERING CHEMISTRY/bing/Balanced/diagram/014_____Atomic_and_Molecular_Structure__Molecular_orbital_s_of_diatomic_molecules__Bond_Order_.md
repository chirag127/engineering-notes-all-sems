### Atomic and Molecular Structure: Molecular orbitals of diatomic molecules, Bond Order

- Atomic orbitals are the regions of space around the nucleus of an atom where electrons are most likely to be found.
- Molecular orbitals are the regions of space around the nuclei of two or more atoms where electrons are most likely to be found when the atoms are bonded together.
- Molecular orbitals are formed by the combination of atomic orbitals of the same or similar energy and symmetry. There are two types of molecular orbitals: bonding and antibonding.
- Bonding molecular orbitals are lower in energy than the atomic orbitals they are formed from, and they have a higher electron density between the nuclei. They are stabilizing and contribute to the formation of a chemical bond.
- Antibonding molecular orbitals are higher in energy than the atomic orbitals they are formed from, and they have a lower electron density between the nuclei. They are destabilizing and weaken the chemical bond.
- The molecular orbital theory can be used to explain the bonding and properties of diatomic molecules, which are molecules composed of two atoms of the same or different elements.
- To construct the molecular orbital diagram of a diatomic molecule, the following steps are followed:
  - Identify the valence atomic orbitals of each atom and their energies.
  - Combine the atomic orbitals of the same symmetry and energy to form molecular orbitals. The number of molecular orbitals formed is equal to the number of atomic orbitals combined.
  - Arrange the molecular orbitals in order of increasing energy, and label them as sigma (σ) or pi (π) depending on their shape and orientation. Sigma orbitals have a single lobe along the internuclear axis, while pi orbitals have two lobes above and below the internuclear axis.
  - Fill the molecular orbitals with the valence electrons of the atoms, following the Aufbau principle, Hund's rule, and the Pauli exclusion principle. The Aufbau principle states that electrons occupy the lowest energy orbitals first. Hund's rule states that electrons occupy orbitals of equal energy with parallel spins before pairing up. The Pauli exclusion principle states that no two electrons can have the same four quantum numbers, which means that each orbital can hold a maximum of two electrons with opposite spins.
  - Determine the bond order, the bond length, and the bond energy of the molecule. The bond order is defined as half the difference between the number of bonding and antibonding electrons. The bond order indicates the strength and stability of the bond. A higher bond order means a stronger and shorter bond, and a lower bond order means a weaker and longer bond. A bond order of zero means that no bond is formed. The bond energy is the amount of energy required to break the bond.
