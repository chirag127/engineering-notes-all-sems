### 2. To enable the students to understand and apply the detailed concepts of spectroscopic techniques and

- Spectroscopy is the study of the interaction of electromagnetic radiation with matter. It can be used to identify the structure, composition, and properties of molecules and materials.
- Spectroscopic techniques are based on the principle that different types of atoms and bonds absorb or emit radiation at specific wavelengths or frequencies, creating characteristic spectra.
- Some of the common spectroscopic techniques are:

  - Ultraviolet-visible (UV-Vis) spectroscopy: measures the absorption of light in the ultraviolet and visible regions of the electromagnetic spectrum. It can be used to determine the concentration, color, and electronic structure of molecules.
  - Infrared (IR) spectroscopy: measures the absorption of infrared radiation by molecular vibrations. It can be used to identify the functional groups and molecular structure of organic compounds.
  - Nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR) spectroscopy: measures the absorption of radiofrequency radiation by nuclei in a magnetic field. It can be used to determine the number, type, and environment of atoms in a molecule.
  - Mass spectrometry (MS): measures the mass-to-charge ratio of ions produced by ionizing a sample. It can be used to determine the molecular weight, formula, and structure of compounds.
  - X-ray spectroscopy: measures the emission or scattering of X-rays by atoms or molecules. It can be used to determine the atomic structure, crystal structure, and chemical bonding of materials.

- To apply the detailed concepts of spectroscopic techniques, the students need to:

  - Understand the basic theory and principles of each technique, such as the selection rules, the energy levels, the transitions, and the instrumentation.
  - Know how to interpret the spectra and extract the relevant information, such as the peak positions, the intensities, the splitting patterns, and the chemical shifts.
  - Know how to correlate the spectra with the molecular structure and properties, such as the functional groups, the stereochemistry, the conformation, and the reactivity.
  - Know how to use the spectroscopic techniques in combination, such as UV-Vis and IR, NMR and MS, or X-ray and NMR, to obtain complementary and confirmatory data.
  - Know how to apply the spectroscopic techniques to solve real-world problems, such as the identification, characterization, and quantification of unknown compounds or materials.
