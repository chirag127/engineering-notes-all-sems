# Values, Gross & Net calorific value, Determination of calorific value by Bomb Calorimeter

- Values are the numerical quantities that represent the quality or quantity of something. For example, the value of a car is the price that someone is willing to pay for it.
- Calorific value is the amount of heat or energy produced by a fuel during complete combustion. It is measured in units of energy per unit mass or volume of fuel. For example, the calorific value of gasoline is about 44 MJ/kg or 32 MJ/L.
- Gross calorific value (GCV) is the calorific value of a fuel as determined by a bomb calorimeter. It is the total heat released when the fuel is burned completely, including the heat of condensation of the water vapour produced. For example, the GCV of coal is about 30 MJ/kg.
- Net calorific value (NCV) is the calorific value of a fuel as determined by subtracting the latent heat of condensation of the water vapour produced from the GCV. It is the actual heat available for use when the fuel is burned in a practical device, such as a boiler or an engine. For example, the NCV of coal is about 25 MJ/kg.
- A bomb calorimeter is an instrument used to measure the GCV of a fuel. It consists of a strong metal container (the bomb) filled with oxygen, a sample of fuel, and a device to ignite the fuel. The bomb is immersed in a known mass of water in an insulated vessel. The heat released by the combustion of the fuel is transferred to the water, and the temperature rise of the water is measured. The GCV of the fuel can be calculated by using the formula:

  GCV = (m * c * ΔT) / w

  where m is the mass of water, c is the specific heat capacity of water, ΔT is the temperature rise of water, and w is the mass of fuel.
