# UV, IR and NMR, Numerical problems

## Introduction

- UV, IR and NMR are spectroscopic techniques that can be used to identify the structure and functional groups of organic compounds.
- UV (ultraviolet) spectroscopy measures the absorption of light in the UV-visible region of the electromagnetic spectrum by molecules with conjugated pi systems.
- IR (infrared) spectroscopy measures the absorption of light in the infrared region of the electromagnetic spectrum by molecules with polar covalent bonds.
- NMR (nuclear magnetic resonance) spectroscopy measures the absorption of radio waves by nuclei with a magnetic moment in a magnetic field.

## Numerical problems

- To solve numerical problems involving UV, IR and NMR spectroscopy, one needs to apply the following steps:
  - Determine the molecular formula of the compound from the mass spectrum or other information.
  - Calculate the degree of unsaturation from the molecular formula to determine the number of rings and/or pi bonds in the compound.
  - Analyze the UV spectrum to identify the presence and extent of conjugation in the compound.
  - Analyze the IR spectrum to identify the functional groups and the types of bonds in the compound.
  - Analyze the NMR spectrum to identify the number and environment of the protons and/or carbons in the compound.
  - Use the information from the spectra to deduce the possible structures of the compound and compare them with the molecular formula and the degree of unsaturation.
  - Confirm the structure of the compound by comparing the predicted and observed chemical shifts, coupling constants, and peak intensities in the NMR spectrum.

## Example problem

- Given the following spectra of an unknown compound with molecular formula C<sub>9</sub>H<sub>10</sub>O, determine its structure.

![UV spectrum](https://www2.chemistry.msu.edu/faculty/reusch/VirtTxtJml/Questions/Spectroscopy/UV/uv-1.gif)

![IR spectrum](https://www2.chemistry.msu.edu/faculty/reusch/VirtTxtJml/Questions/Spectroscopy/IR/ir-1.gif)

![NMR spectrum](https://www2.chemistry.msu.edu/faculty/reusch/VirtTxtJml/Questions/Spectroscopy/NMR/nmr-1.gif)

- Solution:
  - The molecular formula C<sub>9</sub>H<sub>10</sub>O has a degree of unsaturation of 4, which means there are four rings and/or pi bonds in the compound.
  - The UV spectrum shows a peak at 260 nm, which indicates the presence of a conjugated system of at least three pi bonds.
  - The IR spectrum shows a strong peak at 1710 cm<sup>-1</sup>, which indicates the presence of a carbonyl group (C=O). There are also peaks at 3000-2850 cm<sup>-1</sup> and 1500-1400 cm<sup>-1</sup>, which indicate the presence of sp<sup>3</sup> hybridized C-H bonds. There are no peaks for O-H, N-H, or C=C bonds.
  - The NMR spectrum shows four signals in the proton region and two signals in the carbon region. The signal at 9.8 ppm (1H, s) corresponds to the proton of the aldehyde group (CHO). The signal at 7.4 ppm (2H, d, J = 8 Hz) corresponds to the protons of the aromatic ring that are ortho to the aldehyde group. The signal at 7.1 ppm (2H, t, J = 8 Hz) corresponds to the protons of the aromatic ring that are meta to the aldehyde group. The signal at 2.4 ppm (3H, s) corresponds to the protons of the methyl group that is attached to the aromatic ring. The signal at 192 ppm (1C, s) corresponds to the carbon of the aldehyde group. The signal at 137 ppm (4C, s) corresponds to the carbons of the aromatic ring.
  - The structure of the compound that is consistent with the spectra and the molecular formula is 4-methylbenzaldehyde.
