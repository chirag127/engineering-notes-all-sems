# Polymer Composites

- A polymer composite is a multi-phase material in which reinforcing fillers are integrated with a polymer matrix, resulting in synergistic mechanical properties that cannot be achieved from either component alone.
- A polymer is a natural or synthetic substance composed of very large molecules, called macromolecules, that are multiples of simpler chemical units called monomers.
- A polymer composite can be classified according to the type of polymer matrix, the type of reinforcement, the shape and size of reinforcement, and the processing method.
- The most common types of polymer matrices are thermosets and thermoplastics. Thermosets are polymers that undergo irreversible chemical reactions when heated and cured, forming a rigid and cross-linked structure. Thermoplastics are polymers that can be melted and reshaped repeatedly by heating and cooling, without changing their chemical structure.
- The most common types of reinforcements are fibers, particles, and flakes. Fibers are long and slender materials that have high strength and stiffness along their length. Particles are small and irregular materials that have isotropic properties and can improve the wear resistance and toughness of the composite. Flakes are thin and flat materials that have high aspect ratio and can enhance the thermal and electrical conductivity of the composite.
- The most common shapes and sizes of reinforcements are continuous and discontinuous. Continuous reinforcements are long and unbroken fibers that are aligned or randomly oriented in the polymer matrix, providing high strength and stiffness along the fiber direction. Discontinuous reinforcements are short and broken fibers, particles, or flakes that are randomly distributed in the polymer matrix, providing isotropic properties and better processability.
- The most common processing methods are molding, extrusion, injection, and pultrusion. Molding is a process of shaping the composite by applying heat and pressure to a mold that contains the polymer and reinforcement. Extrusion is a process of forcing the composite through a die that has a desired cross-sectional shape. Injection is a process of injecting the molten composite into a mold cavity that has a desired shape. Pultrusion is a process of pulling the continuous fiber reinforcement through a resin bath and a heated die that has a desired cross-sectional shape.
