# Organometallic Compounds (RMgX and LiAlH4)

Organometallic compounds are compounds that contain a metal-carbon bond, R-M, where R is an organic group and M is a metal. Organometallic compounds of Li and Mg are among the most important and widely used reagents in organic synthesis. They are strong nucleophiles and bases that can react with various electrophiles, especially carbonyl compounds.

## RMgX: Grignard Reagents

- RMgX, where X is a halide (Cl, Br, I), are called Grignard reagents, named after Victor Grignard, who discovered them in 1900 and won the Nobel Prize in 1912.
- Grignard reagents are prepared by reacting an alkyl or aryl halide with magnesium metal in an anhydrous ether solvent, such as diethyl ether or tetrahydrofuran (THF).
- Grignard reagents are written as R-Mg-X, but in fact the magnesium(II) center is tetrahedral when dissolved in Lewis basic solvents, as shown here for the bis-adduct of methylmagnesium chloride and THF:

![Methylmagnesium chloride THF adduct](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/9a/Methylmagnesium_chloride_THF_adduct.png/220px-Methylmagnesium_chloride_THF_adduct.png)

- Grignard reagents are very reactive and can react with water, alcohols, amines, and other protic compounds to form alkanes. Therefore, they must be prepared and used under dry and inert conditions.
- Grignard reagents can react with organic carbonyls (aldehydes, ketones, and esters) to yield the alcohol on hydrolysis, as shown below. This synthetic route is useful for the formation of primary, secondary and tertiary alcohols.

![Grignard reaction with carbonyls](https://chem.libretexts.org/@api/deki/files/222463/Grignard_reaction_with_carbonyls.png)

- Grignard reagents can also react with other electrophiles, such as epoxides, nitriles, carbon dioxide, and halogens, to form various organic products.

## LiAlH4: Lithium Aluminium Hydride

- LiAlH4, also known as LAH, is a hydride reagent that can reduce various carbonyl compounds to alcohols, as well as other functional groups, such as carboxylic acids, esters, amides, nitriles, and nitro groups, to their corresponding amines.
- LiAlH4 is prepared by reacting lithium hydride with aluminium chloride in an ether solvent. It is a white solid that is pyrophoric, meaning it can ignite spontaneously in air. Therefore, it must be handled with care and under inert conditions.
- LiAlH4 is written as Li-Al-H4, but in fact the aluminium(III) center is tetrahedral when dissolved in ether solvents, as shown here for the bis-adduct of LiAlH4 and THF:

![LiAlH4 THF adduct](http://colapret.cm.utexas.edu/courses/LiAlH4_THF_adduct.png)

- LiAlH4 can react with water, alcohols, amines, and other protic compounds to form hydrogen gas and the corresponding alkoxide, amide, or hydride salt. Therefore, it must be used under dry and inert conditions.
- LiAlH4 can reduce carbonyl compounds to alcohols by transferring a hydride ion (H-) to the electrophilic carbon of the carbonyl group, followed by hydrolysis, as shown below. This synthetic route is useful for the formation of primary, secondary and tertiary alcohols.
