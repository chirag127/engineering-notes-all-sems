Synthesis of Adipic Acid:

Adipic acid is a dicarboxylic acid used in the production of nylon and other polymers. There are two main methods for synthesizing adipic acid: conventional and green routes.

Conventional Route:

The conventional route for synthesizing adipic acid involves the oxidation of cyclohexanol with nitric acid. This process is energy-intensive and produces significant amounts of nitrogen oxides, which are harmful to the environment.

Advantages of the conventional route:

1. High yield: The conventional route for synthesizing adipic acid results in high yields of the product.

2. Established technology: The conventional route for synthesizing adipic acid is an established technology and has been used for many years.

Disadvantages of the conventional route:

1. Environmental impact: The conventional route for synthesizing adipic acid produces significant amounts of nitrogen oxides, which are harmful to the environment.

2. Energy-intensive: The conventional route for synthesizing adipic acid is energy-intensive, making it expensive and less environmentally friendly.

Green Route:

The green route for synthesizing adipic acid involves the fermentation of glucose to produce cyclohexanol, which is then oxidized to form adipic acid. This process is less energy-intensive and produces fewer harmful by-products compared to the conventional route.

Advantages of the green route:

1. Environmental benefits: The green route for synthesizing adipic acid produces fewer harmful by-products compared to the conventional route, making it a more environmentally friendly option.

2. Energy-efficient: The green route for synthesizing adipic acid is less energy-intensive compared to the conventional route, making it a more energy-efficient option.

Disadvantages of the green route:

1. Lower yield: The green route for synthesizing adipic acid results in lower yields of the product compared to the conventional route.

2. Emerging technology: The green route for synthesizing adipic acid is an emerging technology and may still have some technical challenges.
