### Batteries; Classification and applications of Primary Cells (Dry Cell) and Secondary Cells.

- Primary cells or dry cells: These cells cannot be recharged. The common types are:
-- Alkaline cells: Have an alkaline electrolyte of potassium hydroxide and provide a voltage of 1.5 V. They have a long shelf life and are used in low-drain devices like remote controls, clocks, etc.
-- Carbon-zinc cells: Have an acidic electrolyte and provide 1.5 V. They have a shorter shelf life and lower capacity but are cheaper than alkaline cells. They are used in devices like toys, radios, etc.
-- Lithium cells: Have metallic lithium as anode and provide a high voltage of 3 V. They have a long shelf life but are more expensive. They are used in devices like calculators, digital watches, etc.

- Secondary cells or rechargeable cells: These cells can be recharged to reuse. The common types are:
-- Lead-acid cells: Have lead electrodes and sulfuric acid electrolyte. They provide 2 V per cell and are used in automotive batteries.
-- Nickel-cadmium cells (NiCd): Have nickel hydroxide cathode and cadmium anode. They provide 1.2 V and are used in power tools. However, cadmium is toxic.
-- Nickel-metal hydride cells (NiMH): Similar to NiCd but have hydrogen-absorbing alloy anode. They provide 1.2 V, have higher capacity but suffer from memory effect. They are used in mobile phones and laptops.
-- Lithium-ion cells: Have lithium-intercalated carbon anode and lithiated metal oxide cathode. They provide 3.6-3.7 V and high energy density. They are used in consumer electronics but can be a fire hazard if overcharged or short-circuited.
