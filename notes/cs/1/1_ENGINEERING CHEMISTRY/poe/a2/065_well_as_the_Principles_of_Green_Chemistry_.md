# Principles of Green Chemistry

1. Prevent waste: Design chemical syntheses to prevent waste, leaving no waste to treat or clean up.
2. Design safer chemicals and products: Design chemical products to be fully effective, yet have little or no toxicity.
3. Design less hazardous chemical syntheses: Design syntheses to use and generate substances that possess little or no toxicity to human health and the environment.
4. Use renewable feedstocks: Use raw materials and feedstocks that are renewable rather than depleting. Renewable feedstocks are often made from agricultural products or are the wastes of other processes; depleting feedstocks are made from fossil fuels (petroleum, natural gas, or coal) or are mined.
5. Use catalysts, not stoichiometric reagents: Minimize waste by using catalytic reactions that achieve high atom economy.
6. Design energy efficient processes: Design chemical processes that minimize the need for auxiliary energy by using energy efficient techniques.
7. Use "green" solvents: Avoid using solvents, but if required, use innocuous solvents that are recycled and are not air pollutants.
8. Reduce derivatives: Minimize the use of blocking groups, protection/deprotection chemistry, and other temporary modifications whenever possible.

The principles of green chemistry aim to design and develop chemical products and processes that reduce or eliminate the use and generation of hazardous substances. Following these principles can help make the practice of chemistry more sustainable.
