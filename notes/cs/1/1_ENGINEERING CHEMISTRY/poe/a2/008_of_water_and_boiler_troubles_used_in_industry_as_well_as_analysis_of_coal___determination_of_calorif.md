# Water and Boiler Troubles in Industry

1. Scale Formation: Hard water contains calcium and magnesium salts which get deposited as scale in boilers. Scale reduces heat transfer and can lead to overheating and failure of boiler tubes. Scale inhibitors can be used to prevent scale formation.
2. Corrosion: Boiler water can corrode the metal surface of boilers. Oxygen and acids present in water lead to corrosion which can be prevented using alkalizing agents and oxygen scavengers.
3. Foaming: Impurities like oils, fats and suspended solids can lead to foaming in boilers which reduces heat transfer and can carry over to steam pipes and turbine. Foaming can be reduced using antifoaming agents and better pretreatment of feed water.
4. Priming: Rapid generation of steam bubbles in boilers can lead to priming which is the carryover of water into the steam. It can damage steam pipes and turbines. Priming can be reduced by controlling boiler feed rate and ensuring proper boiler water chemistry.

# Analysis of Coal and Determination of Calorific Value

1. Proximate analysis determines moisture, ash, volatile matter and fixed carbon in coal which indicates the rank of coal and its suitability for different applications.
2. Ultimate analysis determines the elemental composition of carbon, hydrogen, nitrogen, sulfur and oxygen in coal which indicates the energy content and purity of coal.
3. Calorific value can be determined using calorimeters which measure the heat released by burning a known mass of coal sample. Higher calorific value indicates more energy release per unit mass of coal.
