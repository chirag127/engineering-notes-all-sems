# Thiokol, Nylon, Buna-N and Buna-S and their environmental impact on society

## Speciality

- Thiokol: Thiokol is a polymer created from the reaction of epichlorohydrin and sodium or potassium hydroxide. It is resistant to fuels, oils and acids. However, its production releases carcinogenic vinyl chloride and its disposal releases toxic hydrogen chloride gas impacting air and water quality.
- Nylon: Nylon is a synthetic polyamide polymer. Its production requires toxic precursors and releases greenhouse gases. Its non-biodegradable nature leads to accumulation in the environment polluting land and water bodies.
- Buna-N and Buna-S: Buna-N and Buna-S are copolymers of butadiene and acrylonitrile. Their production requires toxic chemicals and releases carcinogenic fumes. Though highly chemical resistant, their non-biodegradable nature and hazardous disposed waste impact the environment negatively.

In summary, though the above polymers have valuable properties and applications, their production using toxic chemicals and non-biodegradable nature puts environmental sustainability at risk by polluting air, water and soil and harming human and ecosystem health. More eco-friendly alternatives and disposal methods need to be researched and adopted to mitigate their environmental impact.
