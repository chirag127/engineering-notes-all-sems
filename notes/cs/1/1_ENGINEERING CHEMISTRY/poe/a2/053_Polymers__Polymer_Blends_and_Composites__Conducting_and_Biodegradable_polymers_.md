# Polymers

- Polymers are long chain molecules composed of repeating structural units.
- The structural units are monomers which are joined together by covalent bonds to form a polymer chain.
- Polymers can be classified into natural and synthetic polymers. Natural polymers include rubber, silk, wool, etc. Synthetic polymers include plastics like polyethylene, polyvinyl chloride, etc.
- Polymers exhibit properties such as high molecular weight, lack of fixed melting and boiling points, etc. These properties make polymers useful for various applications.
- Some applications of polymers include packaging, adhesives, coatings, biomedical applications, etc.

# Polymer Blends and Composites

- Polymer blends are mixtures of two or more polymers. They are prepared by melting and mixing polymers together.
- Polymer composites contain a polymer matrix and a reinforcing filler material. The filler material can be fibers, particles, flakes, etc.
- Blends and composites are prepared to combine desired properties of different polymers or to improve properties like mechanical strength, thermal stability, etc.
- Blends can exhibit properties like immiscibility where the polymers exist as separate phases. Composites can exhibit interfacial interactions between the polymer and filler material.
- Applications of blends and composites include automotive parts, pipes, food packaging, construction materials, electronic components, etc.

# Conducting and Biodegradable Polymers

- Conducting polymers have a π-electron system which makes them electrically conductive. Examples include polyacetylene, polyaniline, polypyrrole, etc.
- These polymers can undergo oxidation and reduction reactions leading to changes in conductivity. This property makes them useful in applications such as batteries, electrochromic devices, electromagnetic shields, etc.
- Biodegradable polymers undergo degradation upon exposure to soil or water. This is useful for applications where degradation is desired like biomedical implants or packaging materials.
- Examples of biodegradable polymers include polylactic acid, polyglycolic acid, polycaprolactone, starch, etc. These polymers can be synthesized from renewable materials and degrade into non-toxic products.
