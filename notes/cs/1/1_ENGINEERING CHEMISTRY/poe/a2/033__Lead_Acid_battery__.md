# Lead Acid battery

- Lead acid batteries consist of lead plates immersed in an electrolyte of sulfuric acid and water.
- When the battery is charging, the lead plates react with the sulfuric acid to form lead sulfate and electrons. The electrons flow out of the battery to power a device.
- When the battery is in use, the lead sulfate decomposes back into lead and sulfuric acid, and the electrons flow from the device back into the battery.
- The sulfuric acid is the conductive electrolyte that transports the electrons between the lead plates. But the acid is not used up in the process. It is regenerated during both charge and discharge cycles.
- The battery capacity depends on the amount of lead used in the plates. The more lead provided, the more electrons can be produced and the more energy can be stored.
- However, the lead plates can deteriorate over many charge-discharge cycles, resulting in battery degradation and eventual failure. The electrolyte can also break down over time due to high temperatures.
- To maximize battery lifespan, slow charge and discharge rates should be used and high temperatures should be avoided. Lead acid batteries should also be fully charged before long term storage.
- Due to the chemical reactions involved, lead acid batteries have relatively low energy densities compared to other rechargeable battery types. But they are inexpensive to manufacture and are very tolerant of abuse like overcharging or short circuiting. This makes them well suited to applications like car batteries where cost and safety are more important than energy density.
