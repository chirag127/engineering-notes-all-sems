### CO-5 Develop the understanding of Chemical structure of polymers and its effect on their various properties.

1. Polymers are long chain molecular structures formed by repeating structural units. The structure of a polymer is determined by the structure of the repeating unit and the chain geometry.
2. The chemical structure of a polymer decides its properties. Some key properties influenced by chemical structure are:
- Molecular weight: Polymers with higher molecular weight have higher viscosity and mechanical strength.
- Branching: More branching leads to lower crystallinity and density. Branched polymers have lower melt viscosity and higher impact strength.
- Cross-linking: Higher cross-linking leads to a three-dimensional network structure with higher strength and stiffness but lower elongation.
- Crystallinity: Higher crystallinity leads to higher density, melting point and mechanical strength but lower transparency and solubility.
- Polar groups: Presence of polar groups leads to higher interaction with other materials, better adhesion but lower chemical resistance.
3. By controlling the chemical structure, polymers can be designed for specific applications to achieve the necessary properties. For example, high molecular weight and less branching is suitable for high mechanical strength while more branching is suitable for improved impact strength. Controlling crystallinity can adjust mechanical, thermal and optical properties. Inclusion of polar groups enables better adhesion while reducing them leads to better chemical resistance.
