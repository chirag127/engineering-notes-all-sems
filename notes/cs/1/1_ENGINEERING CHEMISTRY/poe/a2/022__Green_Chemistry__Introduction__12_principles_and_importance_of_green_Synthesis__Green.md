### Green Chemistry: Introduction, 12 principles and importance of green Synthesis

Green Chemistry refers to the design of chemical products and processes that reduce or eliminate the use or generation of hazardous substances. It focuses on the development of innovative chemical technologies that prevent pollution and minimize adverse health and environmental impacts of chemicals.

The 12 Principles of Green Chemistry are:

1. Prevent waste: Design chemical syntheses to prevent waste, leaving no waste to treat or clean up.
2. Design safer chemicals and products: Design chemical products to be fully effective, yet have little or no toxicity.
3. Design less hazardous chemical syntheses: Design syntheses to use and generate substances that have little or no toxicity to humans and the environment.
4. Use renewable feedstocks: Use raw materials and feedstocks that are renewable rather than depleting. Renewable feedstocks are often made from agricultural products or are the wastes of other processes; depleting feedstocks are made from fossil fuels (petroleum, natural gas, or coal) or mined.
5. Use catalysts, not stoichiometric reagents: Minimize waste by using catalytic reactions. Catalysts are used in small amounts and can carry out a reaction many times. They are preferable to stoichiometric reagents, which are used in excess and discarded.
6. Avoid chemical derivatives: Avoid using blocking groups or protection/ deprotection in synthesis whenever possible. Derivatives use additional reagents and generate waste.
7. Maximize atom economy: Design syntheses to maximize the incorporation of all materials used in the process into the final product.
8. Use safer solvents and separation processes: Avoid using volatile organic compounds (VOCs) as solvents. Use or develop separation processes that recycle solvents and reagents.
9. Increase energy efficiency: Run chemical reactions at ambient temperature and pressure whenever possible. Minimize the need for auxiliary energy consumption.
10. Design chemicals and products to degrade after use: Design chemical products to break down to innocuous substances after use so that they do not persist in the environment.
11. Analyze in real time to prevent pollution: Use analytical methodologies that allow for real-time monitoring and control of synthetic processes.
12. Minimize the potential for accidents: Design processes to minimize the potential for accidents and minimize the consequences if they occur.

Green synthesis refers to sustainable and environmentally-friendly methods of synthesizing chemicals. It has gained significant importance in recent years due to the growing concerns over non-renewable resources and climate change. Green synthesis can help make industrial chemistry more sustainable by reducing solvents, eliminating toxic chemicals and reusing byproducts.
