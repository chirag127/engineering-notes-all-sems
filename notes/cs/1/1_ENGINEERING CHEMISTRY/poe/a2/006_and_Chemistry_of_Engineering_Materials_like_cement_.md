# Chemistry of Engineering Materials: Cement

1. Cement is a binding material used in construction that hardens and adheres to other materials. The key components of cement are calcium silicates and calcium aluminates that form hydrated calcium silicate and calcium aluminate during hydration.
2. The raw materials for cement production are limestone, clay, and gypsum. Limestone contains calcium carbonate that is heated to produce calcium oxide and carbon dioxide. Clay and gypsum contain silica, alumina, and sulfur that react with calcium oxide to form cement.
3. The cement production process involves several steps:
 - Raw material acquisition: Raw materials are extracted from quarries or mines and transported to the cement plant.
 - Raw material preparation: Raw materials are crushed and ground into a fine powder.
 - Burning: The powder is heated to high temperatures to form lumps called clinkers.
 - Clinker grinding: Clinkers are cooled and ground with gypsum to produce cement.
 - Cement grinding: The cement is ground and blended to achieve the desired properties.
4. The key chemical reactions that occur during cement hydration are:
 - Dissolution of cement in water
 - Formation of hydration products: Calcium silicate hydrate (C-S-H) and calcium hydroxide Ca(OH)2
 - Formation of crystalline hydration products by reorganization of C-S-H
 - Changes in pore solution chemistry and pH due to the formation of Ca(OH)2
