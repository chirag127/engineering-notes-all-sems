# Processing Industry, Oil & Gas Industry and Pulp & Paper Industries

## Processing Industry

1. Processing industries involve the transformation of raw materials into useful products through methods such as drying, preserving, packaging, etc. For example: Sugar industry, Jute industry, Cotton industry, etc.
2. These industries are dependent on the availability of raw materials and have a widespread market for their finished products.
3. They generate by-products that can be useful as raw materials for other industries. For example: Molasses from sugar industry is used as cattle feed.
4. They are generally located close to the source of raw materials to minimize costs of transportation and storage.

## Oil & Gas Industry

1. Oil & gas industry involves exploration, extraction, refining, and marketing of petroleum products.
2. Crude oil is the raw material extracted from oil fields using methods such as drilling. It is then transported to refineries by pipelines or ships.
3. In refineries, crude oil is separated into various fractions by fraction distillation process. The fractions are further processed to produce various petroleum products like petrol, diesel, kerosene oil, etc.
4. The location of oil fields and refineries depends on the availability of crude oil and market demand respectively. This industry has a substantial impact on national economies and international relations.

## Pulp & Paper Industry

1. The pulp and paper industry processes wood and recycled fiber into paper products. The key steps involve:
- Harvesting and transporting wood/recycled fiber
- Producing pulp from fiber
- Producing paper from pulp
- Converting paper into various products like notebooks, cartons, etc.
2. This industry is highly resource-intensive and a major consumer of water and electricity. It has a significant environmental impact due to deforestation and pollution.
3. The location of pulp and paper mills depends on the proximity to raw materials and resources as well as the market for paper products. This industry is declining in developed nations due to environmental concerns while expanding in developing countries due to increasing demand.
