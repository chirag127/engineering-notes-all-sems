### Graphite and Fullerene; Introduction, Structure and applications.

1. Introduction
- Graphite: Graphite is a crystalline form of carbon consisting of layered sheets of carbon atoms arranged in a hexagonal lattice. It is a soft, dark gray, slippery solid.
- Fullerene: Fullerenes are a class of allotropes of carbon which form a hollow sphere, ellipsoid, tube, or many other shapes. They are made from 60 to 180 atoms arranged in hexagonal and pentagonal rings. C60 fullerene is the most common structure which resembles a soccer ball.

2. Structure
- Graphite: Each layer of carbon atoms in graphite is arranged in a hexagonal lattice. The layers are held together by weak van der Waals forces, allowing them to slide over each other, giving graphite its soft, slippery properties.
- Fullerene: Fullerenes have a hollow cage-like structure with pentagonal and hexagonal faces. The carbon atoms are arranged in hexagonal and pentagonal rings which join to form a closed polyhedral structure. The C60 fullerene resembles a soccer ball with 20 hexagonal faces and 12 pentagonal faces.

3. Applications
- Graphite: Due to its layered structure, graphite has applications in lubricants and as a dry lubricant. It is also used in pencil lead, steel production, batteries, and nuclear reactors.
- Fullerene: Fullerenes have applications in materials science, electronics, and medicine. C60 is a superconducter and can be used to produce strong and lightweight composite materials. Fullerenes are also candidates for drug delivery and have antioxidant properties which can be harnessed for therapeutic applications.
