### Spectroscopic Techniques and Applications: Elementary idea and simple applications of

1. Spectroscopy: Study of interaction between matter and electromagnetic radiation. It is used to obtain information about the structure, composition and properties of matter.
2. Types of Spectroscopy: Based on the nature of radiation involved.
    - Optical Spectroscopy: Uses visible, ultraviolet and infrared light. Eg: Absorption, emission, fluorescence and Raman spectroscopy.
    - Radiofrequency Spectroscopy: Uses microwave and radio waves. Eg: NMR and EPR spectroscopy.
    - Mass Spectrometry: Uses electron beam or laser beam to ionize the sample and the ions are separated based on their mass to charge ratio.
3. Absorption Spectroscopy: Measures the amount of light absorbed by a sample at specific wavelengths. It is used to determine concentration of a substance and study electronic transitions.
4. Emission Spectroscopy: Samples emit light at specific wavelengths that can be analyzed. Flame emission and atomic emission spectroscopy are examples. Used to analyze metal ions and identify elements.
5. Fluorescence Spectroscopy: Measures emission of light by samples after excitation with light. Provides information on structure, composition and environment of molecules. Used in biochemistry and medicine.
6. Raman Spectroscopy: Measures inelastic scattering of monochromatic light. It provides information on vibrational, rotational and other low-frequency transitions in molecules. Used in chemistry and biochemistry to identify molecules.
