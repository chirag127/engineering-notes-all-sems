### Polymers; Classification, Polymerization processes, Thermosetting and Thermoplastic

Polymers are long chain macromolecules formed by joining together many small molecules called monomers. Based on the structure of the backbone chain, polymers are classified as:

1. Linear polymers: The monomers are arranged in a linear fashion. Examples are polyethylene, polyvinyl chloride, etc.
2. Branched chain polymers: The backbone chain has side branches. Examples are low density polyethylene, etc.
3. Cross-linked polymers: The polymer chains are joined together by cross-links. Examples are vulcanized rubber, epoxy resins, etc.

Polymerization is the process of combining monomers to form polymers. Based on the type of polymerization, the processes are:

1. Addition polymerization: Where monomers are added to the growing chain one after the other. Examples are polyethylene, polypropylene, etc.
2. Condensation polymerization: Where two monomers combine with the loss of a small molecule like water. Examples are nylon, polyester, etc.

Based on their behavior on heating, polymers are classified into:

1. Thermosetting polymers: Polymers that undergo an irreversible change on heating and cannot be melted and remolded. Examples are phenol-formaldehyde, epoxy resins, etc.
2. Thermoplastic polymers: Polymers that soften on heating and can be melted and remolded. Examples are polyethylene, polyvinyl chloride, nylon, polyester, etc.
