### Electrochemistry and Batteries: Basic concepts of electrochemistry.

1. Electrochemistry is the study of production of electricity from energy released during spontaneous chemical reactions and the use of electrical energy to bring about non-spontaneous chemical transformations.
2. A battery is a device that converts the chemical energy into electrical energy. It consists of three main components: an anode, a cathode, and an electrolyte.
3. The anode is the negative electrode where oxidation occurs and electrons are produced. The cathode is the positive electrode where reduction occurs and electrons are consumed. The electrolyte allows the movement of ions between the electrodes.
4. When the battery is in use, the following reaction occurs: the anode material undergoes oxidation by losing electrons to the external circuit, and the cathode undergoes reduction by gaining electrons from the external circuit. The net flow of electrons around the circuit can be used to power an external device.
5. The voltage of a battery depends on the standard electrode potentials of the redox reactions occurring at the two electrodes. The capacity or energy density of the battery depends on the amount of redox materials that can undergo the reversible electrochemical reactions. Rechargeable batteries can undergo multiple discharge-charge cycles.
6. Primary batteries, for example, alkaline batteries, produce electricity from the irreversible reaction of electrode materials and have a finite lifetime. Secondary or rechargeable batteries, for example, lead-acid and lithium-ion batteries, can be recharged by applying an external voltage to reverse the electrochemical reactions, thereby re-depositing the electrode materials.
