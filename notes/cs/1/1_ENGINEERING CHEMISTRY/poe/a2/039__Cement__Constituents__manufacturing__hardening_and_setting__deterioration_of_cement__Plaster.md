### Cement; Constituents, manufacturing, hardening and setting, deterioration of cement, Plaster

**Constituents**: The raw materials for cement are limestone, clay, gypsum, and shale. These materials are mixed in the proper proportions to form raw meal which is heated to produce clinker. The clinker is then ground with gypsum to produce cement.

**Manufacturing**: The manufacturing process involves the following steps:

1. Proportioning: Raw materials are proportioned based on their composition to yield the desired composition of clinker.
2. Grinding: Raw materials are ground to produce a fine powder called raw meal.
3. Burning: The raw meal is burnt in a kiln at high temperature to produce clinker.
4. Grinding: The clinker is ground with gypsum to produce cement.

**Hardening and Setting**: When water is added to cement, the hardening process starts. The hardening and setting of cement occur in two stages:

1. Setting: When water is added to cement, the initial setting of cement starts. During setting, the cement paste loses its plasticity and becomes firm. Setting time can be determined by using Vicat apparatus.
2. Hardening: After setting, the hardening of cement starts. In this process, the cement continues to strengthen and harden. The hardening process may continue for several years. The rate of hardening depends on factors like curing temperature and humidity.

**Deterioration of cement**: The deterioration of cement can occur due to factors like inadequate mixing or curing, excessive heat, sulfates in water, freezing of water in pores, leaching, alkali-aggregate reaction, etc. This can lead to decreased strength and durability of cement.

**Plaster**: Plaster is a mixture of calcined gypsum, sand, and water. When the mixture is applied to a surface, the calcined gypsum reacts with water and sets into a hard mass. Plaster can be finished smooth or textured depending upon application. It is mainly used for decorative purposes on walls and ceilings.
