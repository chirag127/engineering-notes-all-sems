## Hours

1. There are 24 hours in a day. This is the standard way to keep time and is based on the solar day.
2. The 24 hours are divided into two sets of 12 hours each. The 12 hours from midnight to noon are called am (ante meridiem) and the 12 hours from noon to midnight are called pm (post meridiem).
3. Coordinated Universal Time (UTC) is the basis for all other time zones worldwide. It corresponds to the solar time along the prime meridian (0° longitude). Other time zones are defined as offsets from UTC, ranging from UTC-12 to UTC+14.
4. The most commonly used time zones are based on hourly offsets from UTC, such as UTC-5 for Eastern Time Zone (ET) which is used in the eastern United States and Canada, and UTC+8 for Singapore Time. Many locations adjust their time for daylight saving in the summer.
5. The International Date Line (IDL) is an imaginary line extending between the North Pole and South Pole but deviating in places to avoid dividing land areas. When crossing the IDL, one day is either added or subtracted. For example, when flying west from North America to Asia, one day is added.
