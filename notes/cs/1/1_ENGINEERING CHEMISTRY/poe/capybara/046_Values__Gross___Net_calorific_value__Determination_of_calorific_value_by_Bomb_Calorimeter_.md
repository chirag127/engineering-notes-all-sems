# Values

In the field of thermodynamics, there are two types of calorific values: gross and net calorific values.

## Gross Calorific Value

Gross Calorific Value (GCV) is the amount of heat released when a fuel is burned completely. The heat includes the latent heat of vaporization of the water in the fuel, which is released during combustion. The GCV is considered the higher heating value of the fuel.

## Net Calorific Value

Net Calorific Value (NCV) is the amount of heat released when a fuel is burned completely, but the heat of vaporization of the water in the fuel is not considered. NCV is the lower heating value of the fuel.

# Determination of Calorific Value by Bomb Calorimeter

Bomb calorimeter is an instrument used to determine the calorific value of a fuel. It consists of a bomb, which is a strong, sealed container made of metal, and an ignition device to ignite the fuel.

The following steps are followed to determine the calorific value of a fuel:

1. A sample of the fuel is weighed and placed inside the bomb.
2. Oxygen is added to the bomb to ensure complete combustion of the fuel.
3. The bomb is then sealed and placed inside the calorimeter, which is filled with water.
4. The fuel is ignited, and the heat from the combustion is transferred to the water in the calorimeter.
5. The increase in temperature of the water is measured, and the calorific value of the fuel is calculated using the formula:

```
Calorific Value = Heat Released / Mass of Fuel Burnt
```

In conclusion, understanding the values of gross and net calorific value, as well as the process of determining the calorific value by bomb calorimeter, is crucial in the field of thermodynamics and energy.
