# Process: Determination of Hardness and Alkalinity

## Hardness
- Hardness of water is caused by the presence of calcium and magnesium ions.
- Hardness is defined as the sum of the concentrations of calcium and magnesium ions in water.
- The total hardness of water can be determined by titration with a standard solution of ethylenediaminetetraacetic acid (EDTA).
- The reaction between EDTA and calcium or magnesium ions is a complexometric reaction.
- The end point of the titration is determined by using an indicator called Eriochrome Black T (EBT).
- The indicator changes color from wine red to blue when all the calcium and magnesium ions have reacted with EDTA.

### Procedure
1. Take a 50 mL sample of water in a conical flask.
2. Add 3-4 drops of EBT indicator to the sample.
3. Titrate the sample with a standard solution of EDTA until the color changes from wine red to blue.
4. Note the volume of EDTA solution used for titration.
5. Repeat the titration for accuracy.
6. Calculate the total hardness of water using the following formula:

   Total Hardness (in mg/L) = Volume of EDTA solution used x Normality of EDTA solution x 1000 / Volume of water sample

## Alkalinity
- Alkalinity of water is caused by the presence of bicarbonate, carbonate, and hydroxide ions.
- Alkalinity is defined as the sum of the concentrations of bicarbonate, carbonate, and hydroxide ions in water.
- The alkalinity of water can be determined by titration with a standard solution of sulfuric acid (H2SO4).
- The reaction between H2SO4 and bicarbonate, carbonate, and hydroxide ions is an acid-base reaction.
- The end point of the titration is determined by using an indicator called phenolphthalein.
- The indicator changes color from pink to colorless when all the bicarbonate, carbonate, and hydroxide ions have reacted with H2SO4.

### Procedure
1. Take a 50 mL sample of water in a conical flask.
2. Add 3-4 drops of phenolphthalein indicator to the sample.
3. Titrate the sample with a standard solution of H2SO4 until the color changes from pink to colorless.
4. Note the volume of H2SO4 solution used for titration.
5. Repeat the titration for accuracy.
6. Calculate the alkalinity of water using the following formula:

   Alkalinity (in mg/L) = Volume of H2SO4 solution used x Normality of H2SO4 solution x 50000 / Volume of water sample
   
## Numerical Problems
1. A 50 mL sample of water required 25 mL of 0.01 N EDTA solution for titration. Calculate the total hardness of water in mg/L.
   
   Solution:
   
   Total Hardness (in mg/L) = Volume of EDTA solution used x Normality of EDTA solution x 1000 / Volume of water sample
   
                               = 25 x 0.01 x 1000 / 50
                               
                               = 500 mg/L
                               
   Therefore, the total hardness of water is 500 mg/L.
   
2. A 50 mL sample of water required 10 mL of 0.02 N H2SO4 solution for titration. Calculate the alkalinity of water in mg/L.
   
   Solution:
   
   Alkalinity (in mg/L) = Volume of H2SO4 solution used x Normality of H2SO4 solution x 50000 / Volume of water sample
   
                               = 10 x 0.02 x 50000 / 50
                               
                               = 2000 mg/L
                               
   Therefore, the alkalinity of water is 2000 mg/L.
