# UV, IR and NMR, Numerical problems

## UV Spectroscopy

- UV spectroscopy involves the study of electronic transitions within a molecule.
- The UV region covers wavelengths from 200 to 400 nm and is used to identify compounds with conjugated pi systems.
- The absorption of UV radiation causes a promotion of an electron from the highest occupied molecular orbital (HOMO) to the lowest unoccupied molecular orbital (LUMO).
- A conjugated pi system has alternating double and single bonds and the more conjugated the system, the longer the wavelength of absorption.
- The Beer-Lambert law can be used to determine the concentration of a solution from its absorbance reading.
- The molar absorptivity, ε, is a measure of the ability of a molecule to absorb light and is calculated from the slope of a calibration curve.

## IR Spectroscopy

- IR spectroscopy measures the absorption of infrared radiation by a molecule.
- The IR region covers wavelengths from 4000 to 400 cm-1 and is used to identify functional groups.
- The absorption of IR radiation causes a change in the vibrational energy of a molecule.
- The different types of vibrations that can occur in a molecule are stretching and bending vibrations.
- The fingerprint region of an IR spectrum contains a unique pattern of absorption peaks that can be used as a fingerprint for a particular molecule.
- IR spectra can be used to identify functional groups by looking at the characteristic peaks that correspond to each functional group.

## NMR Spectroscopy

- NMR spectroscopy measures the absorption of radio frequency radiation by a molecule.
- NMR spectra are used to determine the structure of a molecule.
- The chemical shift is a measure of the magnetic environment around a nucleus and is measured in parts per million (ppm).
- The integration of signals in an NMR spectrum gives the relative number of protons in each environment.
- The coupling of signals in an NMR spectrum gives information about the number of neighboring protons.
- The splitting pattern of a signal can be used to determine the number of neighboring protons.
