# Stereochemistry

Stereochemistry is the study of the three-dimensional arrangement of atoms in molecules and how this affects the chemical and physical properties of the molecule. Here are some key points to keep in mind when studying stereochemistry:

- Isomers are molecules that have the same molecular formula but different arrangements of atoms in space. Stereoisomers are isomers that differ only in the way their atoms are arranged in space.
- Enantiomers are a type of stereoisomer that are non-superimposable mirror images of each other. They have the same physical and chemical properties except for their interaction with other chiral molecules (those that have a non-superimposable mirror image).
- Chirality is a property of a molecule that describes its non-superimposability on its mirror image. A molecule is chiral if it cannot be superimposed on its mirror image.
- A chiral center is an atom in a molecule that has four different substituents. Molecules with one or more chiral centers can exist as enantiomers.
- Diastereomers are stereoisomers that are not mirror images of each other. They have different physical and chemical properties.
- Cis-trans isomerism is a type of stereoisomerism that arises from the restricted rotation around double bonds. Cis isomers have substituents on the same side of the double bond, while trans isomers have substituents on opposite sides.
- Conformational isomers are different arrangements of atoms in a molecule that arise from rotation around single bonds. The most stable conformation is often the one with the lowest energy.
- The R/S system is used to assign a configuration to a chiral center. The R configuration is assigned to a molecule whose substituents are arranged in a clockwise direction, while the S configuration is assigned to a molecule whose substituents are arranged in a counterclockwise direction.

By understanding these key concepts, you can begin to understand the complex nature of stereochemistry and its importance in various fields of science.
