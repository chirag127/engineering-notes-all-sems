

# UV, IR and NMR, Numerical Problems

## UV Spectroscopy

- UV spectroscopy measures the absorption of UV radiation by a compound.
- Absorption occurs when electrons in a compound transition from a ground state to an excited state.
- UV absorption spectra can be used to determine the presence of certain functional groups, such as carbonyls or conjugated double bonds.
- The wavelength of maximum absorption (λmax) can also be used to identify a compound.

### Numerical Problems

1. What is the λmax of a compound with an absorption spectrum that peaks at 280 nm?
Answer: λmax = 280 nm.

2. A compound has a λmax of 260 nm. What is the energy of the photon absorbed?
Answer: E = hc/λ = (6.626 x 10^-34 J s) x (3.00 x 10^8 m/s) / (260 x 10^-9 m) = 7.63 x 10^-19 J.

## IR Spectroscopy

- IR spectroscopy measures the absorption of infrared radiation by a compound.
- Absorption occurs when the vibrational energy of a bond matches the energy of the IR radiation.
- IR absorption spectra can be used to determine the presence of certain functional groups, such as carbonyls or hydroxyl groups.
- The frequency of absorption (ν) can also be used to identify a compound.

### Numerical Problems

1. A bond in a compound absorbs IR radiation with a frequency of 1700 cm^-1. What is the bond's vibrational energy?
Answer: E = hv = (6.626 x 10^-34 J s) x (3.00 x 10^10 cm/s) / (1700 cm^-1) = 1.11 x 10^-20 J.

2. What is the frequency of IR radiation absorbed by a compound with a bond vibrational energy of 5.57 x 10^-20 J?
Answer: ν = E/h = (5.57 x 10^-20 J) / (6.626 x 10^-34 J s) = 8.40 x 10^13 Hz.

## NMR Spectroscopy

- NMR spectroscopy measures the absorption of radio waves by a compound in a magnetic field.
- Absorption occurs when the nuclear spin of an atom transitions between energy levels in the magnetic field.
- NMR spectra can be used to determine the number and environment of certain nuclei in a compound.
- Chemical shift (δ) can also be used to identify a compound.

### Numerical Problems

1. What is the chemical shift of a proton in a compound that absorbs NMR radiation at 3.5 ppm?
Answer: δ = (3.5 ppm) / (10^6 ppm) = 3.5 x 10^-6.

2. A compound has two types of carbon atoms that absorb NMR radiation at chemical shifts of 25 ppm and 40 ppm. What is the ratio of the number of carbon atoms in each environment?
Answer: The ratio of the number of carbon atoms in each environment is proportional to the area under the respective peaks in the NMR spectrum.
