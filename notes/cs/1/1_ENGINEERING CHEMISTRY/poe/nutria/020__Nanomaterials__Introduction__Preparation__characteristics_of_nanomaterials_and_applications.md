
### Nanomaterials; Introduction, Preparation, characteristics of nanomaterials and applications

Nanomaterials are materials which have at least one dimension in the nanoscale range (1-100 nanometers). They are typically created by a variety of methods, such as chemical synthesis, physical deposition, or self-assembly.

* Introduction: Nanomaterials have unique physical and chemical properties compared to their larger counterparts. These properties make them desirable for a variety of applications, such as electronics, medical diagnostics, and drug delivery.

* Preparation: Nanomaterials can be prepared using a variety of methods, such as chemical synthesis, physical deposition, or self-assembly. Chemical synthesis involves the use of precursors to create a desired nanomaterial, while physical deposition involves the use of a physical force to deposit nanomaterials onto a substrate. Self-assembly involves the use of molecular interactions to form a desired structure.

* Characteristics of Nanomaterials: Nanomaterials have unique physical and chemical characteristics due to their small size. These characteristics include high surface area-to-volume ratio, high reactivity, and quantum confinement. These properties make them desirable for a variety of applications.

* Applications: Nanomaterials have a wide range of potential applications, including electronics, medical diagnostics, drug delivery, and energy storage. They are also being explored for use in environmental remediation, water purification, and catalysis.
