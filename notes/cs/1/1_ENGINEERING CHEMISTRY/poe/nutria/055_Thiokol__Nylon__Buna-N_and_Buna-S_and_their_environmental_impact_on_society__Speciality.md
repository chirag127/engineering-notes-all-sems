
# Thiokol 
Thiokol is a synthetic rubber material that was developed in the 1930s. It is used in a variety of applications, including tires, adhesives, and lubricants. Thiokol is known for its durability and resistance to weathering. It is also resistant to oil and gasoline.

However, Thiokol has been found to be a source of air pollution. It releases volatile organic compounds (VOCs) into the atmosphere, which can contribute to global warming and smog formation.

# Nylon 
Nylon is a synthetic polymer material that was developed in the 1930s. It is used in a variety of applications, including clothing, carpets, and industrial materials. Nylon is known for its strength, durability, and resistance to wear and tear. It is also resistant to mildew and mold.
