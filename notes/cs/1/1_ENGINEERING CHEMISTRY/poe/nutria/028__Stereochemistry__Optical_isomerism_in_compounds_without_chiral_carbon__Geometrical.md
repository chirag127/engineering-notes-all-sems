
### Stereochemistry: Optical isomerism in compounds without chiral carbon, Geometrical

* Geometrical isomerism is a type of optical isomerism that occurs in compounds without chiral carbon atoms. 
* This type of isomerism is caused by restricted rotation around a single bond. 
* Geometrical isomers differ in their spatial arrangement due to the presence of a double bond or ring structure that prevents rotation of the atoms. 
* Geometrical isomers are identified by the prefixes cis and trans, which indicate the relative positions of the two groups attached to the double bond or ring. 
* The two isomers of a compound can differ in physical properties such as boiling point, melting point, solubility, and refractive index. 
* Geometrical isomerism is common in compounds containing a double bond between two carbon atoms, but it can also occur in compounds containing other elements such as nitrogen, sulfur, and phosphorus.
