### Cement; Constituents, manufacturing, hardening and setting, deterioration of cement, Plaster

Cement:

Cement is a binder material used in construction to hold together building materials such as concrete. Cement is made from a mixture of minerals, including limestone and clay, which are ground and heated to produce clinker. The clinker is then ground into a fine powder to produce cement.

Constituents of Cement:

1. Limestone: Limestone is the main constituent of cement and provides calcium oxide, which reacts with other ingredients to form the hardened cement.

2. Clay: Clay provides silica, alumina, and iron oxide, which are also important components of cement.

3. Gypsum: Gypsum is added to control the setting time of the cement.

Manufacturing of Cement:

1. Mining: The raw materials needed for cement production are mined from quarries.

2. Grinding: The raw materials are ground into a fine powder to produce the raw mix.

3. Kiln: The raw mix is heated in a kiln to produce clinker.

4. Grinding: The clinker is ground into a fine powder to produce cement.

Hardening and Setting of Cement:

1. Hydration: The hardening of cement is caused by the chemical reaction between the cement and water, known as hydration.

2. Setting: The setting of cement is the process by which the cement hardens and becomes a solid mass.

Deterioration of Cement:

1. Chemical reactions: Chemical reactions between the cement and the environment can cause the cement to deteriorate over time.

2. Weathering: Weathering, such as rain and temperature changes, can also cause the cement to deteriorate.

3. Alkali-aggregate reaction: Alkali-aggregate reaction, which occurs when the alkalis in the cement react with the aggregate, can cause the cement to expand and crack.

Plaster:

Plaster is a building material used for coating walls and ceilings. Plaster is made from a mixture of gypsum, sand, and water and is applied as a wet mixture to the surface to be coated. Plaster dries to form a hard, durable surface that can be painted or wallpapered.
