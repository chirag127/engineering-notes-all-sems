##### 7. To determine the electrochemical equivalent (ECE) of copper.

7. To Determine the Electrochemical Equivalent (ECE) of Copper:

The electrochemical equivalent (ECE) of a metal is defined as the amount of metal deposited or dissolved at an electrode per unit of electric charge passed through an electrolyte solution. To determine the ECE of copper, a suitable electrochemical cell is set up, and a constant current is passed through the cell. The amount of copper deposited or dissolved at the electrode is measured, and the ECE is calculated based on the amount of electric charge passed through the cell.

1. Equipment: A suitable electrochemical cell, a potentiostat, a reference electrode, and a copper electrode are required to determine the ECE of copper. The reference electrode is used to maintain a constant potential, while the copper electrode is used to deposit or dissolve copper.

2. Procedure: The electrochemical cell is filled with a suitable electrolyte solution, and the reference electrode and copper electrode are placed in the solution. A constant current is passed through the cell, and the amount of copper deposited or dissolved at the electrode is measured over time. The ECE is calculated based on the amount of electric charge passed through the cell and the amount of copper deposited or dissolved.

3. Calculation: The ECE of copper can be calculated using the following formula: ECE = (mass of copper deposited or dissolved) / (electric charge passed through the cell). The units of ECE are typically given in grams per coulomb.

In conclusion, to determine the electrochemical equivalent (ECE) of copper, a suitable electrochemical cell is set up, and a constant current is passed through the cell. The amount of copper deposited or dissolved at the electrode is measured, and the ECE is calculated based on the amount of electric charge passed through the cell. The ECE of copper provides important information about the behavior of copper in electrochemical systems.
