##### 1. To determine the wavelength of sodium light by Newton’s ring experiment.

Experiment 1: To Determine the Wavelength of Sodium Light using Newton’s Ring Experiment:

Objective:
The objective of this experiment is to determine the wavelength of sodium light using the Newton’s ring experiment.

Theory:
Newton’s ring experiment is a classic optical experiment that demonstrates the interference of light. It involves the formation of circular interference fringes between a convex lens and a flat glass plate. The fringes are formed due to the interference of light reflected from the two surfaces of the glass plate.

Procedure:
1. A convex lens and a flat glass plate are placed facing each other, with a small gap between them.
2. Sodium light is directed at the gap between the lens and the plate.
3. The circular interference fringes are observed and the diameter of the fringes is measured.
4. The wavelength of the sodium light can be calculated using the formula:
λ = 2d/n, where d is the diameter of the fringes and n is the order of the fringes.

Conclusion:
The wavelength of sodium light can be determined using the Newton’s ring experiment by measuring the diameter of the circular interference fringes and using the formula to calculate the wavelength.
