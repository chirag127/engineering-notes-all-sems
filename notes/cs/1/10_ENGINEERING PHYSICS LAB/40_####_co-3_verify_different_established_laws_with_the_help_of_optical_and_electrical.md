#### CO-3 Verify different established laws with the help of optical and electrical

To verify different established laws with the help of optical and electrical methods, you can use a number of techniques, including:

1. Ohm's Law: The electrical resistance of a material can be measured using a four-point probe or a Van der Pauw configuration, and the results can be used to verify Ohm's law, which states that the current through a material is proportional to the voltage applied across it.

2. Kirchhoff's Laws: The conservation of charge and energy in an electrical circuit can be verified using Kirchhoff's laws, which state that the sum of the currents entering a node in a circuit must equal the sum of the currents leaving the node, and that the sum of the voltages around a closed loop must equal zero.

3. Snell's Law: The refractive index of a material can be measured using a prism or a grating spectrometer, and the results can be used to verify Snell's law, which states that the ratio of the sine of the angle of incidence to the sine of the angle of refraction is equal to the refractive index of the material.

4. Planck's Law: The spectral radiance of a material can be measured using a spectrophotometer or a spectroradiometer, and the results can be used to verify Planck's law, which states that the energy emitted by a black body is proportional to the frequency of the radiation and inversely proportional to the fourth power of the temperature.

These laws form the basis of many optical and electrical phenomena, and their verification helps to build a deeper understanding of the underlying physics and engineering principles.
