##### 8. To determine the wavelength of sodium light with the help of Fresnel’s bi-prism.

- Fresnel's bi-prism is a device that produces two coherent sources of light from a single source by using a thin prism with a very small refracting angle.
- The principle of the experiment is based on the interference of light waves from the two virtual sources and the measurement of the fringe width.
- The fringe width is given by the formula: $$\beta = \frac{\lambda D}{d}$$ where $\beta$ is the fringe width, $\lambda$ is the wavelength of light, $D$ is the distance between the slit and the screen, and $d$ is the distance between the two virtual sources.
- The distance $d$ can be calculated by using the formula: $$d = \frac{2\mu e}{a}$$ where $\mu$ is the refractive index of the prism, $e$ is the thickness of the prism, and $a$ is the refracting angle of the prism.
- The procedure of the experiment is as follows:
  - Set up the optical bench with a sodium lamp, a slit, a bi-prism, and a screen.
  - Adjust the slit width to get a sharp and bright image of the source on the screen.
  - Move the bi-prism towards the slit until two images of the source are seen on the screen.
  - Adjust the position of the bi-prism and the screen to get clear and equally spaced fringes on the screen.
  - Measure the distance $D$ between the slit and the screen with a meter scale.
  - Measure the fringe width $\beta$ by using a traveling microscope or a vernier scale on the screen.
  - Repeat the measurements for different values of $D$ and take the average of $\beta$.
  - Measure the refracting angle $a$ of the prism by using a spectrometer or a protractor.
  - Measure the thickness $e$ of the prism by using a screw gauge or a vernier caliper.
  - Calculate the refractive index $\mu$ of the prism by using the formula: $$\mu = \frac{\sin\frac{A+B}{2}}{\sin\frac{A}{2}}$$ where $A$ and $B$ are the angles of minimum deviation for the two wavelengths of sodium light.
  - Calculate the distance $d$ between the two virtual sources by using the formula: $$d = \frac{2\mu e}{a}$$
  - Calculate the wavelength $\lambda$ of sodium light by using the formula: $$\lambda = \frac{\beta d}{D}$$
  - Compare the experimental value of $\lambda$ with the theoretical value of 589 nm and calculate the percentage error.
