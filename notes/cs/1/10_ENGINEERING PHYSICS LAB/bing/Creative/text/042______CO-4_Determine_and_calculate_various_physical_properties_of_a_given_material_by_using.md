Physical properties are the traits a material has before it is used, such as colour, hardness, density, etc. They can be observed or measured without changing the composition of the material . Physical properties of materials and systems are often classified as intensive and extensive properties. Intensive properties do not depend on the size or extent of the system or object, such as temperature, pressure, etc. Extensive properties do depend on the size or extent of the system or object, such as mass, volume, etc.

Some of the common physical properties of materials are:

- Density: It is the mass per unit volume of a material. It is represented as ρ (rho) and measured in kg/m^3^ or g/cm^3^. Density can be calculated by dividing the mass of the material by its volume.
- Specific gravity: It is the ratio of density of a material with respect to density of a reference substance, usually water at 4°C. It is represented as SG and measured as a dimensionless quantity. Specific gravity can be calculated by dividing the density of the material by the density of water.
- State of matter: It is the physical form of a material, such as solid, liquid, gas, or plasma. State of matter depends on the temperature and pressure of the material. State of matter can be determined by observing the shape and volume of the material.
- Melting point: It is the temperature at which a solid material changes into a liquid state. It is represented as T_m and measured in °C or K. Melting point can be determined by heating the material until it melts and measuring the temperature.
- Boiling point: It is the temperature at which a liquid material changes into a gas state. It is represented as T_b and measured in °C or K. Boiling point can be determined by heating the material until it boils and measuring the temperature.
- Thermal conductivity: It is the ability of a material to transfer heat through itself. It is represented as k and measured in W/mK or W/m°C. Thermal conductivity can be calculated by dividing the heat flux (rate of heat transfer per unit area) by the temperature gradient (change in temperature per unit length) across the material.
- Electrical conductivity: It is the ability of a material to allow electric current to pass through itself. It is represented as σ (sigma) and measured in S/m or Ω^-1^m^-1^. Electrical conductivity can be calculated by dividing the electric current by the potential difference (voltage) across the material.
- Magnetic properties: They are the characteristics of a material that determine its response to a magnetic field. Some of the magnetic properties are magnetization, magnetic susceptibility, permeability, coercivity, etc. Magnetic properties can be measured by using a magnetometer or other devices that generate or detect magnetic fields.
- Optical properties: They are the characteristics of a material that determine its interaction with light. Some of the optical properties are refractive index, absorption, reflection, transmission, etc. Optical properties can be measured by using a spectrometer or other devices that emit or detect light.
- Mechanical properties: They are the characteristics of a material that determine its behaviour under applied forces or stresses. Some of the mechanical properties are elasticity, plasticity, strength, hardness, toughness, etc. Mechanical properties can be measured by using a tensile tester, a hardness tester, an impact tester, etc.
