##### 2. To determine the wavelength of different spectral lines of mercury light using plane

- The objective of this experiment is to measure the wavelength of different spectral lines of mercury light using a plane transmission diffraction grating and a spectrometer.
- The principle of this experiment is based on the diffraction of light by a grating, which produces a series of bright and dark fringes on either side of the central maximum. The angular position of each bright fringe depends on the wavelength of the light and the grating spacing.
- The formula for the diffraction of light by a grating is given by:

$$d \sin \theta = n \lambda$$

where $d$ is the grating spacing, $\theta$ is the angle of diffraction, $n$ is the order of the fringe, and $\lambda$ is the wavelength of the light.

- The procedure of this experiment is as follows:

  - Set up the spectrometer and the mercury lamp as shown in the figure below. Adjust the collimator and the telescope to obtain parallel rays of light and a clear image of the cross-wires, respectively.

  ![Figure 1: Experimental setup](https://web.cortland.edu/wheeler/phy150/Laboratory/Laboratory1_files/image001.gif)

  - Place the grating on the table of the spectrometer and align it perpendicular to the collimator. Rotate the telescope to observe the central maximum (zeroth order) and record its angular position.
  - Rotate the telescope to observe the first order bright fringes on either side of the central maximum. Record the angular positions and the colors of the fringes. Repeat this for the second order fringes if visible.
  - Calculate the grating spacing $d$ using the formula:

  $$d = \frac{n \lambda}{\sin \theta}$$

  where $n$ is the order of the fringe, $\lambda$ is the known wavelength of the spectral line, and $\theta$ is the angle of diffraction. Use the average of the angles on both sides of the central maximum for better accuracy. The wavelengths of the prominent lines in mercury are :

  | Color       | Wavelength (nm) |
  | ----------- | --------------- |
  | Purple      | 405             |
  | Blue        | 436             |
  | Blue-green  | 492             |
  | Green-yellow | 546             |
  | Yellow      | 577             |
  | Orange      | 623             |
  | Red         | 691             |

  - Calculate the wavelength of each spectral line using the formula:

  $$\lambda = \frac{d \sin \theta}{n}$$

  where $d$ is the grating spacing, $\theta$ is the angle of diffraction, and $n$ is the order of the fringe. Compare the calculated values with the known values and calculate the percentage error.

- The expected results of this experiment are that the calculated wavelengths of the spectral lines are close to the known values, with a small percentage error. The percentage error can be reduced by using a finer grating, a more accurate spectrometer, and a larger number of fringes.
