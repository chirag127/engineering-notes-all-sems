##### 2. To study Hall effect and determine Hall coefficient, carrier density and mobility of a given semiconductor sample.

- The Hall effect is the phenomenon of generating a transverse electric field in a current-carrying conductor when it is placed in a magnetic field perpendicular to the current direction .
- The Hall effect can be used to determine the sign, density and mobility of the charge carriers in a conductor, which are important parameters for characterizing the electrical properties of materials  .
- The Hall effect experiment consists of the following steps  :
  - A thin rectangular sample of a semiconductor material is connected to a constant current source and placed between the poles of a strong electromagnet.
  - A voltmeter is connected across the opposite edges of the sample to measure the potential difference (Hall voltage) generated due to the magnetic field.
  - The current, magnetic field and Hall voltage are varied and recorded for different values of each parameter.
  - The Hall coefficient, carrier density and mobility are calculated from the experimental data using the following formulas  :
    - Hall coefficient: $$R_H = \frac{V_H d}{I B}$$
    - Carrier density: $$n = \frac{1}{R_H e}$$
    - Mobility: $$\mu = \frac{R_H \sigma}{e}$$
  - Where $$V_H$$ is the Hall voltage, $$d$$ is the thickness of the sample, $$I$$ is the current, $$B$$ is the magnetic field, $$R_H$$ is the Hall coefficient, $$n$$ is the carrier density, $$e$$ is the elementary charge, $$\mu$$ is the mobility and $$\sigma$$ is the conductivity of the sample.
- The sign of the Hall coefficient indicates the sign of the charge carriers in the sample. A positive Hall coefficient means that the charge carriers are positive (holes), while a negative Hall coefficient means that the charge carriers are negative (electrons)  .
- The Hall effect experiment is a basic and important tool for studying the physics of solid-state materials, especially semiconductors, and for developing various applications and devices based on the Hall effect, such as magnetic sensors, position detectors, and Hall effect transistors  .
