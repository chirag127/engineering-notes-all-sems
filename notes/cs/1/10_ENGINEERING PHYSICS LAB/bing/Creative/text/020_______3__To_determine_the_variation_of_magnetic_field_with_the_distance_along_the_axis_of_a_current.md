##### 3. To determine the variation of magnetic field with the distance along the axis of a current

- The objective of this experiment is to measure the magnetic field produced by a circular coil carrying a steady current at different distances along its axis, and to verify the theoretical formula for the variation of magnetic field with distance.
- The apparatus required for this experiment are: a circular coil of known radius and number of turns, a constant current source, an ammeter, a magnetic compass, a meter scale, a stand and a clamp.
- The procedure of this experiment is as follows:
  - Connect the circular coil to the constant current source and the ammeter in series.
  - Place the coil horizontally on a table and adjust the current to a suitable value. Note down the current reading from the ammeter.
  - Place the magnetic compass on the stand and clamp it at a distance of about 10 cm from the center of the coil along its axis. The compass needle should be parallel to the plane of the coil.
  - Note down the deflection of the compass needle from the magnetic north. This deflection is due to the magnetic field of the coil along its axis.
  - Repeat the above step for different distances of the compass from the center of the coil along its axis, keeping the current constant. Record the distance and the deflection in a table.
  - Plot a graph of the deflection versus the distance on a graph paper. The graph should be a smooth curve.
  - From the graph, find the distance at which the deflection is maximum. This distance is equal to the radius of the coil.
  - Using the formula for the magnetic field along the axis of a circular coil, calculate the magnetic field at different distances from the center of the coil. The formula is:

  $$B = \frac{\mu_0 NI}{2}\frac{R^2}{(R^2 + x^2)^{3/2}}$$

  where $B$ is the magnetic field, $\mu_0$ is the permeability of free space, $N$ is the number of turns of the coil, $I$ is the current, $R$ is the radius of the coil, and $x$ is the distance from the center of the coil along its axis.
  - Compare the calculated values of the magnetic field with the experimental values obtained from the graph. The percentage error can be calculated as:

  $$\text{Percentage error} = \frac{|\text{Experimental value} - \text{Theoretical value}|}{\text{Theoretical value}} \times 100$$

  - The sources of error in this experiment are: the magnetic field of the earth, the non-uniformity of the coil, the parallax error in reading the compass and the meter scale, and the fluctuations in the current.
  - The precautions to be taken in this experiment are: the coil should be placed away from any magnetic or electric interference, the current should be kept constant throughout the experiment, the compass should be placed at the same level as the coil, and the readings should be taken with care and accuracy.
