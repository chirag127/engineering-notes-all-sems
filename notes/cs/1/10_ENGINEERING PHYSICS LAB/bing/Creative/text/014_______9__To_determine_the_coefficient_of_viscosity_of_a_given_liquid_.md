# 9. To determine the coefficient of viscosity of a given liquid.

- Viscosity is a measure of the resistance of a fluid to flow. It depends on the intermolecular forces and the temperature of the fluid.
- The coefficient of viscosity (η) of a liquid is defined as the ratio of the shear stress (τ) to the rate of shear strain (γ) in the liquid.
- Mathematically, η = τ/γ
- The SI unit of viscosity is pascal-second (Pa s) or newton-second per square meter (N s/m^2).
- One of the methods to determine the coefficient of viscosity of a liquid is by using a Stokes' apparatus, which consists of a glass tube filled with the liquid and a small spherical ball of known mass and radius.
- The procedure is as follows:
  - Measure the mass (m) and radius (r) of the ball using a balance and a screw gauge respectively.
  - Fill the glass tube with the liquid and clamp it vertically. Adjust the height of the liquid column (h) using a stopcock at the bottom of the tube.
  - Drop the ball gently from the top of the tube and start a stopwatch as soon as the ball crosses the upper mark on the tube.
  - Stop the stopwatch when the ball crosses the lower mark on the tube and note the time taken (t) by the ball to cover the distance between the two marks (l).
  - Repeat the experiment for at least three times and take the average time (t̅) as the final result.
  - Calculate the terminal velocity (v) of the ball using the formula v = l/t̅
  - Apply Stokes' law to find the coefficient of viscosity of the liquid using the formula η = 2/9 (ρ_b - ρ_l) g r^2 / v, where ρ_b and ρ_l are the densities of the ball and the liquid respectively, and g is the acceleration due to gravity.
  - Record the observations and calculations in a tabular form and report the final value of η with proper units and significant figures.
