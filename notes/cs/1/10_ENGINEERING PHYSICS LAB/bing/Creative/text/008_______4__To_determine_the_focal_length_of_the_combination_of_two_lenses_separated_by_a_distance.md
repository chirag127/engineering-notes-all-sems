##### 4. To determine the focal length of the combination of two lenses separated by a distance

- The focal length of a combination of two lenses separated by a distance is given by the formula:

  1/f = 1/f1 + 1/f2 - d/f1f2

  where f is the focal length of the combination, f1 and f2 are the focal lengths of the individual lenses, and d is the distance between them.

- To determine the focal length of the combination experimentally, we can use the following steps:

  - Set up a light source, a screen, and a lens holder on an optical bench.
  - Place the first lens on the lens holder and adjust its position until a clear image of the light source is formed on the screen. Measure the distance between the lens and the screen, which is the image distance (v1).
  - Place the second lens on the lens holder behind the first lens and adjust its position until a clear image of the light source is formed on the screen again. Measure the distance between the second lens and the screen, which is the image distance (v2).
  - Measure the distance between the two lenses, which is the separation distance (d).
  - Use the lens formula 1/f = 1/u + 1/v to calculate the object distance (u1) for the first lens and the object distance (u2) for the second lens, where u is the distance between the light source and the lens, and v is the distance between the lens and the screen.
  - Use the formula 1/f = 1/f1 + 1/f2 - d/f1f2 to calculate the focal length of the combination (f), where f1 and f2 are the focal lengths of the individual lenses, which can be obtained from the relation f = uv/(u + v).
  - Repeat the experiment with different values of d and v2 and calculate the average value of f.

- The focal length of the combination of two lenses depends on the focal lengths of the individual lenses and the distance between them. If the lenses have the same focal length and are in contact, the focal length of the combination is half of the focal length of each lens. If the lenses have opposite signs of focal length and are in contact, the focal length of the combination is zero, which means the combination acts as a plane glass. If the lenses are separated by a distance equal to the sum of their focal lengths, the focal length of the combination is infinite, which means the combination acts as a parallel beam.
