##### 1. To determine the energy band gap of a given semiconductor material.

- The energy band gap of a semiconductor is the difference between the energy levels of the valence band and the conduction band. It represents the minimum amount of energy required to excite an electron from the valence band to the conduction band, where it can participate in current flow.
- The energy band gap of a semiconductor depends on its temperature, impurity concentration, and external electric field. It also varies with the type and structure of the semiconductor material, such as silicon, germanium, gallium arsenide, etc.
- The energy band gap of a semiconductor can be determined experimentally by measuring its electrical conductivity or resistivity as a function of temperature. The conductivity or resistivity of a semiconductor changes with temperature due to the variation in the number of charge carriers available in the conduction band. 
- The conductivity or resistivity of a semiconductor can be expressed as:

$$\sigma = n e \mu_e + p e \mu_h$$

or

$$\rho = \frac{1}{\sigma} = \frac{1}{n e \mu_e + p e \mu_h}$$

where $\sigma$ is the conductivity, $\rho$ is the resistivity, $n$ and $p$ are the concentrations of electrons and holes, respectively, $e$ is the elementary charge, and $\mu_e$ and $\mu_h$ are the mobilities of electrons and holes, respectively.

- The concentrations of electrons and holes in a semiconductor can be related to the energy band gap by the following equations:

$$n = N_c e^{-\frac{E_c - E_f}{k_B T}}$$

$$p = N_v e^{-\frac{E_f - E_v}{k_B T}}$$

where $N_c$ and $N_v$ are the effective densities of states in the conduction and valence bands, respectively, $E_c$ and $E_v$ are the energies of the conduction and valence bands, respectively, $E_f$ is the Fermi level, $k_B$ is the Boltzmann constant, and $T$ is the absolute temperature.

- By substituting these equations into the expression for the conductivity or resistivity, and taking the natural logarithm of both sides, we obtain:

$$\ln \sigma = \ln (N_c e \mu_e + N_v e \mu_h) - \frac{E_g}{2 k_B T}$$

or

$$\ln \rho = -\ln (N_c e \mu_e + N_v e \mu_h) + \frac{E_g}{2 k_B T}$$

where $E_g = E_c - E_v$ is the energy band gap.

- By plotting $\ln \sigma$ or $\ln \rho$ versus $\frac{1}{T}$, we obtain a straight line with a slope of $-\frac{E_g}{2 k_B}$. From the slope, we can calculate the value of the energy band gap.

- The experimental procedure to determine the energy band gap of a given semiconductor material is as follows:

  - Prepare a sample of the semiconductor material with known dimensions and contacts for measuring its resistance.
  - Connect the sample to a Wheatstone bridge circuit or an ohmmeter to measure its resistance at different temperatures.
  - Place the sample in a temperature-controlled chamber or a water bath and vary the temperature in a suitable range, such as from 300 K to 500 K.
  - Record the values of the resistance and the temperature at each step.
  - Calculate the conductivity or resistivity of the sample using the formula:

  $$\sigma = \frac{L}{A R}$$

  or

  $$\rho = \frac{A R}{L}$$

  where $L$ is the length, $A$ is the cross-sectional area, and $R$ is the resistance of the sample.
