##### 3. To determine the specific rotation of cane sugar solution using polarimeter.

- A polarimeter is an instrument that measures the angle of rotation of plane-polarized light by an optically active substance, such as cane sugar solution.
- The specific rotation of a substance is defined as the angle of rotation per unit length of the substance per unit concentration of the solution.
- The specific rotation of cane sugar solution can be determined by the following formula:

$$[\alpha]_D = \frac{\alpha}{l \times c}$$

where $[\alpha]_D$ is the specific rotation at the D-line of sodium, $\alpha$ is the observed angle of rotation, $l$ is the length of the polarimeter tube in decimeters, and $c$ is the concentration of the solution in grams per cubic centimeter.

- To perform the experiment, the following steps are followed:

  - Prepare a standard cane sugar solution of known concentration and fill the polarimeter tube with it.
  - Adjust the polarizer and the analyzer of the polarimeter such that no light passes through when the tube is empty.
  - Insert the tube containing the sugar solution and rotate the analyzer until the light passes through again. Note the reading of the vernier scale on the analyzer.
  - Repeat the above step for at least three different concentrations of the sugar solution and note the corresponding readings of the analyzer.
  - Calculate the angle of rotation for each concentration using the difference between the initial and final readings of the analyzer.
  - Calculate the specific rotation for each concentration using the formula given above.
  - Take the mean of the specific rotations obtained for different concentrations as the final result.
