#### Group A

- Group A is a category of blood types that have the A antigen on the surface of red blood cells and the anti-B antibody in the plasma.
- The A antigen is a protein that helps the immune system identify the blood cells as self or non-self.
- The anti-B antibody is a protein that binds to and destroys any foreign blood cells that have the B antigen on their surface.
- Group A blood types can donate blood to other group A or group AB recipients, and can receive blood from other group A or group O donors.
- Group A blood types are the second most common in the world, after group O, and are found in about 30% of the population.
- Group A blood types are associated with certain traits, such as being more prone to stress, having a higher risk of gastric cancer, and being more creative and analytical. However, these associations are not conclusive and may vary depending on other factors.
