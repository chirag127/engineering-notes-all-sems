## Hysteresis and its significance in transformers

- Hysteresis is the phenomenon of lagging of magnetic flux density (B) behind the magnetic field intensity (H) in a magnetic material.
- Hysteresis causes a loop-shaped curve (B-H curve) when B is plotted against H for a cyclic variation of H.
- The area of the B-H curve represents the energy loss per unit volume of the magnetic material due to the reversal of its magnetization. This is called hysteresis loss.
- Hysteresis loss is one of the sources of power loss and heating in transformers, which reduces their efficiency and lifespan.
- Hysteresis loss depends on the frequency of the alternating current, the peak value of the magnetic field intensity, and the properties of the core material.
- Soft magnetic materials, such as silicon steel, have a narrow B-H curve and low hysteresis loss. They are suitable for transformer cores, as they can switch polarity easily and reduce power loss.

## Experimental setup and procedure

- The experimental setup consists of a transformer with a primary winding (N1) and a secondary winding (N2) connected to an AC source and a load resistor (R), respectively.
- A voltmeter (V) and an ammeter (A) are connected across the secondary winding to measure the output voltage and current.
- A search coil (N3) with a known number of turns is wound around the transformer core and connected to an oscilloscope to measure the induced voltage.
- The AC source is adjusted to vary the input voltage and current, which in turn vary the magnetic field intensity (H) in the core.
- The magnetic flux density (B) in the core is proportional to the induced voltage in the search coil, which can be read from the oscilloscope.
- The B-H curve can be plotted by taking the readings of B and H for different values of the input voltage and current.
- The hysteresis loss per cycle can be calculated by multiplying the area of the B-H curve by the volume of the core and dividing by the number of turns of the search coil.
- The coercivity of the core material can be calculated by finding the value of H when B is zero, which corresponds to the point where the B-H curve intersects the H-axis.

- The area of the B-H curve can be estimated by counting the number of squares enclosed by the curve and multiplying by the area of each square. For example, if each square has an area of 0.01 unit, and the curve encloses 50 squares, then the area of the curve is 0.5 unit.
- The hysteresis loss per cycle can be calculated by using the formula:

`hysteresis loss = area of B-H curve * volume of core / number of turns of search coil`

For example, if the volume of the core is 0.1 m^3 and the number of turns of the search coil is 100, then the hysteresis loss per cycle is:

`hysteresis loss = 0.5 * 0.1 / 100 = 0.0005 J`

- The coercivity of the core material can be calculated by finding the value of H when B is zero. For example, if the B-H curve intersects the H-axis at H = -0.2 and H = 0.3, then the coercivity is the average of these values, which is:

`coercivity = (-0.2 + 0.3) / 2 = 0.05 unit`
