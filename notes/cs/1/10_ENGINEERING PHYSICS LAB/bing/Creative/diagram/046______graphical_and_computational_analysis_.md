#### Graphical and computational analysis

- Graphical analysis is a method of visualizing and interpreting data using graphs, charts, diagrams, and other graphical tools.
- Computational analysis is a method of applying mathematical and logical operations to data using algorithms, software, and hardware.
- Both methods can be used to explore, describe, summarize, and infer patterns, trends, relationships, and differences in data.
- Graphical and computational analysis can complement each other and provide different perspectives and insights on the same data.

Some examples of graphical and computational analysis are:

- Scatter plots and correlation coefficients: A scatter plot is a graph that shows the relationship between two variables by plotting their values as points on a coordinate plane. A correlation coefficient is a numerical measure of the strength and direction of the linear relationship between two variables. It ranges from -1 to 1, where -1 indicates a perfect negative correlation, 0 indicates no correlation, and 1 indicates a perfect positive correlation.
- Histograms and descriptive statistics: A histogram is a graph that shows the frequency distribution of a single variable by dividing the data into bins and plotting the number of observations in each bin as a bar. Descriptive statistics are numerical measures that summarize the main features of a data set, such as the mean, median, mode, standard deviation, range, and quartiles.
- Box plots and outliers: A box plot is a graph that shows the distribution of a single variable by displaying the minimum, maximum, median, and quartiles as a box with whiskers. Outliers are observations that are unusually high or low compared to the rest of the data. They can be identified by using the interquartile range (IQR), which is the difference between the third and first quartiles. Any observation that is more than 1.5 times the IQR above the third quartile or below the first quartile is considered an outlier.
- Line graphs and regression analysis: A line graph is a graph that shows the change of a variable over time or across categories by connecting the data points with a line. Regression analysis is a method of modeling the relationship between a dependent variable and one or more independent variables by finding the equation of the best-fitting line or curve that minimizes the sum of squared errors. The slope and intercept of the line or curve can be used to estimate the effect of the independent variables on the dependent variable and to make predictions.
