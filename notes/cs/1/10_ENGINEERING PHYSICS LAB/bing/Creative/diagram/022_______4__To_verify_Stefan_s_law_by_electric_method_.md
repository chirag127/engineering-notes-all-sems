##### 4. To verify Stefan’s law by electric method.

- Stefan’s law states that the total radiant heat energy emitted per unit time per unit area of a black body is directly proportional to the fourth power of its absolute temperature.
- Mathematically, Stefan’s law can be written as:

  E = σT^4

  where E is the radiant heat energy, T is the absolute temperature, and σ is the Stefan-Boltzmann constant.

- To verify Stefan’s law by electric method, we need the following apparatus:

  - A Leslie’s cube, which is a hollow metal cube with four different surfaces (blackened, polished, white, and rough) and a hole at the top to fill it with hot water.
  - A thermometer, to measure the temperature of the water inside the cube.
  - A thermopile, which is a device that converts thermal energy into electrical energy by using a series of thermocouples connected in series or parallel.
  - A galvanometer, to measure the electric current generated by the thermopile.
  - A rheostat, to adjust the resistance of the circuit.
  - A battery, to provide a constant voltage to the circuit.
  - A key, to switch on or off the circuit.
  - A meter scale, to measure the distance between the thermopile and the cube.
