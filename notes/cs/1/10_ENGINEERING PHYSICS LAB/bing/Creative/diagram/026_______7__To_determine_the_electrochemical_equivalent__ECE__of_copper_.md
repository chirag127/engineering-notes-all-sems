##### 7. To determine the electrochemical equivalent (ECE) of copper.

- The electrochemical equivalent (ECE) of a substance is the mass of the substance deposited or liberated at an electrode during electrolysis by one coulomb of charge.
- The ECE of copper can be determined by using a copper voltameter, which consists of two copper electrodes immersed in a solution of copper sulfate.
- The procedure is as follows:
  - Connect the copper voltameter to a direct current (DC) source, such as a battery or a power supply, and a variable resistor or rheostat to control the current.
  - Measure the initial mass of the cathode (the electrode where reduction occurs) using a balance and record it as m1.
  - Close the circuit and adjust the rheostat to obtain a steady current. Measure the current using an ammeter and record it as I.
  - Run the electrolysis for a known time interval t, using a stopwatch or a timer.
  - Open the circuit and disconnect the voltameter. Rinse and dry the cathode and measure its final mass using a balance and record it as m2.
  - Calculate the mass of copper deposited on the cathode as m = m2 - m1.
  - Calculate the charge passed through the voltameter as Q = I x t.
  - Calculate the ECE of copper as E = m / Q.
- The ECE of copper can also be calculated theoretically using Faraday's laws of electrolysis, which state that:
  - The mass of a substance deposited or liberated at an electrode is directly proportional to the charge passed through the electrolyte.
  - The mass of a substance deposited or liberated at an electrode is directly proportional to its equivalent weight, which is the molar mass divided by the valency of the ion.
- The theoretical ECE of copper is given by E = M / (z x F), where M is the molar mass of copper, z is the valency of the copper ion, and F is the Faraday constant (the charge of one mole of electrons).
- The experimental and theoretical ECE of copper should be close to each other, but may differ due to errors such as impurities, side reactions, or measurement uncertainties.
