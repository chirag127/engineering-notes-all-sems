##### 6. To study the resonance condition of a series LCR circuit.

- A series LCR circuit consists of an inductor (L), a capacitor (C) and a resistor (R) connected in series with an alternating voltage source (V).
- The current (I) in the circuit is the same for all the components, but the voltages across them are different and depend on their reactances (X).
- The reactance of an inductor is given by X_L = 2πfL, where f is the frequency of the source and L is the inductance of the coil.
- The reactance of a capacitor is given by X_C = 1/(2πfC), where C is the capacitance of the capacitor.
- The resistance of a resistor is given by R, which is independent of the frequency.
- The total impedance (Z) of the circuit is given by Z = √((R^2) + (X_L - X_C)^2), which is the ratio of the source voltage to the current.
- The phase difference (φ) between the source voltage and the current is given by tan(φ) = (X_L - X_C)/R, which is the angle by which the current lags or leads the voltage.
- The resonance condition of a series LCR circuit is when the inductive reactance is equal to the capacitive reactance, i.e., X_L = X_C.
- At resonance, the impedance of the circuit is minimum and equal to the resistance, i.e., Z = R.
- At resonance, the phase difference between the voltage and the current is zero, i.e., φ = 0.
- At resonance, the current in the circuit is maximum and equal to the source voltage divided by the resistance, i.e., I = V/R.
- The resonant frequency (f_0) of the circuit is given by f_0 = 1/(2π√(LC)), which is the frequency at which the circuit resonates.
- The resonance condition can be verified by measuring the current in the circuit for different frequencies of the source and plotting a graph of current versus frequency. The peak of the graph corresponds to the resonant frequency and the maximum current.
