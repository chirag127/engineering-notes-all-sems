##### 3. To determine the specific rotation of cane sugar solution using polarimeter.

- A polarimeter is an instrument that measures the angle of rotation of plane-polarized light by an optically active substance, such as cane sugar solution.
- The specific rotation of a substance is defined as the angle of rotation per unit length of the substance per unit concentration of the solution.
- The specific rotation of cane sugar solution can be determined by the following steps:

  1. Prepare a standard solution of cane sugar of known concentration (e.g. 10% w/v) by dissolving a known mass of cane sugar in a known volume of water.
  2. Fill a polarimeter tube of known length (e.g. 20 cm) with the cane sugar solution and seal the ends with glass windows.
  3. Place the polarimeter tube in the polarimeter and adjust the analyzer and the vernier scale to zero.
  4. Switch on the sodium lamp and observe the field of view through the eyepiece. You will see two semicircles of different brightness.
  5. Rotate the analyzer until the two semicircles have equal brightness. Note the angle of rotation on the vernier scale. This is the observed angle of rotation of the cane sugar solution.
  6. Repeat the steps 3 to 5 for at least three times and take the mean value of the observed angle of rotation.
  7. Calculate the specific rotation of cane sugar solution using the formula:

     $$[\alpha]_D = \frac{\alpha}{l \times c}$$

     where $[\alpha]_D$ is the specific rotation at the sodium D-line wavelength (589 nm), $\alpha$ is the mean observed angle of rotation, $l$ is the length of the polarimeter tube, and $c$ is the concentration of the cane sugar solution.
  8. Record the result with proper units and significant figures.
