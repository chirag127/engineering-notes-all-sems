##### 1. To determine the energy band gap of a given semiconductor material.

- The energy band gap of a semiconductor is the difference between the energy levels of the valence band and the conduction band. It represents the minimum amount of energy required to excite an electron from the valence band to the conduction band, where it can participate in current flow.
- The energy band gap of a semiconductor depends on its temperature, impurity concentration, and external electric field. It also varies with the type and structure of the semiconductor material, such as silicon, germanium, gallium arsenide, etc.
- The energy band gap of a semiconductor can be determined experimentally by measuring its electrical conductivity or resistivity as a function of temperature. The conductivity or resistivity of a semiconductor changes with temperature due to the variation in the number of charge carriers (electrons and holes) available for current flow.
- The conductivity or resistivity of a semiconductor can be measured by using a four-probe method, which involves passing a constant current through the semiconductor sample and measuring the voltage drop across it. The four-probe method eliminates the effect of contact resistance and lead resistance on the measurement.
- The conductivity or resistivity of a semiconductor can be expressed by the following equation:

$$\sigma = \sigma_0 e^{-E_g/2kT}$$

or

$$\rho = \rho_0 e^{E_g/2kT}$$
