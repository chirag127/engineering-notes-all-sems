# Determining the Hysteresis Loss

- Hysteresis loss is the energy dissipated as heat in a magnetic material when it is subjected to a cyclic magnetization.
- Hysteresis loss occurs because the magnetic domains in the material do not align and reverse instantly with the applied magnetic field, but lag behind it.
- Hysteresis loss depends on the shape and size of the hysteresis loop of the material, which represents the relationship between the magnetic flux density and the magnetic field intensity.
- The area enclosed by the hysteresis loop is proportional to the energy lost per cycle of magnetization.
- To determine the hysteresis loss, we need to measure the hysteresis loop of the material using a device called a hysteresis graph or a B-H curve tracer.
- The hysteresis graph consists of a primary coil that produces a varying magnetic field, a secondary coil that measures the induced voltage, and a galvanometer that measures the current in the secondary coil.
- The material to be tested is placed inside the primary coil, and the secondary coil is connected to the galvanometer and a variable resistor.
- The primary coil is connected to an alternating current source, such as a sinusoidal generator, that can vary the frequency and amplitude of the current.
- The voltage and current in the secondary coil are recorded as the primary current is varied, and the B-H curve is plotted on a graph paper or a computer screen.
- The area of the hysteresis loop can be calculated by using a planimeter, a device that measures the area enclosed by a closed curve, or by using numerical integration methods.
- The hysteresis loss per cycle can be obtained by multiplying the area of the hysteresis loop by the magnetic flux density and dividing by the volume of the material.
- The hysteresis loss per unit time can be obtained by multiplying the hysteresis loss per cycle by the frequency of the alternating current.
