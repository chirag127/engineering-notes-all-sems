##### 10. To determine the value of acceleration due to gravity (g) using compound pendulum.

- A compound pendulum is a rigid body that can oscillate about a horizontal axis passing through it.
- The time period of a compound pendulum depends on its length, mass, and distance of the center of mass from the axis of oscillation.
- The time period of a compound pendulum is given by the formula:

`T = 2π √(I/mgd)`

where T is the time period, I is the moment of inertia of the pendulum about the axis of oscillation, m is the mass of the pendulum, g is the acceleration due to gravity, and d is the distance of the center of mass from the axis of oscillation.

- To determine the value of g using a compound pendulum, the following steps are followed:

  - A compound pendulum of known mass and length is suspended from a rigid support by a thin wire or string.
  - The pendulum is displaced slightly from its equilibrium position and released. The pendulum starts to oscillate with small amplitude.
  - A stopwatch is used to measure the time taken by the pendulum to complete a certain number of oscillations (say, 20).
  - The time period of the pendulum is calculated by dividing the total time by the number of oscillations.
  - The distance of the center of mass from the axis of oscillation is measured using a meter scale or a vernier caliper.
  - The moment of inertia of the pendulum about the axis of oscillation is calculated using the formula:

  `I = ml²/12 + md²`

  where l is the length of the pendulum and m is the mass of the pendulum.
  - The value of g is calculated by rearranging the formula for the time period of the pendulum:

  `g = 4π²I/(mTd²)`

  - The experiment is repeated for different values of d and the average value of g is obtained.

- The sources of error in this experiment are:

  - The friction at the point of suspension of the pendulum.
  - The air resistance on the pendulum.
  - The variation of g with altitude and latitude.
  - The non-uniformity of the pendulum.
  - The human error in measuring the time, length, and distance.
