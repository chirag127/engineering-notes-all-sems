##### 8. To determine the wavelength of sodium light with the help of Fresnel’s bi-prism.

- Fresnel's bi-prism is a thin prism with a very small refracting angle, made by cutting a small portion from the apex of a larger prism.
- When a monochromatic light source, such as sodium light, is placed in front of the bi-prism, it produces two virtual coherent sources by refraction at the two faces of the bi-prism.
- The two virtual sources interfere to form a pattern of alternate bright and dark fringes on a screen placed behind the bi-prism.
- The distance between the two virtual sources can be calculated by measuring the distance between the source and the bi-prism, and the refracting angle of the bi-prism.
- The distance between the screen and the bi-prism can be measured by using a meter scale.
- The fringe width, or the distance between two consecutive bright or dark fringes, can be measured by using a traveling microscope or a vernier scale.
- The wavelength of the sodium light can be determined by using the formula:

  `λ = Dd/D`, where `λ` is the wavelength, `D` is the distance between the screen and the bi-prism, `d` is the distance between the two virtual sources, and `D` is the fringe width.
- The experiment can be repeated by changing the position of the bi-prism or the screen, and taking the average of the wavelength values obtained.
- The sources of error and uncertainty in the experiment can include:

  - The alignment of the source, the bi-prism, and the screen.
  - The measurement of the refracting angle of the bi-prism.
  - The measurement of the distances between the source, the bi-prism, and the screen.
  - The measurement of the fringe width.
  - The variation of the wavelength of the sodium light due to temperature or pressure changes.
