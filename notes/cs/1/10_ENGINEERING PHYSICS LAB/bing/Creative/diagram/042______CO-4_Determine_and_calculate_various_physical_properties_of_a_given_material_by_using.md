#### CO-4 Determine and calculate various physical properties of a given material by using

Physical properties are the traits a material has before it is used or manipulated. They can be observed or measured without changing the composition of the material. Examples of physical properties include colour, hardness, density, melting point, boiling point, electrical conductivity, thermal conductivity, magnetic susceptibility, etc.  

To determine and calculate various physical properties of a given material, we can use different methods and instruments depending on the property we want to measure. Some of the common methods and instruments are:

- **Density**: Density is the mass per unit volume of a material. It can be calculated by dividing the mass of the material by its volume. Density can be measured by using a balance and a measuring cylinder, or by using a hydrometer or a pycnometer. 
- **Specific gravity**: Specific gravity is the ratio of the density of a material to the density of a reference substance, usually water. It can be calculated by dividing the density of the material by the density of water. Specific gravity can be measured by using a hydrometer or a pycnometer. 
- **State of matter**: State of matter is the physical form of a material, such as solid, liquid, gas, or plasma. It can be determined by observing the shape and volume of the material. State of matter can be changed by altering the temperature or pressure of the material. 
- **Melting point**: Melting point is the temperature at which a solid material changes into a liquid. It can be measured by using a thermometer and a heating device, such as a hot plate or a Bunsen burner. Melting point can be affected by the purity and structure of the material. 
- **Boiling point**: Boiling point is the temperature at which a liquid material changes into a gas. It can be measured by using a thermometer and a heating device, such as a hot plate or a Bunsen burner. Boiling point can be affected by the pressure and solutes of the material. 
- **Hardness**: Hardness is the resistance of a material to deformation, indentation, or scratching. It can be measured by using a hardness tester, such as a Brinell, Rockwell, or Vickers tester. Hardness can be affected by the composition and structure of the material. 
- **Electrical conductivity**: Electrical conductivity is the ability of a material to allow electric current to flow through it. It can be measured by using an electrical circuit and a device that measures voltage, current, or resistance, such as a multimeter or an ohmmeter. Electrical conductivity can be affected by the temperature, composition, and structure of the material. 
- **Thermal conductivity**: Thermal conductivity is the ability of a material to transfer heat through it. It can be measured by using a device that measures temperature, such as a thermocouple or a thermometer, and a device that generates or removes heat, such as a heater or a cooler. Thermal conductivity can be affected by the temperature, composition, and structure of the material. 
- **Magnetic susceptibility**: Magnetic susceptibility is the degree to which a material is attracted or repelled by a magnetic field. It can be measured by using a device that generates a magnetic field, such as a magnet or an electromagnet, and a device that measures the force or torque exerted on the material, such as a balance or a torsion pendulum. Magnetic susceptibility can be affected by the temperature, composition, and structure of the material.
