##### 2. To determine the wavelength of different spectral lines of mercury light using plane

- The objective of this experiment is to measure the wavelength of different spectral lines of mercury light using a plane transmission diffraction grating and a spectrometer.
- The principle of this experiment is based on the diffraction of light by a grating, which produces a series of bright and dark fringes on either side of the central maximum. The angular position of each bright fringe depends on the wavelength of the light and the grating spacing.
- The apparatus required for this experiment are: a mercury vapor lamp, a plane transmission diffraction grating, a spectrometer, a slit, a telescope, a scale and a vernier.
- The procedure of this experiment is as follows:
  - Set up the mercury vapor lamp, the slit and the grating on the spectrometer table, such that the light from the lamp passes through the slit and falls on the grating.
  - Adjust the slit width to obtain a sharp and narrow beam of light.
  - Mount the telescope on the spectrometer and focus it on the slit.
  - Rotate the telescope to observe the first order spectrum on either side of the central maximum.
  - Identify the different spectral lines of mercury by their colors and compare them with the standard values  .
  - Measure the angular position of each spectral line using the scale and the vernier on the spectrometer.
  - Repeat the observations for the second order spectrum if possible.
  - Calculate the wavelength of each spectral line using the formula:

    `λ = dsinθ`

    where λ is the wavelength, d is the grating spacing, and θ is the angular position of the spectral line.
  - Compare the calculated values with the standard values and calculate the percentage error.
- The expected results of this experiment are: the wavelength of the different spectral lines of mercury light should be close to the standard values, with a small percentage error. The percentage error should be lower for the higher order spectrum, as the angular separation of the spectral lines increases with the order. The standard values of the wavelength of the prominent lines in mercury are  :

  - Purple: 405 nm
  - Blue: 436 nm
  - Blue-green: 492 nm
  - Greenish yellow: 546 nm
  - Yellow: 577 nm and 579 nm (two lines)
  - Orange: 623 nm
  - Red: 691 nm
