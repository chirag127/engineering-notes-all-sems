# 6. To study the resonance condition of a series LCR circuit.

- A series LCR circuit consists of an inductor (L), a capacitor (C), and a resistor (R) connected in series to an alternating voltage source (V).
- The current (I) in the circuit is the same for all the components, but the voltages across them may differ in magnitude and phase.
- The voltage across the inductor (V_L) leads the current by 90 degrees, the voltage across the capacitor (V_C) lags the current by 90 degrees, and the voltage across the resistor (V_R) is in phase with the current.
- The total voltage across the circuit (V) is the phasor sum of the individual voltages, and the total impedance (Z) of the circuit is the ratio of the total voltage to the current.
- The impedance of the circuit depends on the frequency (f) of the alternating voltage source, and it is given by the formula:

  Z = sqrt(R^2 + (X_L - X_C)^2)

  where X_L = 2πfL is the inductive reactance and X_C = 1/(2πfC) is the capacitive reactance.

- The resonance condition of a series LCR circuit is when the impedance of the circuit is minimum, which means that the current is maximum and the total voltage is equal to the voltage across the resistor.
- The resonance condition occurs when the inductive reactance is equal to the capacitive reactance, which means that the frequency of the alternating voltage source is equal to the resonant frequency (f_0) of the circuit, given by the formula:

  f_0 = 1/(2πsqrt(LC))

- At resonance, the voltages across the inductor and the capacitor are equal in magnitude but opposite in phase, so they cancel each other out and the total voltage is in phase with the current.
- The power dissipated in the circuit at resonance is maximum, and it is equal to the product of the voltage across the resistor and the current, or the square of the current times the resistance, given by the formula:

  P = V_R I = I^2 R
