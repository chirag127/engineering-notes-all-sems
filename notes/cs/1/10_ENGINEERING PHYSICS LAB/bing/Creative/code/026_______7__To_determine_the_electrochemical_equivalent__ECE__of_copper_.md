##### 7. To determine the electrochemical equivalent (ECE) of copper.

- The electrochemical equivalent (ECE) of a substance is the mass of the substance deposited or liberated by one coulomb of electric charge during an electrolysis process.
- The ECE of copper can be determined by using a copper voltameter, which consists of two copper electrodes immersed in a copper sulfate solution.
- The copper voltameter is connected to a direct current (DC) power supply and an ammeter to measure the current passing through the circuit.
- The experiment is performed as follows:

  - The initial mass of the copper cathode (the electrode where reduction occurs) is recorded using a balance.
  - The power supply is switched on and a constant current is maintained for a fixed time interval, such as 10 minutes, using a rheostat.
  - The power supply is switched off and the copper cathode is removed, washed, dried and weighed again using the same balance.
  - The mass of copper deposited on the cathode is calculated by subtracting the initial mass from the final mass.
  - The ECE of copper is calculated by dividing the mass of copper deposited by the product of the current and the time interval.

- The formula for the ECE of copper is:

  - ECE = m / (I x t)
  - where m is the mass of copper deposited in grams, I is the current in amperes, and t is the time interval in seconds.

- The ECE of copper can also be calculated theoretically by using Faraday's laws of electrolysis, which state that:

  - The mass of a substance deposited or liberated by an electric current is directly proportional to the quantity of charge passing through the electrolyte.
  - The mass of a substance deposited or liberated by an electric current is directly proportional to the equivalent weight of the substance.

- The equivalent weight of a substance is the ratio of its molar mass to its valency, which is the number of electrons involved in the redox reaction.
- The equivalent weight of copper is 63.5 / 2 = 31.75 g, since copper has a molar mass of 63.5 g and a valency of 2 in the reaction Cu^2+ + 2e^- -> Cu.
- The theoretical ECE of copper is:

  - ECE = equivalent weight / Faraday constant
  - where the Faraday constant is the charge of one mole of electrons, which is 96485 C.
  - ECE = 31.75 / 96485 = 0.000329 g/C

- The experimental and theoretical ECE of copper should be close to each other, but there may be some sources of error, such as:

  - Impurities in the copper electrodes or the copper sulfate solution
  - Inaccurate measurement of the mass, current or time
  - Loss of copper due to evaporation, spilling or incomplete drying
  - Side reactions or polarization of the electrodes

- To minimize the errors, the following precautions can be taken:

  - Use pure copper electrodes and copper sulfate solution
  - Use a sensitive balance and a calibrated ammeter
  - Use a stopwatch to measure the time accurately
  - Wash and dry the copper cathode thoroughly before and after the experiment
  - Avoid overloading or short-circuiting the power supply
  - Stir the copper sulfate solution gently to ensure uniform concentration
