##### 8. To calibrate the given ammeter and voltmeter by potentiometer.

- Calibration is the process of comparing the readings of an instrument with a standard reference value and adjusting the instrument accordingly.
- A potentiometer is a device that can measure the potential difference between two points in a circuit by balancing it with a known potential difference.
- An ammeter is an instrument that measures the electric current in a circuit. A voltmeter is an instrument that measures the potential difference across a component in a circuit.
- To calibrate the given ammeter and voltmeter by potentiometer, the following steps are followed:

  - Connect the ammeter in series with a battery and a rheostat. Connect the voltmeter in parallel with the battery. Connect the potentiometer in parallel with the battery and the voltmeter. Connect a galvanometer and a jockey to the potentiometer wire.
  - Adjust the rheostat to obtain a suitable current in the circuit. Note down the readings of the ammeter and the voltmeter.
  - Slide the jockey along the potentiometer wire until the galvanometer shows zero deflection. Note down the length of the potentiometer wire from the positive terminal to the jockey position. This length is proportional to the potential difference across the battery.
  - Repeat the above steps for different values of current by varying the rheostat. Record the readings of the ammeter, the voltmeter, and the potentiometer wire length in a table.
  - Plot a graph of the potentiometer wire length (x-axis) versus the ammeter reading (y-axis). The slope of this graph gives the calibration factor for the ammeter.
  - Plot another graph of the potentiometer wire length (x-axis) versus the voltmeter reading (y-axis). The slope of this graph gives the calibration factor for the voltmeter.
  - Compare the calibration factors with the nominal values of the ammeter and the voltmeter. If they are different, adjust the instruments accordingly.
