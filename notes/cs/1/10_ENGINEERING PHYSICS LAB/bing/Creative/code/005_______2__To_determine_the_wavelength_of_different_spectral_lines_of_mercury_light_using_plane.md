##### 2. To determine the wavelength of different spectral lines of mercury light using plane

- Mercury light is a type of gas discharge lamp that emits light when an electric current passes through a vapor of mercury.
- Mercury light has a characteristic spectrum of discrete wavelengths, or spectral lines, that correspond to the energy transitions of the electrons in the mercury atoms.
- The wavelengths of the prominent lines in mercury are purple (405 nm), blue (436 nm), blue-green (492 nm), greenish yellow (546 nm), yellow (577 nm and 579 nm), orange (623 nm), and red (691 nm)    .
- To determine the wavelength of different spectral lines of mercury light using plane, we can use a device called a diffraction grating, which is a thin film with many parallel slits that diffract the light into different angles.
- The diffraction grating equation relates the wavelength of the light, the angle of diffraction, the order of the spectrum, and the distance between the slits as follows:

    `λ = d sin θ / n`

    where λ is the wavelength, d is the distance between the slits, θ is the angle of diffraction, and n is the order of the spectrum.

- To measure the wavelength of different spectral lines of mercury light using plane, we can follow these steps:

    1. Set up the mercury light source, the diffraction grating, and a screen at a fixed distance L from the grating.
    2. Turn on the mercury light source and observe the spectrum of lines on the screen through the grating.
    3. Measure the distance x from the center of the screen to each spectral line of interest on both sides of the spectrum.
    4. Calculate the angle of diffraction θ for each spectral line using the formula:

        `tan θ = x / L`

    5. Substitute the values of θ, d, and n into the diffraction grating equation and solve for λ for each spectral line.
