#### Graphical and Computational Analysis

- Graphical analysis is a method of presenting and interpreting data using graphs, charts, diagrams, and other visual tools.
- Computational analysis is a method of applying mathematical and logical operations to data using algorithms, software, and hardware.
- Both methods are useful for exploring, summarizing, and communicating data in different contexts and domains.
- Some examples of graphical analysis are:
  - Line graphs: show the relationship between two variables over time or along a continuum.
  - Bar graphs: show the frequency or amount of different categories or groups.
  - Pie charts: show the proportion or percentage of different parts of a whole.
  - Scatter plots: show the correlation or association between two variables.
  - Histograms: show the distribution or spread of a single variable.
  - Box plots: show the range, median, and quartiles of a single variable or a group of variables.
- Some examples of computational analysis are:
  - Descriptive statistics: calculate and display measures of central tendency, variability, and shape of a data set, such as mean, median, mode, standard deviation, skewness, and kurtosis.
  - Inferential statistics: test hypotheses and draw conclusions about a population based on a sample, using methods such as confidence intervals, t-tests, ANOVA, chi-square, and regression.
  - Machine learning: train and evaluate models that can learn from data and make predictions or classifications, using methods such as supervised learning, unsupervised learning, and reinforcement learning.
  - Data mining: discover patterns, trends, and associations in large and complex data sets, using methods such as clustering, association rules, and anomaly detection.
  - Optimization: find the best or optimal solution to a problem that involves multiple constraints and objectives, using methods such as linear programming, integer programming, and genetic algorithms.
