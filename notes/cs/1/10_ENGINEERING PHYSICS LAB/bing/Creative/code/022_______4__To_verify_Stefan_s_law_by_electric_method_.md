##### 4. To verify Stefan’s law by electric method.

- Stefan’s law states that the total radiant heat energy emitted per second per unit area of a black body is directly proportional to the fourth power of its absolute temperature.
- The electric method involves heating a metal sphere by passing an electric current through it and measuring its temperature and the power supplied to it.
- The apparatus required for this experiment are: a metal sphere with a small hole, a thermocouple, a galvanometer, a rheostat, a voltmeter, an ammeter, a battery, a key, and connecting wires.
- The procedure is as follows:
  - Connect the circuit as shown in the figure below.
  - Adjust the rheostat to get a steady current through the sphere.
  - Measure the potential difference across the sphere using the voltmeter and the current through it using the ammeter.
  - Calculate the power supplied to the sphere as P = VI, where V is the potential difference and I is the current.
  - Insert the thermocouple into the hole of the sphere and measure the temperature of the sphere using the galvanometer.
  - Repeat the above steps for different values of current and power and record the corresponding temperatures.
  - Plot a graph of P versus T^4, where T is the absolute temperature of the sphere.
  - The graph should be a straight line passing through the origin, verifying Stefan’s law.
