##### 5. To determine resistance per unit length and specific resistance of a given resistance

- Resistance is the property of a material that opposes the flow of electric current through it. The SI unit of resistance is ohm (Ω).
- Resistance per unit length is the ratio of resistance to the length of a material. It is measured in ohm per meter (Ω/m).
- Specific resistance or resistivity is the resistance of a material of unit length and unit cross-sectional area. It is measured in ohm meter (Ωm).
- To determine the resistance per unit length and specific resistance of a given resistance, we need the following apparatus:
  - A resistance wire of known length and cross-sectional area
  - A meter bridge or a Wheatstone bridge
  - A galvanometer
  - A battery
  - A jockey
  - A rheostat
  - A key
  - A resistance box
  - A screw gauge
- The procedure is as follows:
  - Measure the diameter of the resistance wire using the screw gauge and calculate its cross-sectional area.
  - Connect the resistance wire in one arm of the meter bridge or Wheatstone bridge and the resistance box in the other arm.
  - Connect the battery, the key and the rheostat in series with the bridge.
  - Connect the galvanometer between the two junctions of the bridge.
  - Adjust the rheostat to get a suitable current in the circuit.
  - Slide the jockey along the bridge wire and find the null point where the galvanometer shows zero deflection.
  - Note the length of the bridge wire from one end to the null point. This is the balancing length.
  - Repeat the experiment for different values of resistance in the resistance box and find the corresponding balancing lengths.
  - Plot a graph of resistance (R) versus balancing length (l) and draw the best fit line.
  - Find the slope of the graph. This is the resistance per unit length of the given resistance wire.
  - Calculate the specific resistance of the given resistance wire using the formula:

  `ρ = R * A / l`

  where ρ is the specific resistance, R is the resistance, A is the cross-sectional area and l is the length of the wire.
