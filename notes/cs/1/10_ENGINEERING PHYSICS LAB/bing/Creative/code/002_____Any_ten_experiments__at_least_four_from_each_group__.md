Here are some possible experiments you can do for science, grouped by different fields. I have included the materials needed, the steps to follow, and the explanation of the results for each experiment. You can choose any ten experiments from these groups, as long as you have at least four from each group.

### Biology Experiments
- **Dancing popcorn**
  - Materials needed: 1-quart jar, water, baking soda, vinegar, popcorn kernels
  - Steps: Fill the jar halfway with water. Add a teaspoon of baking soda and stir well. Add a handful of popcorn kernels. Pour some vinegar into the jar and watch the popcorn dance up and down.
  - Explanation: The vinegar and baking soda react to produce carbon dioxide gas, which forms bubbles that attach to the popcorn kernels. The bubbles make the kernels lighter and float to the top. When the bubbles pop, the kernels sink back down. This cycle repeats until the reaction stops.
- **Lemon volcano**
  - Materials needed: Lemon, knife, baking soda, food coloring (optional)
  - Steps: Cut the lemon in half and squeeze out some of the juice. Use the knife to make a small hole in the center of the lemon. Sprinkle some baking soda into the hole. Add a few drops of food coloring if you want. Watch the lemon erupt with foam.
  - Explanation: The lemon juice is acidic and the baking soda is basic. When they mix, they produce a chemical reaction that releases carbon dioxide gas, which forms bubbles that create the foam.
- **Turning pennies green**
  - Materials needed: Bowl, paper towels, vinegar, salt, pennies
  - Steps: Line the bowl with paper towels. Pour some vinegar into the bowl and add a teaspoon of salt. Stir well. Place some pennies on the paper towels and make sure they are covered with the vinegar solution. Wait for a few hours or overnight and observe the color change.
  - Explanation: The vinegar and salt form a weak acid that dissolves the copper oxide layer on the pennies. The copper atoms react with oxygen in the air and form a green compound called copper carbonate, which gives the pennies a green patina.

### Chemistry Experiments
- **Making slime**
  - Materials needed: White glue, water, food coloring (optional), borax, mixing bowl, spoon
  - Steps: In the mixing bowl, mix half a cup of glue with half a cup of water. Add some food coloring if you want. In a separate cup, dissolve a teaspoon of borax in a cup of warm water. Slowly add the borax solution to the glue mixture and stir well. You will see the slime start to form. Knead the slime with your hands until it is smooth and stretchy.
  - Explanation: The glue contains a polymer called polyvinyl acetate, which is a long chain of molecules. The borax acts as a cross-linker that connects the polymer chains together, making them stickier and less runny. The slime is a non-Newtonian fluid, which means it can act like a solid or a liquid depending on how you handle it.
- **Exploding baggies**
  - Materials needed: Plastic zip-top bag, white vinegar, baking soda, tissue paper, measuring cup, spoon
  - Steps: Fill the bag about a quarter full with vinegar. Wrap some baking soda in a piece of tissue paper. Carefully drop the tissue paper into the bag and seal it quickly. Shake the bag gently and place it on the ground. Stand back and watch the bag inflate and burst.
  - Explanation: The vinegar and baking soda react to produce carbon dioxide gas, which fills up the bag and increases the pressure inside. When the pressure becomes too high, the bag pops and releases the gas.
- **Hot ice**
  - Materials needed: 4 cups white vinegar, 4 tablespoons baking soda, pot, stove, glass jar, spoon, bowl, ice
  - Steps: In the pot, heat the vinegar on the stove until it boils. Turn off the heat and add the baking soda slowly, stirring well. The mixture will fizz and bubble. Keep stirring until the fizzing stops. Pour the mixture into the glass jar and let it cool completely. You will get a clear liquid that is supersaturated with sodium acetate. To make the hot ice, pour some of the liquid into the bowl and drop an ice cube into it. Watch the liquid crystallize and feel the heat it releases.
