# Combination of Lenses

- A combination of lenses is a system of two or more lenses that are placed close to each other, either in contact or separated by a small distance.
- The combined focal length of a combination of lenses is the distance from the first lens to the final image formed by the system.
- The combined focal length depends on the focal lengths of the individual lenses and the distance between them.
- If the lenses are in contact, the combined focal length is given by the formula :

```
1/f = 1/f1 + 1/f2 + ... + 1/fn
```

where f is the combined focal length, and f1, f2, ..., fn are the focal lengths of the lenses in the system.

- If the lenses are separated by some distance d, the combined focal length is given by the formula :

```
1/f = 1/f1 + 1/f2 - d/(f1f2)
```

where f is the combined focal length, f1 and f2 are the focal lengths of the two lenses, and d is the distance between them.

- The formula for the combined focal length can be derived by using the thin lens equation for each lens and applying the principle of ray tracing.
- The thin lens equation relates the object distance u, the image distance v, and the focal length f of a thin lens as follows:

```
1/f = 1/u + 1/v
```

- Ray tracing is a method of drawing rays of light to show how they are refracted or reflected by a lens or a mirror.
- To find the combined focal length of a combination of lenses, we can trace the rays of light from the object to the final image and use the thin lens equation for each lens in the system.
