##### 8. To determine the wavelength of sodium light with the help of Fresnel’s bi-prism.

- Fresnel's bi-prism is a device that produces two coherent sources of light from a single source by using a thin prism with a very small refracting angle.
- The principle of Fresnel's bi-prism is based on the interference of light waves from two virtual sources that are symmetrically located on either side of the prism.
- The wavelength of sodium light can be determined by measuring the distance between the two virtual sources, the distance between the prism and the screen, and the fringe width on the screen.
- The experimental setup consists of a sodium lamp, a collimator, a Fresnel's bi-prism, a micrometer eyepiece, and a screen.
- The procedure is as follows:
  - Adjust the collimator to obtain a parallel beam of sodium light and place it in front of the sodium lamp.
  - Place the Fresnel's bi-prism on a horizontal optical bench and align it such that the refracting edge of the prism is perpendicular to the incident beam.
  - Place the micrometer eyepiece on the optical bench and move it along the bench until the two images of the slit formed by the prism are seen clearly and superimposed.
  - Adjust the eyepiece to measure the distance between the two images, which is equal to the distance between the two virtual sources. This distance is denoted by D.
  - Place the screen on the optical bench and move it away from the prism until a clear interference pattern of bright and dark fringes is seen on the screen.
  - Adjust the screen to make the fringes parallel and equally spaced.
  - Measure the distance between the prism and the screen. This distance is denoted by L.
  - Measure the width of a few fringes on the screen using a scale or a vernier caliper. The average fringe width is denoted by β.
  - Repeat the measurements for different values of L and calculate the average values of D, L, and β.
- The formula for the wavelength of sodium light is given by:

  λ = (D/L) * β

  where λ is the wavelength of sodium light, D is the distance between the two virtual sources, L is the distance between the prism and the screen, and β is the fringe width.
- The sources of error and precautions are as follows:
  - The prism should be thin and have a small refracting angle to avoid multiple reflections and dispersion of light.
  - The collimator should be adjusted to obtain a parallel beam of sodium light and the slit should be narrow and vertical.
  - The prism and the screen should be aligned perpendicular to the incident beam and the fringes should be parallel and equally spaced on the screen.
  - The eyepiece and the screen should be moved carefully and smoothly along the optical bench and the readings should be taken with minimum parallax error.
  - The measurements should be repeated for different values of L and the average values should be used to calculate the wavelength of sodium light.
