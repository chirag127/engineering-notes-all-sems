1. **Resistance**: The opposition to the flow of electric current through a conductor. It is measured in ohms (Ω) and can be calculated using Ohm's Law, which states that the resistance is equal to the voltage divided by the current.
2. **Capacitance**: The ability of a system to store an electric charge. It is measured in farads (F) and is determined by the distance between the plates of a capacitor, the area of the plates, and the dielectric constant of the material between the plates.
3. **Inductance**: The property of an electric conductor or circuit that causes an electromotive force to be generated by a change in the current flowing. It is measured in henries (H) and is determined by the number of turns in a coil, the cross-sectional area of the coil, and the magnetic permeability of the core material.
4. **Impedance**: The total opposition to the flow of alternating current in an electric circuit. It is measured in ohms (Ω) and is a complex quantity that takes into account both the resistance and the reactance of the circuit.
5. **Conductance**: The ease with which electric current flows through a conductor. It is the reciprocal of resistance and is measured in siemens (S).
6. **Reactance**: The opposition to the flow of alternating current in an electric circuit due to capacitance or inductance. It is measured in ohms (Ω) and is a complex quantity that varies with the frequency of the alternating current.
7. **Admittance**: The ease with which alternating current flows through an electric circuit. It is the reciprocal of impedance and is measured in siemens (S).
8. **Permittivity**: The measure of a material's ability to store an electric charge. It is determined by the dielectric constant of the material and is an important factor in the design of capacitors.
9. **Permeability**: The measure of a material's ability to conduct a magnetic field. It is determined by the magnetic properties of the material and is an important factor in the design of inductors and transformers.
