##### 8. To determine the wavelength of sodium light with the help of Fresnel’s bi-prism.

Fresnel's bi-prism is an optical instrument used to determine the wavelength of light. It consists of two prisms placed base to base, forming a small angle between them. When a beam of light passes through the bi-prism, it is split into two beams, which interfere with each other to produce a pattern of bright and dark fringes.

To determine the wavelength of sodium light using Fresnel's bi-prism, the following steps can be followed:

1. Set up the bi-prism experiment by placing the bi-prism in front of a sodium light source and a screen at a suitable distance.
2. Adjust the position of the bi-prism and the screen until a clear interference pattern is observed on the screen.
3. Measure the distance between the screen and the bi-prism, as well as the distance between the central bright fringe and a fringe of known order.
4. Use the formula for the fringe width to calculate the wavelength of the sodium light.

The formula for the fringe width is given by: w = λD/d, where w is the fringe width, λ is the wavelength of the light, D is the distance between the screen and the bi-prism, and d is the distance between the two virtual sources formed by the bi-prism.

By following these steps and using the formula, the wavelength of sodium light can be accurately determined using Fresnel's bi-prism.
