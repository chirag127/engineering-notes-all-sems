##### 7. To determine the electrochemical equivalent (ECE) of copper.

The electrochemical equivalent (ECE) of a substance is the mass of the substance deposited or dissolved at an electrode during electrolysis by the passage of one coulomb of electric charge. The ECE of copper can be determined by performing an experiment using a copper voltameter.

1. A copper voltameter consists of two copper electrodes immersed in a solution of copper sulfate.
2. A known amount of electric charge is passed through the solution using a constant current power supply.
3. The mass of copper deposited on the cathode is measured before and after the experiment.
4. The difference in mass gives the amount of copper deposited.
5. The ECE of copper is calculated by dividing the mass of copper deposited by the amount of electric charge passed.

This experiment can be repeated with different amounts of electric charge to obtain an average value for the ECE of copper. The ECE of copper can also be calculated theoretically using Faraday's laws of electrolysis. The theoretical and experimental values can be compared to evaluate the accuracy of the experiment.
