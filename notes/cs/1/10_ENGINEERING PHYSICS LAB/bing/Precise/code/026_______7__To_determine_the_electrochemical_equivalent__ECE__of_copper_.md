##### 7. To determine the electrochemical equivalent (ECE) of copper.

The electrochemical equivalent (ECE) of a substance is the mass of the substance deposited or dissolved at an electrode during electrolysis by the passage of one coulomb of electric charge. The ECE of copper can be determined by performing an experiment in which copper is deposited on an electrode during electrolysis.

The steps to determine the ECE of copper are as follows:

1. Set up an electrolytic cell with copper sulfate solution as the electrolyte and two copper electrodes.
2. Connect the electrodes to a power source and allow current to flow through the cell for a fixed amount of time.
3. Measure the mass of the copper deposited on the cathode.
4. Calculate the ECE of copper using the formula: ECE = (mass of copper deposited) / (charge passed).

The ECE of copper can be used to calculate the amount of copper that will be deposited on an electrode during electrolysis, given the amount of charge passed through the cell. This can be useful in various applications, such as electroplating and refining of copper.
