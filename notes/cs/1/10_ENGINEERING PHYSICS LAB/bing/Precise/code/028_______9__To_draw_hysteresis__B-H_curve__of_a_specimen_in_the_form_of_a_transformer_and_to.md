##### 9. To draw hysteresis (B-H curve) of a specimen in the form of a transformer and to

Hysteresis is the dependence of the state of a system on its history. In the context of magnetic materials, it refers to the lagging of the magnetization of a material behind the changes in the applied magnetic field. The hysteresis loop, also known as the B-H curve, is a graphical representation of this phenomenon.

To draw the hysteresis loop of a specimen in the form of a transformer, the following steps can be followed:

1. Set up the transformer with the specimen as the core, and connect it to an AC power source.
2. Connect an ammeter in series with the primary coil to measure the current, and a voltmeter across the secondary coil to measure the induced voltage.
3. Gradually increase the current in the primary coil and record the corresponding values of the induced voltage in the secondary coil.
4. Plot the induced voltage (B) against the current (H) to obtain the hysteresis loop.

The shape of the hysteresis loop depends on the magnetic properties of the specimen. Materials with a large hysteresis loop have a high coercivity and retentivity, meaning they require a large magnetic field to demagnetize and can retain their magnetization even after the applied field is removed. These materials are useful for making permanent magnets. On the other hand, materials with a small hysteresis loop have a low coercivity and retentivity, meaning they can be easily magnetized and demagnetized. These materials are useful for making transformer cores.
