##### 6. To study the resonance condition of a series LCR circuit.

A series LCR circuit consists of an inductor (L), a capacitor (C), and a resistor (R) connected in series. The resonance condition of a series LCR circuit occurs when the inductive reactance (XL) is equal to the capacitive reactance (XC). At this point, the circuit is said to be in resonance and the impedance of the circuit is at its minimum value.

The resonance frequency (fr) of a series LCR circuit can be calculated using the formula:

fr = 1 / (2π * √(LC))

At resonance, the voltage across the capacitor and the voltage across the inductor are equal in magnitude but opposite in phase. The current in the circuit is at its maximum value and is in phase with the voltage across the resistor.

The quality factor (Q) of a series LCR circuit is a measure of the sharpness of the resonance peak and is given by the formula:

Q = (2π * fr * L) / R

The bandwidth (Δf) of a series LCR circuit is the range of frequencies over which the circuit exhibits resonance behavior and is given by the formula:

Δf = fr / Q

In summary, the resonance condition of a series LCR circuit occurs when the inductive reactance is equal to the capacitive reactance. At resonance, the circuit exhibits a minimum impedance, maximum current, and the voltage across the capacitor and inductor are equal in magnitude but opposite in phase. The resonance frequency, quality factor, and bandwidth of the circuit can be calculated using the formulas provided above.
