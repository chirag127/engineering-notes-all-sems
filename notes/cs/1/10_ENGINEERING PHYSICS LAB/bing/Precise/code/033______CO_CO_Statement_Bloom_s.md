#### CO CO Statement Bloom’s

CO (Course Outcome) and CO statement are used to describe the expected outcomes of a course. These outcomes are usually defined in terms of knowledge, skills, and attitudes that students are expected to have acquired by the end of the course.

Bloom’s taxonomy is a framework for categorizing educational goals and objectives. It is often used to help design assessments, curriculum, and instructional methods. The taxonomy consists of six levels of cognitive learning, arranged in a hierarchy from lower-order thinking skills to higher-order thinking skills. These levels are:

1. Remembering: recalling information from memory.
2. Understanding: comprehending the meaning of information.
3. Applying: using information in a new situation.
4. Analyzing: breaking down information into parts and examining their relationships.
5. Evaluating: making judgments about the value of information.
6. Creating: combining information to form a new whole.

CO statements can be written using Bloom’s taxonomy to ensure that the course outcomes are aligned with the desired level of cognitive learning. For example, a CO statement for a course on programming might be: “By the end of this course, students will be able to apply their knowledge of programming concepts to design and implement a simple program (Applying).” This CO statement specifies that the desired outcome of the course is for students to be able to apply their knowledge of programming concepts, which corresponds to the third level of Bloom’s taxonomy.
