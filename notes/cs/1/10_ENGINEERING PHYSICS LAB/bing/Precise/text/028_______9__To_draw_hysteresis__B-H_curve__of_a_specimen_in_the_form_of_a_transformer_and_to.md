##### 9. To draw hysteresis (B-H curve) of a specimen in the form of a transformer and to

Hysteresis is the dependence of the state of a system on its history. In the context of magnetic materials, hysteresis refers to the lagging of the magnetization of a material behind the applied magnetic field. This phenomenon is graphically represented by the B-H curve, also known as the hysteresis loop.

To draw the hysteresis loop of a specimen in the form of a transformer, the following steps can be followed:

1. Set up the transformer with the specimen as the core, and connect it to an AC power source.
2. Connect an ammeter in series with the primary coil to measure the current, and a voltmeter across the secondary coil to measure the induced voltage.
3. Vary the current in the primary coil and record the corresponding values of the induced voltage in the secondary coil.
4. Plot the values of the induced voltage (B) against the current in the primary coil (H) to obtain the B-H curve.

The shape of the hysteresis loop depends on the magnetic properties of the specimen. Materials with a large hysteresis loop, such as hard magnetic materials, have a high coercivity and retain their magnetization even after the applied magnetic field is removed. On the other hand, materials with a small hysteresis loop, such as soft magnetic materials, have a low coercivity and lose their magnetization quickly when the applied magnetic field is removed.
