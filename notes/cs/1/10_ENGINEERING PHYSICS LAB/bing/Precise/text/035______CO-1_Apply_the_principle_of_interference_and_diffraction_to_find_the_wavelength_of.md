#### CO-1 Apply the principle of interference and diffraction to find the wavelength of

- Interference and diffraction are two phenomena that can be used to determine the wavelength of light.
- Interference occurs when two or more waves overlap and combine to form a new wave. The resulting wave can have a larger or smaller amplitude than the original waves, depending on the relative phases of the waves.
- Diffraction is the bending of waves around obstacles or through openings. When light passes through a narrow slit, it spreads out in a pattern called a diffraction pattern.
- The wavelength of light can be determined by measuring the spacing of the fringes in an interference or diffraction pattern.
- One common method for measuring the wavelength of light is to use a double-slit experiment. In this experiment, light passes through two narrow slits and forms an interference pattern on a screen.
- The spacing of the fringes in the interference pattern is related to the wavelength of the light and the distance between the slits. By measuring the fringe spacing and the slit separation, the wavelength of the light can be calculated.
- Another method for measuring the wavelength of light is to use a diffraction grating. A diffraction grating is a surface with many closely spaced parallel lines. When light is shone on the grating, it diffracts and forms a pattern of bright and dark spots on a screen.
- The spacing of the spots in the diffraction pattern is related to the wavelength of the light and the spacing of the lines on the grating. By measuring the spot spacing and the line spacing, the wavelength of the light can be calculated.
- These methods can be used to accurately determine the wavelength of light and are commonly used in scientific experiments and measurements.
