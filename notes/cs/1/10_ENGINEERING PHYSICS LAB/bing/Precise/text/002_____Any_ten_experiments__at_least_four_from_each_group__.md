### Any ten experiments (at least four from each group)

Here are ten experiments, with at least four from each group:

**Group 1:**
1. **The Milgram Experiment:** This experiment, conducted by Stanley Milgram in 1961, aimed to measure the willingness of participants to obey an authority figure who instructed them to perform acts that conflicted with their personal conscience.
2. **The Stanford Prison Experiment:** This experiment, conducted by Philip Zimbardo in 1971, aimed to investigate the psychological effects of perceived power, focusing on the struggle between prisoners and prison guards.
3. **The Asch Conformity Experiment:** This experiment, conducted by Solomon Asch in 1951, aimed to investigate the extent to which social pressure from a majority group could affect a person to conform.
4. **The Bobo Doll Experiment:** This experiment, conducted by Albert Bandura in 1961, aimed to investigate if social behaviors (i.e., aggression) can be acquired by observation and imitation.

**Group 2:**
1. **The Double-Slit Experiment:** This experiment, first performed by Thomas Young in 1801, aimed to demonstrate the wave-particle duality of light.
2. **The Michelson-Morley Experiment:** This experiment, conducted by Albert Michelson and Edward Morley in 1887, aimed to detect the presence of the luminiferous aether, a supposed medium for the propagation of light.
3. **The Rutherford Gold Foil Experiment:** This experiment, conducted by Ernest Rutherford in 1909, aimed to investigate the structure of the atom.
4. **The Cavendish Experiment:** This experiment, conducted by Henry Cavendish in 1798, aimed to measure the force of gravity between masses and determine the gravitational constant.

**Group 3:**
1. **The Pavlov's Dog Experiment:** This experiment, conducted by Ivan Pavlov in the 1890s, aimed to investigate classical conditioning, a type of learning in which an organism comes to associate stimuli.
2. **The Skinner Box Experiment:** This experiment, conducted by B.F. Skinner in the 1930s, aimed to investigate operant conditioning, a type of learning in which behavior is strengthened or weakened by the use of reinforcement or punishment.
