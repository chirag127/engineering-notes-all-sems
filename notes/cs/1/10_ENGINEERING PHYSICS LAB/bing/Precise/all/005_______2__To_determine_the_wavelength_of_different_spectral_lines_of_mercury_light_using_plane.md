##### 2. To determine the wavelength of different spectral lines of mercury light using plane

- The wavelength of different spectral lines of mercury light can be determined using a plane transmission diffraction grating.
- A diffraction grating is an optical component with a periodic structure that splits and diffracts light into several beams traveling in different directions.
- The directions of these beams depend on the spacing of the grating and the wavelength of the light.
- By measuring the angles of the diffracted beams, the wavelength of the light can be calculated using the grating equation.
- The grating equation is given by: nλ = d(sinθi + sinθm), where n is the order of the diffraction, λ is the wavelength of the light, d is the grating spacing, θi is the angle of incidence, and θm is the angle of diffraction.
- To determine the wavelength of different spectral lines of mercury light, the light is passed through a diffraction grating and the angles of the diffracted beams are measured.
- The grating equation is then used to calculate the wavelength of the light for each spectral line.
- This experiment can be performed using a spectrometer, which is an instrument used to measure the properties of light over a specific portion of the electromagnetic spectrum.
- The spectrometer consists of a collimator, a diffraction grating, and a telescope.
- The collimator produces a parallel beam of light, which is diffracted by the grating.
- The telescope is used to measure the angles of the diffracted beams.
- By performing this experiment, the wavelength of different spectral lines of mercury light can be accurately determined.
