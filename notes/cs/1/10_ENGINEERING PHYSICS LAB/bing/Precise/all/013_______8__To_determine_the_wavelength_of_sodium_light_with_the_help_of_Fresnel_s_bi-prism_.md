##### 8. To determine the wavelength of sodium light with the help of Fresnel’s bi-prism.

Fresnel's bi-prism is an optical instrument used to determine the wavelength of light. It consists of two prisms placed base to base, forming a bi-prism. When a beam of light passes through the bi-prism, it is split into two beams, which interfere to produce a pattern of fringes.

To determine the wavelength of sodium light using Fresnel's bi-prism, the following steps can be followed:

1. Set up the apparatus, consisting of a sodium lamp, a collimator, a bi-prism, and a telescope.
2. Adjust the collimator to produce a parallel beam of light.
3. Place the bi-prism in the path of the light beam, such that the light is split into two beams.
4. Observe the interference pattern produced by the two beams using the telescope.
5. Measure the distance between the fringes and the distance between the slit and the screen.
6. Use the formula for the wavelength of light in terms of the fringe spacing, the distance between the slit and the screen, and the angle of the prism to calculate the wavelength of the sodium light.

This experiment can be used to accurately determine the wavelength of sodium light, which is approximately 589 nanometers. It is an important experiment in the field of optics and is commonly performed in physics laboratories.
