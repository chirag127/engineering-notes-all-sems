##### 1. To determine the wavelength of sodium light by Newton’s ring experiment.

- Newton's ring experiment is a method to determine the wavelength of light using the phenomenon of interference.
- In this experiment, a plano-convex lens is placed on a flat glass plate, and a monochromatic light source, such as sodium light, is used to illuminate the setup.
- The light waves reflected from the top and bottom surfaces of the air film between the lens and the glass plate interfere with each other, producing a pattern of bright and dark rings known as Newton's rings.
- The radius of the rings can be measured using a traveling microscope, and the wavelength of the light can be calculated using the formula: `λ = (Dn^2 - D(n-m)^2) / 4R`, where `λ` is the wavelength of light, `Dn` is the diameter of the nth dark ring, `R` is the radius of curvature of the lens, and `m` is the number of rings between the two diameters being measured.
- This experiment is named after Sir Isaac Newton, who first described the phenomenon of interference in his book "Opticks" in 1704.
