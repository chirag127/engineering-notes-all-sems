#### CO-1 Apply the principle of interference and diffraction to find the wavelength of

- Interference is the phenomenon of superposition of two or more waves that results in a new wave pattern. Diffraction is the phenomenon of bending of waves around obstacles or apertures. Both interference and diffraction are based on the principle of superposition, which states that the net displacement of a medium due to two or more waves is the algebraic sum of the displacements due to each wave.
- To find the wavelength of a wave using interference, one can use a device called a diffraction grating, which consists of a large number of equally spaced parallel slits. When a monochromatic light source is incident on the grating, each slit acts as a secondary source of light and produces a diffraction pattern. The diffraction patterns from different slits interfere with each other and form a series of bright and dark fringes on a screen. The position of the bright fringes depends on the wavelength of the light, the slit spacing, and the order of the fringe. The wavelength can be calculated using the formula:

  $$d \sin \theta = m \lambda$$

  where $d$ is the slit spacing, $\theta$ is the angle of the fringe from the central axis, $m$ is the order of the fringe, and $\lambda$ is the wavelength of the light  .
- To find the wavelength of a wave using diffraction, one can use a device called a single-slit diffraction, which consists of a narrow slit that allows a monochromatic light source to pass through. The slit acts as a secondary source of light and produces a diffraction pattern on a screen. The diffraction pattern consists of a central bright fringe and a series of alternating bright and dark fringes on either side. The width of the central bright fringe depends on the wavelength of the light and the slit width. The wavelength can be calculated using the formula:

  $$a \sin \theta = m \lambda$$

  where $a$ is the slit width, $\theta$ is the angle of the fringe from the central axis, $m$ is the order of the fringe, and $\lambda$ is the wavelength of the light .
