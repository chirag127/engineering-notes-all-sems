Transmission grating is a type of diffraction grating that is used to disperse incident light into a spectrum. It is different from reflection grating, which reflects all incident light, as far as it is not absorbed or scattered. Transmission grating can be either volume Bragg grating or surface relief grating.

Some of the main features of transmission grating are:

- It is made by creating a replica of a master grating with a thin, vacuum deposition-coated layer, which has the same grooved surface as the master grating.
- It has no reflective overcoat, which reduces polarization sensitivity and allows more light to pass through.
- It can be used in compact, in-line configurations, as the light is transmitted through the grating, rather than reflected by it.
- It has a high angular dispersion, which depends on the angle of incidence and the groove spacing. The dispersion increases as the angle of incidence increases or as the groove spacing decreases.
- It can deliver record-breaking optical performance, with high diffraction efficiency, low wavefront distortion, and high laser damage threshold.

The diagram shows how the incident light is diffracted into different orders, depending on the angle of incidence and the groove spacing. The 0th order is the undiffracted light, which passes through the grating without changing its direction. The 1st order is the light that is diffracted by one wavelength, and the 2nd order is the light that is diffracted by two wavelengths. The higher the order, the more the light is dispersed and the lower the intensity.
