##### 4. To determine the focal length of the combination of two lenses separated by a distance

- The focal length of a combination of two lenses separated by a distance is given by the formula:

  `1/f = 1/f1 + 1/f2 - d/f1f2`

  where f is the focal length of the combination, f1 and f2 are the focal lengths of the individual lenses, and d is the distance between them.

- To determine the focal length of the combination experimentally, we need the following apparatus:

  - A convex lens and a concave lens of known focal lengths
  - An optical bench with a meter scale
  - A needle fixed on a stand
  - A white screen

- The procedure is as follows:

  - Place the convex lens and the concave lens on the optical bench with a small distance between them. Adjust the distance until the image of a distant object (such as a tree or a building) formed by the combination is clear and sharp on the screen.
  - Measure the distance between the lenses (d) and the distance between the screen and the lens combination (v) using the meter scale.
  - Repeat the above steps for different values of d and v and record the observations in a table.
  - Calculate the focal length of the combination (f) using the formula for each set of observations and take the average value as the final result.
  - Compare the experimental value of f with the theoretical value obtained by substituting the known values of f1 and f2 in the formula.
