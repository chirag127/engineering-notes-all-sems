##### 1. To determine the energy band gap of a given semiconductor material.

- The energy band gap of a semiconductor is the difference between the energy of the highest occupied state in the valence band and the lowest unoccupied state in the conduction band.
- The energy band gap determines the electrical conductivity and the optical properties of a semiconductor. A larger band gap means a lower conductivity and a higher transparency to visible light.
- The energy band gap of a semiconductor can be measured by various methods, such as optical absorption, photoluminescence, electrical conductivity, or Hall effect.
- One of the common methods to measure the energy band gap of a semiconductor is the optical absorption method, which is based on the principle that a semiconductor absorbs photons with energy greater than or equal to its band gap and transmits photons with lower energy.
- The optical absorption method involves shining a monochromatic light of variable wavelength or frequency on a thin film or a wafer of the semiconductor material and measuring the transmitted or reflected intensity of the light.
- The absorption coefficient of the semiconductor material is calculated from the measured intensity and the thickness of the film or wafer. The absorption coefficient is proportional to the number of photons absorbed per unit length of the material.
- The absorption coefficient of the semiconductor material varies with the wavelength or frequency of the incident light. At low frequencies, the absorption coefficient is zero or very small, as the photons do not have enough energy to excite the electrons from the valence band to the conduction band. As the frequency increases, the absorption coefficient increases sharply at a certain threshold frequency, which corresponds to the energy band gap of the material. Above this frequency, the absorption coefficient decreases gradually as the number of available states in the conduction band decreases.
- The energy band gap of the semiconductor material can be determined by plotting the absorption coefficient versus the photon energy and finding the point where the curve shows a sharp increase. Alternatively, the energy band gap can be determined by plotting the square root of the absorption coefficient versus the photon energy and finding the point where the curve intersects the photon energy axis. This point is called the absorption edge or the optical band gap of the material.
