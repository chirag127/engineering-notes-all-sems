# Given semiconductor material using Hall effect setup

The Hall effect is a phenomenon that occurs when a current-carrying conductor is placed in a magnetic field perpendicular to the direction of the current. The magnetic field exerts a force on the moving charge carriers, causing them to accumulate on one side of the conductor. This creates a potential difference across the conductor, known as the Hall voltage.

The Hall effect can be used to determine the type, concentration, and mobility of charge carriers in a given semiconductor material. The setup for this experiment consists of the following components:

- A thin rectangular slab of the semiconductor material, with four electrodes attached to its edges.
- A constant current source that supplies a current I through the slab from one pair of electrodes.
- A voltmeter that measures the Hall voltage V<sub>H</sub> across the other pair of electrodes.
- A pair of magnets that produce a uniform magnetic field B perpendicular to the slab.
