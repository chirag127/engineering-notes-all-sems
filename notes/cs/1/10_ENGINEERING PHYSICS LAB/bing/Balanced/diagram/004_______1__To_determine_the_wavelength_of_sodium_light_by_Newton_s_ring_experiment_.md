##### 1. To determine the wavelength of sodium light by Newton’s ring experiment.

- Newton's ring experiment is a method of measuring the wavelength of light using the phenomenon of interference.
- The setup consists of a plano-convex lens of large radius of curvature placed on a plane glass plate. A monochromatic light source, such as a sodium lamp, is used to illuminate the lens from above.
- The light rays reflected from the upper and lower surfaces of the air film between the lens and the plate interfere to produce a pattern of bright and dark rings, called Newton's rings, centered at the point of contact of the lens and the plate.
- The radius of the nth bright ring is given by the formula:

  `r_n = sqrt[(n + 1/2)Rλ]`

  where R is the radius of curvature of the lens, λ is the wavelength of light, and n is the order of the ring.
- To determine the wavelength of sodium light, the following steps are followed:

  - Measure the diameter of several bright rings using a traveling microscope and note down the corresponding values of n.
  - Plot a graph of D^2 (the square of the diameter) versus n and obtain the slope of the line.
  - Calculate the value of R using the formula:

    `R = (D_m^2 - D_n^2) / 4(m - n)h`

    where D_m and D_n are the diameters of the mth and nth rings, respectively, and h is the thickness of the air film at the point of contact, which can be measured using a micrometer screw gauge.
  - Substitute the values of R and the slope of the graph in the formula for r_n and solve for λ.
  - Compare the obtained value of λ with the known value of sodium light, which is 589 nm.
