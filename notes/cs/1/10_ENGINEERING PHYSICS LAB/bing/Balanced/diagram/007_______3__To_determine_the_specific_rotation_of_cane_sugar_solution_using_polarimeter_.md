##### 3. To determine the specific rotation of cane sugar solution using polarimeter.

- A polarimeter is an instrument that measures the angle of rotation of plane-polarized light by an optically active substance, such as cane sugar solution.
- The specific rotation of a substance is defined as the angle of rotation of plane-polarized light per unit length of the substance and per unit concentration of the solution, at a given temperature and wavelength of light.
- The specific rotation of cane sugar solution can be determined by the following formula:

$$[\alpha]_T^\lambda = \frac{\alpha}{l \times c}$$

where $[\alpha]_T^\lambda$ is the specific rotation at temperature $T$ and wavelength $\lambda$, $\alpha$ is the observed angle of rotation, $l$ is the length of the polarimeter tube, and $c$ is the concentration of the solution.

- To perform the experiment, the following steps are followed:

  - Prepare a standard cane sugar solution of known concentration and fill the polarimeter tube with it.
  - Adjust the polarimeter such that the plane of polarization of the light source is parallel to the plane of the analyzer.
  - Rotate the analyzer until the field of view is equally bright on both sides and note the reading of the vernier scale. This is the zero reading.
  - Rotate the analyzer in the opposite direction until the field of view is equally bright again and note the reading of the vernier scale. This is the reading for the cane sugar solution.
  - Calculate the angle of rotation by subtracting the zero reading from the reading for the cane sugar solution.
  - Repeat the steps for different concentrations of the cane sugar solution and record the observations in a table.
  - Plot a graph of angle of rotation versus concentration and find the slope of the line. This is the specific rotation of the cane sugar solution at the given temperature and wavelength of light.
