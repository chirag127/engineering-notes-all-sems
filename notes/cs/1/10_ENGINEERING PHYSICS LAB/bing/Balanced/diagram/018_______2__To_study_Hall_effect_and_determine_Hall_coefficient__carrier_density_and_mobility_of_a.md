##### Hall effect experiment

- The Hall effect experiment was conducted by Edwin Hall in 1879 to determine the sign of the charge carriers in a current flow .
- The Hall effect is the phenomenon of a transverse electric field (Hall voltage) generated in a conductor when a current flows through it in the presence of a perpendicular magnetic field .
- The Hall effect can be used to measure the magnetic field strength, the type and density of charge carriers, and the Hall mobility of a semiconductor .

##### Hall coefficient, carrier density and mobility

- The Hall coefficient (R_H) is defined as the ratio of the Hall voltage to the product of the current density and the magnetic field .
- R_H = V_H / (j * B)
- The sign of the Hall coefficient indicates the type of charge carriers: positive for holes and negative for electrons .
- The carrier density (n) is the number of charge carriers per unit volume of the semiconductor .
- n = 1 / (R_H * q)
- where q is the elementary charge of the carrier
- The Hall mobility (μ_H) is a measure of how easily the charge carriers can move in the semiconductor under the influence of an electric field .
- μ_H = R_H * σ
- where σ is the electrical conductivity of the semiconductor
- The Hall mobility can also be expressed in terms of the drift velocity (v_d) of the charge carriers and the electric field (E).
- μ_H = v_d / E

- A semiconductor sample is connected to a power supply that provides a current (I) through it.
- A pair of voltmeters measure the potential difference (V) across the sample and the Hall voltage (V_H) between the top and bottom surfaces of the sample.
- A pair of magnets produce a uniform magnetic field (B) perpendicular to the current flow and the sample thickness (t).
- The Hall voltage is proportional to the product of the current, the magnetic field and the Hall coefficient, and inversely proportional to the sample thickness .
- V_H = R_H * I * B / t
