##### 9. To determine the coefficient of viscosity of a given liquid.

- Viscosity is a measure of the resistance of a fluid to flow. It depends on the intermolecular forces and the temperature of the fluid.
- The coefficient of viscosity (η) of a liquid is defined as the ratio of the shear stress (τ) to the rate of shear strain (γ) in the liquid. Mathematically, η = τ/γ.
- The unit of viscosity is pascal-second (Pa s) in SI system and poise (P) in CGS system. 1 Pa s = 10 P.
- The coefficient of viscosity of a liquid can be determined by various methods, such as Stokes' method, Ostwald's viscometer, capillary flow method, etc.
- One of the common methods to determine the coefficient of viscosity of a liquid is by using a capillary tube and measuring the time taken by the liquid to flow through it under a constant pressure difference.
- The apparatus required for this method are a capillary tube, a U-tube manometer, a stopwatch, a thermometer, and a liquid whose viscosity is to be determined.
- The procedure of this method is as follows:

  - Fill the U-tube manometer with the liquid and connect it to the capillary tube.
  - Adjust the pressure difference between the two arms of the U-tube by raising or lowering one arm until the liquid level in the capillary tube is at a convenient height.
  - Note the temperature of the liquid and the pressure difference (h) between the two arms of the U-tube using a thermometer and a scale.
  - Start the stopwatch and note the time (t) taken by the liquid to flow through a known length (l) of the capillary tube.
  - Repeat the experiment for different values of pressure difference and length of the capillary tube and record the observations in a table.
  - Calculate the average time (t) for each set of observations and plot a graph of t versus l/h.
  - Draw the best fit straight line through the points and find its slope (m).
  - The coefficient of viscosity (η) of the liquid can be calculated by using the formula:

    η = (πr^4)/(8m)

    where r is the radius of the capillary tube, which can be determined by measuring the volume (V) and length (L) of the capillary tube and using the formula:

    r = √(V/πL)

- The sources of error and precautions in this method are:

  - The capillary tube should be clean and free from any air bubbles or dust particles.
  - The liquid should be pure and homogeneous and its temperature should be constant throughout the experiment.
  - The pressure difference should be maintained constant and the liquid level in the capillary tube should be read carefully with a horizontal eye.
  - The stopwatch should be started and stopped accurately and the time measurements should be repeated for accuracy.
