##### 2. To study Hall effect and determine Hall coefficient, carrier density and mobility of a semiconductor

- The Hall effect is the phenomenon of a transverse electric field (Hall voltage) generated in a conductor when a current flows through it in the presence of a perpendicular magnetic field .
- The Hall effect can be used to determine the sign, density and mobility of the charge carriers in a material    .
- The Hall coefficient, R_H, is defined as the ratio of the Hall voltage to the product of the current and the magnetic field  .
- The carrier density, n, is the number of charge carriers per unit volume in the material  .
- The mobility, μ, is the measure of how easily the charge carriers can move in the material under the influence of an electric field  .
- The Hall effect experiment consists of the following steps   :
  - A thin rectangular sample of a semiconductor (such as germanium or silicon) is connected to a constant current source and placed between the poles of a magnet.
  - The magnetic field is varied and the Hall voltage across the sample is measured using a voltmeter.
  - The sign of the Hall voltage indicates the sign of the charge carriers in the sample. A positive Hall voltage means that the charge carriers are positive (holes), while a negative Hall voltage means that they are negative (electrons).
  - The Hall coefficient is calculated using the formula R_H = V_H / (I B), where V_H is the Hall voltage, I is the current, and B is the magnetic field.
  - The carrier density is calculated using the formula n = 1 / (q R_H), where q is the elementary charge.
  - The mobility is calculated using the formula μ = R_H / ρ, where ρ is the resistivity of the sample.
