##### 4. To verify Stefan’s law by electric method.

- Stefan’s law states that the total radiant heat energy emitted per second per unit area of a black body is directly proportional to the fourth power of its absolute temperature.
- Mathematically, Stefan’s law can be written as:

`E = σT^4`

where E is the radiant heat energy emitted per second per unit area, T is the absolute temperature of the black body, and σ is the Stefan’s constant.

- To verify Stefan’s law by electric method, we need the following apparatus:

  - A Leslie’s cube, which is a hollow metal cube with blackened surfaces and a small hole at the center of each face. The cube is filled with boiling water and acts as a black body radiator.
  - A thermopile, which is a device that converts thermal energy into electrical energy. It consists of a series of thermocouples connected in series. A thermocouple is a pair of wires of different metals joined at one end. When the junction is heated, an electric current is produced in the circuit.
  - A galvanometer, which is a device that measures the electric current in the circuit.
  - A thermometer, which measures the temperature of the boiling water in the Leslie’s cube.
  - A meter scale, which measures the distance between the thermopile and the Leslie’s cube.

- The experimental setup is as follows:

  - The Leslie’s cube is filled with boiling water and placed on a stand. The thermometer is inserted in the cube to measure the temperature of the water.
  - The thermopile is placed at a fixed distance from the Leslie’s cube, facing one of its faces. The galvanometer is connected to the thermopile to measure the electric current produced by the thermopile.
  - The experiment is repeated for different faces of the Leslie’s cube and for different distances between the thermopile and the cube.

- The observations are recorded in a table as follows:

| Distance (d) | Face | Temperature (T) | Current (I) |
| ------------ | ---- | --------------- | ----------- |
| ...          | ...  | ...             | ...         |

- The calculations are done as follows:

  - The radiant heat energy emitted per second per unit area by the Leslie’s cube is proportional to the electric current produced by the thermopile. Therefore, we can write:

    `E ∝ I`

  - The area of each face of the Leslie’s cube is inversely proportional to the square of the distance between the thermopile and the cube. Therefore, we can write:

    `A ∝ 1/d^2`

  - Combining the above two equations, we get:

    `E/A ∝ I/d^2`

  - Using Stefan’s law, we can write:

    `E/A = σT^4`

  - Comparing the above two equations, we get:

    `σT^4 ∝ I/d^2`

  - Taking logarithms on both sides, we get:

    `log σ + 4 log T = log I - 2 log d`

  - This is the equation of a straight line with slope 4 and intercept log σ.

- The graph is plotted as follows:

  - The values of log T and log I/d^2 are plotted on the x-axis and y-axis respectively.
  - A straight line is drawn passing through the points.
  - The slope of the line is measured and found to be 4.
  - The intercept of the line is measured and found to be log σ.

- The results are as follows:

  - The slope of the graph is 4, which verifies Stefan’s law.
  - The intercept of the graph gives the value of Stefan’s constant, which can be compared with the standard value of 5.67 x 10^-8 W/m^2 K^4.
