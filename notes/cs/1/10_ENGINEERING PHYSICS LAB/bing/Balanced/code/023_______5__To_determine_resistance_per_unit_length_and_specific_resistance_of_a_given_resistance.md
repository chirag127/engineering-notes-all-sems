##### 5. To determine resistance per unit length and specific resistance of a given resistance

- Resistance is the property of a material that opposes the flow of electric current through it. It is measured in ohms (Ω).
- Resistance per unit length is the ratio of resistance to the length of a material. It is measured in ohms per meter (Ω/m).
- Specific resistance or resistivity is the resistance of a material of unit length and unit cross-sectional area. It is measured in ohm-meter (Ωm).
- To determine the resistance per unit length and specific resistance of a given resistance, we need the following apparatus:
  - A resistance wire of known length and cross-sectional area
  - A meter bridge
  - A galvanometer
  - A battery
  - A jockey
  - A rheostat
  - A key
  - A resistance box
  - A screw gauge
- The procedure is as follows:
  - Connect the resistance wire between points A and B of the meter bridge. Connect the resistance box between points C and D of the meter bridge. Connect the galvanometer between the jockey and the point E of the meter bridge. Connect the battery, the rheostat and the key in series with the meter bridge.
  - Adjust the rheostat to get a suitable current in the circuit. Close the key and slide the jockey along the wire AB until the galvanometer shows zero deflection. Note the position of the jockey as l.
  - Repeat the above step for different values of resistance in the resistance box and note the corresponding values of l.
  - Plot a graph of l versus R, where R is the resistance in the resistance box. The slope of the graph gives the resistance per unit length of the wire AB.
  - Measure the diameter of the wire AB using the screw gauge and calculate its cross-sectional area A.
  - Calculate the specific resistance of the wire AB using the formula ρ = RA/l, where ρ is the specific resistance, R is the resistance per unit length, A is the cross-sectional area and l is the length of the wire AB.
