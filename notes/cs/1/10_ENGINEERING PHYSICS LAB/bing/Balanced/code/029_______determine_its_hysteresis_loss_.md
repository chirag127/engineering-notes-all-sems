##### Hysteresis loss

- Hysteresis loss is the loss of energy due to the repeated magnetization and demagnetization of a magnetic material when it is subjected to an alternating magnetic field.
- Hysteresis loss occurs because the magnetic domains in the material do not align and reverse their direction instantly, but lag behind the applied field. This causes friction and heat within the material, which results in energy loss.
- Hysteresis loss can be reduced by using materials with low coercivity and high permeability, such as soft iron or ferrites, which are easily magnetized and demagnetized with minimal external field.
- Hysteresis loss can be calculated by using the following formula:

`Hysteresis loss = η * B_max^1.6 * f * V`

where

`η` is the hysteresis coefficient, which depends on the material and its magnetic history

`B_max` is the maximum flux density in the material

`f` is the frequency of the alternating magnetic field

`V` is the volume of the material

- Hysteresis loss is one of the sources of eddy current loss, which is the total loss of energy due to the induced currents in a magnetic material when it is exposed to an alternating magnetic field. Eddy current loss also includes the loss due to the resistance of the material and the skin effect, which is the tendency of the current to flow near the surface of the material. Eddy current loss can be reduced by using laminated or powdered materials, which increase the resistance and decrease the cross-sectional area of the current paths.
