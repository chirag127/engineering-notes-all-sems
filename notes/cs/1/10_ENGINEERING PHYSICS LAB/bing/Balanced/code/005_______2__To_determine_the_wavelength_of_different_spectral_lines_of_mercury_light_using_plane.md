##### 2. To determine the wavelength of different spectral lines of mercury light using plane

- The aim of this experiment is to measure the wavelength of different spectral lines of mercury light using a plane diffraction grating and a spectrometer .
- The apparatus required are a diffraction grating, a spectrometer, a mercury lamp, and a reading lens .
- The theory behind this experiment is based on the diffraction of light by a grating, which is a periodic array of narrow slits. The grating produces a series of bright and dark fringes on a screen or a telescope, depending on the wavelength of the incident light and the angle of observation.
- The formula for the diffraction grating is given by:

    `sin θ = nλ/N`

    where θ is the angle of diffraction, n is the order of the fringe, λ is the wavelength of the light, and N is the number of lines per unit length of the grating .

- The procedure for the experiment is as follows:

    - Set up the spectrometer and the mercury lamp as shown in the figure below .

        ![Figure 1: Experimental setup](https://gstphysicslab.files.wordpress.com/2018/08/diffraction-grating-normal-incidence.jpg?w=640)

    - Adjust the collimator and the telescope to make them parallel to each other and perpendicular to the grating. This is the zero position of the spectrometer.
    - Place the grating on the prism table and rotate it slightly until the central bright fringe is seen in the telescope. This is the normal incidence position of the grating.
    - Rotate the telescope to one side and observe the spectrum of mercury light. You should see different colored lines corresponding to different wavelengths of light.
    - Identify the green line of mercury, which has a known wavelength of 546.1 nm . Measure the angle of diffraction θ for this line using the vernier scale of the spectrometer. Repeat this for the other side of the spectrum and take the mean value of θ.
    - Calculate the number of lines per meter N of the grating using the formula:

        `N = nλ/sin θ`

        where n is the order of the fringe, which is 1 for the first order.
    - Repeat the steps 4 to 6 for the other prominent lines of mercury, such as the violet line (436 nm), the yellow line (577 nm and 579 nm), and the red line (690 nm) . Calculate the wavelength of each line using the formula:

        `λ = N sin θ/n`

        where N is the number of lines per meter of the grating, θ is the angle of diffraction, and n is the order of the fringe.
    - Compare the calculated wavelengths with the standard values and calculate the percentage error.

- The expected results are that the calculated wavelengths of the spectral lines of mercury should be close to the standard values, with a small percentage error. The error may be due to the limitations of the apparatus, such as the accuracy of the vernier scale, the alignment of the grating, and the resolution of the telescope.
