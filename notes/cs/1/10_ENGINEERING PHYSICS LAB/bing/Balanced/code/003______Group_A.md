#### Group A

- Group A is a category of blood types that have the A antigen on the surface of red blood cells and the anti-B antibody in the plasma.
- The A antigen is a glycoprotein that is attached to the membrane of red blood cells by a glycosylphosphatidylinositol (GPI) anchor.
- The anti-B antibody is an immunoglobulin M (IgM) that binds to the B antigen on foreign red blood cells and causes agglutination and hemolysis.
- Group A blood types can donate blood to other group A and group AB recipients, and can receive blood from group A and group O donors.
- Group A blood types are the second most common in the world, after group O, and are found in about 30% of the population.
- Group A blood types are associated with certain traits and diseases, such as higher risk of gastric cancer, lower risk of malaria, and higher levels of cortisol.
