##### 8. To determine the wavelength of sodium light with the help of Fresnel’s bi-prism.

- Fresnel's bi-prism is a device that produces two coherent sources of light from a single source by using a thin prism with a very small refracting angle.
- The principle of the experiment is based on the interference of light waves from the two virtual sources and the measurement of the fringe width.
- The fringe width is given by the formula: β = λD/d, where λ is the wavelength of light, D is the distance between the slit and the screen, and d is the distance between the two virtual sources.
- The distance d can be calculated by using the formula: d = αa, where α is the refracting angle of the bi-prism and a is the distance between the slit and the bi-prism.
- The refracting angle α can be determined by using the formula: α = (x1 + x2)/a, where x1 and x2 are the distances of the two images of the slit from the central white fringe on the screen.
- The procedure of the experiment is as follows:
  - Set up the optical bench with the sodium lamp, the slit, the bi-prism and the eyepiece as shown in the figure below.
  - Adjust the slit width to get a clear and sharp image of the slit on the eyepiece.
  - Move the eyepiece along the bench and observe the interference fringes. Adjust the bi-prism to get the fringes parallel and equally spaced.
  - Measure the distance D between the slit and the eyepiece using a meter scale.
  - Measure the distance a between the slit and the bi-prism using a meter scale.
  - Measure the distance x1 and x2 between the two images of the slit and the central white fringe using a traveling microscope attached to the eyepiece.
  - Calculate the refracting angle α using the formula: α = (x1 + x2)/a.
  - Calculate the distance d between the two virtual sources using the formula: d = αa.
  - Calculate the fringe width β using the formula: β = λD/d.
  - Repeat the experiment for different values of D and a and take the average of the fringe width β.
  - Calculate the wavelength of sodium light using the formula: λ = βd/D.
