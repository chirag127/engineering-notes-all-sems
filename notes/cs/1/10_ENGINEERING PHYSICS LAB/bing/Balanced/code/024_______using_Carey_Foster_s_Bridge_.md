##### Using Carey Foster's Bridge

- Carey Foster's bridge is a device that can be used to measure the resistance of a wire or a small difference in resistance between two resistors.
- The device consists of a long wire of uniform cross-section and resistance, connected in series with a galvanometer and a battery. The wire is divided into two equal parts by a sliding contact, which can be moved along the wire. The two parts of the wire form the arms of a Wheatstone bridge, with the galvanometer and the battery forming the other two arms.
- To use the device, the sliding contact is adjusted until the galvanometer shows zero deflection, indicating that the bridge is balanced. The resistance of the wire between the sliding contact and one end of the wire is then equal to the resistance of the other arm of the bridge, which can be a known resistor or another wire of the same material and cross-section.
- The resistance of the wire can be calculated by multiplying the length of the wire between the sliding contact and one end of the wire by the resistance per unit length of the wire, which can be determined by measuring the total resistance and length of the wire.
- To measure the difference in resistance between two resistors, the device can be modified by connecting the two resistors in series with the battery and the galvanometer, and placing the sliding contact at the junction of the two resistors. The bridge is then balanced by adjusting the sliding contact along the wire, and the difference in resistance can be calculated by multiplying the length of the wire between the sliding contact and one end of the wire by the resistance per unit length of the wire.
