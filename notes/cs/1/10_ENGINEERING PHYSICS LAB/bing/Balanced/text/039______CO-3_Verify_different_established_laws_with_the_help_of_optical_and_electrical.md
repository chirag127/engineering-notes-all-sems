#### CO-3 Verify different established laws with the help of optical and electrical

- This topic covers the experimental verification of some of the fundamental laws of optics and electricity using simple apparatus and measurements.
- Some of the laws that can be verified are:
  - Snell's law of refraction: This law states that the ratio of the sine of the angle of incidence to the sine of the angle of refraction is constant for a given pair of media. This can be verified by using a glass slab, a ray box, a protractor and a ruler.
  - Ohm's law: This law states that the current flowing through a conductor is directly proportional to the potential difference across it, provided the physical conditions remain constant. This can be verified by using a battery, a rheostat, an ammeter, a voltmeter and a resistor.
  - Kirchhoff's laws: These laws state that the algebraic sum of the currents at any junction in a circuit is zero (junction rule) and the algebraic sum of the products of the currents and resistances in any closed loop is equal to the algebraic sum of the emfs in that loop (loop rule). These can be verified by using a battery, a rheostat, an ammeter, a voltmeter and several resistors arranged in different configurations.
  - Joule's law of heating: This law states that the heat produced by an electric current in a conductor is proportional to the square of the current, the resistance of the conductor and the time for which the current flows. This can be verified by using a battery, a rheostat, an ammeter, a voltmeter, a calorimeter, a thermometer and a coil of wire immersed in water.
- The verification of these laws involves the following steps:
  - Setting up the experimental apparatus according to a given diagram or instruction.
  - Taking the necessary readings or measurements with appropriate accuracy and precision.
  - Plotting the relevant graphs or tables to show the relationship between the variables involved.
  - Comparing the experimental results with the theoretical predictions or formulas.
  - Calculating the percentage error or deviation and discussing the possible sources of error or uncertainty.
  - Drawing the conclusions and stating the validity or limitations of the laws.
