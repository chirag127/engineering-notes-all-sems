### Any ten experiments (at least four from each group)

Here are some examples of experiments that you can perform using simple materials and methods. They are divided into two groups: physical science and life science.

#### Physical science experiments

- **Dancing popcorn**: You can make popcorn kernels dance in a jar of water by adding baking soda and vinegar. This creates carbon dioxide bubbles that lift the kernels up and down.
- **Lemon volcano**: You can create a mini volcano by cutting a lemon in half and squeezing some juice into a bowl. Then, add some baking soda and watch the fizzing reaction.
- **Turning pennies green**: You can make pennies look old and green by soaking them in a bowl of vinegar and salt. This causes a chemical reaction that forms copper carbonate on the surface of the coins.
- **Glitter germs**: You can demonstrate how germs spread by using glitter and water. Sprinkle some glitter on a plate of water and touch it with your finger. Then, touch another plate of water and see how the glitter transfers. You can also try washing your hands with soap and water to see how it removes the glitter.
- **Hot ice**: You can make a solution that turns solid when you touch it by boiling vinegar and baking soda together. Then, let it cool down and pour it into a glass. When you touch it with a spoon or your finger, it will crystallize and form "hot ice".

#### Life science experiments

- **Calm in the storm**: You can build a tornado tube to show how air pressure affects the weather. You need two plastic bottles, water, food coloring, and duct tape. Fill one bottle with water and add some food coloring. Then, tape the necks of the bottles together and flip them over. Swirl the bottles to create a vortex and watch the water flow from one bottle to another.
- **Walking water**: You can make water "walk" from one jar to another by using paper towels and food coloring. You need six small glass jars, water, and three different colors of food coloring. Fill three jars with water and add a different color to each one. Then, place an empty jar between each pair of colored jars. Fold a paper towel and place one end in a colored jar and the other end in an empty jar. Repeat this for all the jars and wait for the water to travel through the paper towels and mix in the empty jars.
- **Milk art**: You can create colorful patterns on milk by using dish soap and food coloring. You need a shallow plate, milk, food coloring, and dish soap. Pour some milk on the plate and add a few drops of different colors of food coloring. Then, dip a cotton swab in dish soap and touch it to the surface of the milk. Watch how the soap breaks the surface tension of the milk and causes the colors to move and mix.
- **Exploding baggies**: You can make a baggie explode by using vinegar and baking soda. You need a plastic zip-top bag, white vinegar, baking soda, and a tissue. Pour some vinegar into the bag and seal it. Then, wrap some baking soda in a tissue and open the bag slightly to insert the tissue. Quickly seal the bag again and shake it. The vinegar and baking soda will react and produce gas that will inflate and burst the bag.
- **Snow fluff**: You can make fake snow by using shaving cream and cornstarch. You need a large bowl, shaving cream, and cornstarch. Pour some shaving cream into the bowl and add some cornstarch. Mix them together with your hands until you get a fluffy and moldable texture. You can also add some glitter or food coloring to make it more fun.
