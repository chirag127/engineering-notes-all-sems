##### 9. To determine the coefficient of viscosity of a given liquid.

- The coefficient of viscosity of a liquid is a measure of its resistance to flow.
- One method to determine the coefficient of viscosity of a liquid is to use a **Stokes' apparatus**, which consists of a glass tube filled with the liquid and a small spherical ball that can be dropped through the tube.
- The procedure is as follows:
  - Measure the mass and diameter of the ball using a balance and a screw gauge respectively.
  - Measure the diameter of the glass tube using a screw gauge.
  - Fill the glass tube with the liquid and clamp it vertically.
  - Mark two points on the tube, A and B, such that the distance between them is about 10 cm.
  - Release the ball from the top of the tube and start a stopwatch when it crosses point A.
  - Stop the stopwatch when the ball crosses point B and note the time taken, t.
  - Repeat the experiment for at least five times and take the average time, t̅.
- The coefficient of viscosity of the liquid, η, can be calculated using **Stokes' law**, which states that the viscous force, F, acting on a spherical body moving through a fluid is given by:

  F = 6πηrv

  where r is the radius of the sphere, v is its terminal velocity, and η is the coefficient of viscosity of the fluid.

- To apply Stokes' law, we need to find the terminal velocity of the ball, v, which is the constant velocity attained by the ball when the viscous force is balanced by the net force of gravity and buoyancy. This can be done by using the following formula:

  v = (2/9)gr^2(ρ - σ)/η

  where g is the acceleration due to gravity, ρ is the density of the ball, and σ is the density of the liquid.

- Rearranging the formula, we get:

  η = (2/9)gr^2(ρ - σ)t̅/d

  where d is the distance between points A and B.

- The coefficient of viscosity of the liquid can be obtained by substituting the values of r, ρ, σ, g, t̅, and d in the formula and using the appropriate units. The unit of viscosity is N s/m^2 or Pa s.
- The sources of error and precautions in this experiment are:
  - The ball should be smooth and spherical to minimize the effect of surface irregularities on the viscous force.
  - The liquid should be free of air bubbles and impurities to ensure uniform viscosity throughout the tube.
  - The temperature of the liquid should be constant and known, as viscosity varies with temperature.
  - The ball should be released gently without imparting any initial velocity or spin to it.
  - The stopwatch should be started and stopped accurately when the ball crosses the marked points.
