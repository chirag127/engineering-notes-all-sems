#### CO-1 Apply the principle of interference and diffraction to find the wavelength of

- Interference is the phenomenon of superposition of two or more waves, resulting in a new wave pattern. Interference can be constructive (when the waves add up) or destructive (when the waves cancel out).
- Diffraction is the phenomenon of bending of waves around obstacles or apertures, resulting in a new wave pattern. Diffraction depends on the size of the obstacle or aperture relative to the wavelength of the wave.
- The principle of interference and diffraction can be used to find the wavelength of a wave source, such as light or sound, by using a device called a diffraction grating. A diffraction grating is a surface with a large number of equally spaced parallel slits, which act as sources of secondary waves.
- When a wave of unknown wavelength passes through a diffraction grating, it produces an interference pattern on a screen or a detector. The interference pattern consists of bright and dark fringes, corresponding to constructive and destructive interference, respectively.
- The position and spacing of the bright fringes depend on the wavelength of the wave, the distance between the slits, and the distance between the grating and the screen. By measuring these quantities, the wavelength of the wave can be calculated using the following formula:

![formula](https://latex.codecogs.com/png.latex?d%5Csin%5Ctheta%20%3D%20n%5Clambda)

where d is the distance between the slits, θ is the angle of the bright fringe from the central axis, n is the order of the fringe (an integer), and λ is the wavelength of the wave.

- This formula is derived from the condition for constructive interference, which states that the path difference between two adjacent slits must be an integer multiple of the wavelength for the waves to add up. The path difference can be expressed as d sin θ, where d is the distance between the slits and θ is the angle of the wave from the central axis. The integer multiple of the wavelength is denoted by nλ, where n is the order of the fringe. Equating these two expressions gives the formula for the wavelength.

- Example: A laser beam of unknown wavelength passes through a diffraction grating with 5000 slits per centimeter. The grating is placed 1 meter away from a screen, where an interference pattern is observed. The first bright fringe (n = 1) is found at an angle of 0.1 degrees from the central axis. What is the wavelength of the laser beam?

- Solution: The distance between the slits can be found by dividing 1 cm by the number of slits per centimeter, which gives d = 2 x 10^-4 cm. The angle of the first bright fringe can be converted to radians by multiplying by π/180, which gives θ = 0.0017 radians. Substituting these values into the formula for the wavelength, we get:

- Therefore, the wavelength of the laser beam is 3.4 x 10^-7 cm, or 340 nanometers. This is in the ultraviolet range of the electromagnetic spectrum.
