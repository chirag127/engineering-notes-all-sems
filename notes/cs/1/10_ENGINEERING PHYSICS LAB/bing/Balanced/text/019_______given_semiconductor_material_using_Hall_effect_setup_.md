##### Given semiconductor material using Hall effect setup

- Hall effect is a phenomenon that occurs when a magnetic field is applied perpendicular to the direction of current flow in a conductor or a semiconductor.
- Hall effect can be used to determine the type, concentration and mobility of charge carriers in a given semiconductor material.
- Hall effect setup consists of a rectangular sample of the semiconductor material, a constant current source, a voltmeter and a magnet.
- The sample is connected to the current source and the voltmeter is connected across its width. The magnet is placed such that its magnetic field is perpendicular to the plane of the sample and the current flow.
- When the current flows through the sample, the charge carriers experience a Lorentz force due to the magnetic field, which deflects them towards one side of the sample. This creates a potential difference across the width of the sample, which is measured by the voltmeter. This potential difference is called the Hall voltage.
- The Hall voltage is proportional to the product of the current, the magnetic field and the Hall coefficient, which depends on the type, concentration and mobility of the charge carriers in the sample.
- The Hall coefficient can be positive or negative, depending on the sign of the charge carriers. A positive Hall coefficient indicates that the charge carriers are positive (holes) and a negative Hall coefficient indicates that they are negative (electrons).
- The Hall coefficient can be calculated by the formula:

$$R_H = \frac{V_H}{I B t}$$

where $R_H$ is the Hall coefficient, $V_H$ is the Hall voltage, $I$ is the current, $B$ is the magnetic field and $t$ is the thickness of the sample.

- The concentration of the charge carriers can be obtained by the formula:

$$n = \frac{1}{R_H e}$$

where $n$ is the concentration, $R_H$ is the Hall coefficient and $e$ is the elementary charge.

- The mobility of the charge carriers can be obtained by the formula:

$$\mu = \frac{R_H \sigma}{e}$$

where $\mu$ is the mobility, $R_H$ is the Hall coefficient, $\sigma$ is the conductivity and $e$ is the elementary charge.
