##### Combination of Lenses

- A combination of lenses is a system of two or more lenses that are placed close to each other, either in contact or separated by a small distance.
- The combined focal length of a combination of lenses is the distance from the first lens to the final image formed by the system.
- The combined focal length depends on the focal lengths of the individual lenses and the distance between them.
- If the lenses are in contact, the combined focal length is given by the formula :

    `1/f = 1/f1 + 1/f2 + ... + 1/fn`

    where f is the combined focal length, and f1, f2, ..., fn are the focal lengths of the individual lenses.

- If the lenses are separated by some distance d, the combined focal length is given by the formula :

    `1/f = 1/f1 + 1/f2 + ... + 1/fn - d/f1f2`

    where d is the distance between the first and the second lens, and f1 and f2 are the focal lengths of the first and the second lens, respectively. This formula can be extended to more than two lenses by using the effective focal length of the first two lenses as the new f1, and the distance between the second and the third lens as the new d, and so on.

- A combination of lenses can be used to obtain different types of images, such as magnified, inverted, or erect, depending on the arrangement and nature of the lenses. Some applications of combination of lenses are telescopes, microscopes, cameras, and eyeglasses .
