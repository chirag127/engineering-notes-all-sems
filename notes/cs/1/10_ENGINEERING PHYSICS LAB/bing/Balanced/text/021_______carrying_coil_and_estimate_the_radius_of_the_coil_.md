##### Carrying coil and estimate the radius of the coil

- A carrying coil is a loop or coil of wire that carries an electric current.
- The current in the coil produces a magnetic field around it, which can be calculated using Ampere's law or the Biot-Savart law.
- The magnetic field inside the coil is uniform and parallel to the axis of the coil, and its magnitude depends on the number of turns, the current, and the radius of the coil.
- The magnetic field outside the coil is similar to that of a bar magnet, with two opposite poles at the ends of the coil.
- To estimate the radius of the coil, one can use a compass needle to measure the direction of the magnetic field at different points along the axis of the coil.
- The radius of the coil is equal to the distance from the center of the coil to the point where the compass needle is perpendicular to the axis of the coil.
- Alternatively, one can use a Hall probe to measure the magnetic flux density at different points along the axis of the coil, and then use the formula B = μ₀NI/2R, where B is the magnetic flux density, μ₀ is the permeability of free space, N is the number of turns, I is the current, and R is the radius of the coil.
