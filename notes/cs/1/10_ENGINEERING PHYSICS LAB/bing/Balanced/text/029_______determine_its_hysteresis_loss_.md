##### Determine its hysteresis loss.

- Hysteresis loss is the energy dissipated as heat in a magnetic material when it is subjected to a changing magnetic field.
- Hysteresis loss depends on the area of the hysteresis loop of the material, which is a plot of the magnetic flux density (B) versus the magnetic field intensity (H).
- The area of the hysteresis loop is proportional to the energy required to magnetize and demagnetize the material in one cycle of the alternating magnetic field.
- To determine the hysteresis loss of a material, the following steps can be followed:

  1. Measure the B-H curve of the material using a magnetometer or a hysteresis graph.
  2. Calculate the area of the hysteresis loop by integrating the B-H curve over one cycle, or by using a planimeter or a computer software.
  3. Multiply the area of the hysteresis loop by the frequency of the alternating magnetic field and the volume of the material to get the hysteresis loss per unit time.
  4. Divide the hysteresis loss per unit time by the mass of the material to get the hysteresis loss per unit mass.

- Alternatively, the hysteresis loss of a material can be estimated using the empirical formula:

  - Hysteresis loss = k * f * B<sub>max</sub><sup>n</sup> * m
  - Where k is a constant that depends on the material, f is the frequency of the alternating magnetic field, B<sub>max</sub> is the maximum value of the magnetic flux density, n is the Steinmetz exponent that ranges from 1.5 to 2.5, and m is the mass of the material.
