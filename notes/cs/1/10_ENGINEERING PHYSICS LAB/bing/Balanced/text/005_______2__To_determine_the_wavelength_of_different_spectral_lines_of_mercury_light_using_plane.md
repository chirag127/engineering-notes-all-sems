##### 2. To determine the wavelength of different spectral lines of mercury light using plane transmission grating.

- The aim of this experiment is to measure the wavelength of different spectral lines of mercury light using a plane transmission grating and a spectrometer.
- The principle of this experiment is based on the diffraction of light by a grating, which is a periodic array of narrow slits. The grating acts as a source of multiple coherent beams of light, which interfere constructively or destructively depending on the angle of observation and the wavelength of light. The condition for constructive interference is given by the grating equation:

$$d(\sin\theta_m - \sin\theta_i) = m\lambda$$

where $d$ is the grating constant (the distance between adjacent slits), $\theta_i$ is the angle of incidence of light on the grating, $\theta_m$ is the angle of diffraction of the $m$th order maximum, $\lambda$ is the wavelength of light, and $m$ is an integer.

- The apparatus required for this experiment are:

  - A mercury lamp as the source of light, which emits several spectral lines of different wavelengths in the visible and ultraviolet regions.
  - A plane transmission grating mounted on a rotatable table, which can be adjusted to make the incident light perpendicular to the grating surface.
  - A spectrometer, which consists of a collimator, a telescope, and a vernier scale. The collimator is used to produce a parallel beam of light from the mercury lamp, which falls on the grating. The telescope is used to observe the diffracted beams of light at different angles. The vernier scale is used to measure the angular positions of the telescope with respect to the grating.
  - A prism, which is used to disperse the light from the mercury lamp into its constituent spectral lines, which can be identified by their colors and positions on a scale.

- The procedure of this experiment is as follows:

  - Set up the apparatus as shown in the figure below:

  ```
  |                 |     |                 |     |                 |
  |    Mercury      |     |    Collimator   |     |    Grating      |
  |     lamp        |     |                 |     |                 |
  |                 |     |                 |     |                 |
  |-----------------|     |-----------------|     |-----------------|
  
                           |-----------------|
                           |                 |
                           |    Telescope    |
                           |                 |
                           |-----------------|
  ```

  - Adjust the collimator and the telescope to make them horizontal and parallel to each other. Adjust the grating table to make it perpendicular to the collimator axis. Adjust the focus of the telescope and the collimator to obtain clear images of the grating and the source respectively.
  - Place the prism in front of the mercury lamp and observe the spectrum of the mercury light through the telescope. Identify the spectral lines by their colors and positions on the scale. Note down the scale readings of the spectral lines.
  - Remove the prism and rotate the grating table slightly to make the incident light oblique to the grating surface. Observe the diffracted beams of light through the telescope. Adjust the telescope to bring the zeroth order maximum (the undiffracted beam) to the center of the cross-wire. Note down the vernier readings of the telescope and the grating table.
  - Rotate the telescope to observe the first order maxima on both sides of the zeroth order maximum. Identify the spectral lines by their colors and note down the vernier readings of the telescope for each line. Repeat the same for the second order maxima if visible.
  - Calculate the angle of incidence $\theta_i$ and the angle of diffraction $\theta_m$ for each spectral line using the vernier readings and the geometry of the apparatus.
