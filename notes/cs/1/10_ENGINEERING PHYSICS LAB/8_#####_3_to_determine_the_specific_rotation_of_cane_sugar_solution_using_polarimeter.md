3. Determining the Specific Rotation of Cane Sugar Solution using a Polarimeter:

A polarimeter is an instrument used to measure the rotation of plane-polarized light caused by the interaction of light with optically active substances. The specific rotation of a substance is a measure of its optical activity and is defined as the angle of rotation of plane-polarized light caused by a solution of the substance.

To determine the specific rotation of a cane sugar solution using a polarimeter, the following steps can be followed:

1. Prepare the cane sugar solution: Dissolve a known amount of cane sugar in a solvent, such as water, to create a solution. The concentration of the solution should be within the range of the polarimeter's measurement capabilities.

2. Calibrate the polarimeter: Calibrate the polarimeter using a standard solution of known specific rotation. This will ensure that the polarimeter is accurately measuring the rotation of plane-polarized light.

3. Measure the rotation of the cane sugar solution: Place the cane sugar solution in the polarimeter and measure the angle of rotation of plane-polarized light. The angle of rotation is directly proportional to the specific rotation of the cane sugar solution.

4. Calculate the specific rotation: Use the angle of rotation and the concentration of the cane sugar solution to calculate the specific rotation. The formula for specific rotation is: [α] = [θ] / l, where [α] is the specific rotation, [θ] is the angle of rotation, and l is the length of the polarimeter cell.

In conclusion, to determine the specific rotation of a cane sugar solution using a polarimeter, a solution of the substance is prepared, the polarimeter is calibrated, the angle of rotation is measured, and the specific rotation is calculated using the formula [α] = [θ] / l. The specific rotation of a substance is a measure of its optical activity and is an important characteristic for identifying and characterizing optically active substances.
