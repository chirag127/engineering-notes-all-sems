Determining the Wavelength of Spectral Lines of Mercury Light Using a Plane:

The wavelength of different spectral lines of mercury light can be determined using a plane diffraction grating. A plane diffraction grating is a type of optical device that splits light into its component wavelengths.

1. Procedure: To determine the wavelength of a spectral line of mercury light using a plane diffraction grating, the following steps can be taken:

- Obtain a plane diffraction grating and a mercury light source.

- Place the diffraction grating in front of the mercury light source and observe the resulting diffraction pattern.

- Measure the angles between the central maximum and the first-order maxima on either side of the central maximum.

- Use the grating equation, nλ = d(sinθi - sinθr), to calculate the wavelength of the spectral line. In this equation, n is the order of the spectral line, λ is the wavelength, d is the grating spacing, θi is the angle of incidence, and θr is the angle of reflection.

2. Importance: Determining the wavelength of spectral lines of mercury light is important for several reasons. Spectral lines can be used to identify elements and to determine their chemical composition. They also provide information about the energy levels of atoms and the interactions between atoms and light.

In conclusion, the wavelength of different spectral lines of mercury light can be determined using a plane diffraction grating. This involves observing the diffraction pattern produced by the grating and using the grating equation to calculate the wavelength of the spectral line.
