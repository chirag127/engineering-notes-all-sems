##### Overview:

In this experiment, we use Newton's rings to determine the wavelength of sodium light. Newton's rings are a series of concentric rings formed by the interference of light waves. By measuring the diameter of the rings, we can determine the wavelength of the light.

##### Materials Required:

- Sodium lamp
- Plano-convex lens
- Glass plate
- Microscope
- Sodium light filter
- Vernier calipers
- Optical bench

##### Procedure:

1. Set up the optical bench and place the sodium lamp and plano-convex lens on it.
2. Place the glass plate on the plano-convex lens.
3. Adjust the position of the glass plate so that it is in contact with the plano-convex lens.
4. Place the sodium light filter in front of the microscope to filter out all light except for the sodium light.
5. Look through the microscope and adjust the focus so that the Newton's rings are visible.
6. Measure the diameter of the central dark spot using the vernier calipers.
7. Measure the diameters of several other rings and record the values.
8. Calculate the radius of each ring using the formula r = (mλR) / (2t), where m is the order of the ring, λ is the wavelength of the light, R is the radius of curvature of the plano-convex lens, and t is the thickness of the glass plate.
9. Plot a graph of r^2 vs m.
10. Determine the slope of the graph and use it to calculate the wavelength of the sodium light.

##### Precautions:

- Care should be taken while handling the sodium lamp as it is fragile.
- The microscope should be properly focused to get accurate readings.
- The vernier calipers should be used carefully to avoid any errors in measurement.
- The glass plate and plano-convex lens should be properly cleaned before the experiment to avoid any interference.

##### Conclusion:

By using the Newton's ring experiment, we can accurately determine the wavelength of sodium light. This experiment is important in the field of optics and is widely used in research and development.
