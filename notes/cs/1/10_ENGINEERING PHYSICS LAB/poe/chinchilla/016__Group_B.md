#### Group B

Group B is a category of rally racing that was popular in the 1980s. It was known for its high-powered cars, advanced technology, and dangerous racing conditions. Here are some key points to know about Group B:

- Group B was introduced in 1982 as a replacement for Group 4, which was the previous top-level rally class.
- Group B cars were required to have a minimum weight of 900kg and a maximum engine displacement of 3.0L for naturally aspirated engines or 1.4L for turbocharged engines.
- Group B cars were allowed to use advanced technology, such as four-wheel drive, turbocharging, and aerodynamic bodywork.
- Group B cars were capable of producing over 500 horsepower, which was a significant increase from previous rally cars.
- Group B rallies were known for their dangerous conditions, with narrow roads, high speeds, and spectators standing close to the course.
- The high speeds and dangerous conditions of Group B rallies led to several fatal accidents, which ultimately led to the category being banned after the 1986 season.
- Despite its short lifespan, Group B remains a popular and iconic era of rally racing, with many fans and collectors still following and restoring the cars today.

Overall, Group B was a revolutionary category of rally racing that pushed the boundaries of technology and performance, but also had a tragic legacy due to its dangerous conditions.
