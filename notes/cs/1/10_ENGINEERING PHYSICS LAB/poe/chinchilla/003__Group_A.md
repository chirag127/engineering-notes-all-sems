#### Group A

Group A refers to a group of substances that have been classified by the International Agency for Research on Cancer (IARC) as carcinogenic to humans. These substances have been shown to cause cancer in humans through various studies and research.

Here are some key points to know about Group A:

- Group A includes substances that are known to cause cancer in humans.
- These substances have been classified as Group A based on sufficient evidence from studies and research.
- Some examples of Group A substances include tobacco smoke, asbestos, and ultraviolet (UV) radiation from the sun.
- Exposure to Group A substances can increase the risk of developing cancer.
- The risk of developing cancer from exposure to Group A substances depends on various factors such as the duration and intensity of exposure, as well as individual factors such as genetics and lifestyle.
- It is important to take precautions to minimize exposure to Group A substances in order to reduce the risk of developing cancer.
- Prevention and early detection are key in reducing the impact of cancer on individuals and society.

In summary, Group A substances are known to cause cancer in humans and it is important to take steps to minimize exposure to these substances in order to reduce the risk of developing cancer.
