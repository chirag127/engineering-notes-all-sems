##### 8. To Calibrate the Given Ammeter and Voltmeter by Potentiometer

Calibration is the process of adjusting and verifying the accuracy of an instrument. It is an essential step in ensuring the reliability and precision of measurements. In this experiment, we will learn how to calibrate the given ammeter and voltmeter by using a potentiometer. Here are the steps to follow:

1. Connect the circuit: Connect the circuit as shown in the diagram below. The potentiometer should be connected in series with the ammeter and the voltmeter should be connected in parallel with the circuit.

2. Adjust the potentiometer: Adjust the potentiometer until the voltmeter reads the desired voltage. This voltage should be equal to the voltage across the resistor R1.

3. Measure the current: Measure the current flowing through the circuit using the ammeter. Record this value as I1.

4. Adjust the potentiometer again: Adjust the potentiometer until the voltmeter reads zero.

5. Measure the current again: Measure the current flowing through the circuit using the ammeter. Record this value as I2.

6. Calculate the calibration constant: The calibration constant is given by K = I1 / (I1 - I2).

7. Calibrate the ammeter: To calibrate the ammeter, multiply the readings obtained from the ammeter by the calibration constant, K.

8. Calibrate the voltmeter: To calibrate the voltmeter, divide the readings obtained from the voltmeter by the calibration constant, K.

9. Verify the calibration: Verify the calibration of the ammeter and voltmeter by measuring the current and voltage in a known circuit.

Note: It is important to ensure that the potentiometer is of high quality and has a low temperature coefficient. This will ensure that the calibration remains accurate over a wide range of temperatures.
