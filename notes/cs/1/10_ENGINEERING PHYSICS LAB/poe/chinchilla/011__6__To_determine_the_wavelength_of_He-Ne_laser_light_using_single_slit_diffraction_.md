##### To determine the wavelength of He-Ne laser light using single slit diffraction.

In this experiment, we will be using the phenomenon of single slit diffraction to determine the wavelength of He-Ne laser light. The basic principle behind this experiment is that when a light wave passes through a narrow slit, it diffracts and produces a pattern of light and dark fringes.

To conduct this experiment, we will need the following materials:

- He-Ne laser
- Single slit apparatus
- Ruler or micrometer
- White screen
- Power supply for the laser

Here's how to conduct the experiment:

1. Set up the single slit apparatus by fixing the slit in front of the laser beam. The slit should be very narrow, preferably around 0.1 mm in width.

2. Place the white screen a few meters away from the slit. The screen should be perpendicular to the laser beam and parallel to the slit.

3. Turn on the laser and adjust the slit until the laser beam passes through it.

4. Observe the pattern of light and dark fringes on the white screen. The central bright fringe should be wider than the other fringes, and the intensity of the fringes decreases as we move away from the center.

5. Measure the distance between the central bright fringe and the first dark fringe on either side using a ruler or a micrometer. Let this distance be denoted by 'd'.

6. Measure the distance between the slit and the screen. Let this distance be denoted by 'D'.

7. Calculate the wavelength of the laser light using the formula:

   λ = (d/D) * (2a)

   where λ is the wavelength of the laser light, 'a' is the width of the slit, and 'd/D' is the distance between the central bright fringe and the first dark fringe on either side.

   Note that the constant factor '2' is included in the formula because the distance between the central bright fringe and the first dark fringe on either side is equal to half the width of the central bright fringe.

8. Repeat the experiment several times and take an average of the values obtained. This will help to reduce errors and improve the accuracy of the measurement.

In conclusion, the single slit diffraction experiment can be used to determine the wavelength of He-Ne laser light. By measuring the distance between the central bright fringe and the first dark fringe on either side, and using the formula described above, we can calculate the wavelength of the laser light with reasonable accuracy.
