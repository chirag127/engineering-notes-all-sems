#### 4. To verify Stefan’s law by electric method.

Stefan's law is a fundamental principle in physics that relates the total amount of energy radiated by a blackbody to its temperature. This law states that the energy radiated per unit surface area per unit time is proportional to the fourth power of the absolute temperature.

To verify Stefan's law by electric method, we can follow the following steps:

1. Set up a blackbody radiator: A blackbody radiator is a device that emits radiation uniformly in all directions. It is made up of a hollow chamber with a small hole, which allows the radiation to escape. The walls of the chamber are made of a material that absorbs all the radiation that falls on it. The blackbody radiator should be connected to a voltage source.

2. Measure the temperature of the blackbody radiator: To measure the temperature of the blackbody radiator, we can use a thermometer or a thermocouple. The temperature should be measured at the center of the radiator.

3. Measure the amount of radiation emitted: To measure the amount of radiation emitted, we can use a radiometer. A radiometer is a device that measures the intensity of radiation. The radiometer should be placed at a fixed distance from the blackbody radiator.

4. Vary the voltage: The voltage applied to the blackbody radiator should be varied in small increments. For each voltage, the temperature and the amount of radiation emitted should be measured.

5. Plot a graph: The data collected should be plotted on a graph with the amount of radiation emitted on the y-axis and the temperature on the x-axis. The graph should be a straight line passing through the origin.

6. Verify Stefan's law: The slope of the graph should be proportional to the fourth power of the absolute temperature. This verifies Stefan's law.

In conclusion, by following the above steps, we can verify Stefan's law by electric method. This principle is important in understanding the behavior of blackbodies and is applicable in various fields of physics, such as thermodynamics and astrophysics.
