#### Monochromatic and Polychromatic Light

- Monochromatic light refers to light of a single wavelength or color. A laser produces monochromatic light.
- Polychromatic light refers to light containing multiple wavelengths or colors. Sunlight and light from regular bulbs are polychromatic as they contain all the colors of the visible light spectrum.
- Monochromatic light is often used in scientific applications to isolate the effect of a single wavelength. For example, monochromatic red light can be used to only excite electrons in a material that interact with red light.
- Polychromatic light contains more energy as it includes all wavelengths, but it can be more difficult to analyze the effect of specific wavelengths. Color filters are often used to produce quasi-monochromatic light from polychromatic sources for analysis.
- In optics, the differences between monochromatic and polychromatic light must be accounted for when analyzing light-matter interactions and designing components/systems. The full spectrum of wavelengths in polychromatic light must be considered rather than just a single wavelength as in monochromatic light.
