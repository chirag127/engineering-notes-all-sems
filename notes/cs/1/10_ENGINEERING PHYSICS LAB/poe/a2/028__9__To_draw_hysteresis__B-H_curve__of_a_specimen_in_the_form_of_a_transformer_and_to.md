##### 9. To draw hysteresis (B-H curve) of a specimen in the form of a transformer and to

1. Take a ferromagnetic specimen (eg. iron nail) and wind insulated copper wire around it to make a coil. Connect the coil to a dc voltage source and milli-ammeter.

2. Note down the magnetic field (B) and magnetization (M) values at different current values. B is directly proportional to the current and M is proportional to the flux density.

3. Plot a graph between B and M. The loop formed is the hysteresis loop or B-H curve of the material. The area of the loop gives the energy loss per cycle.

4. Observe the changes in the shape of the loop when the magnitude of the maximum current is changed. The width of the loop increases with increase in maximum current. This shows the hysteresis loss increases with increasing current.

5. The B-H curve can be approximated to a rectangular loop for low values of M and to a narrow loop for high values of M, thus indicating low and high permeability regions respectively in a ferromagnetic material.

The above points cover the key steps and observations to draw and analyze the hysteresis loop or B-H curve of a ferromagnetic specimen.
