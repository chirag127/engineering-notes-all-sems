
# Determine Its Hysteresis Loss

Hysteresis loss is a form of energy loss that occurs in a material when it is subjected to an alternating magnetic field. This type of energy loss is caused by the material's magnetic properties, which cause it to become magnetically saturated and unable to store any additional energy.

The hysteresis loss can be determined by measuring the energy required to magnetize the material and the energy dissipated during the demagnetization process.

1. The energy required to magnetize the material is known as the coercive force, or the amount of energy needed to bring the material to its saturation point.

2. The energy dissipated during the demagnetization process is known as the hysteresis loss, or the energy lost during the transition from saturation to zero magnetization.

3. The hysteresis loss can be calculated by subtracting the energy required to magnetize the material from the energy dissipated during the demagnetization process.

4. The hysteresis loss can be reduced by using a material with a lower coercive force, or by using a material with a higher permeability.
