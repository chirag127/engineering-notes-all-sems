##### 2. To study Hall effect and determine Hall coefficient, carrier density and mobility of a

Hall effect is an electrical phenomenon where a voltage is induced perpendicular to an electric current in a magnetic field. To study Hall effect and determine Hall coefficient, carrier density and mobility, the following steps are typically followed:

1. Prepare a sample of the material to be tested, typically a thin metal or semiconductor film.
2. Apply a magnetic field to the sample, either by using an electromagnet or by placing the sample in a magnetic field.
3. Apply a direct current (DC) to the sample and measure the voltage across it.
4. Repeat the measurement at different magnetic field strengths and record the results.
5. Plot the Hall voltage as a function of magnetic field strength and fit the data to a theoretical model.
6. Extract the Hall coefficient, carrier density, and mobility from the fit.
7. Repeat the measurements at different temperatures to study temperature dependence of Hall effect.

The Hall coefficient is a measure of the material's response to the magnetic field and is proportional to the carrier density and inversely proportional to the mobility. Carrier density is the number of charge carriers in the material and mobility is a measure of how easily the carriers can move through the material. Understanding the Hall effect and determining these parameters is important for characterizing the electrical and magnetic properties of materials and for developing new technologies.
