### Change of variables for the notes of the Unit 4 - Multiple integration in the subject of ENGINEERING MATHEMATICS-I

Change of variables for multiple integrals is a technique used to evaluate integrals by transforming the integration limits. The goal is to simplify the integrand or to change the region of integration to a simpler shape.

The general form of change of variables is given by:

∫f(x)dx = ∫g(y)dy, where x = g(y)

The transformation function g(y) maps the original interval [a, b] in x to a new interval [c, d] in y. The Jacobian of the transformation is used to convert the integrand from x to y.

There are two common methods for performing change of variables: polar coordinates and cylindrical coordinates.

Polar coordinates:

In polar coordinates, the transformation is given by:

x = rcosθ
y = rsinθ

where r is the radial distance from the origin and θ is the angle between the positive x-axis and the line connecting the origin to the point (x, y).

Cylindrical coordinates:

In cylindrical coordinates, the transformation is given by:

x = rcossθ
y = rsinsθ
z = z

where r is the radial distance from the z-axis and θ is the angle between the positive x-axis and the line connecting the origin to the point (x, y).

In both cases, the Jacobian of the transformation is given by:

J = r

It is important to choose the appropriate coordinate system for the region of integration and to use the correct Jacobian when performing the change of variables.
