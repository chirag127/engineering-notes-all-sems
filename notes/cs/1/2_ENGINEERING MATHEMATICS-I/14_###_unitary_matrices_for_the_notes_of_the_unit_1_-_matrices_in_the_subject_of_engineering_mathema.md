### Unitary Matrices for the notes of the Unit 1 - Matrices in the subject of ENGINEERING MATHEMATICS-I

A Unitary matrix is a square matrix whose conjugate transpose (also called Hermitian transpose) is equal to its inverse. This means that the matrix preserves the inner product of vectors and has determinant 1. Unitary matrices are important in many areas of engineering, including control theory, quantum mechanics, and signal processing. They are also used in numerical linear algebra to solve linear systems and to diagonalize matrices. Some properties of unitary matrices include:

1. Orthogonality: Unitary matrices preserve the orthogonality of vectors, meaning that the dot product of two vectors is zero if and only if the dot product of their images under the matrix is also zero.

2. Normalization: Unitary matrices preserve the norm of vectors, meaning that the length of a vector is unchanged after being multiplied by a unitary matrix.

3. Invariance: Unitary matrices preserve the eigenvalues of a matrix, meaning that the eigenvalues of a matrix are unchanged after being multiplied by a unitary matrix.

4. Uniqueness: If a matrix is unitary, then it is unique up to a complex phase factor.

Unitary matrices are also related to unitary transformations, which are linear transformations that preserve the inner product of vectors. These transformations are important in many areas of engineering, including control theory, quantum mechanics, and signal processing.
