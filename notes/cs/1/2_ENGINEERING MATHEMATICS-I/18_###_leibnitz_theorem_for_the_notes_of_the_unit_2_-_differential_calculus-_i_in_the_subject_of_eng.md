### Leibnitz theorem for the notes of the Unit 2 - Differential Calculus- I in the subject of ENGINEERING MATHEMATICS-I

Leibnitz Theorem, also known as the Second Fundamental Theorem of Calculus, states that if f(x) is a continuous function on [a, b], and F(x) is its antiderivative, then:

d/dx [F(b) - F(a)] = f(b) - f(a)

This theorem provides a connection between the derivative and the definite integral. It states that the derivative of the definite integral of a function over an interval [a, b] is equal to the value of the function at the endpoints of the interval.

This theorem is useful for solving problems in physics, engineering, and other fields where the relationship between the rate of change of a quantity and its accumulated value is important.

Note: Make sure to understand the concept of antiderivative and definite integral before studying Leibnitz theorem.
