## Unit 4 - Multiple integration

Multiple integration is a branch of calculus that deals with finding the volume of a 3D region or the area of a surface that is defined by more than one equation. It is a natural extension of single variable integration. The concept of multiple integration is used in various fields such as physics, engineering, and economics.

In multiple integration, the region of integration is defined by two or more equations in terms of two or more variables. The most common type of multiple integration is double integration, where the region of integration is defined by two equations in two variables. The goal is to find the volume of the region that is enclosed by these two equations.

The process of multiple integration involves iteratively integrating a function with respect to each variable, starting from the innermost variable and proceeding outward. The result of each integration is used as the integrand for the next integration. The final result is the volume of the region defined by the equations.

The most common methods of multiple integration are the rectangular coordinate system, polar coordinate system, and cylindrical coordinate system. In each of these systems, the region of integration is defined in a different way, and the methods of integration vary accordingly.

In conclusion, multiple integration is a powerful tool for finding the volume or area of a region defined by more than one equation. It is widely used in various fields and has numerous applications.
