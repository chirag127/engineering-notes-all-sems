### Expansion of functions by Taylor’s and Maclaurin’s theorems for functions of one and two variables for the notes of the Unit 3 - Differential Calculus-II in the subject of ENGINEERING MATHEMATICS-I

Taylor's Theorem states that any smooth function can be approximated by a polynomial in a neighborhood of a point. In particular, if f(x) is a function and x0 is a point, then there exists a polynomial P(x) such that:

f(x) = P(x) + Rn(x)

where Rn(x) is the remainder term and can be estimated using Lagrange's form of the remainder.

Maclaurin's Theorem is a special case of Taylor's Theorem where x0 = 0. In other words, it states that any smooth function can be approximated by its Taylor series expansion about 0.

For functions of one variable, Taylor's and Maclaurin's theorems provide a way to approximate a function by a polynomial, which can be easier to work with and understand.

For functions of two variables, the idea is similar, but the polynomial is replaced by a multivariate polynomial. In this case, Taylor's theorem states that if f(x, y) is a function and (x0, y0) is a point, then there exists a multivariate polynomial P(x, y) such that:

f(x, y) = P(x, y) + Rn(x, y)

where Rn(x, y) is the remainder term and can be estimated using Lagrange's form of the remainder.

These theorems are useful in engineering and physics for approximating complex functions and solving problems involving derivatives and integrals.
