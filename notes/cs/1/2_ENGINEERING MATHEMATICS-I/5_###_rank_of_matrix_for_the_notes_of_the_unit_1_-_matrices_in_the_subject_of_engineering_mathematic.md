Rank of a Matrix:

The rank of a matrix is a measure of its linear independence, which is the number of linearly independent rows or columns in a matrix. The rank of a matrix is also known as the dimension of the row space or column space of the matrix.

1. Row Rank: The row rank of a matrix is the number of linearly independent rows in the matrix.

2. Column Rank: The column rank of a matrix is the number of linearly independent columns in the matrix.

3. Rank Theorem: The rank theorem states that the row rank and the column rank of a matrix are equal, and the rank of a matrix is equal to the number of non-zero rows in its row echelon form or the number of non-zero pivots in its reduced row echelon form.

Applications of Rank of a Matrix:

1. Solving Systems of Linear Equations: The rank of a matrix is used to determine the number of unknowns in a system of linear equations, and to determine the solvability of the system.

2. Determining the Dimension of a Vector Space: The rank of a matrix is used to determine the dimension of a vector space, which is the number of linearly independent vectors in the space.

3. Inverse of a Matrix: The rank of a matrix is used to determine the existence and uniqueness of the inverse of a matrix. If the rank of a matrix is equal to its number of rows or columns, the matrix is invertible.

In conclusion, the rank of a matrix is a measure of its linear independence, and the row rank and the column rank of a matrix are equal. The rank of a matrix is used in various applications, including solving systems of linear equations, determining the dimension of a vector space, and determining the inverse of a matrix.
