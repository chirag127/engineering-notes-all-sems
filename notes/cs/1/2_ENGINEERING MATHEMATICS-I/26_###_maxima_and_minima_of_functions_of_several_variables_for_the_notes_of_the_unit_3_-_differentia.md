### Maxima and Minima of functions of several variables for the notes of the Unit 3 - Differential Calculus-II in the subject of ENGINEERING MATHEMATICS-I

Maxima and Minima of functions of several variables:

1. Definition: The concept of maxima and minima of a function of several variables is an extension of the concept of maxima and minima of a function of one variable. It refers to the highest and lowest values of a function respectively.

2. Critical Points: A critical point of a function of several variables is a point where the partial derivatives of the function are equal to zero or do not exist.

3. Second-Order Conditions: To determine whether a critical point is a local maximum, minimum, or saddle point, the second-order conditions must be checked.

4. Hessian Matrix: The second-order partial derivatives of a function of several variables can be represented in a matrix called the Hessian matrix. The determinant of the Hessian matrix can be used to determine the nature of the critical point.

5. Lagrange Multipliers: The method of Lagrange multipliers can be used to find the maxima and minima of a function of several variables subject to constraints.

6. Applications: Maxima and minima of functions of several variables are used in various fields such as optimization, economics, physics, and engineering.
