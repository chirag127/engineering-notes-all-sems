### Curl and Divergence and their Physical interpretation for the notes of the Unit 5 - Vector Calculus in the subject of ENGINEERING MATHEMATICS-I

Curl: The curl of a vector field is a measure of the circulation of the field around a point. It is defined as the cross product of the del operator and the vector field. In physics, curl is used to describe the circulation of fluid, the rotation of a rigid body, and the magnetic field.

Divergence: The divergence of a vector field is a measure of the net flow of the field into or out of a point. It is defined as the dot product of the del operator and the vector field. In physics, divergence is used to describe the flow of fluid, the flow of heat, and the electric flux.

Physical Interpretation:

Curl: The curl of a vector field represents the local rotational motion of the field. A non-zero curl indicates that the field is rotating around a point, whereas a zero curl indicates that the field is not rotating.

Divergence: The divergence of a vector field represents the local rate of change of the field. A positive divergence indicates that the field is flowing into a point, whereas a negative divergence indicates that the field is flowing out of a point. A zero divergence indicates that the field is neither flowing into nor out of a point.
