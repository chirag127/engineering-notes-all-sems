# Unitary Matrices

A unitary matrix is a complex square matrix that satisfies the following equation:

U^H U = U U^H = I

where U^H is the conjugate transpose of U and I is the identity matrix. This means that the inverse of a unitary matrix is its conjugate transpose.

Some examples of unitary matrices are:

U = [1/sqrt(2) 1/sqrt(2); -1/sqrt(2) 1/sqrt(2)]

V = [1/sqrt(3) 1/sqrt(3) 1/sqrt(3); -1/sqrt(6) -1/sqrt(6) 2/sqrt(6); 1/sqrt(2) -1/sqrt(2) 0]

W = [1 0 0; 0 i 0; 0 0 -i]

Some properties of unitary matrices are:

- The unitary matrix is a non-singular matrix.
- The unitary matrix is an invertible matrix.
- The product of two unitary matrices is a unitary matrix.
- The inverse of a unitary matrix is another unitary matrix.
- A matrix is unitary, if and only if its transpose is unitary.
- A matrix is unitary if its rows are orthonormal, and the columns are orthonormal.
- The determinant of a unitary matrix has absolute value 1.
- The eigenvalues of a unitary matrix have absolute value 1.
- The trace of a unitary matrix is the sum of its eigenvalues.
- The unitary matrices form a group under matrix multiplication, called the unitary group.
