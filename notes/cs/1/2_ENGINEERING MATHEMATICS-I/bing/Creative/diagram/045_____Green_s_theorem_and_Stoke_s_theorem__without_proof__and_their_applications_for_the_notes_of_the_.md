### Green's theorem and Stoke's theorem (without proof) and their applications

- Green's theorem is a special case of the Kelvin–Stokes theorem, when applied to a region in the xy-plane. It states that the line integral of a vector field around a simple closed curve is equal to the double integral of the curl of the vector field over the region enclosed by the curve.
- Green's theorem can be used to calculate the area of a plane region, the work done by a force field along a curve, the circulation and flux of a vector field, and the moments and centroids of plane regions  .
- Stoke's theorem is a generalization of Green's theorem to three dimensions. It states that the surface integral of the curl of a vector field over a smooth oriented surface is equal to the line integral of the vector field around the boundary curve of the surface.
- Stoke's theorem can be used to calculate the work done by a force field along a curve in space, the circulation and flux of a vector field over a surface, and the angular momentum and torque of a rigid body  .
