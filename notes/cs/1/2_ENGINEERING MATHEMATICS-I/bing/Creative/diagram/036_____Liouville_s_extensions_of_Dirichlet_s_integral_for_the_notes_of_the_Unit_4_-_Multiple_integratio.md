### Liouville’s extensions of Dirichlet’s integral

- Liouville’s extensions of Dirichlet’s integral are a generalization of Dirichlet’s theorem, which states that the integral of a function of the form F(x+y+z) over a tetrahedron with vertices at the origin and three points on the coordinate axes is equal to the integral of F(h) over the interval determined by the sum of the coordinates of the vertices.

- Liouville’s extensions of Dirichlet’s integral allow us to express as a simple integral a large class of multiple integrals of the form

$$\int\int\int_V x^{l-1}y^{m-1}z^{n-1}F(x,y,z)dxdydz$$

where V is a tetrahedron with vertices at the origin and three points on the coordinate axes, and l, m, n are positive constants.

- The formula for Liouville’s extensions of Dirichlet’s integral is given by

$$\int\int\int_V x^{l-1}y^{m-1}z^{n-1}F(x,y,z)dxdydz = \frac{\Gamma(l)\Gamma(m)\Gamma(n)}{\Gamma(l+m+n)}\int_{h_1}^{h_2}F(h)h^{l+m+n-1}dh$$

where h is the sum of the coordinates of any point in V, and h1 and h2 are the minimum and maximum values of h in V, respectively.

- The proof of Liouville’s extensions of Dirichlet’s integral is based on the change of variables x = hu, y = hv, z = hw, where u, v, w are the barycentric coordinates of any point in V, and applying the properties of the gamma function and the beta function.

- Liouville’s extensions of Dirichlet’s integral can be used to evaluate various triple integrals involving functions of x, y, z, or functions of x+y+z, by choosing appropriate values of l, m, n, and F .

- Liouville’s extensions of Dirichlet’s integral can also be applied to other regions that are similar to a tetrahedron, such as a pyramid or a cone, by scaling the coordinates appropriately.
