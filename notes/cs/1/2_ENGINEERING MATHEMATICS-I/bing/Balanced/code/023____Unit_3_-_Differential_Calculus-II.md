## Unit 3 - Differential Calculus-II

This unit covers the following topics:

- Applications of derivatives: optimization problems, related rates, curve sketching, linear approximation and differentials, mean value theorem, L'Hospital's rule, Newton's method.
- Indeterminate forms and improper integrals: evaluation of indeterminate forms using L'Hospital's rule, comparison test, limit comparison test, integral test, alternating series test, absolute and conditional convergence, ratio test, root test, power series, Taylor and Maclaurin series, radius and interval of convergence, term-by-term differentiation and integration of power series, Taylor's theorem with remainder.
- Partial derivatives: functions of several variables, limits and continuity, partial derivatives, chain rule, directional derivatives and gradient, tangent planes and normal lines, extrema of functions of two variables, Lagrange multipliers, double integrals, iterated integrals, double integrals in polar coordinates, applications of double integrals, triple integrals, triple integrals in cylindrical and spherical coordinates, applications of triple integrals, change of variables in multiple integrals, Jacobian.

Some key points to remember are:

- Optimization problems involve finding the maximum or minimum value of a function subject to some constraints. To solve them, we need to identify the objective function, the constraint function, and the feasible region. Then we use the method of Lagrange multipliers or the second derivative test to find the optimal solution.
- Related rates problems involve finding the rate of change of one quantity with respect to another, given the relationship between them and the rate of change of some other quantity. To solve them, we need to differentiate the given equation implicitly with respect to time and substitute the given values.
- Curve sketching involves finding the domain, intercepts, asymptotes, intervals of increase and decrease, local extrema, concavity, inflection points, and global extrema of a function. Then we use these information to draw a graph of the function.
- Linear approximation and differentials are useful tools to estimate the value of a function or its derivative near a given point. The linear approximation of a function f(x) at a point x=a is given by L(x) = f(a) + f'(a)(x-a). The differential of a function f(x) is given by df = f'(x)dx, which represents the change in the function value due to a small change in the input value.
- Mean value theorem states that if a function f(x) is continuous on [a,b] and differentiable on (a,b), then there exists a point c in (a,b) such that f'(c) = (f(b) - f(a))/(b-a). This theorem gives a relationship between the average rate of change and the instantaneous rate of change of a function over an interval.
- L'Hospital's rule is a technique to evaluate the limit of a quotient of two functions that have the form 0/0 or ∞/∞ as x approaches a certain value. The rule states that if f(x) and g(x) are differentiable and have the form 0/0 or ∞/∞ as x approaches a, then lim(x->a) f(x)/g(x) = lim(x->a) f'(x)/g'(x), provided that the latter limit exists or is ∞ or -∞.
- Newton's method is an iterative algorithm to find the root of a function f(x) by using the linear approximation of the function at each iteration. The formula for the nth iteration is given by x(n+1) = x(n) - f(x(n))/f'(x(n)). The method converges to the root if the initial guess is sufficiently close and the function is well-behaved.
- Indeterminate forms are expressions that have no definite value or meaning, such as 0/0, ∞/∞, 0^0, 1^∞, ∞ - ∞, 0 * ∞, and ∞^0. To evaluate the limit of a function that has an indeterminate form, we can use L'Hospital's rule, algebraic manipulation, or other techniques.
- Improper integrals are integrals that have infinite limits of integration or integrable functions that are unbounded on the interval of integration. To evaluate an improper integral, we need to take the limit of a proper integral as one or both of the limits of integration approach infinity or the point of discontinuity.
- A series is the sum of an infinite sequence of terms. A series is convergent if the sum of the terms approaches a finite value as the number of terms increases. A series is divergent if the sum of the terms does not approach a finite value or does not exist.
