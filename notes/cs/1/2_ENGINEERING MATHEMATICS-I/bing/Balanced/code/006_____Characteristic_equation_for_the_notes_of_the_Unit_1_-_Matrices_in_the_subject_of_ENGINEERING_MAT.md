### Characteristic equation

- The characteristic equation of a square matrix A is the equation that is solved to find the eigenvalues of A.
- The characteristic equation of A is given by det(A - xI) = 0, where x is a scalar variable, I is the identity matrix of the same size as A, and det is the determinant function.
- The characteristic equation of A is also called the characteristic polynomial of A, because it is a polynomial in x of degree n, where n is the order of A.
- The characteristic equation of A can be written as

  p(x) = det(A - xI) = a_0 + a_1 x + a_2 x^2 + ... + a_n x^n = 0

  where a_0, a_1, ..., a_n are constants that depend on the entries of A.
- The roots of the characteristic equation are the eigenvalues of A, and the multiplicity of a root is the algebraic multiplicity of the corresponding eigenvalue.
- The characteristic equation of A is invariant under matrix similarity, that is, if B is a matrix similar to A, then they have the same characteristic equation and the same eigenvalues.
- The characteristic equation of A can be used to find the trace and the determinant of A, as well as the rank and the nullity of A - xI for any x.
- The characteristic equation of A can also be used to test whether A is invertible, diagonalizable, or has a certain property such as symmetry, orthogonality, or positive definiteness.
- The characteristic equation of A can be derived by applying the definition of eigenvalues and eigenvectors, or by using the Cayley-Hamilton theorem, which states that A satisfies its own characteristic equation, that is, p(A) = 0.
- The characteristic equation of A can be computed by using various methods, such as cofactor expansion, Laplace expansion, or Gaussian elimination.
