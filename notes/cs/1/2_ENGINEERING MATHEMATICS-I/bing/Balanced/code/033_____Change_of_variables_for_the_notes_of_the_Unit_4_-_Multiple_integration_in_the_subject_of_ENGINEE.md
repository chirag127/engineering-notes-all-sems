### Change of variables for multiple integration

- The change of variables in multiple integrals is a technique that allows us to simplify the integration of a function over a complex region by transforming it to a function over a simpler region.
- The transformation is given by a one-to-one mapping of the variables, such as x = x(u, v) and y = y(u, v), where (u, v) are the new variables and (x, y) are the old variables.
- The region in the (u, v) plane that corresponds to the region in the (x, y) plane is called the image region, and the mapping is called the change of variables.
- To perform the change of variables, we need to find the Jacobian determinant of the transformation, which is given by J(u, v) = | ∂ x ∂ u ∂ x ∂ v ∂ y ∂ u ∂ y ∂ v |. The Jacobian measures how the area element dA in the (x, y) plane changes to the area element dA' in the (u, v) plane.
- The change of variables formula for multiple integrals states that if f(x, y) is a continuous function on a region R in the (x, y) plane, and x = x(u, v) and y = y(u, v) define a one-to-one mapping of a region R' in the (u, v) plane onto R, then

- The change of variables can be applied to double integrals, triple integrals, and iterated integrals, as long as the transformation is one-to-one and the Jacobian is nonzero.
- The change of variables can be useful for evaluating integrals that are difficult or impossible to do in the original variables, such as integrals involving polar, cylindrical, or spherical coordinates, or integrals involving complex regions or functions.
