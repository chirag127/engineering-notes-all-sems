## Unit 5 - Vector Calculus

Vector calculus is the branch of mathematics that studies the properties and applications of vector fields, scalar fields, and differential forms. Vector calculus is used to describe physical phenomena such as fluid flow, electromagnetism, heat transfer, and gravity.

Some of the main topics in vector calculus are:

- Vector functions: These are functions that assign a vector to each point in a domain. Examples of vector functions are position, velocity, acceleration, and force. Vector functions can be differentiated and integrated using the rules of calculus.

- Scalar functions: These are functions that assign a real number to each point in a domain. Examples of scalar functions are temperature, pressure, density, and potential. Scalar functions can also be differentiated and integrated using the rules of calculus.

- Gradient: The gradient of a scalar function is a vector function that points in the direction of the greatest rate of increase of the scalar function. The gradient can be used to find the normal vector to a surface, the direction of steepest ascent or descent, and the rate of change of a scalar function along a curve.

- Divergence: The divergence of a vector function is a scalar function that measures the net outward flux of the vector field per unit volume. The divergence can be used to find the sources and sinks of a vector field, the rate of expansion or contraction of a fluid, and the conservation of mass.

- Curl: The curl of a vector function is a vector function that measures the net circulation of the vector field per unit area. The curl can be used to find the vortices and eddies of a vector field, the angular velocity of a fluid, and the conservation of angular momentum.

- Line integrals: These are integrals that evaluate the sum of a function along a curve. Line integrals can be used to find the work done by a force, the circulation of a vector field, and the arc length of a curve.

- Surface integrals: These are integrals that evaluate the sum of a function over a surface. Surface integrals can be used to find the flux of a vector field, the area of a surface, and the mass of a thin sheet.

- Volume integrals: These are integrals that evaluate the sum of a function over a volume. Volume integrals can be used to find the divergence of a vector field, the volume of a solid, and the mass of a solid.

- Theorems of vector calculus: These are theorems that relate the different types of integrals and differential operators in vector calculus. Some of the most important theorems are:

  - Fundamental theorem of line integrals: This theorem states that the line integral of a conservative vector field along a curve is equal to the difference of the potential function at the endpoints of the curve.

  - Green's theorem: This theorem states that the line integral of a vector field along a closed curve in a plane is equal to the double integral of the curl of the vector field over the region enclosed by the curve.

  - Stokes' theorem: This theorem states that the line integral of a vector field along a closed curve in space is equal to the surface integral of the curl of the vector field over any surface bounded by the curve.

  - Divergence theorem: This theorem states that the surface integral of a vector field over a closed surface in space is equal to the triple integral of the divergence of the vector field over the volume enclosed by the surface.
