### Change of variables for the notes of the Unit 2 - Differential Calculus- I in the subject of ENGINEERING MATHEMATICS-I

- Change of variables is a method of transforming a differential equation into a simpler or more convenient form by introducing a new variable that is related to the original variables.
- Change of variables can be used to solve different types of differential equations, such as separable, homogeneous, linear, or Bernoulli equations.
- Change of variables can also be used to evaluate integrals by applying the reverse chain rule.
- Some examples of change of variables are:

  - Substituting $u = y'$ in a differential equation of the form $y' = Q(x) - P(x)y$ to make it separable.
  - Substituting $y = xV(x)$ in a differential equation of the form $y' = f(x,y)$ to make it homogeneous.
  - Substituting $y = v(x)x^n$ in a differential equation of the form $y' + p(x)y = q(x)y^n$ to make it linear.
  - Substituting $u = g(x)$ in an integral of the form $\int f(g(x))g'(x)dx$ to simplify it.

- The general steps for applying change of variables are:

  - Choose a suitable substitution that relates the new variable to the original variables.
  - Express the differential equation or the integral in terms of the new variable and its derivatives.
  - Solve the transformed equation or evaluate the integral using the appropriate methods.
  - Replace the new variable with the original variables using the inverse substitution.
