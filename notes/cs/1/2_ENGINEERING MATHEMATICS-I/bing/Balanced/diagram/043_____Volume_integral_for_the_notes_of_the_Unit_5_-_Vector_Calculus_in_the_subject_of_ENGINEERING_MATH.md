### Volume integral

- A volume integral is a type of multiple integral that extends the concept of area integral to three-dimensional regions.
- A volume integral can be used to calculate the volume of a solid, the mass of a solid with varying density, the electric charge of a region with varying charge density, and other physical quantities that involve integration over a volume.
- A volume integral can be expressed in Cartesian, cylindrical, or spherical coordinates, depending on the shape and symmetry of the region of integration.
- A volume integral can be evaluated by using the following formula:

$$\iiint_V f(x,y,z) \, dV = \int_a^b \int_{g_1(x)}^{g_2(x)} \int_{h_1(x,y)}^{h_2(x,y)} f(x,y,z) \, dz \, dy \, dx$$

- where $V$ is the region of integration, $f(x,y,z)$ is the integrand function, and $a, b, g_1, g_2, h_1, h_2$ are the limits of integration that define the boundaries of $V$.
- Alternatively, a volume integral can be computed by using the divergence theorem or the Stokes' theorem, which relate the volume integral of a vector field to the surface integral of its divergence or curl, respectively.
