### Expansion of functions by Taylor’s and Maclaurin’s theorems for functions of one and two variables

- A Taylor series is an expansion of a function f(x) around a point a in the form of an infinite sum of terms involving powers of (x-a) and derivatives of f at a  .
- The general formula for the Taylor series of f(x) at a is:

  f(x) = f(a) + f'(a)(x-a) + f''(a)(x-a)^2^/2! + f'''(a)(x-a)^3^/3! + ... + f^n^(a)(x-a)^n^/n! + ...

- The Taylor series can be used to approximate the value of a function near a, or to represent a function as a power series in an interval where the series converges .
- A Maclaurin series is a special case of the Taylor series where a = 0. The formula for the Maclaurin series of f(x) is:

  f(x) = f(0) + f'(0)x + f''(0)x^2^/2! + f'''(0)x^3^/3! + ... + f^n^(0)x^n^/n! + ...

- The Maclaurin series can be used to expand functions that are analytic at 0, or to find the coefficients of the power series representation of such functions.
- For functions of two variables f(x,y), the Taylor series at (a,b) is:

  f(x,y) = f(a,b) + f_x(a,b)(x-a) + f_y(a,b)(y-b) + 1/2! [f_xx(a,b)(x-a)^2^ + 2f_xy(a,b)(x-a)(y-b) + f_yy(a,b)(y-b)^2^] + ...

- where f_x, f_y, f_xx, f_xy, f_yy, ... are the partial derivatives of f with respect to x and y.
- The Taylor series for functions of two variables can be used to approximate the value of a function near (a,b), or to represent a function as a polynomial in (x-a) and (y-b) in a region where the series converges.
- A Maclaurin series for functions of two variables is a special case of the Taylor series where (a,b) = (0,0). The formula for the Maclaurin series of f(x,y) is:

  f(x,y) = f(0,0) + f_x(0,0)x + f_y(0,0)y + 1/2! [f_xx(0,0)x^2^ + 2f_xy(0,0)xy + f_yy(0,0)y^2^] + ...

- The Maclaurin series for functions of two variables can be used to expand functions that are analytic at (0,0), or to find the coefficients of the polynomial representation of such functions.
