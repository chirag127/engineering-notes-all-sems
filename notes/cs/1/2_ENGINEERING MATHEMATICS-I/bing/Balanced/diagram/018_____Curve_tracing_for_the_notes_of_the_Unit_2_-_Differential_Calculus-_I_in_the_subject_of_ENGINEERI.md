# Curve tracing for the notes of the Unit 2 - Differential Calculus- I in the subject of ENGINEERING MATHEMATICS-I

Curve tracing is the process of sketching the graph of a curve given its equation in cartesian, polar or parametric form. It involves finding the important features of the curve, such as its domain, range, intercepts, asymptotes, extrema, inflection points, symmetry and periodicity.

Some general steps for curve tracing are:

- Identify the type of equation (cartesian, polar or parametric) and the type of curve (linear, quadratic, cubic, etc.).
- Find the domain and range of the curve by considering the values of the variables for which the equation is defined and real.
- Find the x- and y-intercepts of the curve by setting one variable to zero and solving for the other.
- Find the vertical and horizontal asymptotes of the curve by considering the limits of the equation as the variables approach infinity or a finite value.
- Find the critical points of the curve by finding the first derivative of the equation and setting it to zero or undefined. These are the possible locations of the local maxima and minima of the curve.
- Find the intervals of increase and decrease of the curve by using the sign of the first derivative on either side of the critical points.
- Find the local maxima and minima of the curve by using the first derivative test or the second derivative test. The first derivative test compares the sign of the first derivative on either side of the critical point, while the second derivative test uses the value of the second derivative at the critical point.
- Find the inflection points of the curve by finding the second derivative of the equation and setting it to zero or undefined. These are the possible locations of the change of concavity of the curve.
- Find the intervals of concave up and concave down of the curve by using the sign of the second derivative on either side of the inflection points.
- Find the points of symmetry of the curve by testing if the equation is invariant under certain transformations, such as reflection, rotation or translation. For example, a curve is symmetric about the x-axis if f(x) = f(-x), symmetric about the y-axis if f(-x) = -f(x), and symmetric about the origin if f(-x) = -f(-x).
- Find the period of the curve if it is periodic, that is, if it repeats itself after a certain interval. For example, a curve is periodic with period T if f(x + T) = f(x) for all x in the domain.
- Sketch the curve by plotting the important points and features and joining them smoothly. Use a dashed line for asymptotes and indicate the direction of the curve at the ends.
