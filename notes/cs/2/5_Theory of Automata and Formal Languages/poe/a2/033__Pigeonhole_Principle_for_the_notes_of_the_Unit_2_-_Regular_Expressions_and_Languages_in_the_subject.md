### Pigeonhole Principle

The Pigeonhole Principle states that if n objects are put into m containers, with n > m, then at least one container must contain more than one object.

* If we have more objects than containers, then at least one container must hold more than one object.
* This is a useful principle used in proof by contradiction to show that a statement cannot be true for all cases.
* For example, to show that not all students in a class can have different birth months, if there are more students than months. At least two students must have the same birth month.
* The Pigeonhole Principle can be generalized to any attributes/parameters - if there are more objects than possible values of an attribute, at least two objects must have the same attribute value.
* This principle is useful in proving upper bounds and impossibility results in algorithms and analysis of algorithms.
