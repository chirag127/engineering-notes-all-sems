### Regular Expressions for the notes of the Unit 2 - Regular Expressions and Languages

1. Regular expressions are patterns used to match character combinations in strings. They are a concise and flexible way to identify strings of text of interest, such as particular characters, words, or patterns of characters.

2. Regular expressions can be used to perform all types of text search and text replace operations. They are supported by many programming languages and text editors.

3. The regular expressions are constructed using a formal language with a specific syntax. They comprise literals, metacharacters, quantifiers, character classes, anchors, grouping constructs, and flags that modify the matching behavior.

4. Literals: The characters you want to find directly such as a, b, c, etc. Metacharacters: Characters with a special meaning such as ., *, ?, +, [, ], etc. Quantifiers: Indicate numbers of occurrences of a substring such as *, +, ?, {n}, {n,}, {n,m}. Character classes: Denote a set of characters such as [abc], [a-z], [0-9]. Anchors: Mark the start or end of the string such as ^, $.

5. Grouping constructs capture substring matches. Flags modify the matching behavior, e.g. case-insensitive matching.

6. Regular expressions can be converted into finite automata and vice-versa. This establishes the connection between regular languages (languages acceptable by finite automata) and regular expressions.

7. The capability of regular expressions to concisely represent patterns makes them useful in numerous applications such as validation, parsing, filtering, and search & replace. However, they have certain limitations and cannot represent languages that are not regular.
