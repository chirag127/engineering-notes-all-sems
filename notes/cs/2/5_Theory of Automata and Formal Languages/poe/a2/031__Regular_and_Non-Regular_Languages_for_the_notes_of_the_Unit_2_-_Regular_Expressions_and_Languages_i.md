### Regular and Non-Regular Languages

**Regular Languages:**

1. Languages accepted by finite automata are called regular languages.
2. Regular languages can be defined by regular expressions.
3. The class of regular languages is the smallest class of languages with useful applications like lexical analysis.
4. Regular languages are closed under union, concatenation, and Kleene closure.
5. Examples of regular languages: {a^n b^n | n >= 0}, {0^i 1^j | i,j >= 0}.

**Non-Regular Languages:**

1. Languages that are not accepted by finite automata are called non-regular languages.
2. Non-regular languages cannot be defined by regular expressions.
3. Examples of non-regular languages: {a^n b^n c^n | n >= 0}, {w in {a,b}* | w has an even number of a's}.
4. The class of non-regular languages is larger than the class of regular languages.
