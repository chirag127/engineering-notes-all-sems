### Symbol for the notes of the Unit 1 - Basic Concepts and Automata Theory in the subject of Theory of Automata and Formal Languages

1. Alphabet: Finite set of symbols. Ex: {0, 1}
2. String: Finite sequence of symbols from an alphabet. Ex: 0101
3. Language: Set of strings from an alphabet. Ex: {01, 10, 11}
4. Automata: Mathematical model of a machine to recognize patterns (languages).
5. Finite Automata: automata with finite number of states and transitions. Classified into:
- Deterministic Finite Automata (DFA): Has one transition from a state on an input symbol.
- Non-Deterministic Finite Automata (NFA): Has zero or more transitions from a state on an input symbol.
6. DFA to NFA Conversion: Subset construction. NFA has more accepting power than DFA for same number of states.
7. Regular Expressions: Denote languages/patterns to be recognized by finite automata.
Formed using symbols, concatenation, union, closure, etc.
8. Regular Languages: Languages that can be recognized by finite automata (DFA/NFA) or described by regular expressions.
Closed under union, concatenation, closure, complement but not intersection or difference.
