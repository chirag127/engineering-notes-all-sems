### Church's Thesis

- Church's Thesis states that the intuitive notion of effective method in logic is captured by the lambda calculus.
- It asserts that the lambda calculus is computationally equivalent to Turing machines.
- In other words, it proposes that lambda calculus and Turing machines can define the same functions.
- This means that any computation that can be performed by a Turing machine can also be performed by the lambda calculus and vice-versa.
- Church's Thesis is not a mathematical theorem but a hypothesis about the nature of computation. It has been shown to be consistent with the standard ZFC axioms of mathematics.
- It has profound implications for the philosophy of mind and the possibility of artificial intelligence as it suggests that the intuitive notion of algorithms is equivalent to Turing-computability.
- However, it does not specify what exactly constitutes an effective method and there can be other models of computation that can also define the same functions as lambda calculus and Turing machines.
