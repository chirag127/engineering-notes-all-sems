### Formal Languages

1. Formal languages are languages that are defined using precise mathematical or machine-based rules and have strict syntax and semantics.
2. The rules specify exactly what is a legal word or string for that language. Any string either belongs to the language or does not belong to the language. There are no partial memberships.
3. Formal languages are classified into four types:

- Regular languages: Can be recognized by finite state machines. They have simple patterns.
- Context-free languages: Can be generated by context-free grammars. They are a subset of recursive languages.
- Context-sensitive languages: Can be generated by context-sensitive grammars. They are a superset of context-free languages.
- Recursive languages: The most powerful class of formal languages. They can describe all computable functions.

4. Formal languages are useful in computer science for defining syntax of programming languages, defining patterns to be matched in text processing applications like lexical analysis, and more. They provide precise ways to describe syntax.
5. Some examples of formal languages are regular expressions, Backus-Naur Form or BNF used to specify syntax of programming languages, and Chomsky hierarchy of languages.
