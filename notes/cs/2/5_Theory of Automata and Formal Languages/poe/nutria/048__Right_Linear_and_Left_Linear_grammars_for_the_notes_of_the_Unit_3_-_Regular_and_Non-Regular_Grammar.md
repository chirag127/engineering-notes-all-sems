
### Right Linear and Left Linear Grammars

* Right linear grammars (RLGs) are grammars that contain only one non-terminal symbol on the right side of a production rule. 
* Left linear grammars (LLGs) are grammars that contain only one non-terminal symbol on the left side of a production rule. 
* A production rule in a right linear grammar can be written as A → aB, where A and B are non-terminal symbols and a is a terminal symbol. 
* Similarly, a production rule in a left linear grammar can be written as A → Ba, where A and B are non-terminal symbols and a is a terminal symbol.
* Right linear and left linear grammars are important in the study of formal languages and automata theory as they are used to generate regular languages. 
* A regular language is a language that can be described using a regular expression or a finite automaton. 
* Right linear and left linear grammars are also useful in the study of parsing algorithms, as they can be used to generate context-free languages. 
* Context-free languages are languages that can be described using context-free grammars.
