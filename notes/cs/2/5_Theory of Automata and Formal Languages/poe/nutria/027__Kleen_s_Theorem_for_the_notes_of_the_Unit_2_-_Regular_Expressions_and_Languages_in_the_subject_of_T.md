
### Kleen’s Theorem
Kleen's Theorem is an important result in the theory of automata and formal languages. It states that the set of regular expressions is equivalent to the set of finite automata.

- Regular Expressions (REs) are strings of characters which define a search pattern. They can be used to match patterns in text or other data.
- Finite Automata (FAs) are machines which accept or reject strings of characters.

Kleen's Theorem states that any regular expression can be translated into an equivalent finite automata, and vice versa. This means that any regular expression can be used to describe a finite automata, and any finite automata can be described by a regular expression.

Kleen's Theorem is an important result in the field of automata and formal languages, as it allows us to use regular expressions to describe the behavior of finite automata. This is useful for designing and analyzing algorithms which use finite automata.
