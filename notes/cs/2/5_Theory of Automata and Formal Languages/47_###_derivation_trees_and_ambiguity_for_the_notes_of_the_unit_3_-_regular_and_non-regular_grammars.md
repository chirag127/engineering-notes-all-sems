### Derivation Trees and Ambiguity for the notes of the Unit 3 - Regular and Non-Regular Grammars in the subject of Theory of Automata and Formal Languages

Unit 3 of the Theory of Automata and Formal Languages course covers the topics of Regular and Non-Regular Grammars. Grammars are a type of formal language that describe the structure of strings in a language.

Derivation trees and ambiguity are important concepts in the study of grammars. A derivation tree is a tree-like structure that shows the steps used to generate a string from a grammar. 

Ambiguity is a property of a grammar that occurs when there is more than one possible parse tree for a given string. Ambiguity can be problematic in certain applications, such as programming languages, where it is important to have a unique interpretation of a string.

In the context of the Theory of Automata and Formal Languages course, students will learn about derivation trees and ambiguity as part of Unit 3 - Regular and Non-Regular Grammars. This may include working with examples of derivation trees, analyzing the behavior of grammars in response to different inputs, and writing code to implement grammars.
