### Decidability for the notes of the Unit 2 - Regular Expressions and Languages in the subject of Theory of Automata and Formal Languages

Decidability is a central concept in the study of formal languages and automata theory. It refers to the ability to determine, in a finite amount of time, whether a given statement or problem is true or false. In the context of formal languages and automata theory, decidability is concerned with the question of whether a given language can be recognized by a finite automaton or not.

In the Unit 2 - Regular Expressions and Languages of the subject of Theory of Automata and Formal Languages, students learn about the concepts and algorithms involved in the representation and manipulation of formal languages. The notes for this unit should include the following topics:

1. Definition of decidability and its importance in formal languages and automata theory
2. Overview of decidability (finite time, true or false)
3. Decidability of regular languages (regular expressions, finite automata)
4. Decidability of context-free languages (context-free grammars, pushdown automata)
5. Decidability of context-sensitive languages (context-sensitive grammars, linear-bounded automata)
6. Undecidability results (Turing machines, halting problem)

Decidability is an important concept in the study of formal languages and automata theory. It provides a foundation for understanding the limitations of automata and formal languages, and helps students to understand the role of decidability in the design and implementation of algorithms and computational systems.
