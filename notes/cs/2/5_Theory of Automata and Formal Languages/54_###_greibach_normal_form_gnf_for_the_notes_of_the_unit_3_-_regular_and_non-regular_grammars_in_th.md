### Greibach Normal Form (GNF) for the notes of the Unit 3 - Regular and Non-Regular Grammars in the subject of Theory of Automata and Formal Languages

Greibach Normal Form (GNF) is a normal form for context-free grammars (CFGs) that is used to simplify the structure of CFGs and to make it easier to analyze the behavior of the grammars. GNF is named after Sheila Greibach, who first published the result in the 1960s.

In GNF, every production rule in the grammar must have the form A -> aB, where A is a non-terminal symbol, a is a terminal symbol, and B is a string of non-terminal symbols. This form ensures that every non-terminal symbol in the grammar generates a string of terminals, and that the string is generated by a single production rule.

GNF is useful for analyzing the behavior of CFGs, as it provides a simple and uniform structure for the grammar. This makes it easier to determine the properties of the grammar, such as its ambiguity and its language, and to construct algorithms for processing the grammar.
