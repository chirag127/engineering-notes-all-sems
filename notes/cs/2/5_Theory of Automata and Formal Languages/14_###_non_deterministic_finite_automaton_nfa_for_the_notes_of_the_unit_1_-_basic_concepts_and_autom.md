### Non Deterministic Finite Automaton (NFA) for the notes of the Unit 1 - Basic Concepts and Automata Theory in the subject of Theory of Automata and Formal Languages

A Non-Deterministic Finite Automaton (NFA) is a type of finite automaton that allows multiple transitions from a single state for a given input symbol. It is a mathematical model used to recognize patterns in strings and is an important concept in the study of automata theory and formal languages.

In the Unit 1 - Basic Concepts and Automata Theory of the subject of Theory of Automata and Formal Languages, students learn about the basic concepts and theories that form the foundation of the field. The notes for this unit should include the following topics:

1. Definition of NFA and its purpose (pattern recognition in strings)
2. Overview of NFA in automata theory (multiple transitions, non-deterministic behavior)
3. NFA representation (states, transitions, start state, final state)
4. NFA operations (transition function, language recognition)
5. Comparison of NFA with Deterministic Finite Automaton (DFA)
6. NFA conversion to DFA (subset construction algorithm)
7. Limitations of NFA and its relationship to computability

NFA is an important concept in the study of automata theory and formal languages. It provides a foundation for understanding the concepts of finite automata and helps students to understand the relationship between automata and the recognition of patterns in strings.
