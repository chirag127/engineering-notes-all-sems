### Representation

- Representation is the process of encoding information in a suitable form for computation or communication.
- There are different types of representation, such as:
  - Symbolic representation: using symbols, such as letters, numbers, or words, to represent information. For example, the word "cat" is a symbolic representation of a certain animal.
  - Graphical representation: using images, diagrams, or charts to represent information. For example, a picture of a cat is a graphical representation of the same animal.
  - Numerical representation: using numbers or numerical systems to represent information. For example, the binary number 1100010 is a numerical representation of the decimal number 98.
  - Logical representation: using logic, such as propositions, predicates, or rules, to represent information. For example, the proposition "All cats are mammals" is a logical representation of a certain fact.
- Representation is important for the study of automata and formal languages, because:
  - Automata are abstract machines that can perform computations or recognize patterns based on their input and output representations. For example, a finite automaton can accept or reject a string of symbols based on its transition function and final states.
  - Formal languages are sets of strings of symbols that follow certain rules or grammars. For example, a regular language is a formal language that can be recognized by a finite automaton.
  - Representation allows us to define, compare, and analyze different types of automata and formal languages, and to understand their properties and limitations. For example, representation allows us to prove that some languages are not regular, or that some automata are equivalent or more powerful than others.
