### Complexity

- Complexity theory is a branch of theoretical computer science that studies the resources (such as time and space) required to solve computational problems using abstract models of computation (such as automata, Turing machines, circuits, etc.).
- Complexity theory aims to classify problems according to their inherent difficulty, and to identify the limits and possibilities of efficient computation.
- Complexity theory can be divided into two main subfields: time complexity and space complexity.
- Time complexity measures how much time (or number of steps) is needed to solve a problem on a given model of computation, as a function of the size of the input.
- Space complexity measures how much memory (or number of cells) is needed to solve a problem on a given model of computation, as a function of the size of the input.
- Complexity theory also studies other types of resources, such as communication, randomness, parallelism, quantum computation, etc.
- Complexity theory also investigates the relationships between different models of computation, and the trade-offs between different resources.
- Complexity theory also explores the existence and properties of hard problems, such as NP-complete problems, which are believed to be intractable for any efficient algorithm.
