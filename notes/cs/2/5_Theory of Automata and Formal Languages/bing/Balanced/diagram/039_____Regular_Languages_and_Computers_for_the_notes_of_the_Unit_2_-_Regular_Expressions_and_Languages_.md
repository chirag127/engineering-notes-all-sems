### Regular Languages and Computers

- Regular languages are a class of formal languages that can be defined by regular expressions or recognized by finite automata  .
- Regular languages are used in parsing and designing programming languages, as well as in searching and manipulating text data .
- Regular languages can model computational problems that require a very small amount of memory, such as checking if a light switch is on or off, or if a word contains a certain pattern.
- Regular languages have a hierarchical structure, meaning that every regular language is also a context-free language, a context-sensitive language, and a recursively enumerable language, but not vice versa.
- Regular languages have several properties and operations that can be used to manipulate and reason about them, such as closure, complement, union, intersection, difference, concatenation, Kleene star, reversal, homomorphism, and minimization .
- Regular languages can be classified into different types, such as finite languages, unary languages, star-free languages, and piecewise testable languages, based on their syntactic and semantic features.
- Regular languages can be equivalently defined by different models of computation, such as deterministic finite automata (DFA), nondeterministic finite automata (NFA), regular grammars, and regular expressions .
- Regular languages can be decided by various algorithms, such as the membership algorithm, the emptiness algorithm, the equivalence algorithm, and the inclusion algorithm.
