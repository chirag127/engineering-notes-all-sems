### Language Acceptability of Turing Machines

- A Turing machine (TM) is a mathematical model of computation that can perform any algorithmic task by manipulating symbols on an infinite tape.
- A TM accepts a language if it enters into a final state for any input string w .
- A language is recursively enumerable (generated by Type-0 grammar) if it is accepted by a TM .
- A TM decides a language if it accepts it and enters into a rejecting state for any input not in the language .
- A language is recursive (generated by Type-1 grammar) if it is decided by a TM.
- A TM can be deterministic (DTM) or nondeterministic (NTM). A DTM has only one possible move for any given configuration, while an NTM can have multiple possible moves.
- A DTM and an NTM are equivalent in terms of the languages they can accept or decide, but an NTM can be faster or more concise than a DTM.
- A TM can be classified into different types based on the number and direction of its tapes, such as single-tape, multi-tape, two-way infinite, one-way infinite, etc.
- A TM can also be classified into different types based on the complexity of its time or space requirements, such as polynomial-time, exponential-time, linear-space, logarithmic-space, etc.
- A TM can simulate other models of computation, such as finite automata, pushdown automata, or lambda calculus, by encoding their states and transitions on its tape.
- A TM can also be simulated by other models of computation, such as universal Turing machines, Turing machines with oracles, or recursive functions, by using their input and output mechanisms.
