### Computability for the notes of the Unit 1 - Basic Concepts and Automata Theory in the subject of Theory of Automata and Formal Languages

- Computability theory, also known as recursion theory, is the branch of mathematics that studies the limits and possibilities of computation.
- The main goal of computability theory is to determine which problems can be solved by a computer (or any other model of computation) and which problems are inherently unsolvable.
- Computability theory also investigates the properties and classifications of different types of computable functions and sets, such as recursive, recursively enumerable, decidable, undecidable, etc.
- Some of the fundamental concepts and results of computability theory are:
  - The Church-Turing thesis, which states that any effective procedure can be simulated by a Turing machine, and vice versa.
  - The halting problem, which is the problem of deciding whether a given Turing machine will eventually halt on a given input. The halting problem is undecidable, meaning that there is no algorithm that can solve it for all possible inputs.
  - The Rice theorem, which states that any non-trivial property of the output of a Turing machine is undecidable, meaning that there is no algorithm that can determine whether a given Turing machine has that property or not.
  - The Turing degrees, which are a way of measuring the relative complexity of undecidable problems. Two problems are Turing equivalent if they can be reduced to each other by a computable function, and a problem is Turing reducible to another problem if it can be solved using an oracle for the other problem.
  - The arithmetic hierarchy, which is a way of classifying sets of natural numbers according to their definability in first-order arithmetic. The hierarchy consists of levels called Σn, Πn, and Δn, where n is a natural number. A set is in Σn if it can be defined by a formula with n alternations of existential and universal quantifiers, starting with an existential quantifier. A set is in Πn if it can be defined by a formula with n alternations of existential and universal quantifiers, starting with a universal quantifier. A set is in Δn if it is both in Σn and Πn.
- Computability theory is closely related to automata theory, which is the study of abstract models of computation, such as finite automata, pushdown automata, Turing machines, etc. Automata theory can be used to prove results about computability, such as the equivalence of different models of computation, the decidability of various languages and problems, and the existence of undecidable languages and problems.
- Automata theory also has applications in other areas of computer science, such as compiler design, formal languages, parsing, pattern matching, etc.
