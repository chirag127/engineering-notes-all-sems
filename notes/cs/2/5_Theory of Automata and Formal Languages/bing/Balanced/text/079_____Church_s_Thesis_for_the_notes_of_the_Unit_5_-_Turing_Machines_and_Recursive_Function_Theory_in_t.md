### Church's Thesis

- Church's thesis, also known as Church's theorem or the Church-Turing thesis, is a principle that states that the recursive functions are the only functions that can be mechanically calculated.
- A recursive function is a function that can be defined by a finite set of rules that involve calling the function itself with smaller arguments.
- A function is mechanically calculable if there exists an algorithm, or a step-by-step procedure, that can be carried out by a human or a machine to compute the function for any given input.
- The thesis is named after the American mathematician Alonzo Church and the British mathematician Alan Turing, who independently proposed the idea in the 1930s.
- The thesis is not a formal statement that can be proved or disproved, but rather a hypothesis that captures the intuitive notion of effective computability.
- The thesis has several variations and extensions, such as the Church-Turing-Deutsch principle, which states that every physical process can be simulated by a Turing machine.
- The thesis has important implications for the foundations of mathematics, logic, computer science, and philosophy, as it sets the limits of what can be computed and what can be proved.
