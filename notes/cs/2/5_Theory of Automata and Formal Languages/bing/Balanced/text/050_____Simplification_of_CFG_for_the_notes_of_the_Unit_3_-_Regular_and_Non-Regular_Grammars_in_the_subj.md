### Simplification of CFG

- Simplification of CFG is the process of removing unnecessary or redundant productions and symbols from a context-free grammar (CFG).
- Simplification of CFG is useful for converting a CFG into a normal form, such as Chomsky normal form or Greibach normal form.
- Simplification of CFG consists of the following steps:
  - Removing useless productions: These are the productions that cannot be used to derive any string in the language generated by the CFG. There are two types of useless productions:
    - Non-terminating productions: These are the productions that contain a non-terminal symbol that cannot be replaced by any sequence of terminal symbols. For example, in the CFG `S -> AB | a, A -> aA, B -> bB`, the production `A -> aA` is non-terminating because `A` cannot be eliminated by any derivation.
    - Unreachable productions: These are the productions that contain a non-terminal symbol that cannot be derived from the start symbol. For example, in the CFG `S -> AB | a, A -> aA, B -> bB, C -> cC`, the production `C -> cC` is unreachable because `C` cannot be derived from `S`.
  - Removing null productions: These are the productions that have an empty string on the right-hand side. For example, in the CFG `S -> AB | a, A -> aA | ε, B -> bB | ε`, the productions `A -> ε` and `B -> ε` are null productions. Null productions can be eliminated by replacing every occurrence of a nullable non-terminal symbol by its right-hand side or by removing it.
  - Removing unit productions: These are the productions that have a single non-terminal symbol on the right-hand side. For example, in the CFG `S -> AB | a, A -> aA | B, B -> bB | a`, the productions `A -> B` and `B -> a` are unit productions. Unit productions can be eliminated by replacing every occurrence of a unit non-terminal symbol by its right-hand side or by removing it.
