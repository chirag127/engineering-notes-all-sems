### Introduction to Theory of Computation

- Theory of computation (TOC) is a branch of computer science that is concerned with how problems can be solved using algorithms and how efficiently they can be solved.
- TOC includes the fundamental mathematical properties of computer hardware, software and their applications.
- TOC deals with what problems can be solved on a model of computation, using an algorithm, how efficiently they can be solved or to what degree (e.g., approximate solutions versus precise ones).
- A model of computation is an abstract representation of a computing device that defines its capabilities and limitations.
- Some examples of models of computation are Turing machines, finite automata, pushdown automata, and lambda calculus.
- TOC also studies the concepts of computability, decidability, reducibility, recursive function theory, complexity classes, completeness, hierarchy theorems, and oracles .
- Computability is the study of what kinds of problems can be solved by a given model of computation.
- Decidability is the study of which problems can be solved by an algorithm that always terminates with a correct answer.
- Reducibility is the study of how one problem can be transformed into another problem that is easier to solve.
- Recursive function theory is the study of the class of functions that can be computed by a Turing machine.
- Complexity classes are the study of how the resources required to solve a problem (such as time and space) vary with the size of the input.
- Completeness is the study of the hardest problems in a given complexity class that can be used to characterize the class.
- Hierarchy theorems are the study of how complexity classes are related to each other by inclusion or separation.
- Inherently complex problems are the study of problems that are provably hard to solve in a given complexity class, regardless of the algorithm used.
- Oracles are the study of hypothetical devices that can answer certain questions that are otherwise undecidable or intractable.

- Unit 1 - Basic Concepts and Automata Theory covers the following topics:

  - Finite automata and regular expressions: These are the simplest models of computation that can recognize regular languages, which are sets of strings that follow a certain pattern.
  - Nondeterminism and closure properties: These are the concepts that allow us to manipulate and compare regular languages using finite automata and regular expressions.
  - Context-free grammars and pushdown automata: These are more powerful models of computation that can recognize context-free languages, which are sets of strings that can be generated by a set of rules.
  - Normal forms and pumping lemma: These are the tools that help us simplify and analyze context-free grammars and languages.
  - Turing machines and variants: These are the most general models of computation that can simulate any other model of computation and recognize any computable language.
  - Church-Turing thesis and decidability: These are the fundamental principles that establish the limits of computation and the existence of undecidable problems.
