### Church's Thesis

- Church's thesis, also called Church's theorem, is a principle formulated by the 20th-century American logician Alonzo Church, stating that the recursive functions are the only functions that can be mechanically calculated.
- Recursive functions are functions that can be defined by a finite set of rules, such as substitution, composition, primitive recursion, and minimization.
- Mechanical calculation means that the function can be computed by a device that follows a fixed set of instructions, such as a Turing machine.
- Church's thesis is not a formal theorem, but a conjecture that expresses the intuition that any function that can be effectively computed can also be computed by a Turing machine.
- Church's thesis is also known as the Church-Turing thesis, because it was independently proposed by the British mathematician Alan Turing around the same time as Church.
- Church's thesis has many implications and applications in logic, mathematics, and computer science, such as the undecidability of the halting problem, the equivalence of different models of computation, and the foundations of constructive mathematics .
- Church's thesis is widely accepted by most mathematicians and computer scientists, but it is not a provable statement, because it involves an informal notion of effective computability .
- There are some variations and extensions of Church's thesis, such as the Church-Turing-Deutsch principle, which states that a quantum computer can simulate any physical process, and the Church's thesis for constructive mathematics, which states that all total functions are computable .
