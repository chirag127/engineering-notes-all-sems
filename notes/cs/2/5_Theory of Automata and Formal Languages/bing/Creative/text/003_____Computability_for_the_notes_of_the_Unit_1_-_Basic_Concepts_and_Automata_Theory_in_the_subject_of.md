### Computability for the notes of the Unit 1 - Basic Concepts and Automata Theory in the subject of Theory of Automata and Formal Languages

- Computability theory, also known as recursion theory, is the area of mathematics dealing with the concept of an effective procedure – a procedure that can be carried out by following specific rules .
- Computability theory originated in the 1930s with the study of computable functions and Turing degrees, which measure the degree of unsolvability of a problem.
- Computability theory also studies generalized computability and definability, which explore the limits of computation and mathematical reasoning in different settings.
- Computability theory is closely related to the theory of computation, which deals with abstract models of computation, such as automata, formal languages, grammars, and algorithms .
- Some of the main topics in computability theory are:
  - Church-Turing thesis: the claim that any effective procedure can be simulated by a Turing machine, a mathematical model of computation .
  - Decidability and undecidability: the study of which problems can or cannot be solved by an algorithm, or a finite set of rules .
  - Reducibility: the study of how one problem can be transformed into another problem, and how this affects the difficulty or solvability of the problems .
  - Recursive function theory: the study of functions that can be defined by recursion, or by applying a function to itself or to simpler arguments .
  - Time and space measures on computation: the study of how much time or space is required to solve a problem on a given model of computation .
  - Completeness and hierarchy theorems: the study of how problems can be classified into different levels of difficulty or complexity, and how these levels relate to each other .
  - Inherently complex problems: the study of problems that are provably hard to solve, even with unlimited resources .
  - Oracles: the study of hypothetical devices that can answer questions that are otherwise undecidable or intractable .
