### Decision Problems of CFL

A decision problem is a problem that can be answered with a yes or no. In the context of context-free languages (CFLs), there are several decision problems that are of interest. These include:

1. **Emptiness Problem**: Given a context-free grammar G, is L(G) = ∅?
2. **Membership Problem**: Given a context-free grammar G and a string w, is w ∈ L(G)?
3. **Finiteness Problem**: Given a context-free grammar G, is L(G) a finite language?
4. **Equivalence Problem**: Given two context-free grammars G1 and G2, is L(G1) = L(G2)?
5. **Inclusion Problem**: Given two context-free grammars G1 and G2, is L(G1) ⊆ L(G2)?

These problems are important because they help us understand the properties of context-free languages and the limitations of context-free grammars. Some of these problems are decidable, meaning that there exists an algorithm that can always provide a correct answer. Others are undecidable, meaning that no such algorithm exists.

In Unit 4 of the course on Theory of Automata and Formal Languages, we will study these decision problems in detail, along with the properties of pushdown automata and context-free languages. This will provide a solid foundation for understanding the power and limitations of context-free grammars and their applications.
