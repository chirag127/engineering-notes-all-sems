### Computability
Computability is a branch of theoretical computer science and mathematical logic that deals with the study of algorithms and their computational capabilities. It is concerned with the question of what problems can be solved by a computer and what problems cannot be solved by a computer. Here are some key points to remember about computability:

1. Computability theory is closely related to the field of automata theory, which studies abstract computational models such as finite state machines and Turing machines.

2. A problem is considered computable if there exists an algorithm that can solve it. An algorithm is a step-by-step procedure for solving a problem that can be implemented on a computer.

3. The Church-Turing thesis states that any problem that can be solved by an algorithm can be solved by a Turing machine. This thesis is widely accepted as a fundamental principle of computer science.

4. There are many problems that are known to be uncomputable, meaning that no algorithm can solve them. Examples of uncomputable problems include the halting problem and the Entscheidungsproblem.

5. The study of computability has important implications for the design of computer programs and the development of new computational methods. It helps us understand the limitations of computation and provides a framework for exploring new approaches to problem-solving.
