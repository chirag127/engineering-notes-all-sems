### Formal Languages

Formal languages are a fundamental concept in the study of automata theory and formal languages. They are used to define and describe the syntax of programming languages, data formats, and other formal systems.

Here are some key points to remember about formal languages:

1. A formal language is a set of strings of symbols, where the symbols are taken from a finite alphabet.
2. The strings in a formal language are called words or sentences.
3. The rules for constructing sentences in a formal language are called the grammar of the language.
4. Formal languages can be classified based on the complexity of their grammar. The Chomsky hierarchy is a commonly used classification system.
5. Regular languages, context-free languages, context-sensitive languages, and recursively enumerable languages are some of the classes of formal languages in the Chomsky hierarchy.
6. Automata theory is the study of abstract machines that can recognize formal languages.
7. Finite automata, pushdown automata, and Turing machines are some of the types of automata used to recognize different classes of formal languages.
