### Church’s Thesis

Church's Thesis, also known as the Church-Turing Thesis, is a statement about the nature of computation. It asserts that any function that can be effectively computed by a human using a well-defined procedure can also be computed by a Turing machine.

The thesis is named after Alonzo Church, who first proposed it in 1936. It is not a formal mathematical theorem, but rather a hypothesis about the limits of computation.

Some key points to consider when studying Church's Thesis include:

1. The thesis is not a formal mathematical theorem, but rather a hypothesis about the limits of computation.
2. It asserts that any function that can be effectively computed by a human using a well-defined procedure can also be computed by a Turing machine.
3. The thesis is named after Alonzo Church, who first proposed it in 1936.
4. The thesis has been widely accepted by the computer science community and has been used as a foundation for the study of computation.
5. There have been attempts to formally prove or disprove the thesis, but it remains an open question.
