### Greibach Normal Form (GNF)

Greibach Normal Form (GNF) is a specific type of context-free grammar (CFG) that is used in the study of formal languages and automata theory. It is named after Sheila Greibach, who introduced the concept in 1965.

Here are some key points to remember about GNF:

1. In GNF, the right-hand side of each production rule must start with a terminal symbol, followed by zero or more non-terminal symbols.
2. GNF is used to convert a CFG into an equivalent grammar that can be recognized by a pushdown automaton (PDA).
3. The conversion process involves removing left recursion and left factoring from the CFG.
4. GNF is useful for parsing algorithms, as it allows for efficient top-down parsing.
5. Every context-free language can be generated by a grammar in GNF.
