### Computability for the notes of the Unit 1 - Basic Concepts and Automata Theory in the subject of Theory of Automata and Formal Languages

Computability refers to the ability of a machine or system to perform a computation. It is a fundamental concept in the study of automata theory and formal languages, and is concerned with the study of algorithms and their relationship to the computational power of machines.

In the Unit 1 - Basic Concepts and Automata Theory of the subject of Theory of Automata and Formal Languages, students learn about the basic concepts and theories that form the foundation of the field. The notes for this unit should include the following topics:

1. Definition of computability and its importance in automata theory and formal languages
2. Overview of computability and its relationship to algorithms and computational power
3. The Church-Turing thesis and its implications for computability
4. The concept of decidability and its relationship to computability
5. The concept of undecidability and its implications for computability
6. The Halting Problem and its relationship to computability
7. The concept of reducibility and its relationship to computability

Computability is a critical concept in the study of automata theory and formal languages. It provides a foundation for understanding the relationship between algorithms and computational power and helps students to understand the limitations of machines and systems in performing computations.
