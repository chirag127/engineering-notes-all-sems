### Context Free Grammar(CFG) for the notes of the Unit 3 - Regular and Non-Regular Grammars in the subject of Theory of Automata and Formal Languages

Context Free Grammar (CFG) is a formalism used to describe the structure of strings in a context-free language. It consists of a set of production rules that specify how to generate strings in the language. The production rules in a CFG consist of a non-terminal symbol on the left-hand side and a string of terminal and non-terminal symbols on the right-hand side.

In the Unit 3 - Regular and Non-Regular Grammars of the subject of Theory of Automata and Formal Languages, students learn about the concepts and algorithms involved in formal languages and grammar. The notes for this unit should include the following topics:

1. Definition of Context Free Grammar (CFG) and its purpose (describing the structure of strings in a context-free language)
2. Overview of CFG (production rules, non-terminal symbols, terminal symbols)
3. Types of CFG (ambiguous, unambiguous, normal form)
4. CFG representation (BNF, EBNF)
5. Derivation of strings in a CFG (leftmost, rightmost)
6. Comparison of CFG with other formalisms (regular expressions, finite automata)
7. Applications of CFG in computer science (compiler design, parsing)

Context Free Grammar is an important concept in the study of formal languages and grammar. It provides a foundation for understanding the concepts and algorithms involved in the description of the structure of strings in a context-free language and helps students to understand the role of CFG in computer science and algorithm design.
