### Pumping Lemma for CFL for the notes of the Unit 4 - Push Down Automata and Properties of Context Free Languages in the subject of Theory of Automata and Formal Languages

The pumping lemma for context free languages (CFL) is a result in the theory of formal languages that provides a way to prove that a language is not context free. The pumping lemma states that if a language is context free, then there exists a constant "p" such that for any string "w" in the language with length greater than "p", it can be broken into three parts "u", "v", and "x" such that:

1. "uv" is non-empty and repeated at least once.
2. "v" is non-empty.
3. "ux" is also in the language.

If a language fails to satisfy the pumping lemma, then it cannot be context free. The pumping lemma provides a useful tool for proving that a language is not context free, and is a fundamental result in the theory of formal languages.
