### Arden’s theorem for the notes of the Unit 2 - Regular Expressions and Languages in the subject of Theory of Automata and Formal Languages

Arden's theorem is a result in the study of formal languages that states that every regular language can be represented by a unique minimal deterministic finite automaton (DFA). It is named after its discoverer, Michael Arden, and is an important concept in the study of regular languages and finite automata.

In the Unit 2 - Regular Expressions and Languages of the subject of Theory of Automata and Formal Languages, students learn about the concepts and algorithms involved in regular expressions and languages. The notes for this unit should include the following topics:

1. Definition of Arden's theorem and its importance in the study of regular languages and finite automata
2. Overview of Arden's theorem (representation of regular languages by unique minimal DFA)
3. The process of constructing a minimal DFA from a regular expression (subset construction, state merging)
4. Comparison of Arden's theorem with other results in the study of regular languages and finite automata (minimization of DFA, equivalence of NFA and DFA)
5. Applications of Arden's theorem in the study of regular languages and finite automata (compiler design, language recognition)
