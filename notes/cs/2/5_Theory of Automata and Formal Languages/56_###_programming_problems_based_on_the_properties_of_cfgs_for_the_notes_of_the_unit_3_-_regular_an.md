### Programming problems based on the properties of CFGs for the notes of the Unit 3 - Regular and Non-Regular Grammars in the subject of Theory of Automata and Formal Languages

Programming problems based on the properties of context-free grammars (CFGs) are a type of problem that involves using algorithms to process and manipulate CFGs. CFGs are a type of grammar that are used to describe the structure of formal languages, and are characterized by a set of production rules that define the relationships between symbols in the language.

Programming problems based on the properties of CFGs can involve tasks such as:

1. Generating strings in a language described by a CFG
2. Recognizing strings in a language described by a CFG
3. Constructing a CFG for a given language
4. Converting a CFG to another type of grammar, such as a regular grammar or a pushdown automaton
5. Determining the properties of a CFG, such as its ambiguity and its generative power

In this unit, we will study the concepts of CFGs, and examine the algorithms used to process and manipulate CFGs. We will also study the properties of CFGs, and examine the trade-offs involved in using different algorithms. This will provide a foundation for understanding the design and implementation of algorithms for processing CFGs, and for exploring the various applications of CFGs in the field of automata theory and formal languages.
