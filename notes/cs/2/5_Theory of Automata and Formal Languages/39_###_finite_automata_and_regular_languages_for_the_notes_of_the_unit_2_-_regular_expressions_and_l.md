### Finite Automata and Regular Languages for the notes of the Unit 2 - Regular Expressions and Languages in the subject of Theory of Automata and Formal Languages

Finite Automata and Regular Languages are fundamental concepts in the theory of automata and formal languages. A finite automaton is a mathematical model of computation that can be used to describe and analyze algorithms and computational processes. A regular language is a language that can be described by a regular expression, and can be recognized by a finite automaton.

A finite automaton is defined by a 5-tuple (Q, Σ, δ, q0, F), where:

1. Q is a finite set of states
2. Σ is a finite set of symbols, called the input alphabet
3. δ is a transition function, which maps a state and an input symbol to a new state
4. q0 is the initial state
5. F is a set of final states, which are the states that indicate a successful pattern match

A regular language is a language that can be described by a regular expression, which is a compact way of describing patterns in strings. Regular expressions are used in many applications, including text processing, lexical analysis, and pattern matching.

We will also study the algorithms used to construct finite automata for regular languages, and examine the trade-offs involved in using different algorithms. This will provide a foundation for understanding the relationship between finite automata and regular languages, and for exploring the various applications of finite automata and regular languages in the field of automata theory and formal languages.
