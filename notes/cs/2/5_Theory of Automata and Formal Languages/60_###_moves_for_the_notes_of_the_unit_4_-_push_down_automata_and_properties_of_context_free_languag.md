### Moves for the notes of the Unit 4 - Push Down Automata and Properties of Context Free Languages in the subject of Theory of Automata and Formal Languages

Moves are the basic operations performed by a Push Down Automaton (PDA) during the processing of a string. A move is a transition from one state to another, based on the current state, the current symbol on the input tape, and the symbol on the top of the stack.

There are two types of moves in a PDA:

1. Push moves: These moves add a symbol to the top of the stack.
2. Pop moves: These moves remove a symbol from the top of the stack.

The moves performed by a PDA determine the behavior of the automaton, and the sequence of moves determines the acceptance or rejection of a string. A string is accepted by a PDA if there is a sequence of moves that takes the PDA from the initial state to an accepting state, while consuming the entire input string and leaving the stack empty.

In this unit, we will study the concept of moves in a PDA, and examine the algorithms used to perform moves. We will also study the properties of moves, and examine the trade-offs involved in using different algorithms. This will provide a foundation for understanding the design and implementation of algorithms for processing strings with a PDA, and for exploring the various applications of PDAs in the field of automata theory and formal languages.
