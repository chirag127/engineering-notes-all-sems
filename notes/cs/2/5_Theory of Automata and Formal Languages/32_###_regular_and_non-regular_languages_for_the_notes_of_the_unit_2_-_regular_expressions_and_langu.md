### Regular and Non-Regular Languages for the notes of the Unit 2 - Regular Expressions and Languages in the subject of Theory of Automata and Formal Languages

Unit 2 of the Theory of Automata and Formal Languages course covers the topics of Regular Expressions and Languages. Regular expressions and languages are used to describe and recognize patterns in strings of symbols.

Regular languages are a type of formal language that can be recognized by finite automata. A regular language is a set of strings that can be described by a regular expression, which is a compact representation of a set of strings.

Non-regular languages are a type of formal language that cannot be recognized by finite automata. Non-regular languages are more complex than regular languages, and require more powerful models of computation, such as pushdown automata or Turing machines, to recognize them.

In the context of the Theory of Automata and Formal Languages course, students will learn about regular and non-regular languages as part of Unit 2 - Regular Expressions and Languages. This may include working with regular expressions, writing code to implement finite automata, and analyzing the behavior of finite automata in response to different inputs.

Regular languages are a type of formal language that can be recognized by finite automata, while non-regular languages are more complex and require more powerful models of computation to recognize them.
