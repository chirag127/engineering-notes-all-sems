### Post’s Correspondance Problem for the notes of the Unit 5 - Turing Machines and Recursive Function Theory in the subject of Theory of Automata and Formal Languages

The Post Correspondence Problem (PCP) is a decision problem in the theory of formal languages, which asks whether two sequences of words can be matched in such a way that the concatenation of the matched words is equal to a third word. The PCP was first introduced by Emil Post in the 1940s, and is one of the earliest examples of an undecidable problem.

The PCP is considered to be an important result in the theory of formal languages, as it demonstrates the limits of what can be computed by a Turing Machine. The PCP is also used as a tool for studying the properties of formal languages, and for exploring the relationship between formal languages and computational complexity.

We will also study the properties of the PCP, and examine the trade-offs involved in using different algorithms.
