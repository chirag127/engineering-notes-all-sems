### Typical Real Time Applications

A real-time application (RTA) is an application that has strict time constraints on its operation and performance. It must respond to events or inputs within a specified time frame, otherwise it may fail or cause undesirable consequences. Real-time applications are often used in domains where timeliness, accuracy, and reliability are critical, such as:

- **Process control systems**: These are systems that monitor and control physical processes, such as chemical plants, power plants, oil refineries, etc. They use sensors, actuators, and feedback loops to maintain the desired state of the process variables, such as temperature, pressure, flow, etc. Process control systems must react to changes in the environment or disturbances in the process within a predefined time limit, otherwise they may cause damage, waste, or safety hazards. For example, a nuclear reactor control system must shut down the reactor if it detects a malfunction or an emergency situation within milliseconds, otherwise it may cause a meltdown or an explosion.
- **Machine vision**: This is the field of computer vision that deals with enabling machines to perceive and understand their surroundings using cameras, sensors, and algorithms. Machine vision is used for various purposes, such as object detection, recognition, tracking, classification, measurement, inspection, etc. Machine vision applications must process and analyze the images or videos captured by the cameras in real time, otherwise they may miss important information or make incorrect decisions. For example, a self-driving car must use machine vision to detect and avoid obstacles, pedestrians, traffic signs, etc. on the road within milliseconds, otherwise it may cause accidents or violations.
- **Robotics**: This is the field of engineering that deals with designing, building, and programming machines that can perform tasks autonomously or semi-autonomously. Robotics applications involve various aspects, such as locomotion, manipulation, navigation, communication, perception, learning, etc. Robotics applications must coordinate and control the actions of the robots in real time, otherwise they may fail to achieve their goals or cause harm. For example, a surgical robot must use robotics to perform delicate and precise operations on human patients within microseconds, otherwise it may cause injuries or complications.
- **Video conferencing**: This is a communication technology that allows people to interact with each other using audio and video over the internet or other networks. Video conferencing applications must transmit and receive the audio and video signals in real time, otherwise they may cause delays, distortions, or interruptions. For example, a business meeting using video conferencing must use video conferencing to maintain a smooth and clear conversation among the participants within milliseconds, otherwise it may cause misunderstandings or inefficiencies.
- **Online gaming**: This is a type of gaming that involves playing games over the internet or other networks with other players or computer-generated opponents. Online gaming applications must synchronize and update the game state and actions of the players in real time, otherwise they may cause lags, glitches, or unfairness. For example, a first-person shooter game using online gaming must use online gaming to ensure a fast and accurate response of the game environment and the players within milliseconds, otherwise it may cause frustration or dissatisfaction.
