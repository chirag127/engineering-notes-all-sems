### Internet and Resource Reservation Protocols for the notes of the Unit 4 - Real Time Communication in the subject of Real Time System

Internet and Resource Reservation Protocols (IRRP) are used to support real-time communication in computer networks. IRRP involves reserving network resources such as bandwidth and delay, to ensure that real-time traffic has priority over other types of traffic.

There are two main IRRP protocols:

1. Resource Reservation Protocol (RSVP): RSVP is a signaling protocol used to reserve resources for specific applications in an IP network. It operates at the transport layer and is used to establish a reservation for a specific flow of data.

2. Differentiated Services (DiffServ): DiffServ is a protocol that provides different levels of service for different types of traffic in an IP network. It operates at the network layer and uses a combination of marking, policing, and shaping to ensure that real-time traffic is given priority over other types of traffic.

Both RSVP and DiffServ are used to support real-time communication in IP networks. However, RSVP is typically used for applications that require strict guarantees on bandwidth and delay, while DiffServ is used for applications that require less stringent guarantees.
