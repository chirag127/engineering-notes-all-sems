# Problem Solving Methods

## Introduction

Problem solving is the process of finding a solution to a given situation or task that satisfies some criteria or constraints. Problem solving is one of the main goals of artificial intelligence, as it involves applying knowledge, reasoning, and creativity to achieve a desired outcome.

## Problem Formulation

Problem formulation is the first step of problem solving, where the problem solver defines the initial state, the goal state, and the actions or operators that can change the state. Problem formulation also involves identifying the relevant information and assumptions about the problem domain.

- The initial state is the situation or configuration of the problem at the beginning.
- The goal state is the situation or configuration of the problem that the problem solver wants to reach.
- The actions or operators are the rules or functions that can transform one state into another.
- The problem domain is the set of facts, concepts, and rules that describe the problem and its context.

## Problem Space

Problem space is the set of all possible states that can be reached by applying the actions or operators to the initial state. Problem space can be represented as a graph or a tree, where the nodes are the states and the edges are the actions or operators.

- The problem space can be finite or infinite, depending on the number and complexity of the actions or operators and the states.
- The problem space can be discrete or continuous, depending on the nature of the states and the actions or operators.
- The problem space can be deterministic or stochastic, depending on the certainty or uncertainty of the outcomes of the actions or operators.

## Search Strategies

Search strategies are the methods or algorithms that the problem solver uses to explore the problem space and find a solution. Search strategies can be classified into two main categories: uninformed search and informed search.

- Uninformed search is also called blind search, as it does not use any domain-specific knowledge or heuristic information to guide the search. Uninformed search relies only on the problem formulation and the problem space to find a solution. Examples of uninformed search are breadth-first search, depth-first search, uniform-cost search, and iterative deepening search.
- Informed search is also called heuristic search, as it uses some domain-specific knowledge or heuristic information to guide the search. Informed search relies on a heuristic function that estimates the cost or the distance from the current state to the goal state. Examples of informed search are greedy search, A* search, hill-climbing search, and beam search.

## Problem Characteristics

Problem characteristics are the features or properties of the problem that affect the performance and the suitability of the problem solving methods. Some of the common problem characteristics are:

- Size: the number of states and actions or operators in the problem space.
- Complexity: the difficulty or the intricacy of the problem and its solution.
- Decomposability: the extent to which the problem can be divided into smaller and simpler subproblems.
- Structure: the organization or the pattern of the problem and its solution.
- Constraints: the limitations or the restrictions on the problem and its solution.
- Uncertainty: the degree of unpredictability or variability of the problem and its solution.
