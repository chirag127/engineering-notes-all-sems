# Argumentation among Agents

- Argumentation is a process of reasoning and communication that involves the exchange and evaluation of arguments, which are claims supported by reasons and evidence.
- Argumentation can be used to model the internal reasoning of an agent, as well as the interaction among agents who have different beliefs, goals, preferences, or values.
- Argumentation can help agents to resolve conflicts, persuade others, negotiate agreements, justify decisions, explain actions, and learn from each other.
- Argumentation can also provide a basis for developing formal and computational models of rationality, dialogue, and social intelligence.

## Basic Concepts and Terminology

- An argument is a structured set of statements that consists of one or more premises and a conclusion. A premise is a statement that provides evidence or support for the conclusion. A conclusion is a statement that follows from the premises.
- An argument can be evaluated in terms of its soundness and strength. A sound argument is one that has true premises and a valid inference, meaning that the conclusion necessarily follows from the premises. A strong argument is one that has plausible premises and a cogent inference, meaning that the conclusion is likely to follow from the premises.
- An argument can be attacked by another argument that challenges either its premises or its inference. An attack can be either a rebuttal or an undercut. A rebuttal is an argument that contradicts the conclusion of another argument. An undercut is an argument that undermines the inference of another argument by questioning its validity or cogency.
- An argument can be defended by another argument that supports either its premises or its inference. A defense can be either a reinforcement or a counter-attack. A reinforcement is an argument that strengthens the premise or the inference of another argument by providing additional evidence or support. A counter-attack is an argument that attacks the attacker of another argument by rebutting or undercutting it.
- An argumentation framework is a formal representation of a set of arguments and their relations of attack and defense. An argumentation framework can be used to analyse the acceptability of arguments based on different criteria or semantics, such as grounded, preferred, stable, or ideal.
- An argumentation system is a computational model of argumentation that specifies how arguments are constructed, exchanged, evaluated, and updated by agents. An argumentation system can be based on different logics, languages, protocols, or architectures, depending on the application domain and the objectives of the agents.

## Applications and Challenges

- Argumentation has been applied to various areas of artificial intelligence and multi-agent systems, such as knowledge representation, reasoning, decision making, planning, learning, natural language processing, dialogue, negotiation, persuasion, and explanation.
- Some of the challenges and open problems in argumentation research include:

  - How to model the dynamics and evolution of argumentation over time and across contexts?
  - How to integrate argumentation with other forms of reasoning and communication, such as logic, probability, ontology, and rhetoric?
  - How to design and implement efficient and scalable algorithms and systems for argumentation, especially for large-scale and distributed settings?
  - How to evaluate and compare the performance and effectiveness of different argumentation methods and systems, both theoretically and empirically?
