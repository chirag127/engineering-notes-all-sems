# Characteristics of Intelligent Agents

An intelligent agent is a software entity that enables artificial intelligence to take action. It senses the environment and uses actuators to initiate action and conducts operations in the place of users. It can also learn from the environment and improve its performance. Some examples of intelligent agents are driverless cars, virtual assistants, chatbots, and robots .

Some of the characteristics of intelligent agents are:

- **Autonomy**: The ability of an agent to make decisions and control its actions without human intervention or guidance. An autonomous agent can act independently and flexibly according to its goals and beliefs.
- **Reactivity**: The ability of an agent to perceive and react to environmental changes in order to achieve its goals. A reactive agent can respond appropriately and timely to stimuli from the external world.
- **Proactivity**: The ability of an agent to initiate actions and pursue goals based on its own initiative. A proactive agent can plan ahead and anticipate future situations and opportunities.
- **Sociality**: The ability of an agent to interact and cooperate with other agents or humans in a shared environment. A social agent can communicate, negotiate, coordinate, and collaborate with others to achieve common or individual goals.
- **Learning**: The ability of an agent to improve its performance and adapt to new situations based on its experience and feedback. A learning agent can acquire new knowledge and skills from data, observation, or instruction.
- **Rationality**: The ability of an agent to act in a way that maximizes its expected outcome or utility given its goals and beliefs. A rational agent can choose the best action among the available alternatives based on its knowledge and preferences.

These characteristics can vary in degree and combination depending on the type and function of the agent. There are different types of agents based on their degree of perceived intelligence and capability, such as simple reflex agents, model-based reflex agents, goal-based agents, utility-based agents, and learning agents. Each type of agent has different advantages and limitations in different domains and scenarios.
