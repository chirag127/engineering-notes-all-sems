### Definition for the notes of the Unit 1 - INTRODUCTION in the subject of ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE KCS

- Artificial intelligence (AI) is the **ability of a machine** to perform tasks that **require human intelligence** such as learning, reasoning, perception, decision making, and problem solving  .
- AI can be classified into **two main types**: weak AI and strong AI .
  - Weak AI, also known as **narrow AI**, is the type of AI that can **only perform a specific task** or a set of tasks within a **limited domain**. For example, a chess-playing program, a face recognition system, or a voice assistant are examples of weak AI .
  - Strong AI, also known as **general AI** or **artificial general intelligence (AGI)**, is the type of AI that can **perform any task** that a human can do, and **understand and reason** about any domain. For example, a hypothetical AI that can write novels, compose music, or invent new technologies is an example of strong AI . Strong AI is still a **theoretical goal** and has not been achieved yet .
- AI can also be classified into **three main levels**: artificial narrow intelligence (ANI), artificial general intelligence (AGI), and artificial superintelligence (ASI).
  - Artificial narrow intelligence (ANI) is the **current state** of AI, where machines can **only perform specific tasks** that are **narrowly defined** and **require a limited amount of intelligence**. For example, a spam filter, a self-driving car, or a chatbot are examples of ANI.
  - Artificial general intelligence (AGI) is the **future state** of AI, where machines can **perform any task** that a human can do, and **understand and reason** about any domain. For example, a hypothetical AI that can pass the Turing test, learn from any source of information, or create original works of art are examples of AGI.
  - Artificial superintelligence (ASI) is the **ultimate state** of AI, where machines can **surpass human intelligence** and **perform any task** that is **beyond human capabilities**. For example, a hypothetical AI that can solve any problem, invent new sciences, or control the world are examples of ASI. ASI is still a **speculative concept** and has not been achieved yet.
- AI can be applied to **various fields** and **industries** such as finance, healthcare, education, entertainment, security, and more  . Some of the **benefits** of AI include improving efficiency, accuracy, productivity, innovation, and quality of life  . Some of the **challenges** of AI include ethical, social, legal, and technical issues such as privacy, bias, accountability, security, and human-AI interaction  .
