## Unit 3 - KNOWLEDGE REPRESENTATION

- Knowledge representation is the process of encoding and storing information in a form that can be used by an intelligent system, such as an AI agent.
- Knowledge representation aims to capture the meaning and structure of the information, as well as the relationships and constraints among the entities and concepts involved.
- Knowledge representation also enables reasoning, inference, and problem-solving based on the available information and the goals of the system.
- Knowledge representation can be divided into two main categories: declarative and procedural.
  - Declarative knowledge representation focuses on what is true or false in the world, and uses logical statements, facts, rules, and axioms to express the information.
  - Procedural knowledge representation focuses on how to do something or achieve a goal, and uses algorithms, procedures, methods, and heuristics to express the information.
- Some of the common methods and techniques for knowledge representation are:
  - Propositional logic: a simple and expressive form of logic that uses atomic propositions and logical connectives to form complex sentences.
  - Predicate logic: an extension of propositional logic that allows the use of variables, quantifiers, and predicates to represent relations and properties of objects.
  - Semantic networks: a graphical representation of concepts and their relations using nodes and links, where nodes represent entities or concepts, and links represent relations or attributes.
  - Frames: a hierarchical representation of concepts and their properties using slots and values, where slots represent attributes or features, and values represent data or pointers to other frames.
  - Scripts: a representation of stereotypical situations and events using a sequence of actions and roles, where actions represent the steps or activities, and roles represent the participants or objects involved.
  - Production rules: a representation of conditional actions using IF-THEN statements, where IF part represents the condition or premise, and THEN part represents the action or conclusion.
  - Ontologies: a representation of a domain of knowledge using a set of concepts, relations, and axioms, where concepts represent classes or categories of objects, relations represent the connections or associations among concepts, and axioms represent the constraints or rules that govern the domain.
