### Typical Intelligent Agents

- An intelligent agent (IA) is an entity that makes a decision, that enables artificial intelligence to be put into action.
- It can also be described as a software entity that conducts operations in the place of users or programs after sensing the environment.
- It uses sensors to perceive the environment and actuators to initiate action in that environment.
- In addition, intelligent agents may learn from the environment to achieve their goals.
- Intelligent agents are often described schematically as an abstract functional system similar to a computer program.
- Abstract descriptions of intelligent agents are called abstract intelligent agents (AIA) to distinguish them from their real-world implementations.
- AI assistants, like Alexa and Siri, are examples of intelligent agents as they use sensors to perceive a request made by the user and the automatically collect data from the internet without the user's help.
- Driverless cars are another example of intelligent agents as they use sensors to perceive the road conditions and the traffic and actuators to control the speed and direction of the vehicle.
- There are different types of intelligent agents based on their degree of rationality, autonomy, learning ability and adaptability.
- Some of the common types of intelligent agents are:
  - Simple reflex agents: These agents act based on the current state of the environment and do not consider the history or the future consequences of their actions. They are the simplest and the least intelligent type of agents.
  - Model-based reflex agents: These agents maintain an internal model of the environment and use it to choose the best action based on the current state and the expected outcome. They are more intelligent than simple reflex agents as they can handle partially observable and dynamic environments.
  - Goal-based agents: These agents have a predefined goal or set of goals that they try to achieve by taking actions. They evaluate the actions based on how well they satisfy the goal. They are more intelligent than model-based reflex agents as they can handle complex and uncertain environments.
  - Utility-based agents: These agents have a utility function that measures how desirable a state is. They choose the action that maximizes the expected utility. They are more intelligent than goal-based agents as they can handle conflicting and multiple goals.
  - Learning agents: These agents have the ability to learn from their experience and improve their performance over time. They consist of four components: a learning element, a performance element, a critic element and a problem generator element. They are the most intelligent type of agents as they can adapt to changing environments and goals.
