## Unit 2 - PROBLEM SOLVING METHODS

- Problem solving is the process of finding solutions to difficult or complex issues.
- Problem solving methods are strategies or techniques that can be used to solve problems effectively and efficiently.
- Some common problem solving methods are:

  - **Trial and error**: Trying different solutions until finding one that works or eliminates the problem.
  - **Algorithm**: Following a set of rules or steps that guarantee a correct solution or outcome.
  - **Heuristic**: Using a rule of thumb or a shortcut that may not guarantee a correct solution but can help narrow down the search space or reduce the complexity of the problem.
  - **Divide and conquer**: Breaking down a large or complex problem into smaller and simpler subproblems that can be solved separately and then combined to form the final solution.
  - **Backtracking**: Going back to a previous state or decision point and trying a different option or path when the current one leads to a dead end or an unsatisfactory solution.
  - **Recursion**: Solving a problem by using the solution of a smaller or simpler version of the same problem as a base case or a building block.
  - **Brute force**: Trying all possible solutions or combinations until finding the optimal or desired one.
  - **Greedy**: Choosing the best or most promising option at each step or stage without considering the long-term consequences or the global optimum.
  - **Dynamic programming**: Storing and reusing the solutions of overlapping subproblems to avoid recomputation and improve efficiency.
  - **Branch and bound**: Pruning or discarding the solutions or branches that are not feasible or optimal based on some criteria or bounds.
