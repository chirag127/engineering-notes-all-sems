### Search Strategies

- A search strategy is a method for finding a solution to a problem by exploring the space of possible states and actions.
- A search strategy consists of two components: a search algorithm and a search heuristic.
- A search algorithm is a procedure that defines how to generate and expand the nodes of a search tree or graph.
- A search heuristic is a function that estimates the cost or quality of a node or a path in the search tree or graph.
- A search strategy can be classified as uninformed or informed, depending on whether it uses a search heuristic or not.
- Uninformed search strategies are also called blind search strategies, because they do not have any domain-specific knowledge to guide the search.
- Informed search strategies are also called heuristic search strategies, because they use a search heuristic to evaluate and prioritize the nodes or paths in the search tree or graph.
- Some examples of uninformed search strategies are breadth-first search, depth-first search, uniform-cost search, and iterative deepening search.
- Some examples of informed search strategies are greedy search, A* search, hill-climbing search, and beam search.
