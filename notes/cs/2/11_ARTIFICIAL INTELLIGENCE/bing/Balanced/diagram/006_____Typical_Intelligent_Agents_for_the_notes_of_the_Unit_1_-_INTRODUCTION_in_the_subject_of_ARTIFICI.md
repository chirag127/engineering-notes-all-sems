### Typical Intelligent Agents

- An **intelligent agent** is an autonomous entity that acts upon an environment using sensors and actuators to achieve its goals .
- An intelligent agent may also learn from the environment to improve its performance.
- An intelligent agent can be described as a software entity that conducts operations in the place of users or programs after sensing the environment.
- An intelligent agent can also be a physical entity, such as a robot or a self-driving car.
- An intelligent agent is closely related to agents in economics, cognitive science, ethics, and philosophy.
- An intelligent agent can be classified into different types based on its characteristics, such as:
  - **Simple reflex agents**: These agents act based on the current percept, without considering the history or the future consequences. They are the simplest and the most limited type of agents. For example, a thermostat that adjusts the temperature based on the current reading.
  - **Model-based reflex agents**: These agents act based on the current percept and a model of the world that they maintain. They can handle partially observable environments and keep track of the changes in the environment. For example, a self-driving car that uses a map and sensors to navigate the road.
  - **Goal-based agents**: These agents act based on the current percept, a model of the world, and a goal that they want to achieve. They can plan ahead and choose the best action that leads to the goal. For example, a chess-playing program that tries to win the game.
  - **Utility-based agents**: These agents act based on the current percept, a model of the world, a goal, and a utility function that measures how desirable a state is. They can handle uncertain and complex environments and choose the action that maximizes the expected utility. For example, a stock-trading program that tries to maximize the profit.
  - **Learning agents**: These agents act based on the current percept, a model of the world, a goal, a utility function, and a learning component that improves the agent's performance over time. They can adapt to changing environments and learn from their own experience and feedback. For example, a Siri virtual assistant that learns from the user's preferences and behavior .
