### Architecture for Intelligent Agents

1. **Reactive Architecture:** This architecture is based on the concept of stimulus-response, where the agent responds to changes in the environment without any internal representation or reasoning. The agent's behavior is determined by a set of pre-defined rules or condition-action pairs.

2. **Belief-Desire-Intention (BDI) Architecture:** This architecture is based on the concept of mental attitudes, where the agent has beliefs about the world, desires or goals to achieve, and intentions or plans to achieve those goals. The agent's behavior is determined by its beliefs, desires, and intentions, and it can reason about its actions and the environment to achieve its goals.

3. **Layered Architecture:** This architecture is based on the concept of hierarchical organization, where the agent's behavior is determined by a set of layers, each responsible for a different level of abstraction. The lower layers are responsible for reactive behavior, while the higher layers are responsible for more complex reasoning and planning.

4. **Hybrid Architecture:** This architecture combines elements of reactive, BDI, and layered architectures to achieve a balance between reactivity and reasoning. The agent's behavior is determined by a combination of pre-defined rules, mental attitudes, and hierarchical organization.

These are some of the common architectures used for designing intelligent agents in the field of artificial intelligence. Each architecture has its own strengths and weaknesses, and the choice of architecture depends on the specific requirements of the agent and the environment in which it operates.
