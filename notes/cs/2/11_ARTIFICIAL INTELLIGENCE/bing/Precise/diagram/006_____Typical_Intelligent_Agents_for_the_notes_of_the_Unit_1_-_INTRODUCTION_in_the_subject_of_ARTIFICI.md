### Typical Intelligent Agents

An intelligent agent is a system that can perceive its environment, reason about it, and take actions to achieve its goals. There are several types of intelligent agents, including:

1. **Simple reflex agents**: These agents select actions based on the current percept, ignoring the rest of the percept history. They are based on condition-action rules, where the condition part matches the current percept and the action part specifies the action to be taken.

2. **Model-based reflex agents**: These agents maintain an internal model of the world, which is used to choose actions based on the current percept and the history of past percepts. The internal model is updated based on the percept history and the agent's actions.

3. **Goal-based agents**: These agents choose actions based on the desirability of their outcomes, as determined by a goal or set of goals. They use a process called goal formulation to generate specific goals based on high-level desires.

4. **Utility-based agents**: These agents choose actions based on the expected utility of their outcomes, where utility is a measure of the desirability of a state. They use a utility function to assign a numerical value to each possible state, and choose the action that leads to the state with the highest expected utility.

5. **Learning agents**: These agents can improve their performance over time by learning from their experiences. They use various learning algorithms to update their internal models, utility functions, or action-selection mechanisms based on the feedback they receive from the environment.

These are some of the typical intelligent agents that can be found in the field of artificial intelligence. Each type of agent has its own strengths and weaknesses, and the choice of agent depends on the specific problem at hand.
