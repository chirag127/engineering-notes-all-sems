### Backward Chaining

Backward chaining is a method of reasoning used in artificial intelligence and is often used in expert systems. It is a technique used in knowledge representation, which is a topic covered in Unit 3 of the subject of Artificial Intelligence KCS.

Here are some key points to note about backward chaining:

1. Backward chaining starts with the goal or conclusion and works backward to determine what facts or evidence would support that conclusion.
2. It is a form of deductive reasoning, where the conclusion is known and the premises are sought.
3. Backward chaining is often used in rule-based systems, where the rules are represented as if-then statements.
4. The system attempts to match the goal with the consequent of a rule, and if a match is found, the antecedent of the rule becomes the new goal.
5. This process is repeated until either the initial facts are reached or no further progress can be made.
6. Backward chaining is useful in situations where there are many possible solutions and the goal is to find one that is supported by the available evidence.
7. It is also useful when the number of rules is large and it is not practical to evaluate all of them in a forward chaining manner.
