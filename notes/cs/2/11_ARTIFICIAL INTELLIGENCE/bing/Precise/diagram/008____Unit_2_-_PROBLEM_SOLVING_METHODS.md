## Unit 2 - PROBLEM SOLVING METHODS

Problem solving is the process of finding a solution to an issue by using a systematic approach. There are several methods that can be used to solve problems, including:

1. **Trial and Error:** This method involves trying different solutions until the problem is solved. It is often used when there is no clear solution or when the problem is not well understood.

2. **Algorithm:** An algorithm is a step-by-step procedure for solving a problem. It is often used when the problem can be broken down into smaller, more manageable parts.

3. **Heuristics:** Heuristics are rules of thumb or mental shortcuts that can be used to quickly find a solution to a problem. They are often used when time is of the essence or when a perfect solution is not necessary.

4. **Divide and Conquer:** This method involves breaking the problem down into smaller, more manageable parts and solving each part separately. It is often used when the problem is complex and difficult to solve as a whole.

5. **Backtracking:** Backtracking involves retracing your steps to find a solution to a problem. It is often used when the solution to a problem is not immediately obvious.

6. **Brainstorming:** Brainstorming involves generating a large number of ideas in a short amount of time. It is often used when creativity is required to solve a problem.

7. **Root Cause Analysis:** Root cause analysis involves identifying the underlying cause of a problem and addressing it. It is often used when the problem is recurring or when a long-term solution is required.

These are some of the common problem-solving methods that can be used to find a solution to an issue. The method chosen will depend on the nature of the problem and the desired outcome. It is important to remember that problem-solving is a skill that can be developed and improved over time.
