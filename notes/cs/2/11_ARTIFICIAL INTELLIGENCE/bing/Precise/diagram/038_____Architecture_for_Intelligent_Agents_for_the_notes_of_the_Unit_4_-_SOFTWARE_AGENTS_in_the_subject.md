### Architecture for Intelligent Agents

1. **Reactive Architecture:** This architecture is based on the concept of stimulus-response. The agent responds to changes in the environment without considering the history of its interactions. This architecture is simple and fast, but it lacks the ability to plan and reason.

2. **Belief-Desire-Intention (BDI) Architecture:** This architecture is based on the concept of mental attitudes. The agent has beliefs about the world, desires or goals it wants to achieve, and intentions or plans to achieve those goals. This architecture allows the agent to reason and plan, but it can be slower than reactive architecture.

3. **Layered Architecture:** This architecture combines the strengths of reactive and BDI architectures. The agent has multiple layers, with the lower layers being reactive and the higher layers being deliberative. This allows the agent to respond quickly to changes in the environment while still being able to reason and plan.

4. **Hybrid Architecture:** This architecture combines multiple architectures to create a more flexible and robust agent. For example, an agent may have a reactive layer for fast responses, a BDI layer for reasoning and planning, and a learning layer for adapting to new situations.

These are some of the common architectures used in the design of intelligent agents. Each architecture has its strengths and weaknesses, and the choice of architecture depends on the specific requirements of the agent and its environment.
