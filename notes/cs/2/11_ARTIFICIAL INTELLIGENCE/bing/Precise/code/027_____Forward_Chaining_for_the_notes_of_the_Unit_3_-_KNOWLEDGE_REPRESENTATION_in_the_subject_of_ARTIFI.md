### Forward Chaining

Forward chaining is a method of reasoning in artificial intelligence where the inference engine starts with the available data and uses inference rules to extract more data until a goal is reached. It is one of the two main methods of reasoning when using an inference engine and can be described logically as repeated application of modus ponens.

Here are some key points to remember about forward chaining:

1. Forward chaining is a data-driven approach, meaning it starts with the available data and uses it to draw conclusions.
2. It is used in rule-based expert systems to simulate the human reasoning process.
3. The inference engine iteratively applies the rules to the available data until a goal is reached or no more conclusions can be drawn.
4. Forward chaining is also known as bottom-up reasoning, as it starts with the available data and works its way up to higher-level conclusions.
5. It is often used in diagnostic systems, where the available data is used to determine the cause of a problem.
