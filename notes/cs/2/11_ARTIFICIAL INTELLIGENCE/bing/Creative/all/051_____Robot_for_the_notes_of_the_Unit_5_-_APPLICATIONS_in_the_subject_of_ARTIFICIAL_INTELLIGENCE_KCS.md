# Unit 5 - APPLICATIONS

## Robot

- A robot is a machine that can perform tasks autonomously or semi-autonomously, often with the help of sensors, actuators, and controllers.
- Robotics is the branch of engineering and science that deals with the design, construction, operation, and application of robots.
- Artificial intelligence (AI) is the field of computer science that aims to create machines and systems that can exhibit intelligent behavior, such as learning, reasoning, planning, perception, and decision making.
- AI can be applied to robotics to enhance the capabilities and performance of robots, especially in complex, dynamic, and uncertain environments.

### Examples of AI in Robotics

- Self-driving cars: These are vehicles that can navigate roads and traffic without human intervention, using AI techniques such as computer vision, deep learning, sensor fusion, and path planning.
- Autonomous drones: These are aerial robots that can fly and perform tasks without human control, using AI techniques such as image recognition, obstacle avoidance, and swarm intelligence.
- Humanoid robots: These are robots that resemble and mimic human appearance and behavior, using AI techniques such as natural language processing, facial expression recognition, and emotion detection.
- Autonomous farming equipment: These are robots that can perform agricultural tasks such as planting, harvesting, and spraying, using AI techniques such as machine vision, GPS, and data analysis.

### Benefits of AI in Robotics

- AI can enable robots to perform tasks that are too dangerous, tedious, or complex for humans, such as bomb disposal, space exploration, and surgery.
- AI can improve the efficiency, accuracy, and productivity of robots, by allowing them to adapt to changing situations, learn from experience, and optimize their actions.
- AI can enhance the interaction and collaboration between robots and humans, by enabling robots to communicate, understand, and assist humans in natural and intuitive ways.

### Challenges of AI in Robotics

- AI can pose ethical, social, and legal issues, such as the responsibility, accountability, and transparency of robot actions and decisions, the impact of robot displacement on human employment and welfare, and the potential misuse or abuse of robot power and intelligence.
- AI can face technical and practical limitations, such as the complexity, uncertainty, and unpredictability of real-world environments, the scalability, robustness, and security of AI systems, and the availability, quality, and privacy of data and resources.
