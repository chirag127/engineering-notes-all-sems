# Problem Solving Methods

- Problem solving is the process of finding a solution to a given situation or goal, given some initial conditions and constraints.
- Problem solving methods are general strategies or techniques that can be applied to different types of problems, such as search, planning, optimization, constraint satisfaction, etc.
- Problem solving methods can be classified into two categories: **knowledge-based** and **search-based**.

## Knowledge-based methods

- Knowledge-based methods use domain-specific information or rules to guide the problem solving process, such as heuristics, expert systems, logic, etc.
- Knowledge-based methods can reduce the search space and increase the efficiency of problem solving, but they require a lot of domain knowledge and may not be applicable to all problems.
- Some examples of knowledge-based methods are:

  - **Heuristic methods**: use rules of thumb or shortcuts to find approximate solutions, such as hill climbing, best-first search, A* search, etc.
  - **Expert systems**: use a knowledge base of facts and rules to infer solutions, such as MYCIN, DENDRAL, etc.
  - **Logic methods**: use formal systems of reasoning to deduce solutions, such as propositional logic, first-order logic, etc.

## Search-based methods

- Search-based methods use a systematic or random exploration of the search space to find solutions, such as brute-force, backtracking, genetic algorithms, etc.
- Search-based methods can be applied to any problem that can be formulated as a state space, but they may require a lot of computational resources and may not guarantee optimality or completeness.
- Some examples of search-based methods are:

  - **Brute-force methods**: try all possible solutions until a goal is found, such as depth-first search, breadth-first search, etc.
  - **Backtracking methods**: try partial solutions and backtrack if a dead end is reached, such as recursive backtracking, branch and bound, etc.
  - **Genetic algorithms**: use a population of candidate solutions that evolve through selection, crossover, and mutation, such as genetic programming, evolutionary strategies, etc.
