# Trust and Reputation in Multi-Agent Systems

- Trust and reputation are important concepts for effective interactions in open multi-agent systems (MAS) where agents can cooperate, coordinate, and negotiate with each other.
- Trust is the subjective belief that an agent has about the reliability, honesty, and competence of another agent based on direct or indirect experiences.
- Reputation is the collective opinion that a group of agents has about the trustworthiness of another agent based on observed or reported behaviors.
- Trust and reputation can help agents to select suitable partners, avoid malicious or incompetent agents, and cope with uncertainty and risk in dynamic and complex environments.
- Several models and mechanisms have been proposed to represent, compute, and update trust and reputation in MAS, based on different assumptions, criteria, and methods.
- Some of the well-known models and mechanisms are:
  - Marsh's model of trust: A formal model that defines trust as a function of basic, general, and situational trust, and uses utility theory to evaluate the outcomes of trusting actions.
  - Sen et al.'s model of trust and reputation: A computational model that combines direct trust (based on personal interactions), witness trust (based on third-party reports), and system trust (based on global reputation) to assess the trustworthiness of agents.
  - SPORAS: A reputation system that uses a scoring function to assign reputation values to agents based on their past performance, and updates the values using a forgetting factor to reflect the recency of information.
  - eBay's feedback system: A reputation system that allows buyers and sellers to rate each other after each transaction, and displays the aggregated ratings as feedback scores and percentages to indicate the reputation of agents.
  - Certified reputation: A reputation system that uses cryptographic techniques to ensure the authenticity, integrity, and non-repudiation of reputation information, and prevents malicious agents from tampering with or fabricating reputation data.
