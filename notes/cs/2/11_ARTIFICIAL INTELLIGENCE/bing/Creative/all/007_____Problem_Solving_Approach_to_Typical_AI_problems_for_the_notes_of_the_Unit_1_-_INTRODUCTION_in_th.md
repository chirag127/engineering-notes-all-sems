# Problem Solving Approach to Typical AI Problems

## Introduction
- Artificial intelligence (AI) is the study of how to create machines or software that can perform tasks that require human intelligence, such as reasoning, learning, planning, decision making, natural language processing, computer vision, etc.
- AI problems are typically complex, ill-defined, uncertain, and dynamic, which means they cannot be solved by simple algorithms or rules.
- AI problems require a problem solving approach that involves the following steps:
  - Problem formulation: defining the goal, the initial state, the actions, and the constraints of the problem.
  - Search: finding a sequence of actions that leads from the initial state to the goal state, using some search strategy and heuristic function.
  - Execution: applying the actions to the real world or a simulation, and monitoring the results and feedback.
  - Evaluation: assessing the performance and quality of the solution, and learning from the experience.

## Examples of Typical AI Problems
- Some examples of typical AI problems are:
  - Chess: the goal is to checkmate the opponent's king, the initial state is the board configuration, the actions are the legal moves, and the constraints are the rules of chess.
  - Route planning: the goal is to find the shortest or fastest path from a source to a destination, the initial state is the source location, the actions are the possible moves, and the constraints are the road network and traffic conditions.
  - Face recognition: the goal is to identify a person from an image, the initial state is the image, the actions are the feature extraction and matching, and the constraints are the database of faces and the accuracy threshold.
  - Machine translation: the goal is to translate a text from one language to another, the initial state is the source text, the actions are the parsing, lexical, syntactic, and semantic analysis, and the constraints are the grammar and vocabulary of the target language.

## Problem Solving Methods
- Some common problem solving methods in AI are:
  - Search methods: these methods use a search space, which is a representation of all the possible states and actions of the problem, and a search strategy, which is a way of choosing which state to explore next. Some examples of search methods are:
    - Uninformed search: these methods do not use any domain-specific knowledge or heuristic function, and rely on brute-force exploration. Some examples are breadth-first search, depth-first search, uniform-cost search, etc.
    - Informed search: these methods use a heuristic function, which is a way of estimating the cost or distance to the goal from a given state, and guide the search towards the most promising states. Some examples are greedy search, A* search, hill-climbing, etc.
    - Local search: these methods do not keep track of the whole search space, but only focus on the current state and its neighbors, and try to find a local optimum or a satisfactory solution. Some examples are simulated annealing, genetic algorithms, tabu search, etc.
  - Constraint satisfaction methods: these methods use a set of variables, each with a domain of possible values, and a set of constraints, which are rules or conditions that must be satisfied by the values of the variables. The goal is to find an assignment of values to the variables that satisfies all the constraints. Some examples of constraint satisfaction methods are backtracking, forward checking, arc consistency, etc.
  - Logic methods: these methods use a formal language, such as propositional logic, first-order logic, or modal logic, to represent the knowledge and reasoning of the problem. The goal is to find a logical proof or a model that supports a given query or hypothesis. Some examples of logic methods are resolution, natural deduction, model checking, etc.
  - Planning methods: these methods use a representation of the actions, the preconditions, and the effects of the actions, and a representation of the initial state and the goal state of the problem. The goal is to find a plan, which is a sequence of actions that transforms the initial state into the goal state. Some examples of planning methods are STRIPS, partial-order planning, hierarchical task network planning, etc.
  - Machine learning methods: these methods use data, such as examples, observations, or feedback, to learn a function, a model, a rule, or a policy that can perform the task or improve the performance of the task.
