### Hardware for Artificial Intelligence

Hardware for artificial intelligence (AI) refers to the specialized computer hardware that is used to execute AI programs faster and with less energy. Hardware for AI can be classified into four main types:

- **CPU** – Central Processing Units are the general-purpose processors that can run various types of instructions. CPUs are good for sequential and logical tasks, but they have limited parallelism and memory bandwidth. CPUs are usually combined with other types of hardware for AI to achieve better performance and efficiency.
- **GPU** – Graphics Processing Units are chips designed to speed up multidimensional data processing, such as images, videos, and matrices. GPUs have thousands of smaller cores that can work independently on a subspace of input data that requires heavy computing. GPUs are good for parallel and numerical tasks, but they have high power consumption and programming complexity. GPUs are the most popular and commonly available hardware for AI systems developed in workstations.
- **FPGA** – Field Programmable Gate Arrays are chips that can be configured to implement any logic function. FPGAs have high flexibility and reconfigurability, which allows them to adapt to different AI algorithms and applications. FPGAs are good for custom and low-latency tasks, but they have low density and high cost. FPGAs are often used as accelerators or co-processors for AI systems.
- **ASIC** – Application Specific Integrated Circuits are chips that are designed and optimized for a specific AI function. ASICs have high performance and efficiency, which enables them to handle large-scale and complex AI workloads. ASICs are good for specialized and high-throughput tasks, but they have low flexibility and high development time. ASICs are usually deployed in cloud or edge AI systems.

Some examples of AI hardware are:

- **Lisp machines** – These are computers that are designed to run Lisp, a programming language that is widely used for AI research and development. Lisp machines have features such as garbage collection, dynamic typing, and symbolic manipulation that facilitate AI programming. Lisp machines were popular in the 1980s, but they were replaced by cheaper and faster general-purpose computers.
- **Neuromorphic engineering** – This is a field that aims to create hardware that mimics the structure and function of biological neural networks. Neuromorphic engineering uses devices such as memristors, spintronics, and photonics to implement artificial neurons and synapses. Neuromorphic engineering can enable AI hardware that is more adaptive, scalable, and energy-efficient .
- **Event cameras** – These are cameras that capture changes in brightness instead of frames. Event cameras can provide high temporal resolution, low latency, and high dynamic range for AI applications that require fast and robust visual processing. Event cameras can enable AI hardware that is more responsive, robust, and efficient.
- **Physical neural networks** – These are networks that use physical phenomena such as light, sound, or fluid dynamics to perform computation. Physical neural networks can exploit the inherent parallelism and nonlinearity of physical systems to implement AI functions. Physical neural networks can enable AI hardware that is more scalable, analog, and novel .
