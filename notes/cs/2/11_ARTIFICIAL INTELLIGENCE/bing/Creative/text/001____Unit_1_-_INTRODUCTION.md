## Unit 1 - INTRODUCTION

- This unit introduces the basic concepts and principles of artificial intelligence (AI).
- AI is the study of how to create machines and systems that can perform tasks that normally require human intelligence, such as reasoning, learning, perception, decision making, and natural language processing.
- AI can be divided into two main branches: symbolic AI and sub-symbolic AI.
- Symbolic AI uses logic, rules, and symbols to represent and manipulate knowledge. Examples of symbolic AI include expert systems, knowledge bases, and logic programming.
- Sub-symbolic AI uses numerical and statistical methods to model and learn from data. Examples of sub-symbolic AI include neural networks, evolutionary algorithms, and reinforcement learning.
- AI can also be classified into different types based on the level of intelligence and the domain of application. Some common types are:
  - Narrow AI: AI that can perform a specific task or function well, but lacks general intelligence or common sense. Examples of narrow AI include speech recognition, face detection, and chess playing.
  - General AI: AI that can perform any intellectual task that a human can, and has human-like reasoning and understanding. Examples of general AI include HAL 9000 from 2001: A Space Odyssey and Data from Star Trek.
  - Super AI: AI that surpasses human intelligence and capabilities in all aspects, and can create and improve itself. Examples of super AI include Skynet from The Terminator and Ultron from The Avengers.
  - Weak AI: AI that only simulates intelligence or behavior, but does not have any understanding or consciousness. Examples of weak AI include chatbots, virtual assistants, and video game characters.
  - Strong AI: AI that has true intelligence and consciousness, and can experience emotions and self-awareness. Examples of strong AI include Samantha from Her and Ava from Ex Machina.
  - Applied AI: AI that is designed for a specific domain or problem, such as medicine, finance, or education. Examples of applied AI include diagnosis systems, recommendation systems, and tutoring systems.
  - Artificial Neural Networks: AI that is inspired by the structure and function of biological neural networks, such as the brain. Examples of artificial neural networks include deep learning, convolutional neural networks, and recurrent neural networks.
