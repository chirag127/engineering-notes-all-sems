## Unit 1 - INTRODUCTION

- This unit introduces the basic concepts and principles of artificial intelligence (AI), which is the study of how to create machines and software that can perform tasks that require human intelligence.
- AI is a broad and interdisciplinary field that draws from computer science, mathematics, logic, psychology, philosophy, linguistics, neuroscience, and other disciplines.
- AI can be divided into two main branches: symbolic AI and sub-symbolic AI.
  - Symbolic AI is based on the idea that intelligent behavior can be achieved by manipulating symbols and rules that represent knowledge and reasoning. Examples of symbolic AI include expert systems, logic programming, natural language processing, and knowledge representation and reasoning.
  - Sub-symbolic AI is based on the idea that intelligent behavior can be achieved by modeling the low-level processes and structures that underlie natural intelligence, such as neural networks, evolutionary algorithms, fuzzy logic, and machine learning.
- AI can also be classified into different types according to the goals and tasks involved, such as:
  - Artificial narrow intelligence (ANI), which is the ability to perform a specific task or domain at or above human level, such as playing chess, recognizing faces, or driving a car.
  - Artificial general intelligence (AGI), which is the ability to perform any intellectual task that a human can do, such as understanding natural language, solving problems, learning from experience, and exhibiting common sense.
  - Artificial superintelligence (ASI), which is the ability to surpass human intelligence in all aspects, such as creativity, wisdom, and social skills.
- AI has many applications and benefits for various domains and industries, such as education, health care, entertainment, business, security, and science. However, AI also poses some challenges and risks, such as ethical, social, legal, and technical issues, that need to be addressed and regulated.
