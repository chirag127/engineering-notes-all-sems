### Backward Chaining for the notes of the Unit 3 - KNOWLEDGE REPRESENTATION in the subject of ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE KCS

- Backward Chaining is a type of inference algorithm that is used in artificial intelligence to deduce the cause of a problem or an effect.
- It is a goal-driven approach where the reasoning begins with the desired outcome, and then works backward to find the solutions.
- In other words, backward chaining starts with the goal and then tries to find the evidence that leads to the goal.
- It is considered as the opposite of forward chaining, which is a data-driven approach that starts with the given information and then derives the conclusion or outcome.
- The backward chaining process starts with a hypothesis and then checks whether it is true or not by looking at the available evidence.
- If the hypothesis is not true, then it will try to find other possible explanations that can lead to the desired outcome.
- The backward chaining algorithm is widely used in expert systems, diagnostic systems, and other AI applications that require reasoning and decision-making.
- The main advantage of backward chaining is that it can handle complex problems and uncertainty more effectively than forward chaining.
- The backward chaining algorithm can be implemented using several techniques such as resolution, semantic tree, and rule-based reasoning.
- In resolution-based backward chaining, the algorithm tries to derive the goal by resolving the negation of the goal with the given knowledge base.
- In semantic tree-based backward chaining, the algorithm constructs a tree of possible solutions by examining the knowledge base and the goal.
- In rule-based reasoning, the algorithm uses a set of rules to deduce the cause of the problem and then work backward to find the solution.
