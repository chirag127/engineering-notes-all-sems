### Resolution for the notes of the Unit 3 - KNOWLEDGE REPRESENTATION in the subject of ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE KCS

1. Knowledge Representation:
- Representing knowledge in a computer-interpretable form is known as Knowledge Representation (KR).
- The goal of KR is to facilitate inferencing, problem solving, and learning.
- There are two approaches to KR:
-- Declarative: Facts about the world are explicitly stated. E.g. logical statements, rules, semantic networks.
-- Procedural: Knowledge is encoded as sequences of steps to achieve a goal. E.g. programs, recipes.

2. Logical Representation:
- Logics provide a formal language to make precise statements about the world.
- The most common logics for KR are:
-- Propositional Logic: Makes statements about propositions which are either true or false. Uses logical connectives like AND, OR, NOT, IF-THEN.
-- First-Order Logic (FOL): Allows statements about objects, their properties and relations. Uses variables, quantifiers, predicates, functions.
- Logics are useful for precise yet flexible representation. However, they can be unintuitive and inefficient for large knowledge bases.

3. Frames:
- A frame is a data structure for representing a stereotypical scenario. It includes slots for attributes of the concept and associated values and rules.
- Frames are easy to understand but incomplete and lack formality. They are suitable for simple domains with typical scenarios.
