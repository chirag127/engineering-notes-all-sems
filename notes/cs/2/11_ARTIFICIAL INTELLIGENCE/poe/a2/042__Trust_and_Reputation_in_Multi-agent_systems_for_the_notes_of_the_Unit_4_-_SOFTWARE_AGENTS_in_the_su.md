### Trust and Reputation in Multi-agent systems

1. Trust: Belief that an agent will behave in a predictable and reliable manner. It is a subjective probability by which an agent expects another agent to perform a particular action, both before he can monitor such an action (or independently of his capacity ever to be able to monitor it) and in a context in which it affects his own action.
2. Reputation: The perception that an agent creates through past actions about its intentions and norms. Reputation is the collective assessment of an agent's trustworthiness based on the reports of others.
3. Importance of Trust and Reputation:
    - In open systems where agents may not know each other, trust and reputation are crucial for cooperation.
    - Reputation systems incentivize trustworthy behavior and discourage malicious behavior.
    - Trust and reputation enable delegation - an agent can rely on the assessment of other agents about a third agent.
4. Challenges:
    - Subjectivity: Different agents may have different opinions about an agent's trustworthiness.
    - Vulnerability to False Information: Reputation systems are vulnerable to false ratings and reports from malicious agents.
    - Free-rider problem: Agents can get benefits from the system without contributing ratings or reports.
    - Computation and Storage overhead: Tracking and computing trust and reputation scores for all agents can be expensive.
5. Approaches to Trust and Reputation modeling:
    - Binary trust models: Simple models that represent trust as just two values - trusted or distrusted.
    - Numeric trust models: More complex models that represent trust as a numeric value. Eg. Bayesian models.
    - Fuzzy trust models: Represent trust as a matter of degree using fuzzy logic.
    - Probabilistic trust models: Represent trust as a probability of trustworthy behavior.
