### Microprocessor evolution and types

- A microprocessor is an electronic device that performs arithmetic and logic operations on digital data. It is the brain of a computer system.
- The evolution of microprocessor can be divided into five generations, based on the technology, architecture, and performance of the devices.
- The first generation microprocessors were introduced in 1971-1972. They were 4-bit devices that could process only one instruction at a time. They had low speed, low memory, and limited applications. Examples are Intel 4004 and Intel 8008 .
- The second generation microprocessors were introduced in 1973-1978. They were 8-bit devices that could process multiple instructions at a time. They had higher speed, larger memory, and wider applications. Examples are Intel 8080, Motorola 6800, and Zilog Z80.
- The third generation microprocessors were introduced in 1979-1985. They were 16-bit devices that could process complex instructions at a time. They had faster speed, larger memory, and more advanced applications. Examples are Intel 8086, Motorola 68000, and Zilog Z8000 .
- The fourth generation microprocessors were introduced in 1986-1995. They were 32-bit devices that could process multiple complex instructions at a time. They had very high speed, very large memory, and more sophisticated applications. Examples are Intel 80386, Motorola 68020, and ARM .
- The fifth generation microprocessors were introduced in 1996-present. They are 64-bit devices that can process multiple parallel instructions at a time. They have very high speed, very large memory, and more diverse applications. Examples are Intel Pentium, AMD Athlon, and ARM Cortex .
- The microprocessors can also be categorized according to the instruction set they use. There are two types of instruction sets: complex instruction set (CISC) and reduced instruction set (RISC).
- CISC microprocessors have a large number of instructions that can perform complex operations. They are designed to minimize the number of instructions per program and ignore the number of cycles per instruction. Examples are Intel 8086, Intel 80386, and Motorola 68000.
- RISC microprocessors have a small number of instructions that can perform simple operations. They are designed to reduce the number of cycles per instruction and ignore the number of instructions per program. Examples are ARM, MIPS, and PowerPC.
