## Pin diagram and internal architecture of 8085 microprocessor

- The 8085 microprocessor is an 8-bit microprocessor that operates on 8 bits of data at a time. It has a 16-bit address bus that can address up to 64 KB of memory. It has 40 pins and operates with +5V power supply.

![Pin diagram of 8085 microprocessor](https://electronicsdesk.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/11/Pin-Diagram-of-8085-Microprocessor.png)

- The pins of 8085 microprocessor can be classified into six groups: address bus, data bus, control and status signals, power supply and frequency signals, externally initiated signals, and serial I/O ports  .
- The address bus is a group of 16 lines (A0-A15) that are used to transfer the memory address of the data that needs to be read or written. The address bus is unidirectional, i.e., bits flow in one direction from the microprocessor to the peripheral devices. The high order address bus (A8-A15) is also used as a multiplexed bus for the low order data bus (D0-D7) by using the ALE (address latch enable) signal.
- The data bus is a group of 8 lines (D0-D7) that are used to transfer the data between the microprocessor and the memory or I/O devices. The data bus is bidirectional, i.e., bits can flow in both directions. The data bus is also multiplexed with the low order address bus (A0-A7) by using the ALE signal.
- The control and status signals are used to control the operations of the microprocessor and to indicate the status of the microprocessor. The control signals are: RD (read), WR (write), IO/M (I/O or memory), S0 and S1 (status). The RD signal is active low and indicates that the microprocessor is reading data from the memory or I/O device. The WR signal is active low and indicates that the microprocessor is writing data to the memory or I/O device. The IO/M signal is used to select between memory and I/O operations. The S0 and S1 signals are used to indicate the type of operation being performed by the microprocessor, such as halt, write, read, fetch, etc .
- The power supply and frequency signals are used to provide the power and clock signals to the microprocessor. The power supply signals are: Vcc (+5V) and Vss (ground). The frequency signals are: X1 and X2 (crystal or R/C network), CLK (clock output), and RESET IN and RESET OUT (reset signals). The X1 and X2 pins are connected to a crystal or R/C network that generates the frequency for the internal clock of the microprocessor. The CLK pin is used to provide the clock signal to other devices. The RESET IN pin is used to reset the microprocessor by applying a high signal for at least three clock cycles. The RESET OUT pin is used to indicate that the microprocessor is being reset .
- The externally initiated signals are used to communicate with external devices or interrupt the normal operation of the microprocessor. The externally initiated signals are: INTR (interrupt request), INTA (interrupt acknowledge), RST 5.5, RST 6.5, RST 7.5 (maskable interrupts), TRAP (non-maskable interrupt), HOLD (hold request), and HLDA (hold acknowledge). The INTR signal is used to request an interrupt from an external device. The INTA signal is used to acknowledge the interrupt request and to receive the interrupt vector from the device. The RST 5.5, RST 6.5, and RST 7.5 signals are used to request maskable interrupts with different priorities.
