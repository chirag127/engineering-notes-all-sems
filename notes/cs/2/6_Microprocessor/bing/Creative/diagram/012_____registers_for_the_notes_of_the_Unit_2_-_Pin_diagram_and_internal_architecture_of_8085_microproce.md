## Pin diagram of 8085 microprocessor

The 8085 microprocessor is an 8-bit microprocessor that operates on 8 bits of data at a time. It has 40 pins and requires a +5V power supply. The pin diagram of 8085 microprocessor is shown below:

![Pin diagram of 8085 microprocessor](https://electronicsdesk.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/08/Pin-Diagram-of-8085-Microprocessor.png)

The pins of 8085 microprocessor can be classified into six groups:

- Address and data bus: These are 16 address lines (A0-A15) and 8 data lines (D0-D7) that are used to communicate with the memory and I/O devices. The address bus is unidirectional, while the data bus is bidirectional. The address and data lines are multiplexed, which means they share the same pins. The lower order address lines (A0-A7) are multiplexed with the data lines (D0-D7), and the higher order address lines (A8-A15) are multiplexed with the status signals (IO/M, S0, S1). To separate the address and data signals, a demultiplexer or a latch is used.
- Control and status signals: These are 4 signals that indicate the status and mode of operation of the microprocessor. They are IO/M, S0, S1, and RD/WR. IO/M is a 1-bit signal that specifies whether the address on the address bus is for an I/O device (IO/M = 1) or a memory device (IO/M = 0). S0 and S1 are 2-bit signals that specify the type of operation being performed by the microprocessor. They can have four possible values: 00 for halt, 01 for write, 10 for read, and 11 for fetch. RD/WR is a 1-bit signal that specifies whether the microprocessor is reading data from (RD/WR = 0) or writing data to (RD/WR = 1) the memory or I/O device.
- Power supply and clock signals: These are 3 signals that provide the power and timing for the microprocessor. They are Vcc, Vss, and CLK. Vcc is the +5V power supply pin, and Vss is the ground pin. CLK is the clock input pin that provides the clock pulses for synchronizing the internal operations of the microprocessor. The frequency of the clock signal is 3 MHz for 8085 microprocessor.
- Interrupt signals: These are 5 signals that are used to request the microprocessor to suspend its current operation and execute a subroutine. They are TRAP, RST 7.5, RST 6.5, RST 5.5, and INTR. TRAP is a non-maskable interrupt that has the highest priority. It cannot be disabled by any instruction or signal. RST 7.5, RST 6.5, and RST 5.5 are maskable interrupts that can be enabled or disabled by the EI (enable interrupt) and DI (disable interrupt) instructions. They have different priorities, with RST 7.5 being the highest and RST 5.5 being the lowest. INTR is a maskable interrupt that can be enabled or disabled by the EI and DI instructions. It has the lowest priority among all the interrupts. It is a general-purpose interrupt that can be used for any external device. When an interrupt is accepted by the microprocessor, it executes a call instruction to a fixed memory location, which is determined by the type of interrupt. For example, TRAP executes a call to 0024H, RST 7.5 executes a call to 003CH, RST 6.5 executes a call to 0034H, RST 5.5 executes a call to 002CH, and INTR executes a call to the address provided by the INTA (interrupt acknowledge) signal.
- Serial I/O signals: These are 2 signals that are used for serial data communication between the microprocessor and external devices. They are SID (serial input data) and SOD (serial output data).
