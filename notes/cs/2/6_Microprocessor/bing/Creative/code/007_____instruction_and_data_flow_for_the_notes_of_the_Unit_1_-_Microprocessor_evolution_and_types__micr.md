# Instruction and Data Flow

## Microprocessor Evolution and Types

- A microprocessor is a controlling unit of a micro-computer, fabricated on a small chip capable of performing ALU (Arithmetic Logical Unit) operations and communicating with the other devices connected to it .
- The microprocessor is also known as the CPU (Central Processing Unit) and it is an essential component of the computer.
- The microprocessor evolution can be divided into four generations: 4-bit, 8-bit, 16-bit and 32-bit.
- The first microprocessor was invented by Intel in 1971 and it was called the 4004. It was a 4-bit microprocessor that could process 4 bits of data at a time.
- The 4-bit microprocessors were followed by the 8-bit microprocessors, such as the 8080, 8085 and Z80, that could process 8 bits of data at a time and had more memory and speed than the 4-bit ones.
- The 16-bit microprocessors, such as the 8086, 8088 and 80286, could process 16 bits of data at a time and had more advanced features, such as segmentation, pipelining and multitasking, than the 8-bit ones.
- The 32-bit microprocessors, such as the 80386, 80486 and Pentium, could process 32 bits of data at a time and had more powerful capabilities, such as virtual memory, floating-point arithmetic and multimedia, than the 16-bit ones.
- The microprocessor types can be classified based on the number of bits, the instruction set, the architecture and the application.
- The number of bits refers to the size of the data that the microprocessor can process in one cycle. The higher the number of bits, the more data the microprocessor can handle.
- The instruction set refers to the set of commands that the microprocessor can execute. The instruction set can be either RISC (Reduced Instruction Set Computer) or CISC (Complex Instruction Set Computer). RISC has fewer and simpler instructions, while CISC has more and complex instructions.
- The architecture refers to the way the microprocessor is designed and organized. The architecture can be either Von Neumann or Harvard. Von Neumann has a single memory for both data and instructions, while Harvard has separate memories for data and instructions.
- The application refers to the purpose and function of the microprocessor. The application can be either general-purpose or special-purpose. General-purpose microprocessors are used for a variety of tasks, such as personal computers, laptops and smartphones, while special-purpose microprocessors are used for specific tasks, such as embedded systems, digital signal processing and robotics.

## Microprocessor Architecture and Operation of its Components

- The microprocessor architecture consists of three main components: the ALU, the register array and the control unit .
- The ALU is the part of the microprocessor that performs arithmetic and logical operations on the data, such as addition, subtraction, multiplication, division, comparison and bitwise operations .
- The register array is the part of the microprocessor that stores the data and instructions temporarily, such as the accumulator, the program counter, the stack pointer, the status register and the general-purpose registers .
- The control unit is the part of the microprocessor that controls the flow of data and instructions between the ALU, the register array and the external devices, such as the memory, the input/output devices and the buses .
- The operation of the microprocessor components can be explained by the following steps :
  - The control unit fetches the instruction from the memory and stores it in the instruction register.
  - The control unit decodes the instruction and generates the appropriate control signals to execute it.
  - The control unit transfers the data from the memory or the input devices to the register array or the ALU, depending on the instruction.
  - The ALU performs the required operation on the data and stores the result in the register array or the memory or the output devices, depending on the instruction.
  - The control unit updates the program counter and the status register according to the instruction and the result.
  - The control unit repeats the above steps until the program is completed or interrupted.
