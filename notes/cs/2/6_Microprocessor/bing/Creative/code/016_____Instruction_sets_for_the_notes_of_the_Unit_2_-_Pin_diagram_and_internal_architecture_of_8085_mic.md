## Pin diagram and internal architecture of 8085 microprocessor

- The 8085 microprocessor is an 8-bit microprocessor that operates on 8 bits of data at a time. It has a 16-bit address bus that can address up to 64 KB of memory. It has 40 pins and operates with +5V power supply.
- The pin diagram of 8085 microprocessor is shown below:

![Pin diagram of 8085 microprocessor](https://electronicsdesk.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/08/Pin-Diagram-of-8085-Microprocessor.png)

- The pins of 8085 microprocessor can be classified into six groups: address bus, data bus, control and status signals, power supply and frequency signals, externally initiated signals, and serial I/O ports .
- The address bus consists of 16 lines (A0-A15) that are used to transfer the memory address of the data that needs to be read or written. The address bus is unidirectional, i.e., bits flow in one direction from the microprocessor to the peripheral devices. The address bus also carries the lower 8 bits of the address during the first clock cycle and the higher 8 bits of the address during the second clock cycle.
- The data bus consists of 8 lines (D0-D7) that are used to transfer the data between the microprocessor and the memory or I/O devices. The data bus is bidirectional, i.e., bits can flow in both directions. The data bus also carries the opcode of the instruction during the first clock cycle and the operand or data during the second clock cycle.
- The control and status signals consist of 6 lines that are used to control the operation of the microprocessor and indicate the status of the microprocessor. The control signals are: RD (read), WR (write), IO/M (I/O or memory), ALE (address latch enable), and S0 and S1 (status signals). The RD signal is active low and indicates that the microprocessor is reading data from the memory or I/O device. The WR signal is active low and indicates that the microprocessor is writing data to the memory or I/O device. The IO/M signal is used to select between the memory and I/O operations. The IO/M signal is high for I/O operations and low for memory operations. The ALE signal is used to latch the lower 8 bits of the address from the address bus to an external latch. The ALE signal is high during the first clock cycle and low during the second clock cycle. The S0 and S1 signals are used to indicate the type of operation being performed by the microprocessor. The S0 and S1 signals can have four possible combinations: 00 for halt, 01 for write, 10 for read, and 11 for fetch  .
- The power supply and frequency signals consist of 3 lines that are used to provide the power supply and clock frequency to the microprocessor. The power supply lines are: Vcc (+5V) and Vss (ground). The frequency signal is: X1 and X2 (crystal or R/C network). The X1 and X2 pins are connected to a crystal or an R/C network that generates the clock frequency for the microprocessor. The clock frequency of 8085 microprocessor is 3 MHz .
- The externally initiated signals consist of 5 lines that are used to communicate with external devices or interrupt the microprocessor. The externally initiated signals are: INTR (interrupt request), INTA (interrupt acknowledge), RST 5.5, RST 6.5, and RST 7.5 (restart interrupts), TRAP (non-maskable interrupt), and HOLD and HLDA (direct memory access). The INTR signal is used to request an interrupt to the microprocessor. The INTR signal is maskable, i.e., it can be enabled or disabled by software.
