### Registers for the notes of the Unit 2 - Pin diagram and internal architecture of 8085 microprocessor

In the study of microprocessors, it is important to understand the various components that make up the internal architecture of the microprocessor. One of the key components that play a crucial role in the functioning of the microprocessor is registers. In this section, we will discuss the different types of registers present in the 8085 microprocessor.

#### Types of Registers in 8085 Microprocessor

1. Accumulator Register - It is an 8-bit register that stores data and intermediate results during arithmetic and logical operations.

2. General Purpose Registers - There are six general-purpose registers in the 8085 microprocessor, namely - B, C, D, E, H, and L. Each register is 8-bits wide and can be used for storing data.

3. Stack Pointer Register - It is a 16-bit register that is used to store the address of the current stack location in memory.

4. Program Counter Register - It is a 16-bit register that stores the address of the next instruction to be executed.

5. Instruction Register - It is an 8-bit register that holds the opcode of the instruction being executed.

6. Status Register - It is an 8-bit register that stores the status of the microprocessor after the execution of an instruction.

#### Functioning of Registers in 8085 Microprocessor

The registers in the 8085 microprocessor are used for storing data, performing arithmetic and logical operations, and controlling the execution of instructions. The accumulator register is used to store the result of the arithmetic and logical operations. The general-purpose registers are used for storing intermediate results during the execution of instructions.

The stack pointer register is used to store the address of the current stack location in memory. The program counter register is used to store the address of the next instruction to be executed. The instruction register is used to hold the opcode of the instruction being executed.

The status register stores the status of the microprocessor after the execution of an instruction. It contains various flags such as carry flag, zero flag, sign flag, etc. that are set or reset depending on the outcome of the instruction being executed.

#### Conclusion

In conclusion, registers are a vital component of the internal architecture of the 8085 microprocessor. They are used for storing data, performing arithmetic and logical operations, and controlling the execution of instructions. A thorough understanding of the different types of registers and their functioning is essential for the study of microprocessors.
