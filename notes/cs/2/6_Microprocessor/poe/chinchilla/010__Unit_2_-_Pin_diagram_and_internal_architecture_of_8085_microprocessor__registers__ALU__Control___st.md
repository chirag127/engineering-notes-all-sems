

## Unit 2 - Pin diagram and internal architecture of 8085 microprocessor, registers, ALU, Control & status, interrupt and machine cycle. Instruction sets. Addressing modes. Instruction formats Instruction Classification: data transfer, arithmetic operations, logical operations, branching operations, machine control and assembler directives.

- The 8085 microprocessor is a 8-bit microprocessor comprising of 40 pins.
- The pin diagram of the 8085 microprocessor consists of various pins for different functionalities such as data bus, address bus, control signals, power supply, etc.
- The internal architecture of the 8085 microprocessor comprises of various registers such as accumulator, program counter, stack pointer, and various other registers for different operations.
- The ALU (Arithmetic Logic Unit) is responsible for various arithmetic and logical operations such as addition, subtraction, AND, OR, etc.
- The Control and Status signals are responsible for various control operations such as enabling interrupts, status of the microprocessor, etc.
- The Interrupt mechanism in the 8085 microprocessor allows for external devices to interrupt the microprocessor during its operation.
- The Machine Cycle is the time taken by the microprocessor to execute one instruction and it comprises of various sub-cycles such as fetch, decode, execute, etc.
- The Instruction Set of the 8085 microprocessor comprises of various instructions for different operations such as data transfer, arithmetic operations, logical operations, branching operations, machine control, and assembler directives.
- The Addressing Modes in the 8085 microprocessor are the different ways in which the operands are specified in the instructions such as immediate addressing, direct addressing, indirect addressing, etc.
- The Instruction Formats in the 8085 microprocessor are the different formats in which the instructions are coded such as one-byte instruction, two-byte instruction, etc.
- The Instruction Classification in the 8085 microprocessor is the categorization of the instructions based on their functionality such as data transfer, arithmetic operations, logical operations, branching operations, machine control, and assembler directives.
