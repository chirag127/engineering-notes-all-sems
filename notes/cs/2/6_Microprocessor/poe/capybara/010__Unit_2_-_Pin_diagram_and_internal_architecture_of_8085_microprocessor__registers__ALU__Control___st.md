The 8085 microprocessor is an 8-bit microprocessor that was introduced by Intel in the year 1977. It is a general-purpose microprocessor and is widely used in various applications such as calculators, computers, and other electronic devices. In this unit, we will discuss the pin diagram and internal architecture of the 8085 microprocessor, its registers, ALU, Control & status, interrupt and machine cycle, instruction sets, addressing modes, instruction formats, and instruction classification.

### Registers

The 8085 microprocessor has six general-purpose registers, i.e., B, C, D, E, H, and L, and two special-purpose registers, i.e., the accumulator (A) and the program counter (PC). The stack pointer (SP) is also used as a register in some cases. These registers are used to store data and perform various operations.

### ALU

The Arithmetic Logic Unit (ALU) of the 8085 microprocessor is used to perform arithmetic and logical operations on the data stored in the registers. The ALU can perform various operations such as addition, subtraction, logical AND, logical OR, etc.

### Control & Status Signals

The control and status signals of the 8085 microprocessor are used to control the operation of the microprocessor and to indicate the status of the microprocessor. Some of the control and status signals are the interrupt request (INT), the restart (RESET), the ready (RDY), the hold (HLDA), etc.

### Interrupt and Machine Cycle

The 8085 microprocessor supports five interrupt signals, i.e., TRAP, RST 7.5, RST 6.5, RST 5.5, and INTR. The machine cycle of the 8085 microprocessor consists of various operations such as memory read, memory write, I/O read, I/O write, etc.

### Instruction Sets

The instruction set of the 8085 microprocessor consists of various instructions that are used to perform various operations such as data transfer, arithmetic operations, logical operations, branching operations, machine control, and assembler directives.

### Addressing Modes

The addressing modes of the 8085 microprocessor specify how the operands are accessed by the instructions. The 8085 microprocessor supports various addressing modes such as immediate, direct, indirect, register, and indexed.

### Instruction Formats

The instruction format of the 8085 microprocessor consists of various fields such as the opcode, the operand, the addressing mode, etc. The instruction format varies depending on the type of instruction.

### Instruction Classification

The instructions of the 8085 microprocessor are classified into various categories such as data transfer, arithmetic operations, logical operations, branching operations, machine control, and assembler directives. These instructions are used to perform various operations on the data stored in the registers.
