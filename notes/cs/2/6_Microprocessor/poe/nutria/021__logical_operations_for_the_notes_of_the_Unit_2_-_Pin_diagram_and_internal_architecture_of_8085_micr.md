

### Logical Operations for the Notes of the Unit 2 - Pin diagram and internal architecture of 8085 Microprocessor, Registers, ALU, Control & Status, Interrupt and Machine Cycle

- Instruction sets: Instructions are basic commands that the microprocessor can understand and execute. The 8085 microprocessor has a total of 77 instructions that are divided into five groups: data transfer, arithmetic operations, logical operations, branching operations, and machine control operations.
- Addressing Modes: Addressing modes determine how the data is accessed and manipulated. The 8085 microprocessor has five addressing modes: immediate, direct, register, register indirect, and indexed.
- Instruction Formats: The 8085 microprocessor has three instruction formats: one-byte, two-byte, and three-byte instructions.
- Instruction Classification: Instructions are classified into five groups: data transfer, arithmetic operations, logical operations, branching operations, and machine control operations.
- Data Transfer Instructions: These instructions move data from one location to another.
- Arithmetic Operations Instructions: These instructions perform arithmetic operations such as addition, subtraction, multiplication, and division.
- Logical Operations Instructions: These instructions perform logical operations such as AND, OR, XOR, and NOT.
- Branching Instructions: These instructions allow the program to jump to a different part of the program depending on the result of a comparison.
- Machine Control Instructions: These instructions control the operation of the microprocessor, such as setting the interrupt mask and resetting the microprocessor.
- Assembler Directives: Assembler directives are instructions that are not executed by the microprocessor, but instead are used to control the assembly process.
