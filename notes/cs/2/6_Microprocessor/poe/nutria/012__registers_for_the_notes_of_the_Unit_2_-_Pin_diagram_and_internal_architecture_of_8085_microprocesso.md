
### Unit 2 - Pin diagram and internal architecture of 8085 microprocessor

* Pin diagram: The 8085 microprocessor has 40 pins, divided into four groups of 10 pins each. These pins are used to provide power, clock signal, reset, interrupt and data/address bus.
* Registers: The 8085 microprocessor has six 8-bit registers: A, B, C, D, E, and H. The registers can be used to store data and perform arithmetic and logical operations.
* ALU: The ALU (Arithmetic and Logic Unit) is responsible for performing arithmetic and logical operations on the data stored in the registers.
* Control & status: The Control & Status Register is used to store the status of the microprocessor and to control the operations of the microprocessor.
* Interrupt: The 8085 microprocessor can be interrupted by an external signal. This signal is used to stop the current operation and start a new one.
* Machine cycle: The machine cycle is the sequence of operations that the microprocessor performs in order to execute an instruction.
* Instruction sets: The instruction set of the 8085 microprocessor consists of instructions for data transfer, arithmetic operations, logical operations, branching operations, and machine control.
* Addressing modes: The 8085 microprocessor supports five addressing modes: immediate, direct, indirect, indexed and relative.
* Instruction formats: The instruction formats of the 8085 microprocessor are one-byte, two-byte, and three-byte instructions.
* Instruction Classification: The instructions of the 8085 microprocessor can be classified into data transfer, arithmetic operations, logical operations, branching operations, machine control and assembler directives.
