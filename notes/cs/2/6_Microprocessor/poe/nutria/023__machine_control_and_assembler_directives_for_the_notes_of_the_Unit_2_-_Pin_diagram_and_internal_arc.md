
### Machine Control and Assembler Directives for Unit 2 - Pin Diagram and Internal Architecture of 8085 Microprocessor

* Pin Diagram: The 8085 microprocessor consists of 40 pins, arranged in two rows of 20 pins each. The pins are used to connect the microprocessor to the external devices such as memory, I/O devices, and other peripheral devices.

* Internal Architecture: The internal architecture of the 8085 microprocessor is divided into five major sections: registers, ALU, control and status, interrupt, and machine cycle.

* Registers: The 8085 microprocessor has six 8-bit registers which are used to store data and instructions. These registers are A, B, C, D, E, and H.

* ALU: The Arithmetic Logic Unit (ALU) of 8085 is used to perform arithmetic and logical operations on data. It consists of two 8-bit registers A and B which are used to store the data.

* Control and Status: The Control and Status section of the 8085 microprocessor is responsible for controlling the flow of the program and monitoring the status of the microprocessor. It consists of two 8-bit registers C and D which are used to store the control and status information.

* Interrupt: The interrupt section of the 8085 microprocessor is responsible for responding to external interrupts. It consists of two 8-bit registers E and H which are used to store the interrupt information.

* Machine Cycle: The machine cycle of the 8085 microprocessor is responsible for executing the instructions. It consists of four steps: fetch, decode, execute, and writeback.

* Instruction Sets: The instruction sets of the 8085 microprocessor are the set of instructions that it can understand and execute. These instructions are divided into three types: data transfer, arithmetic operations, and logical operations.

* Addressing Modes: The addressing modes of the 8085 microprocessor are the ways in which the microprocessor can access the data. These modes are direct, indirect, immediate, and indexed.

* Instruction Formats: The instruction formats of the 8085 microprocessor are the ways in which the instructions are encoded. These formats are one-byte, two-byte, and three-byte.

* Instruction Classification: The instruction classification of the 8085 microprocessor is the categorization of the instructions into data transfer, arithmetic operations, logical operations, branching operations, machine control, and assembler directives.
