### Binary search tree for the notes of the Unit 6 - Trees in the subject of Discrete Structures & Theory of Logic

A binary search tree (BST) is a type of tree data structure that is used to store and retrieve data in a way that is efficient and easy to understand. In a BST, each node has at most two children and the left child is always less than the parent node, while the right child is always greater than the parent node.

Unit 6 of Discrete Structures & Theory of Logic focuses on the study of trees and their properties. The notes for this unit should include the following topics:

1. Definition of a binary search tree and its components (nodes, keys, children)
2. Properties of binary search trees (balanced, complete, full, height)
3. Insertion and deletion algorithms for binary search trees
4. Traversal algorithms for binary search trees (in-order, pre-order, post-order)
5. Applications of binary search trees (data retrieval, searching, sorting)
6. Comparison with other tree data structures (AVL trees, B-trees, red-black trees)

Binary search trees are widely used in computer science and are an important topic in the study of discrete structures and the theory of logic. Understanding the properties and algorithms of binary search trees is essential for solving problems related to trees and for studying more advanced topics in computer science.
