### Multigraphs for the notes of the Unit 7 - Graphs in the subject of Discrete Structures & Theory of Logic

A multigraph is a type of graph that allows for multiple edges between the same pair of vertices. In other words, a multigraph is a graph where there can be more than one edge between two vertices. In contrast, a simple graph only allows for one edge between two vertices.

In the context of Unit 7 - Graphs in the subject of Discrete Structures & Theory of Logic, multigraphs are used to model real-world situations where multiple edges between the same pair of vertices are allowed. For example, a multigraph can be used to model a transportation network where there are multiple roads between two cities.

Multigraphs can also be used to model situations where the edges have different weights or capacities. For example, a multigraph can be used to model a network of pipelines where each pipeline has a different capacity.

In addition to modeling real-world situations, multigraphs are also used in graph algorithms, such as shortest path algorithms and maximum flow algorithms. These algorithms can be applied to multigraphs to find the shortest path between two vertices or to find the maximum flow in a network.

In summary, multigraphs are an important concept in the study of graphs in the subject of Discrete Structures & Theory of Logic. They are used to model real-world situations where multiple edges between the same pair of vertices are allowed, and they are also used in graph algorithms to find the shortest path between two vertices or to find the maximum flow in a network.
