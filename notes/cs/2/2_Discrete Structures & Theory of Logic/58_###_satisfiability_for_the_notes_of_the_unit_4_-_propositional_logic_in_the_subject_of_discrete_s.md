### Satisfiability for the notes of the Unit 4 - Propositional Logic in the subject of Discrete Structures & Theory of Logic

Satisfiability, also known as validity, is a central concept in propositional logic. A propositional formula is said to be satisfiable if there exists an assignment of truth values to its variables that makes the formula true. Conversely, a formula is unsatisfiable if no such assignment exists.

In other words, a formula is satisfiable if it can be made true by assigning appropriate truth values to its variables. For example, the formula (p ∨ ¬p) is always satisfiable, since p can be either true or false. On the other hand, the formula (p ∧ ¬p) is unsatisfiable, since it is impossible for both p and ¬p to be true at the same time.

Satisfiability is an important concept in computer science, as it is used in various algorithms for solving constraint satisfaction problems, automated theorem proving, and model checking. In particular, the problem of determining whether a given formula is satisfiable is known as the satisfiability problem (SAT), and is one of the most widely studied problems in computer science.

In this unit, we will study the concept of satisfiability in more detail, and examine various algorithms for determining whether a formula is satisfiable. We will also study the relationship between satisfiability and validity, and examine the relationship between satisfiability and other concepts in logic and computer science.
