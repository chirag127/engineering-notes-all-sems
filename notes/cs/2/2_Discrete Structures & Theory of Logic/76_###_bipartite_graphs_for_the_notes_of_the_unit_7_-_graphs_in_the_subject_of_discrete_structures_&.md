### Bipartite graphs for the notes of the Unit 7 - Graphs in the subject of Discrete Structures & Theory of Logic

In graph theory, a bipartite graph is a graph whose vertices can be divided into two disjoint sets such that every edge in the graph connects a vertex in one set with a vertex in the other set.

Unit 7 of Discrete Structures & Theory of Logic focuses on the study of graphs and their properties. The notes for this unit should include the following topics:

1. Definition of a graph and its components (vertices, edges, degree)
2. Types of graphs (simple, directed, weighted, bipartite)
3. Representation of graphs (adjacency list, adjacency matrix)
4. Graph algorithms (traversal, shortest path, minimum spanning tree)
5. Applications of graphs (network analysis, social networks, computer science)

Bipartite graphs are a specific type of graph that have important applications in various fields, including computer science, operations research, and social network analysis. Some of the key properties of bipartite graphs include:

1. Bipartite graphs are always 2-colorable, meaning that the vertices can be colored black and white such that no two adjacent vertices have the same color.
2. The degree of a vertex in a bipartite graph is equal to the number of edges incident to that vertex.
3. Bipartite graphs can be used to model relationships between two types of objects, such as employees and departments in an organization.

In summary, bipartite graphs are a type of graph in which the vertices can be divided into two disjoint sets, and have important applications in various fields including computer science, operations research, and social network analysis.
