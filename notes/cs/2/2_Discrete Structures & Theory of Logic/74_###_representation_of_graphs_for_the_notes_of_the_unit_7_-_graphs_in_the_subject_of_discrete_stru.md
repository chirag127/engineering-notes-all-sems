### Representation of graphs for the notes of the Unit 7 - Graphs in the subject of Discrete Structures & Theory of Logic

A graph is a mathematical structure used to represent relationships between objects. In graph theory, the objects are referred to as vertices (or nodes), and the relationships are represented by edges. There are several ways to represent a graph, including:

1. Adjacency matrix: An adjacency matrix is a square matrix that represents the edges between vertices. Each row and column of the matrix corresponds to a vertex, and the entries in the matrix indicate whether there is an edge between the corresponding vertices.

2. Adjacency list: An adjacency list is a list of lists, where each list represents a vertex and contains the vertices that are adjacent to it.

3. Incidence matrix: An incidence matrix is a matrix that represents the edges between vertices. Each row of the matrix corresponds to a vertex, and each column corresponds to an edge. The entries in the matrix indicate whether a vertex is incident to an edge.

4. Graph diagram: A graph diagram is a visual representation of a graph, where vertices are represented by points and edges are represented by lines connecting the points.

The choice of representation depends on the problem being solved and the algorithms being used. Adjacency matrices are useful for dense graphs, where many edges are present, while adjacency lists are more efficient for sparse graphs, where only a few edges are present. Incidence matrices are useful for finding the degree of a vertex and for solving certain problems in graph theory. Graph diagrams are useful for visualizing the structure of a graph and for understanding the relationships between vertices.
