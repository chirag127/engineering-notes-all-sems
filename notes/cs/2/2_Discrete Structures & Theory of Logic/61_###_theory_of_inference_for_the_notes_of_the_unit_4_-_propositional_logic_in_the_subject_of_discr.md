### Theory of Inference for the notes of the Unit 4 - Propositional Logic in the subject of Discrete Structures & Theory of Logic

Theory of Inference is a branch of mathematical logic that deals with the study of reasoning and the derivation of new conclusions from existing information. In propositional logic, the theory of inference involves the use of logical rules and axioms to deduce new propositions from given propositions.

Unit 4 of Discrete Structures & Theory of Logic focuses on propositional logic and the theory of inference. The notes for this unit should include the following topics:

1. Definition of propositional logic and its symbols (propositions, connectives, truth values)
2. Syntax and semantics of propositional logic
3. Logical equivalence and tautologies
4. Rules of inference (modus ponens, modus tollens, contrapositive, etc.)
5. Soundness and completeness of inference rules
6. Normal forms of propositional logic (Conjunctive Normal Form (CNF) and Disjunctive Normal Form (DNF))
7. Proof methods in propositional logic (truth tables, natural deduction, resolution)

The theory of inference is a fundamental aspect of propositional logic and is used to deduce new information from existing information. Understanding the rules of inference and their applications is essential for solving problems in propositional logic and for studying more advanced topics in mathematical logic.
