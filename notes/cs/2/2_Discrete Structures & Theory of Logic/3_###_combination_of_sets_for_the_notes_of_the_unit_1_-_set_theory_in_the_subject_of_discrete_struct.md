### Combination of sets for the notes of the Unit 1 - Set Theory in the subject of Discrete Structures & Theory of Logic

"Combination of Sets" is a topic covered in Unit 1 - Set Theory in the subject of Discrete Structures & Theory of Logic.

In mathematics, a set is a collection of distinct objects, and set theory is the study of sets and their properties. One of the fundamental concepts in set theory is the combination of sets.

1. Union of Sets: The union of two sets is the set of all elements that are in either of the two sets. The union of two sets A and B is denoted by A U B.

2. Intersection of Sets: The intersection of two sets is the set of all elements that are in both of the two sets. The intersection of two sets A and B is denoted by A ∩ B.

3. Difference of Sets: The difference of two sets is the set of all elements that are in one set but not in the other set. The difference of two sets A and B is denoted by A - B.

In Unit 1 - Set Theory, students will learn about the combination of sets, including the different types of set combinations, and the properties and relationships between sets.

This knowledge and understanding is important for students pursuing careers in computer science and mathematics, as it provides the foundation for more advanced topics in the fields of discrete structures and the theory of logic, such as graph theory, Boolean algebra, and formal logic.
