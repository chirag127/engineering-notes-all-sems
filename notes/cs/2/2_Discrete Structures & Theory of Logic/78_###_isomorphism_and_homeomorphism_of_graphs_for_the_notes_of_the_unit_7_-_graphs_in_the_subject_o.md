### Isomorphism and Homeomorphism of graphs for the notes of the Unit 7 - Graphs in the subject of Discrete Structures & Theory of Logic

In graph theory, isomorphism and homeomorphism are two concepts used to compare and classify graphs. 

Isomorphism is a bijective mapping between the vertices of two graphs that preserves the edges and their connections. In other words, if two graphs are isomorphic, there exists a one-to-one correspondence between their vertices such that the edges in one graph correspond to the edges in the other graph. 

Homeomorphism is a continuous mapping between the vertices of two graphs that preserves the edges and their connections. In other words, if two graphs are homeomorphic, there exists a continuous function between their vertices such that the edges in one graph correspond to the edges in the other graph. 

Isomorphism and homeomorphism are important concepts in graph theory as they allow us to classify graphs based on their structural properties. For example, two isomorphic graphs have the same number of vertices, edges, and degree sequences, and they can be used to solve graph problems in a more efficient manner.
