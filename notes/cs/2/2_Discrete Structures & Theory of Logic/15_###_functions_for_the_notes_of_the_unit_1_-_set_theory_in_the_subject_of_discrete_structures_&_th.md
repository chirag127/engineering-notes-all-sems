### Functions for the notes of the Unit 1 - Set Theory in the subject of Discrete Structures & Theory of Logic

"Functions" is a topic covered in Unit 1 - Set Theory in the subject of Discrete Structures & Theory of Logic.

In mathematics, a function is a rule that assigns a unique output to each input. Functions are used to model mathematical relationships between objects, and to study their properties and relationships.

1. Domain and Range: The domain of a function is the set of all possible inputs, and the range is the set of all possible outputs.

2. Injective, Surjective, and Bijective Functions: A function is injective if each element in the range corresponds to a unique element in the domain. A function is surjective if each element in the range is the image of at least one element in the domain. A function is bijective if it is both injective and surjective.

3. Composition of Functions: The composition of two functions is the function that results from applying one function after the other. The composition of two functions f and g is denoted by f o g.

In Unit 1 - Set Theory, students will learn about functions, including the definition and properties of functions, and the methods used to manipulate and study functions.

This knowledge and understanding is important for students pursuing careers in computer science and mathematics, as it provides the foundation for more advanced topics in the fields of discrete structures and the theory of logic, such as graph theory, algorithms, and formal logic.
