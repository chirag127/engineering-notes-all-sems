### Counting Techniques for the notes of the Unit 9 - Combinatorics in the subject of Discrete Structures & Theory of Logic

Counting techniques are methods used in combinatorics to determine the number of possible combinations or arrangements of a set of objects. Combinatorics is the branch of mathematics concerned with counting, arranging, and partitioning sets of objects.

There are several counting techniques used in combinatorics, including the multiplication principle, the addition principle, permutations, combinations, and the pigeonhole principle. 

The multiplication principle states that if there are m ways to perform one task and n ways to perform another task, then there are m x n ways to perform both tasks. The addition principle states that if there are m ways to perform one task and n ways to perform another task, then there are m + n ways to perform either task.

Permutations are arrangements of objects, while combinations are subsets of objects. The number of permutations and combinations of a set of objects can be determined using the formula n!/(r!(n-r)!).

The pigeonhole principle states that if n objects are placed into m containers, then at least one container must contain more than one object if n > m.

In this unit, we will study these counting techniques in more detail, and examine their applications in combinatorics and other areas of mathematics. We will also study algorithms for counting and generating permutations and combinations, and examine the relationship between counting techniques and other concepts in combinatorics.
