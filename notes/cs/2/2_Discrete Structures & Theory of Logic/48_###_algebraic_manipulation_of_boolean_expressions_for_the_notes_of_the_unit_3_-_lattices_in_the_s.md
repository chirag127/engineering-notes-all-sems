### Algebraic manipulation of Boolean expressions for the notes of the Unit 3 - Lattices in the subject of Discrete Structures & Theory of Logic

Algebraic manipulation of Boolean expressions refers to the process of simplifying or transforming a boolean expression using the rules of Boolean algebra.

In Boolean algebra, there are 3 basic operations: AND (represented by .), OR (represented by +) and NOT (represented by ~ or !).

The rules for simplifying boolean expressions are as follows:
1. Commutative laws: 
  a. a + b = b + a
  b. a . b = b . a
2. Associative laws: 
  a. (a + b) + c = a + (b + c)
  b. (a . b) . c = a . (b . c)
3. Distributive laws:
  a. a . (b + c) = (a . b) + (a . c)
  b. a + (b . c) = (a + b) . (a + c)
4. Idempotent laws: 
  a. a + a = a
  b. a . a = a
5. Complement laws: 
  a. a + ~a = 1
  b. a . ~a = 0
6. De Morgan's laws: 
  a. ~(a + b) = ~a . ~b
  b. ~(a . b) = ~a + ~b

By using these laws, we can simplify complex boolean expressions into simpler ones. For example, the expression (a + b) . (a + ~b) can be simplified to a.

It is important to note that the simplification process may not always result in the simplest expression possible. However, by repeatedly applying the rules of Boolean algebra, we can obtain the simplest expression possible.
