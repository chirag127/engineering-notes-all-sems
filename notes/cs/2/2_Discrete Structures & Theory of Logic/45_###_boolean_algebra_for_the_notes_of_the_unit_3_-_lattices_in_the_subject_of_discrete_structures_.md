### Boolean Algebra for the notes of the Unit 3 - Lattices in the subject of Discrete Structures & Theory of Logic

Boolean Algebra is a mathematical system that deals with the study of binary operations and their properties. It was first introduced by George Boole in 1854 in his book "An Investigation of the Laws of Thought".

The basic operations in Boolean Algebra are AND, OR and NOT. These operations are performed on binary values, either 0 or 1, and the result is also a binary value.

The AND operation is represented by a dot (.) and is defined as 1 only if both operands are 1, otherwise it is 0. The OR operation is represented by a plus (+) and is defined as 1 if either operand is 1, otherwise it is 0. The NOT operation is represented by a bar (') and is defined as 1 if the operand is 0, otherwise it is 0.

In Boolean Algebra, there are several laws and theorems that hold true for all binary operations. Some of these laws include: Commutative Law, Associative Law, Distributive Law, De Morgan's Law, and others.

Boolean Algebra is used in many areas of computer science, including digital logic design, computer hardware design, and computer software design. It is also used in the fields of electrical engineering and telecommunications.

In summary, Boolean Algebra is a mathematical system that deals with binary operations and their properties, and is used in many areas of computer science and electrical engineering.
