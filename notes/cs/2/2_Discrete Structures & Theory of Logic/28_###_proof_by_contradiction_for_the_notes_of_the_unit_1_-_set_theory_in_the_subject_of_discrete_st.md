### Proof by contradiction for the notes of the Unit 1 - Set Theory in the subject of Discrete Structures & Theory of Logic

"Proof by Contradiction" is a topic covered in Unit 1 - Set Theory in the subject of Discrete Structures & Theory of Logic.

Proof by contradiction is a method of proof in mathematics that involves assuming the opposite of what is to be proven, and then showing that this assumption leads to a contradiction.

In a proof by contradiction, the proof starts by assuming the negation of the statement to be proven. This assumption is then used to derive a contradiction, which shows that the assumption is false and that the original statement must be true.

Proof by contradiction is a powerful tool in mathematics, as it can be used to prove a wide range of mathematical statements, including statements about sets, functions, and relations.

In Unit 1 - Set Theory in the subject of Discrete Structures & Theory of Logic, students will learn about proof by contradiction, including the definition and method of proof by contradiction, and the types of statements that can be proven using this method.

This knowledge and understanding is important for students pursuing careers in mathematics, computer science, and related fields, as it provides the foundation for more advanced topics in discrete mathematics and theoretical computer science, such as graph theory, algorithms, and formal logic.
