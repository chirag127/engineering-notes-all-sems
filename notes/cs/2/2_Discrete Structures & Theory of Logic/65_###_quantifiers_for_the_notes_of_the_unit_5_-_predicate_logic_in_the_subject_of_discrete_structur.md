### quantifiers for the notes of the Unit 5 - Predicate Logic in the subject of Discrete Structures & Theory of Logic

In predicate logic, quantifiers are symbols that are used to express the scope of variables in a formula. The two most common quantifiers in predicate logic are the universal quantifier (∀) and the existential quantifier (∃).

The universal quantifier (∀) is used to express that a statement holds for all values of a variable. For example, the statement "For all x, x + 1 = 1 + x" can be expressed as ∀x(x + 1 = 1 + x).

The existential quantifier (∃) is used to express that there exists at least one value of a variable for which a statement holds. For example, the statement "There exists an x such that x + 1 = 1 + x" can be expressed as ∃x(x + 1 = 1 + x).

Quantifiers play a crucial role in predicate logic because they allow us to express more complex statements and relationships between variables. By using quantifiers, we can express statements about sets, collections, and functions, and use these statements to reason about mathematical structures.

In summary, quantifiers are symbols in predicate logic that are used to express the scope of variables in a formula. The two most common quantifiers are the universal quantifier (∀) and the existential quantifier (∃), and they play a crucial role in allowing us to express more complex statements and relationships between variables.
