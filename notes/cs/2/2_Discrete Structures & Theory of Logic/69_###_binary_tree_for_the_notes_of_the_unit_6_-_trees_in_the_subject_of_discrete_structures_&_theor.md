### Binary tree for the notes of the Unit 6 - Trees in the subject of Discrete Structures & Theory of Logic

A binary tree is a tree data structure in which each node has at most two children, referred to as the left child and the right child. The root node is the topmost node in the tree and its children are referred to as the left subtree and the right subtree.

Binary trees have several applications in computer science, including data storage and retrieval, searching algorithms, and sorting algorithms.

In the notes for Unit 6 - Trees in the subject of Discrete Structures & Theory of Logic, the following topics should be covered:

1. Definition of a binary tree and its components (nodes, edges, root, children, parent, subtree)
2. Properties of binary trees (height, depth, level, leaf node)
3. Traversal algorithms (pre-order, in-order, post-order)
4. Representation of binary trees (array representation, linked representation)
5. Binary search trees (definition, properties, insertion, deletion, search)
6. AVL trees (definition, properties, insertion, deletion, search)
7. Applications of binary trees (data storage and retrieval, searching algorithms, sorting algorithms)

It is important to understand the concepts and properties of binary trees in order to apply them in various fields and solve problems related to discrete structures and the theory of logic.
