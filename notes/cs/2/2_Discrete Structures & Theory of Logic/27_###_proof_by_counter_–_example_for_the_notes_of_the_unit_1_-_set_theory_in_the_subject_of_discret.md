### Proof by counter – example for the notes of the Unit 1 - Set Theory in the subject of Discrete Structures & Theory of Logic

"Proof by Counter-Example" is a topic covered in Unit 1 - Set Theory in the subject of Discrete Structures & Theory of Logic.

Proof by counter-example is a method of proof in mathematics that shows that a statement is false by providing an example that contradicts the statement. In a proof by counter-example, the proof is complete once a single example is found that contradicts the statement.

Proof by counter-example is a powerful tool for proving theorems and solving problems in mathematics and computer science. It can be used to prove the validity or invalidity of statements in a wide range of mathematical and computational fields, including set theory, graph theory, algorithms, and formal logic.

In Unit 1 - Set Theory, students will learn about proof by counter-example, including the definition and methods used to construct proofs by counter-example, and the applications of proof by counter-example in solving problems in mathematics and computer science.

This knowledge and understanding is important for students pursuing careers in mathematics, computer science, and related fields, as it provides the foundation for more advanced topics in discrete mathematics and theoretical computer science, and helps students develop their skills in critical thinking and problem solving.
