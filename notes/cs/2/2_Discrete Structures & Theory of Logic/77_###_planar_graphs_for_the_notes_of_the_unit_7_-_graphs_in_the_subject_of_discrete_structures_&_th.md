### Planar graphs for the notes of the Unit 7 - Graphs in the subject of Discrete Structures & Theory of Logic

A planar graph is a type of graph that can be drawn on a plane such that no edges intersect. In other words, a planar graph can be represented without any crossings or overlapping edges.

Unit 7 of Discrete Structures & Theory of Logic focuses on the study of graphs and their properties. The notes for this unit should include the following topics:

1. Definition of a planar graph and its components (vertices, edges, faces)
2. Properties of planar graphs (connectivity, degree, Euler's formula)
3. Planarity testing algorithms (Kuratowski's theorem, Boyer-Myrvold algorithm)
4. Representation of planar graphs (dual graphs, planar embeddings)
5. Applications of planar graphs (network design, VLSI design, cartography)
6. Comparison with non-planar graphs (complete graphs, bipartite graphs)

Planar graphs are important in the study of discrete structures and the theory of logic because they provide a way to represent and solve problems in a way that is easy to visualize and understand. Understanding the properties and algorithms of planar graphs is essential for solving problems related to graphs and for studying more advanced topics in computer science.
