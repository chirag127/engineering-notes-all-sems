
### Relations for the Notes of the Unit 1 - Set Theory in the Subject of Discrete Structures & Theory of Logic

1. A relation is a set of ordered pairs of elements from two sets. An example of a relation is a function, which is a relation that satisfies certain conditions.

2. A relation can also be represented as a graph, which is a collection of points and lines that represent the elements and the relation between them.

3. A relation can be represented as a matrix, which is a table of values that represent the elements and the relation between them.

4. A relation can also be represented as a set of ordered pairs, which is a collection of elements and the relation between them.

5. Relations can be classified based on their properties, such as reflexivity, symmetry, and transitivity.

6. The composition of two relations is the result of combining two relations, which is a new relation that is formed by combining the elements of the two relations.

7. The inverse of a relation is a new relation that is formed by reversing the order of the elements in the relation.

8. The equivalence relation is a relation that satisfies certain properties, such as reflexivity, symmetry, and transitivity.

9. The equivalence classes of a relation are the sets of elements that are related to each other under the relation.

10. The power set of a set is the set of all subsets of the set, including the empty set.
