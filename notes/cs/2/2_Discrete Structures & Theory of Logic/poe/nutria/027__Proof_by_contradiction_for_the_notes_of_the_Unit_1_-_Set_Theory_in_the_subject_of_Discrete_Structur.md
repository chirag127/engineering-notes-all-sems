
### Proof by Contradiction for Unit 1 - Set Theory in Discrete Structures & Theory of Logic

1. Proof by contradiction is a form of logical argument in which one assumes the opposite of what is to be proven is true and then shows that this assumption leads to a logical contradiction.

2. In order to prove a statement by contradiction, one must assume the opposite of what is to be proven and then show that this assumption leads to a logical contradiction.

3. A logical contradiction occurs when two statements are logically incompatible, meaning that they cannot both be true at the same time.

4. To prove a statement by contradiction, one must first assume the opposite of what is to be proven and then demonstrate that this assumption leads to a logical contradiction.

5. For example, if one wishes to prove that the statement "All birds can fly" is true, then one must assume that the statement is false and then demonstrate that this leads to a logical contradiction. 

6. In this case, one could assume that there exists a bird that cannot fly, and then show that this assumption leads to a logical contradiction. This could be done by demonstrating that, if the bird cannot fly, then it is not a bird, which is a contradiction.

7. Thus, by assuming the opposite of what is to be proven and then demonstrating that this assumption leads to a logical contradiction, one can prove a statement by contradiction.
