
### Graph Coloring

Graph coloring is a way of assigning colors to the vertices of a graph in such a way that no two adjacent vertices have the same color. This problem has many applications in various fields such as scheduling, timetabling, and register allocation in computer science.

- A graph G is said to be k-colorable if it can be colored with k colors such that no two adjacent vertices have the same color.
- A graph is k-colorable if and only if its chromatic number is less than or equal to k.
- The chromatic number of a graph G is the minimum number of colors needed to color G.
- A graph is said to be k-critical if its chromatic number is k and it is not (k-1)-colorable.
- A graph is said to be k-choosable if it is possible to assign a list of k colors to each vertex such that no two adjacent vertices have the same color.
- The chromatic polynomial of a graph G is a polynomial that encodes the number of possible colorings of G with k colors.
- The four-color theorem states that any planar graph is 4-colorable.
