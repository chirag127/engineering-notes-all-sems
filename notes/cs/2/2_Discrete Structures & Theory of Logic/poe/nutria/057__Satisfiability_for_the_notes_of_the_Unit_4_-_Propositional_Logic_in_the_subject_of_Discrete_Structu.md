
### Satisfiability for the notes of the Unit 4 - Propositional Logic in the subject of Discrete Structures & Theory of Logic

1. Satisfiability is the ability of a logical expression to be true.
2. A propositional logic expression is said to be satisfiable if there exists an assignment of values to its variables such that the expression evaluates to true.
3. Propositional logic is a formal system that uses logical connectives to combine propositions and form compound statements.
4. In propositional logic, logical connectives such as 'and', 'or' and 'not' are used to combine individual statements into more complex statements.
5. The satisfiability problem is to determine whether a given logical expression is satisfiable or not.
6. The satisfiability problem can be solved using a variety of methods, including truth tables, resolution, and model checking.
7. Model checking is a method of verifying the correctness of a system by constructing a finite state machine model of the system and then verifying that the model satisfies the desired properties.
8. Resolution is a technique used in automated theorem proving to determine the validity of a logical expression.
9. Resolution works by taking a set of clauses and combining them to form a single clause that is either true or false.
10. The resolution method can be used to determine the satisfiability of a propositional logic expression.
