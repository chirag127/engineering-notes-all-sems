
### Definition for the notes of the Unit 3 - Lattices in the subject of Discrete Structures & Theory of Logic

* A **lattice** is a partially ordered set in which any two elements have a unique supremum (also called a least upper bound or join) and a unique infimum (also called a greatest lower bound or meet).
* A **lattice diagram** is a visual representation of a lattice, where the elements of the lattice are represented by points and the relationships between them are represented by lines.
* A **complete lattice** is a lattice in which every subset of the elements has a supremum and an infimum.
* A **Boolean lattice** is a lattice in which the elements are either true or false, and the join and meet operations are the logical operations of conjunction (AND) and disjunction (OR).
* A **distributive lattice** is a lattice in which the meet and join operations distribute over each other.
* A **modular lattice** is a lattice in which the meet and join operations satisfy the modular law.
* A **complemented lattice** is a lattice in which there is a complement operation that maps each element to its complement.
* A **bounded lattice** is a lattice in which there is a least element (bottom) and a greatest element (top).
* A **semilattice** is a partially ordered set in which any two elements have a unique supremum.
