
### Equality of Relations for the Notes of Unit 1 - Set Theory in the Subject of Discrete Structures & Theory of Logic

1. Two relations are said to be equal if they have the same domain, range, and set of ordered pairs. 
2. The domain of a relation is the set of all the first elements of the ordered pairs. 
3. The range of a relation is the set of all the second elements of the ordered pairs. 
4. The set of ordered pairs of a relation can be represented in a table, a graph, or a set of ordered pairs. 
5. The graph of a relation contains all the ordered pairs of the relation. 
6. The inverse of a relation is obtained by swapping the first and second elements of all the ordered pairs in the relation. 
7. The composition of two relations is obtained by combining the ordered pairs of the two relations. 
8. The composition of two relations is not necessarily equal to the composition of their inverses. 
9. A relation is said to be reflexive if it contains ordered pairs of the form (x, x) for all x in the domain. 
10. A relation is said to be symmetric if it contains ordered pairs of the form (x, y) and (y, x) for all x and y in the domain. 
11. A relation is said to be transitive if it contains ordered pairs of the form (x, y) and (y, z) implies (x, z) for all x, y, and z in the domain.
