
### Theory of Inference for the notes of the Unit 4 - Propositional Logic in the subject of Discrete Structures & Theory of Logic

1. In propositional logic, inference is the process of deriving logical consequences from given premises. 
2. Inference rules are the methods used to determine which logical conclusions can be drawn from given premises. 
3. The most commonly used inference rules in propositional logic are modus ponens, modus tollens, and hypothetical syllogism. 
4. Modus ponens is an inference rule that states that if a premise is true and a conclusion follows from that premise, then the conclusion must also be true. 
5. Modus tollens is an inference rule that states that if a premise is false and a conclusion follows from that premise, then the conclusion must also be false. 
6. Hypothetical syllogism is an inference rule that states that if two premises are true and a conclusion follows from those premises, then the conclusion must also be true. 
7. Inference rules can be used to prove the validity of arguments and to determine the truth value of statements. 
8. Inference rules can also be used to determine which logical conclusions can be drawn from given premises.
