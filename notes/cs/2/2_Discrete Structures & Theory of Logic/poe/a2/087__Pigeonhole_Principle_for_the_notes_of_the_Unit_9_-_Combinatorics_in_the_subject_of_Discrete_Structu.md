### Pigeonhole Principle

* The Pigeonhole Principle states that if n objects are put into m pigeonholes, with n > m, then at least one pigeonhole must contain more than one object.
* This is a counting argument that can be used to prove that a certain situation is impossible. It is a useful way to show that a system with limited resources cannot accommodate all possible demands.
* Example: If 10 letters are mailed to 8 recipients, then at least one recipient will receive at least 2 letters. There are more letters (10) than recipients (8), so the pigeonholes (recipients) cannot accommodate all letters.
* The Pigeonhole Principle can be generalized to any types of objects and pigeonholes. The key is simply that there must be more objects than pigeonholes. The Principle indicates that some pigeonhole ends up with multiple objects but does not specify which one(s). It only proves that such a situation must exist.
* The Pigeonhole Principle is a useful technique for proving statements of the form "such-and-such cannot be." It is often easy to apply but limited in its power. Stronger theorems are needed to prove statements of the form "such-and-such must be."
