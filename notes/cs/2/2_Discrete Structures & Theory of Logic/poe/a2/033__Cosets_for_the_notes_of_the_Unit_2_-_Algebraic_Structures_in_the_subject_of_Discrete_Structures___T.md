### Cosets for the notes of the Unit 2 - Algebraic Structures

1. A coset is a subset of a group that is shifted by a group element. Cosets are used to classify elements of a group based on their behavior under group operations.
2. Given a group G and a subgroup H, and an element a in G, the left coset of H with respect to a is the set aH = {ah | h is an element of H}. The right coset of H with respect to a is the set Ha = {ha | h is an element of H}.
3. The number of left cosets and the number of right cosets of a subgroup H in a group G are equal. This common number is called the index of H in G, denoted [G:H].
4. If [G:H] is finite, then the elements of G can be partitioned into a finite number of left (or right) cosets of H. The number of cosets in the partition is the index [G:H].
5. Lagrange's Theorem: If H is a subgroup of a finite group G, then the order of H divides the order of G. This means that if |G| is finite, then |H| must also be finite, and [G:H] must be finite.
