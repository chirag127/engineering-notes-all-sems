### Proof by counter – example for the notes of the Unit 1 - Set Theory in the subject of Discrete Structures & Theory of Logic

1. Proof by counter – example is a method of proof by contradiction.
2. It involves assuming the negation of the theorem we want to prove and then deriving a contradiction.
3. If we can derive a contradiction, then the original assumption must be false and hence the theorem must be true.
4. For example, to prove that the square root of 2 is irrational, we assume that it is rational, derive a contradiction, and conclude that the assumption is false.
5. The proof proceeds as follows:
- Assume: square root of 2 = p/q where p and q are integers.
- Since square root of 2 = p/q, we have 2 = p^2/q^2.
- So, 2q^2 = p^2. Since 2 and p^2 are both even, q^2 must be even.
- So q must be even.
- Dividing top and bottom of p/q by 2, we get that square root of 2 = (p/2)/(q/2) where (p/2) and (q/2) are integers.
- Repeat the same reasoning to show that q/2 is even.
- Proceeding in this way, we eventually show that q = 2k for some integer k.
- However, this contradicts the assumption that p/q is in lowest terms.
- Hence, the original assumption that square root of 2 is rational must be false.
- Therefore, square root of 2 is irrational.
