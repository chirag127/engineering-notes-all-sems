### Well Formed Formula of Predicate

1. A predicate is a statement that is either true or false for a given set of objects.
2. A predicate takes variables as input and yields either true or false as output.
3. The well formed formula of a predicate has the following structure:

P(x1, x2, ..., xn)

Where P is a predicate symbol and x1, x2, ..., xn are variables or constant symbols.
4. The number of variables in the predicate formula is called the arity of the predicate.
5. Predicate of arity 0 is called a proposition. It does not have any variable.
6. Predicate of arity 1 is called a unary predicate. It has one variable.
7. Predicate of arity 2 is called a binary predicate. It has two variables.
8. And so on. The arity can be any non-negative integer.

The above points cover the key aspects of well formed formula of a predicate. The formula has a predicate symbol along with required number of variables or constants based on the arity of the predicate. The variables can take values to evaluate the predicate to either true or false.
