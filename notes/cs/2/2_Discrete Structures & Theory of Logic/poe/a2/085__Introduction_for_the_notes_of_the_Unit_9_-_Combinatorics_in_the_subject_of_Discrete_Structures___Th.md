### Introduction for the notes of the Unit 9 - Combinatorics in the subject of Discrete Structures & Theory of Logic

1. Combinatorics is a branch of mathematics concerning the study of finite or countable discrete structures. It deals with objects that are separate and distinct rather than continuous.
2. The problems in combinatorics typically deal with counting the number of possible configurations or arrangements of the objects depending on certain constraints.
3. Some of the core combinatorial ideas are: permutation, combination, binomial coefficient, pigeonhole principle etc. These concepts are used to solve various counting problems and analyze properties of configurations.
4. Permutation: An arrangement of objects in a specific order. Order matters.
5. Combination: A selection of objects without regard to order. Order does not matter.
6. Binomial Coefficient: Used to calculate the number of permutations/combinations. It is computed using the factorial formula.
7. Pigeonhole Principle: If n pigeons are put into m pigeonholes, with n > m, then at least one pigeonhole must contain more than one pigeon. It is used to prove results about constraints and possibilities.
8. The topics covered in Unit 9 will include permutations, combinations, binomial coefficients, the pigeonhole principle and applications of these concepts in various counting problems. Solving problems is an important part of understanding and applying the concepts.
