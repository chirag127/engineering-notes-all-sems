### Normal Subgroups

1. A subgroup H of a group G is said to be normal if for every g in G and h in H, ghg^-1 is also in H.
2. In other words, H is normal in G if the conjugate of every element of H by any element of G is again an element of H.
3. Every subgroup is a subset of G, but not every subgroup is normal.
4. If H is a normal subgroup of G, then the quotient group G/H is defined.
5. The importance of normal subgroups is due to the fact that they permute with the group and hence are invariant under inner automorphisms.
6. Normal subgroups are also closed under taking conjugates.
7. Every subgroup of an abelian group is normal.
8. The kernel of a homomorphism is a normal subgroup.
9. Intersection of normal subgroups is again a normal subgroup.
10. If H and K are normal subgroups of G such that H is a subset of K, then K/H is isomorphic to a subgroup of G/H.
