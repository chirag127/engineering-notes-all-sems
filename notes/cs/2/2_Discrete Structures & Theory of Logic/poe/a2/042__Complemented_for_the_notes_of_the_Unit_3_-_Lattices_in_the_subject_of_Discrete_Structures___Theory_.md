### Complemented for the notes of the Unit 3 - Lattices in the subject of Discrete Structures & Theory of Logic

1. Lattice: A partially ordered set (poset) L is called a lattice if for all x, y in L, there exist greatest lower bound (glb) and least upper bound (lub). The glb of x and y is denoted by x ∧ y and is called the meet of x and y. The lub of x and y is denoted by x ∨ y and is called the join of x and y.

2. Complemented lattice: A lattice L is said to be complemented if for every x in L, there exists an element x' in L such that x ∧ x' = 0 and x ∨ x' = 1. Here 0 and 1 are the least and greatest elements of L respectively.

3. Distributive lattice: A lattice L is said to be distributive if for all x, y, z in L, x ∧ (y ∨ z) = (x ∧ y) ∨ (x ∧ z) and x ∨ (y ∧ z) = (x ∨ y) ∧ (x ∨ z). In other words, join and meet must distribute over each other.

4. Examples:
    1. The set of subsets of a set S ordered by inclusion ⊆ forms a distributive lattice. Here meet is intersection and join is union of subsets.
    2. The set of integers ordered by usual order ≥ forms a distributive lattice. Here meet is minimum and join is maximum of integers.
