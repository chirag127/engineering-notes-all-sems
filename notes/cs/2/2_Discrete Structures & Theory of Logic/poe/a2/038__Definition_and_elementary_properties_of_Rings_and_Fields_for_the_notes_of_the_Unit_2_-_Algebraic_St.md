### Definition and elementary properties of Rings and Fields for the notes of the Unit 2 - Algebraic Structures in the subject of Discrete Structures & Theory of Logic

1. Ring: A ring is a set R together with two binary operations + and * satisfying the following axioms:
- R is an Abelian group under +. (Closure, Associate, Identity, Inverse)
- * is associative.
- * distributes over +.
- There exists an identity element 1 for * (and an identity 0 = 1 + 1).

2. Field: A field is a ring R in which division is always possible, i.e. (∀ a, b ∈ R)(b ≠ 0 ⇒ ∃! q, r ∈ R : a = bq + r and either r = 0 or r = 1).
- Therefore, the multiplicative identity 1 is unique.
- The fields are exactly the rings in which the non-zero elements form a group under multiplication.

3. Examples:
- Integers Z, integers modulo n Zn, rational numbers Q, real numbers R, complex numbers C.
- Z and Zn are rings but not fields. Q, R, C are fields.

4. Properties:
- In a field, 0 ≠ 1 and -a exists for all a.
- In a field, a(b + c) = ab + ac and (a + b)c = ac + bc for all a, b, c.
- A field has exactly one solution to the equation xa = b.
- A field is a division ring.
