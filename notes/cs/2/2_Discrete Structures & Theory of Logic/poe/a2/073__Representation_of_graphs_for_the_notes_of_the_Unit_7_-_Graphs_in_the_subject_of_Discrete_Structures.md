### Representation of graphs for the notes of the Unit 7 - Graphs in the subject of Discrete Structures & Theory of Logic

1. Directed Graph: A graph in which edges have directions associated with them is called a directed graph. The edges are represented by arrows pointing from the initial vertex to the terminal vertex.

2. Undirected Graph: A graph in which edges do not have any direction is called an undirected graph. The edges are represented by lines connecting the vertices.

3. Weighted Graph: A graph in which each edge is assigned a weight or cost is called a weighted graph. The weights are represented by labels on the edges.

4. Unweighted Graph: A graph in which edges do not have any weights or costs assigned to them is called an unweighted graph.

5. Simple Graph: A graph which does not contain self-loops or parallel edges is called a simple graph.

6. Multigraph: A graph which can contain multiple parallel edges between the same pair of vertices but does not contain self-loops is called a multigraph.

7. Pseudograph: A graph which can contain self-loops and parallel edges is called a pseudograph.
