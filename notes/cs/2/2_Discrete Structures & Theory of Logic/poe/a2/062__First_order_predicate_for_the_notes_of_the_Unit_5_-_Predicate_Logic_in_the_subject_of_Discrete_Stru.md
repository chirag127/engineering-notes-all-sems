### First order predicate for the notes of the Unit 5 - Predicate Logic

1. A predicate is a statement that can be true or false depending upon the values of its variables.
2. In predicate logic, variables can range over objects, and predicates are used to express properties of and relationships between these objects.
3. The notation for predicates uses uppercase letters P,Q,R.... with variables as arguments. For example, "P(x,y)" is a predicate with two arguments/variables x and y.
4. Predicate logic uses quantifiers to specify the extent of the variables. The universal quantifier (symbol: ∀) is used to denote "for all" and the existential quantifier (symbol: ∃) is used to denote "there exists".
5. The notation for quantified statements is "quantifier variable: predicate". For example, ∀x: P(x) means "For all x, P(x) is true" and ∃y: Q(y,x) means "There exists a y such that Q(y,x) is true".
6. Predicate logic can be used to express mathematical statements in a precise manner. It provides a formal way to reason about properties and relationships of objects. It is a foundation of automated theorem proving.
