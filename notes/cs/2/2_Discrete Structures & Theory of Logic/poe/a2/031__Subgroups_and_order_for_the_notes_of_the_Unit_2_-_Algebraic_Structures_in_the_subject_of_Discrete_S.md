### Subgroups

- A nonempty subset H of a group G is called a subgroup of G if H is itself a group under the group operation of G.
- This means that the binary operation of G must be defined in such a way that, for all a, b in H, both a*b and a^-1 are in H.
- A subgroup H is a subset of G that is closed under the group operation and also contains the identity element of G.
- Every group G contains at least two subgroups: {e} and G itself. The subgroup {e} containing only the identity element is called the trivial subgroup.
- If H is a subgroup of G, then the set of left cosets of H forms a partition of G. Similarly, the set of right cosets of H forms a partition of G.

### Order of Elements

- For any element a in a group G, the order of a, denoted by ord(a), is the least positive integer n such that a^n = e, where e is the identity element of G.
- If no such positive integer exists, then ord(a) = infinity.
- The order of an element divides the order of the group. If G is finite, then the order of each element of G is also finite.
- An element of order 2 is called an involution. A group all of whose elements have order 2 is called a Boolean group or a group of order 2.
- A group is cyclic if all of its elements are powers of a single element. The minimum number of elements in a cyclic group is 1, and such a group consists of only the identity element.
