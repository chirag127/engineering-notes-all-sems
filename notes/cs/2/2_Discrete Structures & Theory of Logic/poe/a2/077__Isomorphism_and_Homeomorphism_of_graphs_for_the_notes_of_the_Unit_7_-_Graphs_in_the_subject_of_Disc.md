### Isomorphism and Homeomorphism of graphs

1. Isomorphism: Two graphs G1(V1, E1) and G2(V2, E2) are said to be isomorphic if there is a one-to-one correspondence between their vertices such that if two vertices are adjacent in G1 then their corresponding vertices are also adjacent in G2, and vice-versa.
2. Homeomorphism: Two graphs G1(V1, E1) and G2(V2, E2) are said to be homeomorphic if their vertex sets can be put into one-to-one correspondence in such a way that incidence is preserved, i.e. {u,v} is an edge of G1 if and only if {f(u),f(v)} is an edge of G2, where f is the mapping between V1 and V2.
3. Conditions for isomorphism:
    1. The number of vertices must be same in both graphs.
    2. The number of edges must be same in both graphs.
    3. The degree of each vertex must be same in both graphs.
    4. The adjacency of vertices must be preserved.
4. Conditions for homeomorphism:
    1. The incidence relations must be preserved.
5. Differences between isomorphism and homeomorphism:
    - Isomorphism requires vertex correspondences as well as edge correspondences whereas homeomorphism requires only incidence (edge) correspondences.
    - Isomorphic graphs are homeomorphic but the converse is not true. Two non-isomorphic graphs can be homeomorphic.
    - Isomorphism is an equivalence relation but homeomorphism is not.
