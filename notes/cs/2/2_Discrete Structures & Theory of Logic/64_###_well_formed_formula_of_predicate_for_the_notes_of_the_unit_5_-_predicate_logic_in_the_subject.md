### well formed formula of predicate for the notes of the Unit 5 - Predicate Logic in the subject of Discrete Structures & Theory of Logic

In predicate logic, a well-formed formula (WFF) is a formula that is constructed according to the rules of the logic. A WFF is also called a well-formed proposition or a well-formed statement.

A WFF in predicate logic consists of:
1. Predicates: A predicate is a symbol that represents a property or relation. It is usually a letter followed by one or more variables.
2. Variables: Variables are symbols that are used to represent objects. 
3. Quantifiers: Quantifiers are symbols that specify the scope of the variables in the formula. There are two types of quantifiers: universal quantifier (∀) and existential quantifier (∃).
4. Connectives: Connectives are symbols that are used to combine predicates and/or variables. There are three types of connectives: negation (¬), conjunction (∧), and disjunction (∨).

The rules for constructing a WFF in predicate logic are as follows:
1. A predicate applied to variables is a WFF.
2. If P is a WFF, then ¬P is a WFF.
3. If P and Q are WFFs, then (P ∧ Q), (P ∨ Q), (P → Q), and (P ↔ Q) are WFFs.
4. If P is a WFF and x is a variable, then ∀xP and ∃xP are WFFs.

It is important to note that a WFF must conform to the rules of predicate logic in order to be considered well-formed. Any formula that does not conform to the rules is considered to be ill-formed.
