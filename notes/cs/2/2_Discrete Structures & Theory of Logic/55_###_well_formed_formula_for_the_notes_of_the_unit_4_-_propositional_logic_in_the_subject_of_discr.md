A well-formed formula (WFF) is a formula in propositional logic that follows the rules of syntax and is free of any grammatical errors. In propositional logic, a WFF is a formula that is built using logical connectives (such as "and", "or", "not", "implies", etc.) and propositional variables (such as "p", "q", "r", etc.).

A WFF is well-formed if it follows the rules of syntax, which specify how the logical connectives and propositional variables can be combined to form a formula. For example, in most systems of propositional logic, a WFF must begin with a propositional variable or a negation symbol, and it must be properly parenthesized.

The rules of syntax for WFFs are important, as they ensure that the formula is unambiguous and can be evaluated by a computer or a human. In addition, the rules of syntax ensure that the formula has a well-defined meaning, which is essential for the study of propositional logic.

Once a formula has been determined to be a WFF, it can be evaluated using truth tables, which are used to determine the truth value of the formula for all possible combinations of truth values for its propositional variables. The truth value of a WFF is either "true" or "false".
