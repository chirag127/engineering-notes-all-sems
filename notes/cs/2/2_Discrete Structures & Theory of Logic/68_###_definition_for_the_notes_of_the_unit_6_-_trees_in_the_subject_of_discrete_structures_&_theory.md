### Definition for the notes of the Unit 6 - Trees in the subject of Discrete Structures & Theory of Logic

In discrete mathematics, a tree is a type of graph that consists of a set of nodes connected by directed edges. The nodes represent objects and the edges represent relationships between the objects.

Unit 6 of Discrete Structures & Theory of Logic focuses on the study of trees and their properties. The notes for this unit should include the following topics:

1. Definition of a tree and its components (nodes, edges, root, parent, child, sibling, leaf)
2. Properties of trees (acyclic, connected, rooted)
3. Types of trees (binary trees, complete trees, balanced trees)
4. Representation of trees (adjacency list, adjacency matrix)
5. Traversal algorithms for trees (preorder, inorder, postorder)
6. Applications of trees (data structures, algorithms, computer science)

It is important to understand the concepts and properties of trees in order to apply them in various fields and solve problems related to discrete structures and the theory of logic.
