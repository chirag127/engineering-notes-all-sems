### Proofs of some general identities on sets for the notes of the Unit 1 - Set Theory in the subject of Discrete Structures & Theory of Logic

"Proofs of General Identities on Sets" is a topic covered in Unit 1 - Set Theory in the subject of Discrete Structures & Theory of Logic.

Set theory is the mathematical study of sets, which are collections of objects. In set theory, there are several general identities that can be proven using mathematical proofs. These identities include:

1. Commutativity of Union: The commutativity of union states that the union of two sets is the same regardless of the order in which the sets are combined. This can be proven using a proof by contradiction.

2. Associativity of Union: The associativity of union states that the union of three or more sets is the same regardless of the order in which the sets are combined. This can be proven using a proof by induction.

3. Idempotence of Union: The idempotence of union states that the union of a set with itself is the same as the original set. This can be proven using a proof by contradiction.

4. Commutativity of Intersection: The commutativity of intersection states that the intersection of two sets is the same regardless of the order in which the sets are combined. This can be proven using a proof by contradiction.

5. Associativity of Intersection: The associativity of intersection states that the intersection of three or more sets is the same regardless of the order in which the sets are combined. This can be proven using a proof by induction.

6. Idempotence of Intersection: The idempotence of intersection states that the intersection of a set with itself is the same as the original set. This can be proven using a proof by contradiction.
