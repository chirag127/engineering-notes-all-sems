## Unit 6 - Trees

Trees are a fundamental data structure in computer science and are used to model hierarchical relationships. They are a type of graph that consists of nodes and edges, where each node has a parent node and zero or more child nodes. The node at the top of the hierarchy is called the root node, and the nodes without children are called leaves.

Unit 6 of Discrete Structures & Theory of Logic focuses on the study of trees and their properties. The notes for this unit should include the following topics:

1. Definition of a tree and its components (nodes, edges, root, leaves)
2. Types of trees (binary trees, n-ary trees, rooted trees, unrooted trees)
3. Tree traversal algorithms (pre-order, in-order, post-order)
4. Tree representation (adjacency list, adjacency matrix)
5. Tree properties (height, depth, level)
6. Tree algorithms (binary search tree, AVL tree, heap)
7. Applications of trees (file systems, decision trees, expression trees)

It is important to understand the concepts and properties of trees in order to apply them in various fields and solve problems related to discrete structures and the theory of logic.
