### Properties of lattices – Bounded for the notes of the Unit 3 - Lattices in the subject of Discrete Structures & Theory of Logic

A lattice is a partially ordered set (POSET) with the properties of reflexivity, antisymmetry, and transitivity. A lattice is called bounded if it has a least element (denoted by 0) and a greatest element (denoted by 1). These elements are unique and satisfy the following properties:

1. 0 is a lower bound for every element in the lattice.

2. 1 is an upper bound for every element in the lattice.

3. For every pair of elements x and y, there exists a unique least upper bound (denoted by x ∨ y) and a unique greatest lower bound (denoted by x ∧ y).

Bounded lattices have many useful properties and applications in various areas of mathematics and computer science, including formal logic, databases, and algorithms. They are also used in the study of algebraic structures, such as groups, rings, and fields.

In addition to being bounded, lattices may also have additional properties, such as distributivity, modularity, completeness, and more. These properties can be used to classify lattices into different types, such as distributive lattices, modular lattices, and complete lattices.
