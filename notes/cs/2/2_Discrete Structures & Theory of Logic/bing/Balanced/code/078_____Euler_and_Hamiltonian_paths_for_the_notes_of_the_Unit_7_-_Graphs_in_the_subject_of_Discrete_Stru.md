# Euler and Hamiltonian paths

- An **Euler path** is a path that passes through every edge of a graph exactly once . If it ends at the initial vertex, then it is an **Euler cycle** .
- A **Hamiltonian path** is a path that passes through every vertex of a graph exactly once  . If it ends at the initial vertex, then it is a **Hamiltonian cycle**  .
- Euler paths and cycles can exist in both directed and undirected graphs, while Hamiltonian paths and cycles can exist in both directed and undirected graphs as well as mixed graphs .
- Euler paths and cycles have a simple necessary and sufficient condition: a connected graph has an Euler cycle if and only if every vertex has even degree, and it has an Euler path if and only if it has exactly two vertices of odd degree .
- Hamiltonian paths and cycles do not have a simple necessary and sufficient condition, and finding them is an NP-complete problem . However, there are some sufficient conditions and some necessary conditions that can help determine the existence of Hamiltonian paths and cycles in some graphs .
- Some examples of sufficient conditions for Hamiltonian paths and cycles are: Dirac's theorem, Ore's theorem, Bondy-Chvátal theorem, and Pósa's theorem .
- Some examples of necessary conditions for Hamiltonian paths and cycles are: degree sum condition, closure condition, and connectivity condition .
- Euler paths and cycles have applications in problems such as the Seven Bridges of Königsberg, the Chinese Postman Problem, and the Knight's Tour Problem .
- Hamiltonian paths and cycles have applications in problems such as the Traveling Salesman Problem, the Hamiltonian Cycle Problem, and the Icosian Game .
