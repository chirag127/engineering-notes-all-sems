### Variants of Induction

- Mathematical induction is a proof technique that is used to show that a statement is true for all natural numbers or for all elements of a well-ordered set.
- There are different variants of induction that can be used depending on the nature of the statement and the base case.
- Some of the common variants of induction are:

  - **Ordinary induction**: This is the standard form of induction where the base case is usually 0 or 1 and the induction step is to show that if the statement is true for n, then it is true for n+1.
  - **Strong induction**: This is a form of induction where the induction step is to show that if the statement is true for all natural numbers less than or equal to n, then it is true for n+1. This is useful when the statement depends on more than one previous case.
  - **Complete induction**: This is a form of induction where the induction step is to show that if the statement is false for any natural number less than n, then it is false for n. This is useful when the statement is a negation or a contradiction.
  - **Structural induction**: This is a form of induction where the statement is about a recursively defined structure, such as a tree or a list, and the induction step is to show that if the statement is true for all smaller structures, then it is true for the given structure. This is useful when the statement is about a property or a function of the structure.
  - **Transfinite induction**: This is a form of induction where the statement is about all elements of a well-ordered set, such as the ordinal numbers or the cardinal numbers, and the induction step is to show that if the statement is true for all elements less than a given element, then it is true for that element. This is useful when the statement is about a property or a function of the set.
