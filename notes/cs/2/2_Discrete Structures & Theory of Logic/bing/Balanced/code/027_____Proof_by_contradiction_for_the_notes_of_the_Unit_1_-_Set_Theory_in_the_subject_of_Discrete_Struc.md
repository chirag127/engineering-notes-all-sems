### Proof by contradiction

- A proof by contradiction is a method of proving a statement by assuming that it is false and deriving a contradiction from that assumption.
- The contradiction can be either a logical inconsistency or a violation of a known fact or theorem.
- The basic form of a proof by contradiction is:

```
Assume that P is false (or not P is true).
Show that this assumption leads to a contradiction Q and not Q.
Conclude that P is true.
```

- An example of a proof by contradiction is:

```
Prove that √2 is irrational.

Assume that √2 is rational, that is, √2 = a/b for some integers a and b.
Without loss of generality, assume that a and b have no common factors.
Squaring both sides, we get 2 = a^2/b^2, or 2b^2 = a^2.
This implies that a^2 is even, and therefore a is even.
Let a = 2k for some integer k.
Substituting, we get 2b^2 = (2k)^2, or b^2 = 2k^2.
This implies that b^2 is even, and therefore b is even.
But this contradicts the assumption that a and b have no common factors, since they both have 2 as a factor.
Therefore, √2 is irrational.
```
