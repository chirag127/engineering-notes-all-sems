### Proof by counter – example for the notes of the Unit 1 - Set Theory in the subject of Discrete Structures & Theory of Logic

- A proof by counter-example is a method of disproving a universal statement by providing a specific case that contradicts it.
- A universal statement is a statement that claims something is true for all elements of a certain set or domain, such as "all natural numbers are even" or "all dogs are mammals".
- A counter-example is a specific element of the set or domain that makes the statement false, such as 3 for the first statement or a platypus for the second statement.
- To prove by counter-example, one must find a counter-example and show that it satisfies the conditions of the statement and that it makes the statement false.
- For example, to disprove the statement "all prime numbers are odd", one can use 2 as a counter-example. 2 is a prime number, so it satisfies the condition of the statement, but 2 is not odd, so it makes the statement false.
- A proof by counter-example can only be used to disprove universal statements, not to prove them. To prove a universal statement, one must use other methods, such as direct proof, induction, or contradiction.
