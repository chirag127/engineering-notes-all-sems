# Definition for the notes of the Unit 3 - Lattices in the subject of Discrete Structures & Theory of Logic

- A **lattice** is an abstract structure studied in the mathematical subdisciplines of order theory and abstract algebra.
- It consists of a **partially ordered set** in which every pair of elements has a unique **supremum** (also called a **least upper bound** or **join**) and a unique **infimum** (also called a **greatest lower bound** or **meet**).
- A lattice can also be defined as a **non-empty set** closed under two binary operations called **meet** and **join**, denoted by **∧** and **∨**, that satisfy certain axioms such as commutativity, associativity, idempotency, absorption and distributivity .
- A lattice can be represented by a **Hasse diagram**, which is a graphical representation of the partial order relation, where the elements are drawn as nodes and the order relation is shown by edges connecting lower elements to higher elements.
- A lattice is said to be **complete** if every subset of the lattice has a join and meet, such as the divisibility relation for the natural numbers.
- A lattice is said to be **bounded** if it has a least and greatest element, denoted by **0** and **1** respectively.
- A lattice is said to be **complemented** if every element has a unique complement, such that the join of an element and its complement is 1 and the meet of an element and its complement is 0.
- A lattice is said to be **distributive** if the meet and join operations satisfy the distributive laws, such as a ∧ (b ∨ c) = (a ∧ b) ∨ (a ∧ c) and a ∨ (b ∧ c) = (a ∨ b) ∧ (a ∨ c).
- A lattice is said to be **modular** if it satisfies the modular law, such as a ∨ (b ∧ c) = (a ∨ b) ∧ c whenever a ≤ c.
- A lattice is said to be **Boolean** if it is a bounded, complemented and distributive lattice, such as the set of subsets of a given set under the inclusion relation.
