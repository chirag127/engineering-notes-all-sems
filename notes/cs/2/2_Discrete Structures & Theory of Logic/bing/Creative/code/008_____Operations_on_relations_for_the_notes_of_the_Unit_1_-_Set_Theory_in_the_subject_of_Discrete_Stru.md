### Operations on relations

- A relation is a set of ordered pairs that represents a connection or association between two sets, called the domain and the codomain.
- There are different types of relations, such as reflexive, symmetric, transitive, antisymmetric, and equivalence relations.
- There are also different operations that can be performed on relations, such as union, intersection, complement, inverse, composition, and power.
- The union of two relations R and S, denoted by R ∪ S, is the relation that contains all the ordered pairs that belong to either R or S, or both.
- The intersection of two relations R and S, denoted by R ∩ S, is the relation that contains all the ordered pairs that belong to both R and S.
- The complement of a relation R, denoted by R<sup>c</sup>, is the relation that contains all the ordered pairs that do not belong to R.
- The inverse of a relation R, denoted by R<sup>-1</sup>, is the relation that contains all the ordered pairs obtained by reversing the order of the elements in R.
- The composition of two relations R and S, denoted by R ◦ S, is the relation that contains all the ordered pairs (a, c) such that there exists an element b in the codomain of R and the domain of S, for which (a, b) ∈ R and (b, c) ∈ S.
- The power of a relation R, denoted by R<sup>n</sup>, is the relation obtained by composing R with itself n times, where n is a positive integer. For example, R<sup>2</sup> = R ◦ R, R<sup>3</sup> = R ◦ R ◦ R, and so on. R<sup>1</sup> = R and R<sup>0</sup> is the identity relation, which contains all the ordered pairs of the form (a, a) for every element a in the domain and codomain of R.
- These operations on relations have some properties, such as commutativity, associativity, distributivity, identity, and inverse.
