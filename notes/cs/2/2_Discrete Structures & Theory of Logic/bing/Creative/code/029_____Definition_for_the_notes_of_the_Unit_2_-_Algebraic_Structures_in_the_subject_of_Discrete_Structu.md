### Definition for the notes of the Unit 2 - Algebraic Structures in the subject of Discrete Structures & Theory of Logic

- An **algebraic structure** is a mathematical object that consists of a set of elements and one or more operations that satisfy certain properties or axioms.
- **Discrete structures** are mathematical objects that are finite or countable, such as sets, graphs, logic, relations, functions, etc.
- **Discrete mathematics** is the branch of mathematics that studies discrete structures and their applications in computer science, cryptography, combinatorics, etc.
- An **algebraic system** is an algebraic structure that has a specific type of operation, such as addition, multiplication, inversion, etc.
- An **algebraic property** is a property that holds for any algebraic system, such as commutativity, associativity, distributivity, identity, inverse, etc.
- Some examples of algebraic systems are groups, rings, fields, vector spaces, matrices, etc .
