## Unit 4 - Propositional Logic

Propositional logic is a branch of logic that studies the ways of combining or modifying statements, called propositions, using logical connectives, such as `and`, `or`, `not`, `if ... then`, etc. Propositional logic also defines the rules for determining the truth value of a proposition, given the truth values of its components.

Some of the main topics covered in this unit are:

- **Syntax and semantics of propositional logic**: This topic introduces the basic elements of propositional logic, such as atomic propositions, logical connectives, compound propositions, truth tables, and logical equivalence.
- **Normal forms and simplification of propositions**: This topic shows how to transform a proposition into different forms, such as conjunctive normal form (CNF), disjunctive normal form (DNF), and negation normal form (NNF), using logical laws and rules. It also explains how to simplify a proposition using Boolean algebra and Karnaugh maps.
- **Validity and satisfiability of propositions**: This topic explains the concepts of validity and satisfiability of a proposition, and how to test them using truth tables, logical laws, and resolution method. It also introduces the notion of logical consequence and logical entailment.
- **Applications of propositional logic**: This topic illustrates some of the applications of propositional logic in various domains, such as circuit design, artificial intelligence, cryptography, and logic puzzles. It also discusses some of the limitations and extensions of propositional logic, such as modal logic, temporal logic, and fuzzy logic.
