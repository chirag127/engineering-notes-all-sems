### Modular and Complete lattice for the notes of the Unit 3 - Lattices in the subject of Discrete Structures & Theory of Logic

A modular lattice is a partially ordered set (poset) in which every element has a unique decomposition as a meet (infimum) and a join (supremum) of some subset of the lower and upper covers of that element. A complete lattice is a poset in which every subset has a least upper bound (join) and a greatest lower bound (meet). Both modular and complete lattices have important applications in mathematics, computer science, and engineering.

In a modular lattice, the modularity condition states that for any element a and any subset S of the lower covers of a, the meet of S is equal to the meet of S union {a} if and only if a is the join of S. This property ensures that the lattice has a unique decomposition of its elements.

In a complete lattice, the completeness condition states that every subset has a least upper bound (join) and a greatest lower bound (meet). This property ensures that the lattice is closed under arbitrary suprema and infima, making it useful for modeling various mathematical structures.

Some examples of complete lattices include the power set of a set (ordered by inclusion), the set of all subsets of a given set ordered by set inclusion, and the real numbers ordered by the usual ordering. Modular lattices also have many applications, including the study of algebraic systems, the design of algorithms, and the formulation of mathematical models in physics and engineering.
