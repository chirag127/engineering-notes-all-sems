### Proposition for the notes of the Unit 4 - Propositional Logic in the subject of Discrete Structures & Theory of Logic

A proposition is a statement that is either true or false but not both. In propositional logic, propositions are used to build more complex statements and arguments. Propositions are typically represented by symbols or letters, such as "p" or "q."

In propositional logic, the truth value of a proposition can be determined based on the truth values of its constituent propositions and the rules of inference used to connect them. The rules of inference used in propositional logic include modus ponens, modus tollens, and the law of the excluded middle.

A proposition can be combined with other propositions using logical connectives, such as "and" (∧), "or" (∨), "not" (¬), "implies" (→), and "if and only if" (↔). These connectives allow us to build complex statements that can be evaluated for truth or falsity.

Propositions play a central role in many areas of mathematics and computer science, including artificial intelligence, formal verification, and automated theorem proving. Propositional logic is often used as a foundation for more advanced logical systems, such as first-order logic and higher-order logic.
