### Operations on functions for the notes of the Unit 1 - Set Theory in the subject of Discrete Structures & Theory of Logic

Operations on functions are mathematical operations that can be performed on functions to produce a new function. The following are some of the operations that can be performed on functions:

1. Composition of functions: The composition of functions is the process of combining two or more functions to produce a new function. The composition of two functions f and g is denoted by f o g, and is defined as (f o g)(x) = f(g(x)).

2. Inverse of a function: The inverse of a function is a function that "undoes" the effect of the original function. A function f is invertible if and only if it is a bijection, meaning it is both injective (one-to-one) and surjective (onto). The inverse of a function f is denoted by f^-1 and is defined as f^-1(f(x)) = x.

3. Identity function: The identity function is a function that maps every element of its domain to itself. The identity function is denoted by I and is defined as I(x) = x for all x in its domain.

4. Constant function: A constant function is a function that maps every element of its domain to the same value. The constant function is denoted by c, where c is a constant, and is defined as c(x) = k for all x in its domain, where k is a constant.

5. Sum of functions: The sum of functions is the process of adding two or more functions to produce a new function. The sum of two functions f and g is denoted by f + g, and is defined as (f + g)(x) = f(x) + g(x).

6. Product of functions: The product of functions is the process of multiplying two or more functions to produce a new function. The product of two functions f and g is denoted by fg, and is defined as (fg)(x) = f(x)g(x).

These operations on functions are important concepts in the subject of Discrete Structures & Theory of Logic, and are used to study the properties and behavior of functions in various mathematical models.
