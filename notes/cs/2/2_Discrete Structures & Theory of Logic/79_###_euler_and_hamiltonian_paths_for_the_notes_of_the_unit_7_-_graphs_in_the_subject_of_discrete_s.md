### Euler and Hamiltonian paths for the notes of the Unit 7 - Graphs in the subject of Discrete Structures & Theory of Logic

Euler and Hamiltonian paths are two important concepts in graph theory, which is the study of graphs and their properties.

An Euler path is a path in a graph that visits every edge exactly once. An Euler circuit is an Euler path that starts and ends at the same vertex. A graph has an Euler path if and only if it is connected and has exactly two vertices with odd degree.

A Hamiltonian path is a path in a graph that visits every vertex exactly once. A Hamiltonian cycle is a Hamiltonian path that starts and ends at the same vertex. A graph has a Hamiltonian cycle if and only if it is connected and every vertex has degree at least two.

In the notes for Unit 7 - Graphs in the subject of Discrete Structures & Theory of Logic, the following topics should be covered:

1. Definition of Euler and Hamiltonian paths
2. Conditions for the existence of Euler and Hamiltonian paths in a graph
3. Algorithms for finding Euler and Hamiltonian paths (Fleury's algorithm, Hierholzer's algorithm, backtracking)
4. Applications of Euler and Hamiltonian paths (network design, scheduling, optimization)

It is important to understand the concepts and algorithms for Euler and Hamiltonian paths in order to apply them in various fields and solve problems related to discrete structures and the theory of logic.
