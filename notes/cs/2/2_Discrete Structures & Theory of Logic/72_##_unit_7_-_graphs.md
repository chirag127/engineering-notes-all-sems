## Unit 7 - Graphs

Graph theory is a branch of mathematics that deals with the study of graphs, which are mathematical structures used to model relationships between objects. A graph is a collection of vertices (also known as nodes) and edges. The vertices represent objects and the edges represent relationships between the objects.

Unit 7 of Discrete Structures & Theory of Logic focuses on the study of graphs and their properties. The notes for this unit should include the following topics:

1. Definition of a graph and its components (vertices, edges, degree, adjacency)
2. Types of graphs (simple, multigraph, pseudograph, directed, undirected)
3. Representation of graphs (adjacency matrix, adjacency list)
4. Graph operations (subgraph, induced subgraph, complement)
5. Paths and cycles in graphs (simple path, cycle, length, distance)
6. Connectivity in graphs (connected, disconnected, connected component, cut vertex, cut edge)
7. Euler's theorem and Hamiltonian cycles
8. Graph coloring (chromatic number, chromatic polynomial, four color theorem)
9. Planar graphs (planar, non-planar, Euler's formula)
10. Graph algorithms (breadth-first search, depth-first search, minimum spanning tree, shortest path)

The study of graphs is important in many areas of computer science, including computer networks, algorithms, and data structures. Understanding the concepts and properties of graphs is essential for solving problems in these areas and for studying more advanced topics in discrete mathematics.
