### Definition for the notes of the Unit 3 - Lattices in the subject of Discrete Structures & Theory of Logic

In discrete structures, lattices are mathematical structures that consist of a partially ordered set with a binary operation that satisfies certain properties. The most common binary operation is the meet (also known as the greatest lower bound) and the join (also known as the least upper bound). The meet and join operations allow one to compare elements in the lattice and determine relationships between them.

Unit 3 of Discrete Structures & Theory of Logic focuses on the study of lattices and their properties. The notes for this unit should include the following topics:

1. Definition of a lattice and its components (partial order, binary operation, meet, join)
2. Properties of lattices (associativity, commutativity, idempotency, distributivity)
3. Types of lattices (bounded/unbounded, distributive/non-distributive, complemented/non-complemented)
4. Representation of lattices (Hasse diagrams, duality)
5. Examples of lattices (Boolean algebras, modular lattices)
6. Applications of lattices (computer science, information theory, order theory)

It is important to understand the concepts and properties of lattices in order to apply them in various fields and solve problems related to discrete structures and the theory of logic.
