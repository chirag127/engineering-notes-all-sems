### Definition and terminology for the notes of the Unit 7 - Graphs in the subject of Discrete Structures & Theory of Logic

Graph theory is the study of graphs and their properties. A graph is a mathematical structure consisting of a set of vertices (also called nodes) and a set of edges that connect the vertices. The vertices represent objects or entities, while the edges represent relationships or connections between the objects.

In graph theory, there are several important terms and definitions that are used to describe the properties of graphs. Some of these terms include:

1. Degree: The degree of a vertex is the number of edges incident to the vertex.

2. Path: A path is a sequence of vertices connected by edges.

3. Cycle: A cycle is a path that starts and ends at the same vertex.

4. Connected: A graph is connected if there is a path between any two vertices in the graph.

5. Complete: A complete graph is a graph in which every vertex is connected to every other vertex.

6. Subgraph: A subgraph is a graph that is contained within another graph.

7. Weight: A weight is a numerical value assigned to an edge that represents its importance or cost.

8. Adjacency: Two vertices are said to be adjacent if they are connected by an edge.

9. Incident: An edge is said to be incident to a vertex if it is connected to the vertex.

In this unit, we will study the basic definitions and terminology of graph theory, as well as the properties and algorithms for manipulating and analyzing graphs. We will also study the applications of graph theory in computer science, mathematics, and other fields.
