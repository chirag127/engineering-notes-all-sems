Skewness is a measure of the asymmetry of a probability distribution. It is used to describe the shape of a distribution and to determine if it is symmetric or not. Skewness is calculated as the third standardized moment of the distribution, and it is a dimensionless quantity that can be positive, negative, or zero.

A positive skewness indicates that the distribution is skewed to the right, with a longer tail on the positive side. This means that the mean and median of the distribution are greater than the mode, and that the majority of the data is concentrated on the left side of the distribution.

A negative skewness indicates that the distribution is skewed to the left, with a longer tail on the negative side. This means that the mean and median of the distribution are less than the mode, and that the majority of the data is concentrated on the right side of the distribution.

A skewness of zero indicates that the distribution is symmetric, with the mean, median, and mode all equal. This means that the data is evenly distributed around the center of the distribution, and that there are no outliers or skewness in the data.

Skewness is an important measure of the shape of a distribution, as it provides information about the distribution that is not captured by other measures, such as the mean and variance. It is used in a variety of applications, including hypothesis testing, regression analysis, and financial modeling.
