### Moments

- A moment is a measure of the tendency of a distribution to rotate about a point. It is calculated by multiplying each value of a variable by a weight and then summing up the products.
- Moments are useful for describing the shape and features of a distribution, such as its symmetry, skewness, kurtosis, and outliers.
- There are different types of moments, such as raw moments, central moments, standardized moments, and factorial moments. Each type has a different formula and interpretation.
- The raw moments are the simplest type of moments. They are calculated by taking the sum of the powers of the variable values. The formula for the nth raw moment is:

  $$M_n = \sum_{i=1}^n x_i^n$$

  where $x_i$ is the ith value of the variable and n is the order of the moment.

- The central moments are calculated by subtracting the mean from each value of the variable before taking the powers and summing them up. The formula for the nth central moment is:

  $$\mu_n = \sum_{i=1}^n (x_i - \bar{x})^n$$

  where $\bar{x}$ is the mean of the variable.

- The standardized moments are calculated by dividing the central moments by a suitable power of the standard deviation. The formula for the nth standardized moment is:

  $$\gamma_n = \frac{\mu_n}{\sigma^n}$$

  where $\sigma$ is the standard deviation of the variable.

- The factorial moments are calculated by multiplying the powers of the variable values by the falling factorial coefficients. The formula for the nth factorial moment is:

  $$F_n = \sum_{i=1}^n x_i(x_i-1)(x_i-2)...(x_i-n+1)$$

  where the falling factorial coefficient is defined as:

  $$x_i(x_i-1)(x_i-2)...(x_i-n+1) = \frac{x_i!}{(x_i-n)!}$$

- The moments of different orders have different names and interpretations. For example, the first raw moment is the mean, the second central moment is the variance, the third standardized moment is the skewness, and the fourth standardized moment is the kurtosis. The first factorial moment is the mean, the second factorial moment is the second raw moment minus the mean, and so on.
