### Measures of central tendency

- Measures of central tendency are numerical values that summarize the distribution of a set of data by locating the center or the most typical value of the data.
- The most common measures of central tendency are the mean, the median and the mode.
- The mean is the arithmetic average of the data, calculated by adding up all the values and dividing by the number of values.
- The median is the middle value of the data when arranged in ascending or descending order. If there is an even number of values, the median is the average of the middle two values.
- The mode is the most frequently occurring value in the data. There can be more than one mode if there are multiple values with the same frequency.
- The choice of the appropriate measure of central tendency depends on the type and shape of the data, as well as the purpose of the analysis.
- Some advantages and disadvantages of each measure are:

| Measure | Advantages | Disadvantages |
|---------|------------|---------------|
| Mean | - It uses all the data values. <br> - It is easy to calculate and understand. <br> - It is widely used in statistical analysis. | - It is sensitive to outliers and extreme values. <br> - It may not be a representative value for skewed or multimodal distributions. <br> - It may not exist for some types of data, such as nominal or ordinal data. |
| Median | - It is not affected by outliers and extreme values. <br> - It is a representative value for skewed distributions. <br> - It can be calculated for ordinal data. | - It does not use all the data values. <br> - It may not be unique if there are many ties in the data. <br> - It is more difficult to calculate and manipulate than the mean. |
| Mode | - It can be calculated for any type of data, including nominal data. <br> - It reflects the most popular or typical value in the data. <br> - It can be used to describe multimodal distributions. | - It does not use all the data values. <br> - It may not exist if there are no repeated values in the data. <br> - It may not be a representative value for symmetric or uniform distributions. |
