### Statistical Quality Control (SQC)

- Statistical quality control (SQC) is a method of monitoring and controlling the quality of a product or process by using statistical tools and techniques .
- SQC involves collecting data on the quality of a product or process and using statistical analysis to identify trends and patterns in the data .
- SQC can be applied to both the inputs and outputs of a production process.
- SQC can help to ensure that the process operates efficiently, producing more specification-conforming products with less waste and scrap .
- SQC can also help to detect and prevent defects, errors, and variations in the product or process .
- SQC can be classified into two main categories: descriptive statistics and inferential statistics.
- Descriptive statistics summarize the data collected from the product or process, such as mean, median, mode, range, standard deviation, frequency distribution, etc.
- Inferential statistics draw conclusions or make predictions about the product or process based on the data collected, such as hypothesis testing, confidence intervals, control charts, etc.
- Some of the common statistical tools and techniques used in SQC are:
  - Seven basic quality control tools: cause-and-effect diagram, check sheet, control chart, histogram, Pareto chart, scatter diagram, and stratification.
  - Seven supplementary quality control tools: affinity diagram, interrelationship diagram, tree diagram, matrix diagram, prioritization matrix, process decision program chart, and activity network diagram.
  - Other tools and techniques: acceptance sampling, design of experiments, statistical process control, etc.
