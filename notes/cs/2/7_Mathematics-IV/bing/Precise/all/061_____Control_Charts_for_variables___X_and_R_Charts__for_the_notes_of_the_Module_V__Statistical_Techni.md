# Control Charts for Variables (X and R Charts)

Control charts for variables are used to monitor the quality of a process by measuring the variation in the process over time. These charts are commonly used in manufacturing and other industries to ensure that the process is in control and producing consistent results.

There are two types of control charts for variables: X charts and R charts.

## X Charts

X charts, also known as X-bar charts, are used to monitor the mean of a process. The chart plots the average of a sample of measurements taken from the process over time. The center line of the chart represents the average of all the sample means, and the upper and lower control limits are calculated based on the standard deviation of the sample means.

If the points on the chart fall within the control limits, the process is considered to be in control. If the points fall outside the control limits, or if there is a pattern in the points, the process may be out of control and corrective action may be necessary.

## R Charts

R charts, also known as range charts, are used to monitor the variation within a process. The chart plots the range of a sample of measurements taken from the process over time. The center line of the chart represents the average range of all the samples, and the upper and lower control limits are calculated based on the standard deviation of the sample ranges.

Like with X charts, if the points on the R chart fall within the control limits, the process is considered to be in control. If the points fall outside the control limits, or if there is a pattern in the points, the process may be out of control and corrective action may be necessary.

X and R charts are often used together to monitor both the mean and the variation of a process. By using both charts, it is possible to detect changes in the process that may affect the quality of the output.
