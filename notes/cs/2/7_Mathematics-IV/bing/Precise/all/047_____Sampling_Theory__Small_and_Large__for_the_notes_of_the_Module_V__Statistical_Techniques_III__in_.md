# Sampling Theory (Small and Large)

Sampling theory is a branch of statistics that deals with the selection, collection, and analysis of samples from a population. It is used to make inferences about the population based on the information obtained from the sample.

There are two types of sampling: small and large.

## Small Sampling

Small sampling refers to the process of selecting a sample from a population when the sample size is small (usually less than 30). In this case, the sample is not large enough to accurately represent the population, so the results obtained from the sample may not be reliable.

Some common techniques used in small sampling include:

- T-test: used to compare the means of two samples.
- Chi-squared test: used to test the independence of two categorical variables.
- Fisher's exact test: used to test the association between two categorical variables when the sample size is small.

## Large Sampling

Large sampling refers to the process of selecting a sample from a population when the sample size is large (usually greater than 30). In this case, the sample is large enough to accurately represent the population, so the results obtained from the sample are more reliable.

Some common techniques used in large sampling include:

- Z-test: used to compare the means of two large samples.
- Chi-squared test: used to test the independence of two categorical variables.
- Analysis of variance (ANOVA): used to compare the means of more than two samples.

In conclusion, sampling theory is an important tool in statistics that allows us to make inferences about a population based on the information obtained from a sample. The reliability of the results depends on the size of the sample and the techniques used to analyze the data.
