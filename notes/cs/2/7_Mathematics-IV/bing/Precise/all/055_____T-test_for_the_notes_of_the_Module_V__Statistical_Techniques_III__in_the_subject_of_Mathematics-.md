# T-test

A t-test is a statistical hypothesis test that is used to determine if there is a significant difference between the means of two groups. It is commonly used in research to compare the means of two samples.

The t-test is based on the t-distribution, which is a continuous probability distribution that is used to model the behavior of a sample mean when the population standard deviation is unknown. The t-distribution is similar to the normal distribution, but it has heavier tails, which means that it is more likely to produce extreme values.

There are several types of t-tests, including the one-sample t-test, the paired t-test, and the two-sample t-test. Each type of t-test is used in different situations, depending on the research question and the data that is available.

The one-sample t-test is used to compare the mean of a sample to a known population mean. The paired t-test is used to compare the means of two related samples, such as measurements taken before and after an intervention. The two-sample t-test is used to compare the means of two independent samples.

When conducting a t-test, it is important to check the assumptions of the test. These assumptions include normality, independence, and equal variances. If the assumptions are not met, the results of the t-test may not be valid.

In conclusion, the t-test is a powerful statistical tool that can be used to compare the means of two groups. It is important to carefully select the appropriate type of t-test and to check the assumptions of the test before interpreting the results.
