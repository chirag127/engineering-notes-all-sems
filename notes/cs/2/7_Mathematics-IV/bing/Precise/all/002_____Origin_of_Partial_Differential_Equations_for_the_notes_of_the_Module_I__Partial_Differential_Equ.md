# Origin of Partial Differential Equations

Partial Differential Equations (PDEs) are equations that involve partial derivatives of functions of several variables. They are used to model a wide range of physical, biological, and economic phenomena.

The origin of PDEs can be traced back to the 18th century, when mathematicians such as Euler, d'Alembert, and Lagrange began to study the wave equation, which describes the propagation of waves. This equation is a second-order linear PDE, and its study led to the development of techniques for solving PDEs.

Another important PDE that was studied in the 18th century is the heat equation, which describes the diffusion of heat in a medium. This equation is also a second-order linear PDE, and its study led to the development of the method of separation of variables, which is still widely used today to solve PDEs.

In the 19th century, mathematicians such as Gauss, Green, and Riemann made significant contributions to the study of PDEs. They developed the theory of potential functions, which is used to solve PDEs that arise in electrostatics and gravitation.

In the 20th century, the study of PDEs continued to flourish, with mathematicians such as Sobolev, Schwartz, and Nash making significant contributions to the field. Today, PDEs continue to be an active area of research, with applications in fields such as fluid dynamics, finance, and image processing.

In summary, the origin of PDEs can be traced back to the 18th century, when mathematicians began to study equations such as the wave equation and the heat equation. The study of these equations led to the development of techniques for solving PDEs, and the field has continued to evolve and expand ever since.
