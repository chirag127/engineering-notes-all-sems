### Addition and multiplication law of probability

- The addition law of probability states that the probability of the occurrence of at least one of two mutually exclusive events is the sum of their individual probabilities.

- Mathematically, if A and B are two mutually exclusive events, then P(A or B) = P(A) + P(B).

- The multiplication law of probability states that the probability of the occurrence of two independent events is the product of their individual probabilities.

- Mathematically, if A and B are two independent events, then P(A and B) = P(A) * P(B).

- These laws are fundamental to the study of probability and are used to solve a wide range of problems.

- It is important to note that the addition law only applies to mutually exclusive events, while the multiplication law only applies to independent events.

- Mutually exclusive events are events that cannot occur at the same time, while independent events are events whose occurrence does not affect the probability of the other event occurring.

- Understanding these laws and their applications is crucial for solving problems involving probability.
