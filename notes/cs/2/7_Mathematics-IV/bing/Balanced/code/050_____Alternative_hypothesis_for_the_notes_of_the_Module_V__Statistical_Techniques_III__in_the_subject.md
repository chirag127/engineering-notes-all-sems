### Alternative hypothesis for the notes of the Module V: Statistical Techniques III: in the subject of Mathematics-IV KCS

- An alternative hypothesis is a proposed statement or argument in a hypothesis test that indicates the existence of a statistical relationship between variables.
- It is often denoted as Ha or H1 and usually aligns with the research hypothesis .
- It is the complement to the null hypothesis, which is the default assumption that there is no relationship between variables or no difference between groups.
- In statistical hypothesis testing, the null and alternative hypotheses are two mutually exclusive statements that can be tested by using data.
- The alternative hypothesis is what we want to prove or show evidence for, while the null hypothesis is what we want to reject or show evidence against.
- For example, if we suspect that girls take longer to get ready for school than boys, then the alternative hypothesis is: Ha: girls time > boys time, and the null hypothesis is: H0: girls time <= boys time.
- The alternative hypothesis can be one-sided or two-sided, depending on whether we are interested in a specific direction of the relationship or difference, or in any direction.
- For example, a one-sided alternative hypothesis is: Ha: the mean height of men is greater than the mean height of women, and a two-sided alternative hypothesis is: Ha: the mean height of men is not equal to the mean height of women.
- The choice of the alternative hypothesis affects the type of statistical test that can be used and the interpretation of the results.
- The alternative hypothesis should be based on a clear research question, a sound theoretical or empirical background, and a reasonable expectation of the outcome.
