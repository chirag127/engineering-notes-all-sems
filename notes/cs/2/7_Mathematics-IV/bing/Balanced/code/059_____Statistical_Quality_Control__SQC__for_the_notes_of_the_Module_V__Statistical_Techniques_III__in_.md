### Statistical Quality Control (SQC)

- Statistical Quality Control (SQC) is the application of statistical methods to monitor and control the quality of a production process   .
- SQC helps to ensure that the process operates efficiently, producing more specification-conforming products with less waste, scrap, or rework .
- SQC can be divided into two categories: descriptive statistics and inferential statistics.
  - Descriptive statistics summarize the data collected from the process using measures of central tendency (mean, median, mode) and dispersion (range, standard deviation, variance).
  - Inferential statistics use the data to make predictions or decisions about the process using hypothesis testing, confidence intervals, and control charts.
- SQC can also be classified into two types: acceptance sampling and process control .
  - Acceptance sampling is used when a decision must be made to accept or reject a group of parts or items based on the quality found in a sample.
  - Process control is used to monitor and adjust the process parameters (inputs) to ensure that the process outputs (dependent variables) are within acceptable limits .
- Some of the benefits of SQC are:
  - Improved customer satisfaction and loyalty
  - Reduced costs and defects
  - Increased productivity and efficiency
  - Enhanced reputation and competitiveness
  - Better decision making and problem solving
