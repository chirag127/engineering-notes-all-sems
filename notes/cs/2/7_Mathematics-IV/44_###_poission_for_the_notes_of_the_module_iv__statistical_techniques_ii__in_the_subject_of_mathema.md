The Poisson distribution is a discrete probability distribution that is used to model the number of events occurring in a fixed interval of time or space. The Poisson distribution is characterized by a single parameter, the average rate of events, and it is often used to model the number of occurrences of a rare event.

The Poisson distribution is defined as:

P(X=k) = (e^(-λ))(λ^k)/k!

Where X is the number of events, λ is the average rate of events, e is the base of the natural logarithm, and k! is the factorial of k.

The Poisson distribution has several important properties, including:

1. The mean and variance of the Poisson distribution are equal to λ.

2. The Poisson distribution is a limiting distribution for the number of events in a large number of independent, identically distributed trials.

3. The Poisson distribution is often used to model the number of occurrences of a rare event, such as the number of accidents in a given time period or the number of mutations in a DNA sequence.
