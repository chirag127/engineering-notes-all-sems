### Alternative hypothesis for the notes of the Module V: Statistical Techniques III: in the subject of Mathematics-IV KCS

The alternative hypothesis is a statement in hypothesis testing that is the opposite of the null hypothesis. The null hypothesis is a statement that there is no difference or relationship between two variables, while the alternative hypothesis is a statement that there is a difference or relationship between the variables.

The alternative hypothesis is used to test the validity of the null hypothesis. If the evidence supports the alternative hypothesis, then the null hypothesis is rejected. If the evidence does not support the alternative hypothesis, then the null hypothesis is not rejected.

There are two types of alternative hypotheses: one-tailed and two-tailed. A one-tailed alternative hypothesis specifies the direction of the difference or relationship, while a two-tailed alternative hypothesis does not specify the direction.

For example, in a hypothesis test about the mean of a population, the null hypothesis might be that the mean is equal to a specified value, while the one-tailed alternative hypothesis might be that the mean is greater than the specified value. The two-tailed alternative hypothesis might be that the mean is not equal to the specified value.
