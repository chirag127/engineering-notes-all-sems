### Expectation and variance for the notes of the Module IV: Statistical Techniques II: in the subject of Mathematics-IV KCS

Expectation and variance are two important concepts in probability theory and statistics. Expectation, also known as mathematical expectation or mean, is a measure of the center of a probability distribution. Variance is a measure of the spread of a probability distribution.

Expectation is defined as the weighted average of the possible values of a random variable, where the weights are given by the probabilities of the values. The expectation of a discrete random variable X can be calculated as:

E(X) = ∑x_i P(X = x_i)

where x_i are the possible values of X, and P(X = x_i) is the probability of X taking the value x_i.

Variance is defined as the expected value of the squared deviation of a random variable from its mean. The variance of a discrete random variable X can be calculated as:

Var(X) = E((X - E(X))^2)

Variance is a measure of the spread of a distribution, and it can be used to quantify the uncertainty or risk associated with a random variable.
