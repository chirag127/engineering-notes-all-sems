### Expectation and Variance for the Notes of the Module IV: Statistical Techniques II: in the Subject of Mathematics-IV KCS

The concept of expectation and variance is an essential part of statistics that helps in understanding the probability distribution of a random variable. In this module, we will learn about the expectation and variance of a random variable in detail. The following are the key points that you need to understand to master this concept:

1. Expectation:
    - The expectation of a random variable is the weighted average of all possible outcomes of the variable, where the weights are given by the probability of each outcome.
    - It is denoted by E(X).
    - The formula for calculating the expectation of a discrete random variable X is given by E(X) = ΣxP(X=x), where x represents all possible outcomes of X and P(X=x) represents the probability of each outcome.
    - For a continuous random variable X, the formula for calculating the expectation is given by E(X) = ∫xf(x)dx, where f(x) is the probability density function of X.

2. Variance:
    - The variance of a random variable is a measure of how much the values of the variable deviate from the expected value.
    - It is denoted by Var(X).
    - The formula for calculating the variance of a discrete random variable X is given by Var(X) = Σ(x-E(X))^2P(X=x), where x represents all possible outcomes of X and P(X=x) represents the probability of each outcome.
    - For a continuous random variable X, the formula for calculating the variance is given by Var(X) = ∫(x-E(X))^2f(x)dx, where f(x) is the probability density function of X.

3. Properties of Expectation and Variance:
    - Linearity of Expectation: E(aX+b) = aE(X) + b, where a and b are constants.
    - Variance of a Constant: Var(c) = 0, where c is a constant.
    - Variance of a Linear Combination: Var(aX+bY) = a^2Var(X) + b^2Var(Y) + 2abCov(X,Y), where a and b are constants and Cov(X,Y) is the covariance between X and Y.
    - Independence of Random Variables: If X and Y are independent, then Cov(X,Y) = 0, and Var(aX+bY) = a^2Var(X) + b^2Var(Y).

4. Applications of Expectation and Variance:
    - Expected Value of a Game: The expected value of a game is the amount a player can expect to win or lose on average over many plays.
    - Law of Large Numbers: The law of large numbers states that as the number of trials of a random experiment increases, the average of the outcomes approaches the expected value.
    - Central Limit Theorem: The central limit theorem states that the sum of a large number of independent and identically distributed random variables follows a normal distribution.

In conclusion, understanding the concept of expectation and variance is crucial for understanding the probability distribution of a random variable. By mastering these concepts, you will be able to apply them to various statistical problems and make informed decisions based on data analysis.
