

### Skewness for the notes of the Module III: Statistical Techniques I: in the subject of Mathematics-IV KCS

Skewness is a measure of the asymmetry of a probability distribution. It measures the degree of distortion from the symmetrical bell curve, also known as the normal distribution. In this module, we will learn about the concept of skewness, its types, how to calculate it, and its applications.

#### Types of Skewness

There are two types of skewness: 

1. Positive Skewness: A distribution is said to have positive skewness when the tail on the right side of the distribution is longer or fatter than the tail on the left side. In other words, the mean is greater than the median. 

2. Negative Skewness: A distribution is said to have negative skewness when the tail on the left side of the distribution is longer or fatter than the tail on the right side. In other words, the median is greater than the mean.

#### Applications of Skewness

Skewness is used in various fields such as finance, economics, and social sciences. Some of the applications of skewness are:

- In finance, skewness is used to measure the risk associated with an investment portfolio.
- In economics, skewness is used to measure income distribution in a population.
- In social sciences, skewness is used to measure the distribution of scores in tests, surveys, and other assessments.

In conclusion, skewness is an essential statistical concept that helps us understand the shape of a distribution and its asymmetry. It is an important tool in various fields and helps in making informed decisions.
