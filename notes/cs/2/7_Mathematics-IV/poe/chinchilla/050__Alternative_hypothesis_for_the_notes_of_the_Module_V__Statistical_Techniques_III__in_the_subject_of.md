### Alternative Hypothesis for the Notes of the Module V: Statistical Techniques III: in the Subject of Mathematics-IV KCS

In statistics, hypothesis testing is a method used to make decisions about the population's characteristics based on the sample data. The hypothesis testing involves two hypotheses: null hypothesis (H0) and alternative hypothesis (H1). The null hypothesis assumes that there is no significant difference between the sample and population, while the alternative hypothesis assumes that there is a significant difference between the sample and population.

The alternative hypothesis is the opposite of the null hypothesis and is denoted by H1. The alternative hypothesis is the hypothesis that the researcher wants to prove or support. It states that there is a significant difference between the sample and population, and the observed result is not due to chance.

Here are some points to understand the alternative hypothesis:

- The alternative hypothesis is always complementary to the null hypothesis. It states that there is a significant difference between the sample and population, while the null hypothesis states that there is no significant difference between the sample and population.
- The alternative hypothesis can be one-tailed or two-tailed. A one-tailed alternative hypothesis is used when the researcher wants to test the direction of the difference, while a two-tailed alternative hypothesis is used when the researcher wants to test any difference.
- The alternative hypothesis is tested using a significance level (α). The significance level is the probability of rejecting the null hypothesis when it is true. The most common significance level used is 0.05, which means that there is a 5% chance of rejecting the null hypothesis when it is true.
- The alternative hypothesis can be written in different ways, depending on the research question. For example, if the research question is whether the mean of a population is greater than a certain value, the alternative hypothesis can be written as H1: μ > x, where μ is the population mean and x is the value to be tested.
- The alternative hypothesis can be accepted or rejected based on the results of the hypothesis test. If the p-value is less than the significance level, the alternative hypothesis is accepted, and if the p-value is greater than the significance level, the null hypothesis is accepted.

In conclusion, the alternative hypothesis is an important concept in hypothesis testing. It provides an alternative explanation for the observed data and helps the researcher to make a decision about the population's characteristics. By understanding the alternative hypothesis, students can conduct hypothesis testing and interpret the results accurately.
