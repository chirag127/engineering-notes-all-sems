### Kurtosis

Kurtosis is a measure of the degree of peakness, or thickness, of the distribution of a random variable. It is a measure of the "tailedness" of the probability distribution of a real-valued random variable. In other words, it is a measure of the extent to which the distribution of a variable is deviated from a normal distribution.

Kurtosis is an important statistical concept that is used in a variety of fields, including finance, economics, and biology. The following are some key points to keep in mind when studying kurtosis:

- Kurtosis is a measure of the shape of a distribution, specifically the degree of peakedness or flatness.
- Kurtosis can be positive or negative. Positive kurtosis indicates a distribution with a sharper peak and longer tails than a normal distribution, while negative kurtosis indicates a flatter peak and shorter tails than a normal distribution.
- The kurtosis of a normal distribution is 3. A distribution with a kurtosis greater than 3 is said to be leptokurtic, while a distribution with a kurtosis less than 3 is said to be platykurtic.
- Kurtosis is related to the fourth moment of a distribution. The formula for kurtosis involves subtracting 3 from the fourth moment and dividing by the square of the standard deviation.
- Kurtosis can be used to identify outliers in a data set. Outliers are observations that fall outside the expected range of values for the data set, and they can have a significant impact on the results of statistical analyses.

In summary, kurtosis is a measure of the degree of peakedness or flatness of the distribution of a random variable. It is a useful tool for identifying outliers and for understanding the shape of a distribution.
