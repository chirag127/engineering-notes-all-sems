### Null Hypothesis for the Notes of the Module V: Statistical Techniques III: in the Subject of Mathematics-IV KCS

In the field of statistics, hypothesis testing is a widely used technique to determine whether a particular statement about a population is true or not. In hypothesis testing, we start by defining two hypotheses: the null hypothesis and the alternative hypothesis. In this module, we will focus on the null hypothesis.

The null hypothesis is a statement that assumes no relationship or difference between variables. It is often denoted by H0. The null hypothesis is the default position that there is no effect or relationship between two variables. It is a statement of equality between two variables.

Here are some key points about the null hypothesis:

- The null hypothesis is always a statement of no effect or no difference.

- The null hypothesis is often the starting point for statistical analysis.

- The null hypothesis is typically tested against the alternative hypothesis.

- The null hypothesis is rejected only if the evidence against it is strong enough.

- The null hypothesis can never be proven, only rejected.

- The null hypothesis assumes that any observed difference between two groups is due to chance or sampling error.

- The null hypothesis is a critical component of hypothesis testing and statistical analysis.

In conclusion, the null hypothesis is a statement that assumes no relationship or difference between variables. It is a critical component of hypothesis testing and statistical analysis. Understanding the null hypothesis is essential for making meaningful statistical inferences and drawing accurate conclusions from data.
