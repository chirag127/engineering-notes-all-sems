### Fitting of straight lines for the notes of the Module III: Statistical Techniques I: in the subject of Mathematics-IV KCS

In this module, we will learn about the fitting of straight lines to a given set of data points. This technique is widely used in various fields such as engineering, economics, and physics to establish a relationship between two variables.

Here are the key points to remember about fitting of straight lines:

- A straight line can be represented by the equation y = mx + c, where m is the slope of the line, and c is the y-intercept.
- The slope of a line represents the rate of change of the dependent variable with respect to the independent variable.
- The y-intercept represents the value of the dependent variable when the independent variable is zero.
- To fit a straight line to a set of data points, we need to find the values of m and c that minimize the sum of the squares of the vertical distances between the data points and the line. This is known as the method of least squares.
- The method of least squares involves finding the values of m and c that satisfy the following equations:

  ∑(xi - x̄)(yi - ȳ) = n * m * sxy
  
  ∑(xi - x̄)^2 = n * sxx
  
  where xi and yi are the coordinates of the ith data point, x̄ and ȳ are the means of the x and y coordinates, sxx and sxy are the sums of squares of deviations of x and the product of deviations of x and y, and n is the number of data points.
  
- Once we have found the values of m and c, we can use the equation y = mx + c to predict the value of the dependent variable for a given value of the independent variable.
- We can also use the correlation coefficient, r, to measure the strength and direction of the linear relationship between the two variables. The value of r ranges from -1 to 1, where -1 indicates a perfect negative correlation, 0 indicates no correlation, and 1 indicates a perfect positive correlation.
- The coefficient of determination, r^2, is the proportion of the total variation in the dependent variable that is explained by the linear relationship with the independent variable. The value of r^2 ranges from 0 to 1, where 0 indicates no relationship, and 1 indicates a perfect relationship.

In conclusion, the fitting of straight lines is an essential technique in statistical analysis, and it helps us to establish a relationship between two variables.
