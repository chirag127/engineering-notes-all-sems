### One way Analysis of Variance (ANOVA) for the notes of the Module V: Statistical Techniques III: in the subject of Mathematics-IV KCS

- ANOVA is a statistical technique used to determine whether there are any significant differences between the means of two or more groups.
- It is a one-way ANOVA when there is only one independent variable (or factor) involved.
- The null hypothesis for one-way ANOVA is that there is no significant difference between the means of the groups.
- The alternative hypothesis is that at least one group mean is different from the others.
- The ANOVA test calculates the F-statistic, which is the ratio of the variance between groups to the variance within groups.
- If the F-statistic is greater than the critical value, then we reject the null hypothesis and conclude that there is a significant difference between the means of the groups.
- If the F-statistic is less than the critical value, then we fail to reject the null hypothesis and conclude that there is not enough evidence to suggest a significant difference between the means of the groups.
- The critical value for the F-statistic is determined by the degrees of freedom for the between groups and within groups variances.
- The degrees of freedom for the between groups variance is one less than the number of groups, while the degrees of freedom for the within groups variance is equal to the total number of observations minus the total number of groups.
- ANOVA can be performed using software such as SPSS or Excel, or by hand using formulas.
- ANOVA is often used in experimental designs to compare the effects of different treatments or interventions on a dependent variable.
