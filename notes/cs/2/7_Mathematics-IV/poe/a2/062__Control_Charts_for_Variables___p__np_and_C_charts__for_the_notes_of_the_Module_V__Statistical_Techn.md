### Control Charts for Variables ( p, np and C charts)

- Control charts are used to monitor the process variability over time. They help in identifying the presence of assignable causes of variation in a process.
- For variables data, three types of control charts are used:
    - p-chart: Used for fraction defective or proportion nonconforming. The central line is at the target proportion (p') and the control limits are calculated based on the binomial distribution.
    - np-chart: Used for number of nonconforming units. The central line is at the target number of nonconforming units (np') and the control limits are calculated based on the Poisson distribution.
    - C-chart: Used for measurements or counts. The central line is at the target value (C') and the control limits are calculated based on the standard deviation of the samples.
- Steps to create a control chart:
    1. Decide the type of chart (p, np, or C) based on the type of data (proportion, number of nonconforming units, or measurements/counts).
    2. Determine the target value (p', np', or C') based on the process specifications or customer requirements.
    3. Determine the sample size to be used. Larger sample sizes lead to more stable control charts with less variability.
    4. Collect the data and calculate the statistics required to plot the control chart.
    5. Plot the data points and control limits on the chart.
    6. Analyze the chart to identify the presence of any out-of-control signals. This indicates the presence of an assignable cause. Take corrective actions to bring the process back in control.
