### Skewness for the notes of the Module III: Statistical Techniques I: in the subject of Mathematics-IV KCS.

1. Skewness refers to asymmetry of a distribution. A skewed distribution is one where the mean, median and mode are not equal.
2. A distribution is said to be positively skewed if the tail on the right side is longer or fatter than the left side. This means that the mass of the distribution is concentrated on the left side of the figure.
3. A distribution is said to be negatively skewed if the tail on the left side is longer or fatter than the right side. This means that the mass of the distribution is concentrated on the right side of the figure.
4. The skewness of a distribution can be calculated using the formula: Skewness = 3(Mean - Median)/Standard Deviation
5. If the skewness is > 0, the distribution is positively skewed. If the skewness is < 0, the distribution is negatively skewed. If skewness is 0, the distribution is symmetrical.
6. Applications of skewness:
- To identify outliers in a data set. Outliers will skew the distribution.
- To determine appropriate measures of central tendency. Mean is inappropriate for skewed data, median should be used.
- To determine normality of data. A normal distribution will have skewness = 0.
7. Examples of skewed distributions:
- Exam marks: Positively skewed (a few students may score very high)
- Income data: Positively skewed (a few earn very high incomes)
- Waiting times: Positively skewed (most wait times are short but a few are very long)
