### One way Analysis of Variance (ANOVA)

- One way ANOVA is used to compare means of two or more independent groups to check if they are significantly different from each other.
- It assumes the data to follow normal distribution and the variables to be continuous and interval or ratio.
- The steps to perform One way ANOVA are:
1. State the null and alternative hypothesis. Null hypothesis is that the means of all the groups are equal. Alternative hypothesis is that at least two means are not equal.
2. Calculate the total sum of squares (SST). It is the sum of squared deviations of all the observations from the grand mean.
3. Calculate the sum of squares between groups (SSB). It is the sum of squared deviations of group means from the grand mean.
4. Calculate the sum of squares within groups (SSW). It is the sum of squared deviations of observations from their respective group means.
5. Calculate the F-statistic. It is the ratio of MSB and MSW.
6. Check the probability (p-value). If p-value is less than the significance level, reject the null hypothesis.
- The F-statistic follows F distribution with (k-1) and (n-k) degrees of freedom where k is the number of groups and n is the total number of observations.
- ANOVA is used to test if means of at least two groups are significantly different. It does not identify which specific groups differ. Post-hoc tests like Tukey's HSD can be used for that.
