### Control Charts

- Control charts are graphs used to monitor process stability and detect anomalies. They allow visualization of process performance over time.
- The most common types are:
    - X-bar chart: Plots the mean of samples over time. Used to monitor process central tendency.
    - R-chart: Plots the range of samples over time. Used to monitor process variability.
    - S-chart: Plots the standard deviation of samples over time. Also used to monitor process variability.
- Control charts have three horizontal lines:
    - Center line: The mean of the in-control process. Data points near this line indicate stable performance.
    - Upper control limit: Points above this line indicate the process is out of control (too high).
    - Lower control limit: Points below this line indicate the process is out of control (too low).
- To use a control chart:
    1. Take samples and calculate statistics (mean, range, standard deviation, etc.)
    2. Plot the statistics on the control chart.
    3. Look for points outside the control limits or patterns indicating lack of randomness. This means the process is out of control.
    4. If out of control, investigate and remove the assignable cause. Then re-calculate the control limits.
- Control charts help monitor both small and large changes to detect and correct problems early. They are a key tool for statistical process control and quality improvement.
