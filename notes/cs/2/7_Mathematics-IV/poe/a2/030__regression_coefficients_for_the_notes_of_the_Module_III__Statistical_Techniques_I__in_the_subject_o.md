### Regression Coefficients

1. The regression coefficient is the slope of the regression line and represents the change in the dependent variable Y for a unit change in the independent variable X, keeping other variables constant.
2. The regression coefficient is also known as the parameter estimate for the independent variable.
3. A positive regression coefficient means that as X increases, Y increases and they are positively related. A negative regression coefficient means that as X increases, Y decreases and they are negatively related.
4. The regression coefficient is unitless and can be interpreted as the expected change in Y for a one-unit increase in X.
5. The statistical significance of a regression coefficient is assessed using a t-test. If the p-value is less than the significance level, the regression coefficient is statistically significant. This means that the effect of the independent variable on the dependent variable is real and not due to chance.
6. When there are multiple independent variables, the regression coefficient shows the effect of one variable keeping the others constant. The joint effect of the variables can be seen from the overall model fit. The relative importance of the variables can be assessed using the squared multiple correlation coefficient.
