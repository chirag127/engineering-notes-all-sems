### Lagrange's Equations

1. Lagrange's equations are a set of equations that describe how a system evolves over time. They are used to solve problems in mechanics and dynamical systems.
2. The equations are named after Joseph Louis Lagrange who developed them in the 1700s. They provide an alternative way to describe Newton's laws of motion.
3. Lagrange's equations are written as:

d/dt (∂L/∂q̇i) − ∂L/∂qi = 0

Where L is the Lagrangian, qi represents the generalized coordinates of the system, and q̇i represents the time derivative of the generalized coordinates.
4. The Lagrangian is equal to the kinetic energy minus the potential energy of the system. Solving the Lagrange equations yields the trajectories and other properties of the system.
5. Lagrange's equations are useful for analyzing constrained systems and systems with symmetry. They can also be applied to fields other than mechanics, such as optics, quantum mechanics, and fluid dynamics.
