### Origin of Partial Differential Equations

- Partial differential equations (PDEs) arise from attempting to solve real-world problems that involve functions of multiple variables.
- Many physical phenomena are described by equations involving partial derivatives, such as the heat equation, the wave equation, Laplace's equation, and the Navier-Stokes equations.
- The heat equation arises from the problem of finding the temperature distribution in a given region over time. The wave equation arises from models of vibrating strings or membranes and sound waves. Laplace's equation arises from problems involving potential fields, such as gravitational, electric, and fluid flow potentials. The Navier-Stokes equations arise from models of fluid flow.
- The mathematics of PDEs is concerned with the solution, analysis, and approximation of solutions of PDEs. Existence and uniqueness of solutions, as well as properties of solutions such as continuity and differentiability, are common areas of study in the theory of PDEs. Numerical analysis is an important aspect of PDEs, since most PDEs cannot be solved analytically.
- The study of PDEs is a wide area of mathematics, with connections to other fields such as physics, engineering, and economics. PDEs are widely used to model phenomena in many fields, including science, engineering, and economics.
