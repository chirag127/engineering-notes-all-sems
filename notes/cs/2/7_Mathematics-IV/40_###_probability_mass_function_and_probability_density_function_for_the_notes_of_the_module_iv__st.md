### Probability mass function and Probability density function for the notes of the Module IV: Statistical Techniques II: in the subject of Mathematics-IV KCS

Module IV of the Mathematics-IV KCS course covers the topic of Statistical Techniques II, including probability theory. Probability mass function (PMF) and probability density function (PDF) are two important concepts in probability theory.

Probability mass function (PMF) is a function that describes the probabilities of discrete events. PMF is used to model discrete random variables, such as the number of heads in a sequence of coin flips. The PMF of a discrete random variable X is defined as:

P(X = x) = f(x)

where f(x) is the PMF of X and x is a possible value of X.

Probability density function (PDF) is a function that describes the probabilities of continuous events. PDF is used to model continuous random variables, such as the height of a person. The PDF of a continuous random variable X is defined as:

f(x) = dP(X ≤ x)/dx

where f(x) is the PDF of X and P(X ≤ x) is the cumulative distribution function (CDF) of X.

In the context of the Mathematics-IV KCS course, students will learn about probability mass function (PMF) and probability density function (PDF) as part of Module IV. This may include working with examples of PMF and PDF, calculating PMF and PDF for different random variables, and interpreting the results of PMF and PDF analyses.

In summary, Module IV of the Mathematics-IV KCS course covers the topic of Statistical Techniques II, and includes a discussion of probability mass function (PMF) and probability density function (PDF). PMF is used to model discrete random variables, while PDF is used to model continuous random variables. Students will learn about PMF and PDF through interactive activities and hands-on experiences.
