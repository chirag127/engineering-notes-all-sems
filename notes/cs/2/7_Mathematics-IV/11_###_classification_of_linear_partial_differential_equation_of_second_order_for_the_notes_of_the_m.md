The classification of linear partial differential equations (PDEs) of second order is a way of categorizing PDEs based on their properties. Linear PDEs of second order are PDEs that are linear in the unknown function and its partial derivatives, and are of second order with respect to the partial derivatives.

The classification of linear PDEs of second order is based on the number of independent variables, the order of the partial derivatives, and the type of coefficients in the PDE. The most common types of linear PDEs of second order are the elliptic, parabolic, and hyperbolic PDEs.

Elliptic PDEs are PDEs that describe steady-state phenomena, such as the distribution of temperature in a material. They are characterized by having a positive definite leading coefficient, and are usually solved using boundary value methods.

Parabolic PDEs are PDEs that describe time-dependent phenomena, such as the transfer of heat through a material. They are characterized by having a positive definite leading coefficient, and are usually solved using initial value methods.

Hyperbolic PDEs are PDEs that describe wave-like phenomena, such as the propagation of sound waves. They are characterized by having a leading coefficient that can be either positive or negative, and are usually solved using initial boundary value methods.
