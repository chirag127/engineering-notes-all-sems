### Origin of Partial Differential Equations for the notes of the Module I: Partial Differential Equations in the subject of Mathematics-IV KCS

Partial Differential Equations (PDEs) are mathematical models that describe the behavior of physical systems in terms of their partial derivatives. They are used to describe a wide range of phenomena, including heat transfer, fluid flow, electromagnetic fields, and elasticity.

The origin of PDEs can be traced back to the 17th century, when mathematicians first began to study the mathematical modeling of physical systems. One of the earliest examples of a PDE is the heat equation, which was first studied by Joseph Fourier in 1807. Fourier used the heat equation to describe the transfer of heat through a material, and his work laid the foundation for the study of PDEs.

In the 19th century, mathematicians such as Augustin Louis Cauchy, Pierre Simon Laplace, and Carl Friedrich Gauss made significant contributions to the study of PDEs. Cauchy developed the theory of partial differentiation, while Laplace developed the theory of boundary value problems. Gauss made important contributions to the study of partial differential equations of the first order.

The 20th century saw a rapid development of the theory of PDEs, with the development of new techniques for solving PDEs, including the method of characteristics, the method of separation of variables, and the method of Green's functions. In addition, the development of computers and numerical methods has made it possible to solve complex PDEs that were previously unsolvable.

In this module, we will study the theory of PDEs, including the classification of PDEs, the method of characteristics, the method of separation of variables, and the method of Green's functions. We will also study the numerical methods used to solve PDEs, including finite difference methods and finite element methods. This will provide a foundation for understanding the mathematical modeling of physical systems, and for exploring the various applications of PDEs in the field of mathematics.
