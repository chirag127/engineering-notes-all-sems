## Unit 4 - Graphs: Terminology used with Graph, Data Structure for Graph Representations: Adjacency Matrices, Adjacency List, Adjacency. Graph Traversal: Depth First Search and Breadth First Search, Connected Component, Spanning Trees, Minimum Cost Spanning Trees: Prims and Kruskal algorithm. Transitive Closure and Shortest Path algorithm: Warshal Algorithm and Dijikstra Algorithm.

"Unit 4 - Graphs" covers the fundamental concepts and techniques used in graph algorithms. The goal of this unit is to provide students with a comprehensive understanding of graph algorithms, and to develop their skills in designing and implementing these algorithms.

The following are some of the key topics that may be covered in Unit 4 - Graphs:

1. Terminology used with Graphs: This section covers the basic terminology used in graph algorithms, including vertices, edges, and adjacency.

2. Data Structure for Graph Representations: This section covers the data structures used to represent graphs, including adjacency matrices, adjacency lists, and adjacency.

3. Graph Traversal: This section covers the concepts of graph traversal, including depth first search and breadth first search.

4. Connected Component: This section covers the concept of connected components in graphs, including the definition, purpose, and implementation of connected component algorithms.

5. Spanning Trees: This section covers the concept of spanning trees in graphs, including the definition, purpose, and implementation of spanning tree algorithms.

6. Minimum Cost Spanning Trees: This section covers the concept of minimum cost spanning trees, including the Prim's and Kruskal algorithms.

7. Transitive Closure and Shortest Path Algorithm: This section covers the concepts of transitive closure and shortest path algorithms, including the Warshall algorithm and Dijkstra algorithm.

The goal of Unit 4 - Graphs is to provide students with a comprehensive understanding of graph algorithms, including their definition, purpose, implementation, and time and space complexity. This knowledge and understanding is important for students pursuing careers in computer science, as it provides the foundation for more advanced topics in the field.
