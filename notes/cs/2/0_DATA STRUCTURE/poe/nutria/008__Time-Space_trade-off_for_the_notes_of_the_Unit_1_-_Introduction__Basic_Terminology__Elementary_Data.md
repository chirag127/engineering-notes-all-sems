
### Time-Space Trade-Off

Time-space trade-off is an important concept in the subject of Data Structure. It is the balance between the time taken to complete a process and the space (memory) required to do so.

- **Basic Terminology**: The basic terminology used in the context of Time-Space trade-off includes Algorithm, Efficiency of an Algorithm, Time and Space Complexity, Asymptotic notations: Big Oh, Big Theta and Big Omega.

- **Elementary Data Organization**: Elementary data organization is the way data is organized in order to achieve the desired efficiency.

- **Built in Data Types in C**: Built in data types in C include int, char, float, double, etc.

- **Time and Space Complexity**: Time and Space Complexity are two factors which determine the efficiency of an algorithm. Time complexity is the amount of time taken by an algorithm to complete a process, while Space complexity is the amount of memory required by an algorithm to complete a process.

- **Asymptotic Notations**: Asymptotic notations are used to measure the running time of an algorithm. Big Oh, Big Theta and Big Omega are the three most commonly used asymptotic notations.

- **Time-Space Trade-Off**: Time-space trade-off is the balance between the time taken to complete a process and the space (memory) required to do so. It is important to understand this concept in order to design efficient algorithms.

- **Abstract Data Types (ADT)**: Abstract Data Types (ADT) are a set of data types which are used to represent data in a structured way. ADT is an important concept in the subject of Data Structure.
