### Efficiency of an Algorithm

- The efficiency of an algorithm is a measure of the resources it consumes during its execution.
- These resources can include time, memory, and other computational resources.
- The efficiency of an algorithm is typically expressed in terms of its time and space complexity.
- Time complexity refers to the amount of time an algorithm takes to complete its execution as a function of the size of its input.
- Space complexity refers to the amount of memory an algorithm requires as a function of the size of its input.
- Asymptotic notations such as Big Oh, Big Theta, and Big Omega are used to describe the time and space complexity of an algorithm.
- Big Oh notation provides an upper bound on the growth rate of an algorithm's time or space complexity.
- Big Theta notation provides a tight bound on the growth rate of an algorithm's time or space complexity.
- Big Omega notation provides a lower bound on the growth rate of an algorithm's time or space complexity.
- Time-space trade-off refers to the balance between the time and space complexity of an algorithm. In some cases, an algorithm can be made faster by using more memory, or it can be made to use less memory by taking more time to execute.
- Abstract Data Types (ADT) are a way of organizing data and defining the operations that can be performed on that data. ADTs provide a level of abstraction that allows the programmer to focus on the logic of the algorithm rather than the details of the data representation.
