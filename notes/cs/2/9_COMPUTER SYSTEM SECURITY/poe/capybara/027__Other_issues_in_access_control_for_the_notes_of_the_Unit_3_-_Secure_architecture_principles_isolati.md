### Other issues in access control for the notes of the Unit 3 - Secure architecture principles isolation and leas in the subject of COMPUTER SYSTEM SECURITY

Access control is an important concept in computer system security. It refers to the process of limiting the access of users to resources and data within a system. Access control is crucial to maintaining the confidentiality, integrity, and availability of data. In this section, we will discuss some other issues related to access control that you should be aware of.

1. Privilege escalation: Privilege escalation is when an attacker gains access to a system at a low privilege level and then tries to gain access to higher-level privileges. This can be a serious security threat, as it allows attackers to access sensitive data or perform malicious actions on the system. To prevent privilege escalation, you should implement strong authentication and authorization mechanisms.

2. Access creep: Access creep occurs when users accumulate access rights over time without them being revoked when they are no longer needed. This can lead to security vulnerabilities as users may have access to data or resources that they no longer need. To prevent access creep, you should regularly review and audit user access rights.

3. Backdoor access: Backdoor access is when an attacker gains access to a system through a hidden or undocumented method. Backdoor access is a serious security threat, as it allows attackers to bypass normal access controls and gain access to sensitive data or resources. To prevent backdoor access, you should regularly review and audit your system for any unauthorized access points.

4. Social engineering: Social engineering is a technique used by attackers to manipulate users into divulging sensitive information or performing actions that compromise the security of a system. Social engineering attacks can be difficult to prevent, as they rely on human behavior rather than technical vulnerabilities. To prevent social engineering attacks, you should educate users on the risks and how to identify and avoid them.

5. Separation of duties: Separation of duties is the principle of dividing responsibilities between different users to prevent any single user from having too much control over a system. Separation of duties is important to maintain the integrity and security of a system. To implement separation of duties, you should divide tasks between different users based on their roles and responsibilities.

In conclusion, access control is a critical component of computer system security. By being aware of the issues discussed in this section, you can better protect your system from security threats and ensure the confidentiality, integrity, and availability of your data.
