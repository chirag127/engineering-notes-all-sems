## Unit 4 - Basic cryptography

1. Encryption: The process of converting plain text into cipher text (unreadable format) using a key. This is done to secure the data and prevent unauthorized access. Common examples are Caesar cipher, substitution cipher, etc.
2. Decryption: The reverse process of converting cipher text back to plain text using a key. This allows the intended recipient to read the original message.
3. Keys: Keys are secret pieces of information that are used to encrypt and decrypt data. Without the correct key, it is infeasible to decrypt the cipher text. Keys can be symmetrical (same key used for encryption and decryption) or asymmetrical (public and private keys used for encryption and decryption respectively).
4. Ciphers: The algorithms that are used to encrypt and decrypt data. They determine the specific steps and mathematical procedures required to convert plain text to cipher text and vice versa. The strength of a cipher depends on its ability to withstand attempts to decrypt the cipher text without the key.
5. Cryptanalysis: The study of methods to decrypt data without access to the correct key. Cryptanalysts try to identify weaknesses in ciphers and break the encryption. This leads to continuous innovation to make ciphers more robust and difficult to break.
