## Unit 3 - Secure architecture principles isolation and leas

1. Isolation: Isolating components/systems from each other such that a compromise of one does not lead to a compromise of others. This limits lateral movement and contains damage.
2. Least privilege: Giving components/systems only the privileges/access required to perform their legitimate purpose. This limits potential damage from misuse or compromise.
3. Defense in depth: Implementing multiple controls/countermeasures to protect against threats reaching targets. This acknowledges that some controls may be ineffective or may fail, with the goal of having sufficient redundant controls to still prevent compromise.
4. Fail securely: Systems/components fail in a secure state. This limits potential damage from failures and requires deliberate actions to make systems less secure.
5. Simplicity: Keeping systems as simple as possible with a limited attack surface. This makes them easier to secure, maintain, and understand. Complex systems are prone to configuration errors and harder to defend.

The content covers the key secure architecture principles of isolation to contain compromise, least privilege to limit potential damage, defense in depth with redundant controls, failing securely, and simplicity to reduce complexity and attack surface. Following these principles in system design can improve resilience and security.
