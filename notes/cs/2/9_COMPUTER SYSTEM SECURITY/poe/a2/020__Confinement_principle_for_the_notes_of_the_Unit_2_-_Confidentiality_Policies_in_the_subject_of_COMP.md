### Confinement principle for the notes of the Unit 2 - Confidentiality Policies in the subject of COMPUTER SYSTEM SECURITY

1. Confinement: Isolating a process and its data from other processes to limit the impact of security breaches.
- Preventing a process from accessing resources it is not authorized to access.
- A confined process cannot access or modify resources outside its confinement.
- Effective confinement requires multiple layers of protection with different privilege levels.

2. Access control mechanisms help implement confinement by regulating access to system resources.
- Discretionary access control (DAC): Access rights based on identity of the user. The owner of an object can specify who gets access.
- Mandatory access control (MAC): Access rights based on security labels on subjects and objects. A centralized policy defines what labels can access what other labels.
- Role-based access control (RBAC): Access rights based on roles that individual users or processes assume.

3. Virtualization is a mechanism to compartmentalize resources and confine processes.
- Guest VMs are confined by the hypervisor to allocated virtual resources.
- Containers provide operating system-level virtualization by confining applications to allocated resources.

4. Formal verification methods can be applied to prove that a system design achieves confinement properties. However, for complex real-world systems, this can be challenging to do in practice.
