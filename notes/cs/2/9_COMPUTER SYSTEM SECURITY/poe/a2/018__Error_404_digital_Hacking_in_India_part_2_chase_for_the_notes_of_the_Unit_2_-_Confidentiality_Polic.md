1. Confidentiality refers to limiting access to information to only authorized individuals. Some key confidentiality concepts and policies include:
- Need-to-know: Only sharing information with those who need it to do their job.
- Least privilege: Giving individuals the minimum level of access needed.
- Separation of duties: Dividing responsibilities so one person does not control all aspects of a process.
- Information classification: Labeling data based on its sensitivity and restricting access accordingly.
- Secure deletion: properly deleting data when it is no longer needed to prevent unauthorized access.

2. Common threats to confidentiality include:
- Shoulder surfing: Directly observing someone's computer screen or physical documents.
- Phishing emails: Emails pretending to be from a legitimate company to trick users into revealing credentials or other sensitive data.
- Malware: Software designed to steal data, spy on users, or gain remote access to systems.
- Insider threats: Malicious employees or contractors who misuse their legitimate access to data.

3. Technical controls to protect confidentiality include:
- Encryption: Converting data into unreadable code that cannot be deciphered without a key.
- Access controls: Restricting access to data and systems based on authentication and authorization.
- Data loss prevention: Monitoring and controlling data sharing to detect and prevent unauthorized transmission of sensitive data.
- Secure protocols: Using protocols like SSH and TLS to encrypt data in transit and prevent eavesdropping.
- Passwords: Requiring passwords or other credentials to authenticate users and control access.
