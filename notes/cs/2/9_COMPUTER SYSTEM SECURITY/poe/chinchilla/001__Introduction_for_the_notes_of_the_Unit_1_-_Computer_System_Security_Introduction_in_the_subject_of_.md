### Introduction

In this unit, we will be discussing computer system security, which is the practice of protecting computer systems from unauthorized access or damage. Computer system security has become increasingly important in recent years as more and more sensitive information is stored on computers and transmitted over networks. In this unit, we will cover the following topics:

1. Threats to computer system security
   - Malware: software designed to cause harm to a computer system or network
   - Phishing: fraudulent attempts to obtain sensitive information, such as usernames and passwords
   - Social engineering: manipulation of individuals to obtain sensitive information or access to computer systems
   - Insider threats: threats posed by individuals within an organization who have authorized access to sensitive information or computer systems
2. Security measures
   - Access control: limiting access to computer systems and data to authorized individuals
   - Encryption: converting sensitive information into a code that can only be read by authorized individuals
   - Firewalls: software or hardware that monitors and controls incoming and outgoing network traffic
   - Intrusion detection systems: software that monitors for suspicious activity on a computer or network
3. Security policies and procedures
   - Developing and implementing security policies and procedures to ensure that all individuals within an organization understand their roles and responsibilities in maintaining computer system security
   - Regularly reviewing and updating security policies and procedures to ensure that they remain effective in the face of changing threats
4. Ethical and legal issues
   - Understanding and complying with laws and regulations related to computer system security, such as the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) and the Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act (HIPAA)
   - Maintaining ethical standards in the practice of computer system security, such as respecting the privacy of individuals and organizations

By the end of this unit, you should have a solid understanding of the threats to computer system security and the measures and policies that can be used to protect against them. You should also be familiar with the ethical and legal issues related to computer system security. This knowledge will be essential for anyone working in the field of computer system security or anyone interested in protecting their own computer systems and data.
