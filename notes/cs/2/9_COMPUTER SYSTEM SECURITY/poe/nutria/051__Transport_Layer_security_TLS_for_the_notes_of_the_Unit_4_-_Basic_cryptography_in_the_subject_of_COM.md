
### Transport Layer Security (TLS)

- TLS is a cryptographic protocol used to secure communication between two or more parties. It is most commonly used to secure web traffic, but can also be used to secure other types of communication. 
- TLS uses a combination of public-key cryptography, symmetric key cryptography, and message authentication codes to ensure the privacy and integrity of data transmitted over a network. 
- TLS is an important component of computer system security, as it helps to protect data from eavesdropping and tampering. 
- TLS is used to authenticate the identity of the server, and to encrypt the data being transmitted. This ensures that only the intended recipient can read the data, and that the data cannot be modified or tampered with. 
- TLS is based on the Secure Sockets Layer (SSL) protocol, and is the successor to SSL. It is an open standard, and is supported by most web browsers and web servers.
