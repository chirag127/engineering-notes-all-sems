### Advanced Control Hijacking attacks for the notes of the Unit 1 - Computer System Security Introduction in the subject of COMPUTER SYSTEM SECURITY

Advanced Control Hijacking attacks are a type of cyber attack that involves the unauthorized takeover of control systems, such as industrial control systems (ICS) and supervisory control and data acquisition (SCADA) systems. These attacks can result in significant harm to critical infrastructure, including power plants, water treatment facilities, and transportation systems.

Advanced Control Hijacking attacks can be accomplished through a variety of means, including:

1. Exploiting vulnerabilities in software or hardware: Attackers can exploit vulnerabilities in software or hardware to gain access to control systems and take control of them.

2. Social engineering: Attackers can use social engineering techniques, such as phishing and baiting, to trick individuals into giving them access to control systems.

3. Physical access: Attackers can gain physical access to control systems and install malware or other malicious software to take control of the systems.

In order to prevent Advanced Control Hijacking attacks, it is important to implement robust security measures, including:

1. Regular software and hardware updates: Regular software and hardware updates can help to address vulnerabilities and prevent attacks.

2. Employee training: Employee training can help to raise awareness of the dangers of Advanced Control Hijacking attacks and to prevent social engineering attacks.

3. Physical security measures: Physical security measures, such as access controls and monitoring systems, can help to prevent physical access to control systems.
