### Web security definitions, goals and threat models

- Web security is the practice of protecting web applications and services from unauthorized access, manipulation, or damage by malicious actors or events.
- The goals of web security are to ensure the confidentiality, integrity, and availability of web resources and data, as well as to protect the privacy and identity of web users and clients.
- Threat models are systematic ways of identifying and analyzing the potential threats and vulnerabilities that may affect a web system, as well as the countermeasures and mitigations that can be applied to reduce or eliminate the risks.
- There are different methods and frameworks for conducting threat modeling, such as STRIDE, DREAD, PASTA, OCTAVE, and OWASP. They typically involve the following steps:
  - Define the scope and boundaries of the web system, such as the components, interfaces, data flows, and trust boundaries.
  - Identify the assets and resources that need to be protected, such as sensitive data, credentials, keys, and certificates.
  - Identify the threat agents and adversaries that may target the web system, such as hackers, insiders, competitors, or nation-states.
  - Identify the threat scenarios and attack vectors that may exploit the web system's vulnerabilities, such as injection, cross-site scripting, denial-of-service, or phishing.
  - Assess the impact and likelihood of each threat scenario, based on factors such as severity, exploitability, and detectability.
  - Prioritize the threats and vulnerabilities according to their risk level, and determine the appropriate countermeasures and mitigations to address them, such as encryption, authentication, authorization, validation, or logging.
