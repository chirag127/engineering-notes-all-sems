### Sample Attacks

A sample attack is an example of a cyber attack, which is a set of actions performed by threat actors, who try to gain unauthorized access, steal data or cause damage to computers, computer networks, or other computing systems.

Some of the common types and examples of cyber attacks are:

- **Identity theft, fraud, extortion**: These are attacks that aim to obtain personal or financial information from the victims, such as names, passwords, credit card numbers, bank accounts, etc. The attackers may use this information to impersonate the victims, make fraudulent transactions, blackmail them, or sell their data to other criminals.
- **Malware, phishing, spamming, spoofing, spyware, trojans and viruses**: These are attacks that involve malicious software or messages that are designed to infect, damage, or control the victim's devices or systems. Malware can perform various functions, such as deleting or encrypting files, logging keystrokes, stealing data, displaying unwanted ads, etc. Phishing is a type of attack that uses deceptive emails or websites to trick the victims into revealing their credentials or downloading malware. Spamming is a type of attack that involves sending unsolicited or unwanted messages to the victims, often containing malware or phishing links. Spoofing is a type of attack that involves falsifying the identity or source of an email, website, or network packet, to deceive the victims or bypass security measures. Spyware is a type of malware that monitors the victim's online activities, such as browsing history, searches, emails, etc. Trojans are a type of malware that disguise themselves as legitimate or useful programs, but secretly perform malicious functions. Viruses are a type of malware that attach themselves to other files or programs, and replicate themselves to infect more devices or systems.
- **Stolen hardware, such as laptops or mobile devices**: These are attacks that involve physically stealing or accessing the victim's devices, such as laptops, smartphones, tablets, etc. The attackers may use these devices to access the victim's data, accounts, networks, or other resources, or to install malware or spyware on them.
- **Denial-of-service and distributed denial-of-service attacks**: These are attacks that aim to disrupt or degrade the availability or performance of a system or service, by overwhelming it with a large amount of traffic or requests. A denial-of-service (DoS) attack is launched from a single source, while a distributed denial-of-service (DDoS) attack is launched from multiple sources, often using compromised devices or systems as bots.
- **Breach of access**: These are attacks that involve exploiting a vulnerability or weakness in a system or service, to gain unauthorized access to it. The attackers may use this access to steal, modify, or destroy data, execute commands, install malware, or perform other malicious actions.
- **Password sniffing**: These are attacks that involve capturing or intercepting the victim's passwords or other credentials, as they are transmitted over a network or entered on a device. The attackers may use these credentials to access the victim's accounts, systems, or services, or to launch other attacks.
- **System infiltration**: These are attacks that involve gaining persistent or long-term access to a system or network, by installing a backdoor, a rootkit, or other malware that allows the attackers to remotely control or monitor the system or network. The attackers may use this access to perform various malicious actions, such as stealing data, launching other attacks, or hiding their presence.
- **Website defacement**: These are attacks that involve altering or replacing the content or appearance of a website, often with a political, religious, or humorous message. The attackers may use this attack to damage the reputation, credibility, or functionality of the website, or to spread malware or phishing links to the visitors.
