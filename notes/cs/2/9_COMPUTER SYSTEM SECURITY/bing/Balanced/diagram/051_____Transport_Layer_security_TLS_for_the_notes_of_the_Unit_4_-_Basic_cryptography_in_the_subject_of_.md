### Transport Layer Security (TLS)

- Transport Layer Security (TLS) is a cryptographic protocol that provides privacy and data security for communications over the Internet .
- TLS operates on top of the transport layer protocols, such as TCP or UDP, and adds a layer of security and reliability to the data transmission .
- TLS uses both symmetric encryption and public key encryption to securely exchange private data between two parties .
- TLS also provides additional security features, such as authentication, integrity, and non-repudiation .
- Authentication is the process of verifying the identity of the communicating parties, using certificates and digital signatures .
- Integrity is the property that ensures that the data has not been tampered with or modified during transmission, using message authentication codes (MACs) or hashes .
- Non-repudiation is the ability to prove that a message was sent or received by a specific party, using digital signatures or timestamps .
- TLS consists of two sub-protocols: the TLS Handshake Protocol and the TLS Record Protocol .
- The TLS Handshake Protocol is responsible for establishing a secure connection between the two parties, by negotiating the security parameters, such as the encryption algorithms, the key exchange methods, and the certificates .
- The TLS Record Protocol is responsible for encrypting and decrypting the data, as well as ensuring its integrity and authenticity, using the security parameters agreed upon in the handshake .
- TLS supports various encryption algorithms, such as AES, DES, RC4, and ChaCha20, and various key exchange methods, such as RSA, Diffie-Hellman, and Elliptic Curve Diffie-Hellman .
- TLS is widely used in applications such as web browsers, email, instant messaging, and voice over IP, but its most visible use is in securing HTTPS, the protocol for secure web browsing .
