## Unit 2 - Confidentiality Policies

- Confidentiality policies are rules or guidelines that regulate how sensitive or personal information is handled by an organization or an individual.
- Confidentiality policies aim to protect the privacy and security of the information owners, such as customers, employees, partners, or clients.
- Confidentiality policies may cover topics such as:
  - What types of information are considered confidential and how they are classified (e.g., public, internal, restricted, secret, etc.)
  - Who is authorized to access, use, disclose, or dispose of confidential information and under what conditions
  - How confidential information is stored, transmitted, or destroyed
  - What are the responsibilities and obligations of the information users and custodians
  - What are the consequences of breaching confidentiality policies and how to report or respond to incidents
- Confidentiality policies may vary depending on the nature, scope, and context of the information and the organization or the individual involved.
- Confidentiality policies may be influenced by legal, ethical, or professional standards or regulations, such as the Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act (HIPAA), the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR), or the Code of Ethics for Professional Accountants.
- Confidentiality policies may be written in a formal document, such as a policy manual, a contract, or a non-disclosure agreement, or may be implied by the relationship, the situation, or the common practice.
