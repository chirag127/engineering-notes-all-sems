## Unit 2 - Confidentiality Policies

- Confidentiality policies are rules or guidelines that specify how sensitive or confidential information should be protected from unauthorized access, disclosure, or modification.
- Confidentiality policies can be applied to different types of information, such as personal data, trade secrets, intellectual property, health records, financial transactions, etc.
- Confidentiality policies can be enforced by various means, such as encryption, access control, authentication, auditing, backup, etc.
- Confidentiality policies can be classified into two categories: mandatory and discretionary.

### Mandatory Confidentiality Policies

- Mandatory confidentiality policies are based on a predefined classification of information into different levels of sensitivity, such as top secret, secret, confidential, etc.
- Mandatory confidentiality policies require that each information object and each user or process have a security label that indicates their level of sensitivity or clearance.
- Mandatory confidentiality policies enforce the principle of least privilege, which means that users or processes can only access information that is equal or lower than their clearance level.
- Mandatory confidentiality policies are also known as multilevel security policies or lattice-based policies.

### Discretionary Confidentiality Policies

- Discretionary confidentiality policies are based on the owner's or creator's decision on how to protect their information.
- Discretionary confidentiality policies allow the owner or creator to assign access rights or permissions to other users or processes, such as read, write, execute, etc.
- Discretionary confidentiality policies enforce the principle of need to know, which means that users or processes can only access information that is relevant or necessary for their tasks.
- Discretionary confidentiality policies are also known as access control list policies or identity-based policies.
