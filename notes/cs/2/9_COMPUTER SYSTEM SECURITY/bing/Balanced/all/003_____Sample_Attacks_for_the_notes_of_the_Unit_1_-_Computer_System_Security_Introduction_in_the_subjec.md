# Sample Attacks for the notes of the Unit 1 - Computer System Security Introduction in the subject of COMPUTER SYSTEM SECURITY

- A cyber attack is a malicious attempt to breach the security of a computer system, network, or device by an individual or group of attackers.
- Cyber attacks can have various objectives, such as stealing data, extorting money, disrupting services, or damaging systems or reputation.
- Cyber attacks can use different methods and techniques, such as malware, phishing, brute force, denial-of-service, or social engineering.
- Here are some examples of common cyber attacks and their characteristics:

  - Identity theft, fraud, extortion: These attacks aim to obtain personal or financial information from the victims, such as passwords, credit card numbers, or identity documents. The attackers may use this information to access their accounts, make fraudulent transactions, or blackmail them for money. For example, a phishing email may ask the victim to click on a link that leads to a fake website that mimics a legitimate one, and then enter their login credentials or other sensitive data.
  - Malware, phishing, spamming, spoofing, spyware, trojans and viruses: These attacks involve the use of malicious software or code that can infect, damage, or control the victim's computer or device. The malware may be delivered through email attachments, web links, downloads, or removable media. The malware may perform various actions, such as stealing data, displaying unwanted ads, encrypting files, or opening backdoors for remote access. For example, a ransomware virus may lock the victim's files and demand a payment to decrypt them.
  - Stolen hardware, such as laptops or mobile devices: These attacks involve the physical theft or loss of a device that contains sensitive or valuable data. The attackers may try to access the data on the device, or use it to launch further attacks. For example, a stolen laptop may contain confidential documents, passwords, or encryption keys that can compromise the security of an organization or an individual.
  - Denial-of-service and distributed denial-of-service attacks: These attacks aim to overwhelm the resources of a system or a network, such as bandwidth, memory, or processing power, to the point where it cannot respond to legitimate requests or function properly. The attackers may use multiple compromised devices or servers to generate a large amount of traffic or requests that target a specific system or service. For example, a DDoS attack may flood a website with requests that prevent it from serving its customers or users.
  - Breach of access: These attacks involve the unauthorized access or use of a system, network, or service by an attacker who exploits a vulnerability, a misconfiguration, or a weak password. The attacker may gain access to sensitive or confidential data, modify or delete files, execute commands, or install malware. For example, a breach of access may allow an attacker to access a database that contains customer information or trade secrets.
  - Password sniffing: These attacks involve the interception and capture of network traffic that contains passwords or other authentication data. The attacker may use a device or a software tool that can monitor and analyze the network traffic, or compromise a router, a switch, or a proxy server that handles the traffic. The attacker may use the captured passwords to access the victim's accounts or systems. For example, a password sniffer may capture the passwords of users who log in to a web service over an unencrypted or insecure connection.
  - System infiltration: These attacks involve the penetration and persistence of an attacker in a system or a network, often by using a combination of other attack methods, such as malware, phishing, or breach of access. The attacker may establish a foothold or a backdoor in the system or network, and then move laterally to access other systems or data. The attacker may also evade detection or removal by using stealth techniques, such as encryption, obfuscation, or rootkits. For example, a system infiltrator may use a phishing email to deliver a trojan that opens a backdoor in the victim's computer, and then use the backdoor to access other computers or servers in the same network.
  - Website defacement: These attacks involve the alteration or replacement of the content or appearance of a website by an attacker who exploits a vulnerability or a misconfiguration in the web server or the web application. The attacker may deface the website to display a message, an image, or a link that expresses their motive, opinion, or affiliation.
