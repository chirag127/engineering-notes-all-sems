### Summary of weaknesses of internet security for the notes of the Unit 5 - Internet Infrastructure in the subject of COMPUTER SYSTEM SECURITY

- Internet security is the protection of information systems and data from unauthorized access, use, modification, or destruction on the internet.
- Weaknesses of internet security are the factors that make information systems and data vulnerable to cyberattacks and compromise the confidentiality, integrity, and availability of the information.
- Some of the common weaknesses of internet security are:

  - **Lack of a high-level strategy**: Many businesses, especially new and small ones, simply lack a high-level strategy for their cybersecurity needs. They do not have a clear vision, goals, policies, or standards for securing their information systems and data. They also do not have a dedicated team, budget, or resources for cybersecurity. This makes them easy targets for cybercriminals who can exploit their weaknesses and gaps in their security posture.
  - **Unsecured networks**: If the network isn’t secured, it’s trivially easy for nefarious parties to gain access to your system. Unsecured networks are those that do not have proper encryption, authentication, firewall, or antivirus protection. They also do not have regular updates, patches, or backups. Unsecured networks expose the information systems and data to various threats, such as malware, ransomware, phishing, denial-of-service, man-in-the-middle, or brute-force attacks .
  - **Unsecured communication channels**: If the business is using unsecured communication channels, such as email, instant messaging, or social media, to transmit or store sensitive information, they are risking their data being intercepted, altered, or stolen by cybercriminals. Unsecured communication channels do not have adequate encryption, authentication, or verification mechanisms to ensure the security and privacy of the information. They also do not have proper policies or guidelines for the use and management of these channels.
  - **Unknown bugs**: Unknown bugs are the flaws or errors in the software or hardware of the information systems that can cause them to malfunction, crash, or behave unpredictably. Unknown bugs can also create vulnerabilities that can be exploited by cybercriminals to compromise the information systems and data. Unknown bugs can be introduced by the developers, vendors, or users of the software or hardware, and they can be difficult to detect, prevent, or fix .
  - **Outdated systems**: Outdated systems are those that are not updated or maintained regularly with the latest security patches, fixes, or features. Outdated systems are more prone to security breaches, as they may have known vulnerabilities that have been discovered and exploited by cybercriminals. Outdated systems also may not be compatible or interoperable with the current standards, technologies, or regulations for cybersecurity .
  - **Lack of monitoring**: Lack of monitoring is the absence or insufficiency of tools, processes, or personnel to monitor the performance, activity, or status of the information systems and data. Lack of monitoring makes it harder to detect, respond, or recover from cyberattacks, as there may be no or delayed alerts, logs, or reports of the incidents. Lack of monitoring also makes it difficult to measure, evaluate, or improve the effectiveness, efficiency, or compliance of the cybersecurity measures .
  - **IoT and multiple connection points**: IoT (Internet of Things) and multiple connection points are the devices, sensors, or systems that are connected to the internet and can communicate or exchange data with each other or with other networks. IoT and multiple connection points increase the complexity, diversity, and scale of the information systems and data, as well as the potential entry points for cyberattacks. IoT and multiple connection points also pose challenges for the security, privacy, and governance of the information, as they may have different standards, protocols, or regulations .
  - **Untrained employees**: Untrained employees are the staff or users of the information systems and data who do not have the necessary knowledge, skills, or awareness to protect them from cyberthreats. Untrained employees may not follow the best practices, policies, or procedures for cybersecurity, such as using strong passwords, avoiding phishing emails, or reporting incidents. Untrained employees may also unintentionally or intentionally cause or facilitate cyberattacks, such as by downloading malware, sharing passwords, or leaking data .
