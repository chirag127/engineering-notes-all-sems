- Confidentiality policies are rules or guidelines that specify how sensitive or confidential information should be protected from unauthorized access, disclosure, or modification.
- Confidentiality policies can be classified into two types: mandatory and discretionary.
- Mandatory confidentiality policies are enforced by the system based on the security labels or classifications assigned to the information and the subjects (users or processes) that access it. The system prevents any unauthorized access or disclosure of the information based on the security policy rules.
- Discretionary confidentiality policies are based on the owner's or creator's discretion or choice. The owner or creator of the information can grant or revoke access permissions to other subjects based on their trust or need. The system enforces the access permissions specified by the owner or creator.
- Confidentiality policies can be applied to different types of information, such as data, files, messages, communications, etc.
- Confidentiality policies can be implemented using different mechanisms, such as encryption, access control, authentication, auditing, etc.
- Confidentiality policies can be violated by different types of attacks, such as eavesdropping, interception, modification, impersonation, etc.
- Confidentiality policies can be evaluated using different criteria, such as effectiveness, efficiency, usability, scalability, etc.
