### DNS revisited

- Domain Name System (DNS) is a protocol that maps human-readable domain names to numerical IP addresses.
- DNS is essential for the functioning of the Internet, as it allows users to access websites and services by using easy-to-remember names instead of complex numbers.
- DNS also supports other functions, such as email delivery, load balancing, and service discovery.
- However, DNS is vulnerable to various attacks, such as spoofing, hijacking, denial-of-service, and cache poisoning, which can compromise the security, availability, and integrity of the DNS infrastructure and the services that rely on it.
- To mitigate these risks, several security measures have been proposed and implemented, such as:
  - DNS Security Extensions (DNSSEC), which add cryptographic signatures to DNS records to ensure their authenticity and integrity .
  - DNS over HTTPS (DoH) and DNS over TLS (DoT), which encrypt DNS queries and responses to protect them from eavesdropping and tampering.
  - DNS filtering, which blocks malicious or unwanted domains based on predefined rules or policies.
  - DNS monitoring and analysis, which detect and respond to anomalous or malicious DNS activities and traffic .
- Organisations should implement the appropriate security measures for their DNS infrastructure, based on their operating environment, threat model, and risk assessment  .
- Organisations should also educate their users and staff about the importance and benefits of DNS security, and how to use DNS services securely and responsibly.
