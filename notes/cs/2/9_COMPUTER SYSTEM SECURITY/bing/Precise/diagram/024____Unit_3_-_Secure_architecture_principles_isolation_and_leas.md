## Unit 3 - Secure architecture principles: Isolation and Least Privilege

Isolation and least privilege are two fundamental principles of secure architecture. These principles help to minimize the attack surface of a system and reduce the potential impact of a security breach.

1. **Isolation** refers to the separation of components, systems, or processes to prevent unauthorized access or interference. This can be achieved through various means, such as network segmentation, virtualization, and containerization.

2. **Least Privilege** is the principle of granting users and processes the minimum level of access required to perform their tasks. This helps to prevent unauthorized access to sensitive data and reduces the potential impact of a security breach.

By implementing these principles, organizations can improve the security of their systems and protect against common threats such as malware, data breaches, and unauthorized access. It is important to regularly review and update security measures to ensure that they remain effective in the face of evolving threats.
