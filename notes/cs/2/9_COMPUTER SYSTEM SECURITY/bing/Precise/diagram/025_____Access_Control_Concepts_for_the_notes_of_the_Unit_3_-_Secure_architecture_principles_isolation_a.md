### Access Control Concepts

Access control is the process of managing who or what can access resources in a computing environment. It is a fundamental concept in security that minimizes risk to the system. Here are some key concepts related to access control:

1. **Identification:** The process of identifying a user or system entity. This is typically done through the use of unique identifiers, such as usernames or system IDs.

2. **Authentication:** The process of verifying the identity of a user or system entity. This is typically done through the use of passwords, tokens, or other forms of authentication.

3. **Authorization:** The process of determining what actions a user or system entity is allowed to perform. This is typically done through the use of access control lists (ACLs) or other forms of authorization.

4. **Accountability:** The process of tracking and recording user or system entity actions. This is typically done through the use of logs or other forms of auditing.

5. **Least Privilege:** The principle of granting users or system entities the minimum level of access necessary to perform their duties. This helps to minimize the risk of unauthorized access or actions.

These concepts are essential for understanding and implementing secure architecture principles, isolation, and least privilege in computer system security. By properly managing access control, organizations can minimize the risk of security breaches and protect their sensitive data.
