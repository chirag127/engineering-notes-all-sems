### Other issues in access control for the notes of the Unit 3 - Secure architecture principles isolation and least privilege in the subject of COMPUTER SYSTEM SECURITY

- Access control is a fundamental component of computer security that restricts access to resources based on the identity of the user or the device.
- Access control can be implemented through various mechanisms, including authentication, authorization, and auditing.
- Authentication is the process of verifying the identity of a user or device. This can be done through various methods, such as passwords, biometrics, or smart cards.
- Authorization is the process of determining whether a user or device has the necessary permissions to access a resource. This can be done through access control lists, role-based access control, or attribute-based access control.
- Auditing is the process of recording and reviewing access control events to ensure that access control policies are being followed and to detect any unauthorized access.
- Other issues in access control include the need for separation of duties, the principle of least privilege, and the need for secure access control administration.
- Separation of duties is the practice of dividing responsibilities among multiple individuals or roles to prevent conflicts of interest or the abuse of power.
- The principle of least privilege states that users and devices should only be granted the minimum level of access necessary to perform their tasks.
- Secure access control administration involves ensuring that access control policies are properly implemented and maintained, and that any changes to access control are properly authorized and documented.
