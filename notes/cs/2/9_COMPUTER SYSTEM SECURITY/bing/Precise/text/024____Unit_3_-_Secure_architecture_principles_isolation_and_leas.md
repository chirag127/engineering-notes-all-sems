## Unit 3 - Secure architecture principles: Isolation and Least Privilege

1. **Isolation** refers to the separation of systems, processes, or data from one another to prevent unauthorized access or tampering. This can be achieved through various methods such as network segmentation, virtualization, and containerization.

2. **Least Privilege** is a principle that states that a user or process should only have the minimum level of access necessary to perform their tasks. This helps to reduce the attack surface and minimize the potential damage that can be caused by a compromised account or process.

3. Implementing these principles in a secure architecture can help to prevent unauthorized access, data breaches, and other security threats. It is important to regularly review and update access controls to ensure that the principles of isolation and least privilege are being effectively enforced.
