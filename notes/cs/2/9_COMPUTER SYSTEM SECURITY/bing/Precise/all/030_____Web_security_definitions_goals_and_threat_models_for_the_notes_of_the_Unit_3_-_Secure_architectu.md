### Web Security Definitions, Goals, and Threat Models

Web security refers to the measures taken to protect websites and web applications from unauthorized access, use, modification, or destruction. The goals of web security include protecting the confidentiality, integrity, and availability of data and resources.

Threat models are used to identify and analyze potential threats to a system, and to determine the value that potential mitigations would have in reducing or neutralizing those threats. Some common threat models include:

1. **STRIDE**: This model categorizes threats into six categories: Spoofing, Tampering, Repudiation, Information Disclosure, Denial of Service, and Elevation of Privilege.
2. **PASTA**: Process for Attack Simulation and Threat Analysis (PASTA) is a risk-centric threat modeling methodology aimed at identifying viable threat patterns against an application or system environment.
3. **VAST**: Visual, Agile, and Simple Threat modeling (VAST) is a methodology that focuses on providing a visual representation of the system, its components, and potential attack vectors.

These are some of the key concepts in web security, and understanding them is essential for building secure systems and protecting against potential threats.
