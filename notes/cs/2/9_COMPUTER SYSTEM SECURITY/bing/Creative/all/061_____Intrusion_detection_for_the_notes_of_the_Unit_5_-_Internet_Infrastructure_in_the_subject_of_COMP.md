# Intrusion Detection

- Intrusion detection is the process of monitoring and analyzing the events occurring in a computer system or network for signs of possible incidents, which are violations or imminent threats of violation of computer security policies, acceptable use policies, or standard security practices .
- Intrusion detection can help to identify and respond to attacks, prevent data loss or damage, enforce security policies, and collect forensic evidence.
- Intrusion detection can be performed at different levels, such as host-based, network-based, or application-based, depending on the scope and source of the monitored events.
- Intrusion detection can be classified into two main types, based on the detection method: signature-based and anomaly-based .
  - Signature-based intrusion detection compares the observed events with a predefined set of patterns or rules that describe known attacks or vulnerabilities. It can detect known attacks with high accuracy, but it cannot detect new or unknown attacks, and it requires frequent updates of the signature database.
  - Anomaly-based intrusion detection builds a profile of normal or expected behavior of the system or network, and flags any deviation from the baseline as a potential intrusion. It can detect new or unknown attacks, but it may generate false positives or negatives, and it requires a training phase to establish the baseline.
- Intrusion detection systems (IDS) are software or hardware tools that implement intrusion detection techniques and generate alerts or reports when they detect any suspicious or malicious activity .
  - IDS can be passive or active, depending on the response they take when they detect an intrusion. Passive IDS only monitor and report the intrusion, while active IDS can also take actions to block or prevent the intrusion, such as dropping packets, terminating connections, or reconfiguring firewalls. Active IDS are also called intrusion prevention systems (IPS).
  - IDS can be centralized or distributed, depending on the architecture they use to collect and analyze the events. Centralized IDS use a single sensor or agent to monitor the entire system or network, while distributed IDS use multiple sensors or agents to monitor different segments or components of the system or network, and a central controller to coordinate and correlate the analysis.
