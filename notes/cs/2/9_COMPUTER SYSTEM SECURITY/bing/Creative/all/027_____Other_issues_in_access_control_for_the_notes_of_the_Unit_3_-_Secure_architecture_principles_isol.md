# Other issues in access control for computer system security

Access control is the process of granting or denying access to resources based on the identity and attributes of users or groups. Access control systems aim to protect the confidentiality, integrity and availability of resources from unauthorized or malicious actors. However, access control systems can also face some issues that affect their effectiveness and security. Some of these issues are:

- **Not secure enough**: Access control systems can have various vulnerabilities that can be exploited by attackers, such as weak passwords, default settings, misconfigurations, outdated software, network exposure, physical access, etc. These vulnerabilities can allow attackers to bypass or compromise the access control system and gain unauthorized access to resources . Therefore, access control systems should be properly set up, updated, monitored and audited to ensure their security and compliance.
- **Keycards lack true authorization**: Keycards are a common method of authorization in access control systems, but they have some limitations and drawbacks. Keycards can be lost, stolen, duplicated, shared or forgotten, which can compromise the security and accountability of the access control system. Keycards also do not verify the identity of the user, only the possession of the card, which can allow unauthorized users to access resources if they have access to a valid card. Therefore, keycards should be combined with other factors of authentication, such as passwords, biometrics, tokens, etc., to provide stronger and more reliable authorization.
- **Improper setup**: Access control systems can be complex and require careful planning, design, implementation and maintenance. Improper setup of access control systems can result in errors, conflicts, inconsistencies, inefficiencies, loopholes or breaches in the access control policies and mechanisms . For example, granting too many or too few permissions, using inappropriate access control models, not defining clear roles and responsibilities, not enforcing separation of duties, not applying the principle of least privilege, etc. Therefore, access control systems should be set up according to the best practices and standards of the industry and the organization, and should be regularly reviewed and updated to reflect the changing needs and requirements of the system and the users.
- **Lack of management**: Access control systems require ongoing management and administration to ensure their proper functioning and security. Lack of management of access control systems can result in outdated, inaccurate, incomplete or redundant information, such as user accounts, groups, permissions, logs, etc. This can affect the performance, reliability and security of the access control system and the resources it protects . Therefore, access control systems should be managed by qualified and authorized personnel, who can perform tasks such as creating, modifying, deleting and auditing user accounts and groups, assigning and revoking permissions, monitoring and analyzing logs and alerts, reporting and resolving incidents, etc.
