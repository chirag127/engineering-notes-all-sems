# Email security

Email security is the practice of protecting email accounts and communications from unauthorized access, loss, or compromise. Email is often used to spread malware, spam, and phishing attacks, which can compromise the confidentiality, integrity, and availability of email data and systems. Email security also involves establishing policies and using tools to prevent, detect, and respond to these threats.

Some of the topics covered in this unit are:

- **Email encryption**: Email encryption is the process of transforming plain text email messages into ciphertext that can only be read by authorized parties. Email encryption can be done at different levels, such as end-to-end encryption, transport layer security (TLS), or secure/multipurpose internet mail extensions (S/MIME). Email encryption can help protect the privacy and authenticity of email messages and attachments.
- **Email authentication**: Email authentication is the process of verifying the identity and legitimacy of the sender and the recipient of an email message. Email authentication can help prevent spoofing, impersonation, and forgery of email messages, which are common techniques used by attackers to trick users into opening malicious links or attachments, or revealing sensitive information. Some of the email authentication protocols are sender policy framework (SPF), domain keys identified mail (DKIM), and domain-based message authentication, reporting, and conformance (DMARC).
- **Email filtering**: Email filtering is the process of screening incoming and outgoing email messages based on predefined criteria, such as sender, recipient, subject, content, or attachments. Email filtering can help block unwanted or malicious email messages, such as spam, phishing, or malware, from reaching the user's inbox or the organization's network. Email filtering can also help enforce email policies, such as limiting the size or type of attachments, or restricting the use of certain words or phrases.
- **Email archiving**: Email archiving is the process of storing email messages and attachments in a secure and searchable repository for future reference, compliance, or legal purposes. Email archiving can help preserve the integrity and availability of email data, as well as reduce the storage and bandwidth costs of email servers. Email archiving can also help facilitate e-discovery, audit, and litigation processes, as well as comply with regulatory requirements, such as the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) or the Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act (HIPAA).

Some of the best practices for email security include:

- **Educate employees**: Employees are often the weakest link in email security, as they may fall victim to social engineering, phishing, or other human errors. Therefore, it is important to educate employees with periodic training to minimize the risk of human error and ensure that they follow the email security policies and procedures. Employees should also learn how to recognize the signs of a malicious email, such as misspelled domains, urgent requests, or suspicious attachments, and report them to the IT department or the email security provider.
- **Create strong passwords**: Passwords are the first line of defense for email accounts, so they should be strong, unique, and hard to guess. Passwords should also be changed regularly and never shared with anyone. Additionally, employees should use different passwords for different accounts, and avoid using common or personal information, such as names, dates, or hobbies, as passwords. A password manager can help generate and store strong passwords securely.
- **Enable two-factor authentication**: Two-factor authentication (2FA) is a security feature that requires users to provide two pieces of evidence to access their email accounts, such as a password and a code sent to their phone or email. 2FA can help prevent unauthorized access to email accounts, even if the password is compromised. 2FA can also help alert users of any suspicious login attempts or activity on their email accounts.
- **Use antivirus software**: Antivirus software is a program that scans and removes malicious software, such as viruses, worms, or ransomware, from the computer or device. Antivirus software can help protect the email system from malware infections, which can compromise the email data and functionality. Antivirus software should be updated regularly to detect and remove the latest threats.
- **Use secure email providers**: Secure email providers are email services that offer enhanced security features, such as encryption, authentication, filtering, and archiving, to protect the email data and communications. Secure email providers can help prevent unauthorized access, loss, or compromise of email data, as well as comply with the email security standards and regulations. Some examples of secure email providers are ProtonMail, Tutanota, or Hushmail.
