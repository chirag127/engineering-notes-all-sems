# Basic terminologies for the notes of the Unit 4 - Basic cryptography in the subject of COMPUTER SYSTEM SECURITY

- Cryptography: The science or art of designing and using methods to protect information from unauthorized access or modification. Cryptography involves the use of terms like plaintext, ciphertext, algorithm, key, encryption, and decryption .
- Plaintext: The original message or data that needs to be encrypted or hidden. Plaintext is also called cleartext .
- Ciphertext: The transformed message or data that results from applying an encryption algorithm to the plaintext. Ciphertext is also called cryptogram .
- Algorithm: A set of rules or steps that defines how to perform a certain task, such as encryption or decryption. An algorithm can be expressed in various ways, such as in natural language, pseudocode, flowchart, or mathematical notation .
- Key: A piece of information that is used as an input to an encryption or decryption algorithm, along with the plaintext or ciphertext. The key determines the output of the algorithm and is usually chosen randomly or pseudorandomly. The key is also called a secret or a passphrase .
- Encryption: The process of transforming plaintext into ciphertext using an algorithm and a key. Encryption is also called encipherment or encoding. The purpose of encryption is to ensure the confidentiality, integrity, and authenticity of the plaintext .
- Decryption: The reverse process of encryption, which transforms ciphertext back into plaintext using an algorithm and a key. Decryption is also called decipherment or decoding. The purpose of decryption is to recover the original plaintext from the ciphertext .
- Cryptanalysis: The science or art of breaking or analyzing cryptosystems, such as by finding the key, the algorithm, or the plaintext. Cryptanalysis is also called codebreaking or hacking. The purpose of cryptanalysis is to defeat the security or authentication of a system .
- Cryptosystem: A system that consists of the plaintext, the ciphertext, the algorithm, and the key. A cryptosystem defines how encryption and decryption are performed. A cryptosystem can be symmetric or asymmetric, depending on the type of keys used .
- Symmetric cryptosystem: A cryptosystem that uses the same key for both encryption and decryption. The key is shared between the sender and the receiver of the message. Symmetric cryptosystems are also called secret-key or private-key cryptosystems. Examples of symmetric cryptosystems are AES, DES, and RC4 .
- Asymmetric cryptosystem: A cryptosystem that uses different keys for encryption and decryption. The keys are mathematically related but not identical. The encryption key is public and can be distributed to anyone, while the decryption key is private and kept secret by the owner. Asymmetric cryptosystems are also called public-key or public-key cryptosystems. Examples of asymmetric cryptosystems are RSA, Diffie-Hellman, and ECC .
- Hash: A function that maps an arbitrary input (such as a message or a file) to a fixed-length output (such as a string of bits or characters). A hash function is also called a message digest, a checksum, or a fingerprint. The purpose of a hash function is to verify the integrity or authenticity of the input, by comparing the output with a known or expected value. A hash function should be one-way, meaning that it is easy to compute the output from the input, but hard to find the input from the output .
