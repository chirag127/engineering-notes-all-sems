# Digital signature

- A digital signature is a type of electronic signature that encrypts documents with digital codes that are particularly difficult to duplicate.
- A digital signature is a mathematical scheme for verifying the authenticity and integrity of digital messages or documents.
- A digital signature can provide evidence of origin, identity and status of electronic documents, transactions or digital messages.
- A digital signature can also be used to acknowledge informed consent by the signer.
- A digital signature is generated by a signing algorithm that uses a private key and a public key.
- A private key is a secret value that only the signer knows and uses to create the signature.
- A public key is a value that is derived from the private key and can be shared with anyone who wants to verify the signature.
- A verification algorithm is used to check the validity of the signature by using the public key and the original message.
- A digital signature is valid if the verification algorithm confirms that the signature matches the message and the public key belongs to the signer.
- A digital signature is invalid if the verification algorithm detects any alteration in the message or the signature, or if the public key does not belong to the signer.
- A digital signature can be used for various purposes, such as securing online transactions, protecting email communications, authenticating software downloads, and signing legal documents.
- A digital signature can also provide non-repudiation, which means that the signer cannot deny having signed the message or document.
- A digital signature can be implemented using various cryptographic algorithms, such as the Digital Signature Algorithm (DSA), the Rivest-Shamir-Adleman (RSA) algorithm, the Elliptic Curve Digital Signature Algorithm (ECDSA), and the EdDSA algorithm.
- A digital signature can have different formats, such as the XML Signature, the Portable Document Format (PDF) signature, the Cryptographic Message Syntax (CMS) signature, and the JSON Web Signature (JWS).
- A digital signature can have different levels of security, depending on the strength of the cryptographic algorithm, the length of the key, and the protection of the private key.
- A digital signature can also have different legal implications, depending on the jurisdiction, the type of document, and the consent of the parties involved.
