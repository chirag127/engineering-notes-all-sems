## Unit 3 - Secure architecture principles: isolation and least privilege

- Isolation and least privilege are two key principles of secure design that aim to reduce the attack surface and minimize the impact of a breach.
- Isolation means separating different components or layers of a system, such as data, processes, networks, or users, so that they cannot interfere with each other or access unauthorized resources.
- Least privilege means granting only the minimum permissions or access rights needed for a component or user to perform a specific task or function, and revoking them when no longer needed.
- Some benefits of applying isolation and least privilege are:
  - Improved confidentiality, integrity, and availability of data and resources.
  - Reduced risk of unauthorized access, modification, or deletion of data or resources.
  - Reduced risk of lateral movement, privilege escalation, or data exfiltration by attackers.
  - Easier auditing, monitoring, and logging of activities and events.
  - Easier compliance with security policies and regulations.
- Some examples of implementing isolation and least privilege are:
  - Using separate networks, subnets, or virtual machines for different applications, environments, or roles.
  - Using firewalls, network security groups, or access control lists to restrict network traffic and communication between components or users.
  - Using encryption, hashing, or digital signatures to protect data in transit and at rest.
  - Using authentication, authorization, or role-based access control to verify the identity and permissions of components or users.
  - Using sandboxing, containers, or virtualization to isolate processes or applications from the underlying system or other processes or applications.
  - Using code signing, secure boot, or trusted platform modules to verify the integrity and origin of code or firmware.
  - Using password policies, multifactor authentication, or single sign-on to enforce strong and secure credentials for users.
  - Using just-in-time or just-enough administration to grant temporary and limited privileges to users or administrators.
