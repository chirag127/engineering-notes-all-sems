### Intrusion Detection Systems

- An intrusion detection system (IDS) is a device or software application that monitors a network or systems for malicious activity or policy violations.
- An IDS can detect known threats and suspicious or malicious activity based on predefined rules or signatures, and send alerts to IT and security teams when it detects any security risks and threats.
- An IDS can also perform anomaly detection, which is the process of identifying deviations from normal or expected behavior patterns in network traffic or system activities.
- An IDS can be classified into two main types: network-based IDS (NIDS) and host-based IDS (HIDS).
  - A NIDS monitors and analyzes network traffic at strategic points, such as routers, switches, or firewalls, and can detect attacks that target the network infrastructure or multiple hosts.
  - A HIDS monitors and analyzes the activities of a single host, such as a server or a workstation, and can detect attacks that target the host's operating system, applications, or files.
- An IDS can also be combined with an intrusion prevention system (IPS), which is a device or software application that can block or prevent malicious activity or policy violations, rather than just reporting them.
  - An IPS can be network-based (NIPS) or host-based (HIPS), and can operate in inline mode (where it intercepts and filters network traffic) or promiscuous mode (where it only monitors network traffic).
- An IDS can provide several benefits for network and system security, such as :
  - Detecting and alerting on potential intrusions, breaches, or attacks, and providing evidence for forensic analysis and incident response.
  - Enhancing the visibility and awareness of network and system activities, and identifying vulnerabilities, misconfigurations, or policy violations.
  - Complementing other security controls, such as firewalls, antivirus, or encryption, and providing an additional layer of defense.
  - Improving the compliance with regulatory standards, such as PCI DSS, HIPAA, or GDPR, and demonstrating the due diligence of security practices.
- An IDS can also face some challenges and limitations, such as :
  - Generating false positives (alerts on benign or normal activities) or false negatives (missed alerts on malicious or abnormal activities), which can affect the accuracy and reliability of the IDS.
  - Requiring constant updates and maintenance of the rules or signatures, and the tuning and optimization of the anomaly detection algorithms, to keep up with the evolving threat landscape and the changing network or system environment.
  - Consuming significant network or system resources, such as bandwidth, CPU, or memory, and potentially affecting the performance or availability of the network or system.
  - Being vulnerable to evasion or bypass techniques, such as encryption, fragmentation, or obfuscation, which can hide or disguise the malicious activity or policy violation from the IDS.
