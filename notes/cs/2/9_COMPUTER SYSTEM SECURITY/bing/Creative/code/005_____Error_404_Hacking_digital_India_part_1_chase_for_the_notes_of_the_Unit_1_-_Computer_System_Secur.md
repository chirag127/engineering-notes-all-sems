Some of the key concepts and terms related to computer system security that are covered in Part 1 - Chase are:

- Computer system security: The protection of computer systems and networks from unauthorized access, use, modification, or destruction.
- Cyber attack: An attempt to compromise the confidentiality, integrity, or availability of a computer system or network, or the information stored or transmitted on it, by exploiting its vulnerabilities.
- Cyber terrorism: The use of cyber attacks to cause fear, harm, or disruption to a government, organization, or society, for political, religious, or ideological motives.
- Digital India: A flagship initiative of the Indian government, launched in 2015, to transform the country into a digitally empowered society and knowledge economy, by providing digital infrastructure, services, and literacy to all citizens.
- Hacker: A person who uses their skills and knowledge of computer systems and networks to gain unauthorized access, use, or control of them, for various purposes, such as curiosity, exploration, challenge, fun, profit, or malicious intent.
- IP spoofing: A technique of creating and sending packets with a forged source IP address, to hide the true identity or location of the sender, or to impersonate another device or user on the network.
- Packet sniffing: A technique of capturing and analyzing the data packets that are transmitted or received on a network, to monitor the network traffic, or to extract information from the packets, such as passwords, usernames, or credit card numbers.
- Port scanning: A technique of probing a target device or network for open ports, to identify the services or applications that are running on them, or to find potential vulnerabilities or weaknesses that can be exploited.
- Social engineering: A technique of manipulating or deceiving people into revealing sensitive or confidential information, or performing actions that compromise the security of a computer system or network, by exploiting their psychological or emotional vulnerabilities, such as trust, curiosity, fear, or greed.
