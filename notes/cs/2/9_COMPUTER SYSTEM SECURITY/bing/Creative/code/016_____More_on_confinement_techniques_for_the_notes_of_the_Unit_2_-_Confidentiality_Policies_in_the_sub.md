### More on confinement techniques

- Confinement is a mechanism for enforcing the principle of least privilege , which means that a process or a user should only have the minimum access rights necessary to perform its function.
- The confinement problem is that the confined process needs to transmit data to another process, but the confinement mechanism must prevent the leakage of unauthorized or sensitive data .
- Some examples of confinement techniques are:
  - Encryption: This is a method of transforming data into an unreadable form using a secret key, so that only authorized parties can decrypt and access the data. Encryption can protect data confidentiality in storage and in transit.
  - Access controls: These are rules or policies that specify who can access what data and how. Access controls can be based on user identity, role, or context. Access controls can prevent unauthorized or malicious access to data or resources.
  - Sandboxing: This is a technique of isolating a process or an application from the rest of the system, by restricting its access to resources, such as files, network, or memory. Sandboxing can prevent a process from interfering with or compromising other processes or the system.
  - Information flow control: This is a technique of tracking and regulating the flow of data within a system, based on security labels or policies. Information flow control can prevent data from flowing to unauthorized or untrusted destinations or channels.
