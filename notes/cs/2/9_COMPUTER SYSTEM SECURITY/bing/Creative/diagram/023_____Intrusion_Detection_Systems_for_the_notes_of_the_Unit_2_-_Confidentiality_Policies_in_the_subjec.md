### Intrusion Detection Systems

- An intrusion detection system (IDS) is a device or software application that monitors a network or systems for malicious activity or policy violations  .
- An IDS can send alerts to IT and security teams when it detects any security risks and threats, and can also log the events for further analysis .
- An IDS can be classified into two main types: network-based IDS (NIDS) and host-based IDS (HIDS)  .
- A network-based IDS (NIDS) monitors the traffic on a network segment or a device, and analyzes the packets for any signs of intrusion  .
- A host-based IDS (HIDS) monitors the activity on a specific host, such as a server or a workstation, and analyzes the system logs, files, processes, and network connections for any signs of intrusion  .
- An IDS can use different methods to detect intrusions, such as signature-based detection, anomaly-based detection, or behavior-based detection  .
- Signature-based detection compares the network or system activity with a predefined set of patterns or rules that indicate known attacks or vulnerabilities  .
- Anomaly-based detection compares the network or system activity with a baseline of normal behavior, and flags any deviations or anomalies that indicate possible attacks or intrusions  .
- Behavior-based detection monitors the network or system activity for any changes or events that indicate malicious or suspicious behavior, such as unauthorized access, privilege escalation, or data exfiltration  .
- An IDS can be combined with an intrusion prevention system (IPS) to form an intrusion detection and prevention system (IDPS), which can not only detect but also prevent or block intrusions by taking actions such as dropping packets, terminating connections, or reconfiguring firewalls    .
- An IDPS can provide several benefits, such as improving the security posture, enhancing the visibility, reducing the attack surface, and complying with the regulations of a network or system .
- An IDPS can also face some challenges, such as generating false positives or negatives, requiring constant updates, consuming resources, or being evaded by sophisticated attackers .
