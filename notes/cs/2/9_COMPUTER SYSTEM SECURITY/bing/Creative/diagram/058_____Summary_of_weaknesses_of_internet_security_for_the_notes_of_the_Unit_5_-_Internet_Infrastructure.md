### Summary of weaknesses of internet security

- Internet security is the protection of information systems and networks from cyberattacks, unauthorized access, data theft, and other threats.
- Weaknesses of internet security can compromise the confidentiality, integrity, and availability of data and resources, and cause financial, reputational, and legal damages to individuals and organizations.
- Some of the common weaknesses of internet security are:

  - **Lack of a high-level strategy**: Many businesses, especially new and small ones, simply lack a high-level strategy for their cybersecurity needs. They do not have a clear vision, goals, policies, and procedures for securing their systems and data. They also do not allocate sufficient resources, personnel, and training for cybersecurity. This makes them vulnerable to cyberattacks and compliance issues.

  - **Unsecured networks**: If the network is not secured, it is trivially easy for nefarious parties to gain access to the system. Unsecured networks can include public Wi-Fi, wireless routers, firewalls, switches, and other devices that connect to the internet. Unsecured networks can allow attackers to intercept, modify, or redirect network traffic, and launch malware, phishing, denial-of-service, or man-in-the-middle attacks .

  - **Unsecured communication channels**: If the communication channels are not secured, they can expose sensitive data and information to unauthorized parties. Communication channels can include email, instant messaging, voice calls, video conferencing, and file sharing. Unsecured communication channels can allow attackers to eavesdrop, spoof, or tamper with the messages, and steal passwords, personal information, or intellectual property.

  - **Unknown bugs**: Unknown bugs are flaws or errors in the software or hardware that can cause unexpected or undesired behavior. Unknown bugs can be exploited by attackers to bypass security measures, access restricted data or functions, or cause system crashes or malfunctions. Unknown bugs can be introduced by developers, vendors, or users, and can be difficult to detect and fix .

  - **Outdated systems**: Outdated systems are software or hardware that are no longer supported, updated, or patched by the developers or vendors. Outdated systems can have known or unknown vulnerabilities that can be exploited by attackers. Outdated systems can also be incompatible with newer technologies or standards, and cause performance or functionality issues .

  - **Lack of monitoring**: Lack of monitoring is the absence or insufficiency of tools or processes to track, analyze, and report the activities and events on the system or network. Lack of monitoring can prevent the detection, prevention, or response to cyberattacks, incidents, or anomalies. Lack of monitoring can also hinder the identification, assessment, and remediation of security risks or weaknesses.

  - **IoT and multiple connection points**: IoT (Internet of Things) and multiple connection points are the devices or systems that are connected to the internet or network, such as smart phones, tablets, laptops, cameras, sensors, printers, or appliances. IoT and multiple connection points can increase the complexity, diversity, and exposure of the system or network, and create more entry points for attackers. IoT and multiple connection points can also have weak or default passwords, poor encryption, or lack of updates or patches, making them easy targets for cyberattacks .

  - **Untrained employees**: Untrained employees are the staff or users who are not aware, educated, or trained on the best practices, policies, and procedures for cybersecurity. Untrained employees can be the weakest link in the security chain, and cause or facilitate cyberattacks, incidents, or breaches. Untrained employees can fall victim to phishing, social engineering, or insider threats, and compromise their credentials, devices, or data. Untrained employees can also violate or neglect the security rules or guidelines, and expose the system or network to risks or threats .
