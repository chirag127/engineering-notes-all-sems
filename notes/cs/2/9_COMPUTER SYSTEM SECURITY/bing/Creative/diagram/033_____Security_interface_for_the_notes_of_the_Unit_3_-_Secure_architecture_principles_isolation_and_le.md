### Secure Architecture Principles

- Secure architecture principles are guidelines or best practices for designing and implementing secure systems.
- Some of the common secure architecture principles are:

  - Compartmentalization: This principle involves dividing a system into smaller and isolated components or modules, each with a specific function and a minimal set of privileges. This reduces the attack surface and the impact of a compromise. Examples of compartmentalization are process isolation, sandboxing, and virtualization.
  - Principle of least privilege: This principle states that a system or a user should only have the minimum privileges necessary to perform a task, and no more. This limits the damage that can be done by a malicious or compromised entity. Examples of applying the principle of least privilege are using separate accounts for different tasks, enforcing access control policies, and dropping privileges after initialization.
  - Defense in depth: This principle involves using multiple and diverse layers of security mechanisms to protect a system, rather than relying on a single one. This increases the difficulty and cost of an attack, and provides redundancy in case of a failure. Examples of defense in depth are using firewalls, encryption, authentication, and auditing.
  - Keep it simple: This principle advocates for simplicity and clarity in the design and implementation of a system, as complexity often introduces errors and vulnerabilities. A simple system is easier to understand, maintain, and verify. Examples of keeping it simple are using standard and well-tested components, avoiding unnecessary features, and documenting the system.
