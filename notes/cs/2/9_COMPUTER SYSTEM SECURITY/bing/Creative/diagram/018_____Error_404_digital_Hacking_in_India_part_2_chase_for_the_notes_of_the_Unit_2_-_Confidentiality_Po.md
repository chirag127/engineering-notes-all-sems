### Unit 2 - Confidentiality Policies

- Confidentiality policies are rules that specify how sensitive information should be protected from unauthorized access, disclosure, modification, or destruction.
- Confidentiality policies are essential for ensuring the security and privacy of data, as well as the trust and reputation of the data owners and users.
- Confidentiality policies can be classified into two types: mandatory and discretionary.
  - Mandatory policies are enforced by the system and apply to all users and data. They are based on predefined security levels or labels that indicate the sensitivity and clearance of data and users. Examples of mandatory policies are multilevel security (MLS) and role-based access control (RBAC).
  - Discretionary policies are defined by the data owners and can be modified by them. They are based on the principle of least privilege, which means that users are granted only the minimum access rights necessary to perform their tasks. Examples of discretionary policies are access control lists (ACLs) and encryption keys.
- Confidentiality policies can be implemented using various mechanisms, such as:
  - Authentication: verifying the identity of users and devices before granting access to data.
  - Authorization: checking the permissions and privileges of users and devices before allowing access to data.
  - Encryption: transforming data into an unreadable form that can only be decrypted by authorized parties.
  - Auditing: recording and monitoring the activities and events related to data access and usage.
