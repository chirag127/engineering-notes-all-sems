### Routing security

Routing security is the protection of the Internet's routing system from errors and attacks that can compromise its availability, integrity, and confidentiality. Routing security is an important aspect of computer system security, as the Internet relies on the correct and efficient functioning of the routing system to deliver data packets to their intended destinations.

Some of the topics that are covered in routing security are:

- The Border Gateway Protocol (BGP), which is the main protocol used to exchange routing information between autonomous systems (ASes) on the Internet. BGP is vulnerable to various types of attacks, such as prefix hijacking, route leaks, and denial-of-service (DoS) attacks, that can disrupt or manipulate the routing system .
- The security mechanisms and best practices that can be applied to BGP, such as filtering, authentication, encryption, origin validation, path validation, and monitoring, to prevent or mitigate routing attacks and improve routing stability and performance .
- The other routing protocols that are used in different network domains, such as Routing Information Protocol (RIP), Enhanced Interior Gateway Routing Protocol (EIGRP), and Open Shortest Path First (OSPF), and their security features and challenges.
- The emerging technologies and standards that aim to enhance routing security, such as Resource Public Key Infrastructure (RPKI), BGPsec, and Software-Defined Networking (SDN), and their benefits and limitations .

Routing security is a complex and dynamic field that requires constant research and innovation to cope with the evolving threats and challenges of the Internet. Routing security is also a collaborative effort that involves multiple stakeholders, such as network operators, vendors, researchers, and policy makers, to coordinate and cooperate on the development and deployment of routing security solutions.
