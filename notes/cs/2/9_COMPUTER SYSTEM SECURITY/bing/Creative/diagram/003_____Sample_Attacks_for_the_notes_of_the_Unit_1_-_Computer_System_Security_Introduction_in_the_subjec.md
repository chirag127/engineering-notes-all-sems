### Sample Attacks

A sample attack is an example of a cyber attack, which is a set of actions performed by threat actors, who try to gain unauthorized access, steal data or cause damage to computers, computer networks, or other computing systems. A sample attack can be used to illustrate the types, methods, and impacts of cyber attacks, as well as the possible countermeasures and prevention strategies. Here are some sample attacks based on the common types of cyber attacks   :

- **Identity theft, fraud, extortion**: A threat actor may use phishing, spoofing, or malware to obtain personal or financial information from a victim, such as passwords, credit card numbers, or social security numbers. The threat actor may then use this information to impersonate the victim, access their accounts, make fraudulent transactions, or demand ransom for restoring the data or services.
- **Malware, phishing, spamming, spoofing, spyware, trojans and viruses**: A threat actor may use malicious software or code to infect, damage, or control a computer system or network. Malware can be delivered through email attachments, web links, downloads, or removable devices. Malware can perform various functions, such as stealing data, encrypting files, displaying unwanted ads, spying on user activity, or creating backdoors for remote access.
- **Stolen hardware, such as laptops or mobile devices**: A threat actor may physically steal a device that contains sensitive or confidential data, such as a laptop or a smartphone. The threat actor may then access the data on the device, or use the device to access other systems or networks that the device is connected to.
- **Denial-of-service and distributed denial-of-service attacks**: A threat actor may launch a denial-of-service (DoS) attack or a distributed denial-of-service (DDoS) attack to overwhelm the resources of a system or a network, such as bandwidth, memory, or processing power. The attack may prevent the system or the network from responding to legitimate requests, or slow down its performance. The attack may be motivated by sabotage, extortion, or activism.
- **Breach of access**: A threat actor may exploit a vulnerability or a weakness in a system or a network to gain unauthorized access to its data or functions. The threat actor may then modify, delete, or leak the data, or perform malicious actions on the system or the network, such as installing malware, creating accounts, or changing settings.
- **Password sniffing**: A threat actor may use a software or a device to capture or intercept the passwords or other credentials that are transmitted over a network. The threat actor may then use these credentials to access the accounts or systems of the victims, or sell them to other threat actors.
- **System infiltration**: A threat actor may use a technique such as brute force, credential stuffing, or social engineering to bypass the authentication or authorization mechanisms of a system or a network. The threat actor may then access the data or functions of the system or the network, or plant malware, backdoors, or rootkits for future access.
- **Website defacement**: A threat actor may alter the appearance or the content of a website, such as changing its images, text, or layout. The threat actor may do this to express a political or social message, to damage the reputation or the credibility of the website owner, or to redirect the visitors to another website.
