### More on confinement techniques

Confinement techniques are methods to prevent unauthorized information flow from a process or a system to another process or system. Confinement techniques aim to enforce the principle of least privilege, which states that a process or a system should only have the minimum access rights necessary to perform its function. Confinement techniques can be classified into two categories: static and dynamic.

- Static confinement techniques are applied before the execution of a process or a system, and they do not change during the execution. Static confinement techniques include:

  - Access control mechanisms, such as discretionary access control (DAC), mandatory access control (MAC), or role-based access control (RBAC), which restrict the access rights of a process or a system to the resources it needs .
  - Encryption techniques, such as symmetric encryption, asymmetric encryption, or hybrid encryption, which protect the confidentiality of data stored or transmitted by a process or a system.
  - Sandboxing techniques, such as virtual machines, containers, or chroot jails, which isolate a process or a system from the rest of the environment and limit its interactions with other processes or systems.

- Dynamic confinement techniques are applied during the execution of a process or a system, and they can change according to the context or the behavior of the process or the system. Dynamic confinement techniques include:

  - Information flow control mechanisms, such as non-interference, non-deducibility, or declassification, which monitor and regulate the flow of information from a process or a system to another process or a system, and prevent or mitigate covert channels .
  - Audit and logging mechanisms, such as intrusion detection systems, intrusion prevention systems, or security information and event management systems, which record and analyze the activities and events of a process or a system, and detect and respond to any anomalies or violations.
  - Self-protection mechanisms, such as firewalls, antivirus software, or encryption software, which protect a process or a system from external attacks or malicious code, and prevent or mitigate data breaches or data exfiltration.
