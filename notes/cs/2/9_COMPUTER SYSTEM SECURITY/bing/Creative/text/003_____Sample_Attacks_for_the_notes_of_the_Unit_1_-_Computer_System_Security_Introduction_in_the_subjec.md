### Sample Attacks

A sample attack is an example of a cyber attack, which is a set of actions performed by threat actors, who try to gain unauthorized access, steal data or cause damage to computers, computer networks, or other computing systems. A sample attack can be used to illustrate the types, methods, and impacts of cyber attacks, as well as the possible countermeasures and prevention strategies. Here are some sample attacks based on the common types of cyber attacks   :

- **Identity theft, fraud, extortion**: A threat actor may use phishing, spoofing, or malware to obtain personal or financial information from a victim, such as passwords, credit card numbers, or social security numbers. The threat actor may then use this information to impersonate the victim, access their accounts, make fraudulent transactions, or demand ransom for restoring the data or identity.
- **Malware, phishing, spamming, spoofing, spyware, trojans and viruses**: A threat actor may use malicious software or code to infect, damage, or control a computer system or network. Malware can be delivered through email attachments, web links, downloads, or removable devices. Malware can perform various functions, such as stealing data, displaying unwanted ads, encrypting files, logging keystrokes, or opening backdoors for remote access.
- **Stolen hardware, such as laptops or mobile devices**: A threat actor may physically steal a device that contains sensitive or confidential data, such as a laptop, smartphone, or tablet. The threat actor may then access the data on the device, or use the device to access other systems or networks that the device is connected to.
- **Denial-of-service and distributed denial-of-service attacks**: A threat actor may launch a denial-of-service (DoS) attack or a distributed denial-of-service (DDoS) attack to overwhelm the resources of a system or network, such as bandwidth, memory, or processing power. The attack can be performed by sending a large number of requests or packets to the target, or by exploiting a vulnerability in the target. The attack can cause the target to slow down, crash, or become unavailable for legitimate users or services.
- **Breach of access**: A threat actor may exploit a weakness in the authentication, authorization, or encryption mechanisms of a system or network, to gain unauthorized access to the data or resources. The threat actor may then view, modify, delete, or exfiltrate the data, or perform other malicious actions on the system or network.
- **Password sniffing**: A threat actor may use a software or hardware tool to capture or intercept the passwords or other credentials of a user or a system, as they are transmitted over a network. The threat actor may then use the passwords or credentials to access the user's or system's accounts, data, or resources.
- **System infiltration**: A threat actor may use a malware, a vulnerability, or a backdoor to infiltrate a system or network, and establish a persistent presence or control. The threat actor may then perform reconnaissance, lateral movement, privilege escalation, or data exfiltration, or launch further attacks from the compromised system or network.
- **Website defacement**: A threat actor may use a vulnerability, a malware, or a breach of access to alter the appearance or content of a website, such as changing the images, text, or layout. The threat actor may do this to spread a message, damage the reputation, or disrupt the functionality of the website.
