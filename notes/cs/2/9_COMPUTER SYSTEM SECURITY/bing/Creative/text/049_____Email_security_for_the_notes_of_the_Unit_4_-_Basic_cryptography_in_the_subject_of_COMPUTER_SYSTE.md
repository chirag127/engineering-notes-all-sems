### Email security

Email security is the practice of protecting email accounts and communications from unauthorized access, loss, or compromise. Email is often used to spread malware, spam, and phishing attacks, which can compromise the confidentiality, integrity, and availability of email data and systems. Email security also involves establishing policies and using tools to prevent, detect, and respond to these threats.

Some of the topics related to email security are:

- **Encryption**: Encryption is the process of transforming plain text into ciphertext, which can only be read by authorized parties who have the decryption key. Encryption can protect the content of email messages and attachments from being intercepted or modified by unauthorized parties. Encryption can be applied at different levels, such as end-to-end encryption, transport layer security (TLS), or secure/multipurpose internet mail extensions (S/MIME).
- **Authentication**: Authentication is the process of verifying the identity of the sender or the recipient of an email message. Authentication can prevent spoofing, impersonation, and phishing attacks, which can trick users into revealing sensitive information or performing malicious actions. Authentication can be achieved by using various methods, such as passwords, tokens, certificates, or biometrics.
- **Spam filtering**: Spam filtering is the process of identifying and blocking unwanted or unsolicited email messages, which can consume bandwidth, storage, and processing resources, as well as expose users to malware, phishing, or fraud. Spam filtering can be performed by using various techniques, such as blacklists, whitelists, content analysis, or machine learning.
- **Malware scanning**: Malware scanning is the process of detecting and removing malicious software, such as viruses, worms, trojans, or ransomware, that can infect email systems or devices, and cause damage, disruption, or data theft. Malware scanning can be performed by using various tools, such as antivirus software, firewalls, or sandboxing.
- **Phishing detection**: Phishing detection is the process of identifying and preventing phishing attacks, which are fraudulent attempts to obtain sensitive information or credentials from users by impersonating legitimate entities or individuals. Phishing detection can be performed by using various techniques, such as domain name system (DNS) security, sender policy framework (SPF), domain keys identified mail (DKIM), or domain-based message authentication, reporting, and conformance (DMARC).
- **User awareness training**: User awareness training is the process of educating and informing users about the risks and best practices of email security, and how to recognize and avoid common threats, such as malware, spam, or phishing. User awareness training can help to minimize the human error factor and enhance the overall email security posture of an organization or an individual.
