### Intrusion detection

- Intrusion detection is the process of monitoring the events occurring in a computer system or network and analyzing them for signs of possible incidents, which are violations or imminent threats of violation of computer security policies, acceptable use policies, or standard security practices.
- Intrusion detection can be performed at different levels, such as network, host, or application level, depending on the scope and objectives of the monitoring.
- Intrusion detection can be classified into two main types: anomaly-based and signature-based.
  - Anomaly-based intrusion detection compares the current behavior or activity of the system or network with a baseline or normal profile, and flags any deviations as potential intrusions. Anomaly-based intrusion detection can detect unknown or novel attacks, but may also generate false positives due to legitimate changes or variations in the system or network behavior.
  - Signature-based intrusion detection relies on predefined patterns or rules that describe known attacks or malicious activities, and matches them with the observed events or data. Signature-based intrusion detection can detect known attacks with high accuracy, but may fail to detect new or modified attacks that do not match any existing signatures.
- Intrusion detection can be implemented using different techniques, such as statistical analysis, machine learning, rule-based systems, or artificial neural networks, depending on the type and complexity of the data and the detection method.
- Intrusion detection can be complemented by intrusion prevention, which is the process of taking actions to stop or mitigate the detected intrusions, such as blocking, alerting, or quarantining. Intrusion prevention can be performed by the same system that performs intrusion detection, or by a separate system that receives the alerts or reports from the intrusion detection system. Intrusion prevention can also be proactive, which means taking preventive measures to reduce the attack surface or the vulnerability of the system or network, such as patching, hardening, or encrypting.
