### Access Control Concepts for the notes of the Unit 3 - Secure architecture principles isolation and leas in the subject of COMPUTER SYSTEM SECURITY

Access control is a critical component of computer system security, as it involves the process of controlling access to resources and data based on the privileges and permissions of users and processes. Access control concepts are the underlying principles that are used to design and implement access control systems.

1. Authentication: Authentication is the process of verifying the identity of a user or process, and it is used to ensure that only authorized users and processes have access to resources and data.

2. Authorization: Authorization is the process of granting or denying access to resources and data based on the privileges and permissions of the user or process.

3. Access Control Lists (ACLs): ACLs are lists of permissions that are associated with resources and data, and they are used to control access to these resources and data.

4. Role-Based Access Control (RBAC): RBAC is a type of access control that is based on the roles and responsibilities of users and processes, and it is used to control access to resources and data based on the roles of the users and processes.

5. Discretionary Access Control (DAC): DAC is a type of access control that is based on the discretion of the owner or administrator of the resource or data, and it is used to control access to resources and data based on the decisions of the owner or administrator.

In this unit, we will study the access control concepts, including authentication, authorization, ACLs, RBAC, and DAC. We will examine the strategies for implementing access control systems, and explore the use of access control in various applications, such as secure architecture design, isolation, and leasing. This will provide a foundation for understanding the principles and practices of computer system security, and for exploring the various applications of security in the workplace.
