### certificates for the notes of the Unit 4 - Basic cryptography in the subject of COMPUTER SYSTEM SECURITY

Unit 4 of the Computer System Security course focuses on Basic Cryptography, including the aspect of certificates.

A certificate is a digital document that is used to verify the identity of a person, organization, or device. Certificates are issued by trusted third-party organizations, known as certificate authorities (CA), and are used to establish trust between parties.

In the context of cryptography, certificates are used to establish trust in secure communications, such as secure web browsing and secure email. Certificates are used to verify the identity of the parties involved in a communication, and to encrypt the communication to protect its confidentiality.

In the context of the Computer System Security course, students in Unit 4 may learn about certificates as part of the discussion on Basic Cryptography. This may include working with examples of certificates, analyzing the role of certificates in establishing trust in secure communications, and learning about the different types of certificates and certificate authorities.

In summary, Unit 4 of the Computer System Security course focuses on Basic Cryptography, and may include a discussion of certificates. Certificates are digital documents that are used to verify the identity of a person, organization, or device, and are issued by trusted third-party organizations known as certificate authorities (CA). Students will learn about certificates through interactive activities and hands-on experiences, and will learn about the role of certificates in establishing trust in secure communications.
