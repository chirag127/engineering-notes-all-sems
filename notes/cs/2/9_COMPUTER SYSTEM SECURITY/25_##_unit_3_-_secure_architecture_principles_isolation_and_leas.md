## Unit 3 - Secure architecture principles isolation and leas

Unit 3 of the subject of Computer System Security focuses on secure architecture principles, including isolation and least privilege. These principles are critical for ensuring the security and privacy of computer systems and networks, as they help to minimize the risk of security breaches and to ensure the confidentiality, integrity, and availability of information.

1. Isolation: Isolation is a secure architecture principle that involves separating components of a system or network to minimize the risk of security breaches. This may involve physically separating components, such as servers and storage devices, or logically separating components, such as applications or data.

2. Least Privilege: Least privilege is a secure architecture principle that involves limiting the access and permissions of users and processes to only those that are necessary for their intended task. This helps to minimize the risk of security breaches and to ensure the confidentiality, integrity, and availability of information.

In this unit, we will study the secure architecture principles of isolation and least privilege, and their application in computer systems security. We will examine the strategies for implementing these principles, and explore the use of various security technologies, such as access control, firewalls, and encryption, to enforce these principles. This will provide a foundation for understanding the importance of secure architecture principles in computer systems security, and for exploring the various applications of computer systems security in the workplace.
