### Digital signature for the notes of the Unit 4 - Basic cryptography in the subject of COMPUTER SYSTEM SECURITY

A digital signature is a mathematical technique used to validate the authenticity and integrity of a digital message or document. It is used to ensure that the message or document has not been altered or tampered with during transmission, and to verify the identity of the sender.

A digital signature is created using a combination of public key cryptography and hash functions. The sender of the message or document uses their private key to generate a digital signature, which is then appended to the message or document. The recipient of the message or document uses the sender's public key to verify the digital signature and to confirm the authenticity and integrity of the message or document.

In this unit, we will study the basics of cryptography, including the digital signature. We will examine the strategies for implementing digital signatures, and explore the use of digital signatures in various applications, such as secure email, secure file transfer, and secure online transactions. This will provide a foundation for understanding the principles and practices of computer system security, and for exploring the various security measures used to protect computer systems.
