### Rootkits for the notes of the Unit 2 - Confidentiality Policies in the subject of COMPUTER SYSTEM SECURITY

Unit 2 of the Computer System Security course focuses on Confidentiality Policies, including the aspect of rootkits.

Rootkits are a type of malicious software that are designed to hide the presence of other malware on a computer system. Rootkits can be used to gain unauthorized access to sensitive information, steal data, or control a computer system.

Rootkits are particularly dangerous because they are difficult to detect and remove. They can hide themselves from traditional security software, such as antivirus programs, and can persist even after a system is rebooted.

In the context of the Computer System Security course, students in Unit 2 may learn about rootkits as part of the discussion on confidentiality policies. This may include working with examples of rootkits, analyzing the impact of rootkits on computer system security, and learning strategies for detecting and removing rootkits.
