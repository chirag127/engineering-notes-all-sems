### Real world protocols for the notes of the Unit 4 - Basic cryptography in the subject of COMPUTER SYSTEM SECURITY

Real-world protocols refer to the standard methods and procedures used to securely transmit data or information over networks or the internet. In the Unit 4 - Basic cryptography in the subject of COMPUTER SYSTEM SECURITY, students learn about the basic concepts of cryptography and the various types of encryption algorithms used to secure data. The notes for this unit should include the following topics:

1. Definition of real-world protocols (standard methods and procedures used to securely transmit data or information over networks or the internet)
2. Overview of real-world protocols (types, purposes, audiences)
3. Characteristics of effective real-world protocols (confidentiality, integrity, availability)
4. Types of encryption algorithms used in real-world protocols (symmetric, asymmetric, hash)
5. Techniques for implementing real-world protocols (digital signatures, certificates, key exchange)
6. The role of cryptography in real-world protocols (data protection, authentication, non-repudiation)
7. Techniques for improving real-world protocols (risk assessment, security policies, incident response)
8. Applications of real-world protocols in different fields (e-commerce, finance, healthcare)
9. Limitations of real-world protocols (complexity of systems, performance overhead)
10. Techniques for overcoming limitations (optimization, awareness, education)
11. Further reading and resources (books, journals, online resources)

Real-world protocols are essential for securely transmitting data or information over networks or the internet. Understanding the basic concepts of cryptography and the various types of encryption algorithms used to secure data helps students to develop the skills required to implement secure real-world protocols effectively and efficiently in a variety of contexts.
