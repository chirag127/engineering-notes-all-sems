Unit 1 of the Technical Communication course covers the Fundamentals of Technical Communication, including the types of technical communication.

Technical communication can take many forms, including reports, manuals, instructions, proposals, and presentations. Each type of technical communication has its own specific purpose and audience.

Reports are used to present research findings, results of experiments, or data analysis. They are typically written for a technical audience and are used to communicate complex information in a clear and concise manner.

Manuals are written to provide instructions for operating or using a product or system. They are typically written for a non-technical audience and are used to help users understand how to use a product or system effectively.

Instructions are written to provide step-by-step guidance for performing a specific task. They are typically written for a non-technical audience and are used to help users understand how to perform specific tasks.

Proposals are written to request funding or support for a project or initiative. They are typically written for a non-technical audience and are used to convince stakeholders to support a project or initiative.

Presentations are used to communicate information to an audience through visual aids, such as slides and graphics. They are typically used to present research findings, results of experiments, or data analysis.
