### Consonant Sounds for the Notes of Unit 5 - Dimensions of Oral Communication & Voice Dynamics in the Subject of Technical Communication

The following are some of the key consonant sounds to be aware of and practice for effective oral communication:

- **Plosive Sounds:** These sounds are made by blocking and releasing the airflow from the mouth. Examples include the sounds of /p/, /b/, /t/, /d/, /k/, and /g/. It is important to make sure these sounds are pronounced clearly and with proper articulation.

- **Fricative Sounds:** These sounds are made by forcing air through a narrow opening in the mouth. Examples include the sounds of /f/, /v/, /s/, /z/, /sh/, and /th/. Proper pronunciation and articulation of these sounds is crucial for effective oral communication.

- **Nasal Sounds:** These sounds are produced when air is released through the nose. Examples include the sounds of /m/, /n/, and /ng/. It is important to ensure that these sounds are pronounced clearly and with proper nasal resonance.

- **Affricate Sounds:** These sounds are a combination of plosive and fricative sounds. Examples include the sounds of /ch/ and /j/. Proper articulation of these sounds is important for effective oral communication.

- **Lateral Sounds:** These sounds are produced by allowing air to flow over the sides of the tongue. Examples include the sounds of /l/. Proper pronunciation and articulation of these sounds is important for clear oral communication.

- **Approximant Sounds:** These sounds are produced by bringing the articulators close together without creating turbulence. Examples include the sounds of /w/, /r/, and /y/. It is important to ensure that these sounds are pronounced clearly and with proper articulation.

By mastering these consonant sounds, you can improve your oral communication skills and effectively convey your message to your audience. Practice these sounds regularly to ensure clear and effective oral communication.
