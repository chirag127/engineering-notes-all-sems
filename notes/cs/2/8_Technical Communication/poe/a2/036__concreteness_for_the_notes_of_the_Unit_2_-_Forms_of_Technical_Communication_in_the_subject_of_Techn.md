### Concreteness

- Be specific and precise. Avoid vagueness.
- Use concrete and familiar examples to explain concepts. Abstract ideas can be difficult to understand without concrete references.
- Include specific details and quantities. Mention numbers, quantities, and metrics where applicable.
- Use specific language. Avoid ambiguous or abstract words. Choose precise and descriptive words.
- Include references to time. Mention when events occur or actions take place. Adding time references makes the content more concrete and relatable.
- Explain the significance and implications of concepts. What conclusions can be drawn? How does it impact the reader? Explaining the 'so what' helps readers understand the relevance.
- Use an active voice as much as possible. The active voice is more straightforward and concrete than the passive voice.

The key to effective technical communication is being concrete and specific rather than abstract or vague. Supplementing concepts and ideas with concrete examples, numbers, and references helps readers understand the content more deeply and relate it to their own contexts. Being precise and descriptive in language choices also contributes to a concrete style of writing.
