### Pronunciation for the notes of the Unit 5 - Dimensions of Oral Communication & Voice Dynamics in the subject of Technical Communication.

1. Articulation: Clear and distinct utterance of speech sounds. It refers to the accurate production of individual speech sounds.
2. Pronunciation: The way in which a word or language is spoken. It refers to the correct or accepted sound and pronunciation of words.
3. Accent: A distinctive mode of pronunciation of a language, especially one associated with a particular nation, locality, or social class.
4. Dialect: A variety of a language that is spoken in a particular region or community. Dialects differ from the standard language in terms of pronunciation, vocabulary, and grammar.
5. Rate: The speed or tempo of speaking. A medium speaking rate is most preferred for effective communication. A very fast or very slow rate can interfere with comprehension.
6. Pitch: The highness or lowness of a tone. Varied pitch maintains the listener's interest but extreme variations can interfere with comprehension.
7. Volume: The loudness or softness of a speaker's voice. An appropriate volume level is necessary for effective communication as well as listener comfort.
8. Pause: A temporary stop in speaking. Strategic use of pauses allows listeners to absorb the information and provides time for the speaker to organize thoughts. Excessive pausing distracts the listener and disrupts the flow of communication.
