### Consonant sounds for the notes of the Unit 5 - Dimensions of Oral Communication & Voice Dynamics in the subject of Technical Communication

1. Plosive sounds: These are consonant sounds produced by temporarily blocking air flow and then releasing it. Examples: /p/, /b/, /t/, /d/, /k/, /g/.

2. Nasal sounds: These are consonant sounds produced by allowing air to flow through the nose. Examples: /m/, /n/, /ng/.

3. Fricative sounds: These are consonant sounds produced by narrowing the vocal tract sufficiently to cause audible friction. Examples: /f/, /v/, /s/, /z/, /sh/, /zh/.

4. Affricate sounds: These are consonant sounds that begin as stops but release into fricatives. Examples: /ch/, /j/.

5. Lateral sounds: These are consonant sounds produced by allowing air to flow along the sides of the tongue. The only lateral sound in English is /l/.

6. Glide sounds: These are consonant sounds produced by moving articulators to neighbouring vowels. The glides in English are /w/ and /y/.
