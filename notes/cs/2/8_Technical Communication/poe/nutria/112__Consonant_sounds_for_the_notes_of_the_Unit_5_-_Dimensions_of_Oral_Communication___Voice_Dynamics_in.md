
### Consonant Sounds for the Notes of the Unit 5 - Dimensions of Oral Communication & Voice Dynamics in the Subject of Technical Communication

1. Consonants are speech sounds that are produced by blocking the air flow through the mouth and then releasing it. 
2. Consonants can be classified into two categories: voiced and unvoiced consonants. 
3. Voiced consonants are produced by vibrating the vocal cords, while unvoiced consonants are produced without vocal cord vibration. 
4. Consonants can also be further classified according to their place of articulation, manner of articulation and voicing. 
5. The place of articulation refers to the area of the mouth where the sound is produced. 
6. The manner of articulation refers to the manner in which the air is blocked and released. 
7. The voicing refers to whether the vocal cords are vibrating or not. 
8. Examples of consonants include /p/, /t/, /k/, /b/, /d/, /g/, /f/, /v/, /s/, /z/, /h/, /m/, /n/, /l/, /r/, /w/, /j/.
9. Consonants are important in oral communication as they help to convey meaning and emotion. 
10. The use of consonants can also help to create a sense of rhythm and flow in speech.
