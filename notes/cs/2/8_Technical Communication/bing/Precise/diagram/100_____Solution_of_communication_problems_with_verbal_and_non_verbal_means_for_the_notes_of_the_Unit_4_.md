### Solution of communication problems with verbal and non verbal means

Unit 4 - Technical Communication Skills in the subject of Technical Communication

1. **Verbal communication** refers to the use of spoken or written language to convey a message. It includes face-to-face conversations, telephone calls, emails, and text messages.

2. **Non-verbal communication** refers to the use of body language, gestures, facial expressions, and other visual cues to convey a message. It includes eye contact, posture, and tone of voice.

3. To solve communication problems, it is important to identify the root cause of the issue. This can be done by analyzing the situation and asking questions to clarify any misunderstandings.

4. Once the root cause has been identified, appropriate verbal and non-verbal communication techniques can be used to address the issue. For example, if the issue is a lack of understanding, the speaker can use simpler language and provide examples to clarify their message.

5. Active listening is also an important technique for solving communication problems. This involves paying attention to what the other person is saying, asking questions to clarify any misunderstandings, and providing feedback to show that you understand their message.

6. In some cases, non-verbal communication can be more effective than verbal communication. For example, if someone is upset, a comforting gesture or a reassuring touch can be more effective than words.

7. It is important to be aware of cultural differences in communication, as what is considered appropriate in one culture may not be in another. For example, in some cultures, direct eye contact is considered rude, while in others it is a sign of respect.

8. In summary, effective communication involves a combination of verbal and non-verbal techniques. By identifying the root cause of communication problems and using appropriate techniques to address them, it is possible to improve communication and avoid misunderstandings.
