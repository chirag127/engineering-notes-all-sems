# Consonant Sounds

Consonant sounds are produced when the airflow is obstructed in some way by the articulators (lips, teeth, tongue, etc.) in the vocal tract. There are several ways in which the airflow can be obstructed, resulting in different types of consonant sounds.

Here are some key points to remember about consonant sounds:

1. Consonant sounds are categorized based on three main features: voicing, place of articulation, and manner of articulation.
2. Voicing refers to whether the vocal cords vibrate or not during the production of the sound. Consonant sounds can be either voiced or voiceless.
3. Place of articulation refers to the location in the mouth where the airflow is obstructed. Some common places of articulation include bilabial (lips), alveolar (tongue against the alveolar ridge), and velar (tongue against the soft palate).
4. Manner of articulation refers to the way in which the airflow is obstructed. Some common manners of articulation include stops (complete obstruction of the airflow), fricatives (partial obstruction of the airflow), and nasals (airflow is redirected through the nose).
5. Consonant sounds can also be classified as plosives, affricates, or approximants, depending on the manner of articulation.
6. In English, there are 24 consonant sounds.
