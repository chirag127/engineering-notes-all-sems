# Syllables for the notes of the Unit 5 - Dimensions of Oral Communication & Voice Dynamics in the subject of Technical Communication

- A syllable is a unit of sound that forms a word or part of a word.
- Syllables are important for pronunciation, stress, and intonation in oral communication.
- Each syllable contains a vowel sound and may also contain one or more consonant sounds.
- Syllables can be divided into two parts: the onset and the rhyme.
- The onset is the initial consonant or consonant cluster of the syllable.
- The rhyme is the vowel and any following consonants.
- Syllables can be classified as open or closed.
- Open syllables end in a vowel sound, while closed syllables end in a consonant sound.
- The number of syllables in a word can affect its stress pattern and pronunciation.
- In English, syllable stress is important for distinguishing between words and for conveying meaning.
- Syllable stress can also affect the rhythm and intonation of speech.
- Practicing syllable division and stress can improve oral communication skills.
