### Humour

Humour is the quality of being amusing or funny, or the ability to make others laugh. Humour can be used in various types of communication, including technical presentations, to achieve different purposes, such as:

- Capturing and maintaining the audience's attention
- Creating a rapport and connection with the audience
- Making the presentation more memorable and engaging
- Relieving tension and boredom
- Enhancing the credibility and likability of the speaker
- Emphasizing key points or messages
- Illustrating concepts or examples
- Challenging assumptions or stereotypes
- Encouraging participation or feedback

However, humour can also have some drawbacks or risks, such as:

- Offending or alienating some audience members
- Distracting or confusing the audience from the main topic
- Undermining the seriousness or professionalism of the speaker or the presentation
- Backfiring or failing to elicit the desired response
- Clashing with the tone or style of the presentation

Therefore, humour should be used carefully and strategically in technical presentations, following some basic principles and techniques, such as:

- Know your audience and their expectations, preferences, and sensitivities
- Know your topic and its relevance, importance, and complexity
- Know your purpose and your desired outcome or effect
- Know your style and your strengths, weaknesses, and comfort level
- Choose appropriate types and sources of humour that suit your audience, topic, purpose, and style
- Prepare and practice your humour in advance, but also be flexible and adaptable to the situation
- Use humour sparingly and selectively, and balance it with other elements of your presentation
- Use humour ethically and respectfully, and avoid jokes that are offensive, inappropriate, or harmful
- Use humour creatively and originally, and avoid jokes that are clichéd, outdated, or plagiarized
- Use humour effectively and confidently, and deliver it with timing, tone, and body language

Some examples of humour techniques that can be used in technical presentations are:

- Verbal humour: using wordplay, puns, metaphors, similes, irony, sarcasm, exaggeration, understatement, etc.
- Multimedia humour: using images, videos, cartoons, memes, gifs, etc.
- Self-deprecating humour: making fun of yourself or your mistakes
- Anecdotal humour: telling a personal or relevant story that is funny or surprising
- Quotational humour: quoting a famous or humorous person or source
- Interactive humour: involving the audience in a joke or a game
