### Emotion

- Emotion is a natural instinctive state of mind deriving from one's circumstances, mood, or relationships with others.
- Emotions are mental states brought on by neurophysiological changes, variously associated with thoughts, feelings, behavioral responses, and a degree of pleasure or displeasure.
- Emotions are responses to significant internal and external events.
- Emotions can be classified into basic emotions (such as anger, fear, joy, sadness, disgust, and surprise) and complex emotions (such as guilt, shame, pride, envy, jealousy, and love).
- Emotions can be influenced by various factors, such as personality, culture, social context, motivation, cognition, and appraisal.
- Emotions can have various effects on the individual and others, such as enhancing or impairing memory, attention, decision making, communication, and interpersonal relationships.
- Emotions can be expressed verbally (such as through words, tone, and pitch) and nonverbally (such as through facial expressions, gestures, and posture).
- Emotions can be regulated by various strategies, such as reappraisal, distraction, suppression, acceptance, and mindfulness.
- Emotions can be measured by various methods, such as self-report, behavioral observation, physiological recording, and brain imaging.
- Emotions are important for human well-being, as they provide information, motivation, and feedback for adaptive functioning.
