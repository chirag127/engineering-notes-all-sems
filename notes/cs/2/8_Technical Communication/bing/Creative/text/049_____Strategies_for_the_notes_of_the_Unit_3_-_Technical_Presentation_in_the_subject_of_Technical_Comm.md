### Strategies for the notes of the Unit 3 - Technical Presentation in the subject of Technical Communication

- Notes are brief records of the main points, ideas, or facts of a presentation.
- Notes can help the presenter to recall the structure, content, and delivery of the presentation.
- Notes can also help the audience to follow the presentation and review the key points later.
- There are different types of notes, such as outlines, cue cards, mind maps, or slides.
- The choice of notes depends on the purpose, context, and style of the presentation, as well as the preference and comfort of the presenter.
- Some general strategies for preparing and using notes are:

  - Use keywords, phrases, or symbols instead of full sentences or paragraphs.
  - Use bullet points, numbers, or indentation to organize the notes logically and hierarchically.
  - Use colors, fonts, or highlighting to emphasize the main points, transitions, or cues.
  - Use diagrams, charts, graphs, or images to illustrate the data, concepts, or processes.
  - Use consistent and clear formatting and layout for the notes.
  - Use abbreviations, acronyms, or shorthand to save space and time.
  - Use references, citations, or sources to support the claims and arguments.
  - Use examples, anecdotes, or stories to illustrate the points and engage the audience.
  - Use questions, quotations, or statistics to capture the attention and interest of the audience.
  - Use transitions, summaries, or signposts to guide the audience through the presentation.
  - Use feedback, questions, or activities to involve the audience and check their understanding.
  - Use notes sparingly and discreetly, without relying on them too much or reading from them verbatim.
  - Use notes as a backup, not a substitute, for the presentation.
  - Use notes to enhance, not distract, from the presentation.
