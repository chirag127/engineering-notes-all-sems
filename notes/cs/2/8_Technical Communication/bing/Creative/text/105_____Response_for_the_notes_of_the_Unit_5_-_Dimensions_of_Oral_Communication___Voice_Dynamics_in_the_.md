### Response for the notes of the Unit 5 - Dimensions of Oral Communication & Voice Dynamics in the subject of Technical Communication

- Oral communication is the process of expressing information or ideas by word of mouth.
- Oral communication can be formal or informal, depending on the context and purpose of the message.
- Oral communication has several dimensions, such as clarity, coherence, conciseness, courtesy, confidence, and feedback.
- Clarity refers to the use of simple, precise, and unambiguous language that avoids jargon, slang, and technical terms.
- Coherence refers to the logical and consistent organization of the message, with a clear introduction, body, and conclusion.
- Conciseness refers to the use of the minimum words necessary to convey the message, without repetition, redundancy, or irrelevant details.
- Courtesy refers to the use of polite, respectful, and considerate language that shows regard for the listener's feelings, opinions, and needs.
- Confidence refers to the use of a firm, assertive, and positive tone that shows conviction, credibility, and authority.
- Feedback refers to the verbal and non-verbal signals that indicate the listener's understanding, interest, agreement, or disagreement with the message.
- Voice dynamics are the variations in the pitch, volume, rate, and quality of the voice that affect the delivery and impact of the message.
- Pitch refers to the highness or lowness of the voice, which can express emotions, attitudes, and emphasis.
- Volume refers to the loudness or softness of the voice, which can indicate the level of confidence, enthusiasm, and importance of the message.
- Rate refers to the speed or slowness of the voice, which can affect the clarity, comprehension, and interest of the message.
- Quality refers to the tone, timbre, and resonance of the voice, which can reflect the personality, mood, and health of the speaker.
