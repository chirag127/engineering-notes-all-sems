### Unit 1 - Fundamentals of Technical Communication

- Technical communication is the process of conveying information and instructions to a specific audience for a specific purpose.
- Technical communication has four main characteristics: accuracy, clarity, conciseness, and appropriateness.
- Technical communication can be classified into two types: oral and written.
- Oral communication involves speaking and listening, such as presentations, meetings, interviews, and phone calls.
- Written communication involves reading and writing, such as reports, manuals, proposals, emails, and memos.
- Technical communication can also be categorized into three types: informative, instructional, and persuasive.
- Informative communication provides facts, data, and explanations to the audience, such as definitions, descriptions, and summaries.
- Instructional communication provides directions, steps, and procedures to the audience, such as instructions, manuals, and tutorials.
- Persuasive communication provides arguments, opinions, and recommendations to the audience, such as proposals, reviews, and advertisements.
- Technical communication requires careful planning, drafting, revising, and editing to ensure quality and effectiveness.
- Technical communication follows a general process of analysis, design, development, implementation, and evaluation.
- Analysis involves identifying the purpose, audience, context, and constraints of the communication.
- Design involves organizing, structuring, and formatting the content and layout of the communication.
- Development involves creating, writing, and editing the content and visuals of the communication.
- Implementation involves delivering, distributing, and publishing the communication to the intended audience and medium.
- Evaluation involves assessing, testing, and improving the communication based on feedback and results.
