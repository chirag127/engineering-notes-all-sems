### Class room presentation for the notes of the Unit 3 - Technical Presentation in the subject of Technical Communication

- A class room presentation is a form of oral communication that involves delivering information, ideas, or arguments to an audience of students and teachers.
- A class room presentation can have various purposes, such as informing, persuading, instructing, demonstrating, or entertaining.
- A class room presentation can be based on a written report, a research project, a case study, a problem-solving task, or a creative work.
- A class room presentation requires careful planning, preparation, and practice to achieve the desired outcomes and to overcome the challenges of speaking in public.
- A class room presentation consists of three main parts: introduction, body, and conclusion.
- The introduction should capture the attention of the audience, establish the topic and purpose of the presentation, and provide an overview of the main points.
- The body should develop the main points with supporting evidence, examples, facts, statistics, or quotations. The body should also use appropriate transitions, signposts, and summaries to guide the audience through the presentation.
- The conclusion should summarize the main points, restate the purpose of the presentation, and provide a memorable closing remark or a call to action.
- A class room presentation should also use effective visual aids, such as slides, charts, graphs, images, or videos, to enhance the verbal message and to appeal to the audience's senses.
- A class room presentation should also follow the principles of clear, concise, coherent, and courteous communication, and use appropriate language, tone, voice, and nonverbal cues to suit the audience, purpose, and context of the presentation.
- A class room presentation should also be evaluated by the presenter, the audience, and the teacher, based on the criteria of content, organization, delivery, and visual aids. The feedback should be used to improve the presentation skills and to identify the strengths and weaknesses of the presentation.
