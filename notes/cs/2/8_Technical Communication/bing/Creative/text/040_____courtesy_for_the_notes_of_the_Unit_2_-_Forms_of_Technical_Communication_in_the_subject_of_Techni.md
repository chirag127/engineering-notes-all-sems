### Courtesy

- Courtesy is one of the six C's of effective communication, which are clarity, conciseness, completeness, correctness, courtesy, and coherence.
- Courtesy means showing respect, consideration, and goodwill to the receiver of the message.
- Courtesy involves using positive words, tactful language, appropriate modes of addressing the audience, and avoiding potentially offensive terminology, usage, and tone .
- Courtesy helps to establish rapport, trust, and credibility with the audience, and to avoid misunderstandings, conflicts, and negative emotions.
- Courtesy also entails designing a reader-friendly, easy-to-read document, with clear headings, subheadings, lists, tables, graphs, and other visual aids.
- Courtesy requires adapting the message to the needs, expectations, and preferences of the audience, and using the you-attitude, which focuses on the benefits and interests of the reader, rather than the writer .
- Some examples of courteous communication are:

  - Using polite expressions, such as please, thank you, sorry, etc.
  - Using inclusive and non-discriminatory language, such as gender-neutral pronouns, terms, and titles.
  - Using positive and constructive feedback, rather than negative and harsh criticism.
  - Using active voice, rather than passive voice, to show responsibility and accountability.
  - Using direct and sincere language, rather than vague and insincere language, to show honesty and integrity.
  - Using appropriate tone, such as formal, informal, casual, etc., depending on the context, purpose, and audience of the message.
