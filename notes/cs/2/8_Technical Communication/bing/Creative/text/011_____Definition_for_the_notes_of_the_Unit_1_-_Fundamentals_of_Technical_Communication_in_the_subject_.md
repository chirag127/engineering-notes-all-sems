### Definition for the notes of the Unit 1 - Fundamentals of Technical Communication in the subject of Technical Communication

- Technical communication is the process of conveying information and instructions to a specific audience for a specific purpose, using clear, concise, and accurate language and visuals.
- Technical communicators are professionals who create, design, and deliver technical documents, such as manuals, reports, proposals, websites, presentations, and more, for various fields and industries.
- Technical communication has four main characteristics: 
  - It is audience-centered, meaning it focuses on the needs, expectations, and preferences of the intended readers or users.
  - It is purpose-driven, meaning it has a clear and specific goal or objective that guides the content and structure of the document.
  - It is ethical, meaning it follows the principles of honesty, integrity, respect, and responsibility in dealing with information and people.
  - It is dynamic, meaning it adapts to changing situations, technologies, and feedback.
- Technical communication follows a process that involves five steps: 
  - Planning, which involves analyzing the situation, defining the purpose and scope, identifying the audience, and gathering information.
  - Drafting, which involves organizing, writing, and designing the document, using appropriate style, tone, format, and visuals.
  - Revising, which involves reviewing, editing, and proofreading the document, checking for clarity, accuracy, completeness, and consistency.
  - Testing, which involves evaluating the document, using various methods and criteria, such as usability testing, peer review, expert review, and quality standards.
  - Delivering, which involves distributing the document, using the appropriate medium and channel, such as print, online, or oral, and ensuring the accessibility and security of the document.
