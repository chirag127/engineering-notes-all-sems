### Correctness for the notes of the Unit 2 - Forms of Technical Communication

- Correctness is one of the six qualities of effective communication, along with clarity, conciseness, completeness, coherence, and courtesy.
- Correctness means that the details of a message are accurate, the grammar and punctuation are correct, the language is appropriate, and the format and appearance are professional.
- Correctness is important for technical communication because it affects the credibility, reliability, and usability of the information.
- To achieve correctness in technical communication, some of the aspects to consider are:
  - Passive voice: The passive voice is often used in technical writing to emphasize the action or result rather than the agent or performer. However, the passive voice can also make the sentences longer, more complex, and less direct. Therefore, it is advisable to use the passive voice sparingly and only when it is appropriate for the purpose and audience of the document.
  - Commands and directions: Technical writing often contains a lot of commands and directions, such as instructions, procedures, or recommendations. To make these clear and concise, it is important to use the imperative mood, which expresses a command or request directly. For example, "Press the button" instead of "You should press the button" or "The button should be pressed".
  - Bullets and lists: Technical writing often relies heavily on bulleted or numbered lists, which help to organize and highlight the main points, steps, or features of the information. To make these consistent and effective, it is important to use parallel structure, which means that each item in the list has the same grammatical form and level of detail. For example, "To make a cake, you need to: - Preheat the oven. - Mix the ingredients. - Pour the batter into a pan. - Bake for 25 minutes" instead of "To make a cake, you need to: - Preheat the oven. - Ingredients should be mixed. - Pouring the batter into a pan. - Baking time is 25 minutes".
  - Precision and accuracy: Technical writing requires precision and accuracy in the use of words, numbers, symbols, and units of measurement. To avoid ambiguity, confusion, or misinterpretation, it is important to use specific and concrete words, avoid jargon and slang, define technical terms, use consistent terminology, and follow the conventions and standards of the field or discipline.
