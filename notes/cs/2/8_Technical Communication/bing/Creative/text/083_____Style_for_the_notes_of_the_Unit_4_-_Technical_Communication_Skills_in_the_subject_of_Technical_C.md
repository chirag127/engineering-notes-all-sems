### Style for the notes of the Unit 4 - Technical Communication Skills in the subject of Technical Communication

- Style is the way a writer expresses ideas in a document. It includes the choice of words, sentences, and tone that convey the writer's attitude and personality.
- Style is important for technical communication because it affects the clarity, accuracy, and effectiveness of the message. A good style can help the reader understand and follow the information, while a poor style can confuse and frustrate the reader.
- Some general principles of style for technical communication are:

  - Use plain and precise language. Avoid jargon, slang, and vague terms. Choose words that are appropriate for the audience and the purpose of the document.
  - Use active voice and strong verbs. Active voice shows who or what performs the action, while passive voice hides or obscures the agent. Strong verbs express the action clearly and directly, while weak verbs rely on modifiers or auxiliary verbs. For example, "The technician installed the software" is better than "The software was installed by the technician" or "The software installation was done".
  - Use parallel structure. Parallel structure means using the same grammatical form for items in a list or series. It helps the reader see the relationship and comparison among the items. For example, "The report covers the following topics: design, testing, and evaluation" is better than "The report covers design, how it was tested, and an evaluation".
  - Use headings and lists. Headings and lists help the reader scan and organize the information in a document. Headings should be clear, concise, and consistent. Lists should be formatted with bullets or numbers, and each item should start with a capital letter and end with a punctuation mark.
  - Use transitions and signposts. Transitions and signposts are words or phrases that connect ideas and guide the reader through the document. Transitions show the relationship between sentences or paragraphs, such as contrast, addition, or cause and effect. Signposts indicate the structure and direction of the document, such as introduction, summary, or recommendation. For example, "However, there are some drawbacks to this method" is a transition, while "In conclusion, we recommend the following actions" is a signpost.
