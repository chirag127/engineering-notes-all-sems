# Courtesy

Courtesy is one of the key components of effective communication. It means showing respect, consideration and goodwill to the receiver of the message. Courtesy can enhance the quality and impact of the message, as well as the relationship between the sender and the receiver. 

Some of the ways to achieve courtesy in technical communication are:

- Use positive and polite words that reflect the you-attitude. For example, instead of saying "You failed to submit the report on time", say "I appreciate your efforts in completing the report".
- Avoid biased, offensive or insensitive language that may hurt the feelings or dignity of the receiver. For example, avoid using sexist, racist, ageist or derogatory terms, and use inclusive and respectful terms instead.
- Acknowledge the audience by making eye contact, smiling, nodding and using appropriate gestures. You can also engage the audience with relevant humor, stories and examples, but make sure they are appropriate and related to the topic.
- Design a reader-friendly and easy-to-read document that follows the principles of clarity, conciseness, coherence, completeness and correctness. Use headings, lists, tables, graphs and other visual aids to organize and present the information effectively.
- Use tactful and diplomatic language when delivering bad news, making requests, giving feedback or expressing disagreement. For example, instead of saying "Your proposal is unacceptable", say "Your proposal has some good points, but it needs some improvement in these areas".
- Express appreciation, gratitude, apology, sympathy or praise when appropriate. For example, thank the receiver for their time, attention, cooperation or contribution, apologize for any inconvenience or mistake, sympathize with their problem or situation, or praise their work or achievement.
