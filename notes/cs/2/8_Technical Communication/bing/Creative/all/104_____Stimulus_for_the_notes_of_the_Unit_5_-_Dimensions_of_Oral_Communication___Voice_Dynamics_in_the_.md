# Stimulus for the notes of the Unit 5 - Dimensions of Oral Communication & Voice Dynamics in the subject of Technical Communication

- A stimulus is an idea or a concept that is activated by the sender in the communication process .
- A stimulus can be internal or external, depending on the source of the idea or the concept.
- An internal stimulus is generated from the sender's own thoughts, feelings, memories, or imagination.
- An external stimulus is triggered by the sender's observation, experience, or interaction with the environment or other people.
- A stimulus can be verbal or non-verbal, depending on the mode of expression used by the sender.
- A verbal stimulus is conveyed through words, either spoken or written.
- A non-verbal stimulus is conveyed through gestures, facial expressions, body language, or other visual or auditory cues.
- A stimulus can be positive or negative, depending on the effect it has on the receiver or the relationship between the sender and the receiver.
- A positive stimulus is intended to inform, persuade, entertain, or motivate the receiver.
- A negative stimulus is intended to criticize, insult, threaten, or manipulate the receiver.
- A stimulus can be simple or complex, depending on the level of detail, clarity, and organization of the idea or the concept.
- A simple stimulus is easy to understand, remember, and respond to.
- A complex stimulus is difficult to understand, remember, and respond to.
- A stimulus can be factual or opinionated, depending on the degree of objectivity, accuracy, and evidence of the idea or the concept.
- A factual stimulus is based on verifiable data, information, or knowledge.
- An opinionated stimulus is based on personal beliefs, values, or preferences.
- A stimulus can be direct or indirect, depending on the degree of explicitness, honesty, and politeness of the idea or the concept.
- A direct stimulus is clear, straightforward, and unambiguous.
- An indirect stimulus is vague, subtle, or ambiguous.

- The dimensions of oral communication are the aspects or elements that affect the quality, effectiveness, and appropriateness of oral communication.
- The dimensions of oral communication include:

  - The sender: the person who initiates, encodes, and transmits the message.
  - The receiver: the person who receives, decodes, and interprets the message.
  - The message: the content, meaning, and purpose of the communication.
  - The channel: the medium or mode of communication, such as speech, writing, or sign language.
  - The feedback: the response or reaction of the receiver to the message, which can be verbal or non-verbal.
  - The context: the situation, setting, or environment in which the communication takes place, which can affect the tone, style, and norms of communication.
  - The noise: the interference or distraction that hinders the communication process, which can be physical, psychological, or semantic.

- Voice dynamics are the variations or changes in the vocal characteristics of the speaker, such as pitch, volume, rate, tone, and quality.
- Voice dynamics can enhance or impair the oral communication, depending on how they are used by the speaker.
- Voice dynamics can be used to:

  - Emphasize or highlight important points or keywords in the message.
  - Express or convey emotions, attitudes, or moods of the speaker.
  - Engage or attract the attention and interest of the receiver.
  - Persuade or influence the receiver to accept or agree with the message.
  - Clarify or explain the message or avoid misunderstandings.
  - Adapt or adjust to the context or the receiver's needs and expectations.

- Voice dynamics can be influenced by:

  - The stimulus: the idea or the concept that motivates the speaker to communicate.
  - The sender: the speaker's personality, mood, confidence, and motivation.
  - The receiver: the receiver's feedback, reaction, and relationship with the speaker.
  - The message: the content, meaning, and purpose of the communication.
  - The channel: the medium or mode of communication, such as speech, writing, or sign language.
  - The context: the situation, setting, or environment in which the communication takes place, which can affect the tone, style, and norms of communication.
  - The noise: the interference or distraction that hinders the communication process, which can be physical, psychological, or semantic.
