## Dimensions of Oral Communication

Oral communication is the process of expressing information or ideas by word of mouth. It involves the use of spoken words, tone of voice, body language, and other nonverbal cues to convey a message. Oral communication can be classified into two dimensions: verbal and nonverbal.

### Verbal Dimension

The verbal dimension of oral communication refers to the choice and use of words to convey a message. It involves the following aspects:

- Vocabulary: the selection of appropriate and precise words to express the intended meaning
- Grammar: the application of the rules of language to construct clear and correct sentences
- Pronunciation: the articulation of the sounds of words in a standard and intelligible manner
- Fluency: the smoothness and continuity of speech without unnecessary pauses, hesitations, or repetitions
- Coherence: the logical and consistent organization of ideas and information in a speech
- Relevance: the suitability and pertinence of the message to the purpose, audience, and context of the communication

### Nonverbal Dimension

The nonverbal dimension of oral communication refers to the use of gestures, facial expressions, eye contact, posture, voice quality, and other cues to enhance the message and create an impression on the listeners. It involves the following aspects:

- Kinesics: the study of body movements, such as gestures, facial expressions, and eye contact, that convey emotions, attitudes, and intentions
- Proxemics: the study of the use of space and distance in communication, such as personal space, territoriality, and seating arrangements
- Haptics: the study of the use of touch in communication, such as handshakes, hugs, and pats, that convey intimacy, affection, and support
- Paralanguage: the study of the vocal features of speech, such as pitch, volume, rate, tone, and pauses, that convey meaning and emotion
- Chronemics: the study of the use of time in communication, such as punctuality, duration, and timing, that convey respect, interest, and urgency
- Oculesics: the study of the use of eye behavior in communication, such as eye contact, gaze, and blinking, that convey attention, interest, and emotion
- Objectics: the study of the use of objects and artifacts in communication, such as clothing, accessories, and personal belongings, that convey identity, status, and personality

## Voice Dynamics

Voice dynamics are the variations and modifications of the voice quality, pitch, volume, rate, and tone of speech to create emphasis, contrast, and variety in oral communication. Voice dynamics can enhance the effectiveness and attractiveness of a speech by:

- Capturing and maintaining the attention and interest of the listeners
- Expressing and conveying emotions, moods, and attitudes
- Highlighting and stressing important points and ideas
- Modifying and adjusting the message according to the feedback and reactions of the listeners
- Creating and establishing rapport and connection with the listeners

Voice dynamics can be improved by:

- Practicing and exercising the vocal cords and muscles
- Breathing properly and deeply
- Drinking enough water and avoiding substances that can dry or irritate the throat
- Speaking clearly and articulately
- Varying and adjusting the voice quality, pitch, volume, rate, and tone according to the purpose, audience, and context of the speech
- Listening and observing the feedback and reactions of the listeners
- Seeking and applying constructive criticism and feedback from others
