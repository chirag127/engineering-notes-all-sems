# Professional Personality Attributes

Professional personality attributes are the habits, attitudes and physical traits of a person that influence how they perform in their work environment. They are not the same for everyone, but they can be developed and improved with practice and feedback. Some of the professional personality attributes that are important for oral communication and voice dynamics are:

- **Openness to experience**: This is the tendency to be curious, creative and willing to try new things. It helps to adapt to different situations, audiences and topics, and to generate original and innovative ideas. Openness to experience also enhances the quality and variety of one's voice, as it allows for more expression and emotion.
- **Conscientiousness**: This is the tendency to be organized, responsible and diligent. It helps to plan and prepare for oral communication, to follow the rules and norms of the context, and to deliver the message in a clear and accurate way. Conscientiousness also reflects in one's voice, as it shows confidence and professionalism.
- **Extroversion**: This is the tendency to be outgoing, sociable and energetic. It helps to establish rapport and trust with the audience, to engage them in the conversation, and to use appropriate body language and gestures. Extroversion also influences one's voice, as it makes it louder, faster and more expressive.
- **Agreeableness**: This is the tendency to be cooperative, friendly and compassionate. It helps to show respect and empathy for the audience, to listen actively and respond appropriately, and to avoid or resolve conflicts. Agreeableness also affects one's voice, as it makes it softer, warmer and more polite.
- **Neuroticism**: This is the tendency to experience negative emotions, such as anxiety, anger and sadness. It can hinder oral communication, as it can cause nervousness, stress and fear of failure. Neuroticism also impacts one's voice, as it can make it shaky, tense and monotone.

These professional personality attributes can be measured by various tests and tools, such as the Big Five Personality Test, the Myers-Briggs Type Indicator, and the DISC Assessment. They can also be improved by self-awareness, feedback, training and practice. Developing these attributes can help to enhance one's oral communication and voice dynamics, and to achieve personal and professional goals.
