# Synopsis Writing for the Notes of the Unit 2 - Forms of Technical Communication in the Subject of Technical Communication

- A synopsis is a brief summary of the main points and ideas of a text, such as a book, an article, a report, or a speech.
- A synopsis can help the reader to understand the main purpose, scope, and structure of the text, as well as the key arguments, evidence, and conclusions.
- A synopsis can also help the writer to organize and revise their own text, as well as to check for coherence, consistency, and completeness.
- A synopsis should be concise, clear, and accurate, without omitting any essential information or distorting the original meaning of the text.
- A synopsis should follow the same order and logic as the text, but use shorter sentences and simpler words to convey the main points.
- A synopsis should not include any personal opinions, interpretations, or evaluations of the text, unless they are explicitly stated by the author.
- A synopsis should not include any unnecessary details, examples, quotations, or references that are not relevant to the main points of the text.
- A synopsis should not introduce any new information or ideas that are not present in the text.

## Steps for Writing a Synopsis

- Read the text carefully and identify the main purpose, scope, and structure of the text.
- Write down the main points and ideas of each section or paragraph of the text, using your own words and phrases.
- Arrange the main points and ideas in a logical order, following the same structure as the text.
- Combine the main points and ideas into a coherent and cohesive paragraph or paragraphs, using transitions and connectors to link them together.
- Check the synopsis for accuracy, clarity, and completeness, and revise it as needed.
- Compare the synopsis with the original text and make sure that it reflects the main purpose, scope, and structure of the text, as well as the key arguments, evidence, and conclusions.
- Format the synopsis according to the guidelines and conventions of the genre, discipline, and audience.
