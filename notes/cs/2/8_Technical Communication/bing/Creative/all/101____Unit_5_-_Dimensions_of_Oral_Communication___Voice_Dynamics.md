## Unit 5 - Dimensions of Oral Communication & Voice Dynamics

Oral communication is the process of expressing ideas, feelings, and information through spoken words. Oral communication can be classified into two dimensions: verbal and non-verbal.

- Verbal communication refers to the use of language, words, and sounds to convey meaning. Verbal communication can be further divided into vocal and non-vocal elements. Vocal elements include pitch, volume, rate, tone, and accent. Non-vocal elements include silence, pauses, and fillers.
- Non-verbal communication refers to the use of body language, gestures, facial expressions, eye contact, and physical appearance to convey meaning. Non-verbal communication can complement, contradict, or substitute verbal communication.

Voice dynamics are the variations in the quality and characteristics of one's voice that affect the delivery and impact of oral communication. Voice dynamics can be influenced by factors such as emotion, mood, intention, and audience. Voice dynamics can be improved by practicing the following techniques:

- Breathing: Breathing deeply and regularly can help relax the vocal cords and improve the clarity and projection of one's voice.
- Articulation: Articulation is the ability to produce clear and distinct sounds and words. Articulation can be improved by practicing tongue twisters, reading aloud, and enunciating each syllable.
- Pronunciation: Pronunciation is the correct and standard way of saying words according to the rules of a language. Pronunciation can be improved by listening to native speakers, using dictionaries, and learning the phonetic symbols of a language.
- Modulation: Modulation is the ability to vary one's voice according to the context, purpose, and audience of oral communication. Modulation can be achieved by changing the pitch, volume, rate, and tone of one's voice to create interest, emphasis, and emotion.
- Fluency: Fluency is the ability to speak smoothly and continuously without hesitation, repetition, or errors. Fluency can be improved by expanding one's vocabulary, reading widely, and practicing speaking on different topics.
