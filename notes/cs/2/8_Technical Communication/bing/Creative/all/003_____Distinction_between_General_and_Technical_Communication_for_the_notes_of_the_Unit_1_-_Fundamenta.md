# Distinction between General and Technical Communication

General communication and technical communication are two types of communication that differ in their purpose, style, audience, and content. Here are some of the main distinctions between them:

- **Purpose**: General communication is usually aimed at sharing information, expressing opinions, or building relationships. Technical communication is usually aimed at conveying specific instructions, data, or facts to help the audience perform a task, solve a problem, or make a decision.
- **Style**: General communication is often informal, subjective, and personal. Technical communication is mostly formal, objective, and impersonal.
- **Audience**: General communication is not always intended for a specific audience, and may use general or common language. Technical communication is usually intended for a specific audience, and may use technical terms, jargon, or graphics.
- **Content**: General communication may contain a general message that does not follow a set pattern or structure. Technical communication may contain a technical message that follows a set pattern or structure, such as introduction, body, and conclusion.
