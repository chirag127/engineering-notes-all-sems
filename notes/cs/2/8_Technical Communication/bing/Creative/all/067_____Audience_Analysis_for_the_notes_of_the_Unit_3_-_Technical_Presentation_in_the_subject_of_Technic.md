# Audience Analysis

- Audience analysis is the process of identifying and understanding the characteristics, needs, expectations, and preferences of the people who will receive your technical presentation.
- Audience analysis helps you to tailor your presentation to suit the specific audience and context, and to achieve your communication goals effectively.
- Audience analysis involves three main steps: identifying the audience, assessing the audience, and adapting to the audience.

## Identifying the audience

- To identify the audience, you need to answer the following questions:
  - Who are they? Consider their demographic characteristics, such as age, gender, education, profession, culture, etc.
  - What are their roles and responsibilities? Consider their positions, functions, authority, and influence in the organization or situation.
  - What are their prior knowledge and experience? Consider their familiarity with the topic, the level of technicality they can understand, and the background information they need.
  - What are their attitudes and interests? Consider their opinions, values, beliefs, motivations, and expectations regarding the topic and the presentation.
  - What are their needs and goals? Consider their problems, challenges, opportunities, and desired outcomes related to the topic and the presentation.

## Assessing the audience

- To assess the audience, you need to gather and analyze relevant information about them, using various methods, such as:
  - Observation: Observe the audience directly or indirectly, through their behavior, appearance, interactions, feedback, etc.
  - Interview: Ask the audience directly or indirectly, through surveys, questionnaires, focus groups, etc., about their characteristics, needs, expectations, and preferences.
  - Research: Review existing sources of information, such as reports, documents, websites, social media, etc., that provide insights into the audience's profile, context, and situation.
  - Inference: Draw logical conclusions from the available information, using your own knowledge, experience, and judgment.

## Adapting to the audience

- To adapt to the audience, you need to adjust your presentation content, structure, style, and delivery, based on the information you have gathered and analyzed, such as:
  - Content: Select and organize the information that is relevant, useful, and persuasive for the audience, and avoid unnecessary, confusing, or offensive information.
  - Structure: Arrange the information in a clear, logical, and coherent way, and use appropriate transitions, headings, summaries, and signposts to guide the audience.
  - Style: Choose the language, tone, and visuals that are appropriate, respectful, and engaging for the audience, and avoid jargon, slang, or bias.
  - Delivery: Use the voice, body language, and media that are suitable, confident, and effective for the audience, and avoid distractions, errors, or interruptions.
