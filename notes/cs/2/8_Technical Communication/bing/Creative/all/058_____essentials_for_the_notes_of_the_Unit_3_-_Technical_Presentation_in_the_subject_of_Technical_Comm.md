# Essentials for the notes of the Unit 3 - Technical Presentation in the subject of Technical Communication

- A technical presentation is a form of oral communication that conveys technical information to a specific audience, such as engineers, scientists, managers, or customers.
- A technical presentation has the following purposes:
  - To inform: to provide factual and objective information that the audience needs or wants to know.
  - To persuade: to convince the audience to accept a certain point of view, to adopt a certain course of action, or to buy a certain product or service.
  - To instruct: to teach the audience how to do something, such as using a software, operating a machine, or following a procedure.
  - To entertain: to amuse or interest the audience with anecdotes, humor, or visual aids.
- A technical presentation has the following components:
  - Introduction: to capture the attention of the audience, to establish the credibility of the speaker, to state the main purpose and scope of the presentation, and to preview the main points or outline.
  - Body: to develop the main points or arguments in a logical and coherent manner, to support them with evidence, examples, statistics, or testimonials, and to use transitions to connect them.
  - Conclusion: to summarize the main points or arguments, to restate the main purpose and scope of the presentation, to provide a call to action or a recommendation, and to thank the audience for their attention.
  - Questions and answers: to clarify any doubts or queries that the audience may have, to address any objections or counterarguments, and to reinforce the main message of the presentation.
- A technical presentation requires the following skills:
  - Research: to gather relevant and reliable information from various sources, such as books, journals, websites, or experts, and to evaluate its quality, accuracy, and relevance.
  - Analysis: to organize, interpret, and synthesize the information, and to identify the main points or arguments that support the purpose of the presentation.
  - Design: to select and arrange the content, structure, and format of the presentation, and to choose appropriate visual aids, such as graphs, charts, diagrams, or images, to enhance the delivery and comprehension of the message.
  - Delivery: to use effective verbal and nonverbal communication skills, such as voice, eye contact, gestures, posture, and facial expressions, to convey the message clearly, confidently, and persuasively, and to adapt to the audience, the situation, and the feedback.
  - Evaluation: to assess the strengths and weaknesses of the presentation, and to seek and incorporate feedback from the audience, the peers, or the instructor, to improve the performance and the outcome.
