# Combination of Expression for the Notes of the Unit 4 - Technical Communication Skills

The unit 4 of the subject of Technical Communication covers the following topics:

- Interview skills
- Group discussion: objective and method
- Seminar/conferences presentation skills: focus, content, style
- Argumentation skills: devices, analysis, cohesion and emphasis
- Critical thinking
- Nuances: exposition, narration and description

The notes for this unit can be written by combining the expression of these topics in a clear, concise and coherent manner. Some possible points for the notes are:

- Interview skills: These are the abilities and techniques that help a person to communicate effectively and confidently with the interviewer in order to achieve a specific goal, such as getting a job, admission or promotion. Some interview skills are:

  - Researching the organization and the position before the interview
  - Preparing relevant and specific questions to ask the interviewer
  - Dressing appropriately and professionally for the interview
  - Arriving on time and greeting the interviewer politely
  - Maintaining eye contact, positive body language and a calm tone of voice
  - Listening attentively and answering the questions with relevant examples and evidence
  - Highlighting one's strengths, achievements and skills that match the position
  - Avoiding negative or vague statements, such as "I don't know" or "I can't"
  - Asking for feedback and following up with a thank you note after the interview

- Group discussion: This is a form of interactive communication in which a group of people exchange their views and opinions on a given topic or issue in order to reach a consensus, solve a problem or generate ideas. Some group discussion skills are:

  - Understanding the objective and the rules of the group discussion
  - Doing a thorough research on the topic and preparing relevant points and examples
  - Initiating the discussion with a clear and concise introduction
  - Contributing to the discussion with logical and factual arguments
  - Listening to others' perspectives and acknowledging them respectfully
  - Asking questions and clarifying doubts
  - Building on others' ideas and giving constructive feedback
  - Summarizing the main points and conclusions of the discussion

- Seminar/conferences presentation skills: These are the abilities and techniques that help a person to deliver an effective and engaging presentation on a specific topic or issue to an audience in a seminar or a conference. Some presentation skills are:

  - Analyzing the purpose, the audience and the context of the presentation
  - Choosing a suitable topic and narrowing it down to a specific focus
  - Developing a clear and coherent structure for the presentation, with an introduction, a body and a conclusion
  - Using appropriate and relevant content, such as facts, statistics, examples, quotations, etc.
  - Using visual aids, such as slides, charts, graphs, images, etc. to support the content and enhance the presentation
  - Practicing the presentation and rehearsing the timing, the delivery and the transitions
  - Using a confident and expressive voice, with proper pronunciation, intonation and modulation
  - Maintaining eye contact, positive body language and gestures
  - Engaging the audience with questions, anecdotes, humor, etc.
  - Handling questions and feedback from the audience effectively

- Argumentation skills: These are the abilities and techniques that help a person to construct and evaluate arguments on a specific topic or issue in order to persuade, inform or convince others. Some argumentation skills are:

  - Identifying the main claim or thesis of the argument and the supporting reasons or premises
  - Using valid and sound logic and reasoning to connect the claim and the reasons
  - Providing sufficient and relevant evidence, such as facts, statistics, examples, etc. to support the reasons
  - Using argumentative devices, such as analogy, comparison, contrast, cause and effect, etc. to enhance the argument
  - Analyzing the counterarguments and the opposing views and refuting them with counter-evidence and counter-reasons
  - Using cohesive devices, such as connectors, transitions, signposts, etc. to link the parts of the argument
  - Using emphatic devices, such as repetition, parallelism, rhetorical questions, etc. to stress the main points of the argument
  - Using appropriate language, tone and style for the argument, such as formal, informal, academic, etc.
