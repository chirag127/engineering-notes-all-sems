# Interjections

Interjections are words or phrases that express a strong feeling or sudden emotion. They are usually used at the beginning of a sentence or by themselves. Interjections are one of the parts of speech in grammar. They do not have a grammatical connection to the rest of the sentence. They are often followed by an exclamation mark (!) or a comma (,).

Some examples of interjections are:

- Wow! That was an amazing performance.
- Ouch, that hurts.
- Hey, what are you doing?
- Ugh, I hate this weather.
- Oops, I dropped the vase.

Interjections can have different purposes, such as:

- To express surprise, joy, anger, fear, disgust, or other emotions. For example:

  - Yay! I won the lottery.
  - Oh no! I forgot my keys.
  - Eek! There's a spider on the wall.
  - Yuck! This food is rotten.
  - Grr! You make me so mad.

- To attract attention, greet someone, or show agreement or disagreement. For example:

  - Hello, nice to meet you.
  - Psst, can you keep a secret?
  - Hmm, I'm not sure about that.
  - Yeah, I agree with you.
  - No way, that's impossible.

- To fill a pause, hesitate, or show uncertainty. For example:

  - Um, I don't know what to say.
  - Er, can you repeat the question?
  - Well, it's a long story.
  - Uh, I think I made a mistake.
  - Huh, that's strange.

Interjections can also be formed by combining two or more words, such as:

- Oh boy, I'm in trouble.
- Wowza, that's impressive.
- Holy cow, that's a huge cake.
- Phew, that was close.
- Aww, how cute.
