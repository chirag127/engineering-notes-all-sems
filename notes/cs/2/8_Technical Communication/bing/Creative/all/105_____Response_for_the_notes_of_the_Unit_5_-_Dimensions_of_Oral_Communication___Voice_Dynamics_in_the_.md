# Unit 5 - Dimensions of Oral Communication & Voice Dynamics

- Oral communication is the process of expressing information or ideas by word of mouth.
- Oral communication has several dimensions, such as context, purpose, audience, channel, message, feedback, and noise.
- Context refers to the physical, social, cultural, and temporal setting of the communication situation.
- Purpose refers to the goal or objective of the communication, such as to inform, persuade, entertain, or instruct.
- Audience refers to the intended or actual receivers of the message, who may have different needs, expectations, and backgrounds.
- Channel refers to the medium or mode of communication, such as face-to-face, telephone, video conference, or podcast.
- Message refers to the content and structure of the communication, which should be clear, concise, coherent, and relevant.
- Feedback refers to the verbal or nonverbal responses of the audience to the message, which can indicate their level of understanding, interest, agreement, or satisfaction.
- Noise refers to any internal or external interference or distraction that can hinder the communication process, such as physical noise, psychological noise, or semantic noise.

- Voice dynamics are the variations in the pitch, volume, rate, and quality of the voice that can enhance or impair the oral communication.
- Pitch refers to the highness or lowness of the voice, which can convey different emotions, attitudes, and meanings.
- Volume refers to the loudness or softness of the voice, which can affect the audibility, clarity, and emphasis of the message.
- Rate refers to the speed or pace of the speech, which can influence the fluency, comprehension, and retention of the message.
- Quality refers to the tone or timbre of the voice, which can reflect the personality, mood, and credibility of the speaker.
