# Impersonal Technical Communication

- Impersonal technical communication is a style of writing or speaking that avoids using personal pronouns (I, you, he, she, we, etc.) and focuses on conveying data and important information.
- Impersonal technical communication is often used in engineering, science, and business contexts, where the goal is to present facts, results, or instructions in a clear, objective, and concise manner.
- Impersonal technical communication can be achieved by using the passive voice, the third person, or the indefinite pronoun one. For example:
  - Passive voice: "The wire was cut" instead of "I cut the wire".
  - Third person: "The researcher conducted the experiment" instead of "You conducted the experiment".
  - Indefinite pronoun one: "One should follow the safety guidelines" instead of "You should follow the safety guidelines".
- Impersonal technical communication can have some advantages, such as:
  - Avoiding bias, subjectivity, or personal opinions that may distract or confuse the audience.
  - Creating a sense of professionalism, authority, or credibility for the writer or speaker.
  - Emphasizing the content, the process, or the outcome rather than the individual or the role.
- Impersonal technical communication can also have some disadvantages, such as:
  - Losing the connection, engagement, or rapport with the audience.
  - Making the tone too dry, dull, or impersonal.
  - Hiding the responsibility, accountability, or agency of the writer or speaker.
- Impersonal technical communication should be used with caution and balance, depending on the purpose, audience, and context of the communication. Sometimes, using personal pronouns or the active voice can make the communication more clear, direct, or persuasive. For example:
  - Active voice: "We recommend this solution" instead of "This solution is recommended".
  - Personal pronouns: "I appreciate your feedback" instead of "Your feedback is appreciated".
  - Impersonal technical communication is not the same as interpersonal communication, which involves the face-to-face exchange of thoughts, ideas, feelings, and emotions between two or more people. Interpersonal communication requires the use of verbal and nonverbal elements, such as body language, facial expressions, and gestures, to communicate effectively. Interpersonal communication is important for developing and maintaining relationships, teamwork, and business communications.
