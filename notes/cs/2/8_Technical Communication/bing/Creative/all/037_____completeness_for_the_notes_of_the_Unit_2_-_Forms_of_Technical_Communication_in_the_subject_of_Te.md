# Completeness for the notes of the Unit 2 - Forms of Technical Communication in the subject of Technical Communication

- Completeness is one of the seven C's of effective communication, along with clarity, conciseness, correctness, coherence, courtesy, and credibility.
- Completeness means that a message contains all the necessary information for the intended audience and purpose, and does not omit any essential facts, details, or steps.
- Completeness is important for technical communication because it helps to achieve the following objectives:
  - To inform the readers about the topic, problem, or solution in a clear and comprehensive way.
  - To persuade the readers to accept the recommendations, proposals, or arguments presented in the message.
  - To instruct the readers on how to perform a task, use a product, or follow a procedure correctly and safely.
  - To document the processes, methods, results, or outcomes of a project, experiment, or research.
- Completeness can be achieved by following these guidelines:
  - Analyze the needs and expectations of the audience and the purpose of the message.
  - Provide all the relevant information that the audience needs to know, understand, or act on the message.
  - Organize the information in a logical and coherent way, using headings, subheadings, lists, tables, charts, diagrams, etc.
  - Use clear and precise language, avoiding ambiguity, jargon, or unnecessary words.
  - Check the accuracy and validity of the information, citing the sources and references if needed.
  - Review the message for any gaps, errors, or inconsistencies, and revise it accordingly.
