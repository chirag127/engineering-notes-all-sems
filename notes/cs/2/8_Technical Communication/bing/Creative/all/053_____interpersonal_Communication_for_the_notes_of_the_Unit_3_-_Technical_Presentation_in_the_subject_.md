# Interpersonal Communication

Interpersonal communication is the process by which people exchange information, feelings, and meaning through verbal and non-verbal messages . It usually involves verbal, face-to-face communication with two or more people, and relies on tone, pitch, volume, speed, and language of voice, as well as gestures, facial expressions, eye contact, posture, and physical distance.

Interpersonal communication is often defined as communication that takes place between people who are interdependent and have some knowledge of each other. For example, communication between a son and his father, an employer and an employee, two sisters, a teacher and a student, two lovers, two friends, and so on.

Interpersonal communication is an important skill that can help you achieve personal and professional goals, such as building relationships, resolving conflicts, influencing others, expressing yourself, and understanding others.

Some of the characteristics of interpersonal communication are:

- It is dynamic, meaning it changes over time and according to the context and situation.
- It is transactional, meaning it involves mutual influence and exchange of messages between the communicators.
- It is irreversible, meaning it cannot be undone or taken back once it is sent or received.
- It is complex, meaning it involves multiple factors and dimensions, such as verbal and non-verbal codes, emotions, perceptions, attitudes, values, beliefs, culture, and personality.
- It is contextual, meaning it depends on the physical, social, psychological, and cultural environment in which it occurs.

Some of the types of interpersonal communication are:

- Dyadic communication, which is communication between two people, such as a conversation, an interview, or a negotiation.
- Small group communication, which is communication among three or more people who share a common goal, such as a team, a committee, or a family.
- Public communication, which is communication from one person to a large audience, such as a speech, a lecture, or a presentation.
- Intercultural communication, which is communication between people from different cultures, backgrounds, or identities, such as a tourist and a local, a migrant and a host, or a minority and a majority.

Some of the skills and strategies for effective interpersonal communication are:

- Active listening, which is paying attention to the speaker's verbal and non-verbal messages, and providing feedback to show understanding and interest.
- Empathy, which is putting yourself in the other person's shoes and trying to understand their feelings, thoughts, and experiences.
- Assertiveness, which is expressing your opinions, needs, and rights in a respectful and confident way, without being aggressive or passive.
- Clarity, which is using clear, concise, and accurate language, and avoiding ambiguity, jargon, and slang.
- Non-verbal communication, which is using gestures, facial expressions, eye contact, posture, and physical distance to support and enhance your verbal messages.
- Feedback, which is giving and receiving constructive and respectful responses to the messages of others, and using them to improve your communication.
- Conflict resolution, which is managing and resolving disagreements and disputes in a positive and cooperative way, without harming the relationship or the outcome.

Interpersonal communication is a vital part of technical presentation, as it helps you to:

- Establish rapport and trust with your audience, and make them feel comfortable and engaged.
- Adapt your message and style to the needs, expectations, and preferences of your audience, and tailor it to the specific context and situation.
- Deliver your message with confidence, clarity, and credibility, and use appropriate verbal and non-verbal techniques to enhance your presentation.
- Respond to questions, comments, and feedback from your audience, and handle any challenges or objections that may arise.
- Evaluate your presentation and seek feedback from others, and use it to improve your communication skills and performance.
