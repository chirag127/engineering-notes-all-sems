# Interview skills for the notes of the Unit 4 - Technical Communication Skills

- Interview skills are the abilities and techniques that help candidates perform well in job interviews and communicate their value to potential employers.
- Technical communication skills are the abilities and knowledge that help technical communicators create, design, and deliver clear, accurate, and user-friendly information for various audiences and purposes.
- Interview skills and technical communication skills are closely related, as both involve presenting information effectively, adapting to different situations and contexts, and collaborating with others.
- Some of the interview skills that are relevant for technical communication roles are:

  - **Nonverbal communication skills**: These are the skills that involve using body language, eye contact, gestures, and facial expressions to convey messages and emotions. Nonverbal communication skills can help candidates show confidence, interest, and professionalism in interviews. They can also help candidates avoid misunderstandings and create rapport with interviewers.
  - **Verbal communication skills**: These are the skills that involve using words, tone, and voice to express ideas and opinions. Verbal communication skills can help candidates articulate their qualifications, achievements, and goals in interviews. They can also help candidates ask relevant questions, provide feedback, and negotiate terms and conditions.
  - **Friendliness**: This is the skill that involves being polite, courteous, and respectful to interviewers and other candidates. Friendliness can help candidates create a positive impression and establish trust and rapport with interviewers. It can also help candidates show enthusiasm, motivation, and interest in the role and the organization.
  - **Brevity**: This is the skill that involves being concise, clear, and precise in communication. Brevity can help candidates avoid unnecessary details, repetitions, and digressions in interviews. It can also help candidates highlight their main points, emphasize their value, and respect the interviewer's time and attention.
  - **Respect**: This is the skill that involves being considerate, attentive, and appreciative of the interviewer's perspective and needs. Respect can help candidates show professionalism, maturity, and integrity in interviews. It can also help candidates avoid interrupting, arguing, or criticizing the interviewer or the organization.
  - **Empathy**: This is the skill that involves understanding and sharing the feelings and thoughts of others. Empathy can help candidates show compassion, sensitivity, and awareness in interviews. It can also help candidates relate to the interviewer's challenges, expectations, and goals, and tailor their responses accordingly.
  - **Listening skills**: These are the skills that involve paying attention, comprehending, and responding to what others say. Listening skills can help candidates show interest, curiosity, and respect in interviews. They can also help candidates avoid misunderstandings, clarify doubts, and provide relevant information.
  - **Confidence**: This is the skill that involves being self-assured, assertive, and positive in communication. Confidence can help candidates show competence, credibility, and readiness in interviews. It can also help candidates overcome nervousness, anxiety, and fear of rejection.

- Some of the ways to improve interview skills and technical communication skills are:

  - **Researching**: This involves gathering information about the role, the organization, the industry, and the interviewer before the interview. Researching can help candidates prepare relevant and specific answers, questions, and examples for the interview. It can also help candidates show knowledge, interest, and initiative in the interview.
  - **Practicing**: This involves rehearsing common interview questions and scenarios with a friend, a mentor, or a coach before the interview. Practicing can help candidates improve their verbal and nonverbal communication skills, identify and correct their mistakes, and gain confidence and feedback for the interview.
  - **Reviewing**: This involves evaluating one's performance, strengths, and weaknesses after the interview. Reviewing can help candidates learn from their experience, identify and improve their areas of improvement, and follow up with the interviewer and the organization.
