# Cohesion

Cohesion is one of the aspects of technical communication that makes a text coherent and connected. It refers to the ways in which the elements of a text are linked together by grammatical, lexical, semantic, and other means. Cohesion helps the reader to follow the flow of ideas and information in a text, and to understand the relationships between them.

Some of the ways to achieve cohesion in technical communication are:

- Using pronouns, articles, and demonstratives to refer back or forward to other parts of the text. For example, "The device has a sensor that detects the temperature. It can be adjusted by turning the knob."
- Using synonyms, hyponyms, and antonyms to avoid repetition and create variety. For example, "The software is easy to use and user-friendly. It has a simple and intuitive interface."
- Using conjunctions, adverbs, and prepositions to show logical connections between sentences and paragraphs. For example, "However, there are some limitations to this method. Therefore, we suggest further research on this topic."
- Using parallelism, repetition, and enumeration to emphasize key points and create rhythm. For example, "The main advantages of this approach are: it is fast, it is accurate, and it is cost-effective."
- Using headings, subheadings, bullets, and numbering to organize the text and guide the reader. For example, "1. Introduction 2. Literature Review 3. Methodology 4. Results and Discussion 5. Conclusion and Recommendations"

Cohesion is important for technical communication because it helps the reader to comprehend the text and to follow the writer's purpose and argument. A cohesive text is clear, consistent, and coherent. A non-cohesive text is confusing, contradictory, and incoherent.
