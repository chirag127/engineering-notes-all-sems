# Thesis/Project Writing for the Notes of the Unit 2 - Forms of Technical Communication in the Subject of Technical Communication

- A thesis or a project is a form of technical communication that involves creating a document or a resource that conveys information about a specific topic in a suitable format for its intended audience and purpose  .
- A thesis or a project typically consists of the following sections :
  - Introduction: This section introduces the purpose, scope, and significance of the chosen topic; situates the topic within the context of the available literature in the field of technical communication; and explains the research question or problem that the thesis or project aims to address.
  - Literature Review: This section reviews the relevant and current sources that inform the topic and the research question or problem; synthesizes the main themes, gaps, and debates in the literature; and establishes the theoretical or conceptual framework for the thesis or project.
  - Methodology: This section describes the research method employed and the rationale for choosing it; explains the data collection and analysis procedures; and discusses the ethical considerations, limitations, and challenges of the research.
  - Results: This section presents the findings of the data analysis; organizes the results according to the research question or problem; and uses tables, graphs, charts, or other visual aids to illustrate the results.
  - Discussion: This section interprets and evaluates the results; relates the results to the literature review and the research question or problem; and discusses the implications, contributions, and limitations of the research.
  - Conclusion: This section summarizes the main points and arguments of the thesis or project; answers the research question or problem; and provides recommendations for future research or practice.
  - References: This section lists the sources cited in the thesis or project according to a specific citation style (e.g., APA, MLA, IEEE, etc.).
  - Appendices: This section includes any additional or supplementary materials that support the thesis or project, such as data sets, questionnaires, interview transcripts, etc.
- A thesis or a project requires practitioners to have a heightened awareness of the conventions and rhetorical situations in which they are communicating. Some of the factors that affect the design and delivery of a thesis or a project are :
  - Audience: The primary audience of a thesis or a project is usually the supervisor, the committee, and the examiners, who are experts in the field of technical communication. The secondary audience may include other researchers, practitioners, or stakeholders who are interested in the topic. The thesis or project should address the needs, expectations, and backgrounds of both the primary and secondary audiences.
  - Purpose: The purpose of a thesis or a project is to demonstrate the ability to conduct original and rigorous research on a specific topic in technical communication; to contribute new knowledge or insights to the field; and to communicate the research effectively and persuasively to the audience.
  - Context: The context of a thesis or a project includes the academic discipline, the institution, the field of technical communication, and the broader social and cultural environment in which the research is situated. The thesis or a project should reflect the norms, values, and standards of the context and acknowledge the ethical and social implications of the research.
  - Format: The format of a thesis or a project refers to the physical appearance, layout, and organization of the document or the resource. The format should be consistent, clear, and professional; follow the guidelines and requirements of the institution and the discipline; and use appropriate fonts, colors, margins, headings, page numbers, etc.
  - Style: The style of a thesis or a project refers to the language, tone, and voice of the communication. The style should be formal, objective, and precise; use correct grammar, spelling, and punctuation; and avoid jargon, slang, or colloquialisms. The style should also be consistent with the citation style and the format of the thesis or project.
