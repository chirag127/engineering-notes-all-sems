# Downward Communication

Downward communication is the act of relaying important information in a company or organization from higher levels to lower levels. It is a communication process in which information or the chain of command passes from the upper hierarchy level to the lower level. It is also known as top-down communication or vertical communication.

Some of the objectives of downward communication are:

- To provide instructions, directions, feedback, and guidance to the subordinates.
- To inform the employees about the policies, rules, regulations, and goals of the organization.
- To motivate, inspire, and influence the employees to perform better and achieve the desired results.
- To create a sense of unity, cooperation, and loyalty among the employees.
- To ensure clarity, accuracy, and consistency of the information across the organization.

Some of the examples of downward communication are:

- Memos, notices, circulars, newsletters, and reports from the management to the employees.
- Performance appraisals, rewards, recognition, and criticism from the supervisors to the subordinates.
- Job descriptions, roles, responsibilities, and expectations from the leaders to the followers.
- Training, coaching, mentoring, and counseling from the experts to the novices.
- Meetings, presentations, speeches, and announcements from the higher authorities to the lower levels.

Some of the advantages of downward communication are:

- It helps in establishing authority, control, and discipline in the organization.
- It helps in ensuring alignment, coordination, and integration of the activities and functions of the organization.
- It helps in enhancing the efficiency, productivity, and quality of the work and services of the organization.
- It helps in improving the communication skills, knowledge, and understanding of the employees.
- It helps in building trust, confidence, and respect between the management and the employees.

Some of the disadvantages of downward communication are:

- It may create a communication gap, distortion, or misunderstanding due to the filtering, omission, or alteration of the information by the intermediaries.
- It may lead to a lack of feedback, participation, and innovation from the employees due to the one-way or directive nature of the communication.
- It may cause resentment, frustration, or demotivation among the employees due to the excessive or inappropriate use of power, authority, or criticism by the management.
- It may result in a loss of flexibility, adaptability, and responsiveness of the organization due to the rigid or hierarchical structure of the communication.
- It may hamper the development of the interpersonal, social, and emotional skills of the employees due to the lack of interaction, communication, or relationship with the management.
