### Humour

Humour is the quality of being amusing or funny, or the ability to make others laugh. Humour can be used in various forms of communication, including presentations, to achieve different purposes, such as:

- Capturing and maintaining the audience's attention
- Creating a rapport and connection with the audience
- Making the presentation more memorable and engaging
- Relieving tension and boredom
- Enhancing the credibility and likability of the speaker
- Illustrating a point or reinforcing a message
- Encouraging participation and feedback

However, humour can also have some drawbacks or risks, such as:

- Offending or alienating some audience members
- Distracting or confusing the audience from the main topic
- Undermining the seriousness or professionalism of the speaker or the presentation
- Backfiring or failing to elicit the desired response

Therefore, humour should be used carefully and strategically in presentations, especially in technical or scientific ones, where the audience may have high expectations of accuracy, clarity, and relevance. Here are some tips and techniques for using humour effectively in presentations:

- Know your audience and tailor your humour accordingly. Consider their background, culture, interests, expectations, and preferences. Avoid jokes or references that may be insensitive, inappropriate, or incomprehensible to them.
- Know your topic and purpose and align your humour with them. Choose humour that supports or illustrates your main points, rather than distracts or contradicts them. Avoid humour that may undermine your credibility, authority, or expertise on the topic.
- Know your style and personality and express your humour naturally. Use humour that suits your tone, voice, and delivery. Avoid humour that may seem forced, awkward, or out of character for you.
- Use humour sparingly and strategically. Do not overuse or rely on humour as the main attraction of your presentation. Use humour to enhance, not replace, your content and message. Use humour at appropriate moments, such as the opening, the closing, or the transitions, to create interest, impact, or connection.
- Use humour creatively and originally. Do not use clichéd, stale, or recycled jokes or anecdotes. Use humour that is fresh, relevant, and personal. Use humour that surprises, challenges, or delights your audience.
- Use humour respectfully and responsibly. Do not use humour that is offensive, rude, or harmful to anyone or anything. Use humour that is positive, polite, and constructive. Use humour that is self-deprecating, rather than mocking or ridiculing others.

Some examples of humour techniques that can be used in presentations are:

- Anecdotes: Short stories or personal experiences that relate to the topic or the audience, and that have a humorous twist or lesson.
- Quotations: Famous or witty sayings or remarks by well-known or relevant people or sources, that illustrate or support a point or a message.
- Analogies: Comparisons or contrasts between two different or unlikely things or situations, that highlight a similarity or a difference in a humorous way.
- Puns: Words or phrases that have a double meaning or a sound-alike, that create a humorous effect or a play on words.
- Cartoons: Drawings or images that depict a humorous or exaggerated situation or character, that relate to the topic or the audience.
- Videos: Clips or segments of movies, shows, or other sources, that show a humorous or relevant scene or dialogue, that relate to the topic or the audience.
