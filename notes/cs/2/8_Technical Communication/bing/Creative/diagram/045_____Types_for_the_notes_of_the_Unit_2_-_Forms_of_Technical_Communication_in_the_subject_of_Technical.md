### Types for the notes of the Unit 2 - Forms of Technical Communication

- Technical communication is the process of conveying information and instructions to a specific audience for a specific purpose.
- There are different types of technical communication, depending on the context, purpose, audience, and medium of the message.
- Some common types of technical communication are:

  - **Technical reports**: These are formal documents that present the results of research, analysis, or evaluation of a problem or situation. They usually follow a standard structure and format, such as introduction, methods, results, discussion, conclusion, and recommendations. They may also include appendices, tables, figures, and references. Technical reports are often used to communicate scientific or engineering findings, proposals, or recommendations to other experts, managers, or stakeholders.
  - **User manuals**: These are documents that provide instructions on how to use a product, system, or service. They usually include an overview, a description of features and functions, step-by-step procedures, troubleshooting tips, safety warnings, and contact information. User manuals are often written for non-experts who need to operate or maintain a product, system, or service.
  - **Business letters**: These are formal letters that communicate information or requests between individuals or organizations. They usually follow a standard format, such as date, address, salutation, body, closing, and signature. They may also include enclosures, attachments, or copies. Business letters are often used to communicate proposals, inquiries, complaints, acknowledgments, or confirmations to customers, suppliers, partners, or regulators.
  - **Memos**: These are informal documents that communicate information or requests within an organization. They usually follow a simple format, such as to, from, date, subject, and message. They may also include attachments or copies. Memos are often used to communicate updates, announcements, reminders, or feedback to colleagues, managers, or subordinates.
  - **Emails**: These are electronic messages that communicate information or requests between individuals or groups. They usually follow a similar format to memos, but may also include a subject line, a greeting, and a signature. They may also include attachments, links, or images. Emails are often used to communicate quickly and conveniently with anyone who has an email address, such as customers, suppliers, partners, or colleagues.
  - **Presentations**: These are oral or visual messages that communicate information or ideas to an audience. They usually include a speaker, a slide show, and a handout. They may also include audio, video, or interactive elements. Presentations are often used to communicate findings, proposals, or recommendations to managers, stakeholders, or customers.
  - **Websites**: These are collections of web pages that communicate information or services to online users. They usually include a homepage, a navigation menu, and a footer. They may also include text, images, videos, links, or interactive elements. Websites are often used to communicate information, products, or services to anyone who has access to the internet, such as customers, suppliers, partners, or colleagues.
