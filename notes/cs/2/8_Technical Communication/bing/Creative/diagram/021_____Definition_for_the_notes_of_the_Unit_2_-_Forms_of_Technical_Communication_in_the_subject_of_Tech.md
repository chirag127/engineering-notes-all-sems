### Definition for the notes of the Unit 2 - Forms of Technical Communication in the subject of Technical Communication

- Technical communication is the process of conveying information and instructions to a specific audience for a specific purpose, using clear, concise, and accurate language and visuals.
- Forms of technical communication are the different ways of presenting technical information and instructions to different audiences and contexts, such as reports, manuals, proposals, presentations, etc.
- Some common forms of technical communication are:

  - **Reports**: Documents that provide factual information and analysis on a specific topic, such as progress, feasibility, research, etc. Reports can be formal or informal, depending on the purpose and audience. Reports usually have a standard structure, such as title page, abstract, introduction, body, conclusion, recommendations, references, and appendices.
  - **Manuals**: Documents that provide step-by-step instructions on how to use a product, service, or system, such as software, hardware, equipment, etc. Manuals can be user manuals, installation manuals, maintenance manuals, etc. Manuals usually have a table of contents, an overview, a list of components, procedures, troubleshooting, glossary, and index.
  - **Proposals**: Documents that offer a solution to a problem or a plan for a project, such as a grant proposal, a business proposal, a research proposal, etc. Proposals can be solicited or unsolicited, depending on whether the audience has requested them or not. Proposals usually have an executive summary, a background, a statement of the problem or need, a description of the proposed solution or plan, a budget, a timeline, a list of qualifications, and a conclusion.
  - **Presentations**: Oral or visual communication that delivers information and instructions to an audience, such as a lecture, a speech, a demonstration, a webinar, etc. Presentations can be informative or persuasive, depending on the purpose and audience. Presentations usually have an introduction, a body, a conclusion, and a question and answer session. Presentations can be supported by slides, handouts, videos, etc.
