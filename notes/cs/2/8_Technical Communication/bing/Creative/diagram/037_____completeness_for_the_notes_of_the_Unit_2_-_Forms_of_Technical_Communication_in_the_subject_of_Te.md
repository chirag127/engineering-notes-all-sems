### Completeness

- Completeness is one of the seven C's of effective technical communication, along with clarity, conciseness, correctness, coherence, courtesy, and consideration.
- Completeness means that a technical document provides all the information that the reader needs to understand the message and take appropriate action.
- Completeness also means that the document covers the topic in sufficient depth and detail, without leaving out any essential facts or arguments.
- Completeness can be achieved by following these guidelines:

  - Identify the purpose and audience of the document and tailor the content accordingly.
  - Use the inverted pyramid structure to organize the information from the most important to the least important.
  - Provide an overview or summary of the main points at the beginning and a conclusion or recommendation at the end.
  - Answer the five W's and one H questions: who, what, when, where, why, and how.
  - Include relevant examples, illustrations, tables, graphs, or other visual aids to support the text.
  - Anticipate and address any questions, objections, or concerns that the reader may have.
  - Provide references, citations, or links to credible sources for any facts, statistics, or claims that are not common knowledge.
  - Use headings, subheadings, lists, bullets, or numbers to divide the text into manageable chunks and highlight the key points.
  - Use transitions, connectors, or signposts to guide the reader through the document and show the relationships between the ideas.
  - Proofread and revise the document to eliminate any errors, ambiguities, or redundancies.

- Completeness is important for effective technical communication because it:

  - Enhances the credibility and authority of the writer and the organization.
  - Increases the reader's confidence and trust in the message and the sender.
  - Reduces the risk of misunderstanding, confusion, or misinterpretation.
  - Saves time and effort for both the writer and the reader by avoiding the need for follow-up communication.
  - Fulfills the ethical and legal obligations of the writer and the organization.
