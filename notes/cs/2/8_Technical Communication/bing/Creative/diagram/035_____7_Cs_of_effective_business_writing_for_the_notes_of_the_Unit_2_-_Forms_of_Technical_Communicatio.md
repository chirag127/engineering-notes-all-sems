### 7 Cs of effective business writing

Business writing is a form of technical communication that aims to convey information, persuade, instruct, or request action in a clear, concise, and professional manner. Effective business writing follows the 7 Cs of communication, which are:

- **Clear**: The writing should be easy to understand and avoid ambiguity or confusion. The writer should state the main idea, purpose, and message of the document in simple and direct language. The writer should also use appropriate words, phrases, and sentences that suit the intended audience and context.
- **Coherent**: The writing should be logical and consistent, and follow a clear structure and flow. The writer should organize the information in a way that makes sense and expresses the meaning, without gaps and contradictions. The writer should also use transitions, headings, and formatting to guide the reader and highlight the main points.
- **Complete**: The writing should provide all the necessary and relevant information that the reader needs to know or act upon. The writer should not leave out any important details, facts, or evidence that support the message. The writer should also anticipate and answer any questions or objections that the reader might have.
- **Concise**: The writing should be brief and to the point, and avoid unnecessary or redundant words, phrases, or sentences. The writer should use the most precise and effective language to convey the message, and eliminate any filler, jargon, or fluff. The writer should also use active voice, positive tone, and parallel structure to make the writing more concise and clear.
- **Concrete**: The writing should be specific and factual, and use concrete examples, illustrations, or evidence to support the message. The writer should avoid vague or abstract language that might confuse or mislead the reader. The writer should also use sensory details, numbers, or statistics to make the writing more vivid and convincing.
- **Correct**: The writing should be accurate and error-free, and follow the rules and standards of grammar, spelling, punctuation, and format. The writer should proofread and edit the document carefully, and use tools or resources to check for any mistakes. The writer should also use the appropriate tone and style of expression, and avoid any slang, colloquialisms, or offensive language that might harm the credibility or reputation of the writer or the organization.
- **Courteous**: The writing should be polite and respectful, and consider the needs, expectations, and feelings of the reader. The writer should use positive and friendly language, and avoid any negative or harsh words, phrases, or sentences that might offend or annoy the reader. The writer should also express appreciation, gratitude, or apology when appropriate, and address the reader by name or title if possible.
