### Technical writing

- Technical writing is a form of communication that professionals use to convey information about specialized topics .
- Technical writing is often defined as "simplifying the complex". It is a subcategory of technical communication and is primarily used in technical and operational disciplines, such as digital technologies, engineering, science, aeronautics, robotics, finance, medical, electronics, forestry, and biotechnology .
- Technical writing has the following characteristics:
  - It is clear, concise, and accurate.
  - It uses a formal and objective tone.
  - It follows the conventions of grammar, punctuation, and spelling.
  - It uses specialized vocabulary and terminology appropriate for the audience and purpose.
  - It organizes information logically and coherently.
  - It uses visual aids, such as graphs, tables, diagrams, and illustrations, to support the text.
- Technical writing has the following purposes:
  - To inform, instruct, or explain technical concepts or procedures.
  - To persuade, recommend, or propose solutions or actions.
  - To document, record, or report technical data or findings.
  - To communicate, collaborate, or coordinate with other professionals or stakeholders.
- Technical writing has the following types:
  - User manuals, guides, or tutorials.
  - Technical reports, proposals, or feasibility studies.
  - Technical specifications, requirements, or standards.
  - Technical articles, newsletters, or blogs.
  - Technical presentations, webinars, or podcasts.
  - Technical resumes, cover letters, or portfolios.

### How to become a technical writer

- To become a technical writer, one needs to pursue the following steps:
  - Pursue education. Most technical writing positions require applicants to hold at least a bachelor's degree. Those who want to pursue this field may choose majors such as English, communication, journalism, or technical writing. Some employers may also prefer candidates with a degree or background in a specific technical domain, such as engineering, computer science, or medicine.
  - Develop relevant skills. Technical writers need to have a combination of writing, communication, research, technical, and analytical skills. They also need to be proficient in using various software tools, such as word processors, graphic editors, content management systems, and web development platforms. Technical writers can improve their skills by taking courses, attending workshops, reading books, or practicing on their own projects.
  - Learn style guides. Technical writers need to follow the standards and guidelines of different style guides, such as the Microsoft Manual of Style, the Chicago Manual of Style, or the APA Style. Style guides help technical writers to ensure consistency, accuracy, and clarity in their documents. Technical writers can learn style guides by reading them, consulting them, or using online resources, such as Purdue OWL or Grammarly.
  - Become certified. Technical writers can enhance their credibility and employability by obtaining certifications from professional organizations, such as the Society for Technical Communication (STC) or the American Medical Writers Association (AMWA). Certifications can demonstrate technical writers' knowledge, skills, and experience in the field. Technical writers can prepare for certification exams by taking courses, studying materials, or joining study groups.
  - Determine where you want to work. Technical writers can work in various industries, sectors, or domains, depending on their interests, qualifications, and goals. Technical writers can work for companies, agencies, or organizations that produce or use technical products or services. They can also work as freelancers, consultants, or contractors, offering their services to different clients. Technical writers can find job opportunities by searching online platforms, such as Indeed, LinkedIn, or Glassdoor, or by networking with other professionals, such as colleagues, mentors, or alumni.
