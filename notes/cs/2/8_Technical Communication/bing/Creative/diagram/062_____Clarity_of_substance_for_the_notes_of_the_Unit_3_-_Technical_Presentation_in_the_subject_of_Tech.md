### Clarity of substance

- Clarity of substance refers to the quality of the content of a technical presentation, such as the accuracy, relevance, completeness, and organization of the information.
- Clarity of substance is important for effective technical communication, as it helps the audience to understand the purpose, main points, and implications of the presentation, and to evaluate the credibility and reliability of the presenter and the sources.
- Some strategies to achieve clarity of substance are:

  - Define the purpose and scope of the presentation clearly and concisely, and state them at the beginning and the end of the presentation.
  - Identify and analyze the needs, expectations, and background of the audience, and tailor the content accordingly.
  - Select and prioritize the most relevant and essential information for the presentation, and avoid unnecessary details, jargon, or technical terms that may confuse or bore the audience.
  - Use credible and reliable sources to support the claims, arguments, or recommendations of the presentation, and cite them properly using a consistent and appropriate citation style.
  - Organize the content logically and coherently, using an introduction, a body, and a conclusion, and using transitions, headings, and subheadings to guide the audience through the presentation.
  - Use visual aids, such as graphs, charts, tables, diagrams, or images, to supplement and enhance the verbal content, and to illustrate complex or abstract concepts, data, or relationships.
  - Use clear and precise language, and avoid ambiguity, vagueness, or redundancy. Use active voice, simple sentences, and familiar words, and define any acronyms, abbreviations, or technical terms that are necessary for the presentation.
  - Use examples, analogies, anecdotes, or stories to illustrate the points, and to make the content more engaging, relatable, and memorable for the audience.
  - Revise and edit the content for clarity, accuracy, and completeness, and proofread for grammar, spelling, and punctuation errors. Seek feedback from peers, instructors, or experts, and make improvements based on the feedback.
