### Conciseness
- Conciseness is the quality of using only the words that are necessary to convey the intended meaning.
- Conciseness helps the reader to understand the message quickly and easily, and avoids wasting the reader's time and attention.
- Conciseness can be achieved by following some general principles, such as:
  - Eliminating unnecessary words, such as redundancies, fillers, modifiers, and qualifiers.
  - Using specific and concrete words, rather than vague and abstract ones.
  - Using active voice, rather than passive voice, when possible.
  - Using parallel structure, rather than mixed structure, when expressing similar ideas.
  - Using lists, tables, graphs, or diagrams, rather than long paragraphs, when presenting data or information.
  - Using headings, subheadings, bullets, or numbers, rather than long transitions, when organizing the document.
  - Using short sentences and paragraphs, rather than long and complex ones.
  - Revising and editing the document, to remove any errors, ambiguities, or inconsistencies.
- Conciseness is an important skill for technical communication, as it helps the writer to communicate effectively and efficiently with the audience, and to achieve the purpose of the document.
