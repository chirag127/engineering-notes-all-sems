### Emphasis

- Emphasis is the technique of making some words, phrases, sentences, or paragraphs stand out more than others in a text.
- Emphasis can be used to highlight the main idea, the purpose, the audience, the tone, or the structure of a text.
- Emphasis can be achieved by using various methods, such as:

  - Position: placing the most important information at the beginning or the end of a text, paragraph, or sentence.
  - Repetition: repeating the same word, phrase, or idea for emphasis or clarity.
  - Contrast: using opposite or contrasting words, ideas, or formats to create contrast or comparison.
  - Typography: using different fonts, sizes, colors, styles, or formats to make some text more noticeable or attractive.
  - Visual aids: using images, graphs, charts, tables, or diagrams to support or illustrate the text.
  - Parallelism: using the same grammatical structure or pattern for two or more words, phrases, or clauses to show similarity or connection.
  - Subordination: using dependent clauses, phrases, or words to show the relationship between the main and the subordinate ideas.
  - Coordination: using coordinating conjunctions, such as and, but, or, nor, for, so, yet, to join two or more independent clauses of equal importance.
  - Punctuation: using commas, dashes, colons, semicolons, parentheses, quotation marks, or other marks to separate, connect, or emphasize the text.
  - Rhetorical devices: using figures of speech, such as metaphors, similes, personification, hyperbole, alliteration, assonance, or irony to create vivid or memorable images or expressions.
