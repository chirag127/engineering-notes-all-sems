# Concreteness for the notes of the Unit 2 - Forms of Technical Communication

- Concreteness is an aspect of communication that means being specific, definite, and vivid rather than vague and general.
- A concrete communication uses specific facts and figures, examples, explanations, and evidence to support the message  .
- Concreteness is often taught in college communication courses as one of the aspects of effective communication .
- Concreteness helps the audience better understand and interpret the message, as well as enhances the credibility and clarity of the communication  .
- Concreteness also reduces the risk of misunderstanding, confusion, or ambiguity, which are common challenges in the workplace .
- Concreteness can be achieved by using concrete language, which is the opposite of abstract language, that refers to tangible, observable, or measurable things, rather than intangible, subjective, or vague ideas or qualities.
- Concreteness can also be improved by using specific words, rather than adjectives or adverbs, that describe the exact features, attributes, or actions of the subject.
- Concreteness can be checked by asking questions such as: Who? What? When? Where? Why? How? How much? How many? .
- Concreteness is one of the seven C's of effective communication, along with clarity, correctness, completeness, conciseness, courtesy, and coherence .
