# Considerateness for the notes of the Unit 5 - Dimensions of Oral Communication & Voice Dynamics in the subject of Technical Communication

- Considerateness is one of the seven C's of effective communication, along with clarity, conciseness, completeness, correctness, concreteness, and courtesy.
- Considerateness means showing respect and empathy for the audience, and avoiding words or expressions that might offend, hurt, or confuse them  .
- Considerateness also means using a positive and optimistic tone, emphasizing on what is possible rather than what is impossible, and focusing on the benefits and solutions rather than the problems and limitations .
- Considerateness is important for oral communication because it helps to build rapport and trust with the listeners, and to persuade and influence them effectively .
- Some features of considerate oral communication are   :
  - Using the "you" approach, which means addressing the audience directly and showing interest in their needs, wants, and feelings.
  - Using polite and respectful language, such as please, thank you, sorry, etc.
  - Avoiding negative or harsh words, such as no, never, cannot, etc.
  - Avoiding jargon, slang, or technical terms that might confuse or alienate the audience.
  - Using simple and clear sentences that are easy to understand and follow.
  - Using examples, stories, or anecdotes that relate to the audience and illustrate the main points.
  - Using appropriate nonverbal cues, such as eye contact, facial expressions, gestures, posture, etc.
  - Providing constructive and positive feedback, and inviting questions and comments from the audience.
  - Adapting the message to the audience's level of knowledge, interest, and expectations.
  - Being honest, sincere, and ethical in the communication.

- Some examples of considerate oral communication are   :
  - Instead of saying "You are wrong", say "I see your point, but I have a different perspective".
  - Instead of saying "This product is cheap and easy to use", say "This product is affordable and user-friendly".
  - Instead of saying "We have a lot of problems to solve", say "We have some challenges to overcome".
  - Instead of saying "I don't know", say "I will find out and get back to you".
  - Instead of saying "You have to do this", say "I suggest you do this".
  - Instead of saying "This is boring", say "This is important".
  - Instead of saying "You are late", say "I appreciate your presence".
  - Instead of saying "This is complicated", say "Let me explain this in a simple way".
  - Instead of saying "You don't understand", say "Let me clarify this for you".
