### Tone

- Tone is the attitude or emotion that the speaker conveys through their voice and words.
- Tone can affect the meaning and impact of the message, as well as the relationship between the speaker and the listener.
- Tone can be influenced by various factors, such as the purpose, context, audience, mood, and personality of the speaker.
- Tone can be classified into different types, such as formal, informal, friendly, hostile, polite, rude, confident, hesitant, etc.
- Tone can be modified by using different vocal elements, such as pitch, volume, rate, pause, stress, and intonation.
- Tone can also be enhanced by using appropriate language, such as word choice, sentence structure, grammar, and punctuation.

Some examples of different tones and how to achieve them are:

- Formal tone: used in professional, academic, or official situations, where the speaker wants to show respect, authority, or credibility. To achieve a formal tone, the speaker should use standard and precise language, avoid slang, jargon, or colloquialisms, use complete sentences, and follow the rules of grammar and punctuation.
- Informal tone: used in casual, personal, or familiar situations, where the speaker wants to show friendliness, intimacy, or humor. To achieve an informal tone, the speaker can use more relaxed and conversational language, use slang, jargon, or colloquialisms, use contractions, abbreviations, or incomplete sentences, and break some rules of grammar and punctuation.
- Friendly tone: used when the speaker wants to show warmth, kindness, or appreciation to the listener. To achieve a friendly tone, the speaker should use positive and polite language, express gratitude, compliments, or interest, use personal pronouns, and modulate their voice to sound pleasant and cheerful.
- Hostile tone: used when the speaker wants to show anger, resentment, or contempt to the listener. To achieve a hostile tone, the speaker should use negative and rude language, express criticism, insults, or threats, use impersonal pronouns, and modulate their voice to sound harsh and loud.
- Polite tone: used when the speaker wants to show respect, courtesy, or deference to the listener. To achieve a polite tone, the speaker should use respectful and courteous language, express requests, apologies, or permission, use modal verbs, and modulate their voice to sound soft and low.
- Rude tone: used when the speaker wants to show disrespect, impoliteness, or arrogance to the listener. To achieve a rude tone, the speaker should use disrespectful and impolite language, express commands, interruptions, or sarcasm, use imperative verbs, and modulate their voice to sound sharp and high.
- Confident tone: used when the speaker wants to show assurance, competence, or conviction to the listener. To achieve a confident tone, the speaker should use assertive and confident language, express facts, opinions, or arguments, use declarative sentences, and modulate their voice to sound clear and steady.
- Hesitant tone: used when the speaker wants to show uncertainty, doubt, or hesitation to the listener. To achieve a hesitant tone, the speaker should use tentative and cautious language, express questions, qualifiers, or hesitations, use interrogative sentences, and modulate their voice to sound weak and shaky.
