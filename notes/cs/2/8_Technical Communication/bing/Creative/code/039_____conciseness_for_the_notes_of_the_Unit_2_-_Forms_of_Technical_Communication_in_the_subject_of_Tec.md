### Conciseness
- Conciseness is the quality of using only the words that are necessary to convey a clear and accurate message.
- Conciseness is important for effective technical communication because it helps the readers to understand the information quickly and easily, and avoids wasting their time and attention.
- Conciseness can be achieved by following some general principles, such as:
  - Eliminating unnecessary words, such as redundancies, fillers, modifiers, and qualifiers.
  - Using specific and concrete words, rather than vague and abstract ones.
  - Using active voice, rather than passive voice, whenever possible.
  - Using parallel structure, rather than mixed structure, to organize similar ideas.
  - Using lists, tables, graphs, and other visual aids, rather than long paragraphs, to present data and information.
  - Using headings, subheadings, bullets, and numbers, rather than transitions, to divide and highlight the main points.
  - Using short sentences and paragraphs, rather than long and complex ones, to improve readability and clarity.
  - Revising and editing the draft, rather than writing the final version at once, to eliminate errors and improve the flow and coherence of the text.
- Conciseness does not mean sacrificing completeness, accuracy, or courtesy. It means choosing the best words and the best way to convey the message without losing or distorting the meaning or the tone.
