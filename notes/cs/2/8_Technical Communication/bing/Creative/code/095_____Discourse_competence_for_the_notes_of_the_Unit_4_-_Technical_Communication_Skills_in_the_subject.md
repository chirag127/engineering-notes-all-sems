### Discourse competence for the notes of the Unit 4 - Technical Communication Skills in the subject of Technical Communication

- Discourse competence is the ability to understand and express oneself in a given language, combining grammatical forms and meaning to achieve a linked text (oral or written), in different communication situations .
- Discourse competence is one of the components of communicative competence, which also includes linguistic, sociolinguistic, pragmatic, and strategic competence .
- Discourse competence involves the following skills and knowledge:
  - Textual competence: the ability to read and comprehend different types of texts, such as narratives, descriptions, arguments, explanations, etc., and to identify their structure, purpose, and audience.
  - Cohesion and coherence: the ability to use linguistic devices, such as pronouns, conjunctions, transitions, etc., to connect sentences and paragraphs, and to organize ideas logically and clearly .
  - Genre awareness: the ability to recognize and produce different genres of communication, such as reports, proposals, emails, etc., and to follow their conventions and expectations .
  - Lexical competence: the ability to use appropriate vocabulary and terminology for different contexts and purposes, and to avoid ambiguity and confusion .
  - Rhetorical competence: the ability to use persuasive and effective strategies to convey a message, such as ethos, logos, pathos, etc., and to adapt to the needs and interests of the audience .
- Discourse competence is relevant for technical communication because it enables the communicator to produce clear, accurate, and engaging texts that meet the goals and requirements of the situation, and to interact effectively with different discourse communities, such as colleagues, clients, experts, etc. .
