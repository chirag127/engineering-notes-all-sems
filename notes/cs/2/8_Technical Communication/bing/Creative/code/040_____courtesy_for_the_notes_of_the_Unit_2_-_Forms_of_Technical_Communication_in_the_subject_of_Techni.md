### Courtesy

Courtesy is one of the key components of effective communication. It means showing respect, consideration and goodwill to the receiver of the message. Courtesy can enhance the relationship between the sender and the receiver, and create a positive impression of the sender. Courtesy can also avoid misunderstandings, conflicts and negative emotions that may arise from rude, insensitive or biased messages.

Some of the ways to show courtesy in technical communication are:

- Use positive and polite words that reflect the you-attitude . For example, instead of saying "You failed to submit the report on time", say "I appreciate your efforts in completing the report".
- Acknowledge the audience by making eye contact, using appropriate titles and modes of address, and engaging them with relevant humor, stories and examples. For example, instead of saying "Hello everyone", say "Good morning, ladies and gentlemen".
- Avoid potentially offensive terminology, usage and tone that may imply discrimination, stereotyping, prejudice or disrespect. For example, instead of saying "The disabled people", say "The people with disabilities".
- Design a reader-friendly, easy-to-read document that follows the conventions of format, style, grammar and punctuation. For example, use headings, bullet points, white space, clear fonts and consistent layout to organize the information.
- Strive for a neutral tone that does not display obvious emotion, bias or personal opinion. For example, use facts, evidence and logic to support your claims, and avoid exaggeration, sarcasm or emotive language.
