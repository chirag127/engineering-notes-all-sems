### Syllables for the notes of the Unit 5 - Dimensions of Oral Communication & Voice Dynamics in the subject of Technical Communication

- A syllable is a unit of pronunciation that consists of one or more sounds, usually a vowel sound and one or more consonant sounds     .
- Syllables are the building blocks of spoken words and determine the pace and rhythm of how the words are pronounced .
- Syllables can be classified into different types based on the number and position of consonants and vowels in them. Some common types are:
  - Open syllables: syllables that end with a vowel sound, such as `me`, `go`, `sky`, `ba-by` .
  - Closed syllables: syllables that end with a consonant sound, such as `cat`, `dog`, `stop`, `pic-nic` .
  - Vowel-consonant-e syllables: syllables that have a vowel followed by a consonant and a silent e, such as `cake`, `like`, `phone`, `com-pete` .
  - Consonant-le syllables: syllables that have a consonant followed by the letters le, such as `table`, `little`, `cable`, `gen-tle` .
  - R-controlled syllables: syllables that have a vowel followed by the letter r, which changes the vowel sound, such as `car`, `her`, `bird`, `fur-ther` .
  - Vowel pair syllables: syllables that have two vowels together that make one sound, such as `rain`, `boat`, `pie`, `au-tumn` .
- Syllables can also be stressed or unstressed depending on the emphasis given to them when pronouncing a word. Stressed syllables are louder, longer, and higher in pitch than unstressed syllables  .
- The stress pattern of a word can affect its meaning and pronunciation. For example, `record` can be a noun or a verb depending on which syllable is stressed  .
- Syllables are important for oral communication and voice dynamics because they help to:
  - Divide words into smaller and manageable units for pronunciation and spelling .
  - Enhance the clarity, accuracy, and fluency of speech  .
  - Create variety, contrast, and emphasis in speech by using different stress patterns and intonation  .
  - Improve the comprehension and retention of the message by the listeners  .
