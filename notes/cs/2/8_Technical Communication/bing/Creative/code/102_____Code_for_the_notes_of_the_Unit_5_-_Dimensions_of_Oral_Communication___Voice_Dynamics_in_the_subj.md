# Unit 5 - Dimensions of Oral Communication & Voice Dynamics

## Code and Content
- Code is the system of symbols, signs, or words that convey a specific meaning or message.
- Content is the information or meaning that is encoded or decoded by the sender or receiver of the communication.
- Code and content are interrelated and interdependent in oral communication.
- The choice of code affects the clarity, accuracy, and effectiveness of the content.
- The content determines the appropriateness, suitability, and relevance of the code.

## Stimulus and Response
- Stimulus is any event, situation, or message that triggers a reaction or response from the receiver of the communication.
- Response is the reaction or feedback that the receiver gives to the stimulus.
- Stimulus and response are the basic elements of oral communication process.
- The stimulus can be verbal or non-verbal, intentional or unintentional, positive or negative, direct or indirect, etc.
- The response can be verbal or non-verbal, intentional or unintentional, positive or negative, direct or indirect, etc.
- The stimulus and response should be congruent, consistent, and coherent to avoid confusion, misunderstanding, or conflict.

## Encoding Process
- Encoding is the process of transforming the content or meaning into a code or symbol that can be transmitted and understood by the receiver of the communication.
- Encoding involves selecting the appropriate code, organizing the content, and adapting the message to the context, purpose, and audience of the communication.
- Encoding requires the sender to have the following skills:
  - Language skills: the ability to use the correct grammar, vocabulary, pronunciation, and tone of the code.
  - Cognitive skills: the ability to think critically, creatively, and logically about the content and the message.
  - Communication skills: the ability to use the appropriate verbal and non-verbal cues, gestures, and expressions to convey the message effectively.

## Decoding Process
- Decoding is the process of interpreting the code or symbol that is received from the sender of the communication.
- Decoding involves understanding the content, meaning, and intention of the message, and giving a response or feedback to the sender.
- Decoding requires the receiver to have the following skills:
  - Listening skills: the ability to pay attention, comprehend, and retain the message that is received.
  - Analytical skills: the ability to evaluate, infer, and synthesize the information and meaning of the message.
  - Response skills: the ability to give a relevant, appropriate, and constructive feedback or reaction to the message.

## Pronunciation
- Pronunciation is the way of producing and articulating the sounds of a language or code.
- Pronunciation is an important aspect of oral communication as it affects the intelligibility, clarity, and accuracy of the message.
- Pronunciation involves the following components:
  - Vowels: the sounds that are produced by the vibration of the vocal cords and the shape of the mouth and tongue.
  - Consonants: the sounds that are produced by the obstruction or interruption of the airflow by the lips, teeth, tongue, or palate.
  - Syllables: the units of sound that form a word or part of a word.
  - Stress: the emphasis or force that is given to a syllable or word in a sentence or phrase.
  - Intonation: the rise and fall of the pitch or tone of the voice in a sentence or phrase.
  - Rhythm: the pattern or flow of stressed and unstressed syllables in a sentence or phrase.

## Voice Dynamics
- Voice dynamics are the variations and modifications of the voice that are used to enhance the expression, emotion, and impact of the message in oral communication.
- Voice dynamics include the following elements:
  - Volume: the loudness or softness of the voice that indicates the intensity, confidence, and authority of the speaker.
  - Pitch: the highness or lowness of the voice that indicates the mood, attitude, and emotion of the speaker.
  - Rate: the speed or pace of the speech that indicates the urgency, excitement, or nervousness of the speaker.
  - Pause: the silence or break in the speech that indicates the emphasis, transition, or hesitation of the speaker.
  - Tone: the quality or character of the voice that indicates the personality, style, and sincerity of the speaker.
