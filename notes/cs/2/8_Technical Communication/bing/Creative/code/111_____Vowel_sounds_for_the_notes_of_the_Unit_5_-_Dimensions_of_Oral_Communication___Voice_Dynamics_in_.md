### Vowel sounds for the notes of the Unit 5 - Dimensions of Oral Communication & Voice Dynamics in the subject of Technical Communication

- Vowel sounds are created when air flows smoothly, without interruption, through the throat and mouth.
- Different vowel sounds are produced as a speaker changes the shape and placement of articulators (parts of the throat and mouth), such as the tongue, jaws and lips .
- In contrast, consonant sounds happen when the flow of air is obstructed or interrupted by the articulators .
- In English, the vowels are a, e, i, o, and u, although y can sometimes count as a vowel, too.
- Vowels are very important for speech clarity, as they carry the stress and intonation of words and sentences.
- Vowels are broadly divided into two categories: monophthongs (pure vowels) and diphthongs (vowel glides).
- Monophthongs are vowel sounds that have a single, steady quality, such as /i/ in "see" or /a/ in "cat".
- Diphthongs are vowel sounds that have a sliding or gliding quality, such as /aɪ/ in "eye" or /eɪ/ in "day".
- A vowel sound is described by four criteria: the height of the tongue, the front-back position of the tongue, the shape of the lips, and the length of the sound .
- For example, the vowel sound /i/ is high, front, unrounded, and short, while the vowel sound /u/ is high, back, rounded, and long.
- Vowel sounds can be represented by symbols from the International Phonetic Alphabet (IPA), which is a standardized system of notation for speech sounds.
- For example, the IPA symbol for /i/ is [i], while the IPA symbol for /u/ is [u].
- Learning the IPA symbols and the features of vowel sounds can help improve pronunciation and communication skills.
