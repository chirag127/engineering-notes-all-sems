### Style for the notes of the Unit 3 - Technical Presentation in the subject of Technical Communication

- Style is the way you express your ideas and information in a technical presentation. It includes the choice of words, sentences, tone, voice, and visuals.
- Style affects the clarity, accuracy, and effectiveness of your presentation. It also influences the audience's perception of your credibility, professionalism, and personality.
- To achieve a good style for your technical presentation, you should follow these guidelines:

  - Use clear and concise language. Avoid jargon, slang, vague terms, and unnecessary words. Choose words that are appropriate for your audience, purpose, and context.
  - Use active voice and strong verbs. Active voice shows who or what is performing the action, while passive voice hides the agent or makes it unclear. Strong verbs convey specific and precise meanings, while weak verbs are vague and general. For example, instead of saying "The results were obtained by the researchers", say "The researchers obtained the results".
  - Use parallel structure and consistent tense. Parallel structure means using the same grammatical form for items in a list or series. Consistent tense means using the same verb tense throughout your presentation. These techniques help you avoid confusion and maintain coherence. For example, instead of saying "The objectives of this project are to design, testing, and implement a new system", say "The objectives of this project are to design, test, and implement a new system".
  - Use transitions and signposts. Transitions are words or phrases that connect your ideas and show the relationship between them. Signposts are words or phrases that indicate the structure and direction of your presentation. These techniques help you guide your audience and keep them engaged. For example, you can use transitions like "however", "therefore", "in addition", and signposts like "first", "next", "finally", "in conclusion".
  - Use appropriate tone and voice. Tone is the attitude you convey towards your topic, audience, and purpose. Voice is the personality you project through your language. You should use a tone and voice that are respectful, confident, and professional. Avoid being too informal, casual, or humorous, unless it suits your purpose and audience. For example, instead of saying "This system is awesome and you guys are gonna love it", say "This system is innovative and beneficial for your organization".
  - Use effective visuals. Visuals are any images, graphs, charts, tables, or diagrams that support your presentation. They can help you illustrate, explain, or emphasize your points. You should use visuals that are relevant, clear, accurate, and attractive. Avoid using too many, too complex, or too distracting visuals. For example, instead of showing a cluttered and confusing graph, show a simple and neat graph with labels and captions.
