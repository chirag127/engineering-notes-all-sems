- Individual conferencing is a process of meeting one-on-one with your instructor or a peer to discuss your progress, challenges, and feedback on your technical presentation.
- Individual conferencing can help you to improve your presentation skills, clarify your doubts, address your concerns, and receive constructive criticism and suggestions for improvement.
- Individual conferencing can also help you to develop a rapport with your instructor or peer, and to gain confidence and motivation for your presentation.
- To make the most of your individual conferencing, you should follow these steps:

  - Prepare your presentation draft and outline before the conference. You should have a clear idea of your main message, purpose, audience, and structure of your presentation.
  - Bring your presentation materials and notes to the conference. You should have your slides, handouts, visuals, and any other supporting materials ready to show and explain to your instructor or peer.
  - Be open to feedback and criticism. You should listen attentively and respectfully to the comments and suggestions from your instructor or peer, and ask questions if you need clarification or examples.
  - Take notes and revise your presentation accordingly. You should write down the main points and action items from the conference, and use them to improve your presentation content, design, and delivery.
  - Follow up and thank your instructor or peer.
