# Unit 3 - Technical Presentation

## Introduction

- A technical presentation is a form of oral communication that conveys technical information to a specific audience, such as engineers, scientists, managers, or customers.
- A technical presentation has three main objectives: to inform, to persuade, or to instruct.
- A technical presentation requires careful planning, preparation, and practice to achieve its goals and to meet the expectations and needs of the audience.

## Planning a Technical Presentation

- The first step in planning a technical presentation is to analyze the purpose, the audience, and the context of the presentation.
- The purpose defines the main goal or message of the presentation, such as explaining a new product, proposing a solution, or demonstrating a procedure.
- The audience consists of the people who will listen to the presentation, and their characteristics, such as their background, knowledge, interests, and expectations.
- The context refers to the situation and environment in which the presentation will take place, such as the time, place, format, and equipment available.
- Based on the analysis of the purpose, the audience, and the context, the presenter should decide on the scope, the structure, and the style of the presentation.

## Preparing a Technical Presentation

- The second step in preparing a technical presentation is to develop the content, the visuals, and the delivery of the presentation.
- The content consists of the information and arguments that support the main message of the presentation, and should be organized in a clear and logical way, using an introduction, a body, and a conclusion.
- The visuals are the graphical elements that complement the content, such as charts, diagrams, tables, images, or videos, and should be designed to enhance the understanding and retention of the audience, using appropriate colors, fonts, sizes, and layouts.
- The delivery is the way the presenter communicates the content and the visuals to the audience, using verbal and nonverbal skills, such as voice, gestures, eye contact, and posture, and should be practiced to ensure fluency, confidence, and engagement.

## Delivering a Technical Presentation

- The third step in delivering a technical presentation is to perform the presentation in front of the audience, and to handle the feedback and the questions from the audience.
- The performance of the presentation should follow the plan and the preparation, and should adapt to the feedback and the reactions of the audience, using techniques such as pausing, emphasizing, summarizing, and transitioning.
- The feedback from the audience can be positive or negative, and should be acknowledged and appreciated by the presenter, using expressions such as thank you, I appreciate, or I agree.
- The questions from the audience can be clarifying or challenging, and should be answered by the presenter, using strategies such as repeating, rephrasing, elaborating, or redirecting.
