# C.V./Resume writing

- A C.V. (Curriculum Vitae) is a summary of your educational and professional background. It includes a full list of your publications, honors, awards, research, etc. It is usually used for academic, medical, research, and scientific applications .
- A resume is a brief document that highlights your relevant skills, achievements, and qualifications for a specific job. It is usually one or two pages long and tailored to the job you are applying to .
- The main difference between a C.V. and a resume is the length, purpose, and format. A C.V. is longer and more detailed, while a resume is shorter and more concise. A C.V. is used for academic and research positions, while a resume is used for other industries. A C.V. follows a chronological order, while a resume can use different formats, such as functional, chronological, or combination .
- To write a C.V., you should follow these steps:
  - Create a header with your contact information, such as your name, phone number, and email address.
  - Write a professional summary that highlights your main achievements, skills, and goals that make you a suitable candidate for the position you are applying for.
  - List your education in reverse chronological order, starting with your highest degree. Include the name of the institution, the degree, the field of study, and the dates of attendance. You can also include your GPA, honors, awards, scholarships, and relevant coursework.
  - List your work experience in reverse chronological order, starting with your most recent or current position. Include the name of the organization, the location, the title, and the dates of employment. Use bullet points to describe your main duties, responsibilities, and accomplishments. Use action verbs and quantify your results whenever possible.
  - List your publications, presentations, and research projects in reverse chronological order, starting with the most recent or relevant ones. Include the title, the authors, the date, the venue, and the URL if available. Use a consistent citation style, such as APA, MLA, or Chicago.
  - List your skills and competencies that are relevant to the position you are applying for. You can categorize them into hard skills, such as technical or language skills, and soft skills, such as communication or teamwork skills. Use keywords from the job description and provide examples of how you have used them in your previous work or education.
  - List your honors, awards, and grants that you have received for your academic or professional achievements. Include the name, the date, the organization, and the criteria for the award or grant.
  - List your professional affiliations and memberships that are relevant to your field or industry. Include the name of the organization, the position or role, and the dates of involvement.
  - List your references or provide a statement that they are available upon request. You can include the name, the title, the affiliation, the phone number, and the email address of each reference. Make sure to ask for their permission before listing them and inform them about the position you are applying for.
- To write a resume, you should follow these steps:
  - Create a header with your contact information, such as your name, phone number, and email address.
  - Write a resume summary or objective that summarizes your main qualifications, skills, and goals that make you a suitable candidate for the position you are applying for. A resume summary is for candidates with relevant work experience, while a resume objective is for candidates with little or no work experience.

Include the name of the organization, the location, the title, and the dates of employment. Use bullet points to describe your main duties, responsibilities, and achievements. Use action verbs and quantify your results whenever possible. Focus on the most relevant and impressive aspects of your work experience for the job you are applying for.
