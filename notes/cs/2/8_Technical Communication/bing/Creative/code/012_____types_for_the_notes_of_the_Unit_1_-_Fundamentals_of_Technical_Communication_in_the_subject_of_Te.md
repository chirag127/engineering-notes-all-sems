### Types of Notes

- Notes are brief records of information that you gather from various sources, such as books, articles, lectures, interviews, etc.
- Notes can help you to organize, summarize, and remember the main points of your sources, as well as to develop your own ideas and arguments.
- There are different types of notes that you can use for different purposes, such as:

  - **Outlines**: Outlines are hierarchical structures that show the main topics and subtopics of a source or a project. Outlines can help you to plan and structure your writing, as well as to identify the main ideas and supporting details of your sources. Outlines can use symbols, numbers, letters, or indentation to indicate the levels of hierarchy. For example:

    ```
    I. Introduction
      A. Background
      B. Thesis statement
    II. Body
      A. First main point
        1. Supporting detail
        2. Supporting detail
      B. Second main point
        1. Supporting detail
        2. Supporting detail
    III. Conclusion
      A. Summary
      B. Recommendations
    ```

  - **Summaries**: Summaries are concise overviews of the main points and conclusions of a source or a project. Summaries can help you to review and recall the essential information of your sources, as well as to synthesize and integrate your sources into your own writing. Summaries can use your own words or direct quotations, but they should always cite the source and avoid plagiarism. For example:

    ```
    According to Smith (2020), technical communication is the process of conveying information to a specific audience for a specific purpose. Smith argues that technical communication requires clear, concise, and accurate language, as well as attention to the needs and expectations of the audience. Smith also discusses the various types, modes, and principles of technical communication, and provides examples and guidelines for effective communication in different contexts and situations. (Smith, 2020, p. 1-10)
    ```

  - **Paraphrases**: Paraphrases are restatements of the ideas or information of a source in your own words. Paraphrases can help you to understand and interpret your sources, as well as to avoid plagiarism and demonstrate your critical thinking skills. Paraphrases should be similar in length and tone to the original source, but they should use different words and sentence structures. Paraphrases should also cite the source and indicate the page number where the information can be found. For example:

    ```

He claims that technical communication requires using language that is clear, concise, and accurate, as well as considering the needs and expectations of the audience. He also explores the different types, modes, and principles of technical communication, and gives examples and guidelines for effective communication in various contexts and situations. (p. 1-10)
    ```

  - **Quotations**: Quotations are exact reproductions of the words of a source. Quotations can help you to support or illustrate your points, as well as to show respect for the authority or expertise of the source. Quotations should be used sparingly and selectively, and they should be integrated into your own sentences with proper punctuation and citation. Quotations should also indicate the page number where the words can be found. For example:

    ```

    ```
