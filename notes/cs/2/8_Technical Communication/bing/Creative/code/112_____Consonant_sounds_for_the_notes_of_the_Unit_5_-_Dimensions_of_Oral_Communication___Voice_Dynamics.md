### Consonant sounds for the notes of the Unit 5 - Dimensions of Oral Communication & Voice Dynamics in the subject of Technical Communication

- Consonant sounds are speech sounds that are produced by partially or completely blocking the airflow through the vocal tract  .
- Consonants are represented by letters of the alphabet that correspond to those sounds, such as B, T, F, Z, etc. However, some letters can represent more than one consonant sound, such as C, which can be pronounced as /k/ or /s/.
- Consonant sounds can be classified according to different criteria, such as the place of articulation, the manner of articulation, and the voicing of the sound.
- The place of articulation refers to the location of the obstruction in the vocal tract, such as the lips, the teeth, the alveolar ridge, the palate, the velum, etc. For example, the consonant sound /p/ is a bilabial sound, meaning that it is produced by bringing both lips together.
- The manner of articulation refers to the type of obstruction in the vocal tract, such as a complete closure, a narrow opening, a friction, a vibration, etc. For example, the consonant sound /t/ is a plosive sound, meaning that it is produced by a sudden release of air after a complete closure.
- The voicing of the sound refers to the presence or absence of vocal cord vibration during the production of the sound. For example, the consonant sound /b/ is a voiced sound, meaning that the vocal cords vibrate when producing it, while the consonant sound /p/ is a voiceless sound, meaning that the vocal cords do not vibrate when producing it.
- Consonant sounds are important for oral communication and voice dynamics, as they contribute to the clarity, accuracy, and expressiveness of speech. Consonant sounds can also affect the meaning, tone, and mood of the message, as well as the impression and perception of the speaker.
- Some tips for improving the pronunciation and articulation of consonant sounds are:

  - Pay attention to the spelling and the sound of the words, and learn the rules and exceptions for the pronunciation of consonants in different contexts.
  - Practice the minimal pairs of consonant sounds, which are words that differ only by one consonant sound, such as bit and pit, or thin and sin. This can help to distinguish and produce the sounds more accurately.
  - Use a mirror, a recorder, or a feedback tool to monitor and correct the mouth movements, the airflow, and the sound quality of the consonant sounds.
  - Listen to native speakers or models of the target language, and imitate their pronunciation and intonation of consonant sounds.
  - Seek feedback from a teacher, a tutor, or a peer, and work on the areas that need improvement.
