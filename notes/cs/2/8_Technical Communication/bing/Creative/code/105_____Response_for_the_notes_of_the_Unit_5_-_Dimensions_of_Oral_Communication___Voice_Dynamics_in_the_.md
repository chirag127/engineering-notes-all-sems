### Response for the notes of the Unit 5 - Dimensions of Oral Communication & Voice Dynamics in the subject of Technical Communication

- Oral communication is the process of expressing information or ideas by word of mouth.
- Oral communication can be formal or informal, depending on the context and purpose of the communication.
- Oral communication has several dimensions, such as:

  - Content: the message or information that is conveyed by the speaker to the listener.
  - Context: the situation, environment, or background that influences the communication process.
  - Channel: the medium or mode of communication, such as face-to-face, telephone, video conference, etc.
  - Sender: the person who initiates the communication and encodes the message.
  - Receiver: the person who receives the communication and decodes the message.
  - Feedback: the response or reaction of the receiver to the message of the sender.
  - Noise: any disturbance or interference that affects the quality or clarity of the communication.

- Oral communication requires effective use of voice dynamics, such as:

  - Volume: the loudness or softness of the voice, which should be appropriate for the audience and the setting.
  - Pitch: the highness or lowness of the voice, which should vary to convey emotions, emphasis, and interest.
  - Rate: the speed or pace of the speech, which should be neither too fast nor too slow, and should match the content and the listener's comprehension level.
  - Pause: the silence or break in the speech, which should be used to create emphasis, suspense, or transition.
  - Articulation: the clarity and accuracy of the pronunciation of words, which should avoid mumbling, slurring, or mispronouncing.
  - Fluency: the smoothness and continuity of the speech, which should avoid hesitations, fillers, or repetitions.
  - Intonation: the rise and fall of the voice, which should convey the meaning, mood, and attitude of the speaker.
  - Stress: the emphasis or accent on certain words or syllables, which should highlight the important or key points of the message.
  - Modulation: the variation or change of the voice dynamics, which should create interest, variety, and expression in the speech.
