### Seminar/Conferences Presentation Skills

Seminar and conference presentations are important ways of communicating your research and ideas to a wider audience. They can also help you develop your confidence, networking skills and professional reputation. However, presenting at a seminar or conference can also be challenging and stressful, especially if you are not well prepared or experienced. Here are some tips to help you improve your seminar and conference presentation skills:

- **Don't touch that slide deck just yet.** Before you start creating your slides, you need to have a clear idea of what you want to say and how you want to structure your presentation. Think about your main message, your key points, your evidence and your conclusion. Write an outline or a script for your presentation and review it carefully. Make sure it is coherent, logical and concise.
- **Build your presentation within time constraints.** One of the most common mistakes presenters make is to go over their allotted time. This can annoy the audience, the organizers and the other speakers. It can also make you look unprofessional and unprepared. To avoid this, you need to plan your presentation according to the time limit and practice it several times. Use a timer or a stopwatch to keep track of your pace and adjust it if necessary. Leave some time for questions and answers at the end. 
- **Use visuals to illuminate, not obscure.** Slides can be a powerful tool to enhance your presentation, but they can also be a distraction or a hindrance if they are poorly designed or used. To make effective slides, you need to follow some basic principles: use simple and consistent layouts, fonts, colors and animations; use high-quality images, graphs, charts and diagrams; use bullet points, keywords and phrases, not full sentences or paragraphs; use transitions and effects sparingly and only when they add value; avoid clutter, noise and irrelevant information.  
- **Aim for simplicity and consistency.** Your presentation should be easy to follow and understand by your audience. To achieve this, you need to use simple and clear language, avoid jargon, acronyms and technical terms, or explain them if you have to use them. You also need to use consistent terminology, definitions and units throughout your presentation. You should also repeat your main message and key points at the beginning and at the end of your presentation, and use signposts and transitions to guide your audience through your structure.  
- **Know your research audience.** Your presentation should be tailored to the specific audience you are addressing. You need to consider their background, interests, expectations and level of expertise. You should also anticipate their questions, objections and feedback, and prepare your answers accordingly. You should also try to engage your audience by making eye contact, using gestures, modulating your voice, asking questions, telling stories, using humor and showing enthusiasm.   
- **Rehearse your presentation.** One of the best ways to improve your presentation skills is to practice your presentation several times before the actual event. You can practice alone, in front of a mirror, a camera, a recorder or a computer. You can also practice with a friend, a colleague, a mentor or a coach. You can ask for feedback and suggestions, and incorporate them into your presentation. You should also practice in a similar setting and with similar equipment as the one you will use at the seminar or conference.   
- **Prepare, prepare, prepare.** Besides rehearsing your presentation, you also need to prepare for other aspects of your seminar or conference experience. You need to check the venue, the schedule, the format, the equipment and the rules of the event. You need to bring copies of your slides, handouts, notes and references. You need to dress appropriately and professionally. You need to arrive early, check the room, test the equipment and greet the organizers and the audience. You need to be ready for any unexpected situations or problems.
