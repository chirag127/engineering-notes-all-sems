### The flow of Communication

The flow of communication refers to the direction and channels of communication within an organization or between different parties. Communication can be classified into five main types according to the flow of information:

- **Downward communication**: This is communication that flows from the higher levels of an organization to the lower levels, or down the chain of command. For example, a manager may communicate policies, instructions, feedback, or announcements to their subordinates. Downward communication is often used to inform, instruct, or persuade employees. 
- **Upward communication**: This is communication that flows from the lower levels of an organization to the higher levels, or up the chain of command. For example, an employee may communicate suggestions, complaints, reports, or requests to their manager. Upward communication is often used to provide feedback, seek guidance, or express opinions. 
- **Lateral communication**: This is communication that flows among the same level of an organization, or across the chain of command. For example, colleagues may communicate with each other to coordinate tasks, share information, or solve problems. Lateral communication is often used to facilitate collaboration, cooperation, or innovation. 
- **Diagonal communication**: This is communication that flows across different levels and functions of an organization, or across the chain of command. For example, a salesperson may communicate with an engineer to clarify a technical issue, or a project manager may communicate with a senior executive to report on the progress of a project. Diagonal communication is often used to improve efficiency, quality, or customer satisfaction. 
- **External communication**: This is communication that flows between an organization and its external stakeholders, such as customers, suppliers, investors, media, or regulators. For example, a company may communicate its products, services, values, or achievements to the public, or receive feedback, complaints, or inquiries from the customers. External communication is often used to promote, persuade, or maintain relationships. 

The flow of communication is influenced by various factors, such as the organizational structure, culture, technology, and communication skills. Effective communication can enhance the performance, productivity, and satisfaction of the organization and its members.  

Technical communication is a type of communication that involves conveying complex, technical, or specialized information to a specific audience for a specific purpose. Technical communication often involves technology (though not always), and it may use various modes of communication, such as text, image, video, sound, etc. Technical communication responds to a specific rhetorical situation, which means that it considers the goals, audience, context, and constraints of the communication. Technical communication is dependent upon context, which means that it adapts to the situation and the needs of the audience. Technical communication is often used to inform, instruct, explain, or persuade.    

The flow of communication and technical communication are related in several ways. For example:

- Technical communication can occur in any of the five types of communication flow, depending on the purpose, audience, and context of the communication. For instance, a technical writer may produce a user manual for a product (downward communication), a software developer may submit a bug report to their supervisor (upward communication), a graphic designer may collaborate with a web developer on a website (lateral communication), a quality analyst may consult with a customer service representative on a customer issue (diagonal communication), or a marketing manager may create a brochure for a potential client (external communication).
- Technical communication can affect the flow of communication within an organization or between different parties. For example, technical communication can improve the flow of communication by providing clear, accurate, and consistent information, or it can hinder the flow of communication by creating confusion, misunderstanding, or errors.
- Technical communication can be influenced by the flow of communication within an organization or between different parties. For example, the flow of communication can affect the content, style, format, and delivery of technical communication, depending on the expectations, preferences, and feedback of the communicators and the receivers.   

Therefore, technical communicators should be aware of the flow of communication and its implications for their work. They should also be able to use appropriate communication strategies and skills to communicate effectively in different situations and with different audiences.
