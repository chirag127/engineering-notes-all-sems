### Reading and Comprehension

- Reading is the process of decoding written symbols and extracting meaning from them.
- Comprehension is the ability to understand and interpret what is read.
- Reading and comprehension are essential skills for technical communication, as they enable you to access, analyze, and evaluate information from various sources and formats.
- Reading and comprehension involve different levels of cognitive processes, such as:
  - Recognition: identifying the words and their meanings.
  - Interpretation: inferring the main idea, purpose, tone, and context of the text.
  - Application: applying the information to a specific situation or problem.
  - Evaluation: judging the quality, relevance, accuracy, and credibility of the text.
  - Synthesis: integrating and summarizing the information from multiple texts.
- Reading and comprehension strategies are techniques that can help you improve your reading skills and comprehension level, such as:
  - Previewing: skimming the text to get an overview of its structure, content, and style.
  - Predicting: making guesses about what the text will say based on the title, headings, keywords, and prior knowledge.
  - Questioning: generating questions before, during, and after reading to guide your attention and check your understanding.
  - Clarifying: identifying and resolving any confusion or difficulty you encounter while reading, such as unfamiliar words, concepts, or references.
  - Summarizing: condensing the main points and supporting details of the text in your own words.
  - Paraphrasing: restating the text in your own words, using different words and sentence structures.
  - Outlining: organizing the text into a hierarchical structure of main ideas and subtopics.
  - Mapping: representing the text visually using diagrams, charts, graphs, or tables.
  - Annotating: making notes on the text to highlight important information, express your thoughts, or mark questions.
  - Reviewing: revising and consolidating what you have read to enhance your memory and retention.
