### Syllables for the notes of the Unit 5 - Dimensions of Oral Communication & Voice Dynamics in the subject of Technical Communication

- A syllable is a unit of sound that forms a word or a part of a word. It usually consists of a vowel sound and one or more consonant sounds. For example, the word "communication" has five syllables: com-mu-ni-ca-tion.
- Syllables are important for oral communication because they affect the pronunciation, stress, and rhythm of speech. Pronunciation is the way a word or a sound is spoken. Stress is the emphasis given to a syllable or a word. Rhythm is the pattern of stressed and unstressed syllables in a sentence or a phrase.
- To pronounce a word correctly, one needs to know how many syllables it has and which syllable is stressed. For example, the word "technical" has three syllables: tech-ni-cal. The first syllable is stressed, so it is pronounced louder and longer than the other two syllables. The word "communication" has five syllables: com-mu-ni-ca-tion. The third syllable is stressed, so it is pronounced louder and longer than the other four syllables.
- To create a natural and fluent rhythm in speech, one needs to vary the length and stress of syllables. Generally, stressed syllables are longer and louder than unstressed syllables. For example, in the sentence "Oral communication is the verbal and visual transmission of information from one person to another", the stressed syllables are: oral, com, ver, vis, trans, in, per, and an. The unstressed syllables are: -al, -mu, -ni, -ca, -tion, is, the, -bal, and, -ual, -mis, -sion, of, -for, -ma, -tion, from, one, -son, to, -oth, and -er. The stressed syllables create a rhythm that helps the listener follow the meaning and the structure of the sentence.
- To improve one's oral communication skills, one should practice identifying and pronouncing syllables correctly. One can use a dictionary or an online tool to check the syllable division and stress of a word. One can also listen to native speakers and imitate their pronunciation and rhythm. One can also read aloud texts that have different types of syllables and words, such as poems, speeches, and dialogues.
