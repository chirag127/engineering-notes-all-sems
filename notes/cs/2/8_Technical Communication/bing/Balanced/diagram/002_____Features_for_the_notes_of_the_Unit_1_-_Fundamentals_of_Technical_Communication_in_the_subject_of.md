### Features of Technical Communication

Technical communication is a form of communication that involves conveying technical or specialized information to a specific audience for a specific purpose. Some of the features of technical communication are:

- **Clarity**: It is important to use language and terms that are appropriate for the audience and the context. Clarity means avoiding ambiguity, jargon, and unnecessary details that may confuse or distract the reader. Clarity also means organizing the information in a logical and coherent way, using headings, lists, tables, and other visual aids to enhance understanding.   
- **Conciseness**: Information or details should not be sacrificed for the sake of brevity, but technical communication should be concise and to the point. Conciseness means eliminating redundant, irrelevant, or wordy expressions that may obscure the main message or waste the reader's time. Conciseness also means using precise and specific words that convey the intended meaning without ambiguity.   
- **Objectivity**: Objectivity, unbiased opinions, and factual information are essential for technical communication. Objectivity means avoiding personal or emotional language, assumptions, biases, or judgments that may affect the credibility or accuracy of the information. Objectivity also means supporting the claims and arguments with evidence, data, examples, and references from reliable and authoritative sources.   
- **Detail-oriented**: Often, technical communication contains complex or technical information that requires attention to detail and accuracy. Detail-oriented means checking and verifying the information for correctness, completeness, and consistency. Detail-oriented also means following the standards and conventions of the field, such as formatting, style, grammar, spelling, and citation.   
- **Use of visuals**: Visuals, such as graphs, charts, diagrams, images, and videos, can enhance technical communication by providing additional or alternative ways of presenting information. Use of visuals means selecting and creating visuals that are relevant, clear, accurate, and consistent with the text. Use of visuals also means integrating and labeling the visuals properly, and explaining or interpreting them for the reader.    
- **Audience-specific**: Technical communication is tailored to the needs, expectations, and background of the intended audience. Audience-specific means analyzing and understanding the audience's characteristics, such as their level of knowledge, interest, attitude, and purpose. Audience-specific also means adapting the tone, style, content, and format of the communication to suit the audience's preferences and goals.    
- **Compliance with standards and regulations**: Technical communication often follows specific standards and regulations that are set by the field, the organization, or the law. Compliance with standards and regulations means adhering to the rules and guidelines that govern the communication, such as ethical, legal, professional, or technical requirements. Compliance with standards and regulations also means acknowledging and respecting the rights and responsibilities of the communicators and the audience, such as intellectual property, privacy, confidentiality, and safety.
