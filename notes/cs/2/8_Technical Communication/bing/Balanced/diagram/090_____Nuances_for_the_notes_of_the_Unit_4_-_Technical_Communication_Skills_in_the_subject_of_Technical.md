### Nuances for the notes of the Unit 4 - Technical Communication Skills in the subject of Technical Communication

Technical communication is a type of communication that is aimed at providing readers with clear and accurate information on how to use and maintain products, complete tasks, operate equipment, etc.

Technical communication skills are the abilities and knowledge that enable a technical communicator to perform their tasks effectively and efficiently. Some of the most important skills of a technical communicator are:

- Writing skills: Technical communicators need to write in a clear, concise, and coherent manner, using appropriate grammar, vocabulary, tone, and style. They also need to adapt their writing to different audiences, purposes, and contexts, and follow the standards and guidelines of their field and organization.
- Technical skills: Technical communicators need to have a good understanding of the technical or specialized topics they are communicating about, such as computer applications, medical procedures, or environmental regulations. They also need to be able to research, analyze, and synthesize information from various sources, and use relevant terminology, symbols, and formats.
- Oral communication: Technical communicators not only write manuals and instructions, but they may also tell the audience about a product or service, present their findings or proposals, or conduct training sessions. They need to speak in a clear, confident, and engaging manner, using appropriate language, gestures, and visual aids. They also need to listen actively, respond to questions, and handle feedback.
- Software tools: Technical communicators need to be proficient in using various software tools that help them create, edit, and publish their documents, such as word processors, graphic editors, content management systems, or web design tools. They also need to be familiar with the features and functions of the products or services they are communicating about, and be able to test and troubleshoot them.
- Computer languages: Technical communicators may need to know how to use one or more computer languages, such as HTML, CSS, JavaScript, or XML, to create web pages, interactive applications, or structured data. They also need to be able to read and understand code, scripts, or commands, and use them to perform tasks or solve problems.
- Scientific knowledge: Technical communicators may need to have a basic or advanced knowledge of science, mathematics, engineering, or other disciplines that are related to their field or industry. They need to be able to apply scientific principles, methods, and concepts to their work, and use appropriate units, measurements, formulas, or equations.
- Problem-solving skills: Technical communicators need to be able to identify, define, and solve problems that arise in their work, such as technical errors, information gaps, or user feedback. They need to be able to think critically, creatively, and logically, and use various strategies and techniques to find solutions.
- Self-learning skills: Technical communicators need to be able to learn new skills, knowledge, or technologies on their own, as their field or industry is constantly changing and evolving. They need to be curious, motivated, and resourceful, and use various sources and methods to acquire and update their information.
