### Pronunciation
- Pronunciation is the way a word or a language is spoken, or the manner in which someone utters a word.
- Pronunciation is one of the dimensions of oral communication that affects the clarity, accuracy and effectiveness of the message.
- Pronunciation is influenced by various factors, such as the speaker's native language, regional accent, education, social class, age, gender, etc.
- Pronunciation can be improved by learning the rules of phonetics, which is the study of the sounds of human speech.
- Pronunciation can also be improved by listening to native speakers, practicing with a partner, recording and analyzing one's own speech, using online tools and resources, etc.

Some of the aspects of pronunciation that are important for oral communication are:

- Vowels and consonants: Vowels are the sounds that are produced with an open vocal tract, such as /a/, /e/, /i/, /o/, /u/. Consonants are the sounds that are produced with a partial or complete closure of the vocal tract, such as /p/, /t/, /k/, /s/, /m/, /n/, etc.
- Syllables and stress: Syllables are the units of sound that make up a word, such as cat (one syllable), water (two syllables), computer (three syllables), etc. Stress is the emphasis or force given to a syllable or a word in a sentence, such as CONtent (noun) vs conTENT (adjective), or I like CHOColate (verb) vs I like chocolate (noun).
- Intonation and pitch: Intonation is the variation of the tone or pitch of the voice in a sentence, which can convey different meanings or emotions, such as a rising intonation for a question, a falling intonation for a statement, a high pitch for excitement, a low pitch for boredom, etc.
- Rhythm and pause: Rhythm is the pattern of stressed and unstressed syllables in a sentence, which can create a natural and smooth flow of speech, such as baNAna, comPUter, etc. Pause is the brief silence between words or sentences, which can indicate a change of topic, a hesitation, a punctuation, etc.
- Articulation and clarity: Articulation is the way the speaker produces the sounds of a language, which can affect the clarity and intelligibility of the speech, such as pronouncing the /th/ sound as /f/ or /v/, or the /r/ sound as /l/ or /w/, etc. Clarity is the quality of being clear and easy to understand, which can be achieved by speaking at a moderate speed, using appropriate vocabulary, avoiding filler words, etc.
