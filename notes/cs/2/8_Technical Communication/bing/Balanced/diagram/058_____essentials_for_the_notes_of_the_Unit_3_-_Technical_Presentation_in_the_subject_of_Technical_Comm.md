### Essentials for the notes of the Unit 3 - Technical Presentation

- The purpose of a technical presentation is to communicate technical information to a specific audience, such as experts, managers, customers, or the general public.
- A technical presentation should have a clear structure, consisting of an introduction, a body, and a conclusion.
- The introduction should capture the attention of the audience, establish the context and relevance of the topic, state the main objective and scope of the presentation, and provide an overview of the main points.
- The body should present the main points in a logical and coherent order, using appropriate transitions, headings, and subheadings. The body should also support the main points with evidence, such as facts, data, examples, diagrams, graphs, tables, or images.
- The conclusion should summarize the main points, restate the objective and scope of the presentation, and provide a call to action or a recommendation for the audience.
- A technical presentation should use clear and concise language, avoiding jargon, slang, or unnecessary words. The presentation should also use appropriate tone, style, and voice, depending on the audience, purpose, and context of the presentation.
- A technical presentation should use effective visual aids, such as slides, posters, handouts, or videos, to enhance the verbal message and to appeal to different learning styles of the audience. The visual aids should be relevant, accurate, consistent, simple, and attractive.
- A technical presentation should be rehearsed and practiced before the actual delivery, to improve the confidence, fluency, and timing of the presenter. The presenter should also prepare for possible questions and feedback from the audience, and respond to them politely and professionally.
