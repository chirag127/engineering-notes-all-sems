### Strategic competence
- Strategic competence is the ability to use various verbal and nonverbal strategies to communicate effectively and overcome difficulties when communication breakdowns occur .
- Strategic competence performs assessment, planning, and execution functions in determining the most effective means of achieving a communicative or language learning goal .
- Strategic competence connects declarative, procedural, and conditional knowledge .
  - Declarative knowledge is the knowledge of facts, concepts, and rules.
  - Procedural knowledge is the knowledge of how to perform tasks and skills.
  - Conditional knowledge is the knowledge of when, where, and why to apply declarative and procedural knowledge.
- Strategic competence is an aspect of communicative competence, which is the ability to use language appropriately in different contexts and situations .
- Strategic competence is important for technical communication because it helps communicators to:
  - Adapt to different audiences, purposes, and situations.
  - Use appropriate language, tone, and style.
  - Organize and present information clearly and logically.
  - Avoid or resolve misunderstandings and ambiguities.
  - Initiate innovation and bring strategic change.
- Some examples of strategic competence in technical communication are:
  - Using paraphrasing, summarizing, or examples to explain complex or unfamiliar concepts.
  - Asking for clarification, confirmation, or feedback to ensure mutual understanding.
  - Using gestures, diagrams, or visuals to support verbal communication.
  - Using synonyms, antonyms, or definitions to deal with vocabulary gaps.
  - Using humor, politeness, or empathy to establish rapport and trust.
