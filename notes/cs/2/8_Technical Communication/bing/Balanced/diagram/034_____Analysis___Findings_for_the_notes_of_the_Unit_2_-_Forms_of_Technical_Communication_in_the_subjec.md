- Analysis and findings are the results of a technical investigation or research that answer the questions or objectives of the study.
- Analysis and findings can be presented in different forms of technical communication, such as reports, memos, proposals, presentations, articles, etc.
- Analysis and findings should be clear, accurate, relevant, and well-organized to convey the information effectively to the intended audience.
- Analysis and findings should include the following elements:

  - Introduction: This section provides the background, context, purpose, and scope of the study. It also states the main question or problem, the objectives, and the methods of the study.
  - Data: This section presents the raw or processed data that was collected or generated during the study. It can include tables, charts, graphs, diagrams, maps, etc. to illustrate the data visually.
  - Analysis: This section explains how the data was analyzed, interpreted, and evaluated to answer the question or problem of the study. It can include calculations, formulas, statistics, models, etc. to support the analysis.
  - Findings: This section summarizes the main results or outcomes of the analysis. It answers the question or problem of the study and shows how the objectives were met. It can also include implications, limitations, and recommendations for further research or action.
  - Conclusion: This section restates the main question or problem, the objectives, and the methods of the study. It also summarizes the main findings and their significance. It can also provide a brief overview of the main points of the report or document.

- Analysis and findings should follow the guidelines and conventions of the specific form of technical communication, such as the format, style, tone, language, and citation of sources.
