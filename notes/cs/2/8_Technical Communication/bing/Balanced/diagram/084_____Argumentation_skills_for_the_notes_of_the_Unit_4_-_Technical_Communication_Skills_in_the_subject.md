### Argumentation skills for the notes of the Unit 4 - Technical Communication Skills

- Argumentation skills are the abilities to develop and present arguments in a clear, logical and persuasive way.
- Arguments are claims supported by reasons and evidence that aim to convince others of a point of view, a decision or a course of action.
- Argumentation skills are important for technical communication because they help to:
  - Analyze complex problems and propose solutions
  - Communicate technical information to different audiences and contexts
  - Evaluate the validity and reliability of sources and data
  - Critically examine assumptions and biases
  - Collaborate with others and resolve conflicts
- Some key aspects of argumentation skills are:
  - Style: The choice of words, tone, structure and format of the argument. Style should be appropriate for the purpose, audience and medium of the communication.
  - Devices: The use of rhetorical strategies, such as ethos, pathos and logos, to appeal to the credibility, emotions and logic of the audience.
  - Analysis: The process of breaking down the argument into its components, such as claim, reasons, evidence, counterarguments and rebuttals, and evaluating their strength and relevance.
  - Cohesion and Emphasis: The use of transitions, connectors, repetition and highlighting to create a coherent and focused argument that guides the audience's attention and understanding.
  - Critical Thinking: The ability to think independently, creatively and systematically, and to question, challenge and refine one's own and others' arguments.
  - Nuances: The recognition and acknowledgement of the complexity, uncertainty and diversity of perspectives involved in the argument, and the use of qualifiers, concessions and reservations to express them.
  - Exposition, Narration and Description: The use of different modes of writing to explain, illustrate and support the argument, depending on the context and purpose of the communication.
