## Unit 2 - Forms of Technical Communication

Technical communication is the process of conveying information in a clear, concise, and accurate manner to a specific audience for a specific purpose. Technical communication can take various forms, such as:

- **Written communication**: This includes documents such as reports, manuals, proposals, memos, emails, letters, etc. Written communication is used to inform, instruct, persuade, or document technical information.
- **Oral communication**: This includes presentations, speeches, lectures, interviews, meetings, etc. Oral communication is used to explain, demonstrate, or discuss technical information with an audience.
- **Visual communication**: This includes graphics, diagrams, charts, tables, illustrations, photos, videos, etc. Visual communication is used to supplement or replace verbal or written communication by showing technical information in a visual form.
- **Digital communication**: This includes websites, blogs, podcasts, social media, online forums, etc. Digital communication is used to create, share, or access technical information using digital technologies and platforms.

Each form of technical communication has its own advantages and disadvantages, depending on the context, purpose, audience, and medium of communication. Technical communicators should be aware of the characteristics, strengths, and limitations of each form and choose the most appropriate one for their communication goals.
