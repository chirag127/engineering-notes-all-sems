### Discourse competence for the notes of the Unit 4 - Technical Communication Skills in the subject of Technical Communication

- Discourse competence is the ability to understand and express oneself in a given language, combining grammatical forms and meaning to achieve a linked text (oral or written), in different communication situations .
- Discourse competence is one of the components of communicative competence, which also includes linguistic, sociolinguistic, strategic, and intercultural competence .
- Discourse competence involves the following skills and knowledge:
  - Textual competence: the ability to read and comprehend different types of texts, such as narratives, descriptions, arguments, explanations, etc. and to produce coherent and cohesive texts of one's own.
  - Rhetorical competence: the ability to use appropriate language and strategies to achieve one's communicative goals, such as persuading, informing, entertaining, etc. and to adapt to the audience, purpose, and context of communication.
  - Genre competence: the ability to recognize and use the conventions and features of different genres of communication, such as reports, letters, emails, presentations, etc. and to follow the norms and expectations of different discourse communities.
  - Lexical competence: the ability to use a range of vocabulary and expressions that are suitable for the topic, level, and style of communication and to avoid ambiguity, redundancy, and confusion.
- Discourse competence is important for technical communication because it enables the communicator to:
  - Produce clear, concise, and accurate texts that convey the intended message and information to the target audience.
  - Use appropriate tone, style, and format for different types of technical documents, such as manuals, proposals, instructions, etc. and to follow the standards and guidelines of the field or organization.
  - Organize and structure the content of the texts in a logical and coherent way, using transitions, headings, lists, tables, graphs, etc. to enhance readability and comprehension.
  - Use effective rhetorical strategies to persuade, inform, or instruct the audience, such as establishing credibility, providing evidence, addressing objections, using examples, etc. and to avoid fallacies, biases, and errors.
  - Choose and use the appropriate vocabulary and expressions for the technical topic, level, and audience, and to explain or define any unfamiliar terms, acronyms, or symbols.
