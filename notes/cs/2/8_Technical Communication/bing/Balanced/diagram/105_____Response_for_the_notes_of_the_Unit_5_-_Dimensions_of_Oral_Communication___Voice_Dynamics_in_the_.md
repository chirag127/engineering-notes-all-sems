### Response for the notes of the Unit 5 - Dimensions of Oral Communication & Voice Dynamics in the subject of Technical Communication

- Oral communication is the process of expressing information or ideas by word of mouth.
- Oral communication can be formal or informal, depending on the context and purpose of the message.
- Oral communication has several dimensions, such as:

  - Content: the information, facts, opinions, or arguments that are conveyed in the message.
  - Context: the situation, environment, or background that influences the message and its interpretation.
  - Channel: the medium or mode of delivery of the message, such as face-to-face, phone, video, or audio.
  - Receiver: the person or group who listens to or views the message and responds to it.
  - Feedback: the response or reaction of the receiver to the message, which can be verbal or non-verbal.
  - Noise: any factor that interferes with or distorts the message or its reception, such as physical, psychological, or semantic noise.
  - Purpose: the goal or objective of the message, such as to inform, persuade, instruct, or entertain.

- Oral communication requires certain skills and strategies, such as:

  - Planning: the process of identifying the purpose, audience, content, and channel of the message, and organizing the information in a logical and coherent manner.
  - Delivery: the process of presenting the message in a clear, confident, and engaging manner, using appropriate language, tone, pitch, volume, pace, and body language.
  - Listening: the process of paying attention to the message, understanding its meaning, and providing feedback to the speaker.
  - Adapting: the process of adjusting the message or the delivery according to the feedback, the context, or the situation.

- Voice dynamics are the variations in the quality and characteristics of the voice, such as:

  - Tone: the attitude or emotion that is expressed by the voice, such as friendly, angry, sarcastic, or enthusiastic.
  - Pitch: the highness or lowness of the voice, which can indicate the level of interest, excitement, or confidence of the speaker.
  - Volume: the loudness or softness of the voice, which can indicate the level of emphasis, importance, or authority of the message.
  - Pace: the speed or rate of the speech, which can indicate the level of urgency, clarity, or nervousness of the speaker.
  - Pause: the silence or gap between words or sentences, which can indicate the level of thoughtfulness, hesitation, or emphasis of the speaker.
  - Stress: the degree of force or emphasis on certain words or syllables, which can indicate the level of contrast, importance, or meaning of the message.

- Voice dynamics can enhance or hinder the effectiveness of oral communication, depending on how they are used and perceived by the receiver.
- Voice dynamics can be improved by practicing, recording, and evaluating the speech, and by seeking feedback from others.
