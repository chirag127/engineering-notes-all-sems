# Methods for the notes of the Unit 2 - Forms of Technical Communication in the subject of Technical Communication

- The unit 2 of the subject of Technical Communication covers the various forms of technical communication that are used in different contexts and purposes, such as reports, proposals, manuals, instructions, memos, letters, emails, etc.
- To take effective notes for this unit, one can use the following methods:

  - **Cornell method**: This method involves dividing the note page into three sections: a cue column on the left, a note-taking column on the right, and a summary section at the bottom. The cue column is used to write key words, questions, or headings that summarize the main points of the notes. The note-taking column is used to write the detailed information, facts, examples, or explanations that support the main points. The summary section is used to write a brief overview of the notes in one or two sentences. This method helps to organize the notes, review the material, and identify the gaps in understanding.

  - **Outline method**: This method involves using headings, subheadings, and bullet points to structure the notes in a hierarchical way. The headings represent the main topics or categories of the notes, the subheadings represent the subtopics or subcategories, and the bullet points represent the specific details, facts, examples, or explanations that support the subheadings. This method helps to show the relationships between the different parts of the notes, highlight the key points, and make the notes easy to scan and read.

  - **Mapping method**: This method involves using diagrams, charts, graphs, or symbols to represent the notes visually. The diagrams can be of different types, such as flowcharts, mind maps, concept maps, Venn diagrams, etc. The diagrams show the main topics or concepts of the notes as nodes, and the connections or relationships between them as links. The links can be labeled with words, phrases, or symbols to indicate the nature or direction of the connection. This method helps to show the big picture of the notes, stimulate the visual memory, and enhance the creativity and critical thinking skills.
