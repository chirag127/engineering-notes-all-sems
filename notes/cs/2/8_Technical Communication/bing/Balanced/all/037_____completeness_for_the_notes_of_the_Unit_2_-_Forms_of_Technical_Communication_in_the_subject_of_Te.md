# Completeness for the notes of the Unit 2 - Forms of Technical Communication

- Completeness is one of the seven C's of effective communication, which are the qualities of a well-written and well-structured message  .
- Completeness means that the message should convey all the facts and information required by the audience, and answer the questions of who, what, when, where, why, and how .
- Completeness also means that the message should promote goodwill and build trust between the sender and the receiver, by being courteous, respectful, and positive .
- Completeness is especially important in technical communication, which is the process of conveying technical information to a specific audience for a specific purpose.
- Technical communication can take various forms, such as reports, manuals, proposals, instructions, presentations, etc., and it can use different media, such as text, graphics, audio, video, etc.
- To achieve completeness in technical communication, it is essential to examine both the text and the visual components of the document, and ensure that they are clear, accurate, relevant, and consistent.
- Some of the strategies to achieve completeness in technical communication are:
  - Define the purpose and scope of the document, and state them clearly in the introduction.
  - Identify the audience and their needs, expectations, and background knowledge, and tailor the content and tone accordingly.
  - Provide specific details, examples, evidence, and references to support the main points and claims.
  - Use words, terms, acronyms, and abbreviations that the audience can understand, and define them if necessary.
  - Organize the information logically and coherently, using headings, subheadings, lists, transitions, etc.
  - Use visual aids, such as tables, charts, graphs, diagrams, etc., to supplement and illustrate the text, and label them properly.
  - Include a conclusion that summarizes the main points and provides recommendations or actions, if applicable.
  - Proofread and edit the document for grammar, spelling, punctuation, and formatting errors.
  - Solicit feedback from peers, experts, or potential users, and revise the document accordingly.
