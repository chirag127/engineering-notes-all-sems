### Techniques for the notes of the Unit 3 - Technical Presentation in the subject of Technical Communication

- Notes are brief and concise records of the main points, ideas, or facts of a presentation. They help the presenter to recall the information, organize the structure, and deliver the message effectively.
- There are different types of notes, such as outline notes, cue cards, mind maps, and slides. Each type has its own advantages and disadvantages, depending on the purpose, audience, and context of the presentation.
- Outline notes are a hierarchical list of the main topics and subtopics of the presentation, with keywords, phrases, or sentences to indicate the content and sequence. They are useful for planning and revising the presentation, but they may not be sufficient for delivering the presentation without additional cues or support.
- Cue cards are small cards that contain the key points, examples, or data of the presentation, written in bullet points or short sentences. They are easy to carry and refer to during the presentation, but they may limit the eye contact and interaction with the audience, and they may not cover all the details or transitions of the presentation.
- Mind maps are graphical representations of the main topic and its related subtopics, using symbols, colors, images, or words to show the connections and associations. They are helpful for brainstorming and organizing the presentation, but they may be difficult to read or follow during the presentation, and they may not suit all types of presentations or audiences.
- Slides are visual aids that display the key information, graphics, or media of the presentation, using text, images, charts, diagrams, videos, or audio. They are effective for attracting and maintaining the attention of the audience, but they may distract or overwhelm the audience, and they may not convey the tone or emotion of the presentation.

- Some general tips for making effective notes are:

  - Use clear and simple language, avoiding jargon, slang, or abbreviations that may confuse the audience or yourself.
  - Use consistent and appropriate formatting, such as font size, style, color, alignment, spacing, and numbering, to make the notes easy to read and understand.
  - Use keywords, phrases, or sentences that trigger your memory and remind you of the main points, examples, or data, rather than writing full sentences or paragraphs that may be redundant or unnecessary.
  - Use symbols, icons, or highlights to mark the important or critical information, such as the main topic, subtopics, transitions, questions, or conclusions, to guide your attention and focus.
  - Use cues, prompts, or reminders to indicate the actions, gestures, or expressions that you want to use during the presentation, such as pauses, eye contact, voice modulation, or body language, to enhance your delivery and interaction.
  - Use references, sources, or citations to acknowledge the original authors or creators of the information, graphics, or media that you use in your presentation, to avoid plagiarism and show credibility and professionalism.
