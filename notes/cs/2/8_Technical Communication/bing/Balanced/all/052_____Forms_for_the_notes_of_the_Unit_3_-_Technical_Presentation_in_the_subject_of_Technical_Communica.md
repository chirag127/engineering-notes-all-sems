# Forms for the notes of the Unit 3 - Technical Presentation in the subject of Technical Communication

- A technical presentation is a form of oral communication that conveys technical information to a specific audience for a specific purpose.
- A technical presentation can have different forms depending on the context, the audience, the purpose, and the delivery mode.
- Some common forms of technical presentations are:
  - **Informative presentation**: A presentation that aims to inform the audience about a topic, a process, a product, or a service. The purpose is to increase the audience's knowledge or understanding of the subject matter. Examples: a lecture, a tutorial, a demonstration, a report.
  - **Persuasive presentation**: A presentation that aims to persuade the audience to adopt a certain point of view, a course of action, or a decision. The purpose is to influence the audience's attitude, behavior, or opinion. Examples: a proposal, a sales pitch, a recommendation, an argument.
  - **Instructional presentation**: A presentation that aims to instruct the audience on how to do something, how to use something, or how to perform a task. The purpose is to teach the audience a skill, a technique, or a procedure. Examples: a training session, a workshop, a manual, a guide.
  - **Entertaining presentation**: A presentation that aims to entertain the audience by providing amusement, enjoyment, or pleasure. The purpose is to capture the audience's attention, interest, or emotion. Examples: a keynote speech, a storytelling, a comedy, a performance.
- A technical presentation can also have different forms depending on the level of formality, the degree of interaction, and the use of visual aids.
  - **Formality**: A presentation can be formal, informal, or somewhere in between. Formal presentations are usually more structured, planned, and rehearsed. They follow a clear outline, use formal language, and adhere to certain conventions and etiquette. Informal presentations are usually more spontaneous, flexible, and casual. They follow a loose structure, use informal language, and allow for more improvisation and adaptation.
  - **Interaction**: A presentation can be one-way, two-way, or multi-way. One-way presentations are where the speaker delivers the information without any feedback or input from the audience. Two-way presentations are where the speaker and the audience engage in a dialogue or a discussion. Multi-way presentations are where the speaker and the audience interact with each other and with other sources of information, such as online platforms, social media, or multimedia.
  - **Visual aids**: A presentation can use different types of visual aids to support the verbal message, such as slides, charts, graphs, images, videos, animations, or models. Visual aids can help the speaker to illustrate, explain, emphasize, or clarify the information. They can also help the audience to follow, remember, or understand the information. However, visual aids should be used appropriately, effectively, and ethically. They should not distract, confuse, or mislead the audience.
