# Synopsis Writing for the Notes of the Unit 2 - Forms of Technical Communication in the Subject of Technical Communication

- A synopsis is a brief summary of the main points and ideas of a text, such as a book, an article, a report, or a speech.
- A synopsis can help the reader to understand the main purpose, scope, and structure of the text, as well as the key arguments, evidence, and conclusions.
- A synopsis can also help the writer to organize and revise their own text, as well as to check for coherence, clarity, and completeness.
- A synopsis should be concise, accurate, and objective. It should not include the writer's own opinions, interpretations, or evaluations of the text, unless they are explicitly asked for.
- A synopsis should follow the same order and logic as the original text, but it should use shorter sentences, simpler words, and fewer details. It should also avoid direct quotations, unless they are essential to convey the meaning of the text.
- A synopsis should include the following elements:

  - The title and author of the text, as well as the source and date of publication, if applicable.
  - The main topic or theme of the text, and the specific purpose or goal of the writer.
  - The main thesis or argument of the text, and the main supporting points or sub-arguments.
  - The main evidence or examples that the writer uses to support their points or sub-arguments, such as facts, statistics, anecdotes, or quotations.
  - The main conclusions or implications that the writer draws from their points or sub-arguments, and the main recommendations or suggestions that they make, if any.

- A synopsis should be written in the present tense, and use the third person point of view, unless the original text uses a different tense or point of view.
- A synopsis should be formatted according to the guidelines of the specific assignment, such as the length, the style, and the citation method.
- A synopsis should be proofread and edited for grammar, spelling, punctuation, and accuracy, before submitting it to the instructor or the audience.
