# Unit 3 - Technical Presentation

- A technical presentation is a form of oral communication that conveys technical information to a specific audience for a specific purpose.
- Technical presentations can have various purposes, such as informing, persuading, instructing, demonstrating, or proposing.
- Technical presentations can have various audiences, such as experts, managers, customers, students, or the general public.
- Technical presentations require careful planning, preparation, and practice to achieve the desired outcomes.

## Planning a Technical Presentation

- The first step in planning a technical presentation is to analyze the situation, which involves considering the following factors:
  - The purpose of the presentation: What do you want to achieve with your presentation? What are your main points or messages?
  - The audience of the presentation: Who are they? What are their backgrounds, interests, needs, and expectations? How much do they know about the topic?
  - The context of the presentation: Where and when will the presentation take place? What are the physical and technical conditions? How much time do you have?
- The second step in planning a technical presentation is to organize the content, which involves selecting and arranging the information that supports your purpose and meets the needs of your audience. You can use various strategies to organize your content, such as:
  - Chronological order: Presenting the information in the order of time or sequence of events.
  - Spatial order: Presenting the information in the order of space or location.
  - Topical order: Presenting the information in the order of topics or categories.
  - Problem-solution order: Presenting the information in the order of a problem and its solution or solutions.
  - Cause-effect order: Presenting the information in the order of a cause and its effect or effects.
  - Comparison-contrast order: Presenting the information in the order of similarities and differences between two or more items.
- The third step in planning a technical presentation is to design the visuals, which are graphical elements that enhance the understanding and retention of the information. You can use various types of visuals, such as:
  - Tables: Displaying numerical or textual data in rows and columns.
  - Charts: Displaying numerical data in graphical forms, such as bar charts, pie charts, line charts, etc.
  - Diagrams: Displaying the structure, function, or relationship of items, such as flowcharts, schematics, maps, etc.
  - Images: Displaying the appearance, shape, or color of items, such as photographs, drawings, icons, etc.
  - Videos: Displaying the motion, sound, or action of items, such as animations, simulations, demonstrations, etc.
- The fourth step in planning a technical presentation is to write the script, which is the text that accompanies the visuals and guides the delivery of the presentation. You can use various techniques to write an effective script, such as:
  - Using clear and concise language that is appropriate for the audience and the purpose.
  - Using transitions to connect the ideas and sections of the presentation.
  - Using signposts to indicate the structure and progress of the presentation.
  - Using rhetorical devices to emphasize or persuade, such as repetition, parallelism, contrast, etc.
  - Using questions to engage or interact with the audience, such as rhetorical questions, direct questions, etc.

## Preparing a Technical Presentation

- The first step in preparing a technical presentation is to create the slides, which are the visual aids that support the script and the delivery of the presentation.   - Using a consistent and professional design that matches the purpose and the audience of the presentation.

  - Using a balanced and appropriate amount of text and visuals on each slide.
  - Using a logical and coherent sequence of slides that follows the script and the organization of the content.
  - Using animations and transitions to enhance the flow and the impact of the slides.
