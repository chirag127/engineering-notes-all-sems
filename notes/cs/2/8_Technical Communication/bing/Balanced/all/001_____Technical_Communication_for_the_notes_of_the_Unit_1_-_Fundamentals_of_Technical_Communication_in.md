## What is technical communication?

- Technical communication is the process of conveying expert, technical, or highly complex information to a non-expert or more general audience in a clear, concise, and effective way .
- Technical communication is rhetorical: it is goal-driven, audience-focused, and dynamic. It responds to a specific rhetorical situation, which involves the purpose, context, audience, and constraints of the communication.
- Technical communication often involves technology (though not always), such as software, hardware, engineering, medical, scientific, or environmental applications.
- Technical communication often works to convince an audience to take some specific action, such as follow instructions, make a decision, buy a product, or adopt a policy.

## What are the features of technical communication?

- According to , some of the features of technical communication are:

  - Purpose: Getting something done within an organization or helping a customer, client, or colleague get something done.
  - Knowledge of topics: Usually greater than that of the reader.
  - Audience: Often several people with differing technical backgrounds, needs, and expectations.
  - Tone: Objective, impersonal, and respectful.
  - Organization: Logical, clear, and easy to follow.
  - Style: Precise, concise, and consistent.
  - Design: Visually appealing, functional, and accessible.
  - Ethics: Honest, fair, and responsible.

## What is the distinction between general and technical communication?

- General communication is the process of exchanging information, ideas, or opinions with others in a common or public context, such as in a conversation, a speech, a letter, or an email.
- Technical communication is a specialized form of general communication that focuses on communicating expert, technical, or highly complex information to a non-expert or more general audience in a clear, concise, and effective way .
- Some of the differences between general and technical communication are:

  - General communication may have a personal, emotional, or subjective tone, while technical communication has an objective, impersonal, and respectful tone .
  - General communication may have a flexible, informal, or creative style, while technical communication has a precise, concise, and consistent style .
  - General communication may have a varied, diverse, or unpredictable audience, while technical communication has a specific, targeted, or defined audience .
  - General communication may have a broad, general, or vague purpose, while technical communication has a narrow, specific, or clear purpose .

## What are the barriers to technical communication?

- According to , some of the barriers to technical communication are:

  - Physical barriers: These are the external factors that hinder the transmission or reception of the message, such as noise, distance, time, or environment.
  - Semantic barriers: These are the factors that affect the meaning or interpretation of the message, such as language, jargon, symbols, or ambiguity.
  - Psychological barriers: These are the factors that influence the attitude or perception of the sender or receiver, such as emotions, biases, prejudices, or stereotypes.
  - Organizational barriers: These are the factors that relate to the structure or culture of the organization, such as hierarchy, policies, rules, or norms.
  - Cultural barriers: These are the factors that stem from the differences in the values, beliefs, or practices of different groups or individuals, such as religion, ethnicity, gender, or age.

  - Physical barriers: Use appropriate channels, media, and devices to deliver the message, such as phone, email, video, or audio.
