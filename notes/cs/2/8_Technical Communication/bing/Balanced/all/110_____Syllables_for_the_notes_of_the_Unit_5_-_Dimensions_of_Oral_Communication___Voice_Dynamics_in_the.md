# Syllables for the notes of the Unit 5 - Dimensions of Oral Communication & Voice Dynamics in the subject of Technical Communication

- A syllable is a unit of pronunciation that consists of one vowel sound, with or without surrounding consonants, forming the whole or a part of a word     .
- Syllables are the building blocks of spoken words, determining the pace and rhythm of how the words are pronounced.
- Syllables can have more than one letter, but not more than one sound.
- Syllables can have more than one consonant and more than one vowel, as long as they form one sound.
- Syllables can be classified into open and closed syllables, depending on whether they end with a vowel or a consonant.
- Open syllables end with a vowel and have a long vowel sound, such as "me", "go", "hi", etc.
- Closed syllables end with a consonant and have a short vowel sound, such as "cat", "dog", "sit", etc.
- Some syllables can be stressed or unstressed, depending on the emphasis given to them in pronunciation.
- Stressed syllables are louder, longer, and higher in pitch than unstressed syllables.
- The stress pattern of a word can affect its meaning and pronunciation, such as "record" (noun) and "record" (verb).
- Syllables are important for oral communication and voice dynamics, as they help to create clarity, variety, and expression in speech.
- Syllables can be used to divide words into smaller parts, which can help with spelling, reading, and writing.
- Syllables can also help to identify the number of beats or meters in a line of poetry or music.
