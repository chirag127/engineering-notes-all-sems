# Strategies for the notes of the Unit 3 - Technical Presentation in the subject of Technical Communication

- A technical presentation is a form of oral communication that conveys technical information to a specific audience, such as engineers, managers, customers, or the general public.
- A technical presentation requires careful planning, preparation, and practice to achieve its purpose and objectives.
- Some strategies for the notes of the Unit 3 - Technical Presentation are:

  - Identify the purpose and objectives of the presentation. What is the main message or goal of the presentation? Who is the intended audience and what are their needs, expectations, and background knowledge? How will the presentation benefit the audience or persuade them to take action?
  - Conduct a thorough research on the topic and gather relevant and credible information from various sources, such as books, journals, websites, reports, or experts. Evaluate the quality and reliability of the sources and cite them properly in the presentation.
  - Organize the information into a clear and logical structure that follows the introduction-body-conclusion format. The introduction should capture the attention of the audience, provide an overview of the topic, and state the purpose and objectives of the presentation. The body should present the main points and supporting evidence in a coherent and consistent manner, using appropriate transitions and signposts. The conclusion should summarize the main points, restate the purpose and objectives, and provide a call to action or recommendations for the audience.
  - Design effective visual aids, such as slides, charts, graphs, diagrams, or images, that complement and enhance the verbal message of the presentation. Visual aids should be simple, clear, relevant, and consistent with the tone and style of the presentation. They should also follow the principles of contrast, alignment, repetition, and proximity to create a balanced and harmonious layout.
  - Prepare and practice the delivery of the presentation, paying attention to the verbal and nonverbal aspects of communication. Verbal aspects include the choice of words, tone, volume, pace, pitch, and pronunciation. Nonverbal aspects include the eye contact, facial expressions, gestures, posture, and movement. The delivery should be confident, fluent, and engaging, and should adapt to the feedback and reactions of the audience.
  - Revise and refine the presentation based on the feedback and suggestions from peers, instructors, or experts. Identify the strengths and weaknesses of the presentation and make necessary improvements or adjustments. Proofread and edit the presentation for any errors or inconsistencies in the content, format, or style.
