# The flow of Communication

The flow of communication refers to the direction and pattern of information exchange within an organization or between different parties. It is an important aspect of technical communication, as it affects the clarity, efficiency, and effectiveness of the messages.

According to Landry Morren, there are five main types of communication flow within an organization:

- **Downward communication**: This is communication that flows from the higher levels of an organization to the lower levels, or down the chain of command. For example, a manager may communicate policies, instructions, feedback, or announcements to their subordinates. Downward communication is usually formal, authoritative, and directive.

- **Upward communication**: This is communication that flows from the lower levels of an organization to the higher levels, or up the chain of command. For example, an employee may communicate suggestions, complaints, reports, or requests to their manager. Upward communication is usually informal, participative, and constructive.

- **Lateral communication**: This is communication that flows among the same level of an organization, or across the chain of command. For example, colleagues may communicate to coordinate tasks, share information, solve problems, or build relationships. Lateral communication is usually informal, collaborative, and supportive.

- **Diagonal communication**: This is communication that flows across different levels and functions of an organization, or across the chain of command. For example, a technical writer may communicate with a software engineer, a marketing specialist, and a customer service representative to create a user manual. Diagonal communication is usually formal, informative, and integrative.

- **External communication**: This is communication that flows between an organization and its external stakeholders, such as customers, suppliers, partners, investors, media, or the public. For example, a company may communicate its products, services, values, or social responsibility to its potential or existing customers. External communication is usually formal, persuasive, and adaptive.

Technical communicators need to be aware of the different types of communication flow and their implications for the purpose, audience, context, and mode of their messages. They also need to use appropriate strategies and tools to facilitate the flow of communication and overcome any barriers or challenges.
