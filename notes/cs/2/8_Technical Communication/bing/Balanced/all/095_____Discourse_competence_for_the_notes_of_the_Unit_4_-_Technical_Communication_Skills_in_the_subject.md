# Discourse competence for the notes of the Unit 4 - Technical Communication Skills in the subject of Technical Communication

- Discourse competence is the ability to understand and express oneself in a given language, combining grammatical forms and meaning to achieve a linked text (oral or written), in different communication situations .
- Discourse competence is one of the components of communicative competence, which also includes linguistic, sociolinguistic, strategic, and intercultural competence .
- Discourse competence involves the following skills:
  - Textual competence: the ability to read and comprehend different types of texts, such as narratives, descriptions, arguments, etc. and to produce coherent and cohesive texts of one's own.
  - Rhetorical competence: the ability to use appropriate language and strategies to achieve one's communicative goals, such as persuading, informing, entertaining, etc. and to adapt to the audience, context, and purpose of communication.
  - Genre competence: the ability to recognize and use different genres of communication, such as reports, proposals, emails, etc. and to follow their conventions and expectations.
  - Lexical competence: the ability to use a range of vocabulary and expressions that are suitable for the topic, register, and style of communication.
- Discourse competence is important for technical communication because it enables the communicator to:
  - Produce clear, concise, and accurate texts that convey technical information effectively and efficiently.
  - Engage with different discourse communities, such as colleagues, clients, experts, etc. and to understand their common goals, expectations, and terminology.
  - Use appropriate tone, voice, and style to establish rapport, credibility, and trust with the audience.
  - Organize and structure the text according to the genre and purpose of communication, such as using headings, subheadings, lists, tables, etc. to facilitate readability and comprehension.
  - Apply rhetorical strategies, such as logos, ethos, pathos, etc. to persuade, inform, or instruct the audience, depending on the communicative goal.
  - Avoid grammatical, spelling, punctuation, and formatting errors that may distract or confuse the reader or undermine the professionalism of the text.
