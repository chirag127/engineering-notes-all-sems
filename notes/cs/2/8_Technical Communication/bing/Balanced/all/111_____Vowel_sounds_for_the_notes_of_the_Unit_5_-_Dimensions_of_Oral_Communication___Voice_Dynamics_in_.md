# Vowel sounds for the notes of the Unit 5 - Dimensions of Oral Communication & Voice Dynamics in the subject of Technical Communication

- Vowel sounds are created when air flows smoothly, without interruption, through the throat and mouth.
- Different vowel sounds are produced as a speaker changes the shape and placement of articulators (parts of the throat and mouth), such as the tongue, jaws and lips .
- In contrast, consonant sounds happen when the flow of air is obstructed or interrupted by the articulators .
- In English, the vowels are a, e, i, o, and u, although y can sometimes count as a vowel, too.
- Vowels are very important for voice dynamics, as they convey the pitch, tone, stress and intonation of speech.
- Vowels are further divided into two categories: monophthongs (pure vowels) and diphthongs (vowel glides).
- Monophthongs are vowel sounds that have a single, steady quality, such as /i/ in see, /a/ in cat, /u/ in too, etc.
- Diphthongs are vowel sounds that have a sliding or gliding quality, such as /ai/ in buy, /au/ in how, /oi/ in boy, etc.
- A vowel sound is described by four criteria: the height of the tongue, the front-back position of the tongue, the shape of the lips, and the length of the sound.
- For example, the vowel sound /i/ in see is described as a high, front, unrounded, and long vowel.
- The vowel sound /a/ in cat is described as a low, front, unrounded, and short vowel.
- The vowel sound /u/ in too is described as a high, back, rounded, and long vowel.
- The vowel sound /ai/ in buy is described as a diphthong that starts with a low, front, unrounded vowel and ends with a high, front, unrounded vowel.
- The vowel sound /au/ in how is described as a diphthong that starts with a low, back, unrounded vowel and ends with a high, back, rounded vowel.
- The vowel sound /oi/ in boy is described as a diphthong that starts with a mid, back, rounded vowel and ends with a high, front, unrounded vowel.
- To produce clear and accurate vowel sounds, a speaker should pay attention to the position and movement of the tongue, jaws and lips, as well as the duration and stress of the sound.
- Vowel sounds can vary depending on the accent, dialect, or context of the speaker, so it is important to listen to authentic speech samples and practice the sounds in different words and sentences.
- Vowel sounds are essential for effective oral communication, as they convey meaning, emotion, and attitude, and help the listener to understand and respond to the speaker.
