# Unit 2 - Forms of Technical Communication

- Technical communication can be classified into different forms based on the purpose, audience, context, and medium of communication.
- Some of the common forms of technical communication are:

  - **Technical reports**: Written documents that present factual and objective information about a specific topic, such as a research project, a feasibility study, a progress report, etc. Technical reports usually follow a standard format and structure, such as IMRAD (Introduction, Methods, Results, and Discussion).
  - **Technical proposals**: Written documents that propose a solution to a problem or a plan for a project. Technical proposals usually aim to persuade the reader to accept the proposed idea, and may include elements such as executive summary, background, objectives, methodology, budget, timeline, etc.
  - **Technical manuals**: Written documents that provide instructions or guidance on how to use a product, a service, a system, or a process. Technical manuals usually include elements such as title page, table of contents, introduction, body, glossary, index, etc.
  - **Technical presentations**: Oral or visual communication that delivers technical information to an audience, such as a conference presentation, a product demonstration, a training session, etc. Technical presentations usually include elements such as title slide, outline, introduction, body, conclusion, questions, etc.
  - **Technical correspondence**: Written or electronic communication that exchanges technical information between individuals or organizations, such as emails, letters, memos, etc. Technical correspondence usually follows a standard format and style, such as subject line, salutation, body, closing, signature, etc.

- Each form of technical communication has its own advantages and disadvantages, and requires different skills and strategies to produce and deliver effectively.
