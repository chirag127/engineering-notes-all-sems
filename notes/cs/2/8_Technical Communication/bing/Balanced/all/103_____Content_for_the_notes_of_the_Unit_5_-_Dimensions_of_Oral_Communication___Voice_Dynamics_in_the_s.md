# Content for the notes of the Unit 5 - Dimensions of Oral Communication & Voice Dynamics in the subject of Technical Communication

- Oral communication is the verbal and visual transmission of information from one person to another. Presentations, speeches, and dialogues are all examples of oral communication.
- Oral communication has two main dimensions: verbal and nonverbal. Verbal communication refers to the use of language and the choice of words to convey a written message. Nonverbal communication refers to the use of body language, facial expressions, gestures, eye contact, and tone of voice to enhance the message.
- Oral communication has several advantages over written communication, such as immediacy, feedback, personalization, and adaptability. However, oral communication also has some disadvantages, such as lack of permanence, ambiguity, distortion, and noise.
- Oral communication requires effective vocal delivery, which involves the use of voice, articulation, pronunciation, and fluency to convey the message clearly and persuasively.
- Voice is the sound produced by the vibration of the vocal cords. Voice can be influenced by several factors, such as pitch, volume, rate, quality, and emotion. Pitch is the highness or lowness of the voice. Volume is the loudness or softness of the voice. Rate is the speed of the speech. Quality is the tone or timbre of the voice. Emotion is the feeling or mood expressed by the voice.
- Articulation is the clarity and accuracy of the sounds produced by the mouth. Articulation can be affected by the placement and movement of the tongue, teeth, lips, and jaw. Pronunciation is the correctness and appropriateness of the sounds in relation to the meaning and context of the words. Pronunciation can be influenced by the stress, intonation, and accent of the speech.
- Fluency is the smoothness and continuity of the speech. Fluency can be enhanced by the use of pauses, transitions, and connectors. Pauses are brief silences that can be used to emphasize a point, create suspense, or signal a change of topic. Transitions are words or phrases that can be used to link ideas, indicate a shift, or summarize a point. Connectors are words or phrases that can be used to show the relationship between ideas, such as cause and effect, contrast, or comparison.
- Oral communication also requires appropriate audiovisual aids to support the message and engage the audience. Audiovisual aids can be classified into three types: visual, auditory, and audiovisual. Visual aids are those that appeal to the sense of sight, such as charts, graphs, diagrams, pictures, or videos. Auditory aids are those that appeal to the sense of hearing, such as music, sound effects, or recordings. Audiovisual aids are those that combine both visual and auditory elements, such as slides, films, or animations.
- Oral communication can be evaluated based on several criteria, such as content, organization, delivery, language, and audience. Content refers to the accuracy, relevance, and completeness of the information presented. Organization refers to the structure, coherence, and logic of the speech. Delivery refers to the vocal and nonverbal aspects of the speech, such as voice, articulation, pronunciation, fluency, eye contact, gestures, and posture. Language refers to the choice, use, and correctness of the words, sentences, and grammar. Audience refers to the awareness, adaptation, and interaction with the listeners.
