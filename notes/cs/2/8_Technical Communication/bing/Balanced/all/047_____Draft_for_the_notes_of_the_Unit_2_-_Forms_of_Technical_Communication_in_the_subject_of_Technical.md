# Unit 2 - Forms of Technical Communication

## Introduction

- Technical communication is the process of conveying information and instructions to a specific audience for a specific purpose.
- Technical communication can take various forms, such as written, oral, visual, or digital.
- Technical communication can be classified into two main categories: academic and professional.

## Academic Technical Communication

- Academic technical communication is the type of communication that is used in academic settings, such as universities, colleges, or research institutes.
- Academic technical communication is usually aimed at advancing knowledge, informing peers, or educating students.
- Academic technical communication can include forms such as:

  - Research papers: These are formal documents that present original findings or arguments based on rigorous methods and analysis.
  - Theses and dissertations: These are long and comprehensive documents that report the results of a research project conducted by a graduate student.
  - Conference presentations: These are oral or poster presentations that summarize the main points of a research paper or project to an audience of experts or peers.
  - Textbooks and manuals: These are books or guides that provide systematic and comprehensive information on a specific topic or field of study.
  - Lecture notes and slides: These are written or visual aids that accompany a lecture or a class session and help the instructor or the students to follow the main ideas and concepts.

## Professional Technical Communication

- Professional technical communication is the type of communication that is used in professional settings, such as workplaces, industries, or organizations.
- Professional technical communication is usually aimed at solving problems, providing instructions, or persuading clients or customers.
- Professional technical communication can include forms such as:

  - Reports: These are documents that provide information or analysis on a specific issue or situation, such as progress, feasibility, or evaluation.
  - Proposals: These are documents that offer a plan or a solution to a problem or a need, such as a project, a product, or a service.
  - Manuals and instructions: These are documents that provide step-by-step guidance on how to use, operate, or maintain a device, a system, or a process.
  - Memos and letters: These are short and formal documents that communicate information, requests, or decisions to a specific person or group, such as colleagues, managers, or clients.
  - Emails and messages: These are electronic forms of communication that are used to exchange information, queries, or feedback in a quick and convenient way.
  - Websites and blogs: These are online platforms that provide information, updates, or opinions on a specific topic or field of interest.
  - Presentations and pitches: These are oral or visual forms of communication that are used to deliver information, persuade an audience, or sell a product or service.
