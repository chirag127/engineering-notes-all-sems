# Concreteness

- Concreteness is an aspect of communication that means being specific, definite, and vivid rather than vague and general .
- A concrete communication uses specific facts and figures  and supports them with credible sources and examples .
- Concreteness is one of the six C's of effective communication, along with clarity, correctness, completeness, conciseness, and courtesy .
- Concreteness helps the audience better understand and interpret the message as accurately as possible, in the way the sender intended .
- Concreteness also enhances the credibility and persuasiveness of the message, as it shows that the sender has done their research and has evidence to back up their claims .
- Concreteness can be achieved by using concrete language, which is the opposite of abstract language, which refers to intangible ideas or qualities.
- Concrete language is tangible, specific, and measurable, and it avoids ambiguity and generalization.
- Some examples of concrete language are: numbers, dates, names, locations, facts, figures, examples, and explanations.
- Some examples of abstract language are: love, hate, honor, beauty, success, and happiness.
- To make a message more concrete, one can replace adjectives or adverbs with specific words, such as "very good" with "excellent" or "quite fast" with "60 miles per hour" .
- One can also use sensory details, such as color, shape, size, texture, sound, smell, and taste, to make a message more vivid and descriptive.
- One can also use comparisons, analogies, metaphors, and similes to make a message more relatable and memorable.
- Concreteness is a key component of effective communication, as it helps the sender convey their message clearly and convincingly, and it helps the receiver comprehend and retain the message accurately and easily  .
