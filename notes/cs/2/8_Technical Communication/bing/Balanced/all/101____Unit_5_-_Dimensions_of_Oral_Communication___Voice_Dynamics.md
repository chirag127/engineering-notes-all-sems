## Unit 5 - Dimensions of Oral Communication & Voice Dynamics

- Oral communication is the process of expressing information or ideas by word of mouth.
- Oral communication can be formal or informal, depending on the context and purpose of the message.
- Oral communication has several dimensions that affect its effectiveness and quality, such as:

  - Content: the information, ideas, or opinions that are conveyed by the speaker.
  - Structure: the organization, coherence, and clarity of the message.
  - Style: the choice of words, tone, and delivery of the message.
  - Context: the situation, setting, and audience of the communication.
  - Feedback: the response or reaction of the listener to the message.
  - Noise: any interference or distraction that hinders the communication process.

- Voice dynamics are the variations in the pitch, volume, rate, and quality of the voice that convey meaning and emotion in oral communication.
- Voice dynamics can enhance or diminish the effectiveness and impact of the message, depending on how they are used by the speaker.
- Voice dynamics can be influenced by several factors, such as:

  - Physiology: the physical characteristics and condition of the vocal cords, throat, mouth, and lungs.
  - Psychology: the mental state, mood, and attitude of the speaker.
  - Culture: the norms, values, and expectations of the speaker's and listener's social groups.
  - Purpose: the goal, intention, or objective of the communication.

- Some examples of voice dynamics are:

  - Pitch: the highness or lowness of the voice, which can indicate the level of interest, excitement, or emotion of the speaker.
  - Volume: the loudness or softness of the voice, which can indicate the level of confidence, authority, or emphasis of the speaker.
  - Rate: the speed or slowness of the voice, which can indicate the level of urgency, nervousness, or hesitation of the speaker.
  - Quality: the smoothness or roughness of the voice, which can indicate the level of health, energy, or sincerity of the speaker.

- To improve oral communication skills, the speaker should:

  - Plan and prepare the content and structure of the message, considering the purpose, context, and audience of the communication.
  - Practice and rehearse the delivery of the message, using appropriate voice dynamics and nonverbal cues to enhance the message.
  - Listen and respond to the feedback and questions of the listener, using active listening skills and empathy to build rapport and understanding.
  - Evaluate and reflect on the strengths and weaknesses of the communication, seeking feedback and suggestions for improvement.
