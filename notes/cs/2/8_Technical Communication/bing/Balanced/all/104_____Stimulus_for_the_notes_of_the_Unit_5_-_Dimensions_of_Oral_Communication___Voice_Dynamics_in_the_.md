# Stimulus for Oral Communication

- A stimulus is an idea or a concept that is activated by the sender of a message .
- A stimulus can be internal or external, depending on the source of the idea or concept.
- Internal stimuli are generated from the sender's own thoughts, feelings, memories, or imagination.
- External stimuli are triggered by the sender's observation, experience, or interaction with the environment or other people.
- A stimulus can be verbal or nonverbal, depending on the form of the message.
- Verbal stimuli are expressed in words, either spoken or written.
- Nonverbal stimuli are conveyed through gestures, facial expressions, body language, or other visual or auditory cues.
- A stimulus can be simple or complex, depending on the level of detail or abstraction of the message.
- Simple stimuli are easy to understand and communicate, such as facts, opinions, or instructions.
- Complex stimuli are more difficult to comprehend and convey, such as concepts, theories, or arguments.
- A stimulus can be positive or negative, depending on the tone or intention of the message.
- Positive stimuli are meant to encourage, praise, or motivate the receiver of the message.
- Negative stimuli are intended to criticize, warn, or discourage the receiver of the message.

## Examples of Stimuli for Oral Communication

- A teacher asks a student a question based on the lesson. The question is a verbal, external, simple, and positive stimulus for the student to respond orally.
- A friend tells a joke to make another friend laugh. The joke is a verbal, internal, simple, and positive stimulus for the friend to react orally.
- A parent scolds a child for breaking a vase. The scolding is a verbal, external, simple, and negative stimulus for the child to reply orally.
- A speaker presents a new product to a potential customer. The presentation is a verbal, internal, complex, and positive stimulus for the customer to ask questions or give feedback orally.
- A driver honks the horn to alert another driver of a danger. The honking is a nonverbal, external, simple, and negative stimulus for the other driver to respond orally or nonverbally.
- A singer performs a song to entertain an audience. The song is a nonverbal, internal, complex, and positive stimulus for the audience to applaud or sing along orally.
- A doctor shows an X-ray to a patient to explain a diagnosis. The X-ray is a nonverbal, external, complex, and negative stimulus for the patient to express their concerns or emotions orally.

## Importance of Stimuli for Oral Communication

- Stimuli are essential for oral communication because they initiate and sustain the communication process .
- Stimuli help the sender to encode and transmit their message effectively and efficiently .
- Stimuli help the receiver to decode and interpret the message accurately and appropriately .
- Stimuli help the sender and the receiver to engage in a meaningful and productive exchange of information, ideas, or emotions .
- Stimuli help the sender and the receiver to achieve their communication goals, such as informing, persuading, entertaining, or influencing .
- Stimuli help the sender and the receiver to establish and maintain a positive and respectful relationship based on mutual understanding and trust .

## Factors Affecting Stimuli for Oral Communication

- Stimuli for oral communication can be affected by various factors, such as the context, the audience, the purpose, the channel, the feedback, and the noise .
- Context refers to the physical, social, cultural, or historical setting of the communication situation .
- Audience refers to the characteristics, needs, expectations, or preferences of the receiver or receivers of the message .
- Purpose refers to the goal or objective of the sender or the message .
- Channel refers to the medium or mode of communication, such as face-to-face, phone, video, or online .
- Feedback refers to the response or reaction of the receiver or receivers
