# Unit 2 - Forms of Technical Communication

## Introduction

Technical communication is the process of conveying information and instructions to a specific audience for a specific purpose. Technical communication can take various forms, such as manuals, reports, proposals, memos, emails, presentations, websites, blogs, podcasts, etc. Each form of technical communication has its own characteristics, advantages, and limitations.

## Objectives

By the end of this unit, you should be able to:

- Identify and describe the main forms of technical communication
- Analyze the purpose, audience, and context of different forms of technical communication
- Choose the appropriate form of technical communication for a given situation
- Apply the principles of effective technical communication to various forms of technical communication

## Forms of Technical Communication

### Manuals

Manuals are documents that provide instructions or guidance on how to use, operate, maintain, or repair a product, system, or service. Manuals can be printed or digital, and can range from a few pages to hundreds of pages. Manuals typically include the following elements:

- Title page: contains the name of the manual, the name of the product, system, or service, the name and logo of the organization, and the date of publication
- Table of contents: lists the main sections and sub-sections of the manual, along with their page numbers
- Preface: provides an overview of the manual, its purpose, scope, and intended audience, and any acknowledgments, disclaimers, or warnings
- Introduction: introduces the product, system, or service, its features and benefits, and any prerequisites or assumptions
- Body: contains the main instructions or guidance, organized into logical sections and sub-sections, with headings, subheadings, lists, tables, figures, diagrams, etc.
- Glossary: defines any technical terms or acronyms used in the manual
- Index: provides an alphabetical list of keywords and topics, along with their page numbers
- Appendices: provide any additional or supplementary information, such as specifications, troubleshooting, warranty, etc.

Some examples of manuals are:

- User manuals: explain how to use a product, such as a software, a device, or a tool
- Installation manuals: explain how to install a product, such as a software, a device, or a system
- Operation manuals: explain how to operate a system, such as a machine, a vehicle, or a facility
- Maintenance manuals: explain how to maintain or service a product, system, or service, such as a software, a device, or a system
- Repair manuals: explain how to repair or fix a product, system, or service, such as a software, a device, or a system

### Reports

Reports are documents that provide information or analysis on a specific topic, issue, problem, or situation. Reports can be printed or digital, and can vary in length, format, and style, depending on the purpose, audience, and context. Reports typically include the following elements:

- Title page: contains the title of the report, the name of the author(s), the name and logo of the organization, the name of the recipient(s), and the date of submission
- Executive summary: provides a brief overview of the report, its purpose, scope, methods, findings, conclusions, and recommendations
- Table of contents: lists the main sections and sub-sections of the report, along with their page numbers
- Introduction: introduces the topic, issue, problem, or situation, its background, significance, and objectives, and outlines the structure of the report
- Body: contains the main information or analysis, organized into logical sections and sub-sections, with headings, subheadings, lists, tables, figures, graphs, charts, etc.
- Conclusion: summarizes the main findings, implications, and limitations of the report, and answers the objectives or questions posed in the introduction
- Recommendations: suggests any actions or solutions based on the findings and conclusions of the report, and provides justification and evidence for them
- References: cites any sources of information or data used in the report, using a consistent citation style, such as APA, MLA, IEEE, etc.
- Appendices: provide any additional or supplementary information, such as data, calculations, surveys, interviews, etc.

Some examples of reports are:

- Progress reports: provide updates on the status, achievements, challenges, and plans of a project, task, or activity
- Feasibility reports: evaluate the viability, benefits, costs, and risks of a proposed project, solution, or alternative
- Research reports: present the results, methods, and implications of a research study or experiment
- Technical reports: describe the design, development, testing, and evaluation of a technical product, system, or service
