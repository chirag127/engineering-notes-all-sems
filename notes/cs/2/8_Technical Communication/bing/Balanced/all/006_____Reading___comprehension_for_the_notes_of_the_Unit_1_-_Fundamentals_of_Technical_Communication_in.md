# Reading & comprehension for the notes of the Unit 1 - Fundamentals of Technical Communication in the subject of Technical Communication

- Reading is a process of decoding written symbols and constructing meaning from them.
- Comprehension is the ability to understand and interpret what is read.
- Reading and comprehension are essential skills for technical communication, as they enable the reader to access, analyze, and evaluate information from various sources and formats.
- Reading and comprehension involve different levels of processing, such as:
  - Literal: understanding the explicit meaning of words and sentences.
  - Inferential: making logical connections and deductions based on the text and prior knowledge.
  - Evaluative: judging the quality, credibility, and relevance of the text and its arguments.
  - Creative: generating new ideas, insights, and perspectives based on the text and personal experience.
- Reading and comprehension can be improved by applying various strategies, such as:
  - Previewing: skimming the text to get an overview of its structure, purpose, and main points.
  - Predicting: making guesses about what the text will say or imply based on the title, headings, and visuals.
  - Questioning: asking questions before, during, and after reading to clarify and deepen understanding.
  - Summarizing: restating the main ideas and key details of the text in one's own words.
  - Paraphrasing: rewriting a passage or a sentence in one's own words without changing its meaning.
  - Synthesizing: combining information from different sources or texts to create a new understanding or perspective.
  - Analyzing: breaking down the text into its components and examining how they relate to each other and to the whole.
  - Evaluating: assessing the strengths, weaknesses, and validity of the text and its arguments.
  - Applying: using the information or knowledge gained from the text to solve problems, make decisions, or perform tasks.
