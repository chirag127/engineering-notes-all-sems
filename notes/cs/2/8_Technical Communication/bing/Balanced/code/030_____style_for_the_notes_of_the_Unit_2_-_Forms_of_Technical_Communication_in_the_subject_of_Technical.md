### Unit 2 - Forms of Technical Communication

- Technical communication is the process of conveying information and instructions to a specific audience for a specific purpose.
- Technical communication can take various forms, such as written, oral, visual, or multimedia.
- Some common forms of technical communication are:

  - **Reports**: Documents that present facts, analysis, and recommendations on a topic or problem. Reports can be formal or informal, internal or external, analytical or informational, etc.
  - **Proposals**: Documents that offer a plan or a solution to a problem or a need. Proposals can be solicited or unsolicited, internal or external, research or business, etc.
  - **Instructions**: Documents that explain how to do something or how something works. Instructions can be procedural or descriptive, online or offline, user or technical, etc.
  - **Manuals**: Documents that provide comprehensive information and guidance on a product, service, or system. Manuals can be user or technical, online or offline, reference or tutorial, etc.
  - **Presentations**: Oral or visual communication that delivers a message or information to an audience. Presentations can be informative or persuasive, formal or informal, individual or group, etc.
  - **Websites**: Online platforms that provide information, services, or interaction to users. Websites can be static or dynamic, personal or professional, informational or transactional, etc.
  - **Emails**: Electronic messages that communicate information or requests to one or more recipients. Emails can be formal or informal, internal or external, individual or group, etc.
  - **Memos**: Short documents that communicate information or requests within an organization. Memos can be formal or informal, internal or external, individual or group, etc.
  - **Letters**: Written documents that communicate information or requests to an external party. Letters can be formal or informal, personal or professional, individual or group, etc.

- Each form of technical communication has its own purpose, audience, structure, style, and format.
- Technical communicators should choose the appropriate form of technical communication based on the communication situation and the communication objectives.
