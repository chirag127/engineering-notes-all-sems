### Falling Tone for the notes of the Unit 5 - Dimensions of Oral Communication & Voice Dynamics in the subject of Technical Communication

- Falling tone is a type of intonation that involves a downward pitch movement at the end of a sentence or a phrase.
- Falling tone is used to convey finality, certainty, completeness, or confidence in oral communication.
- Falling tone is commonly used in the following situations:
  - Normal, neutral statements that provide information or express opinions, such as "I live in New York." or "I like this book."
  - Information questions that request details and begin with who, what, where, when, why, how, how much, how many, how often, and so on, such as "How long have you been working here?" or "What do you do for a living?"
  - Commands or requests that imply authority or urgency, such as "Stop that!" or "Please close the door."
  - Confirmations or agreements that show understanding or acceptance, such as "Yes." or "I see."
- Falling tone signals to the listener that the speaker is done talking and does not expect a response or a continuation of the conversation. 
- Falling tone can also indicate politeness, respect, or formality in some contexts, such as when apologizing, thanking, or greeting someone, such as "Sorry, I'm not quite sure what you mean by 'valued possessions'." or "Thank you for your time." or "Good morning, sir."
- Falling tone can be contrasted with rising tone, which involves an upward pitch movement at the end of a sentence or a phrase and is used to convey uncertainty, incompleteness, curiosity, or interest in oral communication.  
- Falling tone can be practiced by using a falling hand gesture or by imagining a falling staircase when speaking. 
- Falling tone can be recognized by listening to the stress and pitch of the final syllable of a sentence or a phrase and noticing how it drops down.
