# Types of Technical Communication

Technical communication is a form of communication based on the writing and sharing of technical information with a specialized target audience. Technical communicators create, design, and deliver various forms of technical information, such as user manuals, websites, product specifications, video tutorials, e-books, webinars, and so on.

There are many types of technical communication, depending on the purpose, audience, and context of the communication. Some of the common types are:

- **Instructional documents**: These are documents that provide step-by-step guidance on how to perform a task, use a product, or follow a procedure. Examples are user guides, manuals, tutorials, and training materials.
- **Informative documents**: These are documents that provide factual, objective, and reliable information on a topic, such as a report, an article, a white paper, or a brochure.
- **Persuasive documents**: These are documents that aim to convince an audience to take some specific action, such as a proposal, a recommendation, a sales letter, or a marketing campaign.
- **Definitional documents**: These are documents that explain the meaning, usage, or function of a term, concept, or process, such as a glossary, a dictionary, a specification, or a standard.
- **Scientific documents**: These are documents that communicate scientific research, findings, or methods, such as a journal article, a conference paper, a thesis, or a patent.

These are some of the main types of technical communication, but there are many others that can be classified based on different criteria, such as the medium, the format, the style, or the genre of the communication. Technical communicators should be aware of the different types of technical communication and their characteristics, in order to choose the most appropriate and effective one for their communication goals.
