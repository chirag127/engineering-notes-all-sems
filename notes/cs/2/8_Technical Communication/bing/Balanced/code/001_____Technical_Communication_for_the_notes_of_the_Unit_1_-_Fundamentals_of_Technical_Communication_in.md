### Technical Communication for the notes of the Unit 1 - Fundamentals of Technical Communication

Technical communication is the process of conveying information that is relevant, clear, concise, accurate, and usable for a specific purpose, audience, and context. Technical communication can be written, oral, visual, or multimodal, and it can involve different types of media, such as text, images, audio, video, etc. Technical communication can have various goals, such as informing, instructing, persuading, or collaborating.

Some of the features of technical communication are :

- It is purposeful: it aims to achieve a specific outcome or goal for the sender and the receiver of the message.
- It is audience-centered: it considers the needs, expectations, background, and preferences of the intended audience, and adapts the message accordingly.
- It is context-sensitive: it takes into account the situation, environment, culture, and constraints that affect the communication process and the message.
- It is ethical: it follows the principles of honesty, integrity, respect, and responsibility, and avoids plagiarism, fabrication, distortion, or manipulation of information.
- It is collaborative: it involves working with others, such as subject matter experts, peers, supervisors, clients, or customers, to produce, review, and revise the message.
- It is dynamic: it evolves and changes according to the feedback, response, and interaction of the audience and the sender.

Some of the distinctions between general and technical communication are:

- General communication is more informal, personal, subjective, and emotional, while technical communication is more formal, impersonal, objective, and rational.
- General communication is more general, broad, and abstract, while technical communication is more specific, narrow, and concrete.
- General communication is more descriptive, narrative, or expressive, while technical communication is more analytical, explanatory, or directive.
- General communication is more oriented to the sender's perspective, while technical communication is more oriented to the audience's perspective.
- General communication is more flexible, creative, and artistic, while technical communication is more structured, logical, and systematic.

Language is a tool of technical communication that enables the sender and the receiver to exchange information, ideas, opinions, or emotions. Language can be verbal or non-verbal, and it can have different functions, such as referential, conative, emotive, phatic, metalingual, or poetic. Language can also have different levels, such as phonetic, morphological, syntactic, semantic, or pragmatic. Language can be influenced by various factors, such as culture, context, audience, purpose, medium, or genre.

Some of the benefits of technical communication are :

- It helps to achieve the goals and objectives of the organization or the individual.
- It helps to improve the quality and efficiency of the products, services, or processes.
- It helps to enhance the credibility and reputation of the sender and the receiver.
- It helps to foster positive relationships and collaboration among the stakeholders.
- It helps to prevent or resolve problems, conflicts, or misunderstandings.
- It helps to educate, inform, instruct, persuade, or entertain the audience.
