### Discourse competence for the notes of the Unit 4 - Technical Communication Skills in the subject of Technical Communication

- Discourse competence is the ability to understand and express oneself in a given language, combining grammatical forms and meaning to achieve a linked text (oral or written), in different communication situations .
- Discourse competence is one of the components of communicative competence, which also includes linguistic, sociolinguistic, strategic, and intercultural competence .
- Discourse competence involves the following skills:
  - Textual competence: the ability to read and comprehend different types of texts, such as narratives, descriptions, arguments, etc. and to produce coherent and cohesive texts of one's own.
  - Rhetorical competence: the ability to use appropriate language and strategies to achieve a specific purpose, audience, and context, such as persuading, informing, entertaining, etc. and to contribute effectively to a conversation.
  - Genre competence: the ability to recognize and use different genres of communication, such as reports, letters, emails, etc. and to follow their conventions and expectations.
  - Lexical competence: the ability to use a range of vocabulary and expressions that are suitable for the topic, register, and style of the communication.
  - Pragmatic competence: the ability to use language appropriately and politely, taking into account the social and cultural norms and expectations of the interlocutors.
- Discourse competence is important for technical communication because it enables the communicator to:
  - Communicate clearly and effectively with different audiences, such as experts, clients, colleagues, etc. and to adapt the language and style to their needs and expectations.
  - Organize and present information in a logical and coherent way, using appropriate transitions, connectors, and markers to guide the reader or listener.
  - Use different modes and media of communication, such as oral, written, visual, etc. and to integrate them effectively to enhance the message.
  - Choose and use the appropriate genre and format for the communication, such as reports, proposals, manuals, etc. and to follow their standards and conventions.
  - Use accurate and precise vocabulary and expressions that convey the technical content and avoid ambiguity and confusion.
  - Use language politely and respectfully, taking into account the cultural and social background and expectations of the interlocutors and avoiding offensive or inappropriate language.
