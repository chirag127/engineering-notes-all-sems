### Vowel sounds for the notes of the Unit 5 - Dimensions of Oral Communication & Voice Dynamics in the subject of Technical Communication

- Vowel sounds are created when air flows smoothly, without interruption, through the throat and mouth.
- Different vowel sounds are produced as a speaker changes the shape and placement of articulators (parts of the throat and mouth), such as the tongue, jaws and lips .
- Vowels are contrasted with consonants, which represent sounds where air is obstructed or interrupted, such as the closing of the lips for b, m, and p.
- In English, the vowels are a, e, i, o, and u, although y can sometimes count as a vowel, too.
- Vowels are further divided into two categories that are monophthongs (pure vowels) and diphthongs (vowel glides).
- A monophthong is a vowel sound that has a single quality and does not change in the mouth, such as the sound of a in cat or e in bed.
- A diphthong is a vowel sound that consists of two elements, a beginning and an end, and the quality of the vowel changes as the sound is produced, such as the sound of ou in out or oi in boil.
- A vowel is described taking into account the following criteria:
  - The height of the tongue in the mouth (high, mid, low)
  - The part of the tongue that is raised (front, central, back)
  - The shape of the lips (rounded, unrounded, spread)
  - The length of the vowel (long, short)
  - The tenseness of the vowel (tense, lax)
- Vowel sounds are important for oral communication and voice dynamics because they affect the clarity, tone, pitch, and stress of speech .
- Vowel sounds can also convey emotions, attitudes, and intentions of the speaker, such as surprise, anger, sarcasm, or politeness .
