## Unit 5 - Dimensions of Oral Communication & Voice Dynamics

Oral communication is the process of expressing information or ideas by word of mouth. It involves two or more people exchanging verbal messages in a face-to-face or mediated context. Oral communication has several dimensions that affect its effectiveness and appropriateness, such as:

- **Purpose**: The goal or objective of the communication, such as to inform, persuade, entertain, or instruct. The purpose guides the choice of content, tone, style, and channel of communication.
- **Audience**: The intended or actual recipients of the communication, such as individuals, groups, or organizations. The audience influences the level of formality, language, and structure of the communication.
- **Context**: The physical, social, cultural, and temporal setting of the communication, such as a classroom, a workplace, a family, or a country. The context affects the norms, expectations, and conventions of the communication.
- **Channel**: The medium or mode of transmission of the communication, such as speech, writing, audio, video, or multimedia. The channel determines the technical and logistical aspects of the communication, such as the quality, speed, and feedback of the message.
- **Feedback**: The verbal or nonverbal responses or reactions of the audience to the communication, such as questions, comments, nods, or applause. Feedback provides information about the effectiveness, clarity, and appropriateness of the communication, and allows for adjustment and improvement.

Voice dynamics are the vocal qualities and characteristics that affect the delivery and impact of oral communication, such as:

- **Pitch**: The highness or lowness of the voice, measured by the frequency of the sound waves. Pitch can convey emotion, attitude, and emphasis in oral communication. For example, a high pitch can indicate excitement, surprise, or anger, while a low pitch can indicate calmness, boredom, or authority.
- **Volume**: The loudness or softness of the voice, measured by the intensity of the sound waves. Volume can indicate confidence, interest, and importance in oral communication. For example, a loud volume can indicate enthusiasm, confidence, or urgency, while a soft volume can indicate shyness, hesitation, or secrecy.
- **Rate**: The speed or pace of the voice, measured by the number of words spoken per minute. Rate can affect the clarity, comprehension, and engagement of oral communication. For example, a fast rate can indicate nervousness, excitement, or impatience, while a slow rate can indicate calmness, boredom, or deliberation.
- **Tone**: The quality or color of the voice, influenced by the resonance, breath, and articulation of the speaker. Tone can express emotion, attitude, and personality in oral communication. For example, a warm tone can indicate friendliness, sincerity, or empathy, while a cold tone can indicate hostility, indifference, or sarcasm.
- **Pause**: The deliberate or involuntary break or silence in the voice, occurring between words, phrases, or sentences. Pause can create emphasis, suspense, or transition in oral communication. For example, a long pause can indicate uncertainty, hesitation, or reflection, while a short pause can indicate emphasis, separation, or transition.
