### Technical style
- Technical style is the way of writing and presenting information in a clear, concise, and accurate manner for a specific audience and purpose.
- Technical style has four main characteristics: clarity, conciseness, accuracy, and appropriateness.
- Clarity: The quality of being easily understood by the intended readers. To achieve clarity, technical writers should:
  - Use simple and precise words, avoiding jargon, slang, and vague terms.
  - Use active voice, avoiding passive voice and nominalizations.
  - Use parallel structure, avoiding shifts in tense, mood, voice, and number.
  - Use headings, lists, tables, and graphics to organize and highlight information.
  - Use transitions, topic sentences, and summaries to guide the readers through the document.
- Conciseness: The quality of being brief and to the point, without omitting essential information or details. To achieve conciseness, technical writers should:
  - Eliminate unnecessary words, such as redundancies, modifiers, and fillers.
  - Use short and simple sentences, avoiding long and complex sentences.
  - Use positive statements, avoiding negatives and double negatives.
  - Use specific and concrete examples, avoiding generalizations and abstractions.
- Accuracy: The quality of being correct, precise, and reliable, without errors or mistakes. To achieve accuracy, technical writers should:
  - Verify the facts, data, and sources of information, avoiding assumptions and opinions.
  - Use consistent and standard units, symbols, and formats, avoiding ambiguity and confusion.
  - Use proper grammar, spelling, punctuation, and capitalization, avoiding errors and typos.
  - Cite the sources of information, avoiding plagiarism and ethical issues.
- Appropriateness: The quality of being suitable and relevant for the intended audience and purpose. To achieve appropriateness, technical writers should:
  - Analyze the needs, expectations, and background of the audience, avoiding miscommunication and misunderstanding.
  - Adapt the tone, style, and level of detail to the purpose and context of the document, avoiding inappropriate or offensive language and tone.
  - Follow the conventions and guidelines of the genre and discipline, avoiding deviation and inconsistency.
  - Use design elements, such as fonts, colors, and layout, to enhance the readability and attractiveness of the document, avoiding distraction and clutter.
