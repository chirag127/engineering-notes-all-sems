### Style for the notes of the Unit 3 - Technical Presentation in the subject of Technical Communication

- Style is the way you express your ideas and information in a technical presentation. It includes the choice of words, sentences, tone, voice, and visuals.
- Style affects the clarity, accuracy, and effectiveness of your presentation. It also influences the audience's perception of your credibility, professionalism, and confidence.
- Some general guidelines for style in a technical presentation are:

  - Use clear and concise language. Avoid jargon, slang, and unnecessary words. Define any technical terms or acronyms that the audience may not be familiar with.
  - Use active voice and strong verbs. Active voice shows who or what is performing the action, while passive voice hides the agent or makes it unclear. Strong verbs convey action and energy, while weak verbs are vague and dull.
  - Use parallel structure and transitions. Parallel structure means using the same grammatical form for items in a list or series. Transitions are words or phrases that connect ideas and show the relationship between them. Parallel structure and transitions help the audience follow your logic and organization.
  - Use appropriate tone and voice. Tone is the attitude or emotion that you convey through your words. Voice is the personality or character that you project through your words. Tone and voice should match the purpose, audience, and context of your presentation. For example, you may use a formal tone and voice for a technical report, but a more informal tone and voice for a demonstration or a tutorial.
  - Use effective visuals. Visuals are any images, graphs, charts, tables, diagrams, or videos that you use to support your presentation. Visuals should be clear, relevant, accurate, and consistent. They should complement your verbal message, not distract from it or contradict it. Visuals should also follow the design principles of contrast, alignment, repetition, and proximity.
