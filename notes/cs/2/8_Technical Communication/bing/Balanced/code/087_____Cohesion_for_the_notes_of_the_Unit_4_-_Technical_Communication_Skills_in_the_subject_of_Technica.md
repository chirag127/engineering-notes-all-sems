# Cohesion for the notes of the Unit 4 - Technical Communication Skills

- Cohesion is the quality of a text that makes it easy to follow and understand by connecting its elements logically and smoothly.
- Cohesion can be achieved by using various linguistic devices, such as grammatical, lexical, semantic, metrical, and alliterative ones.
- Cohesion differs from coherence, which is the overall sense and meaning of a text. A text can be cohesive but not coherent, or vice versa.
- Some examples of cohesive devices are:
  - Pronouns: They refer back to a previous noun or noun phrase, avoiding repetition. For example, "John loves his dog. He takes it for a walk every day."
  - Conjunctions: They link sentences or clauses together, showing the relationship between them. For example, "She studied hard for the exam, but she failed."
  - Substitution: It replaces a word or phrase with another one that has a similar meaning or function. For example, "Do you like chocolate? I do."
  - Ellipsis: It omits a word or phrase that can be inferred from the context. For example, "She plays the piano better than I (do)."
  - Reference: It uses words or phrases that point to something outside the text, such as time, place, or person. For example, "This book is very interesting. It was written by a famous author."
  - Repetition: It repeats a word or phrase for emphasis or clarity. For example, "To be or not to be, that is the question."
  - Synonymy: It uses words that have the same or similar meaning. For example, "She is smart and intelligent."
  - Antonymy: It uses words that have opposite meanings. For example, "He is rich but unhappy."
  - Hyponymy: It uses words that belong to the same category or class. For example, "She likes animals, especially cats and dogs."
  - Collocation: It uses words that often occur together. For example, "She made a cup of tea."
  - Parallelism: It uses the same grammatical structure or pattern for two or more elements. For example, "She likes reading, writing, and painting."
- Cohesion is important for effective technical communication because it helps the reader to follow the logic and structure of the text, to grasp the main points and details, and to avoid confusion and misunderstanding.
