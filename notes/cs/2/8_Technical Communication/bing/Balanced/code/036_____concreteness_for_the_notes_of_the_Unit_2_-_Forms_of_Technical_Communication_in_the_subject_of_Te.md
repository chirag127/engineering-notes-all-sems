### Concreteness

- Concreteness is the quality of being specific, definite, and vivid in communication.
- Concreteness helps the reader to understand the message clearly and accurately, without confusion or ambiguity.
- Concreteness also enhances the credibility and persuasiveness of the message, as it shows that the writer has done the research and has the evidence to support the claims.
- To achieve concreteness in technical communication, the writer should follow these guidelines:

  - Use precise and familiar words that convey the intended meaning. Avoid vague, abstract, or general words that can be interpreted differently by different readers.
  - Use concrete examples, illustrations, statistics, facts, and figures to support the main points. Avoid making unsupported assertions or opinions that can be challenged or questioned by the reader.
  - Use specific and measurable terms to describe the quantity, quality, time, frequency, or degree of something. Avoid using modifiers that are too subjective, such as "very", "quite", "somewhat", etc.
  - Use active voice and strong verbs to show who is doing what and how. Avoid using passive voice and weak verbs that obscure the action and the agent.
  - Use sensory details and descriptive language to create a vivid picture of the situation, problem, solution, or outcome. Avoid using clichés, jargon, or technical terms that may confuse or bore the reader.
  - Use comparisons, analogies, metaphors, and similes to make the message more relatable and memorable. Avoid using inappropriate or misleading comparisons that may distort or misrepresent the message.
  - Use headings, subheadings, lists, tables, graphs, charts, diagrams, and other visual aids to organize and present the information in a clear and concise manner. Avoid using too many or too few visual aids that may clutter or omit the important information.
