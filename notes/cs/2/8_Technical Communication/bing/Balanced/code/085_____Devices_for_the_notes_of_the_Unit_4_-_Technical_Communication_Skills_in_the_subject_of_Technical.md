### Devices for the notes of the Unit 4 - Technical Communication Skills in the subject of Technical Communication

- Technical communication is a broad field that includes any form of communication that is about technical or specialized topics, uses technology such as web pages or help files, or provides instruction about how to do something.
- Technical communication is also a process of planning, drafting, and revising technical documents that are clear and concise.
- Technical communication relies not only on text, but also on graphics, images, hyperlinks, video, audio, etc. to impact an audience.
- Devices for the notes of technical communication are tools or methods that help the technical communicator to record, organize, and outline information from various sources.
- Some examples of devices for the notes of technical communication are:

  - Quotations: direct words or phrases from a source that are enclosed in quotation marks and attributed to the original author. Quotations are useful to show the exact wording or tone of a source, or to support a claim or argument. Quotations should be used sparingly and accurately.
  - Paraphrases: restatements of the main ideas or information from a source in the technical communicator's own words. Paraphrases are useful to simplify or clarify a complex or technical source, or to avoid plagiarism. Paraphrases should be faithful to the original meaning and cited properly.
  - Summaries: brief overviews of the main points or purpose of a source. Summaries are useful to provide an overview of a long or detailed source, or to compare or contrast different sources. Summaries should be concise and objective and cited correctly.
  - Graphics: visual representations of data or information, such as charts, graphs, diagrams, maps, etc. Graphics are useful to illustrate or emphasize a point, or to show trends, patterns, or relationships. Graphics should be clear, relevant, and labeled appropriately.
  - Hyperlinks: clickable words or phrases that lead to another web page or document. Hyperlinks are useful to provide additional or related information, or to direct the reader to a source or reference. Hyperlinks should be descriptive, accurate, and functional.
