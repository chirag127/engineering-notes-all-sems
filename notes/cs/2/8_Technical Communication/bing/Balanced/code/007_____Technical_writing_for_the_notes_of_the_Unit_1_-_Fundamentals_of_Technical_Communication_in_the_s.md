### Technical writing
- Technical writing is the process of creating clear, concise, and accurate documents that communicate technical information to a specific audience.
- Technical writing can be used for various purposes, such as:
  - Instruction manuals
  - User guides
  - Reports
  - Proposals
  - Specifications
  - White papers
  - Articles
  - Presentations
  - Websites
  - Blogs
- Technical writing requires the following skills and knowledge:
  - Subject matter expertise
  - Audience analysis
  - Writing style and tone
  - Document design and layout
  - Visual aids and graphics
  - Research and citation
  - Editing and proofreading
  - Ethical and legal considerations

### Fundamentals of Technical Communication
- Technical communication is the exchange of technical information between a sender and a receiver, using various modes and media.
- Technical communication has the following characteristics:
  - Purposeful: It has a clear and specific goal or objective.
  - Audience-centered: It considers the needs, expectations, and preferences of the intended readers or users.
  - Contextual: It adapts to the situation, environment, and constraints of the communication.
  - Accurate: It provides correct and reliable information, without errors or ambiguity.
  - Clear: It uses simple, precise, and consistent language, without jargon or unnecessary words.
  - Concise: It conveys the essential information, without redundancy or irrelevant details.
  - Coherent: It organizes the information in a logical and smooth manner, using transitions and headings.
  - Complete: It covers all the relevant aspects of the topic, without leaving gaps or questions.
  - Courteous: It respects the dignity and values of the audience, without being rude or offensive.
  - Professional: It follows the standards and conventions of the field, genre, and format of the document.
