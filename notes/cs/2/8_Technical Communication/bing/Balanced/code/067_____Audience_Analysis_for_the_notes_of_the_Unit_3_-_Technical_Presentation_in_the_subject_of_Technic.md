### Audience Analysis

- Audience analysis is the process of identifying and understanding the needs, expectations, and preferences of the people who will receive your message.
- Audience analysis helps you to tailor your message to suit your audience's level of knowledge, interest, attitude, and culture.
- Audience analysis can be done before, during, and after your presentation, using various methods such as surveys, interviews, observation, feedback, and adaptation.
- Audience analysis can help you to achieve the following goals:
  - Establish rapport and credibility with your audience
  - Choose the appropriate tone, style, and language for your message
  - Select the most relevant and persuasive content and evidence for your message
  - Organize your message in a clear and logical way
  - Design effective visual aids and delivery techniques for your message
  - Anticipate and address potential questions, objections, and concerns from your audience
  - Evaluate the effectiveness of your message and improve your future presentations

- Audience analysis can be divided into four categories: demographic, psychographic, situational, and environmental.

#### Demographic Analysis

- Demographic analysis is the study of the basic characteristics of your audience, such as age, gender, education, occupation, income, ethnicity, religion, etc.
- Demographic analysis can help you to determine the general background, needs, and expectations of your audience, as well as their common values, beliefs, and attitudes.
- Demographic analysis can also help you to avoid making assumptions, stereotypes, or biases about your audience, and to respect their diversity and individuality.
- Demographic analysis can be done by using secondary sources, such as census data, reports, statistics, or profiles, or by using primary sources, such as surveys, questionnaires, or interviews.

#### Psychographic Analysis

- Psychographic analysis is the study of the psychological characteristics of your audience, such as personality, motivation, interest, attitude, preference, etc.
- Psychographic analysis can help you to determine the specific goals, needs, and expectations of your audience, as well as their level of knowledge, interest, and attitude towards your topic.
- Psychographic analysis can also help you to appeal to your audience's emotions, values, and beliefs, and to persuade them to accept your message or take action.
- Psychographic analysis can be done by using secondary sources, such as articles, reviews, testimonials, or social media, or by using primary sources, such as surveys, questionnaires, or interviews.

#### Situational Analysis

- Situational analysis is the study of the contextual factors that affect your presentation, such as purpose, occasion, time, location, size, composition, and expectations of your audience.
- Situational analysis can help you to determine the appropriate scope, depth, and tone of your message, as well as the best way to organize, deliver, and support your message.
- Situational analysis can also help you to anticipate and adapt to the possible challenges, opportunities, and constraints of your presentation, such as technical issues, distractions, interruptions, or questions.
- Situational analysis can be done by using secondary sources, such as agendas, invitations, or guidelines, or by using primary sources, such as observation, feedback, or adaptation.

#### Environmental Analysis

- Environmental analysis is the study of the physical and social setting of your presentation, such as the room, equipment, layout, lighting, noise, temperature, etc.
- Environmental analysis can help you to determine the optimal conditions for your presentation, such as the visibility, audibility, and accessibility of your message, as well as the comfort, safety, and convenience of your audience.
- Environmental analysis can also help you to enhance the impact and appeal of your message, such as by using visual aids, gestures, eye contact, or humor.
- Environmental analysis can be done by using secondary sources, such as maps, photos, or videos, or by using primary sources, such as observation, feedback, or adaptation.
