### Method for the notes of the Unit 4 - Technical Communication Skills in the subject of Technical Communication

- Unit 4 covers the following topics: oral communication, written communication, visual communication, and non-verbal communication.
- To take notes effectively, you should follow these steps:
  - Before the lecture, review the learning objectives and the outline of the unit. Prepare some questions or comments based on your prior knowledge and interest.
  - During the lecture, listen actively and attentively to the main points and supporting details. Use abbreviations, symbols, and diagrams to record the information. Use headings, subheadings, and bullet points to organize the notes. Highlight the key words, phrases, and concepts. Write down any examples, explanations, or applications that the lecturer provides. Note down any questions or doubts that you have.
  - After the lecture, review your notes as soon as possible. Fill in any gaps or missing information. Compare your notes with other sources, such as textbooks, slides, or handouts. Summarize the main ideas and the main takeaways. Test your understanding by answering the questions or comments that you prepared before the lecture. Revise your notes periodically to reinforce your memory and comprehension.
- To format your notes, you should use a consistent and clear style. You can use one of the following methods or a combination of them:
  - The outline method: This method uses indentation and numbering to show the hierarchy and the relationship of the information. It is suitable for lectures that have a clear and logical structure. For example:

    ```
    I. Oral communication
      A. Definition and importance
        1. Oral communication is the process of exchanging information and ideas through spoken words.
        2. Oral communication is essential for personal, academic, and professional success.
      B. Types and purposes
        1. Informative: to provide information or facts
          a. Example: a news report, a lecture, a presentation
        2. Persuasive: to convince or influence the audience
          b. Example: a debate, a sales pitch, a speech
        3. Expressive: to share feelings or emotions
          c. Example: a story, a poem, a song
        4. Interpersonal: to build and maintain relationships
          d. Example: a conversation, a feedback, a negotiation
    ```

  - The Cornell method: This method divides the page into three sections: the cue column, the note-taking column, and the summary area. It is suitable for lectures that have a lot of details and require critical thinking. For example:
