### Discourse competence for the notes of the Unit 4 - Technical Communication Skills in the subject of Technical Communication

- Discourse competence is the ability to understand and express oneself in a given language, combining grammatical forms and meaning to achieve a linked text (oral or written), in different communication situations .
- Discourse competence is one of the components of communicative competence, which also includes linguistic, sociolinguistic, strategic, and intercultural competence .
- Discourse competence involves the following skills:
  - Textual competence: the ability to read and comprehend different types of texts, such as narratives, descriptions, arguments, etc., and to produce coherent and cohesive texts of one's own.
  - Rhetorical competence: the ability to use appropriate language and strategies to achieve a specific purpose, audience, and context, such as persuading, informing, entertaining, etc., and to contribute effectively to a conversation.
  - Genre competence: the ability to recognize and use the conventions and formats of different genres of communication, such as reports, letters, emails, presentations, etc., and to adapt them to different situations and audiences.
  - Lexical competence: the ability to use a range of vocabulary and expressions that are suitable for the topic, level, and style of communication, and to avoid ambiguity, redundancy, and confusion.
- Discourse competence is important for technical communication because it enables the communicator to:
  - Communicate clearly and accurately the technical information and instructions to the intended audience, such as customers, colleagues, managers, etc.
  - Establish credibility and authority as a technical expert and a professional communicator
  - Adapt to the expectations and needs of different discourse communities, such as academic, industrial, scientific, etc., and to use the appropriate terminology, style, and tone for each one
  - Organize and structure the technical information and arguments in a logical and coherent way, using appropriate transitions, headings, summaries, etc.
  - Use appropriate visual aids, such as graphs, tables, diagrams, etc., to support and enhance the verbal communication
  - Avoid plagiarism and acknowledge the sources of information, using the proper citation and referencing systems
