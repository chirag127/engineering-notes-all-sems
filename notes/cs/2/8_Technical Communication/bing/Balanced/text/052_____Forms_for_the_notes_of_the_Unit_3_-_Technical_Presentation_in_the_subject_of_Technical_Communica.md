### Forms for the technical presentation

- A technical presentation is a form of oral communication that conveys technical information to a specific audience, such as experts, managers, customers, or the general public.
- A technical presentation can have different forms depending on the purpose, context, and audience of the communication. Some common forms are:
  - **Informative presentation**: This form aims to inform the audience about a topic, such as a new product, a research finding, a process, or a problem. The presenter provides factual information and explains the main features, benefits, or implications of the topic. The presenter does not try to persuade or convince the audience, but rather to increase their knowledge or awareness.
  - **Persuasive presentation**: This form aims to persuade the audience to adopt a certain point of view, attitude, or action regarding a topic, such as a proposal, a recommendation, a solution, or a policy. The presenter provides factual information and logical arguments to support their position and to counter possible objections or alternatives. The presenter also appeals to the audience's emotions, values, or interests to motivate them to accept the presenter's message.
  - **Demonstration presentation**: This form aims to show the audience how to do something, such as how to use a product, how to perform a task, how to operate a system, or how to apply a technique. The presenter provides step-by-step instructions and visual aids to illustrate the procedure and to clarify the key points. The presenter also explains the purpose, benefits, and precautions of the activity and answers any questions from the audience.
  - **Training presentation**: This form aims to teach the audience a new skill, knowledge, or behavior related to a topic, such as how to work with a software, how to conduct a test, how to handle a situation, or how to follow a protocol. The presenter provides clear objectives, relevant information, practical examples, and interactive exercises to facilitate the learning process. The presenter also checks the audience's understanding, provides feedback, and summarizes the main points.
- A technical presentation can also combine different forms depending on the scope and complexity of the topic. For example, a presentation can start with an informative introduction, followed by a persuasive argument, and end with a demonstration or a training session. The presenter should choose the appropriate form or forms based on the communication goals, the audience's needs, and the time and resources available.
