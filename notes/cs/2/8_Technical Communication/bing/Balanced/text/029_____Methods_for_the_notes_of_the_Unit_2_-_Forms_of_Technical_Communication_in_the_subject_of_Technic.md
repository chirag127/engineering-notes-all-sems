### Methods for the notes of the Unit 2 - Forms of Technical Communication in the subject of Technical Communication

- Forms of technical communication are the ways in which technical information is conveyed to different audiences and for different purposes.
- Some common forms of technical communication are: reports, proposals, manuals, instructions, memos, letters, emails, presentations, websites, blogs, podcasts, wikis, etc.
- Each form of technical communication has its own characteristics, conventions, and advantages and disadvantages.
- To take effective notes on the forms of technical communication, one can use the following methods:

  - **Outline method**: This method involves writing the main topics and subtopics of the unit in a hierarchical structure, using indentation, bullets, or numbers. This method helps to organize the information in a logical and concise way, and to show the relationships between the topics and subtopics. For example:

    - Forms of technical communication
      - Reports
        - Definition
        - Types
        - Structure
        - Style
      - Proposals
        - Definition
        - Types
        - Structure
        - Style
      - Manuals
        - Definition
        - Types
        - Structure
        - Style
      - ...

  - **Chart method**: This method involves creating a table or a matrix to compare and contrast the different forms of technical communication based on some criteria, such as purpose, audience, format, tone, etc. This method helps to highlight the similarities and differences between the forms, and to visualize the information in a clear and organized way. For example:

    | Form | Purpose | Audience | Format | Tone |
    |------|---------|----------|--------|------|
    | Reports | To inform, analyze, or persuade about a topic or a problem | Technical, managerial, or general | Written, oral, or multimedia | Formal, objective, precise |
    | Proposals | To offer a solution, a plan, or a recommendation for a problem or a need | Technical, managerial, or general | Written, oral, or multimedia | Formal, persuasive, concise |
    | Manuals | To instruct, guide, or explain how to use a product or a system | Technical, non-technical, or mixed | Written, oral, or multimedia | Informal, clear, simple |
    | ...

  - **Summary method**: This method involves writing a brief overview of the main points and key concepts of the unit in your own words, using sentences or paragraphs. This method helps to condense the information in a comprehensive and coherent way, and to test your understanding and recall of the unit. For example:

    - The unit covers the various forms of technical communication that are used to communicate technical information to different audiences and for different purposes. Some of the forms discussed are reports, proposals, manuals, instructions, memos, letters, emails, presentations, websites, blogs, podcasts, and wikis. Each form has its own definition, types, structure, and style, and requires different skills and strategies to produce and deliver effectively. The unit also provides examples and guidelines for each form of technical communication.
