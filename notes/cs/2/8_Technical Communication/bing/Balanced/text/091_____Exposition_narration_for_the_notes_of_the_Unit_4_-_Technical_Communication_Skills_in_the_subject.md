### Exposition narration for the notes of the Unit 4 - Technical Communication Skills in the subject of Technical Communication

- Exposition narration is a type of writing that explains, informs, or describes something to the reader.
- Exposition narration can be used for various purposes, such as:
  - To provide background information or context for a topic or situation.
  - To define or clarify a term, concept, or process.
  - To compare or contrast two or more things or ideas.
  - To classify or categorize something into groups or types.
  - To analyze or evaluate something based on criteria or evidence.
  - To propose or argue for a solution, opinion, or recommendation.
- Exposition narration can be organized in different ways, depending on the purpose and the audience. Some common methods of organization are:
  - Chronological: presenting the information in the order of time or sequence.
  - Spatial: presenting the information in the order of space or location.
  - Cause and effect: presenting the information in terms of the causes and effects or the reasons and results of something.
  - Problem and solution: presenting the information in terms of the problem and the solution or the challenge and the response to something.
  - Topical: presenting the information in terms of the main topics or subtopics related to something.
- Exposition narration should have a clear and specific thesis statement that states the main idea or purpose of the writing. The thesis statement should be supported by relevant and sufficient details, examples, facts, statistics, quotations, or other evidence. The writing should also have a logical and coherent structure, with clear transitions and connections between the paragraphs and sentences. The writing should also use appropriate language, tone, style, and format for the intended audience and purpose. The writing should also be revised and edited for accuracy, clarity, completeness, and correctness.
