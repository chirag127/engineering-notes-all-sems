### Vowel sounds for the notes of the Unit 5 - Dimensions of Oral Communication & Voice Dynamics in the subject of Technical Communication

- Vowel sounds are created when air flows smoothly, without interruption, through the throat and mouth.
- Different vowel sounds are produced as a speaker changes the shape and placement of articulators (parts of the throat and mouth), such as the tongue, jaws and lips .
- Vowels are contrasted with consonants, which represent sounds where air is obstructed or interrupted, such as the closing of the lips for b, m, and p.
- In English, the vowels are a, e, i, o, and u, although y can sometimes count as a vowel, too.
- Vowels are further divided into two categories that are monophthongs (pure vowels) and diphthongs (vowel glides).
- A monophthong is a vowel sound that has a single, steady quality, such as the a in cat, the e in bed, the i in sit, the o in hot, and the u in cup.
- A diphthong is a vowel sound that consists of two elements: a vowel and a glide. The vowel is the first part of the sound and the glide is the second part. The glide moves the tongue from one position to another, such as the ai in rain, the ea in bear, the ou in house, and the oy in boy.
- A vowel sound is described by four criteria: the height of the tongue, the front-back position of the tongue, the shape of the lips, and the length of the sound.
- The height of the tongue refers to how high or low the tongue is in the mouth. There are three levels of tongue height: high, mid, and low.
- The front-back position of the tongue refers to how far forward or backward the tongue is in the mouth. There are three positions of tongue front-back: front, central, and back.
- The shape of the lips refers to how rounded or spread the lips are. There are two shapes of lip rounding: rounded and unrounded.
- The length of the sound refers to how long or short the vowel sound is. There are two lengths of vowel sounds: long and short.
- For example, the vowel sound in see is a high, front, unrounded, long vowel, while the vowel sound in sit is a high, front, unrounded, short vowel.
- Vowel sounds are important for oral communication and voice dynamics because they affect the clarity, tone, and expression of speech.
- Vowel sounds can be modified by stress, intonation, and pitch to convey different meanings and emotions.
- Vowel sounds can also be influenced by the surrounding consonants, the syllable structure, and the word stress.
- To improve the pronunciation and quality of vowel sounds, one should practice listening, speaking, and recording vowel sounds, and compare them with native speakers or standard models.
