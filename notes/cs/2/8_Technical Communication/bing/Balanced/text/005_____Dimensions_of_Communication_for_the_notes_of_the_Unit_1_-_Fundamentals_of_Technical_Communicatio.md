### Dimensions of Communication

Communication is the process of exchanging information, ideas, feelings, emotions, and thoughts between two or more people. Communication can be verbal or nonverbal, written or visual, formal or informal, and intentional or unintentional. Communication can also be classified into different dimensions based on the direction, level, and context of the communication. Some of the common dimensions of communication are:

- **Downward communication**: This is the communication that flows from a higher level to a lower level in an organization or a group. For example, a manager communicating with an employee, a teacher communicating with a student, or a parent communicating with a child. The purpose of downward communication is to provide instructions, feedback, information, or guidance to the subordinates or followers.
- **Upward communication**: This is the communication that flows from a lower level to a higher level in an organization or a group. For example, an employee communicating with a manager, a student communicating with a teacher, or a child communicating with a parent. The purpose of upward communication is to provide feedback, information, suggestions, or complaints to the superiors or leaders.
- **Horizontal or lateral communication**: This is the communication that occurs among people at the same level in an organization or a group. For example, colleagues communicating with each other, classmates communicating with each other, or friends communicating with each other. The purpose of horizontal communication is to coordinate, cooperate, share, or socialize with the peers or equals.
- **Diagonal or cross-wise communication**: This is the communication that occurs among people at different levels and different functions in an organization or a group. For example, a marketing manager communicating with an accounting manager, a student communicating with a principal, or a customer communicating with a salesperson. The purpose of diagonal communication is to facilitate problem-solving, decision-making, or information-sharing across different departments or units.
- **Verbal or nonverbal communication**: This is the communication that involves the use of words or the absence of words to convey a message. Verbal communication can be spoken or written, and it can be formal or informal. Nonverbal communication can be gestures, facial expressions, body language, eye contact, or tone of voice. The purpose of verbal communication is to express, inform, persuade, or instruct. The purpose of nonverbal communication is to complement, reinforce, contradict, or regulate the verbal communication.
- **Written or visual communication**: This is the communication that involves the use of symbols, signs, images, or graphics to convey a message. Written communication can be letters, memos, reports, emails, or texts. Visual communication can be charts, graphs, diagrams, pictures, or videos. The purpose of written communication is to document, record, or communicate information that can be referred to later. The purpose of visual communication is to illustrate, clarify, or emphasize the information that is presented verbally or written.
- **Formal or informal communication**: This is the communication that follows or does not follow the established rules, norms, or protocols of an organization or a group. Formal communication can be official, structured, or planned. Informal communication can be casual, spontaneous, or unplanned. The purpose of formal communication is to ensure accuracy, consistency, or accountability of the communication. The purpose of informal communication is to build rapport, trust, or relationships among the communicators.
- **Intentional or unintentional communication**: This is the communication that is deliberate or accidental in sending or receiving a message. Intentional communication can be conscious, purposeful, or planned. Unintentional communication can be subconscious, unintended, or unplanned. The purpose of intentional communication is to achieve a specific goal, outcome, or effect. The purpose of unintentional communication is to reveal the hidden, underlying, or subconscious feelings, thoughts, or emotions of the communicators.
