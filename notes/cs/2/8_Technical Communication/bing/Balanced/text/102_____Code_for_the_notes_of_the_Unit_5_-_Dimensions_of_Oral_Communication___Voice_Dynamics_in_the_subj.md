### Code for the notes of the Unit 5 - Dimensions of Oral Communication & Voice Dynamics in the subject of Technical Communication

- Dimensions of oral communication are the aspects that affect the effectiveness and quality of spoken messages.
- Some of the dimensions are code and content, stimulus and response, encoding and decoding, pronunciation, syllables, vowel sounds, consonant sounds, stress, intonation, and voice modulation.
- Code and content refer to the choice of words and the meaning conveyed by them in a given context.
- Stimulus and response refer to the factors that trigger and influence the communication process, such as the purpose, audience, situation, and feedback.
- Encoding and decoding refer to the process of transforming thoughts into verbal symbols and interpreting them by the sender and the receiver respectively.
- Pronunciation refers to the correct and clear articulation of words according to the standard norms of a language.
- Syllables refer to the units of sound that make up a word, such as cat (one syllable), happy (two syllables), and banana (three syllables).
- Vowel sounds refer to the sounds produced by the vibration of the vocal cords without any obstruction in the mouth, such as a, e, i, o, u.
- Consonant sounds refer to the sounds produced by the partial or complete obstruction of the air flow in the mouth, such as b, c, d, f, g, etc.
- Stress refers to the emphasis given to a syllable or a word to convey meaning or emotion, such as CONtent (noun) and conTENT (adjective).
- Intonation refers to the rise and fall of the pitch of the voice to express different tones or attitudes, such as a question, a statement, a surprise, etc.
- Voice modulation refers to the variation of the volume, speed, and tone of the voice to make the speech more engaging, expressive, and persuasive.
