### Etiquette for the notes of the Unit 5 - Dimensions of Oral Communication & Voice Dynamics in the subject of Technical Communication

- Etiquette is the set of rules and norms that govern the appropriate and polite behavior in oral communication.
- Etiquette can vary depending on the context, culture, audience, purpose, and mode of oral communication.
- Some general principles of etiquette for oral communication are:

  - Be respectful and courteous to the speaker and the listeners.
  - Be clear, concise, and coherent in your speech.
  - Use appropriate language, tone, and volume for the situation.
  - Avoid interrupting, dominating, or distracting the conversation.
  - Listen actively and attentively to the other participants.
  - Provide constructive feedback and ask relevant questions.
  - Acknowledge and address any misunderstandings or conflicts.
  - Express appreciation and gratitude for the opportunity to communicate.

- Dimensions of oral communication are the aspects or elements that affect the quality and effectiveness of oral communication.
- Some dimensions of oral communication are:

  - Content: the information, ideas, or messages that are conveyed in oral communication.
  - Context: the situation, environment, or circumstances in which oral communication takes place.
  - Audience: the people who receive, interpret, or respond to oral communication.
  - Purpose: the goal, objective, or intention of oral communication.
  - Mode: the medium, channel, or format of oral communication.
  - Style: the manner, tone, or expression of oral communication.

- Voice dynamics are the variations or changes in the vocal characteristics of oral communication.
- Voice dynamics can enhance or diminish the impact and appeal of oral communication.
- Some voice dynamics are:

  - Pitch: the highness or lowness of the voice.
  - Volume: the loudness or softness of the voice.
  - Rate: the speed or pace of the voice.
  - Pause: the silence or break in the voice.
  - Emphasis: the stress or accent on certain words or phrases.
  - Inflection: the rise or fall of the voice.
  - Modulation: the change or adjustment of the voice.
  - Articulation: the clarity or precision of the voice.
  - Pronunciation: the correctness or accuracy of the voice.
  - Fluency: the smoothness or flow of the voice.
