### Humour

- Humour is the quality of being amusing or funny, or the ability to make others laugh.
- Humour can be used in presentations to capture the audience's attention, create a bond with them, and make the presentation more memorable.
- Humour can also help to reduce tension, stress, and boredom, and to enhance the speaker's credibility and likability.
- However, humour should be used carefully and appropriately, as it can also backfire if it is offensive, irrelevant, or overdone.
- Here are some tips and techniques for using humour in technical presentations:

  - Know your audience and their expectations, preferences, and sensitivities. Avoid jokes that are racist, sexist, political, religious, or personal.
  - Know your topic and its seriousness, complexity, and relevance. Avoid jokes that are too trivial, obscure, or unrelated to the main message.
  - Know your purpose and your goal. Use humour to support, not distract from, your main points and arguments. Use humour to persuade, not to entertain.
  - Know your style and your strengths. Use humour that suits your personality, voice, and delivery. Use humour that you are comfortable and confident with, not that you have copied or borrowed from others.
  - Use humour sparingly and strategically. Don't overdo it or force it. Use humour at the beginning, end, or transitions of your presentation, or to illustrate or emphasize a key point.
  - Use humour creatively and effectively. Use different types of humour, such as anecdotes, analogies, metaphors, puns, irony, sarcasm, exaggeration, understatement, or self-deprecation. Use different modes of humour, such as verbal, visual, or audio. Use humour that is relevant, original, and surprising.
