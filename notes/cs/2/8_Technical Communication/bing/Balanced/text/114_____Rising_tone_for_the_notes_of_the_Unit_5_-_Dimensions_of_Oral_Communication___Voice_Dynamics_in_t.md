### Rising tone

- A rising tone is an intonation pattern in which the pitch of the voice rises over time.
- A rising tone is mainly used for:
  - Questions with a yes/no answer , such as "Are you thirsty?"
  - Requests for clarification or confirmation (indicates uncertainty) , such as "So, is that the new doctor?"
  - High energy emotions such as happiness, anger, or shock , such as "Wow!" or "No way!"
- A rising tone can also convey different attitudes or meanings depending on the context and the speaker, such as:
  - Interest, curiosity, or surprise, such as "Really?" or "How interesting!"
  - Impatience, annoyance, or challenge, such as "What do you want?" or "Are you kidding me?"
  - Politeness, hesitation, or deference, such as "Excuse me?" or "Could you please repeat that?"
  - Incompleteness, continuation, or anticipation, such as "And then?" or "So..."
- A rising tone can be marked with a diagonal line (/) above the stressed syllable of the word where the pitch rises, such as /thirsty, /doctor, or /way.
- A rising tone can be practiced by using a musical scale or a slide whistle to mimic the change in pitch, or by listening to and imitating examples of rising tone sentences .
