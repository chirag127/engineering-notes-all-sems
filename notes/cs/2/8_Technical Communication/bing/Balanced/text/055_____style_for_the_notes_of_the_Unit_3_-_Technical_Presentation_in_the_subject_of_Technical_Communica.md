### Style for the notes of the Unit 3 - Technical Presentation in the subject of Technical Communication

- Style is the way a speaker or writer uses language to communicate a message to the audience.
- Style can affect the clarity, persuasiveness, and professionalism of a technical presentation.
- Some elements of style that are important for technical presentations are:

  - Word choice: Use precise, accurate, and familiar words that suit the purpose and audience of the presentation. Avoid jargon, slang, clichés, and vague or ambiguous terms.
  - Sentence structure: Use clear, concise, and varied sentences that express one main idea each. Avoid long, complex, or run-on sentences that may confuse or bore the audience.
  - Tone: Use a tone that is appropriate for the context, purpose, and audience of the presentation. Avoid being too formal or informal, too emotional or detached, too arrogant or humble, or too biased or neutral.
  - Voice: Use a voice that reflects your personality, credibility, and relationship with the audience. Avoid using passive voice, which may sound weak or evasive, or using first-person or second-person pronouns, which may sound too personal or direct.
  - Visuals: Use visuals that support and enhance your verbal message, such as graphs, charts, diagrams, images, or videos. Avoid using visuals that are irrelevant, distracting, or misleading, or that overload the audience with too much information.
