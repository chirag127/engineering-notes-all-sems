### Direct Memory Access

- Direct memory access (DMA) is a feature of computer systems that allows certain hardware subsystems to access main system memory independently of the central processing unit (CPU) .
- DMA can improve the performance and efficiency of memory operations by reducing the CPU involvement and allowing the CPU to perform other tasks concurrently .
- DMA is managed by a dedicated hardware device called a DMA controller (DMAC) that communicates with the CPU and the I/O devices .
- The DMA controller can transfer data between memory and I/O devices in different modes, such as single transfer, block transfer, demand transfer, and burst transfer .
- The DMA controller can also use different addressing schemes, such as fixed, incrementing, decrementing, and modulo .
- The DMA controller can operate in different channels, such as memory-to-memory, memory-to-device, device-to-memory, and device-to-device .
- The DMA controller can generate interrupts to the CPU to signal the completion or error of a transfer .
- The DMA controller can use different arbitration schemes, such as fixed priority, rotating priority, and bus request .
- The advantages of DMA are faster data transfer, reduced CPU overhead, and increased concurrency .
- The disadvantages of DMA are increased hardware complexity, increased memory contention, and increased power consumption .
