### Synchronous & asynchronous communication

Synchronous and asynchronous communication are two modes of data transmission that differ in the timing, speed, and coordination of the sender and receiver.

- **Synchronous communication** occurs when the sender and receiver are both active at the same time and can exchange data in real time. Examples of synchronous communication are phone calls, video calls, and face-to-face conversations. Synchronous communication has the following characteristics:

  - It requires scheduling and coordination between the parties involved.
  - It allows for immediate feedback and clarification of the message.
  - It can be more engaging and interactive than asynchronous communication.
  - It can also be more disruptive and distracting than asynchronous communication.

- **Asynchronous communication** occurs when the sender and receiver are not active at the same time and can exchange data at their own convenience. Examples of asynchronous communication are emails, text messages, and voicemails. Asynchronous communication has the following characteristics:

  - It does not require scheduling and coordination between the parties involved.
  - It allows for more flexibility and convenience of the message.
  - It can be more efficient and productive than synchronous communication.
  - It can also be more delayed and ambiguous than synchronous communication.

Synchronous and asynchronous communication can be used for different purposes and situations, depending on the context, urgency, and complexity of the message. Both modes of communication have advantages and disadvantages, and it is important to choose the appropriate one for the desired outcome.
