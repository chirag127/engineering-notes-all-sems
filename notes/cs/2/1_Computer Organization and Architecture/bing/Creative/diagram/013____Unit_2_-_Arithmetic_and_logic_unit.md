## Unit 2 - Arithmetic and logic unit

- An arithmetic and logic unit (ALU) is a digital circuit that performs arithmetic and logical operations on binary numbers.
- The ALU is one of the core components of the central processing unit (CPU) of a computer, along with the control unit and the memory unit.
- The ALU can perform basic operations such as addition, subtraction, multiplication, division, and bitwise operations such as AND, OR, XOR, and NOT.
- The ALU can also perform more complex operations such as shifting, rotating, comparing, and counting.
- The ALU receives two input operands (A and B) and a set of control signals from the control unit, and produces an output result (R) and a status flag (F).
- The control signals determine which operation the ALU performs on the input operands, and the status flag indicates the outcome of the operation, such as zero, carry, overflow, or sign.
- The ALU can be implemented using combinational logic circuits, such as multiplexers, adders, subtractors, and logic gates.
- The ALU can also be implemented using sequential logic circuits, such as registers, counters, and shifters.
- The ALU can be designed using different architectures, such as ripple-carry, carry-lookahead, carry-select, or carry-save, depending on the trade-off between speed, cost, and complexity.
- The ALU can be optimized for different performance metrics, such as latency, throughput, power consumption, and area.
