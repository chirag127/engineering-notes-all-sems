### Auxiliary memories

- Auxiliary memories are also known as **secondary memories** or **external memories** .
- They are **non-volatile** storage devices that can retain data even when the power is off  .
- They have **higher capacity**, **lower cost**, and **slower access** than primary memories (such as RAM and ROM)  .
- They are used to store programs and data that are **not in direct use** or that need **long-term preservation**   .
- They are connected to the computer system through **input/output** devices and **controllers** .
- Some common examples of auxiliary memories are **magnetic disks**, **magnetic tapes**, **optical disks**, **flash drives**, and **cloud storage**  .

#### Magnetic disks

- Magnetic disks are circular platters coated with a magnetic material that can store data in the form of **magnetic patterns**  .
- They are divided into **tracks** (concentric circles) and **sectors** (pie-shaped wedges) that form the basic units of storage called **blocks** or **clusters**  .
- They can be accessed by a **read/write head** that moves over the surface of the disk and changes the magnetic polarity of the disk  .
- They can be classified into two types: **hard disks** and **floppy disks**  .

##### Hard disks

- Hard disks are rigid platters that are stacked together and enclosed in a sealed case  .
- They have a **high density**, **high speed**, and **high capacity** of storage  .
- They are usually fixed inside the computer system and used as the main storage device  .
- They can store data ranging from **gigabytes** to **terabytes**  .

##### Floppy disks

- Floppy disks are flexible platters that are enclosed in a plastic cover  .
- They have a **low density**, **low speed**, and **low capacity** of storage  .
- They are usually removable and used as a portable storage device  .
- They can store data ranging from **kilobytes** to **megabytes**  .

#### Magnetic tapes

- Magnetic tapes are long strips of plastic coated with a magnetic material that can store data in the form of **magnetic patterns**  .
- They are divided into **frames** that contain fixed-length **records** of data  .
- They can be accessed by a **tape drive** that moves the tape past a read/write head and changes the magnetic polarity of the tape  .
- They have a **sequential access** mode, which means that data can only be accessed in the order it was written  .
- They have a **high capacity** and **low cost** of storage, but a **low speed** and **low reliability**  .
- They are usually used for **backup** and **archival** purposes  .
- They can store data ranging from **megabytes** to **petabytes**  .
