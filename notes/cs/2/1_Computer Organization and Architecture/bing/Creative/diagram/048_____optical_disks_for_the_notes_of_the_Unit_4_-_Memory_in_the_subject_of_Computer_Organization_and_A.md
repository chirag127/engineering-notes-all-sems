### Optical Disks

- Optical disks are electronic data storage media that can be written to and read from using a low-powered laser beam .
- Optical disks can store analog information, digital information, or both on the same disk.
- Optical disks are often stored in special cases sometimes called jewel cases and are most commonly used for digital preservation, storing music, video, or data and programs for personal computers (PC).
- Optical disks can be reflective, where the light source and detector are on the same side of the disk, or transmissive, where light shines through the disk to the be detected on the other side.
- Optical disks can be classified into three types based on how they are written and read: read-only (ROM), write-once (R), and rewritable (RW).
- Read-only optical disks are pre-recorded and cannot be modified by the user. Examples are CD-ROM, DVD-ROM, and BD-ROM.
- Write-once optical disks can be written by the user once and then become read-only. Examples are CD-R, DVD-R, and BD-R.
- Rewritable optical disks can be written and erased multiple times by the user. Examples are CD-RW, DVD-RW, and BD-RE.
- Optical disks have different capacities and data transfer rates depending on the format and the number of layers and sides. The most common formats are compact disks (CD), digital versatile disks (DVD), and Blu-ray disks (BD).
- Compact disks have a capacity of about 700 MB and a data transfer rate of about 1.2 MB/s. They use a red laser with a wavelength of 780 nm to read and write data.
- Digital versatile disks have a capacity of about 4.7 GB for a single-layer, single-sided disk and up to 17 GB for a double-layer, double-sided disk. They use a red laser with a wavelength of 650 nm to read and write data.
- Blu-ray disks have a capacity of about 25 GB for a single-layer, single-sided disk and up to 100 GB for a quadruple-layer, single-sided disk. They use a blue laser with a wavelength of 405 nm to read and write data.
- Optical disks require an optical disc drive (ODD) in a computer system to use them. An ODD consists of a spindle motor, a laser assembly, a tracking mechanism, and a data interface .
- The spindle motor rotates the disk at a constant angular velocity (CAV) or a constant linear velocity (CLV) depending on the format.
- The laser assembly consists of a laser diode, a lens, and a photodiode. The laser diode emits a laser beam that is focused by the lens onto the surface of the disk. The photodiode detects the reflected light from the disk and converts it into electrical signals.
- The tracking mechanism consists of a sled motor and a servo system. The sled motor moves the laser assembly radially across the disk to access different tracks. The servo system controls the position and focus of the laser beam to ensure accurate reading and writing.
- The data interface consists of a controller chip and a buffer memory. The controller chip converts the electrical signals from the photodiode into digital data and vice versa. The buffer memory temporarily stores the data to compensate for the speed difference between the disk and the computer.
