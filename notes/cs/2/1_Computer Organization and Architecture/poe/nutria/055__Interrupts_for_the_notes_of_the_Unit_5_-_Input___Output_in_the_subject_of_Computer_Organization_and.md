
### Interrupts for the Notes of the Unit 5 - Input/Output in the Subject of Computer Organization and Architecture

1. An interrupt is a signal sent to the processor to indicate an event has occurred.
2. Interrupts can be generated by hardware, software, or a combination of both.
3. Hardware interrupts are generated by devices such as a keyboard, mouse, or network card.
4. Software interrupts are generated by the processor itself, usually in response to a system call.
5. Interrupts can be classified into two types: maskable interrupts and non-maskable interrupts.
6. Maskable interrupts can be disabled or enabled by the processor.
7. Non-maskable interrupts cannot be disabled or enabled by the processor and must be serviced immediately.
8. Interrupts can be handled in two ways: polling and vectored interrupts.
9. Polling is a process where the processor continuously checks for interrupts.
10. Vectored interrupts are handled by the processor jumping to a specific address in memory to execute an interrupt service routine.
