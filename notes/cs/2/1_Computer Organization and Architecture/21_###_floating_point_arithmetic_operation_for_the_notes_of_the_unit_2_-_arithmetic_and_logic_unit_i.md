Floating point arithmetic is a method of representing real numbers in a computer system. It is used to perform arithmetic operations on real numbers, such as addition, subtraction, multiplication, and division.

In floating point arithmetic, real numbers are represented as a combination of a mantissa and an exponent. The mantissa represents the significant digits of the number, and the exponent represents the power of 10 by which the mantissa should be multiplied.

Floating point arithmetic is performed using a floating point unit (FPU), which is a specialized hardware unit in the CPU that performs floating point operations. The FPU is designed to perform floating point operations quickly and accurately, and is an important component of the CPU in many computer systems.
