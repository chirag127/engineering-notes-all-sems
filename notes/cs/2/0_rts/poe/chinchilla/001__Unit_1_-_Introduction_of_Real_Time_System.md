## Unit 1 - Introduction of Real Time System

Real-time systems are computer systems that are designed to respond to external events in a timely and predictable manner. These systems are used in a variety of applications, from military and aerospace systems to industrial control and automation systems.

Here are some key points to keep in mind when studying real-time systems:

- Real-time systems must be able to respond to external events within a specified time frame, otherwise the system may fail or produce incorrect results.
- There are two main types of real-time systems: hard real-time systems and soft real-time systems. Hard real-time systems have strict timing requirements and must complete their tasks within a specific time frame, while soft real-time systems have more relaxed timing requirements and can tolerate some delays.
- Real-time systems are often used in safety-critical applications, such as in aircraft and medical devices, where failure could have serious consequences.
- To design real-time systems, it is important to understand the timing requirements of the system and to use appropriate scheduling algorithms to ensure that tasks are executed in a timely and predictable manner.
- Real-time systems often use specialized hardware and software, such as real-time operating systems and dedicated processors, to meet their timing requirements.
- Real-time systems can be challenging to test and debug, since their behavior is often dependent on the timing of external events.
- Real-time systems are becoming increasingly important in the age of the Internet of Things (IoT), where many devices are connected and must respond to external events in a timely manner.

In summary, real-time systems are an important and challenging area of computer science that are used in a variety of critical applications. Understanding the timing requirements of these systems and using appropriate scheduling algorithms and hardware is key to their successful design and implementation.
