
### Priority Driven Approach for the notes of the Unit 2 - Real Time Scheduling in the subject of Real Time System

1. Priority-driven scheduling is a scheduling algorithm used in real-time systems. It ensures that tasks with higher priority are completed before those with lower priority.

2. Priority scheduling algorithms can be either preemptive or non-preemptive. In preemptive scheduling, a higher priority task can interrupt a lower priority task. In non-preemptive scheduling, lower priority tasks must wait until higher priority tasks are completed.

3. Priority scheduling algorithms are based on priority levels assigned to tasks. The priority level assigned to a task determines its place in the queue.

4. Priority scheduling algorithms can be static or dynamic. In static priority scheduling, the priority of a task is fixed and does not change over time. In dynamic priority scheduling, the priority of a task can change over time.

5. Priority scheduling algorithms can also be static or dynamic. In static priority scheduling, the priority of a task is fixed and does not change over time. In dynamic priority scheduling, the priority of a task can change over time.

6. Priority scheduling algorithms can be used to schedule tasks in real-time systems. They can be used to ensure that tasks with higher priority are completed before those with lower priority. This helps to ensure that the system meets its deadlines.
