
### Medium Access Control Protocols for Broadcast Networks

* Medium Access Control (MAC) protocols are responsible for regulating access to shared mediums such as wireless networks.
* MAC protocols for broadcast networks are designed to ensure that all nodes in the network have a fair access to the medium and that no single node can monopolize it.
* The most popular MAC protocols for broadcast networks are Carrier Sense Multiple Access (CSMA) and Token Passing.
* CSMA works by allowing nodes to transmit only when the medium is free, while Token Passing works by allowing only the node that is holding a token to transmit.
* Both CSMA and Token Passing protocols can be further subdivided into various categories such as CSMA/CD and CSMA/CA, and Token Ring and Token Bus.
* Each of these protocols has its own advantages and disadvantages, and should be chosen based on the network’s requirements.
