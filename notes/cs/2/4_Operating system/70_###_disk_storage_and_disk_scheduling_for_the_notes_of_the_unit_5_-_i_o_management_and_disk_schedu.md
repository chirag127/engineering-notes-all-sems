### Disk storage and disk scheduling for the notes of the Unit 5 - I/O Management and Disk Scheduling in the subject of Operating system

Disk storage and disk scheduling are important concepts in operating systems that deal with the management of input/output (I/O) operations. Disk storage refers to the physical storage of data on a disk, while disk scheduling refers to the process of allocating disk resources to processes in a computer system.

In the Unit 5 - I/O Management and Disk Scheduling of the subject of Operating System, students learn about the concepts and algorithms involved in I/O management and disk scheduling. The notes for this unit should include the following topics:

1. Definition of disk storage and disk scheduling and their purpose (I/O management, disk resource allocation)
2. Overview of disk storage and disk scheduling in computer systems (physical storage, process allocation)
3. Disk storage technologies (magnetic disk, solid-state disk)
4. Disk scheduling algorithms (FCFS, SSTF, SCAN, C-SCAN, LOOK, CLOOK)
5. Performance analysis of disk scheduling algorithms (time complexity, disk utilization)
6. Comparison of disk scheduling algorithms (FCFS, SSTF, SCAN, C-SCAN, LOOK, CLOOK)

Disk storage and disk scheduling are important parts of learning I/O management and disk scheduling. They provide a foundation for understanding the concepts and algorithms involved in disk resource allocation and help students to understand the role of operating systems in managing I/O operations and disk resources.
