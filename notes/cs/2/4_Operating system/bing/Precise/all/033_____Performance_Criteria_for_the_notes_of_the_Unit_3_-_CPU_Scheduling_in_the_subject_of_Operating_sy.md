### Performance Criteria for CPU Scheduling in Operating System

CPU scheduling is the process of allocating CPU time to different processes in an operating system. There are several performance criteria that are used to evaluate the effectiveness of a CPU scheduling algorithm. These criteria include:

1. **CPU Utilization**: The main objective of any CPU scheduling algorithm is to keep the CPU as busy as possible. Theoretically, CPU utilization can range from 0 to 100 but in a real-time system, it varies from 40 to 90 percent depending on the load upon the system .

2. **Throughput**: A measure of the work done by the CPU is the number of processes being executed and completed per unit of time .

3. **Turnaround Time**: For a particular process, turnaround time is the amount of time taken from the submission of the process to the completion of the process .

4. **Waiting Time**: Waiting time is the amount of time a process has been waiting in the ready queue .

5. **Response Time**: Response time is the amount of time it takes from when a request was submitted until the first response is produced .

These criteria are used to evaluate the performance of different CPU scheduling algorithms and to determine which algorithm is best suited for a particular system. Different algorithms may perform better or worse depending on the specific workload and system configuration.
