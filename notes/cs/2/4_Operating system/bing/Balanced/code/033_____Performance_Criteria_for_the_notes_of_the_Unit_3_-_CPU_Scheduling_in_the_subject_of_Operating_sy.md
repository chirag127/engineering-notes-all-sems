### Performance Criteria for CPU Scheduling

CPU scheduling is the process of allocating the CPU to different processes that are ready to execute. CPU scheduling aims to optimize the performance of the system by maximizing the CPU utilization, minimizing the waiting time, and ensuring fairness among the processes. Different CPU scheduling algorithms have different advantages and disadvantages, and they can be evaluated based on the following criteria   :

- **CPU utilization**: This is the percentage of time the CPU is busy executing processes. A high CPU utilization means that the CPU is not idle and is efficiently used. A low CPU utilization means that the CPU is wasting time and resources. Theoretically, CPU utilization can range from 0 to 100%, but in a real system, it varies from 40 to 90% depending on the load on the system .
- **Throughput**: This is the number of processes that complete their execution per unit time. A high throughput means that the system can handle more processes in a given time. A low throughput means that the system is slow and unproductive. Throughput depends on the length of the processes and the CPU scheduling algorithm .
- **Turnaround time**: This is the amount of time it takes for a process to finish its execution, from the time it is submitted to the system until the time it is terminated. Turnaround time includes the waiting time, the CPU time, and the I/O time of the process. A low turnaround time means that the process can finish quickly and free up the resources for other processes. A high turnaround time means that the process takes longer to complete and delays the system performance  .
- **Waiting time**: This is the amount of time a process has been waiting in the ready queue before it gets the CPU. Waiting time does not include the CPU time or the I/O time of the process. A low waiting time means that the process can get the CPU soon after it is ready. A high waiting time means that the process has to wait longer in the queue and suffer from starvation. Waiting time affects the turnaround time and the user satisfaction  .
- **Response time**: This is the amount of time it takes from when a request is made by a process until the first response is produced by the system. Response time does not include the total execution time of the process. A low response time means that the system can respond quickly to the user's request. A high response time means that the system is slow and unresponsive. Response time is important for interactive processes that need immediate feedback from the system .

Different CPU scheduling algorithms may have different trade-offs among these criteria. For example, a CPU scheduling algorithm that favors short processes may have a low turnaround time and a high throughput, but it may also have a high waiting time and a low CPU utilization. A CPU scheduling algorithm that favors long processes may have a high CPU utilization and a low waiting time, but it may also have a high turnaround time and a low throughput. Therefore, the choice of the CPU scheduling algorithm depends on the system goals and the characteristics of the processes  .
