### Classical Problems in Concurrency

Concurrency is the ability of different parts or units of a program, algorithm, or problem to be executed out-of-order or in partial order, without affecting the outcome. Concurrency introduces challenges such as synchronization, communication, coordination, deadlock, starvation, and race conditions among concurrent processes or threads. To illustrate these challenges and how to overcome them, several classical problems have been proposed and studied in the literature. These problems are:

- The producer-consumer problem: This problem involves two types of processes, producers and consumers, that share a finite buffer pool to exchange messages. Producers generate messages and put them into the buffer, while consumers take messages from the buffer and process them. The challenge is to synchronize the access to the buffer such that producers do not overwrite messages that have not been consumed, and consumers do not read empty slots or the same message twice .
- The dining philosophers problem: This problem involves n philosophers sitting around a circular table, each with a chopstick between them. There is a bowl of rice in the center of the table. Each philosopher alternates between thinking and eating. To eat, a philosopher needs to pick up both chopsticks on his sides. The challenge is to avoid deadlock, where no philosopher can eat because they are all waiting for a chopstick, and starvation, where some philosophers are deprived of eating for a long time .
- The readers-writers problem: This problem involves two types of processes, readers and writers, that access a shared data structure. Readers only read the data, while writers can read and modify the data. The challenge is to allow multiple readers to access the data simultaneously, but only one writer at a time, and to prevent writers from starving due to the presence of many readers.
- The sleeping barber problem: This problem involves a barber shop with one barber, one barber chair, and n waiting chairs. Customers arrive at the shop randomly. If the barber is free, they get their hair cut immediately. If the barber is busy, they wait in one of the chairs. If all the chairs are occupied, they leave the shop. The challenge is to synchronize the barber and the customers such that the barber does not sleep when there are customers waiting, and the customers do not occupy the chairs when the barber is free.

These problems can be solved using various synchronization mechanisms, such as semaphores, locks, monitors, condition variables, message passing, etc. The solutions should ensure the correctness, safety, and liveness of the concurrent processes or threads.
