### Schedulers for the notes of the Unit 3 - CPU Scheduling in the subject of Operating system

- Schedulers are the components of the operating system that decide which processes should be executed by the CPU and when.
- Schedulers are responsible for managing the degree of multiprogramming, the utilization of the CPU, and the performance of the processes.
- There are three types of schedulers in operating systems: long-term, mid-term, and short-term schedulers.
- Long-term scheduler: Also known as the admission scheduler or the high-level scheduler, it decides which programs are admitted to the system for execution. It controls the degree of multiprogramming, i.e., the number of processes that are active in memory at a time. It runs infrequently and may involve complex criteria for selection. It can also reject some programs if the system is overloaded or if they do not meet some requirements.
- Mid-term scheduler: Also known as the medium-term scheduler or the swapping scheduler, it decides which processes should be swapped out of memory to the disk and which processes should be swapped in from the disk to the memory. It is used to improve the memory utilization and the mix of I/O-bound and CPU-bound processes. It runs less frequently than the short-term scheduler but more frequently than the long-term scheduler. It may involve some degree of memory management.
- Short-term scheduler: Also known as the dispatcher or the low-level scheduler, it decides which process should be assigned to the CPU next. It runs very frequently and involves minimal overhead. It aims to provide a fair and efficient allocation of the CPU time among the ready processes. It can be either preemptive or non-preemptive, depending on whether it can interrupt a running process or not. It uses various algorithms to select the next process, such as first-come first-served, shortest job first, priority, round robin, etc.
