### Time sharing

- Time sharing is a mode of operation in which multiple users can access a computer system simultaneously through terminals.
- Time sharing allows the computer system to switch rapidly among the users, giving each user a small slice of time (called a time quantum) to execute their programs or commands.
- Time sharing creates the illusion of a dedicated or interactive system for each user, even though the system is actually shared by many users.
- Time sharing improves the utilization and efficiency of the computer system, as it can serve more users with less idle time and less waiting time.
- Time sharing also enables resource sharing, as users can access common files, databases, programs, and devices on the system.
- Time sharing requires a special operating system that can manage the allocation of CPU time, memory, disk space, and other resources among the users and their processes.
- Time sharing operating systems must also provide mechanisms for protection, security, communication, and synchronization among the users and their processes.
- Examples of time sharing operating systems are UNIX, Linux, Windows, and MacOS.
