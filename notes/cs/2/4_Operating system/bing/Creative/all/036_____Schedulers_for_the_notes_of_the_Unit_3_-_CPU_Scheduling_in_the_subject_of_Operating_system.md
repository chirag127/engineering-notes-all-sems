# Schedulers for the notes of the Unit 3 - CPU Scheduling in the subject of Operating system

- Schedulers are the components of the operating system that decide which processes or tasks should be executed by the CPU and when.
- Schedulers are essential for achieving efficient utilization of the CPU and other system resources, as well as providing fairness and responsiveness to the users and applications.
- Schedulers can be classified into three types based on the frequency and scope of their decisions: long-term, mid-term, and short-term schedulers.
- Long-term scheduler (also known as admission scheduler or high-level scheduler) is responsible for controlling the degree of multiprogramming in the system, i.e., the number of processes that are present in the ready queue and can compete for the CPU. It decides which processes are admitted to the system and when, based on factors such as process priority, memory requirements, I/O devices needed, etc. It runs relatively infrequently, e.g., once every few seconds or minutes.
- Mid-term scheduler (also known as medium-term scheduler or swapping scheduler) is responsible for managing the swapping of processes between the main memory and the secondary storage, such as disk. It decides which processes are swapped out of the memory to create space for other processes, and which processes are swapped in from the disk to resume their execution. It runs more frequently than the long-term scheduler, e.g., once every few milliseconds or microseconds.
- Short-term scheduler (also known as CPU scheduler or dispatcher) is responsible for selecting the next process to run on the CPU from the ready queue. It decides which process is allocated the CPU and for how long, based on factors such as process state, CPU burst time, arrival time, deadline, etc. It runs very frequently, e.g., once every few nanoseconds or picoseconds, and must be very fast and efficient.
