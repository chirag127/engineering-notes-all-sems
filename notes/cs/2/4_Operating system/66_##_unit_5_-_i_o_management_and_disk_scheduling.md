## Unit 5 - I/O Management and Disk Scheduling

I/O Management and Disk Scheduling are important components of operating systems, responsible for managing the input and output operations of the system. These components play a critical role in ensuring the efficient and effective use of the system's resources, and in providing a reliable and responsive user experience.

I/O Management is responsible for managing the input and output operations of the system, including the transfer of data between the CPU, memory, and I/O devices. This component is responsible for handling the communication between the operating system and the I/O devices, and for ensuring that the I/O operations are performed efficiently and reliably.

Disk Scheduling is a component of I/O Management that is responsible for managing the allocation of disk resources to processes. Disk Scheduling algorithms determine the order in which disk requests are processed, and play a critical role in ensuring the efficient use of disk resources and in improving the overall performance of the system.

There are several disk scheduling algorithms, including:

1. First-Come, First-Served (FCFS)
2. Shortest Seek Time First (SSTF)
3. SCAN (Elevator Algorithm)
4. Circular SCAN (C-SCAN)

In this unit, we will study the concepts of I/O Management and Disk Scheduling, and examine the role of these components in operating systems. We will also study the various disk scheduling algorithms, and examine the trade-offs involved in using different algorithms. This will provide a foundation for understanding the design and implementation of I/O Management and Disk Scheduling in operating systems, and for exploring the various approaches to improving the performance and reliability of these components.
