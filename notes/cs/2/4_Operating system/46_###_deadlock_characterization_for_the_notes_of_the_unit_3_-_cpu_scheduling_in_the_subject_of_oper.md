### Deadlock characterization for the notes of the Unit 3 - CPU Scheduling in the subject of Operating system

Deadlock characterization is the process of identifying and describing the conditions that lead to a deadlock in a computer system. A deadlock is a situation in which two or more processes are blocked, waiting for each other to release resources.

In the Unit 3 - CPU Scheduling of the subject of Operating system, students learn about the concepts and algorithms involved in CPU scheduling and process management. The notes for this unit should include the following topics:

1. Definition of deadlock characterization and its purpose (identifying and describing deadlocks)
2. Overview of deadlocks in computer systems (process blocking, resource allocation)
3. Characterization of deadlocks (four necessary conditions, circular wait, hold and wait, mutual exclusion, no preemption)
4. Prevention and detection of deadlocks (deadlock avoidance, deadlock detection)
5. Performance analysis of deadlock characterization algorithms (time complexity, space complexity)
6. Comparison of deadlock characterization algorithms (four necessary conditions, resource allocation graph)

Deadlock characterization is an important part of learning operating systems and CPU scheduling. It provides a foundation for understanding the concepts and algorithms involved in deadlock management and helps students to understand the role of operating systems in managing deadlocks.
