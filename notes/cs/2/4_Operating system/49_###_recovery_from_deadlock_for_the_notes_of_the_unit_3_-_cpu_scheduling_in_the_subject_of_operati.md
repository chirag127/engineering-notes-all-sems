### Recovery from deadlock for the notes of the Unit 3 - CPU Scheduling in the subject of Operating system

Deadlocks are a common problem in concurrent systems, and occur when two or more processes are blocked, waiting for each other to release a resource. In a deadlock situation, the processes are unable to make progress and the system is stuck.

There are several methods for recovering from deadlocks in operating systems, including:

1. Prevention: This method involves avoiding deadlocks by detecting and avoiding conditions that could lead to deadlocks.

2. Detection: This method involves detecting deadlocks and resolving them by releasing resources or terminating processes.

3. Avoidance: This method involves avoiding deadlocks by allocating resources dynamically, so that deadlocks cannot occur.

4. Recovery: This method involves releasing resources or terminating processes to resolve a deadlock after it has occurred.

We will examine the different methods for recovering from deadlocks, and study the advantages and disadvantages of each method. We will also study the algorithms used for detecting and resolving deadlocks, such as the Banker's algorithm, and examine the trade-offs involved in choosing a recovery method. This will provide a foundation for understanding the design and implementation of concurrent systems, and for exploring the various approaches to resolving deadlocks in operating systems.
