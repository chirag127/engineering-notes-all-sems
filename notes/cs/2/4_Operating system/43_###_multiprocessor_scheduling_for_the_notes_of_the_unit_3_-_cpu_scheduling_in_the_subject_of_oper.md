### Multiprocessor Scheduling for the notes of the Unit 3 - CPU Scheduling in the subject of Operating system

Multiprocessor scheduling is the process of allocating tasks to multiple processors in a computer system. This is necessary to optimize the performance of the system and ensure that all processors are utilized effectively.

In the Unit 3 - CPU Scheduling of the subject of Operating System, students learn about the concepts and algorithms involved in CPU scheduling and process management. The notes for this unit should include the following topics:

1. Definition of multiprocessor scheduling and its purpose (optimizing system performance)
2. Overview of multiprocessor scheduling in computer systems (task allocation, processor utilization)
3. Types of multiprocessor scheduling (global, local, load balancing)
4. Algorithms for multiprocessor scheduling (round-robin, priority, load balancing)
5. Performance analysis of multiprocessor scheduling algorithms (time complexity, space complexity)
6. Comparison of multiprocessor scheduling algorithms (round-robin, priority, load balancing)

Multiprocessor scheduling is an important part of learning CPU scheduling and process management. It provides a foundation for understanding the concepts and algorithms involved in task allocation and helps students to understand the role of operating systems in managing the execution of concurrent processes on multiple processors.
