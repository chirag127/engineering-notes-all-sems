### Protection schemes for the notes of the Unit 4 - Memory Management in the subject of Operating system

Protection schemes are mechanisms used to ensure that processes do not interfere with each other's memory space. Protection schemes are necessary to prevent processes from accessing or modifying memory that they are not authorized to access or modify.

There are several types of protection schemes, including:

1. Memory protection: This type of protection prevents processes from accessing memory that is not assigned to them.

2. Address space protection: This type of protection prevents processes from accessing memory that is not part of their address space.

3. Access control: This type of protection controls access to memory based on the privileges of the process.

4. Memory isolation: This type of protection ensures that processes cannot interfere with each other's memory space.

Protection schemes are implemented using hardware and software mechanisms, such as memory protection hardware, page tables, and access control lists.

In this unit, we will study the concepts of protection schemes in operating systems, and examine the different types of protection schemes and their functions. We will also study the mechanisms used to implement protection schemes, and examine the trade-offs involved in using different protection schemes. This will provide a foundation for understanding the design and implementation of operating systems, and for exploring the various approaches to ensuring the security and stability of operating systems.
