### Avoidance and detection for the notes of the Unit 3 - CPU Scheduling in the subject of Operating system

Deadlocks are a common problem in concurrent systems, and can result in system failures and data loss. To avoid deadlocks, it is important to use appropriate synchronization mechanisms, such as semaphores, monitors, and locks. These mechanisms provide a way to control access to shared resources, and to ensure that the access is performed in a consistent and predictable manner.

There are two main approaches to avoiding deadlocks:

1. Avoidance: This approach involves preventing deadlocks from occurring by ensuring that the system always satisfies the necessary conditions for deadlock freedom.

2. Detection: This approach involves detecting deadlocks after they have occurred, and resolving them by aborting one or more processes.

In this unit, we will study the concepts of deadlock avoidance and detection, and examine the mechanisms used to avoid and detect deadlocks. We will also study the performance and scalability of these mechanisms, and examine the trade-offs involved in using different synchronization mechanisms. This will provide a foundation for understanding the design and implementation of concurrent systems, and for exploring the various approaches to avoiding and detecting deadlocks in concurrent systems.
