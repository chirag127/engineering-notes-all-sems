### Process Concept for the notes of the Unit 2 - Concurrent Processes in the subject of Operating system

Unit 2 of the Operating System course covers the concept of concurrent processes. A concurrent process is a process that can run simultaneously with other processes. Concurrent processes are used to improve the efficiency and responsiveness of a computer system.

The following are the key concepts in the study of concurrent processes:

1. Process Scheduling: The operating system schedules the execution of processes based on various criteria, such as priority, time slice, and waiting time.

2. Process Synchronization: The operating system ensures that concurrent processes do not interfere with each other by synchronizing their access to shared resources, such as files and memory.

3. Deadlocks: A deadlock occurs when two or more processes are blocked and waiting for each other, resulting in a standstill. The operating system must detect and resolve deadlocks to ensure the proper functioning of the system.

4. Inter-process Communication: The operating system provides mechanisms for concurrent processes to communicate with each other, such as pipes, semaphores, and message passing.

In the context of the Operating System course, students will learn about the concept of concurrent processes through interactive activities and hands-on experiences. This may include working with a simulation of a concurrent process system, writing code to implement basic process scheduling and synchronization algorithms, and analyzing the behavior of a concurrent process system in response to different inputs.

In summary, Unit 2 of the Operating System course covers the concept of concurrent processes, including process scheduling, process synchronization, deadlocks, and inter-process communication. Students will learn about these topics through interactive activities and hands-on experiences.
