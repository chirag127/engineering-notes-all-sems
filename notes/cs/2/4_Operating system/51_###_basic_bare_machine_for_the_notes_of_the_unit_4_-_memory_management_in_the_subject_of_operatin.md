### Basic bare machine for the notes of the Unit 4 - Memory Management in the subject of Operating system

A bare machine is a computer system without an operating system. It is a basic computer system that consists of a CPU, memory, and input/output devices, but does not have an operating system installed.

In the absence of an operating system, the bare machine can only execute low-level machine code instructions, and does not provide any higher-level services or abstractions, such as process management, memory management, or file system management.

Bare machines are used in a variety of contexts, including:

1. Embedded systems: Bare machines are often used in embedded systems, where the operating system is not required or is not available.

2. Operating system development: Bare machines are used in the development of operating systems, as a platform for testing and debugging the operating system.

3. Research: Bare machines are used in research, to study the behavior of computer systems at a low level, and to understand the underlying principles of computer systems.

In this unit, we will study the concept of the bare machine, and examine its role in the development and understanding of operating systems. We will also study the basic components of a bare machine, including the CPU, memory, and input/output devices, and examine their function and interaction. This will provide a foundation for understanding the design and implementation of operating systems, and for exploring the various challenges and issues involved in memory management.
