### Inter Process Communication models and Schemes for the notes of the Unit 2 - Concurrent Processes in the subject of Operating system

Inter-process communication (IPC) is the mechanism by which processes exchange information and coordinate their activities. There are several IPC models and schemes that can be used to implement IPC, including message passing, shared memory, and pipes.

In the Unit 2 - Concurrent Processes of the subject of Operating system, students learn about the concepts and algorithms involved in concurrent processes and their management. The notes for this unit should include the following topics:

1. Definition of inter-process communication (IPC) and its importance (process coordination, information exchange)
2. Overview of IPC models and schemes (message passing, shared memory, pipes)
3. Message passing in IPC (synchronous, asynchronous, buffered, unbuffered)
4. Shared memory in IPC (segmentation, paging, allocation, synchronization)
5. Pipes in IPC (named pipes, unnamed pipes)
6. Performance analysis of IPC models and schemes (time complexity, space complexity)
7. Comparison of IPC models and schemes (message passing, shared memory, pipes)

Inter-process communication is an important part of learning operating systems and concurrent processes. It provides a foundation for understanding the concepts and algorithms involved in concurrent processes and helps students to understand the role of operating systems in managing concurrent processes.
