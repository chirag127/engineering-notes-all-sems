## Unit 4 - Digital Signatures
A digital signature is a mathematical technique used to verify the authenticity and integrity of a digital message or document. It is created using a private key and can be verified using the corresponding public key. The process involves hashing the message to create a unique representation, encrypting the hash with the private key, and appending the encrypted hash to the original message. The recipient can then use the public key to decrypt the signature and compare the decrypted hash with a newly computed hash of the received message to verify its authenticity and integrity. Digital signatures provide non-repudiation, meaning the signer cannot deny having signed the message, and are often used in financial transactions, legal agreements, and other sensitive communications.
