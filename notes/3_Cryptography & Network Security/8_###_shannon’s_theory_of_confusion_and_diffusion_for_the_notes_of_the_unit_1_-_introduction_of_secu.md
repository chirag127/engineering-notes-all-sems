### Shannon’s theory of confusion and diffusion for the notes of the Unit 1 - Introduction of security attacks, services and mechanism in the subject of Cryptography & Network Security
Shannon's theory of confusion and diffusion refers to the principles of cryptography introduced by Claude Shannon in 1949. Confusion refers to the process of making the relationship between the plaintext and ciphertext as complex as possible, so that an attacker cannot easily determine the plaintext from the ciphertext. Diffusion refers to the process of spreading the information contained in the plaintext over as many ciphertext symbols as possible, so that a change in the plaintext results in a widespread change in the ciphertext. These two principles are fundamental to modern cryptography, and are used in various encryption algorithms to ensure the security of the encrypted data.
