#### Switching Techniques and Multiplexing in Computer Networks

Switching is the process of connecting two devices so that they can communicate with each other. In computer networks, switching is used to transfer data between devices connected to the network. Multiplexing is the process of combining multiple signals into a single signal so that they can be transmitted over a single communication channel. This helps to use the available bandwidth efficiently.

There are different types of switching techniques and multiplexing techniques used in computer networks. Let's discuss them in detail:

##### Switching Techniques:

1. Circuit Switching: In this technique, a dedicated communication path is established between two devices for the entire duration of the communication. The path remains reserved for the communication until the communication is completed. This technique is used in telephony networks.

2. Packet Switching: In this technique, the data is divided into small packets and each packet is transmitted individually over the network. Each packet is routed independently to its destination. This technique is used in modern computer networks.

3. Message Switching: In this technique, the entire message is stored in the intermediate nodes of the network and is transmitted in a store and forward manner. The message is divided into smaller units called packets, and each packet is forwarded to the next node until it reaches its destination. This technique is rarely used in modern computer networks.

##### Multiplexing Techniques:

1. Frequency Division Multiplexing (FDM): In this technique, multiple signals are combined by assigning different frequency ranges to each signal. The signals are then transmitted simultaneously over a single communication channel. This technique is used in analog communication systems.

2. Time Division Multiplexing (TDM): In this technique, multiple signals are combined by assigning different time slots to each signal. The signals are then transmitted sequentially over a single communication channel. This technique is used in digital communication systems.

3. Statistical Multiplexing: In this technique, the available bandwidth is dynamically allocated to the devices based on their demand. The devices share the available bandwidth, and the transmission rate is adjusted based on the demand. This technique is used in modern computer networks.

Mnemonics and learning tricks for these techniques:

There are no specific mnemonics or learning tricks for these techniques. In conclusion, switching techniques and multiplexing techniques are important concepts in computer networks. They help to efficiently use the available resources and improve the overall performance of the network. It is important to understand these techniques and their applications in different scenarios to design an efficient network.
