#### LAN Standards in Local Area Network

Local Area Network (LAN) is a computer network that connects devices within a limited geographic area such as a home, school, or office. LAN standards are a set of guidelines that specify the physical and data link layer technologies used to build LANs. The most commonly used LAN standards are:

1. Ethernet - Ethernet is a widely used LAN standard that uses a bus or star topology to connect devices. It uses Carrier Sense Multiple Access with Collision Detection (CSMA/CD) as its access method. Ethernet is available in different speeds, including 10 Mbps, 100 Mbps, 1 Gbps, 10 Gbps, and 100 Gbps. Mnemonic: Eat All The Eggs In One Go (EAT EIOG) - represents the different speeds in order: 10 Mbps, 100 Mbps, 1 Gbps, 10 Gbps, and 100 Gbps.

2. Wi-Fi - Wi-Fi is a wireless LAN standard that uses radio waves to transmit data between devices. It uses the IEEE 802.11 family of standards and is available in different speeds and frequencies, including 2.4 GHz and 5 GHz. Mnemonic: Wireless Fidelity Is High (WIFI-H) - represents the high-speed and wireless nature of the standard.

3. Token Ring - Token Ring is a LAN standard that uses a ring topology to connect devices. It uses a token passing protocol to control access to the network and prevent collisions. Token Ring is available in different speeds, including 4 Mbps and 16 Mbps. Mnemonic: Token Ring Is Four Sixteen (TRIFS) - represents the different speeds available.

4. FDDI - Fiber Distributed Data Interface (FDDI) is a LAN standard that uses fiber optic cables to connect devices. It uses a dual ring topology to provide redundancy and reliability. FDDI is available in different speeds, including 100 Mbps and 200 Mbps. Mnemonic: Fiber Dual Data Interface (FDDI) - represents the use of fiber optic cables and dual ring topology.

Advantages of LAN standards:
- Provides a faster and more reliable way to share resources and data between devices.
- Supports multiple users accessing the same resources simultaneously.
- Enables easy and efficient communication and collaboration between devices.
- Allows for easier management and maintenance of the network.

Disadvantages of LAN standards:
- Requires additional hardware and software to set up and maintain the network.
- Limited in range and scalability.
- Security concerns due to the easy sharing of resources and data between devices.

Examples of LAN applications:
- Sharing files and printers between devices in an office or home.
- Providing internet access to multiple devices in a public place such as a coffee shop or airport.
- Facilitating communication and collaboration between students and teachers in a classroom setting.

In conclusion, LAN standards play a crucial role in building and maintaining a reliable and efficient LAN. Understanding the different LAN standards and their advantages and disadvantages can help in making informed decisions when building and managing LANs.
