

#### Signal transmission and encoding in Computer Networks

* Signal transmission is the process of sending and receiving data or signals through a communication channel. It is used to transmit information from one device to another.

* Encoding is the process of converting data from one form to another. In computer networks, encoding is used to ensure that the data is transmitted accurately and efficiently.

* Digital signals are encoded in binary form (0s and 1s). These signals can be transmitted over a variety of media such as copper wires, optical fibers or radio waves.

* Digital signals are divided into two types: analog and digital. Analog signals are continuous, while digital signals are discrete.

* Digital signals are further divided into two types: unipolar and bipolar. Unipolar signals have one level, while bipolar signals have two levels.

* Digital signals are transmitted over a communication channel using modulation. Modulation is the process of changing the characteristics of a signal to make it suitable for transmission.

* The most common modulation techniques are amplitude modulation (AM), frequency modulation (FM), phase modulation (PM) and quadrature amplitude modulation (QAM).

* Digital signals are encoded using various coding techniques such as binary encoding, pulse code modulation (PCM), delta modulation (DM) and adaptive delta modulation (ADM).

* Digital signals are transmitted over a communication channel using various transmission techniques such as time division multiplexing (TDM), frequency division multiplexing (FDM) and code division multiple access (CDMA).

* Digital signals are encoded and transmitted over a communication channel using error-correcting codes (ECCs). ECCs are used to detect and correct errors in the transmitted data.

* Digital signals are transmitted over a communication channel using various protocols such as Ethernet, Wi-Fi, Bluetooth and cellular networks.

* Digital signals are transmitted over a communication channel using various networking technologies such as local area networks (LANs), wide area networks (WANs) and metropolitan area networks (MANs).
