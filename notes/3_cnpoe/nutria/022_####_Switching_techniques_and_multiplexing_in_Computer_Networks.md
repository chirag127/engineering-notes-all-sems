

# Switching Techniques and Multiplexing in Computer Networks

Switching techniques and multiplexing are important concepts in computer networks. They allow for efficient communication between multiple devices on a network.

## Types of Switching

There are two main types of switching techniques used in computer networks: circuit switching and packet switching. 

* **Circuit Switching**: In circuit switching, a dedicated physical connection is established between two nodes on a network. This connection is maintained for the duration of the communication session.

* **Packet Switching**: In packet switching, data is divided into small chunks (packets) and sent over the network. Each packet is routed independently to its destination and reassembled upon arrival.

## Types of Multiplexing

Multiplexing is the process of combining multiple data streams for transmission over a single channel. There are three main types of multiplexing used in computer networks: time-division multiplexing (TDM), frequency-division multiplexing (FDM), and statistical multiplexing (SM). 

* **Time-Division Multiplexing (TDM)**: In TDM, each data stream is assigned a specific time slot in which it can transmit data.

* **Frequency-Division Multiplexing (FDM)**: In FDM, each data stream is assigned a specific frequency range in which it can transmit data.

* **Statistical Multiplexing (SM)**: In SM, each data stream is assigned a variable amount of bandwidth depending on its current needs.

## Advantages and Disadvantages

Switching techniques and multiplexing both have advantages and disadvantages. 

* **Advantages**: Switching techniques and multiplexing allow for efficient communication between multiple devices on a network. They also allow for the sharing of resources and increase the overall throughput of the network. 

* **Disadvantages**: Switching techniques and multiplexing require complex algorithms and protocols, which can be difficult to implement. They also require additional hardware and may increase latency on the network. 

## Mnemonics

* **Circuit Switching**: Think of a circuit as a dedicated phone line between two people. 

* **Packet Switching**: Think of packets as small parcels that are sent over the network. 

* **TDM**: Think of TDM as time slots for each data stream. 

* **FDM**: Think of FDM as frequency ranges for each data stream. 

* **SM**: Think of SM as statistical bandwidth allocation for each data stream.
