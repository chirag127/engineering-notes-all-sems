### Electronic mail in application layer

Electronic mail or e-mail is an important application layer protocol that enables users to exchange messages over the Internet. Some key points about e-mail are:

- E-mail messages are composed, sent, received, and stored electronically. Users can access e-mail from any Internet-connected device.
- E-mail uses a client-server model. The e-mail client is the software used to send and receive messages. The e-mail server stores messages and routes them between senders and recipients.
- E-mail messages consist of two parts: the message header and the message body. The header contains information like the sender's and recipient's addresses, subject, and timestamps. The body contains the actual message content.
- E-mail uses protocols and formats like SMTP, POP3, and IMAP for sending and retrieving messages and MIME for structuring message content. E-mail addresses use the username@domain format.
- Advantages of e-mail include convenience, low cost, and asynchronous communication. Disadvantages include spam, phishing, and security and privacy issues.
- E-mail is a fundamental tool for both personal and business communication. It has become deeply integrated into how we work and stay connected.

Some mnemonics and tips for learning:

- "SMTP sends, POP3 gets" - remember the protocols for sending and receiving e-mail
- "Message in an envelope" - think of an e-mail message as a letter in an envelope, with a header as the envelope and message body as the letter inside
- Practice composing and sending e-mails to get familiar with the process and options
- Review the structure and contents of e-mail messages you receive to understand headers and formatting
