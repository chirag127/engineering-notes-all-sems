#### Network topology design in Computer Networks

The network topology refers to the physical and logical arrangement of the network devices and cables. The design of the network topology depends on the requirements and scale of the network. The commonly used network topologies are:

1. Bus topology: All the devices are connected to a central cable called the bus or backbone. It is easy to set up but difficult to troubleshoot and expand.
Mnemonic: All devices are in a row on a **bus**.

2. Star topology: All the devices are connected to a central switch. It is easy to set up, expand and troubleshoot but the whole network depends on the central switch. If the switch fails, the whole network goes down.
Mnemonic: All devices are connected to a central **star**.

3. Ring topology: All the devices are connected to each other in a closed loop. The data is passed around the ring in one direction. It is difficult to set up and troubleshoot but provides high bandwidth and is resistant to failure.
Mnemonic: All devices are connected in a **ring**.

4. Mesh topology: Each device is connected to every other device in the network. It is highly redundant and resilient to failure but expensive and difficult to set up and manage.
Mnemonic: Each device is connected to the **mess** of other devices.

The network topology to use depends on factors like cost, ease of installation and maintenance, fault-tolerance, scalability, etc. A hybrid of two or more topologies can also be used to leverage their advantages. The logical topology can be different from the physical topology and depends on the network protocols in use.
