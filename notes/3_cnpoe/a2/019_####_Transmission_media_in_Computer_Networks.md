#### Transmission media in Computer Networks

The transmission media refer to the physical pathways between the transmitter and receiver in a computer network. The following are the major types of transmission media used:

1. Twisted pair cable:
- Consists of two insulated copper wires twisted together to reduce interference.
- Used for telephone lines and local area networks.
- Examples: Unshielded Twisted Pair (UTP), Shielded Twisted Pair (STP).
- Advantage: Inexpensive, flexible and easy to install.
- Disadvantage: Susceptible to interference which can degrade signal quality.

 Mnemonic: The twisted wires reduce interference and *twist* the signals to the receiver.

2. Coaxial cable:
- Consists of an inner conductor surrounded by an insulating layer and metallic shield.
- Used for cable TV networks and high-speed computer networks.
- Example: Thick coaxial cable and Thin coaxial cable.
- Advantage: Less susceptible to interference.
- Disadvantage: Difficult to install and bend.

3. Optical fiber cable:
- Consists of a core of glass surrounded by cladding material.
- Light signals from lasers are transmitted through the optical fiber.
- Used for high-speed communications over long distances.
- Advantage: Very high bandwidth, secure from interference and has low attenuation.
- Disadvantage: Expensive equipment and difficult to splice.

 Mnemonic: The light signals travel through the *optical fiber* without any *interference*.
