## Unit 2 - Link layer in Computer Networks and Medium Access Control and Local Area Networks

**Link layer:** The link layer is the lowest layer in the TCP/IP model. It is responsible for providing a reliable communication link between two adjacent nodes on a network and is concerned with transmitting raw data between devices over a physical layer. It handles data link protocols, media access control, error detection and correction, etc. Some of the popular link layer protocols are Ethernet, PPP, HDLC, etc.

**Medium Access Control (MAC):** MAC is a sublayer of the link layer that controls how devices share the bandwidth of a network's physical medium. It coordinates the access of connected devices to the network and allows only one device to transmit at a time to avoid collisions. Some common MAC protocols are CSMA/CD used in Ethernet and token passing used in Token Ring.

**Local Area Networks (LANs):** LANs are computer networks covering a small geographical area, like a home, office, or group of buildings. They allow connected devices to share resources like internet connectivity, files, printers, storage, etc. LANs are high-speed, low-cost, and ideal for communication between devices in a local environment. Some popular LAN technologies are Ethernet, Token Ring, FDDI, etc.

Advantages of LANs:
- Fast speed: LANs are high-speed networks allowing quick transfer of data between connected devices.
- Inexpensive: LAN hardware and software are relatively inexpensive.
- Centralized management: LANs allow centralized management of connected resources making them easy to administer.
- Security: LANs can be secured from external access using firewalls and authentication mechanisms.

Disadvantages of LANs:
- Limited size: The geographical size of LANs is limited to a small local area. They are not suitable for connecting devices spread across a wide area.
- Single point of failure: If the LAN's central device like a switch fails, the entire network goes down affecting all connected devices.
- Upgrading issues: Upgrading a LAN to a newer technology can be difficult and time-consuming. All devices and components have to be upgraded simultaneously to maintain connectivity.
