#### Network performance and transmission impairments in Computer Networks

Network performance can be impacted by various transmission impairments. Some of the major transmission impairments are:

1. Noise - Random fluctuations that distort the signal. It can be external like ambient noise or internal like thermal noise. This leads to errors in data transmission which must be detected and corrected using techniques like parity checks and error-correcting codes.
2. Attenuation - The weakening of a signal over distance. As the signal travels through the medium, it loses energy due to dispersion and absorption. Repeaters and amplifiers are used to boost the signal strength to cover longer distances.
3. Interference - Disruptions in signal transmission due to other signals. It can be due to natural causes like weather events or human activity like other wireless transmissions. Using frequency division multiplexing and shielding can mitigate interference.
4. Delay - The time taken for a signal to be transmitted from source to destination. It can be due to signal processing delays or propagation delays in the medium. Excessive delays can lead to latency in applications and conversations. Techniques like caching and reducing the number of hops can decrease delay.
5. Jitter - Variations in the delay/latency of signal transmissions. It can be due to variable queuing delays or inconsistent processing times. Jitter buffers are used to reduce the effects of jitter and provide smooth playback.

To remember the impairments, a mnemonic like **NIAJD** (Noise, Interference, Attenuation, Jitter, Delay) can be used. Maintaining high signal-to-noise ratios, choosing appropriate frequencies and medium types, and using error control techniques can improve network performance in the presence of these impairments.
