#### Signal transmission and encoding in Computer Networks

- Signals are transmitted as electrical or electromagnetic signals over a physical medium in a computer network. The medium can be wired or wireless.
- For wired transmission, signals are transmitted as electrical signals over cables like twisted pair, coaxial, or fiber optic cables.
- For wireless transmission, signals are transmitted as electromagnetic waves over mediums like air, space, etc.
- The transmitted signals may get distorted, weakened, or interfered due to noise and other factors. Thus, encoding techniques are used to convert the data into signals in a format that is less susceptible to these effects.
- Some common encoding techniques are:
-- Amplitude Shift Keying (ASK) - varies the amplitude of the signal. Example: On-Off Keying (OOK) which encodes data as the presence or absence of a carrier wave.
-- Frequency Shift Keying (FSK) - varies the frequency of the signal.
-- Phase Shift Keying (PSK) - varies the phase of the signal.
-- Quadrare Amplitude Modulation (QAM) - varies both the amplitude and phase of the signal.

Mnemonics:
- ASK shifts amplitude,
- FSK shifts frequency,
- PSK shifts phase,
- QAM shifts both amplitude and phase.

Advantages:
- Reduced susceptibility to noise and interference.
- Increases the amount of data that can be transmitted.

Disadvantages:
- Require synchronized sender and receiver.
- Complex hardware and software required for encoding and decoding.

Applications:
- Wireless networks (Wi-Fi, cellular networks)
- Fiber optic communication
- Ethernet
