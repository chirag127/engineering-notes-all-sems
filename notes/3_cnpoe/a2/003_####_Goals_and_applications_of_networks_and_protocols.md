#### Goals and applications of networks and protocols

The main goals and applications of computer networks and protocols are:

1. Connectivity - Networking enables connectivity between computing devices so that they can share resources and communicate with each other. Protocols like IP, TCP, UDP, etc. are used to route data between networks and devices.
2. Sharing resources - Connected devices can share resources like internet access, servers, storage, software, etc. For e.g. multiple employees in an office can access company servers and software over the office network.
3. Messaging and communication - Networks enable messaging and communication between devices using protocols like SMTP, FTP, HTTP, etc. This allows applications like email, instant messaging, web browsing, streaming, etc.
4. Remote access - Networks enable remote access to resources from anywhere using protocols like SSH, RDP, VNC, etc. This allows employees to access office systems from home or administrators to access systems in data centers.
5. Speed and reliability - Using networking, information can be transferred between devices at high speeds with a high degree of reliability and security. Protocols are designed to be efficient, reliable and robust against errors.

Some mnemonics and learning tricks for networking goals and applications:

- **C**onnect, **S**hare, **M**essage, **R**emote - To remember the 4 main goals
- **IP** addresses devices, **TCP** ensures reliable data transfer, **UDP** enables fast data transfer
- **HTTP** browses the web, **FTP** transfers files, **SMTP** sends email

Advantages of networking include connectivity, resource sharing and cost effectiveness.
Disadvantages include complexity, security issues and dependency on networks.

Networks and protocols have a very wide range of applications in personal and commercial use. Almost all modern devices and technology rely on computer networks and an integrated suite of protocols.
