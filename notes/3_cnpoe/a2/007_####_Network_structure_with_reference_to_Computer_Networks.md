#### Network structure with reference to Computer Networks

The network structure refers to the physical and logical layout of the network. The following are the major types of network structures:

1. Bus topology: All devices are connected to a central cable called a bus. Signals are transmitted across the bus in both directions. All devices receive the signals but only the intended device accepts and processes the signals. Advantage: Inexpensive, easy to connect new devices. Disadvantage: Faults can affect the entire network, difficult to troubleshoot.

2. Star topology: All devices are connected to a central switch. The switch manages and controls all communications between the devices. Advantage: Easy to troubleshoot, expand. Disadvantage: Dependence on the central switch, single point of failure.

3. Ring topology: All devices are connected to each other in a closed loop called a ring. Signals travel through the ring in one direction. Advantage: Equal access to the network, inexpensive. Disadvantage: Difficult to troubleshoot, adding or removing devices disrupts the network.

4. Mesh topology: Each device is connected to every other device in a point-to-point connection. Signals between devices can travel over multiple paths. Advantage: Fault tolerance, highly reliable. Disadvantage: Complex to configure and manage, requires many connections and cables.

Mnemonics:
- Bus: All in one bus
- Star: Central star attraction
- Ring: Loop the ring
- Mesh: All interconnected mesh

Learning tricks:
- Draw diagrams of the topologies to understand the structure and connections.
- Note the advantages and disadvantages to understand the suitability for different requirements.
- Bus and ring are inexpensive but have single point of failure, star and mesh are reliable but complex and expensive. Choose based on needs.
