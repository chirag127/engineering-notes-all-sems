#### Network performance and transmission impairments in Computer Networks

Network performance refers to the level of quality of a network's service, which includes issues such as speed, reliability, and availability. Transmission impairments are disturbances that affect the quality of a signal being transmitted over a communication channel. In computer networks, these impairments can negatively impact network performance, resulting in slow data transfer rates, data loss, and other issues.

Here are some common network performance and transmission impairments that can occur in computer networks:

1. Delay: Delay refers to the amount of time it takes for a packet to travel from the sender to the receiver. It is measured in milliseconds (ms) and can be caused by various factors such as network congestion, distance, and processing time.

2. Jitter: Jitter is the variation in delay that occurs when packets are transmitted over a network. It can cause packet loss, delay, and out-of-order packets.

3. Packet loss: Packet loss occurs when a packet fails to reach its destination. It can be caused by network congestion, faulty equipment, or errors in the transmission.

4. Attenuation: Attenuation is the loss of signal strength as it travels over a communication channel. It can be caused by distance, interference, or poor cable quality.

5. Noise: Noise is unwanted signals that interfere with the transmission of data. It can be caused by electromagnetic interference, poor cable quality, or faulty equipment.

Mnemonics and learning tricks:

1. To remember the common network performance and transmission impairments, you can use the acronym "DJ PAN". D stands for Delay, J for Jitter, P for Packet loss, A for Attenuation, and N for Noise.

2. Another trick is to remember the phrase "Don't Joke, Packets Are Not Nice" to remember the order of the impairments (Delay, Jitter, Packet loss, Attenuation, and Noise).

In conclusion, network performance and transmission impairments can have a significant impact on the quality of service of a computer network. Understanding the causes and effects of these impairments is crucial for network administrators to troubleshoot and maintain an efficient network.
