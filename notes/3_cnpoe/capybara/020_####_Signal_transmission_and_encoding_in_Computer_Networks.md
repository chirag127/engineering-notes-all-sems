#### Signal Transmission and Encoding in Computer Networks

Signal transmission refers to the process of sending data from one device to another over a network. To transmit data, the data needs to be encoded into a format that can be transmitted over the network. Encoding is the process of converting data into a format that can be easily transmitted over a network. In this section, we will discuss the different types of signal transmission and encoding techniques used in computer networks.

##### Types of Signal Transmission

There are two types of signal transmission:

1. Analog Signal Transmission
2. Digital Signal Transmission

##### Analog Signal Transmission

Analog signal transmission is the process of transmitting data in the form of analog signals. In this type of transmission, data is represented by continuously varying signals such as sound waves, light waves, or radio waves. Analog signals are continuous and can have an infinite number of values. The most common example of analog signal transmission is a telephone call.

###### Advantages of Analog Signal Transmission

- Can transmit data over long distances.
- Can transmit data in real-time.

###### Disadvantages of Analog Signal Transmission

- Analog signals are prone to interference, which can cause data loss or corruption.
- Analog signals degrade over long distances.

##### Digital Signal Transmission

Digital signal transmission is the process of transmitting data in the form of digital signals. In this type of transmission, data is represented by a series of 0s and 1s, which are referred to as bits. Digital signals are discrete and can only have two values - 0 or 1. The most common example of digital signal transmission is the transmission of data over the internet.

###### Advantages of Digital Signal Transmission

- Digital signals are less prone to interference, which reduces the chances of data loss or corruption.
- Digital signals can be transmitted over long distances without degradation.

###### Disadvantages of Digital Signal Transmission

- Digital signal transmission requires more bandwidth than analog signal transmission.
- Digital signal transmission is more complex and requires more processing power.

##### Encoding Techniques

Encoding refers to the process of converting data into a format that can be transmitted over a network. There are several encoding techniques used in computer networks.

###### Manchester Encoding

Manchester encoding is a type of digital encoding that is used to transmit data over a network. In Manchester encoding, each bit is represented by a transition from high to low or low to high. A high-to-low transition represents a 0 bit, while a low-to-high transition represents a 1 bit.

###### Differential Manchester Encoding

Differential Manchester encoding is similar to Manchester encoding, but instead of using transitions to represent bits, it uses transitions to represent the start of a bit. A transition at the start of a bit represents a 1 bit, while no transition at the start of a bit represents a 0 bit.

###### NRZ Encoding

NRZ (Non-Return-to-Zero) encoding is a type of digital encoding that uses a single voltage level to represent a 0 bit and a different voltage level to represent a 1 bit. In NRZ encoding, the voltage level remains constant for the duration of the bit.

###### NRZI Encoding

NRZI (Non-Return-to-Zero Inverted) encoding is similar to NRZ encoding, but instead of using a single voltage level to represent a 0 bit, it uses a transition between voltage levels to represent a 0 bit. A transition from high to low represents a 0 bit, while no transition represents a 1 bit.
