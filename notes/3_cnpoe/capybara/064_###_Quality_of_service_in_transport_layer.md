### Quality of service in transport layer

Quality of service (QoS) refers to the ability of the network to provide better service to selected network traffic over various technologies, including frame relay, Asynchronous Transfer Mode (ATM), Ethernet, and 802.1 networks. Quality of Service (QoS) in the transport layer is the ability of the transport protocol to provide different levels of service to different types of traffic.

#### Types of services provided by QoS

QoS in transport layer provides the following types of services:

1. **Guaranteed Service**: In this service, the transport protocol guarantees that a certain amount of bandwidth will be available to a specific flow of traffic.

2. **Controlled Load Service**: In this service, the transport protocol provides a certain level of service to the traffic, but does not guarantee a specific amount of bandwidth.

3. **Best Effort Service**: In this service, the transport protocol provides no guarantees about the level of service provided to the traffic.

#### Mnemonics and tricks for remembering QoS in transport layer

One Mnemonic for remembering the types of services provided by QoS in transport layer is:

"GCB - Guaranteed, Controlled, Best Effort"

This mnemonic can help you remember the three types of services provided by QoS in transport layer, in the order they are listed above.

#### Advantages and disadvantages of QoS in transport layer

Advantages:

1. QoS in transport layer can improve the performance of applications that require a certain amount of bandwidth or a specific level of service.

2. QoS can help prevent network congestion and ensure that mission-critical traffic is given priority over less important traffic.

Disadvantages:

1. Implementing QoS in transport layer can be complex and expensive.

2. QoS can require additional hardware and software, which can add to the cost and complexity of the network.

#### Example of QoS in transport layer

A common example of QoS in transport layer is the use of Differentiated Services (DiffServ) in IP networks. DiffServ is a QoS mechanism that allows network administrators to classify traffic into different classes, based on its priority, and then provide different levels of service to each class.

#### Applications of QoS in transport layer

QoS in transport layer is used in a variety of applications, including:

1. Voice over IP (VoIP) - QoS can ensure that voice traffic is given priority over other types of traffic, to ensure that the call quality is not degraded.

2. Video streaming - QoS can ensure that video traffic is given priority over other types of traffic, to ensure that the video is delivered without buffering or delays.

3. Online gaming - QoS can ensure that gaming traffic is given priority over other types of traffic, to ensure that the game is not affected by network congestion.
