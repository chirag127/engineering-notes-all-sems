#### Switching techniques and multiplexing in Computer Networks

Switching is a technique that allows multiple devices to share the same network resources without interfering with each other's communication. Multiplexing, on the other hand, is a technique that allows multiple signals to be transmitted over a single communication channel.

There are various switching techniques and multiplexing methods used in computer networks. Let's explore them one by one:

##### Switching Techniques:

1. **Circuit Switching:** In this technique, a dedicated path is established between the source and destination devices for the entire duration of the communication. The path remains reserved even if there is no data transmission, which results in wastage of resources. This technique is useful for real-time applications like voice and video conferencing.

2. **Packet Switching:** In this technique, data is broken down into small packets and each packet is sent individually over the network. The packets may follow different paths to reach the destination and are reassembled at the destination to form the original data. This technique is useful for non-real-time applications like file transfer and email.

3. **Message Switching:** In this technique, the entire message is stored at each intermediate node until the next path to the destination is available. This technique is rarely used in modern computer networks.

##### Multiplexing Techniques:

1. **Frequency Division Multiplexing (FDM):** In this technique, multiple signals are transmitted simultaneously over different frequency bands. Each signal is assigned a different frequency band, and the receiver separates the signals based on their frequencies. This technique is used in analog communication systems.

2. **Time Division Multiplexing (TDM):** In this technique, multiple signals are transmitted over the same channel by dividing the channel into smaller time slots. Each signal is assigned a different time slot, and the receiver separates the signals based on their time slots. This technique is used in digital communication systems.

3. **Statistical Multiplexing:** In this technique, multiple signals are transmitted over the same channel based on their demand. The channel is shared dynamically among the signals, and the transmission rate is adjusted based on the demand. This technique is used in packet-switched networks.

Mnemonics can be helpful in remembering these techniques. For example, "Circuit Switching is Constant, Packet Switching is Personalized, and Message Switching is Messy." Similarly, "FDM is for Frequency, TDM is for Time, and Statistical Multiplexing is Smart."

In conclusion, switching and multiplexing techniques are essential for efficient and reliable communication in computer networks. Understanding these techniques and their applications can be helpful for network engineers and administrators.
