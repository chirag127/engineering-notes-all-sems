#### Channel Allocation in Medium Access Control

In telecommunication networks, medium access control (MAC) protocols are used to control access to the shared communication channel. Channel allocation refers to the process of assigning channels to users in a way that maximizes the efficiency of the network.

There are several types of channel allocation schemes, including:

1. Fixed Channel Allocation (FCA): In this scheme, each user is assigned a fixed channel for communication. This method is simple and easy to implement, but it is not efficient because channels may be idle while other users are blocked from accessing them.

2. Dynamic Channel Allocation (DCA): In this scheme, channels are assigned dynamically based on current demand. DCA can be further classified into two categories:

    a. Random Access: In this scheme, users contend for channels randomly. The contention process is based on a set of rules that determine which user gets access to the channel. One of the most common random access MAC protocols is the ALOHA protocol.

    b. Controlled Access: In this scheme, channels are assigned in a controlled manner. The base station controls the channel access of the users. One of the most common controlled access MAC protocols is the reservation protocol.

3. Hybrid Channel Allocation (HCA): In this scheme, a combination of FCA and DCA is used. In HCA, some channels are assigned fixed while others are dynamically allocated. This method provides the benefits of both FCA and DCA.

Some important Mnemonics and learning tricks for channel allocation in MAC include:

1. FCA - Fixed channels are always the same, dynamic channels may change.

2. DCA - Dynamic channels are assigned based on demand.

3. ALOHA - Access is random, but there are rules.

4. Reservation - Access is controlled by the base station.

In conclusion, channel allocation is an important aspect of MAC protocols. The choice of channel allocation scheme depends on the specific requirements of the network. While there are different types of channel allocation schemes, the goal is always to maximize the efficiency of the network.
