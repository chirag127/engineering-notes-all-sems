
### Jain Literature in Pali, Prakrit and Sanskrit

Jain literature is an important source of knowledge for Jains, and it is written in three languages: Pali, Prakrit and Sanskrit. Pali literature is the oldest and most important of the three, and it is the language of the Jain canon. Prakrit is the language of popular literature, and Sanskrit is the language of philosophical and religious literature.

There are several mnemonics and learning tricks that can help students learn and remember Jain literature. For example, the three languages of Jain literature can be remembered by the acronym "PPS", which stands for Pali, Prakrit and Sanskrit. Additionally, the four main divisions of Jain literature can be remembered with the acronym "VVSS", which stands for Vyakarana (grammar), Vyavahara (law), Shastra (philosophy) and Smriti (rituals).

Pali literature is divided into two parts: the Agama and the Nighantu. The Agama consists of twelve Angas, which are the main texts of the Jain canon. The Nighantu is a collection of commentaries and sub-commentaries on the Agama. The most important of these is the Harivamsa, which is a commentary on the twelfth Anga.

Prakrit literature is divided into three parts: the Jataka, the Avadana and the Champu. The Jataka consists of stories about the lives of the Jain tirthankaras, while the Avadana is a collection of stories about the lives of Jain saints and scholars. The Champu is a collection of stories, poems and plays.

Sanskrit literature is divided into two parts: the Siddhanta and the Purana. The Siddhanta is a collection of philosophical and religious texts, while the Purana is a collection of stories about the lives of Jain tirthankaras.
