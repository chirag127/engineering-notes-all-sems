
### Trade in Ancient India

* Trade in Ancient India was an important part of the Indian economy. It was conducted through both domestic and foreign channels. 
* Indian merchants and traders used to travel to different parts of the world for trading purposes. 
* The most important trading ports in ancient India were Broach, Cambay, Surat, and Masulipatnam.
* The main items of trade included cotton, spices, precious stones, textiles, and metal goods.
* Ancient Indian traders used various modes of transport such as carts, bullock-carts, horses, boats, and ships.
* The most common form of payment used in trade was barter.
* The main trading partners of ancient India were the Middle East, China, and Southeast Asia.
* The most important trade routes in ancient India were the Silk Route, the Spice Route, and the Bay of Bengal Route.
* The most important trading centers in ancient India were the ports of Broach, Cambay, Surat, and Masulipatnam.
* The trading activities of ancient India were regulated by the Arthashastra, a treatise written by Kautilya.
* The trading activities of ancient India were also regulated by the guilds, which were organizations of merchants and traders.
* The guilds also provided credit facilities to traders and merchants.
* Ancient India also had a system of taxation, which was used to finance the state’s activities. 
* The main taxes levied on trade were customs duties, tolls, and excise duties.
