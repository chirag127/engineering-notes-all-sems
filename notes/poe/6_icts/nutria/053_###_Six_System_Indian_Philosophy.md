
### Six System Indian Philosophy

The Six System Indian Philosophy is a set of six philosophical systems that provide a framework for understanding the world. The six systems are Samkhya, Yoga, Nyaya, Vaisheshika, Purva Mimamsa, and Uttara Mimamsa. Each system has its own set of theories and principles that can be used to explain the world and to provide a basis for making decisions.

**Samkhya**: Samkhya is the oldest of the six systems and is based on dualism, the idea that the physical world and the spiritual world are separate. It is based on the idea that the physical world is made up of two things: Purusha (spirit) and Prakriti (matter). It is a system of metaphysics and cosmology that seeks to explain the nature of the universe.

**Yoga**: Yoga is a system of spiritual, mental, and physical practices that are designed to help people achieve inner peace and enlightenment. It is based on the idea that the body and mind are connected, and the goal is to achieve harmony between them.

**Nyaya**: Nyaya is a system of logic and epistemology that seeks to explain how we can know things and how we can determine if something is true or false. It is based on the idea that knowledge is gained through observation and reasoning.

**Vaisheshika**: Vaisheshika is a system of atomic theory that seeks to explain the nature of matter. It is based on the idea that all matter is composed of atoms, which are indivisible and indestructible.

**Purva Mimamsa**: Purva Mimamsa is a system of ritualism and philosophy that seeks to explain the nature of religious and social obligations. It is based on the idea that the Vedas are the source of knowledge and that rituals are essential for living a moral life.

**Uttara Mimamsa**: Uttara Mimamsa is a system of Vedanta philosophy that seeks to explain the ultimate reality. It is based on the idea that Brahman (God) is the ultimate reality and that all other things are an illusion.
