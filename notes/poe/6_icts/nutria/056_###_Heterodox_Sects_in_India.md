
### Heterodox Sects in India

Heterodox sects in India are religions or philosophies that differ from the mainstream religious traditions. These sects are often seen as unorthodox or non-traditional, and their beliefs and practices can vary widely. Some of the most prominent heterodox sects in India include Jainism, Buddhism, Sikhism, and various forms of Hinduism.

* Jainism: Jainism is an ancient Indian religion that emphasizes non-violence and spiritual liberation. It is based on the teachings of Mahavira, who was born in the 6th century BCE. Jains believe in the existence of multiple souls, and that each soul has the potential to achieve liberation from the cycle of rebirth.

* Buddhism: Buddhism is a religion founded by Gautama Buddha in the 5th century BCE. It is based on the Four Noble Truths and the Eightfold Path. Buddhists believe in the power of meditation to help achieve enlightenment and nirvana.

* Sikhism: Sikhism is a religion founded by Guru Nanak in the 15th century CE. It is based on the teachings of the ten Sikh Gurus, and is centered around the concept of a single god. Sikhs believe in the equality of all people and the importance of social justice.

* Hinduism: Hinduism is an ancient Indian religion that is based on the Vedas and other sacred texts. It is polytheistic, and has many different gods and goddesses. Hindus believe in the cycle of rebirth, and that karma determines how a person will be reborn.

In addition to these major heterodox sects, there are many smaller sects in India, such as the Lingayats, the Kabir Panthis, and the Ramanandis. Each of these sects has its own unique beliefs and practices, and they can vary widely from one another.
