
### North Indian Literature

- North Indian literature is a broad term that covers literature written in the languages of North India. It includes literature written in Hindi, Urdu, Punjabi, Gujarati, Marathi, Rajasthani, Bengali, Kashmiri, and other regional languages.
- North Indian literature has a long and rich history, with famous works such as the Ramayana, Mahabharata, and Panchatantra being written in North India.
- North Indian literature is often divided into two categories: classical and contemporary. The classical literature includes works written before the 19th century, while the contemporary literature includes works written after the 19th century.
- North Indian literature is known for its exploration of themes such as love, loss, family, and society. It often uses symbolism and allegory to convey its messages.
- North Indian literature also encompasses folk tales, which are stories passed down orally from generation to generation. These stories often have moral lessons, and are used to teach children about values and culture.
- North Indian literature is often seen as a reflection of the culture and values of the region. It is an important part of the region's cultural heritage, and is a source of pride for many North Indians.
