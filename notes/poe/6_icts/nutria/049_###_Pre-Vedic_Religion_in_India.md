
### Pre-Vedic Religion in India

Pre-Vedic religion in India is a term used to refer to the religious beliefs and practices of the people living in the Indian subcontinent prior to the emergence of Vedic culture. The pre-Vedic period is believed to have lasted from around 3000 BCE to 1500 BCE.

The pre-Vedic period is divided into two distinct phases: the Early Harappan period (3000-1900 BCE) and the Later Harappan period (1900-1500 BCE). During these two phases, the people of the Indian subcontinent developed their own religious beliefs and practices, which were distinct from those of the Vedic culture that emerged in the later period.

The pre-Vedic religion was polytheistic, with multiple gods and goddesses. These gods and goddesses were associated with various natural phenomena, such as the sun, the moon, the stars, the wind, the rain, and the fertility of the land. People worshipped these gods and goddesses in order to ensure a good harvest and to protect themselves from natural disasters.

The pre-Vedic religion also included ancestor worship and animal sacrifice. People believed that their ancestors could influence their lives and that animal sacrifice could appease the gods and goddesses.

The pre-Vedic religion had no written scriptures and its beliefs and practices were passed down orally from generation to generation. The Vedic texts, which were written down in Sanskrit, contain references to the pre-Vedic religion and its beliefs and practices.
