

#### Āshrama or the Stages of Life in Society in Ancient India

The ancient Indian society was based on the four stages of life, known as the āshrama system. This system divided life into four stages, each with its own set of duties, responsibilities and goals. The four stages were:

1. Brahmacharya: This was the first stage of life, during which a person was expected to pursue spiritual knowledge and practice celibacy.

2. Grihastha: This was the second stage of life, during which a person was expected to marry, have children and live a life of responsibility.

3. Vanaprastha: This was the third stage of life, during which a person was expected to retire from worldly life and devote time to spiritual pursuits.

4. Sannyasa: This was the fourth and final stage of life, during which a person was expected to renounce all worldly attachments and devote his/her life to spiritual pursuits.

A popular mnemonic to remember these four stages is "BGVS": Brahmacharya, Grihastha, Vanaprastha, Sannyasa.

The āshrama system was a way for people to lead a meaningful and fulfilling life, by following the prescribed duties and responsibilities of each stage. The system also provided a framework for people to live a life of balance, by balancing their spiritual and material pursuits.
