
#### Purusārtha in Society in Ancient India

Purusārtha is a Sanskrit term that is used to refer to the four main goals of human life in Ancient India. These goals are Dharma (righteousness and morality), Artha (wealth and prosperity), Kama (pleasure and enjoyment) and Moksha (liberation and spiritual enlightenment).

**Dharma**: Dharma is the moral law of the universe, which is based on the principles of truth, justice and righteousness. It is the foundation of a harmonious and prosperous society. Dharma involves following the rules of society, respecting the rights of others, and living a life of truthfulness and integrity.

**Artha**: Artha is the pursuit of wealth and prosperity. It involves the acquisition of material goods, the accumulation of wealth, and the attainment of financial security. Artha also involves engaging in activities that are beneficial to society, such as trade, commerce, and industry.

**Kama**: Kama is the pursuit of pleasure and enjoyment. It involves engaging in activities that bring pleasure and satisfaction, such as music, art, literature, and theatre. Kama also involves the pursuit of physical pleasures, such as sexual relationships and other sensual activities.

**Moksha**: Moksha is the ultimate goal of human life, and it involves the attainment of spiritual liberation and enlightenment. It involves the realization of the ultimate truth of the universe, and the attainment of a state of perfect harmony and peace.
