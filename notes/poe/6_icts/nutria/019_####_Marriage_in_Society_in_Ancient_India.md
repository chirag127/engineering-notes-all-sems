
#### Marriage in Society in Ancient India

Marriage in ancient India was an important social institution and was considered to be a sacred bond. It was an essential part of the Hindu religion and was seen as a way to continue the family line and ensure the well-being of the couple. Marriage was seen as a way to ensure the continuity of the family and was seen as a way to ensure the welfare of the couple. 

In ancient India, marriages were arranged by the parents of the bride and groom. The parents would select a suitable match for their children and arrange for a marriage ceremony. The marriage was usually arranged between two families of equal status and wealth. It was also important to ensure that the bride and groom were of similar age and caste. 

The wedding ceremony was an elaborate affair and included several rituals. The rituals included the exchange of garlands, the tying of the mangalsutra (sacred thread), the exchange of rings, and the exchange of gifts. The bride and groom would also exchange vows and the groom would give the bride a dowry. 

In ancient India, the bride and groom were expected to remain loyal and faithful to each other. Divorce was not an option and adultery was severely punished. The bride and groom were expected to live together as husband and wife and take care of each other.
