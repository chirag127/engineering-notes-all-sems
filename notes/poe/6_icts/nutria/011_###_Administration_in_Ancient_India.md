
### Administration in Ancient India

1. Ancient India was ruled by powerful kings and emperors, who were responsible for the administration of their respective kingdoms. 
2. The king was the supreme head of the government and was assisted by a council of ministers, known as the 'Mantriparishad'. 
3. The king was also the head of the judicial system, and the court of justice was known as the 'Dharmasthala'. 
4. The king was responsible for the collection of taxes and the maintenance of law and order in the kingdom. 
5. The kingdom was divided into provinces, which were further divided into districts, and then into villages. 
6. Each district was governed by a 'Dharmasthala', and the villages were governed by a 'Grama Sabha'. 
7. The king appointed officers to collect taxes and maintain law and order in the kingdom. 
8. The king also appointed officers to oversee the administration of justice in the kingdom. 
9. The king also maintained a large army to protect the kingdom from external threats.
10. The king also maintained a large network of spies to keep an eye on the activities of his subjects.
11. Ancient India also had a well-developed system of education, which included both formal and informal learning.
12. Education was mainly imparted through the 'Gurukuls', which were schools run by learned scholars.
13. Ancient India also had a well-developed system of trade and commerce.
14. Merchants and traders used to travel to different parts of the kingdom to trade goods and services. 
15. Ancient India also had a well-developed system of taxation. 
16. The king used to levy taxes on the people in order to finance his kingdom. 
17. Ancient India also had a system of weights and measures to ensure uniformity in transactions. 
18. The 'Manu Smriti' was an important book of law which laid down the rules and regulations for the administration of justice in Ancient India. 
19. Ancient India also had a well-developed system of coinage, which was used for transactions.
