
### Council of Ministers in Ancient India

In ancient India, the Council of Ministers was a group of advisors to the ruler or king who provided advice and counsel on matters of state. The council was made up of wise and experienced members, and the king relied on this body to make decisions on his behalf.

The council was divided into two sections: the inner and outer. The inner council was composed of the most trusted advisors and was responsible for making major decisions. The outer council was made up of less influential advisors who provided advice and counsel on more minor matters.

The Council of Ministers was an important part of the government in ancient India. The council provided the king with valuable advice and helped him make decisions that were in the best interests of the kingdom. 

The council was also responsible for carrying out the king's orders and ensuring that his laws were obeyed. They had the power to punish those who disobeyed the king's orders and to reward those who followed them.
