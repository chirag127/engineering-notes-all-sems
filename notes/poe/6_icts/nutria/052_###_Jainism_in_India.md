
### Jainism in India

Jainism is an ancient religion that originated in India. It is based on the teachings of Mahavir, the 24th and last Tirthankara of the Jain religion. Jains believe in the principle of non-violence (ahimsa), and strive to live a life of simplicity and self-discipline.

Jains follow five main principles:

1. Non-violence (Ahimsa): Jains believe in non-violence towards all living beings. They practice vegetarianism, and abstain from eating meat, eggs, and honey.

2. Truthfulness (Satya): Jains strive to always speak the truth, and to act truthfully.

3. Non-stealing (Asteya): Jains strive to never take anything that does not belong to them.

4. Chastity (Brahmacharya): Jains strive to lead a chaste life and to abstain from sexual activity.

5. Non-possessiveness (Aparigraha): Jains strive to detach themselves from material possessions and to lead a life of moderation.

Jains also practice meditation and spiritual contemplation, and strive to live a life of compassion and non-attachment.

Jains are found mainly in India, but there are also sizeable Jain communities in the United States, United Kingdom, Canada, and other countries. There are several sects within Jainism, including Digambara and Svetambara.
