
### Harappan Technologies in India and Ancient India

- Harappan technology was the technology used by the Harappan civilization, which flourished in the Indus Valley from about 3300 BCE to 1700 BCE.
- Harappan technology was characterized by the use of copper and bronze tools, pottery, and stone tools.
- The Harappans were also the first civilization to use the wheel, which was used for carts and pottery wheels.
- They also used a variety of tools such as axes, adzes, saws, and chisels.
- The Harappans were also the first civilization to use irrigation systems and sewers.
- The Harappans had a complex system of weights and measures, which allowed them to trade with other civilizations.
- The Harappans also had a writing system, which has not yet been deciphered.
- Harappan technology also included the use of a variety of tools for crafting jewelry, such as beads, pendants, and earrings.
- Harappan technology also included the use of terracotta figurines, which were used for religious purposes.
- Harappan technology also included the use of brick-making, which was used for the construction of buildings, walls, and roads.
- Harappan technology also included the use of water-lifting devices, such as the shadoof, which was used to lift water from one level to another.
- The Harappans also developed a system of metallurgy, which allowed them to produce bronze tools and weapons.
- The Harappans were also the first civilization to use cotton, which was used for clothing and other textiles.
- The Harappans were also the first civilization to use a calendar, which was based on the phases of the moon.
