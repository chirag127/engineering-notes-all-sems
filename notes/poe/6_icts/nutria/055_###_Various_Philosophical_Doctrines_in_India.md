
### Various Philosophical Doctrines in India

1. **Vedanta**: Vedanta is a school of thought in Hinduism that believes in the concept of Advaita (non-dualism). It is based on the teachings of the Upanishads, which are part of the Vedas. According to Vedanta, the ultimate reality is one, and all other forms of existence are merely illusions. This concept is known as Brahman.

2. **Samkhya**: Samkhya is the oldest of the six classical schools of Hindu philosophy. It is based on the teachings of the sage Kapila, and is focused on the dualism between Purusha (the conscious self) and Prakriti (the material world). It is a system of analytical thought that seeks to explain the nature of reality and the relationship between the individual and the universe.

3. **Yoga**: Yoga is an ancient Indian philosophical system that seeks to unite the individual with the divine through physical and mental practices. It is based on the teachings of Patanjali, and is divided into eight limbs: yama (self-restraint), niyama (observances), asana (postures), pranayama (breath control), pratyahara (sense withdrawal), dharana (concentration), dhyana (meditation), and samadhi (enlightenment).

4. **Vaisheshika**: Vaisheshika is a school of thought in Hinduism that focuses on the concept of atomism. It is based on the teachings of the sage Kanada, and is focused on the idea that all matter is composed of atoms that are eternal and indestructible. It is a system of analytical thought that seeks to explain the nature of reality and the relationship between the individual and the universe.

5. **Nyaya**: Nyaya is a school of thought in Hinduism that focuses on the concept of logic. It is based on the teachings of the sage Gautama, and is focused on the idea that all knowledge can be acquired through logical reasoning. It is a system of analytical thought that seeks to explain the nature of reality and the relationship between the individual and the universe.

6. **Purva Mimamsa**: Purva Mimamsa is a school of thought in Hinduism that focuses on the interpretation of the Vedas. It is based on the teachings of the sage Jaimini, and is focused on the idea that the Vedas are the source of all knowledge and should be interpreted in a literal manner. It is a system of analytical thought that seeks to explain the nature of reality and the relationship between the individual and the universe.
