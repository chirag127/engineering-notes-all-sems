### Vedic Religion in India

The Vedic religion refers to the religious ideas and practices among most of the Indo-Aryan-speaking peoples of ancient India after about 1500 BCE. The Vedas are four collections of texts compiled between about 1500 and 500 BCE. The Vedic period, from roughly 1500 to 500 BCE, laid the foundation of Indian civilization.

- The Vedas are the primary texts of the Vedic religion. They include hymns, prayers, rituals, and philosophical speculations. The Rigveda is the oldest and most significant.
- The Vedas contain concepts such as dharma, or religious and moral law; karma, the principle of cause and effect; and samsara, the cycle of rebirth. They also introduce the major deities of Hinduism, such as Agni, Indra, and Soma.
- Vedic priests performed sacrifices and rituals intended to maintain the cosmic order. Fire sacrifices and Soma rituals were particularly important and were performed by Brahmin priests.
- The later Vedic Upanishads marked a shift toward more philosophical interests, especially the nature of the soul (Atman) and its relationship to the ultimate ground of being (Brahman). They contain some of the earliest formulations of Brahman and Atman and the path of knowledge to achieve liberation (moksha).
- Some key mnemonics to remember:
    - Veda -> 4 collections; Rigveda (oldest); hymns/prayers; rituals; philosophy
    - Concepts: dharma; karma; samsara; deities (Agni, Indra, Soma)
    - Rituals: fire sacrifices; Soma rituals; performed by Brahmin priests
    - Upanishads: philosophical; Atman; Brahman; path of knowledge
