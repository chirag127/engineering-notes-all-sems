### Shankaracharya and Advaita Vedanta

- Adi Shankaracharya was an Indian philosopher and theologian who consolidated the doctrine of Advaita Vedanta.
- He lived in the 8th century CE and was instrumental in the revival of Hinduism in India.
- According to Advaita Vedanta, Brahman is the sole reality, and the world, as it appears, has no separate existence from Brahman. Brahman is infinite, eternal, conscious, and blissful. It is the ground of all being.
- The individual soul is nothing but Brahman. When a person perceives himself/herself as different from Brahman, he/she experiences misery. The path to liberation is the realization that one is identical with Brahman.
- This is achieved through apprehension of the mahavakyas (great sentences) of the Upanishads, persistent reflection on the teaching, and meditation.
- The most influential text of Advaita Vedanta is Adi Shankaracharya's Brahmasutra Bhashya. His other important works are Upadesasahasri, Vivekachudamani, and Atmavada.
- The core of Shankara's teachings is that the self (atman) is the only reality, while the world is an illusory appearance (maya). The atman is nothing but Brahman. Redemption consists in the recognition of this identity.
- Important concepts in Advaita are adhyasa (superimposition), avidya (ignorance), maya (illusion), and moksha (liberation). The path to moksha involves achieving vidya or true knowledge through which one realizes the identity of atman and Brahman.
