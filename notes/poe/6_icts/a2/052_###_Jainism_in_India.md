### Jainism in India

Jainism is an ancient Indian religion that emphasizes complete non-violence and asceticism. Followers of Jainism are called Jains, and they believe that all life is sacred. Jains reject the idea of a creator God and believe that liberation from karma and rebirth can be attained through strict self-discipline and non-harming behavior.

The key principles of Jainism are:

1. Ahimsa - Non-violence or non-harming. Jains go to great lengths to practice non-violence and minimize harm to all living beings.
2. Anekantavada - Non-absolutism or manifoldness. The idea that truth and reality are complex and have multiple aspects.
3. Karma - The law of cause and effect where intent and actions determine future outcomes, including in future lives after rebirth.
4. Samsara - The cycle of birth, life, death, and rebirth. Jains seek to liberate themselves from this cycle.
5. Moksha - The ultimate spiritual goal of liberation from samsara. This is achieved by following strict ascetic practices and non-harming behavior.

Some mnemonics to remember the key Jain principles:

- Ahimsa: First A, non-violence first
- Anekantavada: Not one-sided, multiple viewpoints
- Karma: Cause and effect, what goes around comes around
- Samsara: Cycle of life, stuck on a wheel
- Moksha: Freedom, liberation from the cycle

The strict moral code and ascetic practices of Jainism can seem challenging to follow. However, Jains believe these practices are worthwhile to minimize harm and achieve ultimate liberation. Jainism is one of the oldest religions of India, with a long philosophical and literary tradition influencing other Indian religions. Today there are about 4-5 million Jains worldwide, with most living in India.
