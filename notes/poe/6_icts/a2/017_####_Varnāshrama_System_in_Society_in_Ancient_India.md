#### Varnāshrama System in Society in Ancient India

The Varnāshrama system was the fundamental social structure in ancient Indian society. It categorized people into 4 broad classes (Varnas) based on their occupations and 4 stages of life (Āshramas).

The 4 Varnas were:

1. Brahmins: Priests, teachers and philosophers. They were the scholars and intellectuals.
2. Kshatriyas: Warriors and rulers. They were the administrative class who defended the society.
3. Vaishyas: Traders and merchants. They were the business class involved in trade and commerce.
4. Shudras: Labourers and service providers. They were the working class who served the other 3 Varnas.

The 4 Āshramas were:

1. Brahmacharya: The student stage where one learns Vedas and acquires education.
2. Grihastha: The householder stage where one gets married, earns a living and raises a family.
3. Vanaprastha: The retirement stage where one retires from worldly life and lives in a forest as an ascetic.
4. Sannyasa: The renunciation stage where one renounces all desires and attachments and lives as a wandering monk.

The Varnāshrama system aimed to ensure stability and order in society by assigning responsibilities and duties to different segments of people based on their qualities and capacities. However, over time the system became rigid and the Varnas transformed into hereditary castes leading to social stratification and inequality. The Āshrama system emphasized the importance of the spiritual journey of life through the 4 progressive stages.

The key points to remember are:

- The 4 Varnas: Brahmins, Kshatriyas, Vaishyas, Shudras
- The 4 Āshramas: Brahmacharya, Grihastha, Vanaprastha, Sannyasa
- The system aimed to ensure order but led to inequality and stratification
- The Āshrama system represented the spiritual journey of life through 4 stages
