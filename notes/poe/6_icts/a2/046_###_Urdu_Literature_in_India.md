### Urdu Literature in India

- Urdu literature flourished in medieval India, especially under the Delhi Sultanate and later the Mughal Empire. Prominent poets of this era include Amir Khusrau, who is credited with pioneering the qawwali, masnavi, and ghazal forms of poetry, and Mirza Ghalib.
- The Delhi Sultanate and later the Mughals encouraged the development of Urdu literature, which incorporated elements of Persian poetry. Urdu poetry reached its peak under Emperor Akbar, with poets like Faizi and Abdul Qadir Badayuni. Badayuni's history, called Muntakhab al-Tawarikh, is considered an important contemporary account of Akbar's era.
- The Urdu novel developed in the 19th century alongside the growth of prose. Some well-known Urdu novelists include Mirza Hadi Ruswa, whose novels highlighted social issues, and Munshi Premchand, regarded as one of the most famous and influential writers of modern Urdu and Hindi literature. His works include Godan and Sevasadan.
- During the rise of nationalism in the mid-20th century, several Urdu poets incorporated themes of social reform and progress in their works. These include Allama Iqbal, regarded as one of the most famous poets in South Asian history, and Muhammad Iqbal, a prominent philosopher and poet behind the idea of Pakistan. Faiz Ahmed Faiz is considered one of the most prominent leftist Urdu poets.
- Here are some useful mnemonics and learning tricks for Urdu literature:
- Remember the three prominent forms of poetry pioneered by Amir Khusrau - qawwali, masnavi, ghazal
- Associate Mughal emperors like Akbar with prominent poets and writers of their era for easy recall
- Note distinctive themes of different eras, e.g. social issues for 19th century novelists, nationalism for 20th century poets
- Read extracts and translations of famous poems and stories to understand context and style
- Study Urdu script to understand original texts and appreciate poetic devices and forms
