### Pottery as Cultural Heritage of India

- Pottery is one of the oldest industries/art forms in India with a rich history spanning over 5000 years. The pottery tradition is a cultural heritage of India with each region developing its own unique style, techniques and artistic traditions.
- Some key points about pottery as cultural heritage of India:

1. Origin and History: Pottery originated in the Indus Valley civilization around 3300 BCE. The pottery fragments excavated from sites like Harappa and Mohenjodaro show that pottery was made on wheel and also had painted motifs and decorative patterns. Over time, various regional styles emerged showcasing the rich cultural diversity of India.

2. Materials and Techniques: The raw materials used include clay, quartz, feldspar, etc. Techniques like coiling, pinching, wheel-throwing, slipcasting, glazing, etc. are used. Each region uses locally available materials and has its own techniques to produce distinct styles of pottery. For e.g. Terracotta is common in Bengal, Blue Pottery in Rajasthan, Khurja Pottery, etc.

3. Motifs and Patterns: The pottery exhibits a variety of decorative patterns and motifs inspired from regional flora and fauna as well as cultural patterns. Geometrical patterns, folk art, miniature paintings, etc. are commonly used in the decoration. The motifs represent the cultural ethos of the region.

4. Utility and Significance: Pottery has been used for various utilities like storage of water and grains, cooking, etc. The pottery artifacts are also significant as cultural symbols representing the regional heritage. They are collected as decorative pieces and the craft is promoted to preserve this heritage.

- Thus, pottery is an important part of Indian cultural heritage with a long history and tradition showcasing the diversity of materials, techniques, patterns across regions of India. The craft continues to be promoted as a cultural art form and for utility today.
