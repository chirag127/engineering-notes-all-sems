### Socio religious reform movement of 19th century in India

- The 19th century saw major socio-religious reform movements in India as a result of interaction with the West and growth of rational, liberal and scientific temper.
- The movements aimed to remove social evils and promote modern and scientific thought.
- Brahma Samaj founded by Raja Ram Mohan Roy in 1828 - rejected idol worship and emphasized monotheism and Upanishads. Also fought against Sati system and child marriage.
- Prarthana Samaj founded in 1867 by Atmaram Pandurang which propagated ideas of Brahmo Samaj in Western India.
- Arya Samaj founded by Swami Dayananda Saraswati in 1875 - rejected idolatry and emphasized the infallibility of the Vedas. Promoted widow remarriage and women education.
- Theosophical Society founded by Madame Blavatsky and Henry Olcott in 1875 which aimed to present Hinduism as a rational philosophy to the West.
- Ramakrishna Mission founded by Swami Vivekananda in 1897 which preached Vedanta and harmony of all religions.

*Advantages*: The movements attacked social evils, promoted women rights and education, portrayed Indian philosophy to the West in a rational way.
*Disadvantages*: The movements were confined to upper castes and elites. They did not sufficiently address caste inequality and untouchability. Overly emphasized Vedic tradition and rejected non-Vedic practices which were integral to Indian culture.
