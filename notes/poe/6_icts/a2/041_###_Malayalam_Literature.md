### Malayalam Literature

- Malayalam literature refers to writings, songs, plays, and poetry created in Malayalam, a South-Dravidian language spoken in the Indian state of Kerala. Malayalam literature is some of the oldest in India, with the earliest literary works roughly dated from between the 9th and 11th centuries.
- The earliest extant literary works in Malayalam are the Vazhappalli inscription and the Kerala Legislative Assembly oath. The early literature of Malayalam comprised of three types of composition: Malayalam songs, Malayalam poems, and Malayalam proses.
- Some of the earliest examples of Malayalam poetry include Unnuneeli Sandesam, Vasanavikkritiyin Kathaprasangam, and Vriththavilasam. Unnuneeli Sandesam, written in the 14th century by Nila-thampi-tampuran, is one of the most well-known of the ancient Malayalam poetic works. It was written as a love-token to a Nair lady named Unnyeli and follows the pattern of Valmiki's Sanskrit epic poem, the Ramayana's Sundara Kandam.
- Later, from the 17th century onwards, modern literature took shape with the influence of Sanskrit scholars and English missionaries. Modernity was reflected in the use of new literary forms like drama, essay, novel, etc.
- Some major writers in Malayalam literature include Kunchan Nambiar, Kumaran Asan, Ulloor S. Parameswara Iyer, Kadammanitta Ramakrishnan, Vaikom Muhammad Basheer, M. T. Vasudevan Nair, O. V. Vijayan, and M. Mukundan.
