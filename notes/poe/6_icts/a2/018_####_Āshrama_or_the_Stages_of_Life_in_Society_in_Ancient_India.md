#### Āshrama or the Stages of Life in Society in Ancient India

The ancient Indian society divided a man's life into four stages or āshramas:

1. Brahmacharya (student) - In this stage, a person undergoes rigorous training in a gurukula under the guidance of a guru. This training period lasts for 12-25 years, wherein the student acquires knowledge of scriptures and learns self-discipline and self-control.

2. Grihastha (householder) - In this stage, a person gets married, starts a family, and becomes a responsible member of the society. The Hindu texts consider this stage crucial as 'dharma' or righteous duty can be performed best as a householder.

3. Vānaprastha (retirement) - In this third stage, a person hands over familial responsibilities to the next generation, renounces material life and retires to the forest to devote life to spirituality.

4. Sannyāsa (renunciation) - In the final stage, a person renounces all worldly desires and attachments and becomes a wandering ascetic. The goal is to attain moksha or liberation from the cycles of birth and death.

**Mnemonics**:

1. Brahmacharya: *Brahman* (supreme truth) is the *charya* (conduct or goal)
2. Grihastha: *Griha* (house) is where a person lives, fulfils *sthā* (duties)
3. Vānaprastha: *Vāna* (forest) *prastha* (settling in) for ultimate *pra* (goal) of liberation
4. Sannyāsa: Complete *sannyāsa* (renunciation) of all to achieve *moksha* (liberation)

**Advantages**: The āshrama system inculcated discipline and purpose in an individual's life. It ensured that responsibilities towards family and society are duly fulfilled before ultimately attaining liberation.

**Disadvantages**: The system was rigid and did not account for variations in individuals' abilities and inclinations. Also, attaining the higher stages was a privilege of the upper castes, limiting the lower castes to the first two stages.
