### The Upanishads

The Upanishads are ancient Indian texts that contain some of the central philosophical concepts and ideas of Hinduism, primarily regarding the nature of ultimate reality (Brahman) and the relationship between Brahman and the individualself (Atman). They are considered as part of the Vedanta or the "last chapters, parts of the Vedas," and are commonly referred to as Vedanta.

- The Upanishads represent some of the most important spiritual and philosophical writings of ancient India. They are more than 200 in number, varying between 200 and 800 verses.
- The Upanishads are a genre of Indian texts that comment on the Vedas, dealing especially with Il or realizing consciousness and atman, the individual soul. They are also called Jnana Kanda, which means 'knowledge section', as they primarily deal with philosophical and spiritual ideas about the nature of ultimate reality (Brahman) and the goal of life.
- The core ideas of Upanishads are that Atman (soul) and Brahman (ultimate reality) are one and the same, and moksha or liberation can be attained by realizing this truth. This is expressed in the mahavakya 'tat tvam asi' (Thou art that).
- Some of the important Upanishads are: Isa Upanishad, Kena Upanishad, Katha Upanishad, Prasna Upanishad, Mundaka Upanishad, Mandukya Upanishad, Taittiriya Upanishad, and Chhandogya Upanishad. These contain teachings about Brahman, Atman, the path to liberation, and meditation.
- Key concepts in the Upanishads include: Brahman as the eternal truth and material cause of all that exists; Atman or soul as being one with Brahman; Karma and rebirth; Moksha or liberation as the goal of life; Yoga and meditation as the path to liberation.
- The Upanishads have been influential to Indian philosophy, as well as Western thinkers, and their ideas have resonated with concepts in Western metaphysics, philosophy of mind, and theories of the self.
