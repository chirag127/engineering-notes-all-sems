### Seals as Cultural Heritage of India

- Seals were an important part of the administrative system and economic activities in the Indus Valley Civilization. They were mainly used for stamping impressions on clay tags attached to goods.
- The seals were usually made of steatite and had animal motifs and pictographic inscriptions. The most common animal found on the seals is the unicorn, an animal with a single horn. Other animals include the zebu (humped bull), tiger, rhinoceros, and antelope.
- The seals prove the existence of a complex system of trade and administration. They indicate that the people of the Indus Valley Civilization traded with other contemporary civilizations in West Asia.
- The seals are an important cultural heritage as they provide insights into the art, craft, trade, administration, and religious beliefs of the people of the Indus Valley Civilization. They are archaeological artifacts that give us a glimpse into the lives of one of the earliest urban settlements in the Indian subcontinent.
- Some important seals found:
-- The Pashupati Seal: It depicts a seated figure surrounded by animals. It is considered to probably represent a proto-Shiva figure and provides evidence of possible early forms of Hinduism.
-- The Unicorn Seal: It depicts the unicorn, a mythical animal with a single horn. It is one of the most common seals found and its repeated use indicates that it probably had religious or symbolic significance.
