### Mathematics in India and Ancient India

- Mathematics in ancient India flourished in the Indus Valley civilization around 3000 BCE. The people built planned cities and harbour facilities requiring advanced engineering skills.
- The Indus script has not yet been deciphered. However, archaeological evidence and signs found suggest a decimal place value system with a symbol for zero.
- The Sulba Sutras (800-500 BCE) contained geometry formulas for the construction of altars in fire sacrifices, including the squaring of the circle and geometric approximations of surds.
- The Sutras contained formulas for the construction of regular polygons and provided two approximate formulas for π: `π ≈ 25/8 = 3.1250` and `π ≈ (√10)2/4 = 3.1622`.
- Aryabhata's Aryabhatiya (499 CE) introduced fundamental concepts of mathematics, including integers, fractions, proportions, algebraic equations, exact formulae for areas, and methods for calculating the lengths of chords of circles. He provided the formula `4(a - b) ≈ π(a2 + b2)` for calculating approximate areas.
- Brahmagupta's Brahmasphutasiddhanta (628 CE) contained quadratic equations, linear equations, and the Brahmagupta–Fibonacci identity. He gave the formula for solving a quadratic equation. He established fundamental rules for arithmetic involving zero and negative numbers. His work included the solutions of indeterminate linear equations.
- Between the 7th to 12th century, eminent mathematicians from Kerala School of Astronomy and Mathematics made contributions to calculus and infinite series. Madhava of Sangamagrama (14th century) calculated the approximate value of π to eleven decimal places. Nilakantha Somayaji (15th-16th century) composed the Tantra Samgraha which elaborated on infinite series approximations for π.
- Mnemonics: Aryabhata's formula `π ≈ (√10)2/4` can be remembered as `half of ten squared` and Brahmagupta's rule for quadratics as `plus/minus/same as b/2a`. The approximate value of π from Madhava can be remembered through `The digits of pi, Madhava did decree`.
