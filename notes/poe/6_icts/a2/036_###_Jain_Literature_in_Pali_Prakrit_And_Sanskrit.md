### Jain Literature in Pali,Prakrit And Sanskrit

Jain literature comprises Jain Agamas and subsequent commentatorial exegetical literature written in Prakrit, Sanskrit, Apabhramsha, Tamil, Kannada, Marathi, Hindi, and English.

- Jain Agamas: The Jain Agamas are the earliest literature composed by the Jain ascetics known as Kevalins. The Agamas were composed in Ardhamagadhi Prakrit and include the Angas which deal with philosophy and the rituals, and the Upangas which deal with sciences such as grammar, metaphysics, logic, and medicine. Important Agamas include:
-- Anguttara Nikaya which deals with numbers and sums
-- Sutrakrtanga which deals with the path to liberation
-- Uttaradhyayana Sutra which deals with rules of conduct for monks
-- Samavayanga Sutra which deals with legal issues

- Commentaries (anuvad): Important commentaries (anuvad) were written on the Agamas in Sanskrit by Kundakunda, Umaswati, Samantabhadra, Akalanka, Virasena and others. They include Pravacanasara, Niyamasara, Panchastikayasara, Pramanasamucchaya, Syadvadamañjarī, etc. which provide logical and metaphysical analysis.

- Later literature: Later Jain literature in Sanskrit includes Dhavala's massive Jñānasāra, the Yogaśāstra by Hemachandra and poetry by Muni Nemichandra, Yashovijaya, and others. Apabhramsha literature includes Prakrit poems by Padyavandana Siddhasena.
