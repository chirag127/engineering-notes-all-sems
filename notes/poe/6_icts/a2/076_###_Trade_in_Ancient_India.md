### Trade in Ancient India

- Trade was an important economic activity in Ancient India. The Indus Valley Civilization engaged in trade with Mesopotamia and other regions of Western Asia.
- The main exports from Ancient India were cotton textiles, spices like black pepper, indigo, ivory, pearls, etc. The imports included gold, silver, copper, tin, lead, etc.
- The overland trade routes or Silk Road facilitated trade with China & Central Asia. The sea routes connected the ports on the west coast with Egypt, Greece, and Rome.
- Barter system was prevalent where goods were exchanged for goods. Gradually, punch-marked coins made of silver and copper came into usage for trade around 6th century BCE.
- The Mauryan empire under Ashoka further boosted trade by building roads and rest houses. The Silk Road became more accessible. Taxila and Ujjain emerged as important trade centers.
- During the Gupta period, the trade of cotton textiles flourished. Fa-Hien recorded the presence of wealthy merchants in cities. The decline of the Gupta empire impacted trade and led to a decline in urban centers.
- Some key points to remember:
• Indus Valley traded with Mesopotamia: cotton, spices for gold, silver
• Overland (north): Silk Route; Sea (southwest): with Egypt, Greece, Rome
• Barter to silver/copper coins (6th century BCE)
• Mauryan empire: more roads; flourishing trade centers like Taxila, Ujjain
• Gupta: thriving cotton textile trade; wealthy merchants in cities
• Decline of Gupta empire: decline in trade and urban centers
