### Northern Indian languages

The major Northern Indian languages are:

1. Hindi - the official language of the Indian government and the most widely spoken. Mnemonics to remember Devanagari script vowels: AH EI OH OO UH.
2. Punjabi - the primary language of the Punjab region, spoken by over 100 million people. It uses the Gurmukhi script.
3. Urdu - the official language of Pakistan, also spoken by many in Northern India. It is written in a Perso-Arabic script and uses many consonants and vowels.
4. Bengali - the official language of West Bengal and Bangladesh. The Bengali script is an abugida, or alphabetic writing system. Some consonants are written in slightly different ways depending on whether they appear at the start, middle or end of a word.

Advantages of learning Northern Indian languages:

- Gain insight into the history and culture of the region. Languages reflect the historical influences on a region.
- Improve job prospects in Northern India or Pakistan. Being multilingual is desirable for many careers.
- Read ancient literature and poetry in the original language. Much gets lost in translation.
- Travel and immerse yourself in Northern Indian communities. Speaking the local language opens up more opportunities for connection.

Disadvantages:

- Northern Indian languages can be quite different from European languages, with different scripts and sounds, so can be challenging to learn.
- Many Northern Indian languages have complex grammar, with genders, cases and verb conjugations.
- Resources for learning some Northern Indian languages may be more limited than for more popular languages.
