### Geography in India and Ancient India

* The Indian subcontinent is located in southern Asia and is surrounded by the Himalayas in the north, the Bay of Bengal in the east, the Arabian Sea in the west, and the Indian Ocean to the south.
* The Indus Valley Civilization flourished around 2500 BCE in what is now Pakistan and northwest India. It had well-planned cities and a writing system.
* The Vedic period followed, in which the Vedas, ancient scriptures of Hinduism, were composed. This period saw the rise of major kingdoms known as the Mahajanapadas.
* The Mauryan Empire unified most of the Indian subcontinent in the 3rd century BCE under Emperor Ashoka, who converted to Buddhism and promoted the spread of Buddhist practices.
* The Gupta Empire ruled from the 4th to the 6th century CE and is considered a golden age of Hindu culture, characterized by advances in science, technology, engineering, art, dialectic, literature, logic, mathematics, astronomy, religion and philosophy.
* The Delhi Sultanate ruled parts of northern India from 1206 to 1526, when the Mughal Empire took control until 1858. The Mughal period saw growth of Indo-Islamic architecture, painting, and culture.
* Ancient Indian geography consisted of the Indus river valley civilization and the Ganges river valley civilization, along with the Thar desert and Deccan plateaus in between. The Himalayas to the north acted as a natural boundary.
* The country had a diverse range of climates and vegetation, including the Thar desert, the monsoon forests of the Western Ghats, and the Himalayan slopes. Wildlife included tigers, elephants, and Indian rhinoceros.
* The caste system divided people into rigid hereditary groups, while there were many tribal groups and indigenous forest dwellers outside the caste system. Family and religion were important in ancient Indian culture.
