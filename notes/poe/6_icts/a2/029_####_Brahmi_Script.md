#### Brahmi Script

- Brahmi script is one of the oldest writing systems of India and the ancestor of most modern Indian scripts. It was primarily used to write Sanskrit and Prakrit.
- The earliest Brahmi inscriptions are found in the 6th century BCE. The Brahmi script consisted of syllabic characters and was written from right to left.
- The Brahmi script consists of 34 consonants and is phonetic, i.e. the characters stand for distinct sounds. The characters were simplified and amplified over centuries to develop into modern Indic scripts such as Devanagari, Tamil, Kannada, etc.
- Mnemonics to remember the order of Brahmi consonants: "Kapi Ganayo Jivanti Bhoota" where each word starts with a consonant (in order) of Brahmi - Ka, Pa, Ga, etc.
- Advantages: Phonetic, ancestral to many scripts, able to represent syllables rather than individual sounds.
- Disadvantages: Ambiguous characters, many variants evolved leading to difficulties in decipherment.
- Applications: Earliest Brahmi inscriptions provide valuable insights into language, society, and culture of ancient India. They are important sources to study political, social, economic and cultural history.
