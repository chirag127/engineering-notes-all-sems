### Puppetry as Cultural Heritage of India

Puppetry is a traditional form of entertainment and folk art in India with a long historical tradition. Puppets are inanimate objects, often resembling some type of human or animal figure, that are animated or manipulated by a puppeteer. In India, puppets are mostly made of wood, cloth and leather. The tradition of puppetry in India is more than 2000 years old and has a magical quality that fascinates children and adults alike. The four main types of traditional puppets in India are:

- String puppets or Marionette: Puppets are suspended and controlled by strings. Example: Rajasthani Kathputli
- Rod puppets: Puppets are attached to rods and controlled by the puppeteer. Example: Yampuri puppetry of Bihar
- Shadow puppets: Puppets are made of transparent animal hide and manipulated behind a screen with a light source. Example: Togalu Gombeyata of Karnataka
- Glove puppets: Puppets wear hands like gloves and are manipulated by fingers. Example: Kundhei nata of Odisha

Some key points about puppetry in India:

- The themes of traditional puppetry draw from Indian epics like Ramayana and Mahabharata which helps to preserve cultural heritage and values.
- The puppeteers are highly skilled artists who train under gurus to learn the art. The puppeteers sing songs and deliver dialogues while performing to enhance the entertainment value.
- The puppet shows earlier narrated moral stories and lessons but now also include contemporary social topics to remain relevant.
- The puppetry art form faces challenges from modern entertainment and requires support to sustain this cultural heritage.
- The Indian government has established national puppetry institutes and holds festivals to promote awareness about this art form.
