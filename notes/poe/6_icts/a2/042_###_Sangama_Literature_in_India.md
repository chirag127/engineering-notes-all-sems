### Sangama Literature in India

- Sangama literature refers to the body of literature composed in the Tamil language during the Sangam Period which lasted from circa 300 BCE to 300 CE.
- The Sangam literature deals with emotional and material topics such as love, war, governance, and bereavement.
- The Sangam literature consists of 2,381 known poems written by 473 poets, and is dated to between the 1st century BCE and 4th century CE.
- The Sangam literature is categorized into 'akam' poetry which deals with emotional matters and 'puram' poetry which deals with external affairs and heroism.
- The Tolkappiyam, a Tamil poetic work on grammar and poetics, is dated to 3rd century BCE based on linguistic analysis, and thus represents the earliest extant work of Tamil literature.
- Some key works of Sangam literature are the Eight Anthologies (Ettuthokai) and the Ten Idylls (Pattupattu). The Thirukkural by Thiruvalluvar is among the most celebrated works of Sangam literature.
- The Sangam literature provides remarkable insight into the social, political, economic and cultural life of early Tamil-speaking people. It is a valuable historical source material, reflecting Tamil society between the 3rd century BCE and 3rd century CE.
- Mnemonic: Think of 'Sangam' as sand resting on an ocean shore ('Sangam' - joining; 'sand' - seashore). The early Tamil literature came together and joined at this seashore of history. The literature deals with the joys and sorrows of life ('ocean' - emotions; 'shore' - material world) in ancient Tamil society.
