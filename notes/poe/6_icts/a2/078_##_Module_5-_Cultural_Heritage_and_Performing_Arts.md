## Module 5- Cultural Heritage and Performing Arts

- Cultural Heritage refers to the legacy of physical artifacts and intangible attributes of a group or society that are inherited from past generations, maintained in the present and bestowed for the benefit of future generations. It includes tangible culture (such as buildings, monuments, artifacts, etc.) and intangible culture (such as traditions, languages, practices, etc.).
- Some important aspects of India's cultural heritage are:
- Architectural heritage like Taj Mahal, Red Fort, Konark Sun Temple, Khajuraho Temples, etc. depicting the architectural brilliance of ancient India.
- Monuments and historical sites like Hampi, Ajanta and Ellora caves, etc. providing glimpses into India's rich history.
- Folk and tribal art forms like Madhubani paintings, Warli paintings, Pattachitra paintings, etc. depicting the creative imagination of folk and tribal artists of India.
- Classical dance forms like Bharatnatyam, Kathak, Odissi, Kuchipudi, Mohiniyattam, etc. combining rhythmic foot movements, hand gestures and facial expressions to represent emotions and stories.
- Classical music like Hindustani and Carnatic music with melodious ragas and talas which are meditative, soothing and spiritually uplifting.

- Performing arts refer to art forms like music, dance, theatre, opera, mime, etc. which are performed live in front of an audience. Some key aspects are:
- They are performative, i.e. artists perform in front of live audiences.
- They are temporal, i.e. the art is experienced as it is performed and then disappears.
- They involve collaboration between artists and mutual coordination to enact the performance.
- They entertain, inspire and move audiences emotionally and intellectually.
- Important performing arts in India are classical dances, theatre forms like folk theatre and Sanskrit theatre, and musical traditions like Hindustani and Carnatic music.
