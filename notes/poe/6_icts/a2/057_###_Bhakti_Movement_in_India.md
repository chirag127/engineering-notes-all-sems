### Bhakti Movement in India

The Bhakti Movement was a revivalist movement that originated in southern India in the 7th-8th centuries CE. It emphasized devotional worship of a personal God, instead of the ritualistic worship of Vedic deities. The movement was led by saint-poets known as Alvars and Nayanars, who came from varied social backgrounds - from Brahmins to Dalits and women.

Some key features of the Bhakti Movement:

- Rejection of Brahmanical dominance and caste hierarchies. Bhakti saints like Kabir, Ravidas rejected the caste system and asserted that devotion to God is the only path to salvation.
- Worship of a personal God. The idea of an abstract Brahman was rejected in favor of a saguna Brahman, or God with qualities and form. Vishnu and Shiva were the most popular deities.
- Use of vernacular languages instead of Sanskrit. The saint-poets expressed their devotional poetry in regional languages to make the ideas accessible to common people.
- Eclectic philosophies. The movement drew elements from various traditions like Vedanta, Vaishnavism and Shaivism and harmonized them. It also accommodated folk traditions and non-Vedic beliefs.

Some Mnemonics/learning tricks:

- Think of the 6 Cs: Criticism of caste; Concept of saguna Brahman; Use of Common languages; Eclectic philosophies
- Remember key saints: Namadeva (Maharashtra), Kabir & Ravidas (North India), Meera & Surdas (Rajasthan), Purandaradasa (Karnataka), Annamacharya (Andhra)
- Think of how Bhakti democratized worship, emphasizing devotion over rigid rituals and social hierarchies. The saints' simple verses and songs made the ideas relatable to common people.

The Bhakti Movement strengthened the cultural unity of India and had a lasting influence on Indian society. It shaped the nature of modern Hinduism and contributed to a vernacular literary tradition in Indian languages.
