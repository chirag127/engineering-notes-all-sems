#### Force Theory of origin of State

According to Force Theory, the State came into existence as a result of the exertion of force by a conquering victor. The history of the ancient world shows that tribes, chiefdoms, and kingdoms emerged as victors forcibly subjected the vanquished. The State was the creation of the conqueror as he extracted obedience and tribute from the conquered through the exercise of force.

- Mnemonic: The mighty rules over the weak by FORCE. The origin of the State lies in FORCE.
- The theory stresses the role of force and violence in the origin of the State. The chief, king, or ruler derives his authority from the force that he exerts over his subjects. Without the ability and willingness to use force, the ruler cannot maintain his position or the State.
- Examples: The Egyptian, Assyrian, and Roman empires which were formed through military conquests and exertion of force over the vanquished support this theory. Many contemporary States have also originated through wars and revolutions.
- Advantage: It highlights the importance of military power and use of force in the origin of the State. The State rests ultimately on force.
- Disadvantage: It takes a one-sided view that force is the only factor in the origin of the State. Other factors like mutual benefit, cooperation, and social contract are ignored. The use of force may be a condition but not the sole cause of the State.
- Applications: The theory stresses the role of military in the State. However, relying only on force and violence is not sustainable in the long run. Other institutions and shared beliefs are required to legitimize the State. A balance of force and consent is ideal for the smooth functioning of the State.
