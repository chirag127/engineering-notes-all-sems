### State in Ancient India

The ancient Indian subcontinent consisted of many states and kingdoms. Some of the major states and their key characteristics are:

- Mahajanapadas: The Mahajanapadas were 16 major kingdoms or oligarchies that existed during 600 BC to 300 BC, including states such as Magadha, Anga, Vajji, etc. They had planned cities, currency, standing armies, and agriculture was the main occupation.
- Magadha: Magadha emerged as the most powerful Mahajanapada, with its capital at Rajagriha and later Pataliputra. The Haryanka, Shishunaga, Nanda, and Mauryan dynasties ruled from Magadha. Chandragupta Maurya founded the Mauryan Empire, which became the first pan-Indian empire.
- Mauryan Empire: The Mauryan Empire was founded by Chandragupta Maurya in 324 BC and reached its peak under Ashoka the Great. It covered most of the subcontinent and parts of Afghanistan. An efficient bureaucracy and a large army were maintained. Buddhism flourished during this period.
- Gupta Empire: The Gupta Empire ruled from 320 to 550 CE. It is considered the Golden Age of ancient India, with advancements in science, technology, engineering, art, dialectics, literature, logic, mathematics, astronomy, religion and philosophy. The empire was decentralised and had an efficient system of taxation.
