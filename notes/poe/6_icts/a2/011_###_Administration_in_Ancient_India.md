### Administration in Ancient India

- The ancient Indian polity was highly decentralized with several small kingdoms and a few large empires.
- The Mauryan empire under Chandragupta Maurya and Ashoka was one of the first large-scale administrations.
- The Mauryan empire was divided into provinces, districts, and villages. The officials were appointed at various levels of administration.
- The village was the basic unit of administration with the Gram Panchayat managing village affairs. It looked after irrigation, sanitation, and trade in villages.
- The Arthashastra by Kautilya discusses a complex system of administration with government machinery for taxation, law and order, etc.
- The Gupta empire had a decentralized administration with local councils and officials. The empire was divided into provinces, districts, and villages.
- The Chola empire in South India had an effective administrative system with officials appointed for taxation, maintaining law and order, irrigation, local judiciary, etc.
- The Vijayanagara empire had a hierarchical system of administration with the King at the top, followed by nobles, officials, and local councils at the village level.
- Key officials in ancient Indian administration included the Pradhan (chief minister), Senapati (commander-in-chief), Bhandagarika (treasurer), and Dandapashika (police chief).
- Revenue was collected as tax on agriculture produce, trade, and from mines. This was used to fund the administrative machinery and public works.
- Many principles of Hindu philosophy like Dharma, Artha, Kama, and Moksha influenced the system of administration aiming for a just and efficient governance.
