### Indian Architecture in Ancient India and present times

- Ancient Indian architecture can be traced back to the Indus Valley civilization (3000 BCE - 1500 BCE). The excavated ruins of the ancient city of Mohenjo-Daro in present-day Pakistan reveal an ancient city with buildings made of baked brick, public baths, and a covered drainage system.
- During the Mauryan Empire (322 BCE - 185 BCE), palaces and pillars were built using stones and polished sandstone. The iconic Mauryan pillar at Sarnath is an example of the sophisticated stone-working skills of the craftsmen during this period.
- The Gupta period (320 CE - 550 CE) is known as the "Golden Age" of Indian architecture. This period saw the construction of Hindu temples, including the Dashavatara Temple in Deogarh and the temple ruins at Sanchi. These temples were often small, made of stone and brick, and comprised of a square shrine with a flat roof and pyramidal tower.
- The rise of the Chalukya, Pallava, and Chola dynasties in southern and central India (6th century CE - 12th century CE) led to the creation of structural temples in stone. These temples featured towering gopurams (entrance towers), halls, and shrines. The magnificent stone temples at Mahabalipuram and Kanchipuram stand as testimony to the architectural achievements of the Pallava dynasty. The Brihadeeswarar Temple at Thanjavur, built by the Chola king Rajaraja Chola I, is considered a masterpiece of Dravidian architecture.
- Islamic architecture in India flourished with the establishment of the Delhi Sultanate in the 13th century and later the Mughal Empire. Examples of Islamic architecture include the Qutub Minar in Delhi, the Taj Mahal in Agra, and the Fatehpur Sikri in Uttar Pradesh. These grand structures incorporated Islamic architectural elements such as domes, arches, and gardens within their design.
- In modern India, architectural styles demonstrate a blend of cultural influences from domestic and foreign sources. Contemporary Indian architects are fusing traditional elements with modern designs to create innovative structures that reflect India's rich architectural heritage as well as its progressive outlook.
