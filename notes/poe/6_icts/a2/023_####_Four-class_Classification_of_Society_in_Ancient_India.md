#### Four-class Classification of Society in Ancient India

The ancient Indian society was divided into four classes based on occupation and economic status:

1. Brahmins: The priests and teachers. They were at the top of the social hierarchy and were exempt from taxation. They were traditionally supposed to acquire knowledge and teach.

2. Kshatriyas: The warriors and rulers. They were the military elite and political leaders. They were the protectors of the society.

3. Vaishyas: The traders and merchants. They were involved in business, trade, and agriculture. They produced goods and paid taxes.

4. Shudras: The labourers and service providers. They did manual labour and served the other three classes. They were at the bottom of the social hierarchy.

This four-fold classification was an ideal model mentioned in ancient scriptures. In reality, there were more complex social divisions and hierarchies. The classification was not strictly based on birth but was relatively rigid.
