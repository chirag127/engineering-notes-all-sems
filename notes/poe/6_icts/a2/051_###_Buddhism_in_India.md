### Buddhism in India

- Buddhism was founded in ancient India by Siddhartha Gautama, who is known as the Buddha, meaning 'enlightened one'.
- Siddhartha Gautama was born into an aristocratic family in present-day Nepal around 500 BCE. According to Buddhist tradition, he spent years meditating on the nature of life and attained enlightenment at the age of 35.
- The four noble truths are one of the core teachings of Buddhism: (1) Life is suffering; (2) The cause of suffering is desire; (3) The end of desire leads to the end of suffering; (4) The eightfold path can end desire.
- The eightfold path describes ways to end suffering by developing wisdom, morality, and meditation. It includes guidelines for moral behavior, meditation, and wisdom to reach enlightenment.
- After the Buddha's death, Buddhism spread through much of Asia, developing into a variety of forms and doctrines along the way. Buddhism did not survive in the land of its birth, India, where it gradually declined after the 12th century CE due to the expansion of Hinduism and later Islam. However, it remains an influential religion and philosophy today with over 500 million followers worldwide.

- Picture the eightfold path as an eight-spoked wheel to remember the eight elements.
- Associate different Buddhist teachings and terms with visual images and stories to remember them more easily, e.g. think of a desire-free state as an empty glass with no water (desire) to remember the second noble truth.
