### North Indian Literature

* The literatures of various North Indian languages such as Sanskrit, Prakrit, Apabhramsha, Hindi, Urdu, Punjabi, Kashmiri, Nepali, etc. are collectively known as North Indian Literature.
* Sanskrit literature is considered as the foundation stone of Indian literature. Some of the major works are:
- Vedas and Upanishads which contain mystical and philosophical ideas.
- Ramayana and Mahabharata which are the most celebrated epics.
- Kālidāsa's lyrical poetry and plays like Abhijñānaśākuntala.
- Panchatantra which contains animal fables and stories with moral teachings.
* Apabhramsha literature includes works such as the Rasovaiśeṣika Kaha of Dhanapala.
* Hindi literature begins with the works of Āmīr Khosrow and Kabīr in the 13th and 15th centuries respectively. Major works are Premchand's Godān and Hindi novels of Munshi Premchand.
* Urdu literature flourished under the Delhi Sultanate and the Mughal Empire. Major poets are Amir Khusrow, Mirza Ghalib, Iqbal, Faiz Ahmad Faiz, etc.
* Other notable literatures with a distinct cultural heritage are Nepali, Punjabi, Kashmiri, etc.

* Some Mnemonics to remember:
- to remember major Sanskrit works: 'VEDAs, rAmAyaNa, mahAbhArata, kAlidAsa'

* Some tips: Focus on understanding themes and ideas rather than just memorizing names. Read translations or transliterations to get a sense of style and language. Identify unique characteristics of each literature to differentiate them.
