#### Theories of origin of State in India

The major theories regarding the origin of the State in ancient India are:

1. Divine origin theory: According to this theory, the State derived its authority from the divine. The kings were considered to be representatives of God on earth. They derived their power and authority from the Brahmins who were considered as the repositories of Dharma. This theory was propounded in ancient Indian religious texts like Vedas, Puranas, etc.

2. Social contract theory: According to this theory, the State originated from an implicit social contract between the ruler and the ruled. The people in the primitive society surrendered their freedom in the interest of peace, order, and security and received protection from the ruler, who in turn got his authority from the people. This theory was proposed by ancient Indian political thinkers like Kautilya.

3. Evolution theory: According to this theory, the State evolved from the primitive tribal system as a result of the forces of socio-economic developments as well as historical circumstances. The tribal oligarchy evolved into monarchy and then despotism. This view was proposed by modern historians like Vincent Smith.

Advantages of these theories:
- They explain the origin and evolution of the State and political institution from simple to complex forms.
- They lay emphasis on the relationship between the ruler and the ruled.

Disadvantages:
- These theories are speculative as there is no concrete historical evidence to prove them.
- They ignore the material forces of production in the origin of the State.

Overall, all the theories partially explain the origin of the State. The actual origin was likely due to a combination of factors - historical, economic, social as well as religious.
