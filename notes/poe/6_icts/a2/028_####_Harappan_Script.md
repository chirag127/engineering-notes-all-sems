#### Harappan Script

The Harappan script is the script used by the Indus Valley Civilization. It was used between c. 3300 BCE and 1900 BCE. The script has not yet been deciphered, despite numerous attempts. The following are some key points about the Harappan script:

- The script consists of around 400-600 signs. The signs are mostly pictorial but include many abstract signs as well.
- The script was written from right to left.
- The script has been found on seals, tablets, ceramics, tools, coins, and other artifacts.
- The script shows minimal variation over space and time, suggesting it was well-developed early on in the civilization.
- Attempts at decipherment suggest the script may be logo-syllabic, encoding both ideas and sounds. However, the short length of inscriptions makes decipherment challenging.
- Some scholars suggest the script may have connections to later Brahmi scripts, while others believe it is a separate indigenous development. More evidence is needed to determine which theory is correct.

To memorize the key points:

- 400-600 signs, pictorial and abstract
- Right to left script
- Found on many artifacts
- Little variation, well-developed early
- May be logo-syllabic
- Connections to Brahmi uncertain
