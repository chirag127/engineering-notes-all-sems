### Pre-Vedic Religion in India

- The Pre-Vedic religion in India refers to the religious beliefs and practices of the Indus Valley Civilization (3300 BCE - 1300 BCE).
- The Indus Valley people worshipped a Mother Goddess and a horned deity (possibly a proto-Shiva figure), in addition to animal totems and other symbols.
- They had a well-developed sense of aesthetics as evident from their town-planning, sanitation systems, and art such as the famous bronze Dancing Girl statuette.
- The Indus script has not yet been deciphered, so little is known about their specific religious beliefs. However, seals and other artifacts suggest probable worship of a Mother Goddess and a Proto-Shiva figure.
- Possible Mnemonic: Think of the 'Mother Goddess' and 'horned deity' as the two main figures of Pre-Vedic religion. The well planned 'towns' and 'sanitation systems' show their sense of organization. The 'Dancing Girl' statuette displays their artistic skills.
- The decline of the Indus Valley civilization around 1900 BCE led to the development of Vedic religion and culture associated with Indo-Aryan migrations into South Asia. The Vedas became foundational scriptures of Hinduism which absorbed some elements of the earlier Pre-Vedic religion.
- Possible learning trick: Remember, 'Mother Goddess' and 'town planning' for Pre-Vedic. 'Vedas' and 'Indo-Aryans' for the later development. The 'horned deity' connect Pre-Vedic with later Hindu figures like Shiva.
