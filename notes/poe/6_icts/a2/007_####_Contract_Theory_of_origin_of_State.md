#### Contract Theory of origin of State

According to the Contract Theory, the State originated as a result of a social contract between individuals in a pre-political state (state of nature). In the state of nature, life was solitary, poor, nasty, brutish and short. There was no security of life or property. So, individuals came together and voluntarily agreed to form a political society through a social contract by surrendering some of their natural rights in return for order, security, and other social benefits. The key proponents of this theory were Thomas Hobbes, John Locke and Jean-Jacques Rousseau.

- According to Hobbes, in the state of nature life was 'solitary, poor, nasty, brutish and short'. So, individuals agreed to establish a sovereign with absolute power to maintain law and order. In return, individuals got security of life and property.
- According to Locke, individuals in the state of nature had natural rights to life, liberty and property. But there was insecurity due to conflicts. So, individuals agreed to form a society and establish a government through a social contract to protect natural rights. Government's power is limited and if it fails to protect rights, people can overthrow it.
- According to Rousseau, in the state of nature humans were free and equal. But the development of private property led to corruption and inequality. So, individuals agreed to form a society by entering into a social contract and surrendering their individual rights to the 'general will' or collective sovereignty. This resulted in a system where 'force is made legitimate' and people are 'forced to be free'.

Advantages: Explains the origin of the State as a result of a voluntary agreement which protects the natural rights and interests of individuals.
Disadvantages: The state of nature is hypothetical. The social contract is a historical fiction. It fails to explain the origin of inequality and domination in society. The absolute sovereignty version does not protect individual rights.
