### Buddhist Literature in Pali,Prakrit And Sanskrit

The Buddhist literature developed in three major languages:

1. Pali: The Theravada school of Buddhism uses Pali as its primary language. The Pali Canon or Tipitaka contains the teachings of the Buddha in the form of Suttas or Discourses. It includes Vinaya Pitaka (on monastic discipline), Sutta Pitaka (teachings of the Buddha), and Abhidhamma Pitaka (philosophical and psychological analysis). Some important Pali texts are Dhammapada, Jataka tales, Visuddhimagga, etc.

2. Prakrit: The literature of the Mahasanghikas and other early Buddhist schools were composed in Prakrit, including the now lost Sarvastivada texts. Some extant Prakrit texts include the Mahavastu of the Lokottaravadins and the Divyavadana.

3. Sanskrit: The Mahayana and Vajrayana schools produced a vast amount of literature in Sanskrit, including the Prajnaparamita Sutras, Avatamsaka Sutra, Lotus Sutra, Diamond Sutra, etc. Important commentarial works include the Mulamadhyamakakarika of Nagarjuna and the Yogacara texts like the Sandhinirmocana Sutra and the writings of Asanga and Vasubandhu.

Some Mnemonics and learning tricks for the topics:

1. 'Pali Pitaka' rhymes and is easy to remember as the canon of Theravada school in Pali
2. 'Prakrit' sounds like 'practical' so easy to remember as the language of early schools
3. 'Sanskrit' has 'script' in it, so easy to associate with the varied Mahayana literature
