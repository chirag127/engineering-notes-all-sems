### India's Dominance up to Pre-colonial Times and Ancient India

- Ancient India was one of the most technologically and economically advanced regions in the world from around 3000 BCE to 1800 CE.
- The Indus Valley Civilization (3300 BCE - 1300 BCE) had well-planned cities, efficient infrastructure, and trade with Mesopotamia. They had advances in metallurgy, wheeled vehicles, and stoneweights.
- The Vedic Period (1500 BCE - 500 BCE) saw the rise of the Vedas (religious texts), the caste system, and new crops/farming techniques leading to surplus agricultural production. The Mahajanapadas were powerful entities with majestic capitals, assemblies, and armies.
- The Mauryan Empire (322 BCE - 185 BCE) under Chandragupta Maurya and Ashoka was one of the largest empires of the time, thriving on efficient administration, taxation, and a vast espionage system. Ashoka promoted dhammavijaya (conquest by righteousness).
- The Gupta Empire (320 CE - 550 CE) under Chandragupta II was a "Golden Age" with advances in science, technology, engineering, art, dialectics, literature, logic, mathematics, astronomy, religion, and philosophy. The decimal number system and chess were developed, and Aryabhatta proposed the heliocentric theory.
- The Chalukya, Pallava, Pandya, and Chola dynasties of the South (230 BCE - 1279 CE) had flourishing trade and built magnificent temples displaying Dravidian architecture. Fine arts, literature, and dance flourished. The Chola navy was a major sea power in the Indian Ocean.
- Other major dynasties included the Kushanas who re-established the Silk Route and the Mughals who led a flourishing composite culture under Akbar promoting "Sulh-e-Kul" (peace to all). There were advances in all fields during this period.
