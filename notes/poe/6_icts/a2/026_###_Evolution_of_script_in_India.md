### Evolution of script in India

The evolution of writing systems in India can be classified into the following major phases:

1. Pre-Harappan script (3500 BCE - 2600 BCE): The Indus script is considered to be the earliest form of writing system developed in the Indus Valley civilization. However, the script has not yet been deciphered and there is no consensus on whether it constitutes a complete writing system.

2. Brahmi script (3rd century BCE): Developed in the Mauryan period, Brahmi is considered the first indigenous Indian script. It was developed for writing Prakrit and Sanskrit. Major scripts such as Kharoshthi, Gupta, and Tamil evolved from Brahmi.

Mnemonics: "MahaRaj Bṛhaspati Vākham" - Brahmi was created by the 'Great King Bṛhaspati's speech'

Advantages: phonetic, clear, and precise system of writing

3. Nagari script (4th century CE): Evolved from Brahmi and became popular in northern and western India. It is an abugida writing system used to write several languages including Sanskrit, Hindi, Marathi, and Nepali. The Devanagari script is the most widely used form today for writing Hindi, Marathi, and Nepali.

Learning trick: 'Nāgarī' which means 'of the city' as it was more popular in urban areas.

Disadvantages: complex write system leading to longer learning time.
