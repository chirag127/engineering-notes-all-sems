### Persian Literature in India

- Persian literature flourished in India during the Delhi Sultanate and Mughal rule from the 13th to 19th centuries.
- Many Persian poets, writers and scholars migrated to India and contributed to the Persian literary tradition of the subcontinent.
- Prominent Persian poets of India include Amir Khusrau, Nizami Ganjavi and Muhammad Iqbal who wrote primarily in Persian and have buildings, institutions and roads named after them in modern-day India and Pakistan.
- Persian poetry was greatly valued at the courts of the Mughal emperors, who were also major patrons of Persian literature and had their own works created. The most famous Mughal Persian poets include:
-- Akbar - composed verses in Persian under the pen-name 'Baadshah'
-- Jahangir - wrote an account of his reign Tuzk-e Jahangiri in Persian
-- Shah Jahan - built famous architectural marvel Taj Mahal which features Persian inscriptions & poetry
- Persian was the official court language and second language of the Mughal nobility. Translators, poets, scholars and administrators were patronized by the Mughal courts.
- Major Persian literary forms in India include: Masnavi (poems), Qasida (panegyrics), Ghazal (lyric poetry), Rubai (quatrains). Famous epic poems include Shahnameh & Khamsa-e Nizami. Prose works include biographies, histories and hagiographies of Sufi saints.
- Many terms from Persian literature have endured in South Asian languages, including Urdu. Examples are she'r (poetry), masnavi (poem), qissa (tale), dastan (story). Useful mnemonics: Remember 'Masnavi', the title of 2 famous Persian poems, to associate Persian poetry in India with narrative poems.
