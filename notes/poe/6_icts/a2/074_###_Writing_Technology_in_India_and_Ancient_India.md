### Writing Technology in India and Ancient India

- The Indus script was the writing system developed by the Indus Valley Civilization and used between 2600 and 1900 BCE. The script appears to have had about 400 distinct symbols but the meaning of the symbols remained undeciphered.
- The Brahmi script was the first writing system developed in India around the 3rd century BCE. It was the ancestor of many Indian scripts including Tamil, Kannada, and Devanagari. Major religions including Buddhism, Jainism, and Hinduism were recorded in Brahmi script.
- The Kharoshthi script developed in Gandhara around the 4th century BCE was used to write the Gandhari and Sanskrit languages. It was written from right to left and was used until about the 4th century CE before being displaced by the Gupta script.
- **Mnemonic:** Remember 'Brahmi' as 'Brahma's script' as it was the first Indian script developed around the time when the Brahma concept emerged in Indian religions.

- Palm leaf manuscripts were commonly used in ancient India for writing and were more durable than paper. The palm leaves were inscribed with a metal stylus to create the letters of the script. The scripts or letters were often ornate or artistic in design.
- Inscriptions on stones, pillars, and seals were also commonly used for recording important information. The Prakrit inscriptions of Indian Emperor Ashoka in the 3rd century BCE were some of the earliest stone inscriptions. They contained edicts promoting Buddhist philosophy.
- The invention of paper in ancient China spread to India around the 13th century CE allowing for longer scrolls and manuscripts to be produced more easily than palm leaves. Paper allowed literature, religious texts, and other writings to spread more easily in India.
