### Physics in India and Ancient India

- Ancient Indian philosophers made seminal contributions to physics and cosmology. Notable examples include Kanada (founder of the Vaisheshika School), Kashyapa (who classified motors and their categories), and Panca??ikha (who discussed light and its properties).
- The Vedas especially the oldest Rigveda had speculative theories on the origin of the universe. Nasadiya sukta, a famous hymn mentions the origin of universe from "nothing": "Darkness there was at first, by darkness hidden" without any fixed date proposed.
- The Samkhya and Yoga schools of philosophy proposed theories on the origin and structure of the universe from Prakriti (primal nature). Samkhya proposed the idea of Puru??a (consciousness) and Prakriti (primal nature) interacting to create the phenomenal world. Yoga's corpus includes the Yoga Sutras of Pata??jali.
- Jain philosophy also had theories of cosmology with space and time as substances and the universe going through repeated cycles of existence. The Jain text Surya Prajnapti classifies the universe into 3 parts: hells, middle-world, and heavens.
- The Upanishads and other texts discussed ideas related to psychology, metaphysics, and epistemology. The Upanishads contain some speculations about the nature of space, time, and infinity with notable examples including the Nasadiya Sukta and the Kena Upanishad.
- Indian mathematicians and astronomers made some of the earliest studies in trigonometry (particularly spherical trigonometry to calculate distances and positions of planets and stars), arithmetic and algebra, and the calculation of the motions and positions of planets and stars. Notable examples include Aryabhata, Brahmagupta, and Bhaskara II.
