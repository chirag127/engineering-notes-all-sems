### Council of Ministers in Ancient India

The king in ancient Indian political system was assisted by a council of ministers in administering the state. The council of ministers was known by different names such as Mantri Parishad, Pradhana Parishad, etc.

The size of the council of ministers varied from state to state. The prominent ministers were:

- Purohita or Chief Priest - He was the chief religious advisor to the king. He performed religious ceremonies and sacrifices.
- Senapati or Commander-in-Chief - He was the head of the army and was responsible for the military administration.
- Mahamatya or Chief Minister - He was the prime minister and the chief of the council of ministers. He advised the king on various state matters and supervised other ministers.
- Samahartr or Chancellor of Exchequer - He was in-charge of the revenue administration. He supervised the collection of taxes and disbursal of funds.
- Duta or Foreign Minister - He looked after foreign relations, communication with other states, and conduct of envoys.

Some Mnemonics to remember the ministers:

- Purohita - P for Priest
- Senapati - S for Soldier (army head)
- Mahamatya - M for Minister (chief minister)
- Samahartr - S for Revenue (in-charge of revenue)
- Duta - D for Diplomacy (foreign minister)

The council assisted the king in arriving at key decisions and implementing them. Though the king had the final say in all matters, he depended on the advice and assistance of the council of ministers in administering the state.
