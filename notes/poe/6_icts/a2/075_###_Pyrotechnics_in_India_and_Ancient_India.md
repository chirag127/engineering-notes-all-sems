### Pyrotechnics in India and Ancient India

- Pyrotechnics refers to the use of fire and burning materials for entertainment or festivals. India has a long history of using pyrotechnics dating back to the ancient times.
- Some important points about pyrotechnics in ancient India:

1. The use of firecrackers and sparklers was common in ancient Indian festivals and celebrations. Materials like charcoal, sulfur and potassium nitrate were used to produce sparks and explosions.
2. The manufacture and use of fireworks was well developed. Rockets of different sizes were made using bamboo and metal cylinders along with fuel mixtures. These were used in warfare as well as celebrations.
3. The knowledge of gunpowder and its use in fireworks and rockets originated in ancient India. The first references to the fireworks and rockets are found in the texts like Arthashastra and the Ramayana and Mahabharata.
4. The famous Iron pillar near Qutub Minar in Delhi was built using a metallurgical process that involved creating a phosphate coating by a special recipe of chemicals in absence of oxygen at high temperatures. This is a feat of amazing ancient pyrotechnology and metallurgy.

- Think of festivals like Diwali with firecrackers going off as a memory aid for use of pyrotechnics in ancient Indian festivals.
- Remember the "Ravan" rockets from Ramayana as an example of ancient Indian rockets.
- The rust-free Iron Pillar is a striking example of advanced ancient Indian pyrotechnology.
