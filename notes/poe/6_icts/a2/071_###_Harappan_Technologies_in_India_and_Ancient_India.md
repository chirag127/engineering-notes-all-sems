### Harappan Technologies in India and Ancient India

The Harappan civilization flourished around 3300 BCE to 1300 BCE in the Indus Valley region. They were pioneers in establishing planned urban settlements and also developed various technologies in the fields of engineering, hydraulics, and metallurgy. Some key Harappan technologies are:

- Brick-making: The Harappans made kiln-fired bricks of a standard size. Bricks were used for constructing buildings, drains, wells, and other structures.
- Measures and weights: The Harappans developed a uniform system of weights based on the decimal system. Cubical stone weights and scales have been found in excavations.
- Metallurgy: The Harappans smelted and worked copper, bronze, lead, and tin. Tools, weapons, and other artifacts of copper and bronze have been found.
- Crafts and artifacts: The Harappans produced various crafts and artifacts displaying a high level of skill such as pottery, jewelry, seals, toys, sculptures, etc.
- Sanitation and hydraulic engineering: The Harappans had a well-developed drainage system and latrines in their cities. They also constructed reservoirs and wells.
- Writing: The Harappans developed a script that has not yet been fully deciphered. Around 400 distinct signs have been found on seals and other artifacts.

Some mnemonics for remembering Harappan technologies:

- Bricks: Big Red Cubes Made By Harappans
- Metals: Melted Copper Makes Bronze Artifacts
- Script: 400 Mystery Signs Sealed In Clay

The ancient Indians after the Harappan civilization also made significant technological advancements, especially in the fields of metallurgy and mathematics. The invention of zero and the decimal number system made complex calculations easier. Iron and steel were produced using furnaces. Various medical procedures and medicinal formulations were also developed. Temples, palaces, and other grand structures were constructed. Advances were made in the production of textiles and various crafts. Overall, ancient India had a flourishing tradition of technological innovation.
