### The Seven Limbs of the State in Ancient India

The ancient Indian political theory conceived of the state as a living organism with seven constituent parts or 'limbs' (saptanga) -

1. The King or the Monarch (Swamin) - The head of the state. The fountainhead of the executive, legislative and judicial powers.

2. Ministers or Council of Ministers (Amatya) - Assist the king in administration, forming the cabinet.

3. Territory (Janapada) - The area or region under the control of the state.

4. Fortified Capital (Durga) - The fortified seat of the government.

5. Treasury (Kosa) - The repository of the funds of the state collected as taxes.

6. Army (Danda) - The military force that protects the state from external aggression and internal threats. Upholds law and order.

7. Allies (Mitra) - The neighboring states with which the state has relationships such as friendships or military alliances.

* Mnemonics: 'S'wamin, 'A'matya, 'J'anapada, 'D'urga, 'K'osa, 'D'anda, 'M'itra
* The state is conceived as an organic whole with interdependent parts. The proper functioning of each 'limb' is necessary for the well-being of the entire 'body' i.e. the state.
* The principles of this theory influenced and found echoes in the Arthashastra - the treatise on statecraft, economics and politics composed by Kautilya, the prime minister of the Mauryan emperor Chandragupta Maurya.
