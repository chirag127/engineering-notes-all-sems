#### Marriage in Society in Ancient India

- Marriage was considered a sacred bond between two individuals in Ancient Indian society. It was one of the most important institutions and was necessary for progeny and continuation of family lineage.
- There were 8 forms of marriage recognized - Brahma, Daiva, Arsha, Prajapatya, Asura, Gandharva, Rakshasa, Paishacha. The first 4 were considered proper while the last 4 were considered improper.
- Child marriage was prevalent, especially among the nobility. The Brahmins married their children early. Kshatriyas married their children in youth. Vaishyas married their children after education. Shudras married their children after learning skills.
- Monogamy was most common but polygamy was also prevalent among kings and nobles. Polyandry was accepted in certain communities like the Nairs of Kerala where the eldest daughter inherited the property and married all brothers in the family.
- Inter-caste and inter-religious marriages were forbidden. Hypergamy was preferred where women married men of higher castes.
- Marriage was seen as a religious duty and not merely a relationship between two individuals. It was essentially to continue the family line and have sons to perform funeral rites. The newly married couple had to continue living with parents/in-laws and follow the household norms.
- There were elaborate rituals for marriage like engagement, arrival of the bride, kanyadan, panigrahana, saptpadi, etc. Dowry was an essential part of marriage and reflected the social status of the bride's family.
- Regional variations existed in marriage customs, for example, in South India tying Thali or Mangalsutra was important while in North India circling the fire and taking seven steps was significant.
