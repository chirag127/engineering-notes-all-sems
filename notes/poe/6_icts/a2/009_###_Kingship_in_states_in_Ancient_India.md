### Kingship in states in Ancient India

- In Ancient India, most states were monarchies with kings as the supreme rulers. The kingship was hereditary and passed on from father to son.
- The king was very powerful and considered as a representative of gods on earth. He had military, administrative and judicial powers.
- The king's main duties were:
-- Protecting the state from external attacks and internal disorder.
-- Dispensing justice and upholding dharma.
-- Encouraging trade and commerce and collecting taxes.
-- Performing various religious sacrifices and rituals.
- The concept of divine right of kings was prevalent. The king's authority was justified on religious and moral grounds. Challenging the king was considered a sin.
- The two most prominent theories of state in Ancient India were:
-- The rājadharma theory which emphasized the king's dharma or duties. The king had to rule justly and protect his subjects.
-- The daṇḍanīti theory which emphasized the use of punishment and force. The king had to be powerful and use force to maintain law and order.
- Some examples of powerful ancient Indian kingdoms are Mauryas, Guptas, Cholas, Chalukyas, etc. They had an efficient administration with a hierarchical structure and officials to aid the king.
