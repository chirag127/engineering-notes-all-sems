### Six System Indian Philosophy

The six systems or schools of Indian philosophy are:

1. Samkhya: Dualism; matter and spirit are two distinct principles. It propounded the theory of gunas - sattva, rajas and tamas. Liberation results from knowledge of the 25 tattvas or principles.

2. Yoga: Path of meditation and physical/mental discipline aimed at attaining liberation. It accepts the Samkhya metaphysics but emphasizes the practice of yoga as the way to achieve liberation. The eight limbs of yoga of Patanjali are: yama, niyama, asana, pranayama, pratyahara, dharana, dhyana and samadhi.

3. Nyaya: Logic and epistemology; emphasizes proper logic and the scientific method. It investigated the sources of knowledge, logical reasoning, analysis and critique. The Nyaya Sutras by Gautama lays down the methodology for philosophical debate.

4. Vaisheshika: Analysis of the nature of reality through atoms and their combinations. It propounded that all objects in the physical universe are reducible to a finite number of atoms and can be understood through rational analysis. According to it, there are nine substances - earth, water, fire, air, ether, time, space, self (atman) and mind.

5. Purva Mimamsa: Focus on the earlier portions of the Vedas, especially the Brahmanas and Aranyakas. It aimed to develop a philosophy and interpret the ritual injunctions in the Vedas through systematic exegesis. Liberation results from correct performance of Vedic rituals.

6. Vedanta: Considered the culmination of Indian philosophy. There are sub-schools - Advaita, Vishishtadvaita and Dvaita. It aims to comprehend the meaning of the Upanishads and interpret them in a coherent, logical philosophic system. Liberation is achieved by realizing the identity of the individual self and the ultimate reality - Brahman.

Advantages: Provides a holistic framework to understand existence, reality and path to liberation.
Disadvantages: Some systems are intricately tied to the Vedas and rituals which limits their applicability in the modern context.
Examples: Samkhya - Purusha and Prakriti; Yoga - eight limbs; Nyaya - logical reasoning; Vaisheshika - atoms and substances; Purva Mimamsa - Vedic exegesis; Vedanta - Brahma, Atman, Brahman.
Applications: Influence on Indian philosophy, culture, principles of yoga and ethics. Survival of some practices like yoga and meditation in the modern world.
