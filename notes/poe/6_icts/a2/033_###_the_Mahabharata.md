### The Mahabharata

The Mahabharata is one of the two major Sanskrit epics of ancient India, the other being the Ramayana. It consists of over 100,000 shlokas or over 200,000 individual verse lines (each shloka is a couplet), and is the longest epic poem in the world.

- The story revolves around the Kurukshetra War and the fates of the Kaurava and Pandava princes. It highlights the battle between dharma and adharma.
- The Pandavas are the five sons of Pandu - Yudhishthir, Bhim, Arjun, Nakul and Sahadev. The Kauravas are the 100 sons of Dhritarashtra.
- After the Pandavas' cousin Duryodhana refuses to give them their share of the kingdom, war breaks out between the Pandavas and Kauravas. Lord Krishna supports the Pandavas, who are ultimately victorious.
- Some important characters are Krishna, the wise advisor and close friend of the Pandavas; Arjuna and Karna, great warriors; Draupadi, wife of the Pandavas; Duryodhana, chief of the Kauravas; and Bhishma and Shalya, elders and chieftains.
- The Mahabharata contains several stories and teachings, like the Bhagavad Gita which consists of Krishna's advice to Arjuna on the battlefield. It highlights duties, virtues, purpose of life and morality.
- Mnemonics: "Paanch Pandav, Saat Kaurav, Beech Mein Hai Krishna Parmatma" (Five Pandavas, hundred Kauravas, in between is Krishna the Supreme Soul)
- Easy to remember as it rhymes and sums up the key characters and groups in few words.
