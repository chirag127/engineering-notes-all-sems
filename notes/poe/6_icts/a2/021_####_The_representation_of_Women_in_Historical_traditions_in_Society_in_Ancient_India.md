#### The representation of Women in Historical traditions in Society in Ancient India

- In ancient Indian society, women were considered as inferior to men. They had a subordinate status.
- According to religious scriptures like Manusmriti, women were expected to obey their fathers, husbands and sons. Their freedom was restricted.
- However, women did have property rights and could inherit property. They also had the right to education and some women were educated in arts, science and religion.
- The concept of pativrata or wifely devotion required women to dedicate themselves to their husbands and family. Their identities were tied to their families and husbands.
- In epics like Ramayana and Mahabharata, women like Sita and Draupadi are portrayed as dutiful wives who epitomize virtues like chastity and sacrifice. However, there are also portrayals of strong women like Kunti who wielded power and influence.
- The practice of sati where women immolated themselves on their husband's pyre was followed in some communities though it was not universal. Sati symbolized the idea of a woman's life being dependent on her husband.
- The devadasi system where women were dedicated to temples as servants of the deity involved a loss of autonomy and agency for women. Though they had a higher status, their lives were decided by the temple and they often had to rely on sex work for a livelihood.
- In conclusion, while ancient Indian society accorded women some rights, their overall status was one of subordination to men. Norms of female chastity, sacrifice and duty bound them to patriarchal control. However, there were also counter-examples of women who demonstrated strength and power.
