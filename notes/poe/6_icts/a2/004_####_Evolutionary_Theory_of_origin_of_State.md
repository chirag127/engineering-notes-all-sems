#### Evolutionary Theory of origin of State

According to the Evolutionary theory, the State originated from the primitive tribal society. The theory states that the State evolved gradually due to the increasing complexity of society. The key steps in the evolution are:

1. Primitive Tribal Society: In the primitive tribal society, people lived in small kinship groups. There was no formal political organization or government. The tribal chief had very limited authority.

2. Chiefdom: As the population increased and life became more complex, tribal chieftainship emerged. The tribal chief gained more power and authority. He had greater control over the tribe. However, his power was still limited.

3. Early States: As agriculture developed and property came into existence, territorial conquests occurred. This led to the emergence of early states with definite boundaries and more complex administrative systems headed by monarchs. The monarchs had greater power than tribal chiefs. Taxes were collected and armies were maintained.

4. Development of Modern States: The early states gradually evolved into modern states with even more complex institutions and bureaucracies. The power of the monarchs became limited by parliaments and constitutions. Democratic values and institutions emerged.

The key advantages of this theory are that it sees the origin of the state as a natural consequence of social evolution and increasing complexity. However, the theory is too simplistic and does not account for the role of force and violence in the origin of states. It also does not explain the origin of tribal societies themselves.

The key mnemonics to remember the steps are:

1. Tribe: Kin groups, no govt
2. Chief: Gaining power
3. State: Conquests, monarchs, armies
4. Modern: Complex, democratic

The theory can be represented pictorially showing the steps in a flow diagram. The examples of early states can be ancient Egypt, China, Mesopotamia, etc. The modern states can be examples of democracies like USA, India, etc.
