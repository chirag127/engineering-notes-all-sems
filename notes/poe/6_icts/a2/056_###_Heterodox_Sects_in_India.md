### Heterodox Sects in India

The heterodox sects or the Nastika schools of Indian philosophy rejected the authority of the Vedas and the caste system. The major heterodox sects that arose in India were:

1. Charvaka or Lokayata: It was founded by Charvaka in the 6th century BCE. It rejected the idea of an afterworld, and held that the only goal of humans was happiness in this life. Hence, one should seek pleasure and avoid pain. It denied the authority of the Vedas and rejected the concepts of atman and moksha.

2. Jainism: It was founded by Mahavira in the 6th century BCE. Jains believe that liberation can be achieved by one's own efforts through following the teachings of the Jina. The core beliefs of Jainism are ahimsa (non-violence), anekantavada (multiplicity of viewpoints), and karma. Jains do not accept the authority of the Vedas or the caste system.

3. Buddhism: It was founded by Gautama Buddha in the 6th century BCE. Buddhists believe that liberation from suffering can be attained by following the Noble Eightfold Path. The core beliefs of Buddhism are the Four Noble Truths, anatta (non-self), and the idea that nirvana can be attained through one's own efforts. Buddhism rejected the Vedas and the caste system.

The Mnemonics and learning tricks to remember the heterodox sects in India along with their founders are:

C - Charvaka, founded by Charvaka

J - Jainism, founded by Mahavira

B - Buddhism, founded by Gautama Buddha

The advantages of the heterodox sects were that they rejected blind faith and dogma, and emphasized liberation through one's own efforts. The disadvantages were that they rejected the authority of the Vedas and the caste system which were deeply entrenched in Indian society. The heterodox sects had a profound influence on Indian philosophy, religion, art, and culture.
