### Chemistry in India and Ancient India

- Ancient Indian scholars made remarkable contributions to chemistry and alchemy. The earlier sources of information on Indian alchemy are the ancient texts of the Vedas which contain clues to chemical operations of smelting, alloying, and purification.
- The Sushruta Samhita and Charaka Samhita (4th to 6th century CE) mention chemical processes like sublimation, distillation, calcination, and crystallization. The Jain text Rasa-Jala-Nidhi (12th century CE) by Acharya Nemichandra Siddhasena Mahaprajna deals with systematically classifying the metals and minerals, and dividing the ancient Indian chemistry in three main types - Jhar-Karan (incineration), Samhara-Karan (precipitation), and Marana-Karan (oxidation).
- Rasavata (practitioner of Rasa Shastra) developed many new methods of chemical processes and extraction of metals and optimized ancient methods. Munjistup, a mercury compound, was used to treat skin diseases. Parad, or mercury compounds, were used to rejuvenate the body. The eight metals (astha dhatu) including gold, silver, copper, zinc, mercury, arsenic, lead, and iron and their various combinations (dhatu pariksha) were studied in detail. Different acids and bases were prepared and used for the purification and fixation of metals.
- The concepts of atoms (paramanu) and atomic structure were described in several ancient Indian philosophical systems including Vaiseshika, Nyaya, and Vedanta. Ancient scholars theorized that matter is composed of atoms and paramanus (sub-atoms) which could not be destroyed and that the properties of substances are determined by the arrangement of paramanus. They speculated that atoms combine or dissociate under conditions of heat and established concepts on the polarity of atoms and substances.
