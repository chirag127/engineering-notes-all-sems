## Module 1- Society State and Polity in India

### Indian Society

- India is a diverse country with many ethnic groups, languages, religions, and cultures.
- The caste system is a major form of social stratification in India with hierarchical divisions based on traditional occupation and social class. The 4 broad categories are Brahmins, Kshatriyas, Vaishyas, and Sudras. Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes are officially designated groups.
- Joint family system was prevalent where extended families lived together. However, the nuclear family system is increasing with urbanization and modernization.
- Marriage is an important social institution in India. Arranged marriages are common where families are involved in selecting the spouses. However, the concept of love marriages is gaining acceptance, especially in urban areas.
- The Patriarchal system is prevalent where men are considered the head of the family and inherit property. However, women's rights and equality are improving with laws preventing discrimination and violence against women.
- India is a secular country with people following various religions like Hinduism, Islam, Christianity, Sikhism, Buddhism, Jainism, etc. The Constitution guarantees freedom of religion while prohibiting discrimination on religious grounds.

### Indian State

- India is a federal republic with an elected parliamentary system of government. The President is the head of state while the Prime Minister is the head of government and leader of the majority party in Parliament.
- The Constitution defines the structure, functions, and powers of the legislative, executive, and judicial branches of the government. The Parliament, Council of Ministers, and Supreme Court are the main institutions that check and balance each other's power.
- The Constitution guarantees Fundamental Rights to all citizens including equality, freedom, and right against exploitation. It also contains Directive Principles of State Policy which guide the government in enacting laws and policies to achieve social, economic, and political objectives.
- Local self-governments operates at the city and village level enabling citizens to participate in decision making regarding local affairs. Panchayati Raj institutions were established to decentralize power and strengthen grassroots democracy in rural areas.
