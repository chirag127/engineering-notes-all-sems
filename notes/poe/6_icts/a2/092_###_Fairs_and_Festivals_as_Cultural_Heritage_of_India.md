### Fairs and Festivals as Cultural Heritage of India

- India is a land of fairs and festivals. Fairs and festivals are an integral part of India's cultural heritage. They reflect the country's rich cultural diversity and traditions.
- Some of the popular fairs and festivals of India are:
- Kumbh Mela: It is one of the largest religious gatherings in the world that is held every 12 years at four holy locations in India - Prayagraj, Haridwar, Ujjain and Nashik. Devotees gather to take a holy dip in the rivers at these locations.
- Pushkar Fair: It is one of the largest camel fairs held in Pushkar, Rajasthan. Apart from camel trading, the fair features cattle trading, horse dealing, and cultural programs.
- Onam: It is a harvest festival celebrated in Kerala. It celebrates the homecoming of the mythological King Mahabali. The festival is marked by boat races, tiger dances, colorful floral decorations, traditional cuisine, etc.
- Diwali: It is one of the biggest Hindu festivals that celebrates the victory of light over darkness. People light up their homes and streets with diyas and candles and exchange gifts and sweets.
- Holi: It is a popular spring festival celebrated in India. People play with colored water and powder and greet each other. It signifies the victory of good over evil.

Some learning tricks to remember:

- Kumbh Mela: Think of Kumbh as a large holy pot and Mela as a fair. Hence, Kumbh Mela refers to a large holy fair.
- Pushkar Fair: Think of Pushkar as a place in Rajasthan and Fair as a camel fair. Hence, Pushkar Fair refers to the camel fair held in Pushkar, Rajasthan.
- Onam: Think of Ona as a harvest and festival. Hence, Onam refers to the harvest festival celebrated in Kerala.
