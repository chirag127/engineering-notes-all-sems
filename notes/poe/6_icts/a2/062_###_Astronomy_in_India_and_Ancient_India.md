### Astronomy in India and Ancient India

- Ancient Indian astronomy flourished from around the 4th century BCE. Astronomers of the classical Gupta period proposed complex motion models to explain the motions of the planets and Moon. They made important advances in astronomical theory, and were among the first to use mathematics to calculate the motions of astronomical objects.
- Key discoveries and accomplishments of Indian astronomy include:
-- Concept of the ecliptic, equinoxes and solstices.
-- Calculations of the lunar and solar calendars and prediction of solar and lunar eclipses.
-- Calculations of the planetary periods, apogee, perigee and planetary transits.
-- Concept of the celestial sphere and positional astronomy.
-- Creation of various astronomical instruments like the gnomon, astrolabe, armillary sphere, etc.
- Some important treatises on Indian astronomy include:
-- Vedanga Jyotisha (~500 BCE): One of the earliest texts containing astronomical knowledge including rules for tracking the motions of the Sun and Moon.
-- Aryabhata's Aryabhatiya (499 CE): Influential work containing mathematical and astronomical theories; calculated the length of the solar year and lunar month, and mentioned that the Moon and planets shine by reflected sunlight.
-- Brahmagupta's Brahmasphutasiddhanta (628 CE): Important treatise which rejected the idea of a flat Earth and stated that the Earth was spherical. Correctly described gravity and the causes of lunar and solar eclipses.
- Key astronomers from this period include:
-- Aryabhata (476-550 CE): Calculated the length of the year and lunar month; proposed a heliocentric model where the Earth rotates on its axis.
-- Brahmagupta (598-668 CE): Proposed laws of gravitation; correctly described the causes of eclipses and the motions of the planets.
-- Bhaskara II (1114-1185 CE): Continued the tradition of important theoretical advances; described the principles of calculus and existence of gravity.
