#### Purusārtha in Society in Ancient India

The ancient Indian society emphasized on achieving four purusārthas or goals of life:

1. Dharma - Performance of moral duties and religious rituals. Following the path of righteousness.
Mnemonics: Dharma means to hold together. It holds the society together.

2. Artha - Pursuit of wealth and power for the satisfaction of desires. Economic development and political ascendancy.
Mnemonics: Artha involves earning and acquiring wealth.

3. Kāma - Fulfillment of desires and pleasures. Gratification of senses.
Mnemonics: Kāma is related to passion and lust.

4. Mokṣa - liberation from the cycle of birth and death. Achieving salvation through spiritual knowledge and practice of austerities.
Mnemonics: Mokṣa means freeing oneself from worldly attachments and achieving freedom.

According to ancient Indian philosophy, for the harmonious development of an individual and the society, the four purusārthas must be pursued simultaneously in a balanced way. No one purusārtha is superior to the other. Excessive emphasis on artha or kāma led to materialism while only focusing on dharma or mokṣa led to neglect of worldly responsibilities. A balanced pursuit of all the four purusārthas was ideal for an ethical and cultured living.
