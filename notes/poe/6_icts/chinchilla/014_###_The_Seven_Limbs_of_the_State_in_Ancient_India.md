### The Seven Limbs of the State in Ancient India

In Ancient India, the concept of state was developed with the idea of maintaining peace, security, and welfare of its citizens. The administrative structure of the state was divided into seven limbs or components, each having its own role and function. These seven limbs of the state are as follows:

1. Swami: The King or the ruler of the state. He was responsible for the overall governance of the state and was the highest authority in the administrative structure.

2. Amatya: The Prime Minister or the Chief Advisor to the King. He was responsible for the day-to-day administration of the state and was the second highest authority in the administrative structure.

3. Janapada: The territory or the geographical area of the state. It was divided into smaller units known as Vishayas or districts, which were governed by Vishayapatis or district governors.

4. Durga: The Fortress or the military strength of the state. It was responsible for the defense of the state and maintaining law and order.

5. Kosha: The Treasury or the finance department of the state. It was responsible for managing the state's finances, collecting taxes, and maintaining accounts.

6. Sabha: The Council or the legislative body of the state. It was responsible for making laws and regulations for the welfare of the state and its citizens.

7. Mitra: The Ally or the diplomatic relations of the state. It was responsible for maintaining friendly relations with neighboring states and foreign powers.

These seven limbs of the state were interdependent and worked together to maintain the stability and welfare of the state. The King or the ruler was the head of the state, but he could not function without the support and cooperation of the other limbs.
