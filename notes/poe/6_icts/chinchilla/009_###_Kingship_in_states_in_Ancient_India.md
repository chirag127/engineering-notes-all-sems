### Kingship in states in Ancient India

In Ancient India, kingship was an essential element of the political and social structure. The king was considered to be the head of the state and the protector of its people. The system of kingship varied across different regions of Ancient India, but there were some common features that defined the institution of kingship. 

Here are the key points to understand the kingship in states in Ancient India:

1. **The King's Role**: The king was the primary authority in the state and was responsible for maintaining law and order, protecting his subjects, and ensuring the welfare of the people. He was also responsible for promoting trade, agriculture, and other economic activities in his kingdom. 

2. **The King's Power**: The king had both administrative and military powers. He was the final authority in all matters of governance, and his decisions were binding on all his subjects. The king's power was absolute, and he had the right to impose taxes, levy fines, and punish lawbreakers. 

3. **The King's Council**: The king was advised by a council of ministers, who were responsible for assisting him in matters of governance. The council was usually composed of learned scholars, experienced administrators, and military commanders. 

4. **The King's Succession**: The succession to the throne was usually hereditary, and the eldest son of the king was the heir to the throne. However, in some cases, the king could choose his successor from among his family members or close associates. 

5. **The King's Titles**: The king was addressed by various titles such as Maharaja, Raja, Samrat, and Chakravartin, depending on his rank and power.
