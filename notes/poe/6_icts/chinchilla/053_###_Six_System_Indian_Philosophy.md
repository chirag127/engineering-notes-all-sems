### Six System Indian Philosophy

Indian Philosophy is a vast and complex system of thought that has evolved over thousands of years. It encompasses a wide range of philosophical ideas and concepts that have been developed by different schools of thought. The Six System Indian Philosophy refers to the six main schools of Indian thought that have been traditionally recognized as the most important and influential.

1. **Samkhya Philosophy** - The Samkhya school is one of the oldest and most influential schools of Indian philosophy. It is based on the dualistic view of the world, which means that it sees the world as consisting of two fundamental principles: Purusha (consciousness) and Prakriti (matter). Samkhya philosophy is concerned with the liberation of the individual soul from the bondage of matter.

2. **Yoga Philosophy** - The Yoga school is based on the idea of attaining liberation through the practice of yoga. It involves the practice of physical postures, breathing exercises, meditation, and moral and ethical conduct.

3. **Nyaya Philosophy** - The Nyaya school is concerned with logic and reasoning. It seeks to establish the principles of valid reasoning and methods of knowledge acquisition. It is based on the idea that knowledge can be gained through direct perception, inference, comparison, and testimony.

4. **Vaisheshika Philosophy** - The Vaisheshika school is concerned with the nature of reality and the classification of objects. It seeks to establish the principles of ontology and epistemology. It classifies objects into six categories: substance, quality, motion, universality, particularity, and inherence.

5. **Mimamsa Philosophy** - The Mimamsa school is concerned with the interpretation of the Vedas, which are the oldest sacred texts of Hinduism. It seeks to establish the principles of interpretation and exegesis. It is based on the idea that the Vedas are eternal and authoritative, and that their correct interpretation is essential for the attainment of liberation.

6. **Vedanta Philosophy** - The Vedanta school is concerned with the nature of consciousness and the ultimate reality. It is based on the idea that the ultimate reality is Brahman, which is the source of all existence. It seeks to establish the principles of ontology, epistemology, and ethics.

#### Mnemonics and Learning Tricks

There are several mnemonics and learning tricks that can be helpful in remembering the Six System Indian Philosophy:

- **S-Y-N-V-M-V** - This mnemonic can be used to remember the first letter of each of the six schools of Indian philosophy: Samkhya, Yoga, Nyaya, Vaisheshika, Mimamsa, and Vedanta.

- **S-Y-N-V-M-V** - This mnemonic can also be used to remember the order of the six schools of Indian philosophy.

- **P-P-V-M-M-V** - This mnemonic can be used to remember the six categories of objects in the Vaisheshika school of philosophy: substance, quality, motion, universality, particularity, and inherence.

- **P-U-N-C-H** - This mnemonic can be used to remember the five types of knowledge acquisition in the Nyaya school of philosophy: perception, inference, comparison, testimony, and non-perception.

- **B-R-H-M-N** - This mnemonic can be used to remember the ultimate reality in the Vedanta school of philosophy: Brahman.

While these mnemonics and learning tricks may be helpful in remembering the Six System Indian Philosophy, it is important to also understand the concepts and ideas behind each school of thought in order to fully grasp the complexity and depth of Indian philosophy.
