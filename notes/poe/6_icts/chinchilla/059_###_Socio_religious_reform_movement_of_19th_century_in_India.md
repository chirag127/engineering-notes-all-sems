### Socio Religious Reform Movement of 19th Century in India

The 19th century in India witnessed a wave of socio-religious reforms that aimed to bring about change in the traditional social and religious practices prevalent in Indian society. The movement aimed to promote rationality, modernity, equality, and social justice. Here are some of the key features of the Socio-Religious Reform Movement of 19th Century in India:

1. The movement was sparked by the influence of Western ideas and the impact of colonialism. The British introduced modern ideas of secularism, democracy, and human rights, which transformed the way Indians thought about their society.

2. The movement aimed to reform the traditional social and religious practices that had been prevalent in Indian society for centuries. These practices included caste system, child marriage, sati, illiteracy, superstitions, and discrimination against women.

3. The movement was led by a group of enlightened leaders who were educated, progressive, and forward-thinking. They included Raja Ram Mohan Roy, Swami Vivekananda, Dayanand Saraswati, Ishwar Chandra Vidyasagar, and many others.

4. Raja Ram Mohan Roy was one of the key figures of the movement. He founded the Brahmo Samaj, which aimed to promote monotheism, rationality, and social reform. He also played a key role in the abolition of sati and the promotion of women's education.

5. Swami Vivekananda was another prominent leader of the movement. He founded the Ramakrishna Mission, which aimed to spread the message of Vedanta and promote social welfare. He also played a key role in the promotion of Hinduism and its philosophy to the West.

6. Dayanand Saraswati founded the Arya Samaj, which aimed to promote Vedic values, social reform, and education. He also played a key role in the promotion of Hindi as the national language of India.

7. Ishwar Chandra Vidyasagar was a key figure in the promotion of women's rights and education. He fought for the abolition of child marriage and the promotion of widow remarriage.

8. The movement also led to the emergence of several women leaders who played a key role in the promotion of women's rights and education. They included Pandita Ramabai, Begum Rokeya, and Sarojini Naidu.

9. The movement brought about significant changes in Indian society. It led to the abolition of several social evils, the promotion of education and women's rights, and the emergence of a modern India.
