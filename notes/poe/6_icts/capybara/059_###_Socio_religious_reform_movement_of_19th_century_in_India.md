### Socio Religious Reform Movement of 19th Century in India

The 19th century in India was marked by a series of socio-religious reform movements aimed at bringing about a change in the society. These movements were led by various reformers who sought to eradicate social evils and promote a more equal and just society. In this article, we will discuss the socio-religious reform movement of the 19th century in India.

#### Background

The 19th century in India was a time of great turmoil and change. The British had established their rule in India, and this had a profound impact on Indian society. The traditional social and religious practices of the Indians were challenged, and this led to a series of social and religious reforms. The socio-religious reform movement of the 19th century in India was a response to these changes.

#### Key Reformers

The socio-religious reform movement of the 19th century in India was led by a number of key reformers. These reformers included Raja Ram Mohan Roy, Swami Vivekananda, Dayananda Saraswati, and many others. These reformers were instrumental in promoting social and religious reform in India.

#### Objectives

The socio-religious reform movement of the 19th century in India had a number of objectives. These objectives included:

- Eradication of social evils such as caste system, untouchability, child marriage, and dowry system
- Promotion of education, especially for women
- Promotion of religious tolerance and harmony
- Modernization of Indian society

#### Key Reforms

The socio-religious reform movement of the 19th century in India led to a number of key reforms. These reforms included:

- Abolition of sati
- Abolition of child marriage
- Abolition of the caste system
- Promotion of women's education
- Promotion of religious tolerance and harmony
