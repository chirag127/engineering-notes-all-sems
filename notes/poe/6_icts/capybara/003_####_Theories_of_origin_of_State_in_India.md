#### Theories of origin of State in India

Theories of origin of state in India are the different approaches and interpretations that have been put forward by scholars and historians to explain the evolution of the state in India. Some of the major theories are as follows:

1. Divine Origin Theory: According to this theory, the state was established by God or the divine power. The ruler was believed to have been divinely appointed and responsible for the welfare of his subjects. This theory was popular in ancient India and was used to justify the rule of monarchs.

2. Social Contract Theory: This theory suggests that the state is a result of a social contract between the ruler and the ruled. The ruler is given power by the people to maintain law and order and protect their rights. This theory was popularized by the European philosopher, Rousseau.

3. Evolutionary Theory: According to this theory, the state evolved gradually from small tribal communities. As these communities grew in size, they needed a centralized authority to maintain law and order. This theory was popularized by the British historian, Henry Maine.

4. Marxist Theory: According to Marxist theory, the state is a product of class struggle. The ruling class uses the state to maintain its power and suppress the working class. This theory was popularized by Karl Marx and Friedrich Engels.

5. Buddhist Theory: According to Buddhist theory, the state is a result of the desire for power and control. The ruler is expected to follow the principles of dharma and work for the welfare of his subjects. This theory was popular in ancient India and influenced the rule of Buddhist kings.

Mnemonic: "DSEBM" - Divine, Social Contract, Evolutionary, Marxist, Buddhist.

These theories provide different perspectives on the origin of state in India and are useful in understanding the evolution of the state in India. However, it should be noted that the actual process of state formation in India was complex and influenced by various factors such as religion, culture, economy, and politics.
