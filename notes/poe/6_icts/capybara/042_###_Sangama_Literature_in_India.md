### Sangama Literature in India

Sangama literature is a collection of Kannada literature composed during the reign of the Sangama dynasty in the Vijayanagara Empire. It is one of the most important literary movements in the history of Kannada literature.

#### Origin and Significance

- The Sangama dynasty ruled over the Vijayanagara Empire from 1336 to 1485 CE.
- During their reign, a significant amount of Kannada literature was produced, which came to be known as Sangama literature.
- The Sangama literature is significant because it marks the beginning of a new era in Kannada literature.
- Before Sangama literature, Kannada literature was dominated by Jain writers, but Sangama literature saw the emergence of new writers who were influenced by Virashaivism.

#### Characteristics

- Sangama literature is characterized by its focus on Virashaivism, which is a form of Shaivism that emphasizes the worship of Lord Shiva.
- The literature is also characterized by its use of simple language and the inclusion of folk elements.
- Sangama literature includes a variety of genres, such as poetry, drama, and prose.

#### Notable Works

- The most famous work of Sangama literature is the "Kavirajamarga" (The Royal Path of Poetry), which was written by the Rashtrakuta king Amoghavarsha I in the 9th century CE. The Kavirajamarga is considered to be the first literary work in Kannada and is a comprehensive guide to Kannada grammar and poetics.
- Other notable works of Sangama literature include the "Sivatatva Ratnakara" by Keladi Chennamma, the "Raghavendra Vijaya" by Narayana Panditacharya, and the "Bhakti Ratnakara" by Narahari Tirtha.
