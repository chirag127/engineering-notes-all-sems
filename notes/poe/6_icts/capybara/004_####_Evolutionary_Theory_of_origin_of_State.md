#### Evolutionary Theory of origin of State

The evolutionary theory of the origin of state is one of the important theories explaining the origin of the state. This theory believes that the state evolved from the family, which further expanded into a clan or tribe, and finally transformed into a state. The following are the key points of the evolutionary theory of origin of the state:

1. Family: The family is considered the basic unit of society. The evolutionary theory believes that the family was the first social unit that emerged in human society. The family provided a sense of security and protection to its members.

2. Clan: As the family grew in numbers, it evolved into a clan. The clan was a larger social unit that consisted of several families. The members of the clan shared a common ancestry and had a sense of belongingness towards each other.

3. Tribe: The clan further evolved into a tribe. The tribe was a larger social unit that consisted of several clans. The members of the tribe shared a common language, culture, and territory. The tribe was a self-sufficient unit that had its own economic, social, and political systems.

4. State: The tribe further evolved into a state. The state was a political entity that had a defined territory, a centralized government, and a system of laws. The state was responsible for maintaining law and order, providing public services, and protecting the rights of its citizens.

Advantages of the Evolutionary Theory of Origin of State:
- This theory helps in understanding the gradual evolution of the state from simple social units.
- It explains the importance of the family, clan, and tribe in the evolution of the state.
- It emphasizes the role of cultural factors in the evolution of the state.

Disadvantages of the Evolutionary Theory of Origin of State:
- This theory does not explain the origin of the state in some societies where the state was not evolved from the family, clan, or tribe.
- It ignores the role of conflict and competition in the evolution of the state.
- It does not explain the emergence of the state in complex societies where there were multiple social units.

In conclusion, the evolutionary theory of the origin of the state provides a significant insight into the gradual evolution of the state from simple social units. The theory emphasizes the importance of family, clan, and tribe in the evolution of the state. However, the theory has some limitations as it does not explain the origin of the state in some societies and ignores the role of conflict and competition in the evolution of the state.
