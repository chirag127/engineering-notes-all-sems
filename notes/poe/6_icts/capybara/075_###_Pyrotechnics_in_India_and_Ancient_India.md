### Pyrotechnics in India and Ancient India

Pyrotechnics refers to the art of creating fireworks and explosives. India has a rich history in pyrotechnics, dating back to ancient times. Here are some important points about pyrotechnics in India and ancient India:

1. The use of fireworks in India can be traced back to the Indus Valley Civilization, which existed around 2500 BCE. Archaeologists have discovered evidence of fireworks in the form of clay pots filled with gunpowder.

2. In ancient India, pyrotechnics were used for religious and cultural events. For example, fireworks were used during Diwali, a Hindu festival of lights, to symbolize the triumph of good over evil.

3. The Mughal Empire, which ruled India from the 16th to the 19th century, was known for its elaborate displays of fireworks. The Mughal emperors would often hold grand celebrations featuring fireworks as a symbol of their wealth and power.

4. One famous example of Mughal pyrotechnics is the Peacock Throne, which was a throne made of gold and studded with precious stones. The throne was said to have been so magnificent that it had a canopy made of peacock feathers that would move in the breeze, and it was also rigged with fireworks that would shoot out from the throne when the emperor sat on it.

5. In modern times, India has become one of the world's largest manufacturers of fireworks. The city of Sivakasi in Tamil Nadu is known as the "fireworks capital of India" and produces over 90% of the country's fireworks.
