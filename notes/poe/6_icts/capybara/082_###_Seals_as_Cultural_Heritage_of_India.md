### Seals as Cultural Heritage of India

Seals have been an important part of India's cultural heritage since ancient times. They were used to mark official documents, authenticate important papers, and signify ownership of property. Here are some important points to learn about seals as cultural heritage of India:

1. **Indus Valley Civilization Seals:** The Indus Valley Civilization, which existed from 3300 BCE to 1300 BCE, is known for its use of seals. The seals were made of steatite and featured intricate designs and symbols. These seals were used to mark goods and containers, as well as to identify the owners of property.

2. **Mohenjo-daro and Harappa Seals:** The cities of Mohenjo-daro and Harappa, which were part of the Indus Valley Civilization, have yielded thousands of seals. These seals provide a glimpse into the ancient civilization's trade and commerce, as well as its religious and social practices. Some of the seals depict animals, while others show human figures engaged in various activities.

3. **Buddhist Seals:** In the 3rd century BCE, Emperor Ashoka of the Maurya Dynasty used seals to propagate Buddhism. These seals featured inscriptions in Pali, the language used by the Buddha, and were used to authenticate important Buddhist texts and documents.

4. **Mughal Seals:** The Mughal Empire, which ruled India from 1526 to 1857, also used seals extensively. The Mughal seals were made of precious metals and featured intricate designs and calligraphy. These seals were used to authenticate imperial decrees, as well as to mark important documents and letters.

5. **Learning Trick:** One mnemonic to remember the importance of seals as cultural heritage of India is "IAS BM," which stands for Indus Valley Civilization, Ashoka, and Mughal.

Seals continue to be an important part of India's cultural heritage, and their intricate designs and symbols provide a fascinating glimpse into the country's ancient past.
