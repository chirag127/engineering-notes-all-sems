### Shankaracharya and Advaita Vedanta

Shankaracharya was an ancient Indian philosopher who lived in the 8th century. He was the founder of the Advaita Vedanta philosophy, which is one of the six orthodox schools of Hindu philosophy.

Advaita Vedanta is a non-dualistic philosophy that emphasizes the oneness of Brahman and Atman. It is based on the Upanishads and the Brahma Sutras.

Here are some key points about Shankaracharya and Advaita Vedanta:

- Shankaracharya was born in Kerala, India in the 8th century. He is believed to have died at the age of 32.
- He wrote commentaries on the Upanishads, the Bhagavad Gita, and the Brahma Sutras. His works are considered to be some of the most important texts in Hindu philosophy.
- The Advaita Vedanta philosophy teaches that Brahman is the only reality and that all other things are illusions. Atman, the individual soul, is identical to Brahman.
- The philosophy teaches that the ultimate goal of life is to realize the oneness of Brahman and Atman. This can be achieved through meditation, self-inquiry, and the study of scripture.
- Advaita Vedanta is often associated with the practice of Jnana Yoga, which is the path of knowledge. It is one of the four main paths of yoga, along with Bhakti Yoga, Karma Yoga, and Raja Yoga.
- Advaita Vedanta has had a significant influence on Indian philosophy, as well as on modern Western philosophy. It has been studied by philosophers such as Arthur Schopenhauer and Aldous Huxley.
- Mnemonic: "Advaita Vedanta" can be remembered by breaking down the word into "adva" and "ita," meaning "not two." This reflects the non-dualistic nature of the philosophy.
- Learning trick: To remember the key teachings of Advaita Vedanta, you can use the acronym "BRAHMA." B stands for Brahman, R stands for realization, A stands for Atman, H stands for Hinduism, M stands for meditation, and A stands for Advaita.

In conclusion, Shankaracharya and Advaita Vedanta are important figures and philosophies in Hinduism and Indian philosophy. Their teachings emphasize the oneness of Brahman and Atman and offer a path to the realization of this oneness through meditation, self-inquiry, and the study of scripture.
