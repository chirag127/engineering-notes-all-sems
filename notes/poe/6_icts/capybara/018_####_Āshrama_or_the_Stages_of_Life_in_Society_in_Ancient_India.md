#### Āshrama or the Stages of Life in Society in Ancient India

In ancient India, the society was divided into four stages of life, also known as Āshrama. Each stage had its own duties and responsibilities, and every person was expected to follow them accordingly. These stages were:

1. Brahmacharya: This stage was considered as the student stage, which began at the age of 8 and lasted till the age of 25. The main aim of this stage was to acquire knowledge from the guru, learn scriptures and discipline oneself. The students were taught to live a simple life, observe celibacy, and practice austerities.

2. Grihastha: This stage was considered as the householder stage, which began at the age of 25 and lasted till the age of 50. The main aim of this stage was to marry, raise a family, and fulfill one's duties towards society. The people in this stage were expected to earn a livelihood, take care of their family, and contribute towards the welfare of society.

3. Vanaprastha: This stage was considered as the stage of retirement, which began at the age of 50 and lasted till the age of 75. The main aim of this stage was to detach oneself from worldly desires and prepare for the spiritual journey. The people in this stage were expected to go to a forest, live a simple life, and practice meditation.

4. Sannyasa: This stage was considered as the stage of renunciation, which began at the age of 75 and lasted till the end of one's life. The main aim of this stage was to attain moksha or liberation from the cycle of birth and death. The people in this stage were expected to renounce all material possessions, live a life of austerity, and focus on spiritual practices.

Mnemonics and Learning Tricks: 

There are various mnemonics and learning tricks that can help in remembering the stages of life in ancient India. One such trick is to remember the acronym BGVS, which stands for Brahmacharya, Grihastha, Vanaprastha, and Sannyasa. Another trick is to remember the phrase "Boys Go Very Slow" where Boys represents Brahmacharya, Go represents Grihastha, Very represents Vanaprastha, and Slow represents Sannyasa. 

However, it is important to note that these tricks may not be helpful for everyone and it is best to understand the concept thoroughly to remember it better for exams.
