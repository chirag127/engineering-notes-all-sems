### Jain Literature in Pali, Prakrit And Sanskrit

Jainism is one of the oldest religions in the world, and its literature is vast and diverse. Jain literature is written in several languages, including Pali, Prakrit, and Sanskrit. Jain literature in Pali, Prakrit, and Sanskrit is a rich source of information about the Jain religion, its teachings, and its history. Here are some important points about Jain literature in Pali, Prakrit, and Sanskrit:

1. Pali Literature:
   - Pali is the language of the Theravada Buddhist canon, but it is also used by Jains.
   - The Pali canon contains some Jain texts, including the Bhagavati Sutra, Jivaka Sutta, and Akaranga Sutra.
   - The Pali literature has influenced Jain literature to a great extent.

2. Prakrit Literature:
   - Prakrit was the common language of India during the time of the Jains.
   - Prakrit literature includes the Jain Agamas, which are the primary scriptures of the Jain religion.
   - The Jain Agamas are divided into 12 Angas, 12 Upangas, and several other texts. They are written in Ardhamagadhi Prakrit, which is a form of Prakrit.

3. Sanskrit Literature:
   - Sanskrit was the language of learning and scholarship in ancient India.
   - The Jain Sanskrit literature includes various texts, such as the Tattvartha Sutra, the Yogashastra, and the Samayasara.
   - The Jain Sanskrit literature is known for its philosophical and metaphysical discussions.

Conclusion:
Jain literature in Pali, Prakrit, and Sanskrit is a rich source of information about the Jain religion and its teachings.
