
### Context Free Grammars

A context-free grammar (CFG) is a formal system used to describe the syntax of a language. It is composed of a set of production rules that define how the symbols of a language can be combined to form valid sentences. A CFG consists of four components:

* **Terminals:** These are the symbols that are used to form words and sentences in the language.
* **Non-terminals:** These are symbols that represent the various parts of a sentence.
* **Start Symbol:** This is the symbol that indicates the beginning of a sentence.
* **Production Rules:** These are the rules that define how the symbols in a language can be combined to form valid sentences.

CFGs are used in compiler design to define the syntax of a programming language. They are also used to generate parsers, which are programs that can analyze the structure of a sentence and determine whether it is valid or not. CFGs are also used in natural language processing to analyze the structure of natural language sentences.

Advantages of CFGs include:

* They are simple to understand and use.
* They can be used to define the syntax of a programming language.
* They can be used to generate parsers.

Disadvantages of CFGs include:

* They are limited in their ability to represent complex grammatical structures.
* They are not able to represent ambiguity.
* They are not able to represent context-sensitive languages.
