The Travelling Salesman Problem (TSP) is a classic problem in computer science and is one of the most studied problems in the field of algorithms. It involves finding the shortest route for a salesman to visit a set of cities, with the constraint that each city must be visited exactly once.

The TSP is an example of a combinatorial optimization problem, which means that it is a problem that can be solved using a combination of algorithms and heuristics. In order to solve the TSP, the most common approach is to use a combination of an exact algorithm and a heuristic algorithm.

The exact algorithm is used to find the shortest route between the cities, while the heuristic algorithm is used to refine the route and find a solution that is close to the optimal solution.

The most common heuristic algorithms used to solve the TSP are the nearest neighbour algorithm and the 2-opt algorithm. The nearest neighbour algorithm starts at a random city and then finds the nearest unvisited city and adds it to the route. This process is repeated until all cities have been visited.

The 2-opt algorithm is a more sophisticated approach to solving the TSP. It works by taking a route and then swapping two cities in the route to see if the total distance of the route is reduced. If the total distance is reduced, the two cities are kept in the new route. If the total distance is not reduced, the two cities are swapped back. This process is repeated until an optimal solution is found.
