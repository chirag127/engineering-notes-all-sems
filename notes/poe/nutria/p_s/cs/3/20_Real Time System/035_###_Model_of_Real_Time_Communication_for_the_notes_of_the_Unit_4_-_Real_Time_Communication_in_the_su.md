
### Model of Real Time Communication 
Real Time Communication (RTC) is a model of communication that enables data to be exchanged between two or more parties in a timely manner. It is used in many applications such as telecommunication, video conferencing, remote control, and other real-time applications.

RTC typically involves the transmission of data from one point to another in a continuous stream, with the data being received in the same order as it was sent. This type of communication is often used for applications where a response is required within a certain amount of time, such as in a video conference or remote control system.

The model of Real Time Communication consists of several components, including the sender, the receiver, the communication channel, and the protocol. The sender is the party that initiates the communication, while the receiver is the party that receives the communication. The communication channel is the medium through which the data is sent, such as a telephone line, the internet, or a radio frequency. The protocol is the set of rules and conventions that govern the communication process.

The model of Real Time Communication also includes the concept of latency, which is the amount of time it takes for a message to be sent and received. This is important in applications where a response is required within a certain amount of time, such as in a video conference or remote control system.

The model of Real Time Communication is used in many applications, such as telecommunication, video conferencing, remote control, and other real-time applications. It is important to understand the components of the model and how latency affects the communication process in order to effectively use Real Time Communication in these applications.
