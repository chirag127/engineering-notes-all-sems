
## Unit 2 - Real Time Scheduling

Real-time scheduling is a type of scheduling algorithm that is used to assign resources to tasks in a timely manner. It is used in many applications, such as operating systems, embedded systems, and robotics.

Real-time scheduling algorithms are designed to ensure that tasks are completed in a timely manner, and are often used to ensure that critical processes are completed on time. The scheduling algorithm must take into account the deadlines of the tasks, the resources available, and the priorities of the tasks.

Real-time scheduling algorithms can be divided into two main categories: static and dynamic.

Static scheduling algorithms are used when the number of tasks, their deadlines, and the resources required are known in advance. In this case, the scheduling algorithm can be used to assign the tasks to the resources.

Dynamic scheduling algorithms are used when the number of tasks, their deadlines, and the resources required are not known in advance. In this case, the scheduling algorithm must be able to adjust the assignments of the tasks to the resources as the tasks arrive.

Advantages of Real-Time Scheduling:

- Guarantees that tasks are completed on time.
- Can be used to ensure that critical processes are completed on time.
- Can be used to assign resources to tasks in a timely manner.

Disadvantages of Real-Time Scheduling:

- Can be difficult to implement.
- Can be computationally intensive.
- Can be difficult to debug.

Examples of Real-Time Scheduling:

- Operating systems
- Embedded systems
- Robotics
- Industrial control systems
- Networked systems
- Mobile systems
- Multimedia systems
- Real-time databases
