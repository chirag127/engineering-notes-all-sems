
### Problem Solving Approach to Typical AI Problems

AI problems can be broken down into two main categories: **search problems** and **inference problems**. Search problems involve finding the optimal solution to a given problem, while inference problems involve making predictions or decisions based on existing data.

#### Search Problems

Search problems involve finding the optimal solution to a given problem. Examples of search problems include pathfinding, constraint satisfaction, and game playing. Pathfinding involves finding the shortest route between two points, while constraint satisfaction involves finding a solution that satisfies a given set of constraints. Game playing involves finding a strategy that maximizes the player's chance of winning.

Search problems can be solved using a variety of techniques, including heuristics, local search, and evolutionary algorithms. Heuristics are rules of thumb that can be used to find a solution quickly. Local search involves iteratively improving a solution until it is optimal, while evolutionary algorithms involve using genetic algorithms or simulated annealing to find a solution.

#### Inference Problems

Inference problems involve making predictions or decisions based on existing data. Examples of inference problems include classification, clustering, and regression. Classification involves predicting the class of an object based on its features, while clustering involves grouping objects based on their similarities. Regression involves predicting a continuous value based on a set of features.

Inference problems can be solved using a variety of techniques, including decision trees, support vector machines, and neural networks. Decision trees involve using a tree-like structure to make decisions, while support vector machines involve maximizing the margin between two classes. Neural networks involve using a network of neurons to make predictions.
