
### Properties of SVM for the Notes of Unit 2 - Regression in the Subject of Machine Learning Techniques

* Support Vector Machines (SVMs) are supervised learning models with associated learning algorithms that analyze data and recognize patterns, used for classification and regression analysis.
* SVMs are based on the concept of a hyperplane, which is a line that divides a set of data into two categories. The hyperplane is determined by the support vectors, which are the data points closest to the hyperplane.
* SVMs are used in a variety of applications, including text and hypertext categorization, image classification, and bioinformatics.
* Advantages of SVMs include:
    * High accuracy: SVMs have a high degree of accuracy in both classification and regression.
    * Robustness: SVMs are robust to outliers and can handle a large number of features.
    * Versatility: SVMs can be used for both linear and non-linear problems.
    * Sparsity: SVMs are memory efficient, as they only use a subset of training points in the decision function.
* Disadvantages of SVMs include:
    * Long training time: SVMs can take a long time to train, especially on large datasets.
    * Limited kernel functions: SVMs are limited to a few kernel functions, such as the linear and radial basis function (RBF).
    * Black box: SVMs are considered a “black box”, as it is difficult to interpret the results.
* Examples of applications of SVMs include image recognition, natural language processing, and gene expression analysis.
