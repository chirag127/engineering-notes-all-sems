### Protocols for the notes of the Unit 1 - Introduction to Blockchain in the subject of Block chain Architecture Design

Blockchain technology has emerged as a revolutionary technology that has the potential to transform various industries. It is a decentralized ledger that can be used to store and manage data in a secure and transparent manner. In order to understand blockchain technology, it is important to have an understanding of the protocols that are used in this technology. In this article, we will discuss the protocols for the notes of the Unit 1 - Introduction to Blockchain in the subject of Block chain Architecture Design.

#### 1. Proof of Work (PoW)
Proof of Work is a protocol used in blockchain technology to validate transactions and create new blocks. It is a process that involves solving complex mathematical problems to validate transactions and create new blocks. This protocol is used in Bitcoin and other cryptocurrencies. The main advantage of this protocol is that it is secure and resistant to attacks.

#### 2. Proof of Stake (PoS)
Proof of Stake is a protocol used in blockchain technology to validate transactions and create new blocks. In this protocol, validators are chosen based on the amount of cryptocurrency they hold. Validators are incentivized to validate transactions and create new blocks by receiving a reward in the form of cryptocurrency. The main advantage of this protocol is that it is energy-efficient compared to Proof of Work.

#### 3. Delegated Proof of Stake (DPoS)
Delegated Proof of Stake is a protocol used in blockchain technology that is similar to Proof of Stake. In this protocol, validators are chosen by the stakeholders through a voting system. Validators are incentivized to validate transactions and create new blocks by receiving a reward in the form of cryptocurrency. The main advantage of this protocol is that it is more efficient than Proof of Work and Proof of Stake.

#### 4. Byzantine Fault Tolerance (BFT)
Byzantine Fault Tolerance is a protocol used in blockchain technology to ensure that the system remains functional even in the presence of malicious actors. This protocol is used in permissioned blockchains where the participants are known and trusted. The main advantage of this protocol is that it is highly fault-tolerant and can tolerate up to a certain percentage of malicious actors.

#### 5. Practical Byzantine Fault Tolerance (PBFT)
Practical Byzantine Fault Tolerance is a protocol used in blockchain technology that is similar to Byzantine Fault Tolerance. In this protocol, a consensus is reached among the participants through a voting system. The main advantage of this protocol is that it is highly fault-tolerant and can tolerate up to a certain percentage of malicious actors.

- One mnemonic for remembering the difference between Proof of Work and Proof of Stake is "PoW is for miners, PoS is for holders". This is because in Proof of Work, miners solve complex mathematical problems to validate transactions and create new blocks, while in Proof of Stake, validators are chosen based on the amount of cryptocurrency they hold.
- Another mnemonic for remembering the difference between Byzantine Fault Tolerance and Practical Byzantine Fault Tolerance is "BFT is theoretical, PBFT is practical". This is because Byzantine Fault Tolerance is a theoretical protocol that is used in permissioned blockchains where the participants are known and trusted, while Practical Byzantine Fault Tolerance is a practical protocol that is used in permissioned blockchains where the participants are known and trusted.
