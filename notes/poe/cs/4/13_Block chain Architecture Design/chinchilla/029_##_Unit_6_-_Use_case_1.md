## Unit 6 - Use case 1

Use case 1 is a technique used in software development to identify and define the functional requirements of a system. It involves creating scenarios that describe how a user interacts with the system to achieve a specific goal or task. Use case 1 is an essential part of the requirements engineering process and helps to ensure that the software meets the needs of its users. Here are some key points to understand about Use case 1:

1. Definition: Use case 1 is a technique used in software development to identify and define the functional requirements of a system.

2. Scenario creation: Use case 1 involves creating scenarios that describe how a user interacts with the system to achieve a specific goal or task.

3. Importance: Use case 1 is an essential part of the requirements engineering process and helps to ensure that the software meets the needs of its users.

4. Use case diagram: A use case diagram is a graphical representation of the actors (users) and use cases (scenarios) involved in a system. It is used to visualize the system's functionality and identify the relationships between the different components.

5. Advantages: Some of the advantages of using Use case 1 include:

    - Helps to ensure that the software meets the needs of its users
    - Provides a clear understanding of the system's functionality
    - Helps to identify potential problems early in the development process
    - Provides a basis for testing and validation

6. Disadvantages: Some potential disadvantages of Use case 1 include:

    - Can be time-consuming and costly to create and maintain
    - Can be difficult to keep up-to-date as system requirements change
    - May not capture all the non-functional requirements of a system

7. Example: An example of a Use case 1 scenario could be a user logging into an online banking system to transfer funds between accounts. The Use case 1 would describe the steps involved in logging in, selecting the accounts to transfer funds between, and completing the transaction.

8. Applications: Use case 1 is widely used in software development and is applicable in various domains, such as finance, healthcare, and e-commerce.

Mnemonics and learning tricks for Use case 1:

- "Use case 1" can be remembered as the first step in understanding how a user interacts with a system to achieve a specific goal or task.
- To remember the advantages of Use case 1, think of the acronym "HAPPY" - Helps to ensure user needs are met, Provides clear understanding, Potential problem identification, Basis for testing and validation, and Yield better software.
- To remember the potential disadvantages of Use case 1, think of the acronym "TACO" - Time-consuming and costly, Difficult to keep up-to-date, May not capture non-functional requirements, and Only captures functional requirements.
