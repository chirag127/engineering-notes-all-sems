## Unit 3 - Permissioned Blockchains

Permissioned Blockchains are a type of blockchain that allows only a select group of individuals or entities to participate in the network. In this unit, we will dive into the details of permissioned blockchains and understand how they work.

### What are Permissioned Blockchains?

Permissioned blockchains, also known as private blockchains, are a type of blockchain that allows only a restricted group of participants to access the network. These participants are usually known and trusted entities, such as companies, organizations, or government bodies. The permissioned blockchain network is managed by a central authority or a group of authorities who control the access and validation of transactions on the network.

### How do Permissioned Blockchains Work?

Permissioned blockchains work differently from public blockchains, where anyone can join the network and participate in transaction validation. In permissioned blockchains, only the authorized participants can join the network, and they are usually required to undergo a vetting process to ensure they meet the network's criteria. Once the participants are approved, they are given access to the network, and they can initiate transactions and participate in the consensus mechanism of the network.

The consensus mechanism used in permissioned blockchains is usually a variant of the Proof-of-Authority (PoA) consensus protocol or the Practical Byzantine Fault Tolerance (PBFT) protocol. These consensus mechanisms rely on a group of trusted validators to validate transactions and add them to the blockchain. The validators are typically chosen based on their reputation, expertise, or stake in the network.

### Advantages of Permissioned Blockchains

Permissioned blockchains offer several advantages over public blockchains, including:

- Increased privacy and security: Permissioned blockchains restrict access to the network, making it more secure and less prone to attacks.

- Better scalability: Permissioned blockchains can handle a higher volume of transactions and have faster confirmation times than public blockchains.

- Controlled governance: Permissioned blockchains are managed by a central authority or a group of authorities, allowing for more control over the network's governance.

### Disadvantages of Permissioned Blockchains

While permissioned blockchains offer several advantages, they also have some disadvantages, including:

- Limited decentralization: Permissioned blockchains are managed by a central authority or a group of authorities, making them less decentralized than public blockchains.

- Lack of transparency: Permissioned blockchains restrict access to the network, making it difficult for external parties to audit the network's activities.

### Mnemonics and Learning Tricks

- Remember that permissioned blockchains are like private clubs that only allow certain individuals to join. This can help you remember that permissioned blockchains restrict access to the network.

- Think of the central authority or group of authorities as a bouncer at a club who decides who gets to enter and who doesn't. This can help you remember that permissioned blockchains are managed by a central authority or a group of authorities.

- Remember that PoA stands for Proof-of-Authority, which can help you remember that the consensus mechanism used in permissioned blockchains relies on a group of trusted validators.

### Examples and Applications

Permissioned blockchains are commonly used in industries where privacy and security are paramount, such as finance, healthcare, and government. Some examples of permissioned blockchains include:

- Hyperledger Fabric: An open-source permissioned blockchain platform designed for enterprise use.

- Corda: A permissioned blockchain platform designed for financial services companies.

- Quorum: A permissioned blockchain platform designed for financial institutions and enterprise use.

In conclusion, permissioned blockchains are a type of blockchain that restricts access to the network to a select group of individuals or entities. They offer increased privacy and security, better scalability, and controlled governance. However, they also have limitations in terms of decentralization and transparency. Understanding permissioned blockchains and their applications is essential for anyone interested in blockchain technology.
