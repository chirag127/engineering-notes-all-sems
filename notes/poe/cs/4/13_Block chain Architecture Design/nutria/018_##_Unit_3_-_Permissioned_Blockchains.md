
## Unit 3 - Permissioned Blockchains

Permissioned blockchains are a type of distributed ledger technology (DLT) that allow access to the network only to those with permission. They are also known as private or consortium blockchains. Unlike public blockchains, such as Bitcoin and Ethereum, permissioned blockchains are not open to the public, and only authorized participants can access and view the data stored on them.

### How Permissioned Blockchains Work

Permissioned blockchains operate in a similar way to public blockchains, but with the added layer of access control. All participants in the network must be authenticated before they can join. This authentication is typically done by a central authority, such as a consortium of banks or a government agency.

Once a participant is authenticated, they can access the data stored on the blockchain and submit new transactions. The transactions are then verified by the network and added to the blockchain.

### Benefits of Permissioned Blockchains

Permissioned blockchains offer several benefits over public blockchains, such as increased security, privacy, and scalability.

- **Security:** Because the network is only accessible to approved participants, the risk of malicious actors is greatly reduced. This makes permissioned blockchains more secure than public blockchains.

- **Privacy:** Permissioned blockchains are more private than public blockchains, as only authorized participants can view the data stored on the blockchain.

- **Scalability:** Permissioned blockchains can be more easily scaled than public blockchains, as the number of participants is limited. This makes them better suited for enterprise applications.

### Examples of Permissioned Blockchains

One of the most well-known examples of a permissioned blockchain is Hyperledger Fabric. Hyperledger Fabric is an open-source blockchain platform developed by the Linux Foundation. It is used for developing enterprise-grade applications, such as supply chain management and financial services.

R3 Corda is another example of a permissioned blockchain. It is a distributed ledger platform designed for financial services. It is used by banks and other financial institutions to facilitate transactions and streamline processes.

### Mnemonics and Learning Tricks

When learning about permissioned blockchains, it can be helpful to remember the acronym "PBS" which stands for "Permissioned Blockchain System". This can help you recall the key characteristics of permissioned blockchains.

Additionally, it can be useful to think of permissioned blockchains as "closed networks" that are only accessible to approved participants. This can help you recall that permissioned blockchains are not open to the public.
