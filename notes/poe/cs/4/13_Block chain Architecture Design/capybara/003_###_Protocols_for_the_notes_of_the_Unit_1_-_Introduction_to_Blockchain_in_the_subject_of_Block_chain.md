### Protocols for the notes of the Unit 1 - Introduction to Blockchain in the subject of Blockchain Architecture Design

Blockchain is a distributed ledger technology that allows secure and transparent transactions without the need for intermediaries. It has become increasingly popular in recent years, with businesses and governments looking to leverage its benefits. In this unit, we will cover the fundamentals of blockchain architecture design, including protocols that enable secure and efficient transactions. Below are the protocols that you should take note of:

1. Proof-of-Work (PoW) Protocol: This protocol is used to validate and process transactions in a blockchain network. It requires miners to solve a complex mathematical puzzle, which involves finding a hash value that matches a predefined target. Once the puzzle is solved, the miner can add a new block to the blockchain and receive a reward in the form of cryptocurrency. PoW is used in the Bitcoin blockchain.

2. Proof-of-Stake (PoS) Protocol: This protocol is an alternative to PoW and is used in some blockchain networks. It requires validators to have a certain amount of cryptocurrency to participate in the validation process. Validators are selected randomly to validate transactions, and they receive a reward in the form of cryptocurrency. PoS is used in the Ethereum blockchain.

3. Delegated Proof-of-Stake (DPoS) Protocol: This protocol is a variant of PoS and is used in some blockchain networks. It involves a smaller group of validators who are elected by the community to validate transactions. The validators are responsible for maintaining the network and receive a reward in the form of cryptocurrency. DPoS is used in the EOS blockchain.

4. Byzantine Fault Tolerance (BFT) Protocol: This protocol is used to ensure that a blockchain network can function even if some nodes are faulty or malicious. It involves a consensus mechanism where all nodes in the network agree on the validity of transactions. BFT is used in some permissioned blockchains.

5. Directed Acyclic Graph (DAG) Protocol: This protocol is a different approach to blockchain architecture design, where transactions are validated in a network of nodes rather than in blocks. It allows for faster and more efficient transactions, as all nodes in the network can validate transactions simultaneously. DAG is used in the IOTA blockchain.

Mnemonics and learning tricks:

- PoW can be remembered as "Proof-of-Work," where the "W" stands for "Work" and the protocol involves miners solving complex puzzles to add new blocks to the blockchain.
- PoS can be remembered as "Proof-of-Stake," where the "S" stands for "Stake" and the protocol requires validators to have a certain amount of cryptocurrency to participate in the validation process.
- DPoS can be remembered as "Delegated Proof-of-Stake," where the "D" stands for "Delegated" and the protocol involves a smaller group of validators elected by the community to validate transactions.
- BFT can be remembered as "Byzantine Fault Tolerance," where the "B" stands for "Byzantine" and the protocol ensures that the network can function even if some nodes are faulty or malicious.
- DAG can be remembered as "Directed Acyclic Graph," where the "DAG" refers to a network of nodes that validate transactions rather than blocks.
