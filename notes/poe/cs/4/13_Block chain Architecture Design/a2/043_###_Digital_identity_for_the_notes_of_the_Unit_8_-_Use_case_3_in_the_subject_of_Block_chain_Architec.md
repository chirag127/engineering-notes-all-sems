### Digital identity

- Digital identity refers to the data associated with an individual that makes them unique in the digital world. It includes information like name, email id, date of birth, etc.
- In blockchain, digital identity can be used to verify individuals and allow access to services. The identity data is stored securely on the blockchain and can be accessed only by the individual.
- This eliminates the need for third parties to store and verify identity, thereby increasing security and privacy. The individual has complete control over their digital identity.
- The identity data on blockchain is immutable and tamper-proof. This prevents identity theft and fraudulent use of someone else's identity.
- However, if the private keys to access the identity are lost or stolen, it can lead to issues. Also, storing identity data on blockchain may not comply with regulations in some cases.

Advantages:
- Secure - Data is encrypted and immutable
- Private - Individual controls identity data
- Tamper-proof - Prevents fraud

Disadvantages:
- Reliance on private keys - Risk of losing access if keys are lost
- Regulatory compliance - May not comply with all regulations

Applications:
- KYC verification
- Access to banking, insurance and other services
- Voting
- Healthcare records

Mnemonics:
- Blockchain identity - Secure, private, tamper-proof
- But keys matter - Lose them and lose access
- Laws apply - Regulations may not allow blockchain ID

Learning tricks:
- Think of digital identity as a secure locker on the blockchain. You have the key (private key) to access your identity data. If you lose the key, you lose access.
- Imagine using your blockchain identity to access bank account. It's secure but regulations may not allow it.
