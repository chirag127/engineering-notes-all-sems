### Consensus protocols for Permissioned Blockchains for the notes of the Unit 3 - Permissioned Blockchains in the subject of Block chain Architecture Design

- Practical Byzantine Fault Tolerance (PBFT) - It is a consensus protocol for state machine replication in asynchronous environments with Byzantine faults. It ensures high throughput and low latency. PBFT uses a group membership service to determine the current set of replicas. It has a leader-based rotating design, where one replica is elected as the primary and sequences requests. The primary then broadcasts requests to other replicas, and they send back responses/pre-prepare messages. Once 2f+1 matching pre-prepare messages are received, a prepare phase is completed and then a commit phase. Advantage is efficient and appropriate for permissioned blockchains. Disadvantage is complex.
- RAFT - It is a consensus algorithm that is designed as an understandable, practical, and robust alternative to Paxos. It uses a leader-based approach to replicate log entries across a cluster of servers/nodes. Leaders are elected through votes from other servers. The leader accepts client requests, assigns log indices to each request, and then replicates the requests to other servers. Once a majority of servers indicate they have replicated the log entries, the leader commits the entries. Advantage is easier to understand than Paxos. Disadvantage is less efficient than Paxos.
- Proof of Authority (PoA) - It is a consensus mechanism for blockchains where transactions and blocks are validated by approved accounts, known as validators. The validators take turns verifying and signing blocks. There is no mining and the validator set can be static or dynamic. The goal is fast and low cost consensus since no mining is involved. The security depends on the validator set, so it must be carefully chosen. Used in permissioned blockchains like Gether and Kovan.
- Practical Byzantine Fault Tolerance with deterministic finality (PBFT-DF) - It extends PBFT and provides deterministic finality, ensuring that all non-faulty replicas agree on the order of requests and execute them in the same order. This provides predictable latency bounds and resilience to asynchronicity. The downside is a loss of some throughput compared to PBFT. Used in Hyperledger Fabric.
