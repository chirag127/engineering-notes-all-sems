## Digital Money to Distributed Ledgers

- Digital money refers to the electronic transfer of funds via methods like credit/debit cards, wire transfers, e-wallets, etc. They are managed by central authorities like banks and payment processors.
- Bitcoin introduced the first digital currency that is decentralized and peer-to-peer. It uses a distributed ledger called blockchain to track ownership and transactions of the digital currency.
- A distributed ledger is a consensus of shared, trusted, public databases that are maintained by a network of participants. There is no central authority or single administrator.
- Blockchain is a type of distributed ledger that maintains a continuously growing list of records called blocks. Each block contains a timestamp and a link to the previous block. By design, blockchains are resistant to modification of the data. So, the data in them is open, verifiable, and permanent.
- Advantages of distributed ledgers: decentralization, immutability, transparency, efficiency, and security.
- Disadvantages: complexity, scalability issues, lack of widespread adoption and understanding.
- Examples of distributed ledger technologies: Bitcoin's blockchain, Ethereum, Hyperledger, Corda, etc.
- Applications: cryptocurrencies, smart contracts, supply chain management, health records, etc.

**Mnemonics:**

- Centralized digital money -> bank accounts
- Decentralized digital money -> Bitcoin and blockchain
- Blockchain -> distributed, open, secure ledger

**Learning tricks:**

- Understand how current digital money systems are centralized and intermediaries are single points of control and failure.
- Learn how blockchain decentralizes trust and distributes consensus across a network.
- Read about Bitcoin and the core structure/components of a blockchain to understand how distributed ledgers work.
- Work through code examples of blockchains to really grasp the key concepts.
