### Consensus for the notes of the Unit 1 - Introduction to Blockchain

Consensus is a crucial concept in blockchain technology. It refers to an agreement on the validity of data and transactions in the blockchain network. Since blockchain is a decentralized system, there is no central authority to validate transactions and data. Instead, consensus is achieved through various algorithms and protocols among the participating nodes in the network. The consensus algorithms ensure that the nodes agree on the order and validity of transactions in the blockchain. The most popular consensus algorithms are:

- Proof-of-Work: Used in Bitcoin and Ethereum networks. Nodes solve complex cryptographic puzzles to validate transactions and earn mining rewards. The first node to solve the puzzle shares the solution with the network and earns the right to add the next block.
- Proof-of-Stake: Validators stake their own coins to validate transactions. The validator with the maximum stake gets the chance to add the next block. Used in Cardano, EOS, etc.
- Delegated Proof-of-Stake: Validators are selected by the token holders of the network. The selected validators stake their coins to add and validate blocks. Used in EOS.
- Practical Byzantine Fault Tolerance: Duplicate, independent nodes check each other to reach consensus. Used in Hyperledger Fabric.

Advantages of consensus algorithms:
- Ensure integrity of data and transactions.
- Prevent double-spending and fraud.
- Enable decentralized control.

Disadvantages:
- Proof-of-Work is energy intensive due to continuous solving of complex puzzles.
- Vulnerable to Sybil attacks where a single node creates multiple malicious identities to disrupt the consensus.

Applications: Cryptocurrencies, smart contracts, supply chain management, etc.
