### Blockchain in trade/supply chain for the notes of the Unit 7 - Use case 2 in the subject of Block chain Architecture Design

- Blockchain can bring transparency and efficiency to supply chain management. Every transaction in the supply chain like purchase orders, change in inventories, payments, etc. can be recorded immutably on the blockchain. This reduces the possibility of fraud and fastens the dispute resolution.
- Smart contracts can automate the transfer of assets as certain conditions are met. For example, when a shipment arrives at a port, the smart contract can automatically release the payment to the shipping company. This reduces the manual paperwork and saves time.
- The provenance of goods can be tracked all the way back to their origin. This increases accountability and helps in determining the responsible parties in case of any issues like defects or illegal practices. Consumers can see the complete history of a product by scanning a QR code or RFID tag.
- However, there are certain challenges in adopting blockchain for supply chain:
-- Scalability - The amount of data generated by all the transactions in a supply chain network can be huge which can affect the performance of blockchain. Solutions like sharding and off-chain storage are being explored to address this.
-- Interoperability - Different companies in a supply chain may be using different blockchain networks. Interoperability standards are required to facilitate the secure and seamless transfer of data between different networks.
-- Data privacy - While blockchain provides transparency, the data on the blockchain needs to be secured and accessed only by authorized parties. Techniques like zero-knowledge proofs and encrypted data storage can be used to ensure privacy.

- Some examples of blockchain applications in supply chain are IBM Food Trust network, Walmart's food safety blockchain, and Everledger for diamond tracking. Blockchain has the potential to bring transparency and efficiency to supply chain management through its properties of immutability, security, and automation via smart contracts. However, scalability and interoperability issues need to be solved for widespread adoption.
