## Unit 1 - Introduction to Blockchain

### What is Blockchain?

- Blockchain is a distributed ledger of transactions maintained by a network of computers.
- New transactions are recorded in blocks that are added to the blockchain in a linear, chronological order.
- The blocks are all secured and bound to previous blocks using cryptography.
- This creates an immutable, tamper-resistant ledger of all the transactions.
- The blockchain allows multiple parties to have a secure and consistent view of digital information without the need for a trusted, third-party intermediary.

### How does Blockchain work?

- New transactions are recorded in a new block that is added to the blockchain.
- Blocks are secured and linked together using cryptographic hashes.
- Each block contains a hash of the previous block, a timestamp, and the transaction data.
- By design, blockchain is resistant to modification of the data. So, the data in any given block cannot be altered retroactively without altering all subsequent blocks.
- This ensures the integrity of the blockchain and makes the records tamper-evident.

### Properties of Blockchain

- Decentralization - There is no single point of failure and no single entity controls the data.
- Transparency - The blockchain is a distributed ledger, so the data is open and visible to all nodes in the network.
- Immutability - Records in the blockchain are immutable, tamper-resistant, and cannot be deleted.
- Security - The blockchain uses cryptography to ensure the integrity and security of the data.
- Consensus - There is a consensus mechanism to ensure all nodes agree on the validity of the blockchain.
