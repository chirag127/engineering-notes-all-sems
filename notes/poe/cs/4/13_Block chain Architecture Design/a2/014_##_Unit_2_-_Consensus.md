## Unit 2 - Consensus

Consensus is a distributed system mechanism ensuring multiple nodes in a network reach to an agreement. It is a key concept in blockchain technology for maintaining a trusted network without a centralized authority. Following are the key points to learn about consensus in blockchain:

- **Consensus types:** The popular consensus types are Proof-of-Work, Proof-of-Stake, Delegated Proof-of-Stake, and Practical Byzantine Fault Tolerance. Each has its pros and cons in terms of security, scalability, and energy efficiency.
- **Longest chain rule:** In Proof-of-Work, the longest chain of blocks is considered the valid chain. Miners keep mining on top of the longest chain, making it longer and more difficult to attack. This is the basis of consensus and immutability in blockchain.
- **Incentives:** Incentives are crucial for driving participation in consensus. In Proof-of-Work, miners are incentivized through block rewards and transaction fees. In Proof-of-Stake, validators are incentivized through staking rewards. Proper incentive design is important for security and reliability of the consensus system.
- **Finality:** Finality refers to the certainty of a consensus decision. Some consensus mechanisms like basic Proof-of-Work have probabilistic finality, where there is a possibility of forks. Others like Practical Byzantine Fault Tolerance have faster but probabilistic finality. Absolute finality is desirable but often at the cost of scalability.
- **Fault tolerance:** A consensus system should tolerate certain types of faults or adversarial attacks to maintain integrity. For example, the Byzantine Generals Problem deals with tolerating Byzantine faults, where some nodes may act maliciously. The consensus system should be robust to such faults.
