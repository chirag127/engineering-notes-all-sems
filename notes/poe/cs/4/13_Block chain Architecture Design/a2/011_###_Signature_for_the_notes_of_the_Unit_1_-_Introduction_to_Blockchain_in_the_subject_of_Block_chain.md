### Signature for the notes of the Unit 1 - Introduction to Blockchain in the subject of Block chain Architecture Design

1. Blockchain is a distributed ledger of transactions maintained by a network of computers. The ledger is append-only, so new records are added to the end of the ledger.
2. The ledger is decentralized and distributed across many nodes in the network. The nodes constantly verify and reconcile transactions via the network. This makes the records more transparent, secure, and tamper-resistant.
3. New transactions are recorded in blocks that are added to the blockchain in a linear, chronological order. Each block contains a timestamp and a link to the previous block in the chain.
4. The blockchain is a trustless system, so no single entity controls the data or transactions. This makes blockchain networks robust, transparent, and difficult to hack or corrupt.
5. Blockchain has applications in finance, supply chain management, health care, and other fields. The secure and transparent nature of blockchain makes it useful for recording and tracking transactions, assets, or other records.

**Mnemonics:**

Blockchain is append-only,
Decentralized and distributed,
Blocks chained chronologically,
Trustless and tamper-resistant.

**Advantages:**

- Secure - Cryptography and decentralization make blockchain very secure.
- Transparent - The distributed ledger is transparent and immutable. All participants have a copy of the complete transaction history.
- Efficient - Decentralized consensus and verification allow for fast and inexpensive transactions.
- Robust - The decentralized, distributed nature of blockchain makes it difficult to shut down or corrupt the network.

**Disadvantages:**

- Complexity - The technology can be complex to implement and understand.
- Limited scalability - There are technical challenges to scaling blockchain networks and maintaining speed and efficiency with many transactions.
- Energy intensive - The proof-of-work consensus algorithm used in some blockchains requires a lot of computing power and electricity.
- Regulation uncertainty - The regulatory status of blockchain and cryptocurrencies is still unclear in many countries. This uncertainty can limit adoption of the technology.
