### Protocols for the notes of the Unit 1 - Introduction to Blockchain in the subject of Block chain Architecture Design

- Bitcoin Protocol: The Bitcoin protocol is the set of rules that nodes in the Bitcoin network use to securely broadcast transactions and blocks, validate them, and reach consensus without a central authority. The Bitcoin protocol uses proof-of-work to prevent Sybil attacks and ensure that consensus rules are followed.
- Ethereum Protocol: The Ethereum protocol defines a set of rules for a blockchain-based system that can execute smart contracts. It uses proof-of-work mining to prevent Sybil attacks, includes mechanisms for reaching consensus on the state of smart contracts, and defines how transactions and smart contract executions are validated and recorded on the blockchain.
- Hyperledger Protocol: Hyperledger is an open-source umbrella project incubated by the Linux Foundation to advance cross-industry blockchain technologies. Hyperledger projects like Hyperledger Fabric offer flexible blockchain protocols with pluggable consensus algorithms and smart contract engines to suit a variety of industry use cases.

Advantages of protocols:
- Provide standard ways for devices and systems to communicate and share data.
- Enable interoperability between various networks, systems, and applications.
- Facilitate automation and scalability.

Disadvantages of protocols:
- Can be complex to implement.
- May not be efficient for all use cases.
- Can become outdated or insecure over time and require upgrades.

Examples of blockchain protocols: Bitcoin, Ethereum, Hyperledger
Applications: Cryptocurrencies, smart contracts, supply chain tracking, etc.
