
### Measurement for the Notes of Unit 2 - Quantum Computation in the Subject of Quantum Computing

* In quantum computing, measurement is the process of determining the state of a quantum system. It is an essential part of the quantum computing process and is used to extract information from a quantum system.
* Measurement in quantum computing involves the use of a quantum observables, which are physical quantities that can be measured. Examples of quantum observables include spin, energy, and position.
* Measurement is performed using a quantum measurement device, which is a device that is able to measure the state of a quantum system. Examples of quantum measurement devices include the Stern-Gerlach device and the quantum point contact.
* Measurement in quantum computing is a probabilistic process, meaning that the outcome of a measurement is not known in advance. This is due to the fact that the state of a quantum system is determined by its wave function, which is a mathematical function that describes the state of the system.
* The outcome of a measurement can be either a discrete value, such as 0 or 1, or a continuous value, such as a real number.
* Measurement in quantum computing can be used to determine the state of a quantum system, to extract information from a quantum system, and to perform computations.
* Measurement in quantum computing can also be used to create entanglement, which is a quantum phenomenon where two particles become linked together in such a way that the state of one particle affects the state of the other.
* Measurement in quantum computing is a complex process and requires a thorough understanding of the principles of quantum mechanics.
