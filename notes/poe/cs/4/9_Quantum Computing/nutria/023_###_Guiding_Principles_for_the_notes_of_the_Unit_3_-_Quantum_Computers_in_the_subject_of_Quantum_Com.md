
### Guiding Principles for the Notes of the Unit 3 - Quantum Computers in the Subject of Quantum Computing

1. The **superposition principle** states that a quantum system can exist in multiple states at the same time. This allows quantum computers to process multiple pieces of data simultaneously, leading to a significant speedup in computation.

2. The **entanglement principle** states that two or more particles can be linked together in such a way that their states are correlated. This allows for faster communication between qubits, leading to a more efficient computation.

3. The **quantum tunneling effect** states that a particle can "tunnel" through an energy barrier, allowing it to move through space-time faster than light. This effect can be used to speed up certain computations.

4. The **quantum annealing principle** states that a quantum system can be cooled down to a low temperature, allowing it to find the optimal solution to a problem. This technique is used in quantum computing to solve difficult optimization problems.

5. The **Heisenberg uncertainty principle** states that it is impossible to measure the exact state of a quantum system. This means that quantum computers must rely on probabilistic algorithms to solve problems.

6. The **quantum noise principle** states that quantum systems are subject to random fluctuations in their state, which can lead to errors in computation. This means that quantum computers must use error-correcting codes to ensure accuracy.
