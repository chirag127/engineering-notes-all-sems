
### Quantum Bits for the notes of the Unit 1 - Fundamental Concepts in the subject of Quantum Computing

Quantum bits, commonly referred to as qubits, are the basic unit of information in a quantum computer. Qubits are the quantum equivalent of classical bits, which are the basic unit of information in a classical computer. 

Qubits are the building blocks of quantum computers, and they can store more information than classical bits. A qubit can store a 0, a 1, or a superposition of both. This means that a qubit can represent more information than a classical bit, which can only represent a 0 or a 1. 

Qubits can also be entangled, meaning that they can be linked together so that the state of one qubit is dependent on the state of the other. Entanglement allows quantum computers to perform computations that would be impossible on a classical computer. 

Qubits are typically represented by a two-state quantum system. This means that the qubit can be in either state 0 or state 1, and the state of the qubit is determined by the quantum state of the system. 

To store information in a qubit, the quantum state of the system must be manipulated. This is done by applying quantum operations, such as single-qubit operations and two-qubit operations. Single-qubit operations are used to manipulate the state of a single qubit, while two-qubit operations are used to manipulate the state of two qubits. 

Qubits are also used to represent quantum algorithms, which are algorithms that use quantum operations to solve problems. Quantum algorithms are used to solve problems that are too difficult for classical computers to solve. 

In conclusion, quantum bits, or qubits, are the basic unit of information in a quantum computer. Qubits can store more information than classical bits, and they can be used to represent quantum algorithms. Qubits can also be entangled, allowing quantum computers to perform computations that would be impossible on a classical computer.
