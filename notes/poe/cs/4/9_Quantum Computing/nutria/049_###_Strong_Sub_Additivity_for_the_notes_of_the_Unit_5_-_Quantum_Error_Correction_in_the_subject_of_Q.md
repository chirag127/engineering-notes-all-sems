
### Strong Sub Additivity for the Notes of Unit 5 - Quantum Error Correction in the Subject of Quantum Computing

Strong subadditivity is an important concept in quantum computing, which states that the von Neumann entropy of a composite system is less than or equal to the sum of the von Neumann entropies of its subsystems. This concept is important in the study of quantum information theory and quantum error correction.

In quantum computing, the von Neumann entropy is a measure of the amount of information contained in a quantum system. It is defined as the Shannon entropy of the eigenvalues of the density matrix of the system.

The strong subadditivity of the von Neumann entropy can be expressed mathematically as follows:

$$S(A, B, C) \leq S(A, B) + S(B, C)$$

where $S(A, B, C)$ is the von Neumann entropy of the composite system $A, B, C$ and $S(A, B)$ and $S(B, C)$ are the von Neumann entropies of the subsystems $A, B$ and $B, C$ respectively.

This concept is important in the study of quantum error correction because it implies that the errors that occur in a quantum system are bounded. This means that the errors can be corrected with a certain degree of accuracy, which is determined by the strong subadditivity of the von Neumann entropy.

In addition, the strong subadditivity of the von Neumann entropy is also important in the study of quantum cryptography, as it implies that the information stored in a quantum system can be securely transmitted over a quantum channel without any leakage of information.

To remember this concept, one can use the mnemonic "SSAVNE" which stands for Strong Subadditivity of the Von Neumann Entropy.
