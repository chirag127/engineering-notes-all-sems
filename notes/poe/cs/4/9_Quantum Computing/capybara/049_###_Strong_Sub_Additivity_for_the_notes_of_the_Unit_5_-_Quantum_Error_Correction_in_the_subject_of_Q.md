### Strong Sub Additivity for the notes of the Unit 5 - Quantum Error Correction in the subject of Quantum Computing

The Strong Sub Additivity (SSA) is an important concept in the field of quantum information theory. It is a fundamental principle that applies to quantum systems that are composed of multiple subsystems. In this section, we will discuss SSA in detail and understand its significance in the context of Quantum Error Correction.

#### Definition

SSA is a mathematical principle that states that the amount of information contained in the combined system of two subsystems is always equal to or greater than the sum of the information contained in the individual subsystems. In other words, if we have two quantum systems, A and B, the total amount of information contained in the combined system AB will always be greater than or equal to the sum of the information contained in A and B individually.

#### Importance in Quantum Error Correction

SSA has important implications in the field of Quantum Error Correction. Quantum Error Correction is a technique used to protect quantum information from errors caused by decoherence and other environmental factors. In this technique, quantum information is encoded in a way that makes it resilient to errors. However, errors can still occur due to the interaction of the quantum system with its environment.

SSA helps us understand the limits of Quantum Error Correction. It tells us that the amount of information that can be protected by quantum error correction codes is limited by the amount of information contained in the subsystems. Thus, SSA helps us determine the maximum amount of information that can be protected by quantum error correction codes.
