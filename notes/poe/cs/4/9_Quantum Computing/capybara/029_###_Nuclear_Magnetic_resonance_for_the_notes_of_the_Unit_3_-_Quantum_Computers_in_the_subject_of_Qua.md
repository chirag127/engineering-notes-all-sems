### Nuclear Magnetic Resonance for the Notes of Unit 3 - Quantum Computers in the Subject of Quantum Computing

Nuclear Magnetic Resonance (NMR) is an important technique used in quantum computing for the study of the properties of nuclei in a magnetic field. NMR is commonly used in the field of chemistry, but it also has applications in quantum computing. In this section, we will discuss the basics of NMR and its relevance to quantum computing.

#### Basics of NMR

NMR is a technique that uses the magnetic properties of certain atomic nuclei to study the structure and properties of molecules. When a molecule is placed in a magnetic field, the nuclei of the atoms within the molecule will align with the magnetic field. The frequency at which the nuclei align is called the Larmor frequency, which is proportional to the strength of the magnetic field.

NMR works by applying a radiofrequency pulse to the sample, causing the nuclei to absorb energy and move from their aligned state. When the pulse is turned off, the nuclei return to their aligned state, releasing energy in the form of electromagnetic radiation. This radiation is detected by a receiver, and the resulting signal is used to construct a spectrum that can be used to study the properties of the molecule.

#### Relevance to Quantum Computing

NMR is used in quantum computing for the study of qubits, which are the basic building blocks of quantum computers. Qubits are typically made from the nuclei of atoms, and their magnetic properties are used to store and manipulate quantum information. NMR is used to measure the properties of qubits and to manipulate them in order to perform calculations.
