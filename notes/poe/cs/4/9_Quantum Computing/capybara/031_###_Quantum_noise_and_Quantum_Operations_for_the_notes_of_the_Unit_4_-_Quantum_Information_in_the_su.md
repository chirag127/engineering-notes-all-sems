### Quantum noise and Quantum Operations for the notes of the Unit 4 - Quantum Information in the subject of Quantum Computing

Quantum computing is a rapidly growing field, and its potential applications are endless. However, quantum systems are susceptible to noise, which can cause errors in quantum computations. Understanding quantum noise and quantum operations is crucial for the development of reliable quantum computing systems. In this section, we will discuss quantum noise and quantum operations in detail.

#### Quantum Noise

Quantum noise is the random fluctuations of the quantum state that can occur due to various factors, such as thermal effects, electromagnetic radiation, and quantum entanglement. These fluctuations can cause errors in quantum computations, making it difficult to obtain accurate results.

There are two types of quantum noise: coherent noise and incoherent noise. Coherent noise is a type of noise that preserves the coherence of the quantum state. Incoherent noise, on the other hand, destroys the coherence of the quantum state.

#### Quantum Operations

Quantum operations are a set of mathematical operations that are used to manipulate the quantum state. Quantum operations can be divided into two categories: unitary operations and non-unitary operations.

Unitary operations are reversible operations that preserve the norm of the quantum state. They are used to manipulate the quantum state without causing any loss of information. Examples of unitary operations include the Hadamard gate, the Pauli-X gate, and the CNOT gate.

Non-unitary operations, on the other hand, are irreversible operations that do not preserve the norm of the quantum state. They are used to introduce noise into the quantum system or to measure the quantum state. Examples of non-unitary operations include the measurement operation and the amplitude damping operation.
