### Postulates of Quantum Mechanisms for the notes of the Unit 1 - Fundamental Concepts in the subject of Quantum Computing

Quantum mechanics is a fundamental concept in the field of Quantum Computing. It is based on several postulates that define the behavior of quantum systems. These postulates are as follows:

1. State postulate: A quantum system is described by a state vector, which is a complex vector in a Hilbert space. The state vector represents the probability amplitude of the system being in a particular state.

2. Superposition postulate: A quantum system can exist in a superposition of states, which means that it can be in multiple states simultaneously. The state vector is a linear combination of basis states, and the coefficients represent the probability amplitudes of each state.

3. Measurement postulate: When a measurement is made on a quantum system, the system collapses into one of its basis states with a probability equal to the square of the coefficient of that state in the state vector.

4. Uncertainty postulate: The uncertainty principle states that it is impossible to simultaneously measure certain pairs of physical properties, such as position and momentum, with arbitrary precision.

These postulates form the basis of quantum mechanics and are crucial for understanding the behavior of quantum systems. Here are some Mnemonics that can help you remember these postulates:

- State postulate: "S" stands for "State vector" and "S" also stands for "System".
- Superposition postulate: "Superposition" sounds like "Soup in a position". Imagine a soup that can be in multiple positions at once.
- Measurement postulate: "M" stands for "Measurement" and "M" also stands for "Matrix". The measurement postulate involves the projection of a state vector onto a basis state, which can be represented by a matrix.
- Uncertainty postulate: "U" stands for "Uncertainty" and "U" also stands for "Unknowable". The uncertainty postulate states that certain properties of a quantum system are unknowable with arbitrary precision.

Remembering these postulates can be challenging, but with the help of these Mnemonics, you can easily recall them during your exams.
