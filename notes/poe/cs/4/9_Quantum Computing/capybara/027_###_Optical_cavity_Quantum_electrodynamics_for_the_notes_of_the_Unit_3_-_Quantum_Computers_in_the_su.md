### Optical cavity Quantum electrodynamics for the notes of the Unit 3 - Quantum Computers in the subject of Quantum Computing

Optical cavity Quantum electrodynamics (QED) is a branch of quantum physics that deals with the interaction between light and matter. It is an important topic in the field of Quantum Computing and has various applications in the development of quantum technologies. Here are some important points to understand about Optical cavity QED:

1. Optical cavity QED involves the study of the interaction between light and matter in a confined space, such as an optical cavity. The optical cavity is an enclosed area between two mirrors that can trap light for a certain period of time.

2. In Optical cavity QED, the interaction between light and matter is described using the theory of Quantum Electrodynamics. This theory explains how photons (particles of light) and atoms interact with each other.

3. The interaction between light and matter in an optical cavity can be used to create entangled states of photons and atoms. Entanglement is a phenomenon in quantum mechanics where two particles become connected in such a way that the state of one particle is dependent on the state of the other particle, regardless of the distance between them.

4. Optical cavity QED can be used to create qubits, which are the basic building blocks of quantum computers. Qubits are different from classical bits in that they can exist in a superposition of states, allowing for more complex computations to be performed.

5. One mnemonic to remember the basics of Optical cavity QED is the acronym CAVITY, which stands for:

- Confined space: Optical cavity is an enclosed space.
- Atoms: Study of interaction between light and matter.
- Quantum Electrodynamics: Theory used to explain the interaction.
- Entanglement: Phenomenon created in the interaction.
- Qubits: Basic building blocks of quantum computers.
- Yield: Optical cavity QED can yield more complex computations.

6. Optical cavity QED also has some disadvantages, such as the difficulty in creating and maintaining the entangled states of photons and atoms. However, the potential benefits of using Optical cavity QED in quantum computing make it an important topic to study.

In summary, Optical cavity Quantum electrodynamics is an important topic in the field of Quantum Computing, which involves the study of the interaction between light and matter in a confined space. It has various applications in the development of quantum technologies, including the creation of qubits and entangled states. Understanding the basics of Optical cavity QED can help in the development of more efficient and advanced quantum computers.
