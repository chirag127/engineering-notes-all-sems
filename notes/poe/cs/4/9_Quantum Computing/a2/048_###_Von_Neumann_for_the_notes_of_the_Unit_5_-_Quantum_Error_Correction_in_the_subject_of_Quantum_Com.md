### Von Neumann for the notes of the Unit 5 - Quantum Error Correction in the subject of Quantum Computing

- Von Neumann architecture is a design model for a stored-program digital computer. It consists of a central processing unit with an arithmetic logical unit and processor registers, a control unit containing an instruction register and program counter, memory to store both data and instructions, and input/output mechanisms.
- In quantum computing, the Von Neumann bottleneck is the limitation that the processor and the memory use the same channel (data bus) to communicate. This leads to a bottleneck in performance as the processor has to wait until it fetches the next instruction or data from memory. This limits the performance scaling of quantum computers.
- To overcome the Von Neumann bottleneck in quantum computing and enable fault-tolerant quantum error correction, researchers proposed a non-Von Neumann architecture with separate data and instruction buses so that data transfer and computational processing can be done concurrently. Some examples are the liquid-state machine and the quantum Turing machine.
- Advantages: Concurrent processing leading to faster speed. Enables quantum error correction.
- Disadvantages: Increases system complexity. Require separate data and instruction buses leading to higher resource requirements.
- Examples: Quantum Turing machine, Liquid-state machine.
- Applications: Enable fault-tolerant quantum computing and quantum error correction. Allow for faster quantum algorithms and computations.
