### Entanglement as a physical resource for the notes of the Unit 5 - Quantum Error Correction in the subject of Quantum Computing

- Entanglement is a quantum mechanical phenomenon in which the quantum states of two or more objects have to be described with reference to each other, even though the individual objects may be spatially separated. This leads to correlations between observable physical properties of the systems.
- For example, if two particles are entangled, then measuring a property of one particle instantly affects the other, no matter if they are separated by a large distance.
- Entanglement is a necessary ingredient for many proposed quantum information processing and quantum computing tasks. It is a key feature that distinguishes quantum mechanics from classical physics.
- The quantum states of two entangled particles have components that are described by the state vector of the system as a whole, not as a separable state of two individual particles. This non-local connection between the particles makes the system behave in ways that seem paradoxical from a classical perspective. However, it has been demonstrated experimentally many times and forms the basis of proposed applications such as quantum cryptography and quantum computing.
- The counterintuitive effects of entanglement have been demonstrated experimentally many times, and found to be exponentially unlikely to be explained by classical physics. In quantum mechanics, these effects are real and necessary features of quantum systems.
- Some of the ways in which entanglement can be exploited as a resource are:
- Quantum teleportation: Transferring quantum states between two particles that may be very far apart.
- Quantum superdense coding: Communicating two bits of classical information using just one qubit.
- Quantum key distribution: Generating a secret key between two parties that can then be used to encrypt and decrypt messages.
- Error correction codes: Quantum error correction codes that exploit entanglement can be more efficient than classical codes.
- Quantum algorithms: Many quantum algorithms like quantum Fourier transform and Shor's algorithm exploit entanglement.
