### Quantum counting for the notes of the Unit 2 - Quantum Computation in the subject of Quantum Computing

- Quantum counting refers to using quantum bits or qubits to represent and manipulate numbers in superposition for quantum computation.
- Qubits can be in superposition of states, so a quantum register of n qubits can represent 2^n values simultaneously. This enables parallelism in quantum algorithms.
- Some key points to remember:
	- Qubits can be represented by spin-1/2 particles, photons, ions, etc. and their quantum states.
	- Qubits can be in superposition of states, meaning a qubit can be in state |0> and |1> at the same time with certain amplitudes. This is the core of quantum parallelism.
	- To read the value of a quantum register, measurement is performed which collapses the superposition to classical bits.
	- Quantum gates like Hadamard gate, Phase shift gates, CNOT gate, etc. are used to manipulate qubits and perform operations like putting qubits in superposition, entangling them, etc.
	- Quantum counting is the key to quantum Fourier transform and Shor's algorithm for integer factorization.
- Some mnemonics to remember:
	- Qubits are the quantum counterparts of bits.
	- A quantum register of n qubits can represent 2^n values in superposition.
	- Qubit states are represented by |0> and |1> and their superposition.
	- Quantum gates manipulate qubit states - superposition, entanglement, etc.
	- Measurement collapses superposition to get classical bits.

- The key applications of quantum counting are in quantum algorithms like quantum Fourier transform and Shor's algorithm. Quantum counting enables the exponential speedup in these algorithms over classical algorithms.
