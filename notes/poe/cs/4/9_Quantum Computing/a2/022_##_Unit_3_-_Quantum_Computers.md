## Unit 3 - Quantum Computers

Quantum Computers are a new type of computers that harness the properties of quantum mechanics to solve complex computational problems that are intractable for conventional computers. Some key points about Quantum Computers are:

1. Qubits: Quantum bits or 'Qubits' are the basic units of information in a quantum computer. They can hold values of 1, 0, or a superposition of both. This allows a quantum computer to perform certain types of calculations much faster than conventional computers.

2. Superposition: Qubits can be in a superposition of states, meaning they can have a probability distribution of multiple states until they are measured. This allows a quantum computer to explore many possible solutions in parallel. This is known as quantum parallelism and enables quantum algorithms like Shor's algorithm to be more efficient than their classical counterparts.

3. Entanglement: When two or more qubits are entangled, their states are dependent on each other, even if they are separated by a large distance. Measuring one qubit will instantly influence the other, no matter how far apart they are. This 'spooky action at a distance' as Einstein called it, is a bizarre yet fundamental feature of quantum mechanics and enables quantum computers to solve certain problems much faster than conventional computers.

4. Decoherence: The delicate quantum states of qubits are fragile and easily decohere or lose their quantum nature due to interactions with the environment. This is one of the major challenges in building practical quantum computers as decoherence errors accumulate rapidly with increasing number of qubits. Error correction codes and quantum error correction techniques are required to mitigate decoherence and build scalable quantum computers.

Some of the promising applications of quantum computers are:

- Shor's algorithm: To factor large numbers and break cryptographic codes like RSA encryption.
- Quantum machine learning: To train machine learning models on large datasets.
- Quantum simulation: To simulate quantum systems and accelerate material discovery and drug design.
- Quantum algorithms: For linear systems of equations, graph connectivity, search, etc. can offer speedups over best known classical algorithms.

However, quantum computing is still in its infancy and there are a lot of engineering challenges to scale quantum computers and make them practical. Despite active research in academia and companies like Google, IBM, Rigetti computing, etc., we are still far from achieving 'quantum supremacy' or solving problems that are intractable for classical supercomputers. More work is required to unleash the true potential of quantum computers.
