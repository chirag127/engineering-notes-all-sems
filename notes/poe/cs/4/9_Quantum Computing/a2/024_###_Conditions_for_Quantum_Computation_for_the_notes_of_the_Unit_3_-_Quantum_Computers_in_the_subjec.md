### Conditions for Quantum Computation

The following conditions must be met for a quantum computer to function:

1. Isolation from the environment: The quantum system must be isolated from its surrounding environment to prevent decoherence i.e. the loss of quantum behaviour. This is difficult to achieve as the quantum system interacts with the environment. Special techniques like error correction codes, quantum error correction, and topological quantum computation are used to reduce decoherence.

2. Precise control: The quantum system must be precisely controlled. The quantum gates must be applied accurately without any fluctuations or noise. Small deviations can cause the quantum system to decohere and lose the quantum behaviour. Strong magnetic fields and electromagnetic pulses with precise shapes and durations are used to control the quantum system.

3. Low temperatures: The quantum system must be maintained at extremely low temperatures close to absolute zero. This is because the thermal energy causes the quantum system to decohere. The low temperatures are achieved using dilution refrigerators and laser cooling techniques.

4. Quantum entanglement: The quantum system makes use of quantum entanglement to exhibit quantum behaviour. The quantum gates apply operations on the quantum bits or 'qubits' in an entangled state. The entanglement is fragile and decoheres easily. Methods to preserve entanglement are an active area of research in quantum computing.

The above conditions pose significant challenges in building a practical and scalable quantum computer. However, despite the difficulties, there have been some promising advances and we may achieve a quantum computer in the near future that can outperform classical supercomputers. Quantum computing holds a lot of promise for applications like quantum machine learning, quantum material science, quantum chemistry, etc.
