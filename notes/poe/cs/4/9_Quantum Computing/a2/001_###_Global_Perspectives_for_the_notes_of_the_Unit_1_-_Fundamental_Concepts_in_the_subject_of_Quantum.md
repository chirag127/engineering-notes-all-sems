### Global Perspectives for the notes of the Unit 1 - Fundamental Concepts in the subject of Quantum Computing

- Quantum Computing is an emerging field of computing which harness the properties of quantum mechanics to solve complex computational problems that are intractable for traditional computers.
- Some of the key concepts in quantum computing are:
	- Qubits: They are the basic units of information in a quantum computer, analogous to bits in a classical computer. Qubits can be in a superposition of states, meaning they can have values of 0 and 1 at the same time. This allows a quantum computer to explore many possible solutions in parallel.
	- Superposition: It refers to the ability of a quantum system to exist in multiple states simultaneously. For example, a qubit can be in a superposition of states 0 and 1 at once. This is what enables quantum algorithms to be more efficient than classical algorithms.
	- Entanglement: It refers to the phenomenon where two or more quantum particles become "entangled" and their properties directly depend on each other, even if the particles are separated by a large distance. This leads to correlations between the particles which can be exploited for quantum computing and cryptography applications.
	- Quantum Gates: They are the basic operations that can be performed on qubits. Analogous to logic gates in classical computing, quantum gates manipulate the state of qubits. Some common types of quantum gates are Hadamard gate, Phase shift gate, Controlled-NOT gate, etc.
	- Quantum Circuits: They are created by applying a sequence of quantum gates on qubits. The output state depends on the specific sequence of gates applied. Quantum circuits are used to implement quantum algorithms.
- Some of the promising applications of quantum computing are:
	- Quantum Machine Learning: Quantum machine learning algorithms can exploit quantum entanglement to learn complex patterns in large data sets. Some examples are quantum principal component analysis and quantum support vector machine.
	- Quantum Chemistry: Quantum computers can be used to simulate quantum systems to solve complex chemical problems that are intractable for classical supercomputers. This can enable the design of new materials and drug discovery.
	- Quantum Cryptography: Quantum cryptography enables secure communication using quantum mechanical effects like entanglement. It allows detection of any eavesdropping, thereby providing unconditional security.
