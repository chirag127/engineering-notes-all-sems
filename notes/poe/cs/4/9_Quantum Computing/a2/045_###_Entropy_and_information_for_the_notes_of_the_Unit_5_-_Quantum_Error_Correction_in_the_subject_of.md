### Entropy and information for the notes of the Unit 5 - Quantum Error Correction in the subject of Quantum Computing

- Entropy is a measure of randomness or uncertainty of a quantum system. Higher the entropy, higher is the randomness and lesser is the information. Lower the entropy, lesser is the randomness and higher is the information.
- For a quantum system in a pure state, the entropy is zero and the information is maximum. For a quantum system in a completely mixed state, the entropy is maximum and the information is zero.
- The entropy of a quantum system can increase due to decoherence or noise. This leads to loss of quantum information. Quantum error correction is required to counteract this increase in entropy and loss of quantum information.
- Quantum error correcting codes introduce redundancy in the quantum state which can be used to detect and correct errors due to decoherence and recover the original quantum information. The amount of redundancy added depends on the noise levels and desired accuracy of decoding.
- Good mnemonics: More mixing leads to more messing (with information). Pure state is neat state (with maximum information).
- The key is to add judicious redundancy to combat entropy increase and preserve quantum information. Too little redundancy leads to errors not being correctable. Too much redundancy leads to inefficient use of quantum resources. Achieving the right balance is crucial for practical quantum error correction.
