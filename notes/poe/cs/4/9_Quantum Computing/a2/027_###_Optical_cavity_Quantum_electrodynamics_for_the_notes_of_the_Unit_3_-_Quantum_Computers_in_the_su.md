## Optical cavity Quantum electrodynamics

- QED in optical cavities studies the interaction of light and matter in a highly controlled manner.
- An optical cavity is formed by two mirrors which confine light between them. This allows to study the interaction of light and matter at the single photon level.
- Some key features of optical cavities are:
-- They enhance the interaction between light and matter.
-- They allow to study quantum effects of light and its interaction with atoms.
-- They are used to build efficient single photon sources and quantum memories.

**Mnemonics:**

- Think of an optical cavity as a tube of light between two mirrors to remember that it confines light between mirrors.
- Relate the enhancement in light-matter interaction in cavities to focusing of light using lenses to get stronger effects.

**Advantages:**

- Highly controlled experimental setup to study QED.
- Enhanced light-matter interaction allows to observe quantum effects.
- Applications in quantum information processing using single photons.

**Disadvantages:**

- Challenging to implement and the systems are complex.
- Decoherence can affect the quantum effects which need to be minimized.

**Applications:**

- Single photon sources
- Quantum memories
- Cavity QED experiments to test quantum physics
- Building blocks for quantum information processing using single photons
