### Optical Photon Quantum Computer

- Optical quantum computers use photons (light particles) instead of atoms or electrons to represent quantum bits or 'qubits'.
- The polarization state of a photon can be used to represent a qubit. A photon polarization state can be in a superposition of vertical and horizontal polarizations which can be used to perform quantum computations.
- The optical qubits are manipulated using linear optical elements like beam splitters, phase shifters, etc. The qubits are entangled using spontaneous parametric down-conversion process which converts a photon into an entangled pair of photons.
- The read-out of the quantum state is done by measuring the photon polarizations. If the polarization matches the desired output state, it signifies that the quantum algorithm has been successfully executed.
- **Advantages:** The opticalphoton quantum computer has low decoherence as photons interact weakly with the environment. The optical elements are well developed and easy to implement. It can be easily scaled to large numbers of qubits.
- **Disadvantages:** It is difficult to implement two-qubit gates and generate entanglement in optical quantum computers. The photon detectors have limited efficiency which can lead to loss of information. Error correction is challenging to implement.
- **Applications:** Optical quantum computers can be used to solve optimization problems like finding minimum vertex cover in a graph which has applications in machine learning and artificial intelligence. It can also be used to simulate quantum systems which can aid in development of new medicines and materials.
