### Examples of Quantum noise and Quantum Operations for the notes of the Unit 4 - Quantum Information in the subject of Quantum Computing

Quantum noise - The unwanted disturbance to the quantum state of a quantum system is known as quantum noise. It arises due to interactions with the environment. Some examples of quantum noise are -

- Decoherence - It is the loss of quantum coherence of a quantum system due to its interaction with the environment. It leads to the transition of a pure state into a mixed state.
- Dephasing - It is the loss of quantum phase relation between the components of a quantum system. It leads to the loss of interference effects.
- Dissipation - It is the loss of energy of a quantum system to the environment. It leads to the relaxation of the system into thermal equilibrium states.

Some common quantum operations are -

- Measurement - It is the process of obtaining information about a quantum system. It causes the collapse of the quantum state into an eigenstate of the measured observable.
- Preparation - It is the process of placing a quantum system into a desired quantum state. It is done by subjecting the system to electromagnetic pulses.
- Evolution - It is the process in which a quantum system changes with time according to the Schrodinger's equation or quantum jumping process. The evolution can be unitary or non-unitary depending on whether the system is isolated or interacting with the environment.
- Control - It is the process of manipulating the quantum state of a system by applying external fields to drive transitions between energy levels or force desired interactions. It is used to implement quantum logic gates and quantum algorithms.

The advantages of quantum operations are -

- They can create and manipulate fragile quantum states not easily achieved with classical means.
- They enable the implementation of quantum algorithms to solve complex problems efficiently.
The disadvantages are -

- Quantum operations are highly sensitive to noise and decoherence which can destroy quantum effects.
- It is challenging to scale up quantum systems and operations to larger sizes due to decoherence and other technical limitations.
