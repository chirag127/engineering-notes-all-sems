## Unit 4 - Quantum Information

Quantum Information refers to the information that is encoded in the state of a quantum system. It holds some properties that are not possible with classical information alone. Some key points about Quantum Information are:

- Qubits: The basic unit of quantum information is the qubit. A qubit can be in a superposition of states, having a probability distribution of multiple states at once. This allows a quantum system to hold more information than a classical bit.
- Entanglement: When two or more qubits are entangled, their states are dependent on each other, even if they are separated by a large distance. Measuring one qubit will instantly influence the other, which leads to effects like quantum teleportation. Entanglement is a key feature for many proposed quantum technologies like quantum cryptography and quantum computing.
- Superdense Coding: Using entanglement, it is possible to transmit two bits of classical information using just one qubit. This is called superdense coding and allows for more information to be sent using less physical resources than classical communication would allow.
- Quantum Teleportation: The exact state of one qubit can be transmitted to another qubit that may be very far away. This is not faster-than-light communication but it is a way of transmitting quantum information without sending a physical carrier particle. It utilizes entanglement and classical communication.
- Quantum Cryptography: Quantum key distribution allows two parties to produce a shared random secret key which can be used to encrypt and decrypt messages. Because of the properties of quantum information like entanglement, it is not possible for an eavesdropper to copy the key without disturbing it and revealing their presence. This enables encryption that can be proven secure.

Some mnemonics/learning tricks for quantum information:
- Think of qubits as quantum bits that can be in superposition, holding multiple states at once (unlike classical bits).
- Imagine two entangled qubits as two friends who instantly know what the other is thinking or doing, even from far away. Measuring one friend instantly affects the other.
- For superdense coding, visualize packing 2 bits into 1 qubit through entanglement, allowing more information to be sent with fewer resources.
- Quantum teleportation can be thought of as a quantum fax machine that transmits the exact state of one qubit to another qubit far away. It's not faster-than-light but uses entanglement and classical communication.
