### Stabilizer codes for the notes of the Unit 5 - Quantum Error Correction in the subject of Quantum Computing

- Stabilizer codes are a class of quantum error-correcting codes that have a simple description in terms of "stabilizer operators".
- A stabilizer operator is an operator composed of tensor products of Pauli matrices (I, X, Y, Z) that commutes with the codespace. The group of stabilizer operators forms an Abelian group called the stabilizer group.
- The codespace is the joint +1-eigenspace of the stabilizer group. The stabilizer formalism provides an efficient way to describe the logical operations on encoded quantum states and can be used to prove properties of stabilizer codes, such as the quantum Hamming bound.
- Examples of stabilizer codes include the Steane [[7,1,3]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steane_code) code, the triorthogonal code, and the[ [5,1,3]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quasi-cyclic_code_(quantum)) quasi-cyclic code.
- Advantages: The stabilizer formalism provides an efficient way to describe certain quantum codes. The structure of stabilizer codes can help in analyzing their properties.
- Disadvantages: The stabilizer formalism does not apply to all quantum codes, and non-stabilizer codes may enable better performance. Not all quantum codes have an efficient description in terms of stabilizer operators.
- Mnemonics: "Stabilizer keeps the quantum state stable by its operators"
- To learn: Understand the definition of stabilizer operator and stabilizer group. Know how the codespace is obtained as the joint +1-eigenspace of the stabilizer group. Learn examples of stabilizer codes. Know the advantages and disadvantages.
