### Strong Sub Additivity for the notes of the Unit 5 - Quantum Error Correction in the subject of Quantum Computing

- Strong subadditivity is a key property that enables quantum error correction. It states that for any tripartite quantum system ABC, the quantum mutual information satisfies I(A:BC) ??? I(A:B) + I(A:C).
- In other words, the total correlation that A shares with the joint system BC is greater than or equal to the sum of correlations that A shares with B and C individually.
- This property implies that quantum information can be redundantly encoded across multiple quantum systems such that it is robust to losing some of the systems. The encoded quantum information can still be recovered as long as a sufficient subset of the systems is available.
- For example, a quantum bit (qubit) can be encoded across three physical qubits such that it can be recovered as long as at least two of the three physical qubits are available. This is the essence of quantum error-correcting codes.
- Strong subadditivity is a key reason why quantum error correction is possible, even though the no-cloning theorem prevents simply copying quantum information. By distributing quantum information across multiple systems, it can be made robust to losing some of the systems.
