### Limitations of the Quantum operations formalism for the notes of the Unit 4 - Quantum Information in the subject of Quantum Computing

The quantum operations formalism has the following limitations:

1. It does not capture the dynamics of quantum systems. The quantum operations formalism only describes how the state of a quantum system changes with discrete quantum operations. It does not describe how the state evolves continuously in time under a Hamiltonian. To describe dynamics, we need to use the quantum dynamical formalism involving quantum states and Hamiltonians.
2. It does not describe measurements. The quantum operations formalism only describes reversible transformations on quantum states. However, measurements are irreversible processes that provide us information about a quantum system. To fully describe quantum systems, we need to augment the quantum operations formalism with a description of quantum measurements.
3. It does not capture decoherence and noise. The quantum operations formalism assumes that quantum operations can be performed perfectly without any noise or decoherence. However, in practice, interactions with the environment leads to decoherence that cannot be captured by the idealized quantum operations. To model realistic quantum systems, we need to extend the quantum operations formalism to include the effects of decoherence and noise.

Some mnemonics for remembering the limitations are:

1. QOF only describes state changes, not dynamics (time evolution)
2. QOF misses measurements, which are irreversible
3. QOF assumes perfect operations, lacks decoherence and noise

The quantum operations formalism provides a useful abstract framework for thinking about manipulating and processing quantum information. However, we must recognize its limitations and use other formalisms such as the quantum dynamical formalism or decoherent quantum dynamical formalism to fully understand and model realistic quantum systems.
