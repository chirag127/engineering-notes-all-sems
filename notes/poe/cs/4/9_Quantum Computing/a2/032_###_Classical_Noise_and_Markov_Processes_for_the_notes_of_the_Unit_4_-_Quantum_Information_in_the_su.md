### Classical Noise and Markov Processes

- Classical noise refers to random fluctuations in a quantum system's environment that can disrupt the quantum state. This causes decoherence i.e. loss of quantum coherence.
- Some common sources of classical noise are:
-- Thermal fluctuations: Random thermal vibrations of atoms/molecules in the system's environment.
-- Random electromagnetic fields: Fluctuating electric and magnetic fields surrounding the system.
- To analyze the effect of classical noise, we model the environment as a Markov process. This means that the next state depends only on the present state and not the past states. The noise is assumed to be random and memoryless.
- Advantages of Markov process model:
-- It captures the key effects of classical noise like decoherence and relaxation.
-- It leads to a soluble mathematical framework (master equation) to study the dynamics of open quantum systems.
- Disadvantages:
-- It ignores memory effects and non-random components in the noise. The noise may not always be Markovian in practice.
-- The Markov approximation breaks down for strong system-environment coupling or non-weak external driving.
