### Nuclear Magnetic Resonance

Nuclear Magnetic Resonance (NMR) is a phenomenon in which nuclei in a magnetic field absorb and re-emit electromagnetic radiation. This is used in Nuclear Magnetic Resonance Spectroscopy to study the structure, dynamics, reaction state, and chemical composition of molecules.

#### How does NMR work?

- Nuclei with odd atomic mass numbers like 1H, 13C, 15N, 31P, etc. have spin and act like tiny magnets.
- When placed in a magnetic field, these nuclei align with the field and precess around it.
- Absorption of energy from an oscillating magnetic field causes the nuclei to flip and resonate at a frequency dependent on the strength of the magnetic field and the properties of the nucleus.
- This resonance is detected and used to identify the nucleus and its environment.

#### Advantages of NMR

- Non-destructive and non-invasive. Samples can be recovered.
- Provides detailed information about molecular structure.
- Can be used for a wide range of samples - liquids, solutions, solids, gases.
- Highly sensitive and can detect minor components in a mixture.

#### Applications of NMR

- Analysis of composition of mixtures and purity of compounds.
- Elucidation of 3D structure of complex biomolecules like proteins.
- Study of reaction mechanisms and dynamics.
- Magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) in medical diagnosis.
- Oil and gas exploration.
- Food quality control.
