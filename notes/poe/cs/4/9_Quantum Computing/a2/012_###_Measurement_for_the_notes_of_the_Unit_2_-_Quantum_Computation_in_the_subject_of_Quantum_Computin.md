### Measurement for the notes of the Unit 2 - Quantum Computation in the subject of Quantum Computing

Measurement is a process of obtaining information about a quantum system by performing measurements on it. Following are the key points about measurement in quantum computation -

1. Measurement causes the quantum system to collapse from a superposition of states to an eigenstate of the observable being measured. This is known as the measurement problem and results in the loss of quantum information.
2. The measurement process must be fast compared to the decoherence timescales of the system to obtain meaningful results. Slower measurements will lead to loss of quantum coherence and indistinguishable outcomes.
3. The measurement apparatus must be well isolated from the environment to avoid decoherence of the quantum system being measured. This is challenging to achieve in practice due to the fragile nature of quantum states.
4. The measurement process can be destructive or non-destructive. Destructive measurement disturbs the quantum state after measurement while non-destructive measurement can measure a quantum observable without disturbing the quantum state. Non-destructive measurements are more useful for quantum information processing tasks.
5. Measurement in quantum computation can be used to obtain outputs from a quantum algorithm, verify the functioning of a quantum processor and debug quantum programs. Efficient quantum measurements with high precision are key to build a scalable quantum computer.

Some Mnemonics for remembering the key points -

1. Qubit collapses - Superposition dies
2. Fast measure - Coherence survives
3. Isolate the system - Decoherence deprives
4. Destructive vs Non-destructive - Choose non-invasive
5. Measure outputs and verify - Scalable quantum achieve
