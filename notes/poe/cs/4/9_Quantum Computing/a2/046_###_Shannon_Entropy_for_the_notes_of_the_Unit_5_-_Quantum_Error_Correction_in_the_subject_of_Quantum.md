### Shannon Entropy for the notes of the Unit 5 - Quantum Error Correction in the subject of Quantum Computing

- Shannon Entropy is a measure of unpredictability or randomness of a system. It is used to quantify the amount of noise or errors in a quantum system.
- For a quantum system with states |0> and |1> and probabilities of being in these states as p(|0>) and p(|1>) respectively, the Shannon Entropy is given as:
H = -p(|0>)*log2[p(|0>)] - p(|1>)*log2[p(|1>)]
- The base of the logarithm can be taken as 2 since we are talking about binary states. The unit is bits.
- Higher the entropy, higher is the noise or errors. The goal of quantum error correction is to bring down the entropy and reduce errors.
- A good mnemonic to remember is: Entropy Entropies - More randomness means more mess. So reduce entropy to reduce errors.
- Shannon Entropy can be generalized for a system with more than 2 states. The formula will have a summation over the probabilities of all the states and log of each probability term.
- Diagrams and examples can be included to show how entropy changes with changing probabilities of states and how it helps in analyzing and correcting errors in quantum systems.
- The key advantages of using Shannon Entropy for quantum error correction are:
-- It is easy to calculate.
-- It captures all types of errors - bit flip, phase flip and bit-phase flip.
-- It can be measured experimentally and gives a quantitative measure of errors.
