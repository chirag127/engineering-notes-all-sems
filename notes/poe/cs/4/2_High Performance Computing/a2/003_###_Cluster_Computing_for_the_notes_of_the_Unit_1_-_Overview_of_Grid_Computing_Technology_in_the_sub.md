### Cluster Computing

- A cluster is a group of loosely coupled computers that work together so that in many respects they can be viewed as a single system.
- Clusters are used for parallel processing, which is dividing a large computational task among multiple computers to reduce the time required to complete the task.
- Cluster computing is a cost-effective way to deliver very high performance computational power. Instead of purchasing an expensive high-performance computer, multiple low-cost computers are combined into a cluster to provide computational power at a lower cost.
- Advantages:
    - Cost effectiveness - Using existing computers and networking components.
    - Scalability - Clusters can grow in performance and power by simply adding more computers.
    - High availability - With multiple computers, if one fails, the others can take over its work.
- Disadvantages:
    - Complex software - Special software is required to coordinate the activities of the computers and balance the workload.
    - Reliability - As the cluster grows larger, the chance of one of the computers failing increases. The software must be able to handle such failures gracefully.
    - Special administration - A cluster requires additional work to set up, administer, and maintain.
- Examples:
    - Beowulf cluster - A cluster of personal computers networked together to act as a single powerful computer.
    - Compute cluster - A group of servers or computers connected by a network that work together to perform intense computing tasks.
    - Data cluster - A group of servers that store and manage data, providing data and services to other systems.
- Applications:
    - High-performance computing - Simulations, data mining, computational science, etc.
    - Load balancing - Distributing workload across multiple computers to improve response time and throughput.
    - High availability - Critical applications can continue to operate with little or no downtime in the event of a failure.
