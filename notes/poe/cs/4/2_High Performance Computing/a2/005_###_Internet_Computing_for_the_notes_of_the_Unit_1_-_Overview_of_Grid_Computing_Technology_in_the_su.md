### Internet Computing

- Internet Computing refers to the use of the internet to harness the power of distributed computing resources to solve large-scale computational problems.
- In Internet Computing, the resources of multiple computers connected via the internet are pooled together to solve a problem collaboratively. This is also known as Grid Computing.
- The key characteristics of Internet Computing are:
    - Heterogeneity: The resources are diverse and heterogeneous in nature like different architectures, operating systems, networks, etc.
    - Autonomy: The resources are independent and autonomous. They voluntarily join and leave the grid.
    - Distribution: The resources are geographically distributed across multiple administrative domains connected via the internet.
    - Scalability: It is possible to aggregate more resources to tackle bigger problems. The grid can scale dynamically.
    - Fault Tolerance: The grid must be fault tolerant as resources can fail or leave at any time. The tasks must be checkpointed and reassigned to other resources.
- The key components of an Internet Computing system are:
    - Resources: The computers, servers, sensors, etc that contribute their spare resources to the grid.
    - Resource Brokers: Match resources to tasks and tasks to resources.
    - Software Frameworks: Enable secure sharing, coordination, and monitoring of resources. Example: Globus Toolkit.
    - Applications: The problems that are solved using the pooled resources like parameter sweep, data mining, etc.
- Advantages: Increased computing power at lower cost, efficient utilization of resources, collaborative solving of large problems.
- Disadvantages: Complex to develop and manage, heterogeneous resources lead to compatibility issues, security and policy issues in resource sharing across domains.
- Applications: Drug design, weather forecasting, physics simulations, server load balancing, etc.
