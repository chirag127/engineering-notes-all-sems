### Peer‐to‐Peer Computing

- Peer-to-peer (P2P) computing refers to the decentralized interactions between peer nodes in a network.
- In a P2P network, the nodes function both as clients and servers. The nodes share resources and services directly with each other, without the need for centralized administrative systems.
- The P2P network does not rely on centralized servers and the nodes can join or leave the network at will. The resources and services are distributed across the network and the workload is shared by all the nodes.
- Some of the advantages of P2P networks are scalability, robustness, and efficient resource utilization. However, issues like security, anonymity, fairness, and trust management are challenges in P2P networks.
- Popular P2P applications include file sharing systems, distributed computing systems, and VoIP systems. Bitcoin is a well-known decentralized P2P payment network.

**Mnemonics:**

- P2P: Peer to Peer,
- Nodes act as clients and servers,
- No centralized control,
- Resources and services distributed and shared.

**Learning tricks:**

- Think of P2P network as a decentralized network of friends sharing resources and helping each other. There is no central authority organizing things.
- The workload is distributed across all the nodes in the network, just like work is distributed and shared among friends.
- The challenges in P2P networks are similar to challenges faced in decentralized friend networks - security, anonymity, trust, etc.
