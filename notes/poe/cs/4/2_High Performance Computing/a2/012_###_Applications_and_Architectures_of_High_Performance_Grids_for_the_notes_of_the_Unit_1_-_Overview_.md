### Applications of High Performance Grids

- Scientific and Engineering Applications: Simulation of complex physical and biological systems, seismic analysis, aircraft design, weather forecasting, etc. These applications require large amounts of computing power and data storage.
- Large-scale Data Analysis: Astronomy, high energy physics, bioinformatics, etc. deal with extremely large data sets that need to be analyzed, simulated and visualized. Grids provide the infrastructure to share and process these data sets.
- Distributed Supercomputing: Grids allow leveraging geographically distributed computing resources to solve Grand Challenge problems that require more computing power than available with any single system.

### Architectures of High Performance Grids

- Computational Grid: Focuses on sharing of heterogeneous computational resources and delivering high performance for compute-intensive applications. Eg: SETI@home.
- Data Grid: Focuses on sharing, selection, replication and migration of large data collections and delivering efficient access to these data. Eg: NASA's Information Power Grid.
- Service Grid: Focuses on sharing specialized services and resources in addition to computational and data resources. Eg: Biomedical Informatics Research Network (BIRN).

Advantages: Scalability, heterogeneity, resource sharing, cost-effectiveness, virtual organizations.
Disadvantages: Complex software, security, resource management, fault tolerance.

Examples: Worldwide LHC Computing Grid, NASA's Information Power Grid, EU DataGrid, PlanetLab, SETI@home.

Applications: Scientific and engineering simulations, large-scale data analysis, parameter sweep studies, distributed supercomputing.
