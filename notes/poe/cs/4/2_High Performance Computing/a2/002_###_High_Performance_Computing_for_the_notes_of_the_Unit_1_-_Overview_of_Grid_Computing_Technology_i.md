### High Performance Computing for the notes of the Unit 1 - Overview of Grid Computing Technology in the subject of High Performance Computing

- High Performance Computing (HPC) is the use of parallel processing techniques to solve complex computational problems. HPC systems are designed for running applications as fast as possible.
- Grid Computing is a type of distributed computing that combines computer resources from multiple administrative domains to reach a common goal. It is focused on large scale resource sharing, innovative applications, and heterogeneous and distributed systems.
- Grid Computing enables the virtualization and sharing of distributed heterogeneous resources such as supercomputers, compute clusters, data storage systems, data sources, and specialized devices.
- The key characteristics of Grid Computing are:
    - Heterogeneity - The resources are diverse and geographically distributed.
    - Autonomy - The resources are independently managed.
    - Distribution - The resources are connected over a network.
    - Coordinated problem solving - The resources cooperate to solve large-scale problems.
- The goals of Grid Computing are:
    - Increase resource utilization and reduce idle times.
    - Provide users with transparent access to resources.
    - Enable new ways of working together and new applications.
- The key concepts and components of a Grid Computing system are:
    - Virtual Organizations - Mechanism to share resources among a dynamic set of individuals and institutions.
    - Resource Brokering - Mechanism to allocate resources to users based on policies.
    - Data Management - Mechanism to replicate, locate, and access data across distributed resources.
    - Security - Mechanism to authenticate users and control access to resources.
    - Monitoring and Discovery - Mechanism to discover available resources and monitor their status.
- Some advantages of Grid Computing are increased computing power, efficient utilization of resources, collaboration, and economical access to resources. Some disadvantages are complex software, security issues, unreliable resources, and difficult data management.
- Grid Computing has applications in scientific computing, parameter sweep experiments, high throughput computing, collaboration, and enterprise computing.
