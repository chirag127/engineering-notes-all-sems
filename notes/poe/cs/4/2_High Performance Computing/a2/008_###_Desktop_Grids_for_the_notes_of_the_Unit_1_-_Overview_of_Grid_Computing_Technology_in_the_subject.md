### Desktop Grids

- Desktop Grids utilize the unused computational resources of computers connected over the Internet to solve large-scale computation problems.
- The computers that donate their resources are called **hosts** or **nodes**.
- The software that is used to coordinate the resources and distribute the tasks is called **middleware**.
- The key characteristics of Desktop Grids are:
    - Heterogeneous systems with different hardware and software configurations.
    - Volatile resources as hosts can join or leave the grid at any time.
    - Resources are utilized only when hosts are idle.
- Some advantages of Desktop Grids are:
    - Inexpensive as they utilize already available resources.
    - Scalable as more hosts can join to provide more resources.
- Some disadvantages of Desktop Grids are:
    - No guarantees on resource availability and performance.
    - Security and privacy concerns as hosts are not under a single administrative control.
- Example Desktop Grid systems are BOINC, XtremWeb, OurGrid, etc.
- Desktop Grids are suitable for loosely-coupled and bag-of-tasks applications like parameter sweeps, Monte Carlo simulations, protein folding, etc.

- A mnemonic to remember the key characteristics of Desktop Grids:
    - Heterogeneous, Volatile, Idle resources (HVIdle)

- To learn the components of Desktop Grids:
    - Imagine hosts as personal computers connected to the Internet.
    - Think of middleware as the software that coordinates the hosts.
    - Visualize tasks getting distributed to the hosts through the middleware and results getting collected back.
