### Peer-to-Peer Computing for the notes of the Unit 1 - Overview of Grid Computing Technology in the subject of High Performance Computing

Peer-to-peer (P2P) computing is a distributed computing paradigm that enables nodes in a network to share resources and collaborate with one another without the need for a centralized server. In P2P computing, each node in the network serves both as a client and a server, and can initiate or respond to requests from other nodes.

#### Characteristics of P2P Computing

- Decentralized: P2P computing does not rely on a centralized server to manage and control the network. Instead, each node in the network operates independently and collaborates with others in a peer-to-peer fashion.

- Self-organizing: P2P networks are self-organizing, meaning that nodes can join or leave the network at any time without disrupting the overall operation of the network.

- Scalable: P2P computing is highly scalable, as the addition of new nodes to the network can increase its overall capacity and performance.

- Fault-tolerant: P2P networks are inherently fault-tolerant, as the failure of a single node does not affect the overall operation of the network.

#### Advantages of P2P Computing

- Resource sharing: P2P computing enables nodes in the network to share resources such as processing power, storage, and bandwidth, which can lead to increased efficiency and reduced costs.

- Robustness: P2P networks are highly robust and resilient, as they can continue to operate even in the face of node failures or network congestion.

- Scalability: P2P networks can be easily scaled by adding new nodes to the network, which can increase its overall capacity and performance.

#### Disadvantages of P2P Computing

- Security: P2P networks can be vulnerable to security threats such as malware, distributed denial-of-service (DDoS) attacks, and unauthorized access.

- Quality of service: P2P networks do not provide guaranteed quality of service, as the performance of the network can vary depending on the number and behavior of nodes in the network.

#### Applications of P2P Computing

- File sharing: P2P networks are commonly used for file sharing, as they enable users to share files with one another without the need for a centralized server.

- Content distribution: P2P networks can be used for content distribution, as they enable large files or data sets to be distributed across multiple nodes in the network.

- Distributed computing: P2P networks can also be used for distributed computing, as they enable nodes in the network to collaborate on computation-intensive tasks.

#### Mnemonic

One possible mnemonic to remember the characteristics of P2P computing is "DSSR", which stands for Decentralized, Self-organizing, Scalable, and Fault-tolerant.
