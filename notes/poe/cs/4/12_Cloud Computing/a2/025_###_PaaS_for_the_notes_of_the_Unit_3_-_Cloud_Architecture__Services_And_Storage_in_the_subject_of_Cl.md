### PaaS

- PaaS stands for Platform as a Service. It provides a computing platform and a solution stack as a service.
- In PaaS, the consumer does not manage or control the underlying cloud infrastructure but has control over the deployed applications and possibly application hosting environment configurations.
- Providers offer computing solutions stack, operating system, programming-language execution environment, database, and web server as a service.
- Consumer does not manage or control OS, hardware, servers, storage, or networking resources.
- Instead, consumer focuses on management of their applications and services.
- Examples: Heroku, Engine Yard, Google App Engine, Azure, etc.
- Advantages: No cost of infrastructure, easy deployment, auto-scaling, high availability.
- Disadvantages: Vendor lock-in, limited customization.
- Applications: Websites, Web apps, Mobile backends.

Some tips to learn:

- Remember PaaS as Platform as a Service. The platform is provided as a service to deploy applications.
- Focus is on applications, not infrastructure. Providers manage the infrastructure.
- Heroku and Google App Engine are popular PaaS providers.
