### Wireless Networks

Wireless networks enable mobile computing devices to connect to the Internet or other network without the need for physical cables. This allows for greater mobility and convenience. Some key points about wireless networks:

- WiFi is a common wireless network technology that uses radio waves to provide wireless high-speed Internet and network connections. WiFi hotspots can be found in many public places like coffee shops, airports, etc. WiFi uses the IEEE 802.11 standards (802.11a/b/g/n/ax).
- Bluetooth is a short-range wireless technology standard for exchanging data over short distances (up to 30 feet) using radio waves. It is often used to connect wireless headphones, speakers, keyboards, etc. to mobile devices.
- Cellular networks provide wireless data and voice connectivity over a wide area using radio frequencies and cell towers. This includes 3G, 4G LTE, and 5G networks which provide mobile Internet connectivity.
- Wireless networks have the advantage of mobility and convenience but can have lower bandwidth and security vulnerabilities compared to wired networks. Interference from other devices can also affect performance.
- To secure wireless networks, ensure strong passwords, encryption, MAC address filtering, etc. are implemented. VPNs can also be used to secure connections and communications over wireless networks.

Some mnemonics for remembering WiFi standards:
- **a** gift for **a** ll - 802.11**a** uses 5 GHz band
- **b** etter go **b** uy - 802.11**b** uses 2.4 GHz band, slower than 802.11a
- **g** oing at **g** igabit - 802.11**g** uses 2.4 GHz band, faster than 802.11b
- **n** ew and even **n** ew - 802.11**n** uses both 2.4 and 5 GHz bands, faster and wider range than a/b/g
- **ax** ing old standards - 802.11**ax** (WiFi 6) is the latest standard, fastest speeds and more efficient
