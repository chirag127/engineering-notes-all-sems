### Channel Allocation in Cellular Systems

- Cellular systems divide the service area into small geographical areas called cells. Each cell is served by a fixed-location transceiver known as a base station.
- Adjacent cells use different channels to avoid interference. This is known as frequency reuse.
- Channel allocation is the process of assigning channels to the cells in a way that minimizes interference.
- Fixed channel allocation assigns a fixed set of channels to each cell. This is simple but can lead to inefficiency.
- Dynamic channel allocation allocates channels dynamically based on traffic. This can improve spectrum utilization but requires coordination between base stations.
- Hybrid schemes that combine fixed and dynamic channel allocation are commonly used.
- Frequency planning is the process of selecting frequencies and allocating them to cells to minimize interference. This is a complex optimization problem.
- Learning trick: Remember FCC for fixed channel allocation, DCA for dynamic channel allocation and hybrid for hybrid schemes.

Advantages of dynamic and hybrid channel allocation:
- Higher spectrum efficiency and reduced interference leading to increased capacity
- Ability to handle varying traffic loads

Disadvantages:
- Increased complexity of system and base stations
- Requirement of tight coordination between base stations

Applications: Used in 2G, 3G and 4G cellular systems to efficiently utilize spectrum and serve varying user demands.

Examples: GSM uses a hybrid of fixed and dynamic channel allocation.
