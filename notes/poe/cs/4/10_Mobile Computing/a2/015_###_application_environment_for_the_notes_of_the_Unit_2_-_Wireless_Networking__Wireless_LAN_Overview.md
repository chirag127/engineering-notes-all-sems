### Application environment for the notes of the Unit 2 - Wireless Networking, Wireless LAN Overview: MAC issues, IEEE 802.11, Blue Tooth, Wireless in the subject of Mobile Computing

- Wireless networking enables devices to communicate without the need for cables or wires. This allows devices to be mobile and stay connected to the network.
- The medium access control (MAC) layer is responsible for sharing the wireless medium between multiple devices. This requires coordination to avoid collisions and ensure timely delivery of data.
- The IEEE 802.11 standard (Wi-Fi) is the dominant wireless LAN technology. It defines specifications for the MAC layer and physical layer (PHY). The most widely used variants are 802.11a, b, g, and n, which specify different frequency bands and maximum speeds.
- Bluetooth is a short-range wireless technology primarily designed for personal area networks. It is commonly used to connect nearby devices such as headphones, speakers, and other peripherals to computers, tablets, and smartphones.
- Wireless networking has the advantage of convenience but can face interference and security issues due to the open nature of the wireless medium. Strong encryption and authentication are important for wireless networks.

Some useful mnemonics/tips:
- IEEE 802.11: Think "eight-oh-two-eleven"
- 802.11 variants: "a" is for Apple (5 GHz), "b" is for Bit (2.4 GHz), "g" is for Google (2.4 GHz, faster than b), "n" is for Nitro (can use 2.4 GHz and/or 5 GHz, fastest)
- Bluetooth: Think "blue tooth" as in a tooth that's blue in color. The Bluetooth logo is also a combination of the Nordic runes representing the letters H and B for Harald Bluetooth.
