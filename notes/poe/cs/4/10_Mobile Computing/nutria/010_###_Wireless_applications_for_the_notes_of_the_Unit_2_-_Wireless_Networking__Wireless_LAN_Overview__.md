
### Wireless applications for the notes of the Unit 2 - Wireless Networking, Wireless LAN Overview: MAC issues, IEEE 802.11, Blue Tooth, Wireless in the subject of Mobile Computing

Wireless applications are used to enable communication between two or more devices without the need for a physical connection. This type of communication is useful for applications that require mobility, such as mobile phones, laptops, and tablets. The most commonly used wireless communication protocols are IEEE 802.11, Bluetooth, and Wireless LAN.

#### IEEE 802.11
IEEE 802.11 is a standard for wireless local area networks (WLANs). It is used to enable communication between computers, laptops, and other devices that are within a certain range of each other. The most common version of this protocol is 802.11n, which offers speeds of up to 600 Mbps.

#### Bluetooth
Bluetooth is a wireless technology used to enable communication between two or more devices. It is most commonly used for short-range communication, such as connecting a wireless headset to a mobile phone, or connecting a computer to a printer. It is also used for connecting devices to the internet, such as a laptop to a Wi-Fi router.

#### Wireless LAN
A wireless LAN (WLAN) is a type of local area network (LAN) that uses radio waves to enable communication between two or more devices. WLANs are used to provide wireless internet access in homes, offices, and public areas. They are also used to connect devices, such as laptops and tablets, to the internet.

#### MAC Issues
A MAC (Media Access Control) address is a unique identifier assigned to each device in a network. It is used to identify the device on the network and ensure that data is sent to the correct device. MAC addresses can be used to restrict access to a network, as well as to track devices on the network.

#### Advantages and Disadvantages
Wireless applications offer a number of advantages over wired connections, such as increased mobility and the ability to connect devices without the need for physical cables. However, wireless connections are also more vulnerable to interference, and the signal can be weakened by walls or other objects.

#### Mnemonics and Learning Tricks
When learning about wireless applications, it can be helpful to remember the following mnemonics:

- **IEEE 802.11**: "IEEE" stands for "Institute of Electrical and Electronics Engineers."
- **Bluetooth**: "Blue" stands for the color of the logo, and "tooth" is a reference to the tooth-like shape of the logo.
- **Wireless LAN**: "Wireless" refers to the lack of physical cables, and "LAN" stands for "local area network."
- **MAC Issues**: "MAC" stands for "media access control."
