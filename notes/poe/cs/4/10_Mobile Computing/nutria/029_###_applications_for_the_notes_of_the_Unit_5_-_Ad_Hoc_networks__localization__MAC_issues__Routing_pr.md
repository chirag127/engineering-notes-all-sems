

### Applications for the Notes of the Unit 5 - Ad Hoc Networks, Localization, MAC Issues, Routing Protocols, Global State Routing (GSR), in the Subject of Mobile Computing

* Ad Hoc Networks: Ad hoc networks are wireless networks which are established without the aid of any centralized access point. They are self-organizing and self-configuring, meaning that nodes can join or leave the network at any time. Ad hoc networks are used in situations where a permanent infrastructure is not available, such as in disaster relief or military operations.

* Localization: Localization is the process of determining the physical location of a node in a network. This information can be used to provide services such as navigation, or to optimize the performance of the network.

* MAC Issues: Medium Access Control (MAC) is a protocol which regulates the access of nodes to the wireless medium. It is responsible for ensuring that nodes do not interfere with each other’s transmissions, and for providing fair access to the wireless medium.

* Routing Protocols: Routing protocols are responsible for finding paths through the network which allow data to be transmitted from one node to another. Different routing protocols have different characteristics, such as the amount of control they provide over the path taken by the data.

* Global State Routing (GSR): GSR is a routing protocol which is designed to provide a global view of the network. It is based on the concept of “flooding”, where each node in the network broadcasts its own state to all other nodes in the network. GSR is often used in ad hoc networks, where the topology of the network is constantly changing.
