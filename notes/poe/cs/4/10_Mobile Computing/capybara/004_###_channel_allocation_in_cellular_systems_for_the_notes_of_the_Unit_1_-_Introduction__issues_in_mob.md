### Channel Allocation in Cellular Systems

In cellular systems, the available frequency spectrum is divided into smaller frequency bands, known as channels. These channels are then allocated to mobile users to establish a connection and facilitate communication. The process of allocating channels in a cellular system is known as channel allocation.

There are three types of channel allocation techniques - fixed channel allocation, dynamic channel allocation, and hybrid channel allocation.

#### Fixed Channel Allocation

In fixed channel allocation, a fixed set of channels is allocated to each cell in the network. The channels are then further divided among the mobile users in the cell. This technique is simple and easy to implement, but it is not efficient as channels may remain idle in cells where there are no users, leading to wastage of resources.

#### Dynamic Channel Allocation

In dynamic channel allocation, channels are allocated to mobile users on demand. The base station monitors the traffic in the cell and dynamically assigns channels to users as and when required. This technique is more efficient than fixed channel allocation as it reduces channel wastage. However, it requires more complex algorithms for channel assignment.

#### Hybrid Channel Allocation

In hybrid channel allocation, a combination of fixed and dynamic channel allocation techniques is used. A fixed set of channels is allocated to each cell, and additional channels are allocated dynamically as and when required. This technique provides a balance between efficiency and simplicity.

#### Channel Allocation in GSM

GSM (Global System for Mobile Communications) is a widely used cellular system. In GSM, the frequency spectrum is divided into 124 carrier frequencies, each of which is further divided into eight time slots. Each time slot can support one conversation, and thus, a single carrier frequency can support up to eight conversations simultaneously.

GSM uses a hybrid channel allocation technique, where a fixed set of frequencies is allocated to each cell, and additional frequencies are assigned dynamically as and when required. The base station uses a complex algorithm known as the Dynamic Channel Allocation (DCA) algorithm to allocate channels to users in the cell.
