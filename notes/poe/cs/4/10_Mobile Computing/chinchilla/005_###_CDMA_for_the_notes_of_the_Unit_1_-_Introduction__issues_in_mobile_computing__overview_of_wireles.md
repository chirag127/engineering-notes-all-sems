### CDMA for the Notes of Unit 1 - Introduction, Issues in Mobile Computing, Overview of Wireless Telephony: Cellular Concept, GSM

CDMA or Code Division Multiple Access is a digital cellular technology that transmits multiple signals simultaneously over a single communication channel. This technology is widely used in mobile communication systems around the world, including 3G and 4G networks. Below are some of the key points to understand CDMA technology for the Mobile Computing subject:

1. CDMA uses a unique code to differentiate between different users and signals. Each user has a unique code that is assigned to them, which allows multiple users to transmit and receive signals at the same time and on the same frequency band.

2. The CDMA technology uses spread spectrum techniques to spread the signal across a wide frequency band. This allows for more efficient use of the frequency spectrum, as well as higher security and privacy for users.

3. One of the main advantages of CDMA is its ability to provide better call quality and fewer dropped calls. This is because the technology can handle more users simultaneously than other cellular technologies, such as GSM.

4. CDMA also offers better data transmission speeds and capacity, making it more suitable for data-intensive applications, such as video streaming, online gaming, and other high-bandwidth activities.

5. CDMA technology is widely used in North America and parts of Asia, including Japan and South Korea. However, it is less popular in Europe and other parts of the world, where GSM is the dominant cellular technology.

6. A popular example of CDMA technology is the CDMA2000 network, which is used by many mobile carriers around the world, including Verizon and Sprint in the United States.

In summary, CDMA technology is a key component of modern mobile communication systems, offering better call quality, data transmission speeds, and capacity than other cellular technologies. Its unique coding scheme and spread spectrum techniques make it a secure and efficient way to transmit multiple signals simultaneously over a single channel.
