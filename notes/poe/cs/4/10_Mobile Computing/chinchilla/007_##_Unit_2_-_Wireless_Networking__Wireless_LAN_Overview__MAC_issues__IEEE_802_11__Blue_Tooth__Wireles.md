## Unit 2 - Wireless Networking, Wireless LAN Overview: MAC issues, IEEE 802.11, Blue Tooth, Wireless

Wireless Local Area Network (WLAN) is a type of wireless networking that is used to connect devices within a limited area, such as a building, campus, or home. In this unit, we will discuss the overview of WLAN, including MAC issues, IEEE 802.11 standards, Bluetooth, and wireless technologies.

### MAC Issues

Media Access Control (MAC) issues are the challenges that arise in wireless networking due to the shared medium. The shared medium refers to the wireless frequency that is used by all the devices to send and receive data. Some of the MAC issues in WLAN are:

- Hidden Node Problem: It occurs when two wireless devices are out of range of each other, but they are within range of the access point (AP). As a result, they cannot detect each other's presence and may transmit data simultaneously, leading to a collision.
- Exposed Node Problem: It occurs when a wireless device is within range of the AP and another device, but it refrains from transmitting data because it senses the other device's transmission. This can lead to a decrease in the network's throughput.
- Access Point Congestion: It occurs when too many devices try to connect to a single access point, leading to congestion and a decrease in the network's throughput.

### IEEE 802.11 Standards

The IEEE 802.11 standards are a set of protocols that define how WLANs should operate. Some of the IEEE 802.11 standards are:

- IEEE 802.11a: It operates in the 5 GHz frequency band and supports a maximum data rate of up to 54 Mbps.
- IEEE 802.11b: It operates in the 2.4 GHz frequency band and supports a maximum data rate of up to 11 Mbps.
- IEEE 802.11g: It operates in the 2.4 GHz frequency band and supports a maximum data rate of up to 54 Mbps.
- IEEE 802.11n: It operates in both the 2.4 GHz and 5 GHz frequency bands and supports a maximum data rate of up to 600 Mbps.
- IEEE 802.11ac: It operates in the 5 GHz frequency band and supports a maximum data rate of up to 6.77 Gbps.

### Bluetooth

Bluetooth is a wireless technology that is used to connect devices over short distances. It operates in the 2.4 GHz frequency band and supports a maximum data rate of up to 3 Mbps. Some of the advantages of Bluetooth are:

- Low power consumption
- Low cost
- Easy to use

However, some of the disadvantages of Bluetooth are:

- Limited range
- Interference from other devices in the 2.4 GHz frequency band

### Wireless Technologies

There are several wireless technologies used in WLAN. Some of them are:

- Infrared (IR): It uses light waves in the infrared range to transmit data between devices.
- ZigBee: It is a low-power wireless technology that is used for communication between devices in a wireless personal area network (WPAN).
- Wi-Fi: It is a wireless technology that is used for WLANs and operates in the 2.4 GHz and 5 GHz frequency bands.
- WiMAX: It is a wireless technology that is used for metropolitan area networks (MANs) and supports a maximum data rate of up to 70 Mbps.

Mnemonics and learning tricks:

- To remember the IEEE 802.11 standards, you can use the phrase "A Big Gig Noted by All": A for IEEE 802.11a, B for IEEE 802.11b, G for IEEE 802.11g, N for IEEE 802.11n, and AC for IEEE 802.11ac.
- To remember the advantages of Bluetooth, you can use the acronym "LE": Low power consumption and Low cost.
