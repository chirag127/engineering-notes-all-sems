### Application Environment for the Notes of Unit 2 - Wireless Networking, Wireless LAN Overview: MAC issues, IEEE 802.11, Bluetooth, Wireless in the Subject of Mobile Computing

Wireless networking has become an essential part of our daily lives, particularly in mobile computing. In this unit, we will discuss the application environment of wireless networking, covering topics such as wireless LAN overview, MAC issues, IEEE 802.11, Bluetooth, and wireless technologies.

#### Wireless LAN Overview

A wireless LAN (WLAN) is a type of local area network that uses wireless radio signals to transmit data between devices. WLANs are commonly used in environments where cables are difficult to install, such as in homes, offices, and public places.

Some important aspects to consider in WLAN overview are:

- WLAN architecture: The architecture of a WLAN can be centralized, where a wireless access point (WAP) acts as a hub for all wireless devices, or decentralized, where devices communicate with each other directly.
- WLAN topology: The topology of a WLAN can be ad-hoc, where devices communicate with each other directly without any centralized infrastructure, or infrastructure, where devices communicate with each other through a WAP.
- WLAN security: WLANs are vulnerable to security threats such as eavesdropping, unauthorized access, and denial of service attacks. Therefore, it is essential to implement security measures such as encryption, authentication, and access control.

#### MAC Issues

The media access control (MAC) layer is responsible for controlling access to the wireless medium in a WLAN. Some important MAC issues to consider are:

- Hidden node problem: This occurs when two devices cannot directly communicate with each other because they are out of range or blocked by an obstacle.
- Exposed node problem: This occurs when a device refrains from transmitting data because it assumes that another device is already using the wireless medium.
- Fairness: This refers to the fair distribution of access to the wireless medium among all devices.

#### IEEE 802.11

IEEE 802.11 is a set of standards for WLANs established by the Institute of Electrical and Electronics Engineers (IEEE). Some important features of IEEE 802.11 are:

- Frequency bands: IEEE 802.11 operates in two frequency bands: 2.4 GHz and 5 GHz.
- Data rates: IEEE 802.11 supports various data rates depending on the modulation scheme and channel bandwidth.
- Security: IEEE 802.11 provides several security mechanisms such as Wired Equivalent Privacy (WEP), Wi-Fi Protected Access (WPA), and Wi-Fi Protected Access II (WPA2).

#### Bluetooth

Bluetooth is a wireless technology used for short-range communication between devices. Some important features of Bluetooth are:

- Range: Bluetooth has a short range of up to 10 meters.
- Speed: Bluetooth supports data rates of up to 3 Mbps.
- Profiles: Bluetooth supports various profiles such as Headset Profile (HSP), Phone Book Access Profile (PBAP), and Advanced Audio Distribution Profile (A2DP).

#### Wireless Technologies

Wireless technologies are evolving rapidly, and many new technologies are being developed to meet the increasing demand for wireless communication. Some important wireless technologies to consider are:

- Wi-Fi 6: Wi-Fi 6 is the latest version of the IEEE 802.11 standard, which offers higher data rates, improved performance in crowded environments, and better power efficiency.
- 5G: 5G is the fifth generation of wireless technology, which offers higher data rates, lower latency, and better reliability than previous generations.
- Zigbee: Zigbee is a wireless technology used for low-power, low-data-rate communication between devices in home automation, industrial control, and other applications.

In conclusion, wireless networking has become an essential part of mobile computing, and it is important to understand the application environment, MAC issues, IEEE 802.11, Bluetooth, and other wireless technologies to design and deploy wireless systems effectively.
