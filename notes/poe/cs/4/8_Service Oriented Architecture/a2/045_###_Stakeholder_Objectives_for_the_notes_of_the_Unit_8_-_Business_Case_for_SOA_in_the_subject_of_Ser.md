### Stakeholder Objectives for the notes of the Unit 8 - Business Case for SOA in the subject of Service Oriented Architecture

1. Business:
- Reduce IT costs through reuse of services and increased operational efficiency
- Increase business agility through faster development and deployment of new applications/services
- Enable new business models and revenue streams through exposing business services/data to partners and customers

2. Developers:
- Increase developer productivity through service reuse and reduced integration effort
- Enable focus on business logic rather than infrastructure/integration concerns

3. IT Operations:
- Increase manageability and governance through a service-oriented approach
- Enable increased automation of management tasks through management interfaces for services

Some key benefits of SOA from a business perspective are:

- Increased business agility through faster creation and changes to services
- Improved ROI through reuse of services
- Enablement of new business models (e.g. business services/data exposed to partners/customers)

From a technical perspective key benefits include:

- Increased developer productivity through reduced integration effort (service reuse)
- Platform/infrastructure independence through loose coupling
- Improved manageability and governance through a service-oriented approach

However, it's important to note that SOA adoption does bring some challenges, such as:

- Governance overhead in managing services
- Performance implications of service-oriented architectures
- Version control and impact analysis of changes
- Potential vendor lock-in due to proprietary standards or technologies

 Mnemonics:

SOA - Save Our Assets (time, money, resources)

SOAP - Simple Object Access Protocol

WSDL - Web Services Description Language

Learning tricks:
