### Return on Investment (ROI) for the notes of the Unit 8 - Business Case for SOA in the subject of Service Oriented Architecture

- ROI is the benefit an organization gets in return for investing in a service oriented architecture (SOA). Some of the benefits and ROI of adopting SOA are:
- Reduced Integration Costs: SOA enables reuse of services which reduces the integration costs as the same services can be reused by multiple applications. This reduces the efforts and costs involved in integrating applications.
- Increased Agility: SOA makes applications more flexible to changes. The services can be reconfigured and reused to cater to changing business needs. This increases the agility and speed of change for the organization.
- Improved Productivity: With SOA, the development is more streamlined through reusable services. This reduces the development time and effort, thereby increasing the productivity of the enterprise.
- Business & IT Alignment: SOA provides a business centric view of IT infrastructure. This increases the alignment between business and IT as business processes and services are modeled around business functions rather than technical applications. This makes it easier for business to understand and leverage the IT capabilities.
- Vendor Independence: With SOA, applications are built using standards based services. This makes the applications independent of vendor products and technologies. The applications can work with services from any vendor, thereby increasing independence and avoiding vendor lock-in.

Some mnemonics and learning tricks for remembering the ROI of SOA:

- Reduces integration COST (COST)
- Increases business AGILITY (AGILE)
- Improves developer PRODUCTIVITY (PRODUCT)
- Aligns BUSINESS and IT (BUSINESS)
- Provides VENDOR independence (VENDOR)

The mnemonics map the first letter of each benefit to spell out COST, AGILE, PRODUCT, BUSINESS, VENDOR which can make it easier to remember the key benefits and ROI of adopting a service oriented architecture.
