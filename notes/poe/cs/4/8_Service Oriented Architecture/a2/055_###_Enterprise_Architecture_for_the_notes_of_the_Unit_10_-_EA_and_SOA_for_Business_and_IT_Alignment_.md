### Enterprise Architecture for the notes of the Unit 10 - EA and SOA for Business and IT Alignment in the subject of Service Oriented Architecture

Enterprise Architecture (EA) is a comprehensive framework used to describe and map the architecture of an organization. It integrates business aspects with information technology to achieve strategic business goals.

Key points about EA:

- It provides a holistic view of the enterprise and helps align business and IT.
- It enables effective communication across various layers of the organization.
- It allows optimization of resources and reduces wastage.
- It facilitates incremental changes and evolution of the enterprise.

Methods to develop EA:

- Top-down approach: Starting from business strategy to drive IT strategy.
- Bottom-up approach: Starting from existing IT systems and mapping to business goals.
- Meet-in-the-middle approach: Combining top-down and bottom-up approaches.

EA frameworks:

- The Zachman Framework: Focuses on interrogatives (what, how, when, who, why, where) and provides a holistic perspective.
- The TOGAF Framework: Provides a methodological approach to EA using ADM (Architecture Development Method).
- The Gartner Framework: Classifies architecture into business, data, application, and technology domains.

Significance of EA for SOA and Business-IT alignment:

- EA acts as a blueprint to identify service candidates and assist in service orientation.
- It helps in optimizing SOA resources and reducing redundancy.
- It enables mapping of business goals to SOA and ensures alignment.
- It provides a roadmap for SOA evolution and helps in managing changes.

**Mnemonics:**

- EACH (acronym) - Enterprise Architecture Communicates Holistically
- WT-DM (acronym) - Zachman interrogatives: What, How, When, Who, Why, Where, Do, Manage

**Advantages:** Strategic planning, cost optimization, standardized communication, controlled evolution.
**Disadvantages:** Complex to develop, time-consuming, requires skilled resources.
**Applications:** Enterprise transformation, mergers and acquisitions, regulatory compliance.
