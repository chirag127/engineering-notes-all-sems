### Service Orientation in Daily Life

- Services are all around us in daily life:
- Electricity service: We get electricity supply at home which powers appliances. The electricity generation companies provide the service and we consume it.
- Internet service: We get internet connectivity at home through internet service providers. They provide the internet service and we use it to access content and resources.
- Transport service: We use transportation services like buses, trains, flights to travel from one place to another. The transport companies provide the services and we utilize them to commute.
- Banking service: Banks provide various banking services like savings account, loans, credit cards, etc. We use these services for financial transactions and money management.

Thus, the concept of loosely coupled services which can be consumed by users is prevalent in the outside world. In Service Oriented Architecture, applications are built as services which can be discovered and consumed by other applications, mimicking the service orientation in daily life.

**Advantages**: The systems built on microservices are flexible, scalable, modular and interoperable similar to real-world services. They can be reused and repurposed for other uses easily.

**Disadvantages**: There is additional overhead in building, deploying and managing numerous microservices. Integration and synchronization between services can be challenging. Fault tolerance and resilience needs extra effort.

**Applications**: Service Oriented Architecture is best suited for large, complex systems which need to be highly scalable and flexible. Examples are e-commerce applications, banking systems, airline reservation systems, etc.
