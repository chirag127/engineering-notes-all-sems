## Unit 1 - Introduction: SOA and MSA Basics

- Service-Oriented Architecture (SOA) is a style of software design where applications are built as a suite of interoperable services. These services are loosely coupled and can be used within multiple computing domains.
- The key benefits of SOA are:
- Increased interoperability: Services can be accessed across a network, allowing systems and applications to interoperate.
- Increased reusability: Services can be reused across domains and applications, reducing duplication.
- Decoupling: Services have platform-independent interfaces, allowing them to be developed, deployed and scaled independently.
- Microservice Architecture (MSA) is a variant of the service-oriented architecture architectural style that focuses on building single-purpose, modular services. Services, known as microservices, are independently deployable and communicate via messaging.
- The key benefits of MSA are:
- Improved maintainability: Small, focused services are easier to maintain.
- Improved scalability: Services can be scaled independently based on demand.
- Improved fault tolerance: Failure of a single service will not crash the entire system. Other services will continue to function.
- Faster and easier deployment: Smaller codebases allow for quicker deployment and rollback.
- Tool and technology diversity: Different services can use different frameworks and databases as appropriate.

Some key terms to remember:
- Service: A software component with a defined interface that can be accessed remotely.
- Microservice: A lightweight service with a narrow focus and well-defined interface.
- Message: A communication between services containing data.
- Endpoint: The address or port where a service can be accessed.

Mnemonics:
- SOA is like a empire (large scale), MSA is like states (small and independent).
- SOA reuses servants (services), MSA uses specialized workers (microservices).
