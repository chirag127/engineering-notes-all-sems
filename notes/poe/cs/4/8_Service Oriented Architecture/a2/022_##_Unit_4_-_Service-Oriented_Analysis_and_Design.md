## Unit 4 - Service-Oriented Analysis and Design

Service-Oriented Analysis and Design (SOAD) is an architectural approach for developing software applications. It involves designing applications as a set of loosely coupled services that can be reused for different purposes.

Some key points about SOAD:

- It focuses on loosely coupled services that are self-contained and can be reused.
- Services communicate with each other via interfaces and messages.
- Services are independent and can be developed/deployed independently.
- The underlying implementation of a service can change without impacting other services.
- Services are discoverable and accessible over a network.

Benefits of SOAD:

- Increased reusability of services.
- Easier maintenance as services can be modified independently.
- Scalability through decentralized services.
- Flexibility to integrate new services or replace existing ones.
- Language/platform independence. Services can be built in any language/platform as long as they conform to the interface.

Steps in SOAD:

1. Determine business processes and key functionality
2. Identify candidate services by looking for reusable functionality
3. Define service interfaces/contracts
4. Implement the services
5. Test and deploy the services
6. Handle service discovery and composition

Some tips for SOAD:

- Focus on business capabilities, not technical capabilities, when identifying services
- Keep services small and focused on a specific task
- Use consistent messaging/interface standards for service communication
- Plan for service versioning and governance
- Consider scalability, security, logging, monitoring, etc. for services
