### Emergence of MSA

- As applications grew larger and more complex, the monolithic architecture had scaling issues.
- Every change required redeployment of the entire application.
- Maintaining and updating the application became difficult.
- These issues led to the emergence of Service-Oriented Architecture (SOA).
- In SOA, applications are broken down into smaller independent services that communicate with each other.
- This made applications more flexible, scalable, and maintainable.
- However, SOA still had some issues:
    - Services were heavyweight and did too much.
    - Loose coupling led to remote calls and latency.
    - Orchestration of services led to fragile systems.
- To solve these problems, Microservice Architecture (MSA) evolved from SOA.
- In MSA, applications are broken down into very small, focused, and independent services.
- Services are lightweight and do one job.
- Local calls and inter-process communication replace remote calls.
- Systems are more robust and fault-tolerant.
- MSA enables continuous delivery and scalable architecture.

Some key points to remember:

- Monolithic apps had scaling and maintainability issues.
- SOA solved these issues but had some limitations.
- MSA evolved from SOA to build more robust and scalable systems.
- MSA uses independent, focused, and lightweight services.
- Local communication and decentralization make MSA more efficient.
