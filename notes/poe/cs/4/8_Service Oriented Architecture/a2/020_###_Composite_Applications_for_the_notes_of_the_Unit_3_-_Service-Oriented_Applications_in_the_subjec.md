### Composite Applications

- Composite applications are applications that are built by aggregating various distinct services.
- These services are independent but orchestrated together to provide complex business functions and use cases.
- The services are loosely coupled and can be reused in various business scenarios.
- Composite applications provide flexibility, maintainability and scalability as the individual services can be modified, replaced or scaled independently.
- Examples of composite applications are e-commerce applications, travel booking applications, etc. that provide various functionalities by orchestrating many underlying services like payment, booking, inventory, etc.

**Advantages:**

- Increased flexibility and agility through loose coupling of services
- Better maintainability as individual services can be modified or replaced independently
- Increased scalability as independent services can be scaled as per demand
- Reusability of services across various composite applications

**Disadvantages:**

- Increased complexity in development and monitoring
- Challenges in service discovery, orchestration, fault tolerance, etc.
- Versioning issues with dependent services
- Performance issues if not designed properly

_Mnemonics:_

- Think of a composite application as a dish made of various ingredients (services) orchestrated together into a meal (use case).
- The ingredients can be changed or the quantities can be varied (flexibility and scalability) but the overall taste of the dish should remain good (freely coupled but orchestrated services).
