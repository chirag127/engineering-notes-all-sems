### Cost Savings for the notes of the Unit 8 - Business Case for SOA in the subject of Service Oriented Architecture

The following are some of the major cost savings that can be achieved by adopting Service Oriented Architecture:

1. Reduced Cost of Integration: In SOA, services are designed to be loosely coupled and reusable. This means that when new applications are built, existing services can be reused thereby reducing the cost and effort required for integration.
2. Increased Operational Efficiency: With SOA, services can be monitored, optimized and managed centrally leading to increased operational efficiency and reduced costs.
3. Reduced Duplication of Effort: By reusing existing services in SOA, duplicate functionality across applications and systems can be avoided. This leads to significant cost savings in terms of effort and resources.
4. Flexibility and Agility: SOA enables applications to be built rapidly by reusing and combining various services. This increases the flexibility and agility of the IT infrastructure leading to reduced costs for accommodating changes.
5. Improved ROI: With the above benefits of cost savings and increased operational efficiency, SOA can lead to an improved return on investment for organizations. The initial costs of migration to SOA can be more than offset by the subsequent savings and benefits.

Some mnemonics and tips to remember the cost savings:

- RICE - Reduced Integration Cost, Increased Efficiency
- DORE - Duplication Avoidance, Operational Efficiency, Reusability, Flexibility
- SOA pays off - The initial investment in SOA can pay off in the long run due to the significant cost savings and benefits
