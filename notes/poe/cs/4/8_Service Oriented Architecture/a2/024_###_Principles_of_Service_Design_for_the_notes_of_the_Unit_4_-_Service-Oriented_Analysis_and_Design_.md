### Principles of Service Design

The key principles of service design are:

1. Service encapsulation - A service should be a self-contained unit that encapsulates the logic and data it operates on. It should minimize dependencies on other services.
2. Loose coupling - Services should have loose connections and dependencies with other services. This allows for flexibility and reusability of services.
3. Abstractions - Services should expose clear and consistent abstractions of their capabilities and hide internal implementation details.
4. Composability - Services should be composable so that they can be combined and reused to build richer and more complex applications and processes.
5. Statelessness - Services should be stateless, keeping no client state. All needed data should be passed to the service. This allows for easier scalability and resilience.
6. Discoverability - The availability and functionality of services should be easily discoverable. Services should expose descriptions or definitions of their capabilities so they can be found and utilized.
7. Synchronous vs Asynchronous - Services can be exposed synchronously or asynchronously depending on requirements. Asynchronous is often preferable for scalability and performance but adds complexity.
8. Reliability - Services should be robust and reliable, even in the face of errors and faults. They should include resilience patterns like timeouts, retries, and fault tolerance.
9. Performance efficiency - Services should be efficiently performant to minimize latency and maximize throughput. Optimization techniques like caching and scaling can be employed.
10. Versioning - Services will evolve over time, so robust versioning strategies should be employed to support backwards compatibility and gradual migration to new versions.

Some mnemonics and learning tricks for remembering the principles:

- SEAD: Service Encapsulation, Abstraction, Decoupling
- SLATE: Stateless, Loosely coupled, Abstractions, Transactional, Efficient
- A service is like a LEGO block - composable, reusable, hide implementation
