### SOA Governance – Best Practices

Some of the key best practices for SOA Governance are:

1. Define a Governance Framework: Establish a governance framework that defines responsibilities, processes, and controls for the various aspects of SOA governance like - design time governance, run time governance, change management governance, etc. This framework should be well documented and communicated to all stakeholders.

2. Ensure Business Alignment: All SOA initiatives should be aligned to business goals. Governance should ensure that SOA programs are delivering business value and meeting key business metrics.

3. Standardization: Governance should drive the creation and adoption of standards for areas like - service naming conventions, terminology, use of design patterns, etc. These standards help to increase reusability, reduce complexity, and enable interoperability.

4. Reuse Promotion: Effective governance needs to put focus on promoting service reuse. This can be achieved by proper service modeling, categorization, and rationalization. Governance can also introduce financial incentives for service reuse and chargebacks for redundant service creation.

5. Lifecycle Management: Strong lifecycle management processes should be in place to track the lifecycle of services from ideation to retirement. This includes processes for service version management, where multiple versions of a service have to be governed.

6. Change Management: Rigorous change management processes are required to control the changes to services. This is important to minimize the impact of changes and avoid breaking dependencies with service consumers. Automated change impact analysis tools can be leveraged to aid change governance.

7. Run-time Monitoring: Continuous run-time monitoring of services and the SOA infrastructure is necessary for governance. This includes monitoring of service availability, performance, logging/auditing, error handling, etc. The monitoring data should be used to get insights and drive improvements.

8. Security and Compliance: Governance is responsible for defining and enforcing security policies and compliance requirements for services and the overall SOA. This includes areas like authentication, authorization, auditing, regulatory compliance, etc.

9. Communication: Regular communication about SOA governance processes, standards, changes, issues, etc. should be done with all stakeholders. This helps in gaining acceptance and participation from service consumers and providers.
