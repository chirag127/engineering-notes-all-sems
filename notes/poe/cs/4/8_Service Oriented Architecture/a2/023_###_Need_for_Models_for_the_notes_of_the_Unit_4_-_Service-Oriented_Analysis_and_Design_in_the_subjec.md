### Need for Models for the notes of the Unit 4 - Service-Oriented Analysis and Design in the subject of Service Oriented Architecture

Models are essential for Service-Oriented Analysis and Design as they help in:

- Abstracting the complex problem into simpler terms for easy understanding and solving.
- Communicating the ideas and concepts of the system to be designed with stakeholders.
- Capturing the essential aspects of the system.
- Providing a blueprint for the implementation.

Some of the important models used are:

- Service Model - Represents services, their interfaces and relationships. Helps in identifying services and defining service contracts.
- Business Process Model - Captures the business processes and logic. Helps in determining service boundaries and orchestration.
- Domain Model - Represents the key concepts and terminology of the problem domain. Helps in understanding the domain and determining service granularity.
- Object Model - Represents the object types, attributes and relationships. Helps in translation to a service-oriented architecture.

Advantages of using models:

- Abstraction - Hides complexity and provides simplicity.
- Communication - Easy to explain ideas to stakeholders.
- Blueprint - Acts as input for implementation.
- Analysis - Helps in finding flaws and inconsistencies early.

Disadvantages of using models:

- May introduce inaccuracies if not designed properly.
- Can be misinterpreted if not explained properly.
- Difficult to keep models up-to-date with changes.
- Requires effort and skill to create good models.

In summary, models should be used to gain a proper understanding of the system to be designed but should not be viewed as a replacement for the actual system. They have to be designed, reviewed and updated with care to reap the real benefits of modeling.
