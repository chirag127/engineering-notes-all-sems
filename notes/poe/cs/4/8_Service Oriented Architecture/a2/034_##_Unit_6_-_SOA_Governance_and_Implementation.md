## Unit 6 - SOA Governance and Implementation

### SOA Governance
- SOA governance refers to the processes, roles, policies, and metrics that are put in place to ensure a service-oriented architecture (SOA) delivers business value.
- The main goals of SOA governance are to maximize business benefits, reduce risk, and ensure regulatory compliance.
- Key aspects of SOA governance include:
    - Business optimization - Aligning SOA with business goals and tracking business benefits
    - Portfolio management - Managing the SOA portfolio of services and service-related assets
    - Architectural compliance - Ensuring services comply with the organization's SOA reference architecture
    - Operational management - Operational management of services and handling service-level agreements (SLAs), monitoring, logging, security, etc.
    - Quality management - Managing the quality of services and the service development lifecycle
- Challenges with SOA governance include the organizational change management needed, a sufficient level of maturity in SOA adoption, and dealing with legacy systems.

### SOA Implementation
- The implementation of a SOA involves several steps:
    1. Define the business requirements and the business services that are needed.
    2. Model the services and define the interfaces. This is done by service-oriented modeling and analysis.
    3. Design the technical services and determine how the services will be developed, hosted, and delivered.
    4. Develop the technical services and service clients. This includes considerations for service composition, service contracts, data and message formats, security, reliability, and other quality-of-service characteristics.
    5. Deploy and operate the services. This includes deployment, monitoring, management, metering, billing, and support.
    6. Continuously evaluate and monitor the SOA to ensure it meets business needs and make improvements.
- Key implementation considerations include building vs. buying services, creating web services vs. more flexible service interfaces, SOA infrastructure and middleware choices, legacy system integration, and security and manageability across distributed services.
- Mnemonics: **SDLC** - Service-oriented modeling and analysis, Design, Develop, Deploy and operate, Continuously evaluate and monitor
- Advantages: Increased business agility, Reuse of services, Interoperability
- Disadvantages: Complexity, Vendor dependence, Security issues
