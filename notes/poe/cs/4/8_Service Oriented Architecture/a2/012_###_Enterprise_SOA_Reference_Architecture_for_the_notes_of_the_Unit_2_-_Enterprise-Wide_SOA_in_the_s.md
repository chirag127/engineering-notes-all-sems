### Enterprise SOA Reference Architecture

- The reference architecture provides a blueprint for designing and implementing a service-oriented architecture in an enterprise.
- It defines the basic layers and components in an SOA and how they interact with each other.
- The main layers in an enterprise SOA reference architecture are:

1. Service consumer layer - Consists of applications that consume services.
2. Service provider layer - Consists of services that are exposed for consumption.
3. Service registry - Used for registration and discovery of services.
4. Transport layer - Used for communication between service consumers and providers.
5. Management layer - Used for managing the operational aspects of an SOA.

- The components in each layer and their interactions can be represented using an SOA reference architecture diagram.
- Some advantages of following a reference architecture are:

- Reduced complexity in designing and implementing an SOA solution.
- Increased reusability of services across an enterprise.
- Improved governance and management of services.
- Alignment with industry best practices.

- However, the reference architecture may not fit all enterprise scenarios and needs customization based on specific requirements and constraints. The layers and components should be tailored to the enterprise landscape.

- Here are some mnemonics and tips to remember the layers:

- Think of service consumers as users and service providers as servers to remember the first two layers.
- Remember 'registry' has a similar spelling as 'register' to recall the service registry layer.
- Transport layer is for communication, so think of it enabling conversation between consumers and providers.
- Management layer is for administration, so associate it with managing the SOA.
