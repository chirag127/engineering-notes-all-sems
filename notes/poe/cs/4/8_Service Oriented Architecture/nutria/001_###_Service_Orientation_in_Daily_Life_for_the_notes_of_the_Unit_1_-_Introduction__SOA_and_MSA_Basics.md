
### Service Orientation in Daily Life for the notes of the Unit 1 - Introduction: SOA and MSA Basics in the subject of Service Oriented Architecture

Service Oriented Architecture (SOA) and Microservice Architecture (MSA) are two distinct approaches to designing software systems. SOA is an architectural style that promotes the use of services to build distributed systems, while MSA is a more granular approach that focuses on building small, independent services that can be composed together to build larger systems.

- **Service Oriented Architecture (SOA):** SOA is an architectural style that promotes the use of services to build distributed systems. It emphasizes the separation of concerns and the use of standards to enable interoperability between services. SOA encourages the reuse of existing services and the development of new services that can be quickly deployed and integrated into existing systems.

- **Microservice Architecture (MSA):** MSA is a more granular approach that focuses on building small, independent services that can be composed together to build larger systems. It emphasizes the use of lightweight protocols and messaging systems to enable communication between services. MSA also encourages the use of containers and DevOps practices to enable rapid deployment and scaling of services.

- **Mnemonics and Learning Tricks:** SOA and MSA can be remembered by using the acronym "SOMA", which stands for Service Oriented Architecture and Microservice Architecture. Additionally, the phrase "separate and scale" can be used to remember the core principles of SOA and MSA.
