
## Unit 4 - Service-Oriented Analysis and Design

Service-oriented analysis and design is a methodology for creating software applications that are built on services. The services are designed to be loosely coupled, meaning that they can be used independently of each other and can be changed or replaced without affecting the other services.

The goal of service-oriented analysis and design is to create applications that are flexible, scalable, and maintainable. It focuses on the design of services that can be reused across multiple applications.

### Principles of Service-Oriented Analysis and Design

1. Services should be loosely coupled. Services should be designed in such a way that they can be used independently of each other and can be changed or replaced without affecting the other services.

2. Services should be self-contained. Services should be designed to be self-contained, meaning they should not rely on any external services or data sources.

3. Services should be discoverable. Services should be designed to be discoverable, meaning they should be easily found and used by other applications.

4. Services should be secure. Services should be designed to be secure, meaning they should be protected from unauthorized access.

5. Services should be scalable. Services should be designed to be scalable, meaning they should be able to handle increased traffic and load without compromising performance.

### Mnemonics and Learning Tricks

1. SOAAD: Service-Oriented Analysis and Design.

2. Loosely Coupled: Think of two magnets, when they are close together, they are tightly coupled, but when they are far apart, they are loosely coupled.

3. Self-Contained: Think of a self-contained apartment, it has everything it needs within its own walls.

4. Discoverable: Think of a lighthouse, it can be seen from far away and is used to help guide ships to safety.

5. Secure: Think of a locked door, it is designed to keep people out who do not have the key.

6. Scalable: Think of a tree, it can grow and expand as it gets bigger.
