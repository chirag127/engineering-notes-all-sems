
### Cost Savings for the Notes of Unit 8 - Business Case for SOA in the Subject of Service Oriented Architecture

1. Service Oriented Architecture (SOA) is an architecture that allows for the creation of applications and services that can be used across multiple platforms and devices.
2. SOA is based on the concept of services, which are components of a system that can be reused and shared across different applications and platforms.
3. SOA allows for cost savings by reducing the need for expensive hardware and software, as well as reducing the time and effort needed to develop and maintain applications.
4. SOA also enables businesses to access and use services from multiple vendors, allowing them to get the best deals and services.
5. SOA is also beneficial in terms of scalability, as it allows businesses to add and remove services as needed, without having to invest in additional hardware or software.
6. SOA also enables businesses to easily integrate their services with other systems, allowing for a more efficient and cost-effective way of doing business.
7. Mnemonic for SOA: Service Oriented Architecture = Scalable, Reusable, Accessible, and Economical.
8. Advantages of SOA include:
    - Reduced cost of ownership
    - Increased flexibility and scalability
    - Improved system integration
    - Improved security
    - Improved performance
9. Disadvantages of SOA include:
    - Complexity of implementation
    - Potential for vendor lock-in
    - Difficulty in managing and monitoring services
10. Examples of SOA applications include:
    - Cloud-based applications
    - Mobile applications
    - Internet of Things (IoT) applications
    - Business process management (BPM) applications
11. SOA is widely used in many industries, including banking, healthcare, retail, manufacturing, and transportation.
