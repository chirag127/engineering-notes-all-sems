
### Enterprise SOA Reference Architecture for the notes of the Unit 2 - Enterprise-Wide SOA in the subject of Service Oriented Architecture

* Service Oriented Architecture (SOA) is a software design approach that enables applications to be developed in a way that allows them to be reused and integrated with other applications.
* SOA is based on the idea of services, which are pieces of software that can be used by other applications to perform specific tasks.
* The Enterprise SOA Reference Architecture (ESRA) is a set of guidelines for designing and building service-oriented architectures for enterprises.
* ESRA is based on the principles of service-orientation, which include loose coupling, autonomy, reusability, and composability.
* ESRA has four main components: services, service providers, service consumers, and service brokers.
* Services are the building blocks of the architecture, and are responsible for performing a specific task or providing a specific piece of data.
* Service providers are responsible for providing the services to the service consumers.
* Service consumers are the applications that use the services provided by the service providers.
* Service brokers are responsible for managing the interactions between the service providers and service consumers.
* ESRA also includes guidelines for designing and building service-oriented applications, such as using the right design patterns, creating the right service contracts, and using the right messaging protocols.
* ESRA also includes guidelines for managing and monitoring the performance of service-oriented applications.
* ESRA provides a robust and scalable way of building service-oriented architectures that can be used in any enterprise environment.
