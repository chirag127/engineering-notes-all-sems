
### Patterns for SOA for the notes of the Unit 3 - Service-Oriented Applications in the subject of Service Oriented Architecture

- Service-Oriented Architecture (SOA) is an architectural style that allows organizations to use services to build applications.
- SOA is based on the concept of services that are autonomous, self-contained, and loosely coupled. 
- Services are independent components that can be reused and combined with other services to create applications. 
- SOA provides a way to structure and manage complex applications.
- The key patterns of SOA are:
  - Service Discovery: This is the process of locating services that are available in a given environment.
  - Service Registry: This is a repository of information about services that are available in a given environment.
  - Service Composition: This is the process of combining multiple services to create a new application.
  - Service Orchestration: This is the process of managing and coordinating the execution of services.
  - Service Abstraction: This is the process of hiding the details of how a service is implemented from the consumer.
  - Service Invocation: This is the process of sending a request to a service and receiving a response.
  - Service Security: This is the process of protecting services from unauthorized access.
- Mnemonics:
  - Service Discovery: "Find Me Services"
  - Service Registry: "Register Services"
  - Service Composition: "Compose Services"
  - Service Orchestration: "Orchestrate Services"
  - Service Abstraction: "Abstract Services"
  - Service Invocation: "Invoke Services"
  - Service Security: "Secure Services"
