### Patterns for SOA for the notes of the Unit 3 - Service-Oriented Applications in the subject of Service Oriented Architecture

Service-Oriented Architecture (SOA) is an architectural pattern that helps in building distributed applications. There are several patterns available for SOA that help in building scalable and reliable applications. Let's take a look at some of the commonly used patterns for SOA.

#### 1. Service Registry Pattern
- This pattern is used to manage and register services in a centralized location. 
- It helps in discovering and invoking services. 
- The registry acts as a mediator between the service consumer and the service provider.

#### 2. Service Broker Pattern
- This pattern is used to route requests between the service consumer and the service provider. 
- It helps in managing and controlling the flow of messages between services.

#### 3. Service Gateway Pattern
- This pattern is used to provide a single point of entry for all service requests. 
- It helps in managing and controlling the access to services. 
- The gateway can be used to implement security, routing, and transformation of messages.

#### 4. Service Chaining Pattern
- This pattern is used to combine multiple services to form a new service. 
- It helps in reducing the complexity of the application by breaking down the functionality into smaller services. 
- This pattern is also known as the composition pattern.

#### 5. Service Layer Pattern
- This pattern is used to separate the business logic from the presentation layer. 
- It helps in building loosely coupled applications that are easy to maintain and modify. 
- The service layer is responsible for implementing the business logic and exposing it as a service.

#### 6. Service Façade Pattern
- This pattern is used to provide a simplified interface to the service consumer. 
- It helps in hiding the complexity of the underlying services. 
- The façade acts as a mediator between the service consumer and the underlying services.

#### 7. Service Decomposition Pattern
- This pattern is used to break down a monolithic application into smaller, independent services. 
- It helps in improving the scalability and flexibility of the application. 
- Each service can be developed, tested, and deployed independently.

#### 8. Service Versioning Pattern
- This pattern is used to manage the changes in the service interface over time. 
- It helps in maintaining backward compatibility and managing the evolution of the service. 
- Each version of the service can be maintained and deployed independently.

#### 9. Service Orchestration Pattern
- This pattern is used to coordinate the execution of multiple services to achieve a specific business goal. 
- It helps in building complex business processes that involve multiple services. 
- The orchestration engine is responsible for coordinating the execution of services.
