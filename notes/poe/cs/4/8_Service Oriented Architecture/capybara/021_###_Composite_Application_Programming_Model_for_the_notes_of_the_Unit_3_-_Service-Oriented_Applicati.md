### Composite Application Programming Model for the notes of the Unit 3 - Service-Oriented Applications in the subject of Service Oriented Architecture

The Composite Application Programming Model (CAPM) is a design pattern used to build service-oriented applications that are composed of multiple services. It is based on the principles of Service-Oriented Architecture (SOA) and aims to provide a flexible and scalable way to develop and deploy applications.

The CAPM consists of three main components:

1. **Service Consumer**: It is the component that consumes the services provided by the service provider. The service consumer sends requests to the service provider and receives responses in return.

2. **Service Provider**: It is the component that provides the services to the service consumer. The service provider receives requests from the service consumer and sends responses back.

3. **Service Registry**: It is the component that keeps track of all the available services and their locations. The service registry provides a way for the service consumer to discover the available services and their endpoints.

The CAPM follows the following steps to provide a flexible and scalable way to develop and deploy applications:

1. **Service Discovery**: The service consumer queries the service registry for the available services and their endpoints.

2. **Service Invocation**: The service consumer sends a request to the service provider using its endpoint.

3. **Service Response**: The service provider receives the request, processes it, and sends a response back to the service consumer.

4. **Service Composition**: The service consumer can combine multiple services to create a composite application that provides more complex functionality.

The CAPM provides several benefits, such as:

1. **Flexibility**: The CAPM allows for the development of applications that can easily adapt to changing business requirements.

2. **Scalability**: The CAPM allows for the deployment of applications that can handle a large number of service requests.

3. **Service Reusability**: The CAPM promotes the reuse of services, which can reduce development time and cost.

4. **Service Interoperability**: The CAPM promotes the development of interoperable services that can be used across different platforms and technologies.

Mnemonics and learning tricks for the CAPM:

1. **CAPM = Consumer, Provider, Registry, Model**: This simple mnemonic can help you remember the components of the CAPM.

2. **Think of CAPM as a Lego set**: Just like a Lego set has different blocks that can be combined to create different structures, the CAPM has different services that can be combined to create different applications.

3. **Remember the four steps of the CAPM**: Service Discovery, Service Invocation, Service Response, and Service Composition. You can use the acronym "DSRC" to remember these steps.
