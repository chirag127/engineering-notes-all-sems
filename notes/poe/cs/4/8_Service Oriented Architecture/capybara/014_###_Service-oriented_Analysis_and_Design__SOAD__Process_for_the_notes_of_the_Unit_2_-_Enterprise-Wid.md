### Service-oriented Analysis and Design (SOAD) Process for the notes of the Unit 2 - Enterprise-Wide SOA in the subject of Service Oriented Architecture

The Service-oriented Analysis and Design (SOAD) process is a critical step in the development of enterprise-wide Service-Oriented Architecture (SOA) systems. It involves a systematic approach to identifying, analyzing, and designing services that meet the needs of the organization.

The SOAD process can be divided into the following stages:

1. Discovering Services: The first step in the SOAD process is to identify the services that the organization requires. This involves a comprehensive analysis of the organization's business processes and the identification of the key services that are required to support these processes.

2. Defining Services: Once the key services have been identified, the next step is to define them. This involves identifying the inputs, outputs, and behavior of each service. It is essential to ensure that each service is designed to meet the specific needs of the organization.

3. Analyzing Services: The next step is to analyze the services to ensure that they are of high quality and meet the needs of the organization. This involves analyzing the services for their functionality, reliability, performance, and security.

4. Designing Services: Once the services have been analyzed, the next step is to design them. This involves designing the service interfaces, service contracts, and service implementations. The service design must be flexible, scalable, and able to support the changing needs of the organization.

5. Implementing Services: The final step in the SOAD process is to implement the services. This involves developing the service code, testing the services, and deploying them. It is essential to ensure that the services are integrated with the other systems in the organization.

Mnemonics and learning tricks:

- To remember the stages of the SOAD process, you can use the acronym DADDI, which stands for Discovering, Defining, Analyzing, Designing, and Implementing Services.
- Another trick is to remember the acronym FRED, which stands for Functionality, Reliability, Performance, and Security. These are the key areas that need to be analyzed when analyzing the services.

Advantages of the SOAD process:

- The SOAD process helps organizations to identify the key services that are required to support their business processes.
- It ensures that the services are of high quality and meet the needs of the organization.
- The SOAD process ensures that the services are flexible, scalable, and able to support the changing needs of the organization.

Disadvantages of the SOAD process:

- The SOAD process can be time-consuming and expensive.
- It requires a high level of expertise in service-oriented architecture and design.

Example of the SOAD process:

An organization wants to develop an enterprise-wide SOA system to support its business processes. The organization uses the SOAD process to identify, analyze, and design the services required for the system. The organization discovers the key services required, defines them, analyzes them, designs them, and implements them. The result is a flexible, scalable, and reliable SOA system that supports the changing needs of the organization.

Applications of the SOAD process:

The SOAD process can be used in a wide range of applications, including:

- Developing enterprise-wide SOA systems
- Developing web services
- Developing mobile applications
- Developing cloud-based services
