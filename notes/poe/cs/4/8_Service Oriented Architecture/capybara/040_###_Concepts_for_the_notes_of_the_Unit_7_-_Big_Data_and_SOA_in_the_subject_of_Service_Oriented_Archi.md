### Concepts for the notes of the Unit 7 - Big Data and SOA in the subject of Service Oriented Architecture

Service Oriented Architecture (SOA) is a design approach that focuses on the creation of modular and reusable software components, known as services, that can be easily integrated to create more complex systems. In this unit, we will explore the concepts related to Big Data and SOA in the context of Service Oriented Architecture.

Here are some important concepts that you should keep in mind while studying Unit 7:

1. Big Data: It refers to the large and complex data sets that cannot be processed using traditional data processing techniques. Big Data is characterized by the 3Vs - Volume, Velocity, and Variety.

2. SOA: It is an architectural style that enables the creation of modular and reusable software components, known as services, that can be easily integrated to create more complex systems.

3. SOA Principles: There are some principles that should be followed while designing and implementing SOA-based systems. These include loose coupling, service abstraction, service reusability, service autonomy, service discoverability, and service composability.

4. Service-Oriented Analysis and Design (SOAD): It is a process that involves the identification, specification, and modeling of services that will be used to build a SOA-based system. The SOAD process includes four phases - Discovery, Analysis, Design, and Implementation.

5. Service Registry: It is a central repository that stores information about available services, including their location, interfaces, and capabilities. Service consumers can use the registry to discover and access the services they need.

6. Enterprise Service Bus (ESB): It is a software architecture that facilitates the integration of different applications and services within an enterprise. An ESB provides a central location for managing and routing messages between applications and services.

7. Web Services: They are a standardized way of communicating between software applications over the internet. Web services use XML-based protocols, such as SOAP and REST, to exchange data between applications.

Mnemonics and learning tricks:

1. Remember the 3Vs of Big Data as Volume, Velocity, and Variety.

2. To remember the SOA principles, use the acronym LARS-DC. LARS stands for Loose coupling, Service Abstraction, Reusability, and Service Autonomy. DC stands for Discoverability and Composability.

3. Use the acronym DADI to remember the four phases of the SOAD process - Discovery, Analysis, Design, and Implementation.

By understanding these concepts and principles, you will be able to design and implement effective SOA-based systems that can handle Big Data efficiently. Keep practicing and applying these concepts to real-world scenarios to become proficient in this field.
