
### Issues in PoS tagging for the notes of the Unit 2 - WORD LEVEL ANALYSIS in the subject of Natural Language Processing

* Part-of-Speech (POS) tagging is the process of assigning a part of speech (noun, verb, adjective, etc.) to each word in a sentence.
* POS tagging is a crucial step in natural language processing (NLP) tasks such as machine translation, question answering, text summarization, and information extraction.
* However, there are some issues that make POS tagging a difficult task.
  * Ambiguity: Many words can have multiple parts of speech depending on the context in which they are used. For example, the word 'bank' can be either a verb or a noun.
  * Out-of-Vocabulary (OOV) words: Words that are not present in the training data can be difficult to tag correctly.
  * Unknown words: Words that are not present in the language’s lexicon can be difficult to tag correctly.
  * Morphological complexity: Some languages, such as English, have complex morphological systems which can make it difficult to accurately tag words.
* To address these issues, some methods have been proposed, such as using contextual information, using a morphological analyzer, and using a rule-based approach.
* Mnemonics can be used to help remember the parts of speech and their corresponding tags. For example, "Nouns are NICE" (N - Noun, I - Indefinite Article, C - Common Noun, E - Proper Noun). 
* Learning tricks such as using mnemonics and contextual information can be helpful when learning POS tagging.
