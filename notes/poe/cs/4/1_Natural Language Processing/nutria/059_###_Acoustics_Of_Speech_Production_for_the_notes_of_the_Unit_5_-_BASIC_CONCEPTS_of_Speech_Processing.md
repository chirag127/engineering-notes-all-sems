
### Acoustics Of Speech Production

Speech production is the process of producing sound through the vocal cords, which are controlled by the muscles of the larynx and the lungs. The resulting sound is then shaped by the articulators (the lips, tongue, and teeth) to produce intelligible speech.

The acoustics of speech production is the study of how this sound is produced and how it is modified by the articulators. It is concerned with the physical properties of the sound and how it is perceived by the listener.

##### The Properties of Speech Sounds

The sound produced by the vocal cords is a periodic waveform, which is modified by the articulators to create the sounds of speech. The properties of a speech sound include:

- Frequency: The frequency of a speech sound is the number of times the waveform repeats itself in a given time period. This is usually measured in Hertz (Hz).
- Amplitude: The amplitude of a speech sound is the intensity of the sound. This is usually measured in Decibels (dB).
- Duration: The duration of a speech sound is the length of time it is sustained. This is usually measured in milliseconds (ms).

##### Mnemonics and Learning Tricks

When studying the acoustics of speech production, it can be helpful to remember the following mnemonics:

- **F**requency is measured in **H**ertz (Hz).
- **A**mplitude is measured in **D**ecibels (dB).
- **D**uration is measured in **M**illiseconds (ms).
