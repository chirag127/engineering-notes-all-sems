
# Normal Forms for Grammar for the Notes of the Unit 3 - SYNTACTIC ANALYSIS in the Subject of Natural Language Processing

Normal forms are a set of rules used to analyze and understand the structure of a sentence. They are important for understanding how a sentence is constructed and how the words within it interact. In the context of Natural Language Processing (NLP), normal forms are used to create a uniform structure for a sentence that can be understood by computers.

The following are the three main normal forms used in NLP:

1. **Chomsky Normal Form (CNF)**: This form is used to represent a sentence as a set of phrase structure rules. It is often used to parse sentences and analyze their structure.

2. **Greibach Normal Form (GNF)**: This form is used to represent a sentence as a set of productions. It is often used to generate sentences from a given grammar.

3. **Backus-Naur Form (BNF)**: This form is used to represent a sentence as a set of context-free grammar rules. It is often used to generate sentences from a given grammar.

In addition, there are other normal forms that are used in NLP, such as the Extended Backus-Naur Form (EBNF) and the Generalized Phrase Structure Grammar (GPSG).

It is important to note that normal forms are not used to determine the meaning of a sentence, but instead to analyze its structure. The interpretation of a sentence is left up to the language processor.

Mnemonics and learning tricks for the normal forms can be helpful when learning and understanding them. For example, CNF can be remembered as “Chomsky’s Normal Form”, GNF can be remembered as “Greibach’s Normal Form”, and BNF can be remembered as “Backus-Naur Form”.
