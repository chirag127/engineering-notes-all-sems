
### Acoustic Phonetics for the notes of the Unit 5 - BASIC CONCEPTS of Speech Processing in the subject of Natural Language Processing

Acoustic phonetics is a branch of phonetics that studies the physical properties of speech sounds. It is concerned with the physical properties of speech sounds, such as their frequency, amplitude, and duration. Acoustic phonetics focuses on the production, transmission, and perception of speech sounds.

#### Production

When producing speech, the vocal cords vibrate, producing sound waves that travel through the air. The vocal cords vibrate at different frequencies, depending on the sound being produced. For example, a higher frequency is produced when producing a high-pitched sound, such as the letter “i” in the word “bit”.

The vocal cords also produce different amplitudes of sound waves, depending on the sound being produced. For example, a higher amplitude is produced when producing a loud sound, such as the letter “l” in the word “loud”.

The duration of a sound is also important in acoustic phonetics. For example, a longer duration is produced when producing a long sound, such as the letter “o” in the word “boat”.

#### Transmission

When sound waves travel through the air, they are affected by the environment. The sound waves can be distorted by obstacles, such as walls, or by the air itself. The sound waves can also be affected by the temperature and humidity of the air.

#### Perception

When we perceive speech, we are using our auditory system to interpret the sound waves that have been transmitted through the air. Our auditory system is able to detect differences in frequency, amplitude, and duration. It is also able to detect changes in the environment that can affect the sound waves.

#### Mnemonics

To remember the three components of acoustic phonetics (frequency, amplitude, and duration), you can use the mnemonic “FAD”.
