
### Finite-State Automata for the notes of the Unit 1 - INTRODUCTION in the subject of Natural Language Processing

Finite-state automata (FSA) are a type of mathematical model used in computer science and linguistics to represent the behavior of a system. They are used to recognize patterns in a given input and to generate output based on these patterns. Finite-state automata are composed of a finite set of states, transitions between these states, and a start state.

A finite-state automaton is a machine that can be in only one of a finite number of states at any given time. It can change from one state to another based on the input it receives. The states and the transitions between them are defined by the user. 

Mnemonics and Learning Tricks: 
- F: Finite-state automata 
- S: Set of states 
- A: Automaton 
- T: Transitions between states 
- S: Start state 

Advantages: 
- FSA are simple to design and implement. 
- They are computationally efficient. 
- They can recognize complex patterns. 

Disadvantages: 
- FSA are limited in their capabilities. 
- They cannot recognize non-deterministic patterns. 
- They cannot recognize infinitely long patterns. 

Applications: 
- FSA are used in applications such as speech recognition, natural language processing, and compilers. 
- They are also used in computer networks to detect malicious traffic. 
- They are used in robotics to recognize and respond to certain stimuli.
