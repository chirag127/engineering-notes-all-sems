

### Ambiguity for the notes of the Unit 3 - SYNTACTIC ANALYSIS in the subject of Natural Language Processing

Ambiguity is an important concept in natural language processing. It arises when a sentence can have multiple interpretations. Ambiguity can be of two types: lexical ambiguity and syntactic ambiguity. 

Lexical Ambiguity: Lexical ambiguity occurs when a word or phrase has multiple meanings. For example, the word "bank" could mean a financial institution or the edge of a river. 

Syntactic Ambiguity: Syntactic ambiguity occurs when a sentence has multiple syntactic structures. For example, the sentence "The man saw the girl with the telescope" could mean that the man saw the girl who was holding a telescope, or that the man saw the girl using a telescope to look at something. 

Mnemonics and Learning Tricks: 

1. To remember the difference between lexical and syntactic ambiguity, think of lexical ambiguity as related to the words in a sentence, and syntactic ambiguity as related to the structure of a sentence. 

2. To remember the definition of lexical ambiguity, think of the phrase "multiple meanings" - lexical ambiguity occurs when a word or phrase has multiple meanings. 

3. To remember the definition of syntactic ambiguity, think of the phrase "multiple structures" - syntactic ambiguity occurs when a sentence has multiple syntactic structures. 

4. To remember an example of lexical ambiguity, think of the phrase "bank" - it could mean a financial institution or the edge of a river. 

5. To remember an example of syntactic ambiguity, think of the sentence "The man saw the girl with the telescope" - it could mean that the man saw the girl who was holding a telescope, or that the man saw the girl using a telescope to look at something.
