
### Articulatory Phonetics for the notes of the Unit 5 - BASIC CONCEPTS of Speech Processing in the subject of Natural Language Processing

Articulatory phonetics is the study of the production of speech sounds by the articulatory and vocal tract. It is a branch of phonetics and deals with the physical properties of speech such as the position and movement of the lips, tongue, and other speech organs, as well as the air pressure and airflow during speech production. 

The main goal of articulatory phonetics is to describe the production of speech sounds in terms of the physical movements of the vocal tract and the articulators. This includes the position of the lips, tongue, jaw, and other speech organs, as well as the pressure and airflow during speech production.

The main focus of articulatory phonetics is on the physical production of speech sounds, and not on the acoustic properties of speech. This is because the physical production of speech sounds is the basis of the acoustic properties of speech.

Mnemonics and Learning Tricks: 

1. Lips: Think of the lips as the gatekeepers of speech production. They can open and close to allow air to flow out, and they can move to shape the sound. 

2. Tongue: The tongue is the main articulator in speech production. It is responsible for shaping the sounds and can move to different positions to create different sounds. 

3. Jaw: The jaw can move up and down, and side to side to create different sounds. 

4. Air Pressure and Flow: The pressure and flow of air is important in speech production. The pressure and flow of air can be controlled to create different sounds. 

Advantages: 

1. Articulatory phonetics allows us to understand how speech sounds are produced. 

2. It helps us to understand how different sounds are produced, and how to pronounce them correctly. 

3. It can help us to understand how to improve our pronunciation of certain sounds.

Disadvantages: 

1. Articulatory phonetics does not take into account the acoustic properties of speech. 

2. It is difficult to measure the exact movements of the articulators and vocal tract during speech production. 

3. It is difficult to measure the exact pressure and flow of air during speech production.
