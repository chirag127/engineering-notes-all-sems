
### Word Senses for the notes of the Unit 4 - SEMANTICS AND PRAGMATICS in the subject of Natural Language Processing

Word senses are the different meanings of a single word. In Natural Language Processing, the word sense is used to determine the meaning of a word in a given context.

Word senses can be classified into two categories:

1. **Literal senses**: These are the most common senses of a word and are the most basic meaning of a word. Examples of literal senses include:
    - **Nouns**: A dog is a four-legged animal.
    - **Verbs**: To run is to move quickly on foot.
2. **Figurative senses**: These are senses that are not literal and are used to convey a deeper or more complex meaning. Examples of figurative senses include:
    - **Metaphors**: She is a rock, meaning she is strong and dependable.
    - **Similes**: He is as brave as a lion, meaning he is very brave.

In Natural Language Processing, word senses can be used to determine the meaning of a word in a given context. For example, if the word "run" is used in the sentence "I run to the store", it is clear that the literal sense of the word is being used. However, if the word "run" is used in the sentence "She ran her business into the ground", it is clear that the figurative sense of the word is being used.

Mnemonics and learning tricks can be helpful in understanding and remembering word senses. For example, one could use the acronym "LITERAL" to remember the literal senses of a word:
- **L**: Nouns
- **I**: Verbs
- **T**: Adjectives
- **E**: Adverbs
- **R**: Prepositions
- **A**: Conjunctions
- **L**: Interjections

Another helpful mnemonic is to remember the figurative senses of a word with the acronym "FIGURATIVE":
- **F**: Metaphors
- **I**: Similes
- **G**: Personifications
- **U**: Hyperboles
- **R**: Ironies
- **A**: Allusions
- **T**: Paradoxes
- **I**: Understatements
- **V**: Overstatements
- **E**: Idioms
