
### Language Modeling for the notes of the Unit 1 - INTRODUCTION in the subject of Natural Language Processing

Language Modeling is a subfield of Natural Language Processing (NLP) that deals with predicting the probability of a sequence of words. Language modeling is used to create language-based applications such as machine translation, speech recognition, and speech synthesis.

In language modeling, a model is trained to assign a probability to a given sequence of words. This probability is used to determine the likelihood of a given sequence of words appearing in a given context. For example, a language model can be used to determine the likelihood of a sentence being grammatically correct.

The most commonly used language model is the n-gram model. An n-gram is a sequence of n words. The n-gram model assigns a probability to each n-gram by counting the number of times it appears in a corpus. The probability of an n-gram is calculated by dividing the number of times it appears in the corpus by the total number of n-grams in the corpus.

The n-gram model is a simple but powerful language model. It can be used to generate text that is grammatically correct, and it can also be used to detect anomalies in text.

In addition to the n-gram model, there are other types of language models such as neural network-based language models, recurrent neural network-based language models, and generative adversarial network-based language models. Each of these models has its own advantages and disadvantages.

Language modeling can be used in a variety of applications such as machine translation, speech recognition, and text summarization. It can also be used to create chatbots and virtual assistants.
