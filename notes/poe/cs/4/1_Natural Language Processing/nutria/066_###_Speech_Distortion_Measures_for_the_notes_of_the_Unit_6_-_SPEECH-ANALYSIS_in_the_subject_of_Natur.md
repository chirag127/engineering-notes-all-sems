
### Speech Distortion Measures for the Notes of the Unit 6 - SPEECH-ANALYSIS in the Subject of Natural Language Processing

* Speech Distortion Measures (SDMs) are a set of metrics used to measure the quality of speech signals. They are used to evaluate the performance of speech recognition systems, as well as to assess the intelligibility of speech in noisy environments.

* The most commonly used SDMs are the Signal-to-Noise Ratio (SNR) and the Perceptual Evaluation of Speech Quality (PESQ). The SNR measures the ratio between the signal power and the noise power, while PESQ is a measure of the perceived speech quality.

* Other SDMs include the Mean Opinion Score (MOS), which is a subjective measure of speech quality, and the Log Spectral Distance (LSD), which is a measure of the difference between two signals.

* In addition to these metrics, there are also several techniques used to reduce the effect of distortion on speech signals. These techniques include noise reduction, echo cancellation, and signal enhancement.

* Mnemonics and learning tricks for SDMs can be helpful in remembering the different metrics and techniques used to measure and reduce distortion. For example, the acronym "SNR PESQ MOS LSD" can be used to remember the most commonly used SDMs. Additionally, diagrams and tables can be used to illustrate the differences between the various metrics and techniques.
