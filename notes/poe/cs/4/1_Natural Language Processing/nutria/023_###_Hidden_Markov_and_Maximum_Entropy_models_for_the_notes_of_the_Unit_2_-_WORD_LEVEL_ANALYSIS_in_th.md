
### Hidden Markov and Maximum Entropy models for the notes of the Unit 2 - WORD LEVEL ANALYSIS in the subject of Natural Language Processing

- Hidden Markov Models (HMM) are probabilistic models used in natural language processing to assign a sequence of observations to a sequence of states. The states can represent words, phrases or any other linguistic unit. HMM is a generative model, meaning that it can generate new sequences of observations and states.

- Maximum Entropy Models (MEM) are probabilistic models that are used to model the probability of a sequence of observations given a set of features. MEMs are discriminative models, meaning that they can be used to identify the most likely sequence of observations given a set of features.

- HMM and MEM can be used together to create a more accurate model of natural language. HMM can be used to generate new sequences of observations and states, while MEM can be used to identify the most likely sequence of observations given a set of features.

- HMM and MEM can be used to identify the most likely word or phrase given a sequence of observations. This is known as part-of-speech tagging, or POS tagging.

- HMM and MEM can also be used to identify the most likely syntactic structure of a sentence, known as parsing.

- HMM and MEM can be used in other areas of natural language processing, such as machine translation, text summarization, and question answering.
