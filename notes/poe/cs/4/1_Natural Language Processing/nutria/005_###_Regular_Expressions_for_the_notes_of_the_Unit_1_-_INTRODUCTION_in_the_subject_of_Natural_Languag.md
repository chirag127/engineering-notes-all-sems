
### Regular Expressions for the notes of the Unit 1 - INTRODUCTION in the subject of Natural Language Processing

Regular expressions (or regex) are powerful tools used in computing to match text patterns. They are used for searching, replacing and manipulating text. Regex can be used to validate user input, search for patterns in a large dataset, or even for extracting data from a text file.

Regular expressions are composed of characters and symbols that define a pattern. For example, the pattern `/[0-9]/` will match any single digit number. The pattern `/[a-zA-Z]/` will match any single letter.

Regular expressions can be used to match words, phrases, and even whole sentences. For example, the pattern `/[a-zA-Z]+/` will match any word, while the pattern `/[a-zA-Z]+ [a-zA-Z]+/` will match any two words.

Regular expressions can also be used to match more complex patterns. For example, the pattern `/[0-9]{3}-[0-9]{3}-[0-9]{4}/` will match any US phone number in the format `123-456-7890`.

Regular expressions can also be used to capture specific parts of a pattern. For example, the pattern `/([a-zA-Z]+) ([a-zA-Z]+)/` will capture any two words, and store them in a variable.

Regular expressions are a powerful tool for natural language processing, and are widely used in applications such as spell-checkers, text-parsers, and sentiment analysis.

Mnemonics and learning tricks for Regular Expressions:
- `^` matches the beginning of a string.
- `$` matches the end of a string.
- `.` matches any character.
- `*` matches zero or more occurrences of the preceding character or group.
- `+` matches one or more occurrences of the preceding character or group.
- `?` matches zero or one occurrence of the preceding character or group.
- `[ ]` matches any character within the brackets.
- `[^ ]` matches any character not within the brackets.
- `|` is used to separate multiple patterns.
- `( )` is used to group multiple characters.
- `\` is used to escape special characters.
