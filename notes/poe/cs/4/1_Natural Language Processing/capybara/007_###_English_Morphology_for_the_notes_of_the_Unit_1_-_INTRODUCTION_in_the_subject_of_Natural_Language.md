### English Morphology for the notes of the Unit 1 - INTRODUCTION in the subject of Natural Language Processing

Morphology is the study of the internal structure of words and how they are formed. English morphology deals with the study of the structure of English words. Understanding the structure of English words is crucial for natural language processing. Here are some important points to understand English morphology:

1. A word is the smallest unit of language that carries meaning. Words are made up of morphemes, which are the smallest units of meaning in a language.

2. There are two types of morphemes: free morphemes and bound morphemes. Free morphemes are standalone words that can be used by themselves, such as "cat" or "dog". Bound morphemes, on the other hand, cannot stand alone and must be attached to a free morpheme to form a word. Examples of bound morphemes include prefixes like "un-" and suffixes like "-able".

3. Affixes are morphemes that are added to the beginning (prefixes) or end (suffixes) of a word to change its meaning. For example, adding the prefix "un-" to "happy" changes the meaning to "unhappy".

4. Inflectional morphemes are a type of bound morpheme that do not change the meaning of a word, but indicate grammatical information such as tense, number, and case. Examples of inflectional morphemes include "-s" for plural nouns and "-ed" for past tense verbs.

5. Derivational morphemes are a type of bound morpheme that change the meaning and sometimes the part of speech of a word. Examples of derivational morphemes include the suffix "-ness", which changes an adjective into a noun (e.g. "happy" to "happiness"), and the prefix "un-", which changes a word's meaning to its opposite (e.g. "happy" to "unhappy").

Mnemonics:
- To remember the difference between free and bound morphemes, think of free morphemes as "free-standing" words that can stand alone, and bound morphemes as being "bound" to another word to form a complete word.
- To remember the difference between inflectional and derivational morphemes, think of inflectional morphemes as "inflecting" or changing the grammatical information of a word, and derivational morphemes as "deriving" a new word from an existing one.

In conclusion, understanding English morphology is essential for natural language processing. It helps in identifying the structure of words and their meanings, which is necessary for tasks such as language translation and text analysis.
