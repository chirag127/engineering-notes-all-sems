### Word Classes for the notes of the Unit 2 - WORD LEVEL ANALYSIS in the subject of Natural Language Processing

In natural language processing, word level analysis is an essential task that involves identifying and categorizing words into different classes based on their grammatical and semantic properties. The different types of word classes or parts of speech are as follows:

1. Noun: A noun is a word that refers to a person, place, thing, or idea. It can be further classified into common nouns, proper nouns, and abstract nouns.

2. Pronoun: A pronoun is a word that is used in place of a noun. It can be further classified into personal, possessive, reflexive, and demonstrative pronouns.

3. Verb: A verb is a word that describes an action or a state of being. It can be further classified into transitive, intransitive, and linking verbs.

4. Adverb: An adverb is a word that describes or modifies a verb, adjective, or other adverb. It can be further classified into adverbs of manner, place, time, and degree.

5. Adjective: An adjective is a word that describes or modifies a noun or pronoun. It can be further classified into descriptive, comparative, and superlative adjectives.

6. Preposition: A preposition is a word that shows the relationship between a noun or pronoun and other words in a sentence. It can be further classified into simple and compound prepositions.

7. Conjunction: A conjunction is a word that connects words, phrases, or clauses. It can be further classified into coordinating and subordinating conjunctions.

8. Interjection: An interjection is a word that expresses strong feelings or emotions. It can be used to convey excitement, surprise, or other emotions.

Some mnemonics and learning tricks for remembering the different word classes are:

- Nouns are the names of people, places, and things. They can be common or proper, and they can also be abstract.
- Pronouns are words that take the place of nouns. Some examples of pronouns are he, she, it, and they.
- Verbs are action words that describe what someone or something is doing. They can be transitive or intransitive, and they can also be linking verbs.
- Adverbs are words that modify verbs, adjectives, or other adverbs. They can be used to describe how, when, where, or to what extent something is happening.
- Adjectives are words that describe or modify nouns and pronouns. They can be used to describe size, color, shape, or other qualities.
- Prepositions are words that show the relationship between nouns or pronouns and other words in a sentence. Some examples of prepositions are in, on, under, and beside.
- Conjunctions are words that connect words, phrases, or clauses. Some examples of conjunctions are and, but, or, and so.
- Interjections are words that express strong emotions, such as surprise, excitement, or frustration. Some examples of interjections are wow, oh no, and ouch.

By understanding the different word classes and their properties, we can better analyze and understand the structure and meaning of natural language sentences.
