## Unit 4 - SEMANTICS AND PRAGMATICS

Semantics and pragmatics are two important branches of linguistics that deal with the study of meaning in language. In this unit, we will explore the concepts of semantics and pragmatics and their applications in natural language processing.

### Semantics

Semantics is the study of meaning in language. It deals with how words and sentences convey meaning and how this meaning is interpreted by the listener or reader. Some important concepts in semantics are:

- **Lexical semantics**: It deals with the meaning of words and their relationships with other words in the language. It includes concepts such as synonymy, antonymy, hyponymy, and polysemy.
- **Compositional semantics**: It deals with how the meanings of words combine to form the meaning of a sentence. It includes concepts such as truth conditions, entailment, and presupposition.
- **Pragmatics**: It deals with the study of meaning in context. It includes concepts such as implicature, presupposition, and speech acts.

Some important applications of semantics in natural language processing include:

- **Word sense disambiguation**: It is the process of identifying the correct sense of a word in a given context.
- **Sentiment analysis**: It is the process of identifying the sentiment expressed in a text, such as positive, negative, or neutral.
- **Question answering**: It is the process of answering questions posed in natural language.

### Pragmatics

Pragmatics is the study of meaning in context. It deals with how language is used to convey meaning beyond the literal meaning of the words. Some important concepts in pragmatics are:

- **Speech acts**: It deals with the different functions of language, such as making statements, asking questions, giving orders, and making promises.
- **Implicature**: It deals with the meaning that is conveyed indirectly, through inference or implication.
- **Presupposition**: It deals with the assumptions that are made by the speaker or writer about the listener or reader's knowledge and beliefs.

Some important applications of pragmatics in natural language processing include:

- **Dialogue systems**: It is the process of building systems that can engage in natural language conversations with humans.
- **Information retrieval**: It is the process of retrieving relevant information from a large corpus of text.
- **Machine translation**: It is the process of translating text from one language to another.

#### Learning Tricks

- To remember the difference between semantics and pragmatics, you can think of semantics as the study of the meaning of words and sentences in isolation, while pragmatics is the study of how meaning is conveyed through context.
- To remember the different types of speech acts, you can use the acronym Searle's Taxonomy, which includes five categories: representatives, directives, commissives, expressives, and declarations.
- To remember the concepts of implicature and presupposition, you can think of implicatures as the meaning that is conveyed indirectly, while presuppositions are the assumptions that underlie the meaning of a sentence.
