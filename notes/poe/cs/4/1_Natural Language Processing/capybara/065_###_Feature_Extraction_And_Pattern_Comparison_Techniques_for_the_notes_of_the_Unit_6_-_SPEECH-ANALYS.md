### Feature Extraction And Pattern Comparison Techniques for the notes of the Unit 6 - SPEECH-ANALYSIS in the subject of Natural Language Processing

In speech analysis, feature extraction and pattern comparison techniques are used to analyze and interpret speech signals. These techniques involve extracting features from speech signals and comparing them to identify patterns and similarities. Below are some key points to remember about these techniques:

#### Feature Extraction Techniques

- Feature extraction involves extracting relevant information from speech signals that can be used to identify patterns and similarities.
- Some common feature extraction techniques include Mel-Frequency Cepstral Coefficients (MFCCs), Linear Predictive Coding (LPC), and Perceptual Linear Prediction (PLP).
- MFCCs are the most commonly used feature extraction technique in speech analysis. They involve taking the log of the magnitude of the Fourier Transform of the speech signal and mapping it onto a Mel scale.
- LPC is a technique that involves modeling the speech signal as a linear combination of past samples. This allows for the extraction of spectral information from the speech signal.
- PLP is a technique that involves modeling the speech signal as a linear combination of perceptual filters. This allows for the extraction of features that are more closely related to the human auditory system.

#### Pattern Comparison Techniques

- Pattern comparison involves comparing the features extracted from speech signals to identify patterns and similarities.
- Some common pattern comparison techniques include Dynamic Time Warping (DTW), Hidden Markov Models (HMMs), and Gaussian Mixture Models (GMMs).
- DTW is a technique that involves comparing two sequences of feature vectors by warping one sequence in time to match the other sequence.
- HMMs are a type of probabilistic model that can be used to model the temporal structure of speech signals.
- GMMs are a type of statistical model that can be used to model the probability distribution of the feature vectors extracted from speech signals.

- To remember the difference between DTW, HMMs, and GMMs, you can use the mnemonic "Dancing Time Warping, Hidden Markov Models, and Gaussian Mixture Models."
