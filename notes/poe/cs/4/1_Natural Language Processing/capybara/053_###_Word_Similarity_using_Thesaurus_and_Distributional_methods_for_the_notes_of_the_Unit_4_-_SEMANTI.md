### Word Similarity using Thesaurus and Distributional methods for the notes of the Unit 4 - SEMANTICS AND PRAGMATICS in the subject of Natural Language Processing

Word similarity is an important task in natural language processing that aims to measure the degree of relatedness between two words. There are several methods to calculate word similarity, including thesaurus-based and distributional methods. In this section, we will discuss both methods in detail.

#### Thesaurus-based Methods

Thesaurus-based methods rely on pre-defined knowledge resources, such as WordNet, to calculate word similarity. WordNet is a lexical database that groups English words into sets of synonyms, called synsets, and provides short definitions and usage examples for each word. Thesaurus-based methods calculate word similarity based on the similarity between the synsets of two words. Some popular thesaurus-based methods are:

- Wu-Palmer Similarity
- Resnik Similarity
- Jiang-Conrath Similarity
- Lin Similarity

#### Distributional Methods

Distributional methods calculate word similarity based on the distributional patterns of words in a corpus. These methods assume that words that appear in similar contexts are semantically similar. Distributional methods represent words as vectors in a high-dimensional space, where the dimensions correspond to the co-occurrence frequencies of words in a corpus. Some popular distributional methods are:

- Cosine Similarity
- Jaccard Similarity
- Euclidean Distance
- Manhattan Distance

#### Advantages and Disadvantages

Thesaurus-based methods have the advantage of relying on pre-defined knowledge resources, which can provide accurate results for words in the resource. However, these methods may not perform well for words that are not present in the resource or for words with multiple senses.

Distributional methods, on the other hand, do not require pre-defined knowledge resources and can be applied to any corpus. These methods can capture the nuances of word meanings and can handle polysemy, the phenomenon where a word has multiple meanings. However, distributional methods require large amounts of data and can be computationally expensive.

#### Applications

Word similarity has several applications in natural language processing, including:

- Information retrieval
- Text classification
- Machine translation
- Sentiment analysis
