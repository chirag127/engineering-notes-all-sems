### Articulatory Phonetics for the notes of the Unit 5 - BASIC CONCEPTS of Speech Processing in the subject of Natural Language Processing

Articulatory phonetics is one of the subfields of phonetics that deals with the physical aspects of speech production. It involves the study of the organs of speech, such as the tongue, lips, teeth, and vocal cords, and how they are used to create speech sounds. In this section, we will discuss the basic concepts of articulatory phonetics.

#### Organs of Speech

The organs of speech are the parts of the body that are involved in producing speech sounds. These include:

- Lungs: The lungs are responsible for providing the air that is used to create speech sounds.

- Trachea: The trachea, also known as the windpipe, connects the lungs to the larynx.

- Larynx: The larynx contains the vocal cords, which are responsible for producing sound.

- Pharynx: The pharynx is the cavity behind the mouth and nose that connects the nasal and oral cavities to the larynx.

- Oral cavity: The oral cavity includes the lips, teeth, tongue, and hard and soft palates. It is responsible for producing consonant and vowel sounds.

- Nasal cavity: The nasal cavity is responsible for producing nasal sounds.

#### Consonants and Vowels

Consonants and vowels are the two basic types of speech sounds. Consonants are produced by obstructing the flow of air through the vocal tract, while vowels are produced by allowing the air to flow freely through the vocal tract.

- Consonants: Consonants are classified based on the place of articulation, manner of articulation, and voicing. The place of articulation refers to where the obstruction of the airflow occurs, such as the lips, teeth, or tongue. The manner of articulation refers to how the airflow is obstructed, such as a complete closure or a partial constriction. Voicing refers to whether the vocal cords are vibrating or not.

- Vowels: Vowels are classified based on the position of the tongue in the oral cavity. The position of the tongue can be high, mid, or low, and the front, central, or back of the oral cavity.

#### Articulatory Phonetics Mnemonics and Learning Tricks

- To remember the organs of speech, use the mnemonic "LTLPOFN" (Lungs, Trachea, Larynx, Pharynx, Oral cavity, Nasal cavity).

- To remember the classification of consonants, use the mnemonic "PAM VOICED" (Place of articulation, Manner of articulation, Voicing).

- To remember the classification of vowels, use the mnemonic "HIPFACOB" (High, Mid, Low, Front, Central, Back).

In conclusion, articulatory phonetics is an important subfield of phonetics that deals with the physical aspects of speech production. Understanding the organs of speech, consonants, and vowels is essential for studying speech processing in natural language processing.
