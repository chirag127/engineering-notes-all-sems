### Grammar-based LM for the notes of the Unit 1 - INTRODUCTION in the subject of Natural Language Processing

Grammar-based Language Models (LMs) are a type of statistical language model that uses a formal grammar to generate sentences. These models are based on the idea that the meaning of a sentence can be decomposed into its constituent parts, such as nouns, verbs, and adjectives, and that the probability of a sentence can be calculated based on the probability of its parts.

In this unit of Natural Language Processing, you will learn about the concept of Grammar-based LM and its working. Here are some important points to keep in mind:

- Grammar-based LMs use formal grammars, such as Context-Free Grammars (CFGs) or Dependency Grammars, to generate sentences. These grammars define a set of rules or constraints that determine the structure of a sentence.
- The probability of a sentence is calculated based on the probability of its constituent parts or the probability of a parse tree that represents the sentence.
- The probability of a parse tree is calculated by multiplying the probabilities of its constituent parts based on the grammar rules.
- Grammar-based LMs can be used for various Natural Language Processing tasks, such as speech recognition, machine translation, and text generation.
- One of the advantages of Grammar-based LMs is that they can handle complex sentence structures and capture the relationships between different parts of a sentence.
- However, Grammar-based LMs can be computationally expensive and require large amounts of training data to achieve high accuracy.
- To improve the performance of Grammar-based LMs, various techniques can be used, such as smoothing and backoff strategies, which help to handle unseen or rare words and phrases.
