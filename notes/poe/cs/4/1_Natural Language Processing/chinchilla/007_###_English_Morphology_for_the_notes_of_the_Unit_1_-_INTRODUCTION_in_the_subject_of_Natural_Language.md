### English Morphology for the notes of the Unit 1 - INTRODUCTION in the subject of Natural Language Processing

English morphology is the study of the structure of words and the rules governing their formation. It is an essential component of natural language processing (NLP) that deals with the automatic processing of human language by computers. Here are some important concepts and terms related to English morphology:

1. Morpheme: The smallest unit of meaning in a language is called a morpheme. It can be a word or a part of a word that carries meaning. For example, "cat" is a morpheme, and so is "un-" in "unhappy."

2. Inflection: Inflection is the process of adding affixes to a word to indicate tense, number, case, gender, or other grammatical categories. For example, "walk" changes to "walks" in the third person singular present tense.

3. Derivation: Derivation is the process of adding affixes to a word to create a new word with a different meaning. For example, "happy" becomes "unhappy" when the prefix "un-" is added.

4. Stem: The stem is the core meaning of a word, to which affixes can be added. For example, the stem of "walking" is "walk."

5. Root: The root is the basic form of a word, which cannot be further analyzed into smaller parts. For example, the root of "unhappiness" is "happy."

6. Lexical categories: Words can be classified into different categories based on their grammatical and semantic properties. The major lexical categories in English are nouns, verbs, adjectives, adverbs, pronouns, prepositions, and conjunctions.

7. Inflectional affixes: Affixes that are added to a word to indicate grammatical categories like tense, number, and case are called inflectional affixes. Examples of inflectional affixes in English include "-s" for plural nouns, "-ed" for past tense verbs, and "-ing" for present participle verbs.

8. Derivational affixes: Affixes that are added to a word to create a new word with a different meaning are called derivational affixes. Examples of derivational affixes in English include "-able" to form adjectives, "-ly" to form adverbs, and "-er" to form nouns indicating a person or thing that performs the action of the verb.

Mnemonics and Learning Tricks:

1. To remember the difference between inflection and derivation, think of inflection as "bending" a word to indicate different grammatical categories, while derivation is "creating" a new word with a different meaning.

2. To remember the major lexical categories, use the acronym "NAVAPPC" (Nouns, Verbs, Adjectives, Pronouns, Prepositions, Adverbs, Conjunctions).

English morphology is a complex and important aspect of NLP that is essential for building accurate and efficient language models. By understanding the basic concepts and terms of English morphology, you can better analyze and process natural language data.
