## Unit 4 - SEMANTICS AND PRAGMATICS

Semantics and pragmatics are two important branches of linguistics that deal with the study of meaning in language. This unit focuses on the key concepts and theories related to semantics and pragmatics.

### Semantics

Semantics is the branch of linguistics that deals with the study of meaning in language. It involves the analysis of the relationships between words, phrases, and sentences and how they convey meaning. The following are some important concepts in semantics:

- **Lexical semantics**: This involves the study of the meanings of individual words and how they combine to form larger units of meaning.
- **Compositionality**: This refers to the principle that the meaning of a complex expression is determined by the meanings of its constituent parts and how they are combined.
- **Sense and reference**: Sense refers to the mental concept or image that a word or phrase evokes, while reference refers to the actual object or entity in the world that the word or phrase refers to.
- **Truth conditions**: This refers to the conditions under which a sentence is true or false.

### Pragmatics

Pragmatics is the branch of linguistics that deals with the study of language use in context. It involves the analysis of the ways in which people use language to achieve their communicative goals. The following are some important concepts in pragmatics:

- **Speech acts**: This refers to the actions that speakers perform through their use of language, such as making requests, giving orders, and making promises.
- **Grice's cooperative principle**: This principle states that speakers and listeners assume that they are cooperating with each other to achieve their communicative goals, and that they will follow certain rules of conversation to ensure mutual understanding.
- **Presupposition**: This refers to the assumptions that speakers make about what their listeners already know or believe, and how they use language to convey new information or ideas.
- **Implicature**: This refers to the meaning that is conveyed indirectly through the use of language, such as by implying something without explicitly stating it.

### Mnemonics and Learning Tricks

- To remember the difference between sense and reference, think of sense as the subjective concept in your mind, while reference is the objective thing in the world that the word refers to.
- To remember the components of Grice's cooperative principle (quality, quantity, relevance, and manner), think of the acronym "QMR-Quality Matters Relevantly".
- To remember the difference between presupposition and implicature, think of presupposition as the assumptions that you make before you say something, while implicature is the meaning that you convey indirectly through your choice of words.

Overall, understanding semantics and pragmatics is crucial for developing a deeper understanding of how language works and how we use it to communicate effectively with others. By studying these concepts, we can gain insights into the complex mechanisms that underlie our ability to use language to convey meaning and achieve our communicative goals.
