### Speech Fundamentals for the notes of the Unit 5 - BASIC CONCEPTS of Speech Processing in the subject of Natural Language Processing

Speech is the primary mode of communication between humans. Speech processing involves the study of how speech signals are generated, transmitted, and received. In this unit, we will cover the fundamentals of speech processing, which include the following:

1. Speech Production: Speech production refers to the process of generating speech sounds. It involves the coordination of various organs such as the lungs, vocal cords, and the articulators (tongue, lips, and jaw). The sounds produced by the articulators are called phonemes, which are the basic units of speech.

2. Speech Signal: A speech signal is a time-varying waveform that represents the acoustic energy produced by speech. It is a complex signal that contains information about the phonemes, prosody, and other characteristics of speech.

3. Speech Perception: Speech perception refers to the process of decoding the speech signal into meaningful units such as words and sentences. It involves the recognition of phonemes, the identification of words based on their context, and the interpretation of the speaker's intent.

4. Spectrogram: A spectrogram is a visual representation of the speech signal that shows the frequency content of the signal over time. It is a useful tool for analyzing the spectral characteristics of speech.

5. Pitch and Formants: Pitch refers to the perceived frequency of a sound, and it is related to the fundamental frequency of the speech signal. Formants are the resonant frequencies of the vocal tract, which are responsible for the distinctive sounds of different vowels.

6. Mel-Frequency Cepstral Coefficients (MFCCs): MFCCs are a feature extraction technique used in speech processing. They are derived from the spectral envelope of the speech signal and are used to represent the acoustic characteristics of speech.

7. Speech Recognition: Speech recognition involves the automatic transcription of speech into text or other symbolic representations. It is a challenging task due to the variability of speech signals and the complexity of language.

Mnemonics and learning tricks:

1. Remember the acronym PFS (Production, Signal, Perception) to recall the three main components of speech processing.

2. To remember the difference between pitch and formants, think of pitch as the "highness" or "lowness" of a sound, while formants are the "shape" of the sound.

3. To remember MFCCs, think of them as a "fingerprint" of the speech signal, capturing its unique acoustic characteristics.

In conclusion, speech processing is a complex and fascinating field that involves the study of how humans produce, perceive, and recognize speech. Understanding the fundamentals of speech processing is essential for developing effective speech recognition and synthesis systems.
