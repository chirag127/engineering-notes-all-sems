### Issues in PoS tagging for the notes of the Unit 2 - WORD LEVEL ANALYSIS in the subject of Natural Language Processing

Part of speech (PoS) tagging is a crucial task in natural language processing (NLP). It involves assigning a specific part of speech to every word in a sentence. However, PoS tagging is not a straightforward task, and there are several issues that need to be addressed for accurate tagging.

Here are some of the most significant issues in PoS tagging:

1. Ambiguity: One of the primary challenges in PoS tagging is ambiguity. Many words can have multiple meanings, and their part of speech can change depending on the context they are used in. For example, the word "bank" can be a noun (a financial institution) or a verb (to deposit money). To overcome this ambiguity, PoS taggers use statistical models that consider the probability of a word being a particular part of speech based on the surrounding words.

2. Rare Words: PoS taggers may struggle with rare and uncommon words that are not present in their training data. For instance, if a tagger has never encountered the word "cryptocurrency," it may not know how to tag it correctly. One way to overcome this issue is to use a combination of rule-based and statistical models to increase the accuracy of PoS tagging.

3. Idioms and Collocations: Idioms and collocations refer to a group of words that are commonly used together but may not make sense if taken literally. For example, "kick the bucket" is an idiom that means "to die." PoS taggers may struggle with these expressions, as they may not be able to assign the correct part of speech for each word in the expression. One way to address this issue is to use a specialized lexicon that contains information about idioms and collocations.

4. Morphology: Morphology refers to the study of the structure of words and how they are formed from smaller units called morphemes. PoS taggers need to consider the morphology of words to assign the correct part of speech. For example, the word "cats" is a plural noun, while "cat's" is a possessive noun. To overcome this issue, PoS taggers use morphological analysis to identify the root form of a word and assign the appropriate part of speech.

5. Domain-specific Language: PoS taggers may not be effective in tagging domain-specific language, such as legal or medical terms, that are not commonly used in everyday language. One way to address this issue is to train the tagger on a corpus specific to the domain.

Mnemonics and learning tricks:

1. Use context clues: Look at the words surrounding the target word to determine its part of speech.

2. Memorize common collocations and idioms: Familiarize yourself with common expressions to help with PoS tagging.

3. Practice, practice, practice: The more you practice PoS tagging, the easier it becomes to identify the correct part of speech.

In conclusion, PoS tagging is a challenging task in NLP, and there are several issues that need to be addressed for accurate tagging. By understanding these issues and using appropriate techniques, PoS taggers can improve their accuracy and effectiveness.
