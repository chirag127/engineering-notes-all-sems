### Word Classes for the notes of the Unit 2 - WORD LEVEL ANALYSIS in the subject of Natural Language Processing

In natural language processing, word classes, also known as parts of speech, are used to categorize words based on their syntactic and semantic roles in a sentence. Understanding word classes is important for tasks such as text classification, information retrieval, and machine translation. Here are the most common word classes:

1. Nouns - words that refer to people, places, things, or ideas. Mnemonic: Think of a "noun" as a "name" for something.

2. Verbs - words that describe actions or states of being. Mnemonic: Think of "verbing" as "doing" something.

3. Adjectives - words that describe or modify nouns or pronouns. Mnemonic: Think of "adjectives" as adding "adjectives" to a person or thing - describing their qualities.

4. Adverbs - words that modify verbs, adjectives, or other adverbs. Mnemonic: Think of "adverbs" as adding "adverbial" information about how, when, or where something is done.

5. Pronouns - words that replace nouns to avoid repetition. Mnemonic: Think of "pro" as "in place of".

6. Prepositions - words that indicate the relationship between a noun or pronoun and other words in a sentence. Mnemonic: Think of "prepositions" as providing "pre" information about where something is located or how it relates to other things.

7. Conjunctions - words that connect words, phrases, or clauses. Mnemonic: Think of "conjunctions" as "joining" words.

8. Interjections - words that express emotion or feeling. Mnemonic: Think of "interjections" as "interrupting" the flow of a sentence with emotion.

It's important to note that some words can belong to multiple word classes depending on their usage in a sentence. For example, in the sentence "I will book a flight", "book" is a verb, but in the sentence "This is my book", "book" is a noun.

Knowing the word classes can help in tasks such as part-of-speech tagging, which involves labeling each word in a sentence with its corresponding word class. This can be useful in text analysis and language modeling.
