### Production And Classification Of Speech Sounds

Speech sounds are the basic units of spoken language. They are produced by the organs of speech, including the lungs, larynx, pharynx, tongue, and lips. In this section, we will learn about the production and classification of speech sounds.

#### Production of Speech Sounds

The production of speech sounds involves the following processes:

1. **Respiration:** The lungs provide the air supply for speech.

2. **Phonation:** The vocal cords vibrate to produce sound.

3. **Resonation:** The sound waves produced by the vocal cords are shaped by the vocal tract, including the pharynx, mouth, and nasal cavity.

4. **Articulation:** The tongue, lips, and other articulators modify the sound waves to produce specific speech sounds.

#### Classification of Speech Sounds

Speech sounds can be classified into two major categories:

1. **Consonants:** Consonants are produced by obstructing or restricting the airflow in the vocal tract. They can be further classified based on the manner of articulation, place of articulation, and voicing.

    - **Manner of articulation:** This refers to how the airflow is obstructed or restricted. Consonants can be classified as stops, fricatives, affricates, nasals, liquids, or glides.

    - **Place of articulation:** This refers to where in the vocal tract the airflow is obstructed or restricted. Consonants can be classified as bilabial, labiodental, dental, alveolar, post-alveolar, palatal, velar, or glottal.

    - **Voicing:** This refers to whether the vocal cords are vibrating or not. Consonants can be classified as voiced or voiceless.

2. **Vowels:** Vowels are produced without obstructing or restricting the airflow in the vocal tract. They can be classified based on the position of the tongue and lips.

    - **Position of the tongue:** Vowels can be classified as front, central, or back vowels, depending on the position of the tongue.

    - **Position of the lips:** Vowels can be classified as rounded or unrounded, depending on the position of the lips.

By understanding the production and classification of speech sounds, we can better analyze and understand spoken language.
