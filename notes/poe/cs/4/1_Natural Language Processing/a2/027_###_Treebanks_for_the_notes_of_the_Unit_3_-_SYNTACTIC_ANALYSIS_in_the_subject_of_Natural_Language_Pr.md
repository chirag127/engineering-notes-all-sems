### Treebanks

- Treebanks are collections of syntactically annotated sentences. They contain syntactic trees representing the structure of sentences.
- They are useful for training Statistical Parsers and studying syntactic phenomena of a language.
- Some popular treebanks are:
- Penn Treebank: Contains syntactic trees of Wall Street Journal articles. Used to train Statistical Parsers for English.
- Negra Treebank: Contains syntactic trees of German newspaper articles. Used to train Statistical Parsers for German.
- Prague Dependency Treebank: Contains dependency trees of Czech sentences. Used to train Dependency Parsers for Czech.

Advantages:

- Provide large amounts of syntactically annotated data required to train Statistical Parsers.
- Help in analyzing the syntax of a language and identifying syntactic phenomena and patterns.

Disadvantages:

- Tedious and time-consuming to create treebanks manually.
- Might contain annotation errors and inconsistencies which can affect parser performance.
- Do not cover all syntactic constructions and styles. Limited coverage.

Applications:

- Train Statistical Parsers and improve their accuracy.
- Study syntactic properties of a language.
- Compare syntactic differences across languages.

Mnemonics/Learning Tricks:

- Remember treebanks as collections of 'syntax trees' which are like 'family trees' of sentences helping understand their 'family structure'.
- The more the 'family members' (sentences) in the treebank, the better we can understand the 'family' (language) syntax.
