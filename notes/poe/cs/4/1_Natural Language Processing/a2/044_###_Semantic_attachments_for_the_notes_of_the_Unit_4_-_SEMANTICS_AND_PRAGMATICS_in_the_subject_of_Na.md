### Semantic attachments for the notes of the Unit 4 - SEMANTICS AND PRAGMATICS in the subject of Natural Language Processing

1. Semantics - The study of meaning in language. It focuses on the relation between linguistic expressions and entities in the world.
- Referential semantics - Study of how words and phrases refer to objects, people or concepts in the real world.
- Truth-conditional semantics - Study of the conditions under which statements are considered true or false.
- Conceptual semantics - Study of the cognitive structures and concepts that underlie the meaning of words and phrases.

Mnemonics:
- Words have senses, not just reference
- Meaning depends on context, not just words

2. Concepts - Mental representations of categories or classes of objects, events, activities, etc.
- Prototypes - The most typical or central members of a category.
- Family resemblance - Members of a category share a complex pattern of attributes, with no one attribute being common to all members.

3. Semantic features - The attributes, properties or characteristics that determine the meanings of words and concepts.
- Necessary features - Features that must be present for a word or concept to have meaning.
- Sufficient features - Features that are enough to determine the meaning of a word or concept.

4. Semantic relations - The relationships that exist between the meanings of words and phrases.
- Synonymy - Words have the same or similar meanings.
- Antonymy - Words have opposite meanings.
- Hyponymy - A word representing a subclass of the entities denoted by another word.
- Meronymy - A word representing a part of the whole denoted by another word.
