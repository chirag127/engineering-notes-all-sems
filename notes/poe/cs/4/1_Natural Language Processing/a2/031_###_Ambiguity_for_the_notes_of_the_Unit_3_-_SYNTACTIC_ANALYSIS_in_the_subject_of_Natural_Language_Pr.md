### Ambiguity for the notes of the Unit 3 - SYNTACTIC ANALYSIS in the subject of Natural Language Processing

- Ambiguity refers to uncertainty or inconsistency in meaning. In NLP, ambiguity arises when a sentence can have more than one possible meaning due to the grammatical structure.
- Types of ambiguity:
    - Lexical ambiguity: When a word has more than one meaning like 'light' can refer to not heavy or bright.
    - Structural ambiguity: When the structure of a sentence can lead to multiple interpretations like 'flying planes can be dangerous'. Here, it is ambiguous whether the planes are flying or the act of flying planes is dangerous.
    - Referential ambiguity: When the pronoun reference is unclear like in 'Mary said she would help John after he helped her'. It is unclear who 'he' and 'her' refer to.
- Resolving ambiguity: Ambiguity needs to be resolved for NLP tasks like machine translation, summarization, intent classification, etc. Some ways to resolve ambiguity are:
    - Using context: The context surrounding the ambiguous portion can help determine the correct meaning.
    - Using world knowledge: Common sense and world knowledge about the typical scenarios can help deduce the correct interpretation.
    - Considering probabilities: The interpretation that is more probable or frequent can be selected. For example, 'light' is more likely to refer to 'not heavy' than 'bright' in most cases.
- Advantages of resolving ambiguity:
    - Improves the performance of NLP systems and chatbots.
    - Enables more accurate intent classification and response generation.
    - Results in better machine translation and summarization.
- Disadvantages of resolving ambiguity:
    - Context and world knowledge are not always sufficient to determine the correct meaning.
    - Probabilistic methods can lead to incorrect interpretations for rare or novel cases.
    - Ambiguity resolution is a challenging task and can be time-consuming.
