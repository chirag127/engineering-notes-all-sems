### Acoustics Of Speech Production

The production of speech can be divided into three main stages:

1. Respiration - Air is drawn into the lungs and then exhaled. The rate of exhalation controls parameters such as speech rate and volume.
2. Phonation - The exhaled air vibrates the vocal cords, which produces a periodic pulsating flow of air (voiced sound) or non-periodic turbulent air flow (voiceless sounds). The frequency of vocal cord vibration controls the pitch of the speech.
3. Articulation - The vocal tract acts as a resonance cavity that filters the sound from the vocal cords. Changing the shape and properties of the vocal tract by moving the lips, tongue, and jaw produces the individual speech sounds.

Some key points about the acoustics of speech production:

- The vocal cords can vibrate at different frequencies, controlling the pitch of the voice. A higher vibration frequency produces a higher pitch.
- Voiced sounds are produced by vibrating vocal cords, voiceless sounds are produced by turbulent air flow.
- The shape of the vocal tract acts as a filter on the sound from the vocal cords. Changing the shape can produce different speech sounds.
- Nasal sounds are produced by lowering the velum to allow air to flow through the nasal cavity.
- Plosive sounds are produced by briefly blocking and then releasing air flow.
- Fricative sounds are produced by creating turbulent air flow through a narrow channel.
