### Word Senses for the notes of the Unit 4 - SEMANTICS AND PRAGMATICS in the subject of Natural Language Processing

- Polysemy: A word having multiple related senses. For example, *bank* can refer to a financial institution or the land alongside a river. Mnemonic: *Poly* means many, referring to multiple senses.
- Homonymy: Words that have the same spelling and pronunciation but differ in meaning. For example, *tear* as in crying vs. *tear* as in ripping paper. Mnemonic: The homonyms are *homos* (same) in terms of spelling/pronunciation but *nyms* (different) in terms of meaning.
- Synonymy: Words that have the same or similar meanings. For example, *big*, *large*, *huge*. Mnemonic: *Syn* means same/together, referring to same/similar meanings.
- Hyponymy: A hyponym is a more specific term encompassed by a more general hypernym. For example, *dog* is a hyponym of *animal* (the hypernym). Mnemonic: The hyponym (*hypo* meaning under) is under/part of the hypernym in a is-a relationship.
- Meronymy: A meronym refers to a part of a whole. For example, *wheel* is a meronym of *car*. Mnemonic: The meronym is a *part* of the whole.

Advantages of understanding word senses:
- Reduces ambiguity in language understanding
- Enables inference of implied meanings
- Useful for tasks like semantic similarity/relation classification

Applications:
- Question answering systems
- Summarization
- Sentiment analysis
- Recommendation systems
