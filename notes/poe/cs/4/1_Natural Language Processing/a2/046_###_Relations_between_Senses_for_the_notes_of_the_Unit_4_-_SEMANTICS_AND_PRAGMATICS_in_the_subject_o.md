### Relations between Senses for the notes of the Unit 4 - SEMANTICS AND PRAGMATICS in the subject of Natural Language Processing

The senses or meanings of words are related to each other in various ways:

1. Synonymy: Words that have the same or similar meanings are called synonyms e.g. big and large, car and automobile. Synonyms are useful as they allow us to avoid repetition and make our language more interesting.

2. Antonymy: Words that have opposite meanings are called antonyms e.g. good and bad, up and down, left and right. Antonyms are useful in creating contrasts and adding variety to our language.

3. Hyponymy: The relationship between a generic term and a more specific term is called hyponymy. The specific term is called the hyponym. For example, the generic term vehicle is a hyponym of the more specific terms like car, bus, truck, etc. Hyponymy is a hierarchical relation where the hyponym is a type of the generic term.

4. Meronymy: The meronymic relation holds between a whole and its parts. For example, wheel is a meronym of car, where wheel is a part of the whole car. This relation is also hierarchical but in the opposite direction as that of hyponymy.

5. Polysemy: Words that have multiple related senses are said to be polysemous. The various senses of a polysemous word are usually closely related and often originate from a single basic sense. For example, the word 'light' can refer to brightness, weight, or a flame, though the senses are closely linked. Polysemy allows economy of vocabulary.
