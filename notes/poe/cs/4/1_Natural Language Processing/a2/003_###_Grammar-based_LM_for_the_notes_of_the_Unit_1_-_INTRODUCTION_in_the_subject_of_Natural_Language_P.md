### Grammar-based LM for the notes of the Unit 1 - INTRODUCTION in the subject of Natural Language Processing

- Grammar-based models use handcrafted rules to model language. They are rule-based models that encode linguistic knowledge and use it to assign probabilities to sentences and sequences of words.
- Some examples of grammar-based models are:
    - Probabilistic context-free grammars (PCFGs)
    - Head-driven phrase structure grammar (HPSG)
    - Lexical-functional grammar (LFG)
- Advantages:
    - Incorporate human linguistic knowledge
    - Often more interpretable
- Disadvantages:
    - Difficult to build large, broad-coverage grammars
    - Hard to learn parameters from data
- Examples: Sentence generation, machine translation
- Applications: Grammar checkers, speech recognition

**Mnemonics:**
Think of grammar-based models as following a set of linguistic rules to understand language, just like how you learned grammar in school. They are rule-based and incorporate human knowledge of grammar.

**Learning trick:**
Compare grammar-based models to how you learned grammar in school. Think of them as following rules and probabilities to understand language based on those rules, similar to how you learned to understand correct/incorrect sentences via grammar lessons. This can help in remembering their properties and differences from other models.
