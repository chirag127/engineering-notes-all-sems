### Production And Classification Of Speech Sounds

Speech sounds are produced by a complex production mechanism involving various parts of vocal tract like larynx, pharynx, tongue, lips, etc. The manner in which these articulators are adjusted results in different speech sounds.

Based on the manner of production, speech sounds can be classified into:

- Vowels: Produced with relatively open vocal tract. Examples: /a/, /e/, /i/, /o/, /u/.
- Consonants: Produced with constriction or closure at some point in the vocal tract. Examples: /k/, /p/, /t/, /b/, /d/, /g/, etc.

Consonants can be further classified into:

- Plosives or stops: Complete closure of vocal tract which is then released. Examples: /p/, /b/, /t/, /d/, /k/, /g/
- Fricatives: Narrow constriction in vocal tract causing turbulence. Examples: /s/, /z/, /f/, /v/, /sh/, /zh/
- Nasals: Open nasal cavity resulting in nasalized sound. Examples: /m/, /n/
- Liquids: Produced with lateral flow of air. Examples: /l/, /r/
- Affricates: Combination of plosive and fricative. Examples: /ch/, /jh/

Some key points to remember:

- Vowels are produced with relatively open vocal tract while consonants are produced with constriction or closure in vocal tract.
- Consonants can be classified into plosives, fricatives, nasals, liquids and affricates based on manner of articulation.
- Places of articulation can be bilabial, labiodental, dental, alveolar, palatal, velar, glottal, etc. depending on which articulator is involved.
- Voicing determines whether vocal cords vibrate or not resulting in voiced and unvoiced sounds respectively.

The above classification and points can help in distinguishing between different speech sounds and understanding the process of speech production in detail. Mnemonics and other learning tricks can be used to memorize the classification categories and examples of different speech sounds.
