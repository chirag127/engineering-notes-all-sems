### Requirements for representation for the notes of the Unit 4 - SEMANTICS AND PRAGMATICS in the subject of Natural Language Processing

1. Meaning Representation
- Semantic networks: Graphical models representing word meanings and relations between them. Nodes represent concepts and edges represent relations.
- Conceptual dependency theory: Sentences are represented in terms of relationships between concepts.
- Frame semantics: Meaning is represented in terms of frames or schematic structures. Words evoke these frames which represent stereotypical situations.
- Prototype theory: Categories are represented by prototypes i.e. the clearest examples of a category. Other examples are compared to the prototype.

2. Context and Pragmatics
- Context: Linguistic, situational and cultural context is important to determine meaning. Ambiguity can be resolved using context.
- Implicature: What is implied or suggested rather than literally expressed. Based on Grice's cooperative principle and maxims of conversation.
- Speech acts: Utterances can be used to perform actions like requests, promises, apologies, etc. apart from just conveying meaning.

Mnemonics:
- Semantic networks: Crazy concepts connected crazily
- Conceptual dependency theory: Concepts depend on relationships
- Frame semantics: Meaning fits into frames
- Prototype theory: Best examples form categories

Advantages: Provide a structured way to represent meaning. Help in resolving ambiguity and inference. Useful for NLP applications like semantic analysis, question answering systems, machine translation, etc.
Disadvantages: Difficult to create comprehensive representations. Lack consensus on which approach is best. Context-dependent and pragmatics are hard to capture.
