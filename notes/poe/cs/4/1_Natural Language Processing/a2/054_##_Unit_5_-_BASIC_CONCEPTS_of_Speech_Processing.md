## Unit 5 - BASIC CONCEPTS of Speech Processing

* Speech Processing refers to the study of techniques for digitally representing, processing and recognizing speech signals. The main areas of speech processing are:
** Speech Coding - Techniques for compressing speech signals and storing or transmitting them efficiently. Examples: Linear Predictive Coding (LPC), Code Excited Linear Prediction (CELP).
** Speech Enhancement - Techniques for removing noise from speech signals and improving speech quality. Examples: Spectral Subtraction, Wiener Filter.
** Speech Recognition - Techniques for converting speech signals into text. Examples: Hidden Markov Models, Deep Neural Networks.
* The basic steps in a speech recognition system are:
** Speech Acquisition - Capturing the speech wave using a microphone.
** Framing - Dividing the speech wave into short overlapping frames of 20-50ms to facilitate further processing.
** Feature Extraction - Extracting characteristic features from each frame that represent the short-term speech signal. Examples: Mel-Frequency Cepstral Coefficients (MFCCs), Linear Predictive Coding (LPC) coefficients.
** Modelling - Creating statistical models that can map features to phonemes or words. Examples: Hidden Markov Models, Neural Networks.
** Decoding - Using the statistical models to convert features into corresponding phonemes or words.
* Some advantages of speech recognition are:
** Hands-free - No physical devices needed.
** Fast - Can be faster than typing in many use cases.
** Convenient - Useful for people unable to use their hands.
* Some disadvantages of speech recognition are:
** Background noise sensitivity - Performance degrades in noisy environments.
** Limited vocabulary - Systems are often limited to specific domains or vocabularies.
** Speech variations - Systems may not handle variations in speech rate, accent, pronunciation, etc. well.
** Limited accuracy - Despite recent advances, speech recognition is not yet as accurate as human transcription.
