### Review Of Digital Signal Processing Concepts for the notes of the Unit 5 - BASIC CONCEPTS of Speech Processing in the subject of Natural Language Processing

- A digital signal is a signal that is represented by a sequence of numbers that may correspond to physical quantities such as voltage or magnetic intensity. Digital signal processing (DSP) is the use of digital processing, such as by computers or more specialized digital signal processors, to perform a wide variety of signal processing operations.
- The digital signal is usually quantized in amplitude and sampled in time. Common characteristics of digital signals include:
    - Discreteness: The signal takes on only certain discrete values
    - Accuracy: The values have finite precision due to quantization
- Some important applications of DSP include:
    - Audio signal processing - noise reduction, MP3 compression, cellular telephones
    - Image processing - compression, enhancement
    - Speech processing - speech recognition, synthesis
    - Biomedical signal processing - ECG, EEG, EMG analysis
    - Radar signal processing
    - Seismology - studying earthquakes
    - Spectral analysis - finding the frequencies that make up a signal
- The main components of a DSP system are:
    - Digitization: Conversion of a continuous-time analog signal to a discrete digital signal.
    - Storage: Saving the digital data.
    - Processing: Performing operations on the digital data.
    - Reconstruction: Conversion of the processed digital signal back to a continuous-time analog signal.
- Examples of operations performed on digital signals:
    - Filtering - selective frequency suppression or amplification
    - Correlation - finding similarities between signals
    - Convolution - linear filtering process
    - Fast Fourier transform (FFT) - decomposing a signal into its frequency components
    - Discrete Fourier transform (DFT) - frequency analysis of discrete-time signals
- Some advantages of DSP over analog signal processing are:
    - Increased accuracy and precision
    - Easier signal manipulation and mathematical processing
    - Adaptability and programmability
    - Automation through software control
    - Standardized interfaces and components
- Some disadvantages of DSP are:
    - Requirement for high-speed hardware for real-time processing
    - Requirement for analog-to-digital and digital-to-analog converters
    - Prone to quantization errors if insufficient precision is used
