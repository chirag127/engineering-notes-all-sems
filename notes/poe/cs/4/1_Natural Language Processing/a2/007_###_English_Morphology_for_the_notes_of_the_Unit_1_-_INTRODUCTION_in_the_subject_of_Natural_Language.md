### English Morphology for the notes of the Unit 1 - INTRODUCTION in the subject of Natural Language Processing

- **Morphology** is the study of word structure and formation. It is concerned with the internal structure of words and the rules by which words are formed.
- In English, words can be formed by affixation (adding prefixes and suffixes), compounding (joining two or more words together), shortening, and other processes.
- Some key concepts in morphology are:

- **Root** - The core part of a word that carries the main semantic content. For example, 'friend' is the root of 'friendship' and 'friendly'.
- **Stem** - The part of the word that is left after all affixes are removed. The stem is not necessarily the same as the root. For example, the stem of 'friends' is 'friend', but the root is just 'friend'.
- **Affix** - A prefix or suffix attached to a root or stem to form a new word or new form of a word. For example, '-s' and '-ed' are affixes in 'friends' and 'played'.
- **Inflectional affix** - An affix that is used to indicate grammatical functions like plurality, tense, etc. For example, '-s' and '-ed' in the previous examples are inflectional affixes.
- **Derivational affix** - An affix that is used to derive a new word from an existing word, often changing the meaning or part of speech. For example, '-ness' in 'successfulness', '-ful' in 'careful', etc. are derivational affixes.

- Some useful mnemonics and learning tricks for remembering the key concepts -

- Think of roots as the base of a tree and stems/words as the branches growing from the root. Affixes are what create the branches.
- Inflectional affixes are like accessories that change the look but not the core nature of the word. Derivational affixes are like adding extensions that change the core nature of the word.
- Memorize common English inflectional affixes like '-s', '-ed', '-ing', '-er', '-est', etc. Derivational affixes have broader categories like '-ness', '-ful', '-less', etc.
