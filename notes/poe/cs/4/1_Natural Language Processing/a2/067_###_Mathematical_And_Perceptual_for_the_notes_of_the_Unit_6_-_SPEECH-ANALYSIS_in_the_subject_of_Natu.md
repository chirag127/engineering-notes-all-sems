### Mathematical And Perceptual for the notes of the Unit 6 - SPEECH-ANALYSIS in the subject of Natural Language Processing

- Speech signals are analyzed mathematically using Fourier Transforms to represent them in the frequency domain. The basic idea is to decompose the speech signal into basic sinusoids of different frequencies.
- The Fourier Transform gives the frequency spectrum of the speech signal which contains formant frequencies. Formants are peaks in the frequency spectrum and contain resonant frequencies of the vocal tract. They are useful cues for speech recognition.
- The speech signal can also be analyzed perceptually using auditory models which try to mimic human perception of speech. They analyze the speech signal based on critical band filtering, loudness, pitch, etc. Perceptual features are more useful for speech recognition than mathematical features as they closely relate to human perception.
- Some advantages of perceptual analysis are - they are more robust to noise, speaker variations, etc. However, they are complex to implement and computationally intensive. Mathematical analysis is simpler but not very robust. A combination of both is often used in speech recognition systems.
- Examples of features extracted - Mel-frequency cepstral coefficients (MFCCs), Linear predictive coding (LPC) coefficients, etc. These features are then used to train Machine Learning models like HMMs or Neural Networks for speech recognition.
- Applications - Speech recognition systems, Speaker identification systems, Speech coding, Speech synthesis, etc.

Here are some mnemonics and tips for learning:

- Remember 'Four' for Fourier Transform which gives the 'Four'-mant frequencies
- Critical band filtering in auditory models mimics the 'Band'-width of human auditory perception
- 'CCC' - Cepstral coefficients are one of the most common perceptual features from speech
- Speech recognition is all about recognizing the 'Hidden' patterns in speech signals (HMMs)
