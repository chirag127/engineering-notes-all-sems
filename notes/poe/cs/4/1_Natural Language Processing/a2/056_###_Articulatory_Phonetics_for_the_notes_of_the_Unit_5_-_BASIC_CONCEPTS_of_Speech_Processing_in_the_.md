### Articulatory Phonetics for the notes of the Unit 5 - BASIC CONCEPTS of Speech Processing in the subject of Natural Language Processing

Articulatory phonetics is the study of how the vocal organs are used to produce speech sounds. The main vocal organs used to produce speech sounds are:

- Lungs - provide air flow required to produce speech
- Larynx - produces voice by generating pitched vibrations
- Vocal tract - modifies the sound generated by the larynx to produce different speech sounds

The vocal tract is divided into the following sections:

- Supraglottal vocal tract - portion above the larynx
- Glottis - opening between the vocal folds in the larynx
- Subglottal vocal tract - portion below the larynx

The shape and configuration of the supraglottal vocal tract determines the characteristic sound of speech. By changing the shape and configuration of the vocal tract, we can produce different speech sounds.

Some key terms in articulatory phonetics are:

- Constriction - narrowing of the vocal tract which produces turbulence in the air flow resulting in friction noise
- Nasal cavity - side passages leading to the nose which produce nasal sounds
- Lip position - shape and rounding of lips which can produce lip sounds like /b/, /p/, /m/
- Tongue position - positioning of the tongue in the mouth which is critical in producing many speech sounds

The [IPA chart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Phonetic_Alphabet) shows the place and manner of articulation for the different speech sounds.
