### Thematic Roles for the notes of the Unit 4 - SEMANTICS AND PRAGMATICS in the subject of Natural Language Processing

Thematic roles are used to indicate the role that a noun phrase plays with respect to the action or state described by a verb. Some common thematic roles are:

- Agent: The person or thing that performs the action of the verb. e.g. "John kicked the ball."
- Theme: The person or thing that is affected by the action of the verb. e.g. "The ball was kicked by John."
- Instrument: The tool or means used to perform the action of the verb. e.g. "John kicked the ball with his foot."
- Location: The place where the action occurs. e.g. "John kicked the ball in the park."
- Beneficiary: The person or thing that benefits from the action of the verb. e.g. "John kicked the ball for his son."

Some mnemonics and learning tricks to remember the thematic roles:

- Agent is the 'doer' of the action. The one who 'acts' is the agent.
- Theme is the one 'acted upon'. It is the object of the action.
- Instrument is the 'means' to perform the action. The tool used is the instrument.
- Location is where the action 'takes place'. The place of occurrence is the location.
- Beneficiary is the one for whose 'benefit' the action is performed. Who profits is the beneficiary.

Thematic roles are important in determining the meaning of a sentence. They provide semantic relationships between the verb and noun phrases in a sentence. Identifying the thematic roles of constituents can help in tasks such as question answering, information extraction, machine translation, etc.
