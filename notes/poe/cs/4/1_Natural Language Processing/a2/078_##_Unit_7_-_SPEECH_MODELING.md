## Unit 7 - SPEECH MODELING

- Speech modeling is the process of representing speech signals by a mathematical equation or algorithm.
- The basic steps involved in speech modeling are:

1. Sampling speech signal: The continuous speech signal is sampled at a particular sampling rate to get discrete speech samples.

2. Extracting features: Important features are extracted from the speech samples that contain linguistic information. Example - Mel-frequency cepstral coefficients (MFCCs).

3. Choosing a model: An appropriate statistical model is chosen for the speech signal based on the application. Example - Hidden Markov Model (HMM) for speech recognition.

4. Training the model: The model parameters are estimated from a large amount of training data to get a trained model.

5. Testing the model: The trained model is tested on new speech data to evaluate its performance.

- Some commonly used speech models are:

- Hidden Markov Model (HMM): Models speech as a statistical process where the underlying states are not observable. Used in speech recognition.
- Gaussian Mixture Model (GMM): Represents probability density function of feature vectors as a weighted sum of Gaussian distributions. Used in speech recognition.
- Artificial Neural Network (ANN): Models complex relationships between input and output speech features. Used in speech synthesis and recognition.
- Linear Predictive Coding (LPC): Models speech signal as a linear function of past samples. Used in speech compression and synthesis.

- Sampling is the first step,
- Features come next, choose a model,
- Train the model with data for the test.
