### Cepstral Distances for the notes of the Unit 6 - SPEECH-ANALYSIS in the subject of Natural Language Processing

Cepstral distance is a measure of distance between two speech spectra. It is commonly used in speech recognition to compare an unknown speech signal against a set of known reference templates.

- The cepstrum is the Fourier transform of the log magnitude spectrum, which converts the spectrum from the frequency domain to the quefrency domain.
- The cepstral distance between two speech signals is the Euclidean distance between their cepstral coefficients.
- It has the advantage of being robust to loudness differences and some linear filtering effects.
- Cepstral distances are particularly useful for speaker recognition and speech recognition tasks, where small differences in speech sounds need to be detected.
- A simple mnemonic to remember cepstral distances is:
Cepstral distances = FFT of log-magnitude spectrum's distances

Applications:
- Speaker recognition
- Speech recognition
- Pitch detection

Advantages:
- Robust to loudness differences and linear filtering effects
- Effective in detecting small changes in speech

Disadvantages:
- Computationally expensive to calculate cepstral distances
- Loss of spectral details due to taking the log magnitude
