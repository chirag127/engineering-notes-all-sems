## Unit 4 - SEMANTICS AND PRAGMATICS

Semantics refers to the meaning of linguistic expressions. It is the study of the relation between linguistic expressions and their meanings.

Some key points about semantics:

- Meaning is context-dependent: The meaning of a word or sentence can vary based on the context in which it is used. For example, the meaning of 'bank' changes based on whether it refers to a financial institution or the land alongside a river.
- Ambiguity: Ambiguity refers to situations where a linguistic expression can have more than one meaning. For example, the sentence 'Visiting relatives can be boring' is ambiguous because it is unclear whether the relatives are the ones visiting or being visited.
- Vagueness: The boundaries of meaning are not always sharp. For example, terms like 'tall', 'expensive', 'young' are vague because there are borderline cases where it is difficult to determine if something fits the category or not.
- Connotation vs denotation: The denotation refers to the literal or dictionary meaning of a word. The connotation refers to the implied or associated meanings. For example, 'homeless' and 'vagrant' may have the same denotation but different connotations.

Pragmatics refers to the study of how context influences meaning. It is concerned with the ways in which context contributes to meaning. Some key concepts in pragmatics are:

- Implicature: Implicatures are meanings that are implied or suggested, rather than stated directly. They arise from the context and our shared assumptions and knowledge.
- Presupposition: Presuppositions are things that are assumed to be already known or true in order to understand an utterance. For example, the sentence 'Have you stopped stealing money from your company?' presupposes that you were stealing money from your company.
- Speech acts: Speech acts refer to the actions performed via utterances, such as promising, requesting, apologizing, etc. For example, 'I promise I will be there' is a speech act that performs the action of promising.

Some mnemonics and learning tricks for semantics and pragmatics:

- CICERO: Context, Implicature, Connotation, Extension, Reference, Obligation. This summarizes key concepts in semantics and pragmatics.
- DENOTATION DOTS CONNOTATION: A mnemonic to remember the difference between denotation and connotation. Denotation refers to the literal meaning while connotation refers to the implied meanings.
