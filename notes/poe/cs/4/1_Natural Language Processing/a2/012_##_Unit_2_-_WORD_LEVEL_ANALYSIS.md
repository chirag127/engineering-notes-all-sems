## Unit 2 - WORD LEVEL ANALYSIS

- Word level analysis is the process of analyzing individual words in a text. It includes examining:
- The form of words - inflections, derivations, grammatical category
- The meaning of words - semantics, lexical relations
- The usage of words - collocations, semantic prosody

Some tips for word level analysis:

- Look for word patterns: Are there repetitions of sounds, roots, prefixes or suffixes? Are there clusters of related words?
- Note the grammatical categories of words: Are verbs concentrated in certain parts of the text? What is the balance of nouns, adjectives, adverbs, etc.?
- Examine the meanings of words: Are there emotive or evaluative words? Are there technical terms or jargon? What images or metaphors are used?
- Consider collocations: Which words often co-occur? Are there words that resist common collocations?
- Think about lexical relations: Are synonyms, antonyms or hyponyms used? Is there use of metaphor or metonymy?

Advantages of word level analysis:

- It provides a detailed understanding of the linguistic composition of a text.
- It can reveal key terms, patterns and themes which may not be obvious from just reading the text.
- It equips you with a set of analytical tools and techniques which can be applied to any text.

Disadvantages of word level analysis:

- It can be time-consuming for long or complex texts.
- It may focus too much on the micro-level of the text rather than the overall meaning or themes.
- The tools and techniques require practice to apply effectively.
