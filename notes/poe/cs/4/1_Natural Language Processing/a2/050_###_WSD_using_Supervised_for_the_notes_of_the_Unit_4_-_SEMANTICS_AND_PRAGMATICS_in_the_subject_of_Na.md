### WSD using Supervised for the notes of the Unit 4 - SEMANTICS AND PRAGMATICS in the subject of Natural Language Processing

WSD or Word Sense Disambiguation is the task of identifying the correct sense of a word when it has multiple meanings. Supervised WSD uses labeled training data to learn the mapping between words and their senses.

- supervised learning algorithm is trained on a corpus of words annotated with their correct senses. The algorithm learns classifiers or models to predict the correct sense of new words.
- Supervised approaches include machine learning techniques like Naive Bayes, Maximum Entropy, and Support Vector Machines which use features like context words, part-of-speech tags, etc. to train a model for disambiguation.
- Advantage: High accuracy if large amounts of annotated data is available.
- Disadvantage: Labor-intensive and time-consuming to create annotated data. The performance depends on the amount and quality of training data.
- Applications: Improving the performance of NLP applications like machine translation, information retrieval, etc.

Some tips to remember:

- Learn the characteristics of different supervised learning algorithms like what features they use and their strengths and weaknesses.
- Understand how training data is created by annotating senses for words.
- Remember that supervised WSD gives good results if large annotated data is available, otherwise, unsupervised or knowledge-based approaches are used.
