## Unit 10 - Replication

Replication is the process of copying and manufacturing the DNA. It is a fundamental process for cell division and growth.

### Steps involved in Replication:

1. Initiation: replication begins at specific sites called origins of replication. The DNA double helix unwinds and separates locally at the origin. Proteins called helicases unwind the helix and single-stranded binding proteins stabilize the single strands.

2. Elongation: On each single strand, primase lays down an RNA primer. DNA polymerase III then elongates the primer by adding DNA nucleotides complementary to the template strand. Multiple replication forks can form, moving away from the origin in both directions.

3. Termination: As the forks meet, termination sitessignal the completion of replication. The gaps are filled in and the RNA primers are removed, replaced with DNA. The end result is two identical copies of the original DNA molecule.

**Mnemonics:** *"Helicase to open, primase for a primer, DNA pol III for elongation, RNA primer removal and gaps filling for termination"* can be used to remember the steps.

**Advantages:** Allows for exact copying of DNA which is essential for cell division and growth. High fidelity and speed due to enzymes proofreading and high processivity of DNA pol III.

**Disadvantages:** Risk of mutations if not regulated properly. Requirement of high energy in the form of NTPs. Requirement for time and resources.

**Examples and applications:** DNA replication is fundamental to life and occurs in all living organisms during cell division. It is a key process in molecular biology techniques like PCR and DNA sequencing. Understanding DNA replication helps understand and treat replication-based diseases and cancers.
