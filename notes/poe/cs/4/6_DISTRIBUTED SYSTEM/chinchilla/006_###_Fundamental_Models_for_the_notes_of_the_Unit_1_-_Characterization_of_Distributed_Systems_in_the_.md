### Fundamental Models for the notes of the Unit 1 - Characterization of Distributed Systems in the subject of DISTRIBUTED SYSTEM

Distributed systems are a collection of autonomous computers that work together towards a common goal. These systems are highly complex, and it is important to have a clear understanding of their fundamental models to design and develop distributed systems. In this unit, we will be discussing the fundamental models of distributed systems. 

Here are the key points that you need to know about the fundamental models of distributed systems:

1. Client-Server Model: This is the most commonly used model in distributed systems. In this model, the system is divided into two parts - the client and the server. The client initiates the request, and the server responds to the request with the required data. The client-server model is simple and easy to implement, making it a popular choice for many distributed systems.

2. Peer-to-Peer Model: In this model, all the nodes in the network are equal and have the same capabilities. Each node acts as both a client and a server, and there is no central server that manages the network. This model is highly scalable and fault-tolerant, making it suitable for large-scale distributed systems.

3. Hybrid Model: The hybrid model combines the client-server and peer-to-peer models. In this model, some nodes act as clients, while others act as servers. This model is flexible and can be used in a variety of distributed systems.

4. Message Passing Model: In this model, communication between nodes is achieved through messages. Each node has a message queue, and messages are sent and received from these queues. This model is highly reliable and fault-tolerant, making it suitable for distributed systems that require high levels of reliability.

5. Shared Memory Model: In this model, all the nodes in the network share a common memory space. Nodes can read and write to this memory space, allowing for fast and efficient communication between nodes. However, this model is not fault-tolerant and can be difficult to implement in large-scale distributed systems.

Mnemonics and learning tricks:

1. Remember the acronym CPSHM to remember the five fundamental models of distributed systems - Client-Server, Peer-to-Peer, Hybrid, Message Passing, and Shared Memory.

2. Think of the client-server model as a restaurant - the client is the customer who places an order, and the server is the waiter who delivers the order.

3. Think of the peer-to-peer model as a potluck dinner - everyone brings their own dish to share, and everyone has an equal role in the dinner.

4. Think of the hybrid model as a company - some employees are managers who act as servers, while others are workers who act as clients.

In conclusion, understanding the fundamental models of distributed systems is crucial for designing and developing distributed systems. By knowing these models, you can choose the one that is most suitable for your specific use case. Use the mnemonics and learning tricks to remember these models easily and ace your exams!
