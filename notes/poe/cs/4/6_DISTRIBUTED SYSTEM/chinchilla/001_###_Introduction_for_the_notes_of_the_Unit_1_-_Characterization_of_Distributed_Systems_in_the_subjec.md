### Introduction for the notes of the Unit 1 - Characterization of Distributed Systems in the subject of DISTRIBUTED SYSTEM

Distributed systems are complex systems that consist of multiple interconnected components that work together to achieve a common goal. These components can be hardware or software and can be located in different geographical locations. The goal of a distributed system is to provide a reliable, scalable, and efficient way of processing and sharing data among different components.

The Unit 1 of the subject of DISTRIBUTED SYSTEM focuses on the characterization of distributed systems. In this unit, we will learn about the different characteristics of distributed systems that make them unique and challenging to design and implement. We will also learn about the various approaches that can be used to design and implement distributed systems.

#### Key topics covered in Unit 1:

1. Introduction to distributed systems
2. Characteristics of distributed systems
3. Design issues of distributed systems
4. Approaches to designing distributed systems
5. Challenges of distributed systems
6. Advantages and disadvantages of distributed systems

#### Mnemonics and learning tricks:

1. To remember the key topics covered in Unit 1, you can use the mnemonic "ICDDAC," which stands for Introduction, Characteristics, Design issues, Approaches, Challenges.

2. To remember the advantages of distributed systems, you can use the acronym "FAST," which stands for Fault-tolerant, Scalable, Available, and Transparent.

Overall, the Unit 1 of the subject of DISTRIBUTED SYSTEM is an essential foundation for understanding the complexities of distributed systems. By the end of this unit, you should have a good understanding of the characteristics, design issues, approaches, and challenges of distributed systems. This knowledge will be helpful in the subsequent units of this subject, where we will delve deeper into the design and implementation of distributed systems.
