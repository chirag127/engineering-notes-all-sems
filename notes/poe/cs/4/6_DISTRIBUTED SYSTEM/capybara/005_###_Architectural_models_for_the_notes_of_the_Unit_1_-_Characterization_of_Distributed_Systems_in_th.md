### Architectural models for the notes of the Unit 1 - Characterization of Distributed Systems in the subject of DISTRIBUTED SYSTEM

Distributed Systems are computer systems that are composed of multiple autonomous computers that communicate with each other to achieve a common goal. There are different architectural models that can be used to design distributed systems. Here are some of them:

1. Client/Server model: In this model, the system is divided into two parts: client and server. The client sends requests to the server, and the server responds with the requested data. This model is commonly used in network-based applications such as web servers and database servers.

2. Peer-to-Peer model: In this model, all computers are equal and can act as both clients and servers. Each computer can request and provide services to other computers in the network. This model is commonly used in file-sharing applications.

3. Hybrid model: This model is a combination of the client/server and peer-to-peer models. In this model, some computers act as servers, while others act as clients and servers. This model is commonly used in collaborative applications such as online gaming.

Some Mnemonics and learning tricks for remembering these models are:

- The Client/Server model is like a restaurant where the client is the customer who orders food and the server is the waiter who brings the food.
- The Peer-to-Peer model is like a potluck dinner where everyone brings their own dish to share with others.
- The Hybrid model is like a potluck dinner where some people bring their own dish, while others contribute to a common dish.

Advantages of distributed systems:

- Improved performance: Distributed systems can handle a large amount of data and processing by distributing the workload among multiple computers.
- Scalability: Distributed systems can be easily scaled by adding more computers to the network.
- High availability: Distributed systems can provide high availability by replicating data and services across multiple computers.

Disadvantages of distributed systems:

- Complexity: Distributed systems are more complex than centralized systems and require additional management and coordination.
- Security: Distributed systems are more vulnerable to security threats than centralized systems, as data and services are distributed across multiple computers.

Examples of distributed systems:

- Google Search: Google's search engine is a distributed system that uses multiple servers to process search queries and deliver results.
- Bitcoin: Bitcoin is a decentralized digital currency that uses a peer-to-peer network to verify transactions and maintain the blockchain.

Applications of distributed systems:

- Cloud computing: Cloud computing uses a distributed system to provide on-demand access to shared computing resources such as servers, storage, and applications.
- Internet of Things (IoT): IoT devices can be connected to a distributed system to enable communication and data sharing among devices.
