
### Introduction for the Notes of Unit 4 - Agreement Protocols in the Subject of DISTRIBUTED SYSTEM

- Agreement protocols are a type of distributed system protocol that helps to ensure that all nodes in a distributed system achieve consensus on the state of the system.
- Agreement protocols are used to ensure the consistency of data in a distributed system and to prevent the system from entering an inconsistent state.
- Agreement protocols are also used to ensure fault tolerance in a distributed system by providing mechanisms for detecting and recovering from faults.
- Agreement protocols can be classified into two types: leader-based protocols and non-leader-based protocols. 
- Leader-based protocols are protocols in which one node is selected to act as the leader and coordinate the actions of the other nodes in the system. 
- Non-leader-based protocols are protocols in which the nodes in the system cooperate to reach a consensus. 
- Agreement protocols can also be classified into two categories: synchronous protocols and asynchronous protocols. 
- Synchronous protocols are protocols in which all nodes must agree on the state of the system before any action is taken. 
- Asynchronous protocols are protocols in which the nodes in the system can take actions independently and the system will eventually reach a consensus.
- Agreement protocols can be used to achieve various goals, such as consensus on the order of transactions, agreement on the state of the system, or agreement on the result of a computation.
