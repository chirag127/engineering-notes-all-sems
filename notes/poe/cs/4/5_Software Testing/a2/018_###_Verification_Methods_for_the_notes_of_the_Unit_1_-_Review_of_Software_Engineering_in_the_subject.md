### Verification Methods for the notes of the Unit 1 - Review of Software Engineering in the subject of Software Testing

The following are the verification methods used for Software Testing:

1. Reviews:
- Formal technical analysis of software documents (like requirements, design, code, test plan) for defects
- Detects defects and ensures correctness, completeness, consistency, and quality
- Performed by Team members or experts
- Two types: informal reviews and technical reviews (walkthroughs and inspections)
- Advantages: cost-effective, early detection of defects, additional perspectives
- Disadvantages: time-consuming, needs experienced and trained reviewers

2. Walkthroughs:
- Led by the author, reviewers follow the document step-by-step
- Reviewers can ask questions and point out issues
- Objective is to identify misunderstandings, assumptions, and find ambiguities and logic errors
- Less formal and thorough than inspections

3. Inspections:
- Led by a trained moderator (not the author)
- Distinct stages: overview, preparation, inspection meeting, rework, follow-up
- Detects defects and ensures quality by systematic checking of documents
- More formal, structured, and rigorous than walkthroughs
- Checklists and metrics can be used

4. Testing:
- Execution of software using test cases to identify actual defects
- Both functional testing (verifying functional requirements) and non-functional testing (verifying non-functional requirements like performance, security)
- Types: unit testing, integration testing, system testing, acceptance testing
- Advantages: detects presence of defects, simulates real usage
- Disadvantages: time-consuming, difficult to achieve good coverage
