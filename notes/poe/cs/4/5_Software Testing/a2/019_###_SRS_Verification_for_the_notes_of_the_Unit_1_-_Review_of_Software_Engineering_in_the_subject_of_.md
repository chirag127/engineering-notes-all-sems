### SRS Verification for the notes of the Unit 1 - Review of Software Engineering in the subject of Software Testing

- SRS stands for Software Requirements Specification. It is a detailed description of the intended software product in terms of functions, interfaces, performance, and quality attributes.
- Verification of SRS involves reviewing the SRS document for completeness, consistency, unambiguousness, verifiability, modifiability, and traceability.
- Some key points to verify in SRS:
    - All functional and non-functional requirements are addressed. No requirements are missing.
    - There are no contradictions or conflicts between different requirements. Requirements are consistent with each other.
    - Requirements are clearly and unambiguously stated. There is no confusion or multiple interpretations possible.
    - It is possible to verify if the final software product meets the specified requirements. Requirements are verifiable.
    - Requirements can accommodate changes if needed. The SRS is modifiable.
    - The origin of each requirement is traceable. Traceability is maintained.
- Advantages of SRS verification:
    - Requirement gaps and inconsistencies are identified early.
    - Ambiguities and conflicts are resolved before software design and development.
    - The final software product is more likely to meet the actual customer needs.
    - Helps in estimating costs and resource requirements more accurately.
- Some tips for effective SRS verification:
    - Involve people who will be affected by the software like users and developers.
    - Review the SRS section by section and requirement by requirement.
    - Apply a systematic and structured approach to SRS verification.
    - Automate parts of the verification process using requirement management tools.
    - Track issues found and ensure they are addressed properly.
