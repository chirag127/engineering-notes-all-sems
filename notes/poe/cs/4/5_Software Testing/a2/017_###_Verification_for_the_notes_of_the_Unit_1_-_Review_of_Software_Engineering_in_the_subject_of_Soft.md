### Verification for the notes of the Unit 1 - Review of Software Engineering in the subject of Software Testing

- Verification is the process of checking whether the software meets the specified requirements. It is a systematic process to determine whether the system meets the functional and non-functional requirements as initially defined.
- The different types of verification are:
	- Inspection: Manually checking the work products like requirements, design, code, etc. to find faults and ensure quality.
	- Reviews: Systematic examination of a work product to identify problems and collect feedback. Examples are formal technical reviews, informal reviews, etc.
	- Testing: Executing the software to identify actual faults and failures. Testing is a dynamic verification technique.
- Advantages of verification:
	- Detects faults early and reduces cost of correction.
	- Improves quality and reliability of the software.
	- Increases customer satisfaction by delivering defect-free software.
- Disadvantages of verification:
	- Requires time and effort.
	- Does not guarantee complete removal of faults. Some faults may remain undetected.
	- Can be expensive if exhaustive verification is performed.
- Examples of verification techniques: Code reviews, unit testing, integration testing, system testing, etc.
- Applications of verification: Verification is performed at every stage of the software development life cycle to reduce risks and ensure high quality software. It is a prerequisite for validation.
