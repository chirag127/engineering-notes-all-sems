### Configuration Audits for the Notes of Unit 1 - Review of Software Engineering in the Subject of Software Testing

Configuration Audits are an important part of the Software Testing process. They help in ensuring that the software product is developed as per the requirements and specifications. Configuration Audits verify that the changes made to the software product are consistent with the requirements and specifications and are properly documented. In this section, we will discuss the concept of Configuration Audits and their importance in the Software Testing process.

#### What is Configuration Audit?

Configuration Audit is a process of reviewing the software product to ensure that the configuration items are consistent with the requirements and specifications. The purpose of Configuration Audit is to verify that the software product is built as per the design, requirements, and specifications. Configuration Audit is performed at various stages of the Software Testing process, such as during the development phase, after the testing phase, and before the release of the software product.

#### Importance of Configuration Audits

Configuration Audits are important for the following reasons:

- To ensure that the software product is built as per the design, requirements, and specifications.
- To identify any deviations from the requirements and specifications.
- To ensure that the changes made to the software product are properly documented.
- To ensure that the software product is consistent with the quality standards.
- To ensure that the software product is ready for release.

#### Types of Configuration Audits

There are two types of Configuration Audits:

1. Functional Configuration Audit (FCA)

Functional Configuration Audit (FCA) is performed after the software product has been tested and is ready for release. FCA verifies that the software product meets the requirements and specifications and is ready for release. FCA is also known as the Production Readiness Review.

2. Physical Configuration Audit (PCA)

Physical Configuration Audit (PCA) is performed during the development phase. PCA verifies that the software product is being built as per the design, requirements, and specifications. PCA also ensures that the software product is properly documented and is consistent with the quality standards.

#### Steps Involved in Configuration Audits

The following are the steps involved in Configuration Audits:

1. Planning and Preparation

In this step, the objectives and scope of the Configuration Audit are defined. The Configuration Audit team is formed, and the audit plan is prepared.

2. Conducting the Audit

In this step, the Configuration Audit team verifies that the software product is built as per the design, requirements, and specifications. The team also verifies that the software product is properly documented and is consistent with the quality standards.

3. Reporting the Findings

In this step, the Configuration Audit team prepares a report of the findings. The report includes the deviations from the requirements and specifications and the corrective actions that need to be taken.

4. Corrective Action

In this step, the corrective actions are taken to address the deviations from the requirements and specifications identified in the audit report.

#### Mnemonics and Learning Tricks

There are no specific Mnemonics or Learning Tricks for Configuration Audits. However, it is important to understand the concept and steps involved in Configuration Audits to perform them effectively. It is recommended to practice Configuration Audits using sample software products to gain hands-on experience.
