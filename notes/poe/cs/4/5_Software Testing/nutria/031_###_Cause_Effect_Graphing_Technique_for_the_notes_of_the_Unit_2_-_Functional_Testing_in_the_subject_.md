
### Cause Effect Graphing Technique for the notes of the Unit 2 - Functional Testing in the subject of Software Testing

Cause-Effect Graphing Technique (CEGT) is a method used in software testing to identify test cases based on the conditions and effects of the system under test. It is used to identify all possible combinations of conditions and effects in a system.

The CEGT consists of two steps:

1. Identify the conditions and effects of the system under test.
2. Create a cause-effect graph using the identified conditions and effects.

The conditions and effects of the system under test can be identified by analyzing the system requirements and design documents. The cause-effect graph is then created by connecting the conditions and effects with arrows. 

The advantages of using the CEGT include:

1. It is a systematic way of identifying test cases.
2. It is easy to use and understand.
3. It helps to identify redundant and missing test cases.
4. It helps to identify test cases that are difficult to test.

The disadvantages of using the CEGT include:

1. It can be time-consuming to identify all the conditions and effects.
2. It can be difficult to identify all the conditions and effects in complex systems.
3. It can be difficult to identify the relationships between conditions and effects.

CEGT is a useful tool for identifying test cases in software testing. It helps to identify redundant and missing test cases, and can also help to identify test cases that are difficult to test. However, it can be time-consuming to identify all the conditions and effects, and it can be difficult to identify the relationships between conditions and effects in complex systems.
