
### MAC Protocol Survey for the Notes of the Unit 4 - Network & Communication Aspects in IoT in the Subject of Internet of Things

- Media Access Control (MAC) protocols are a set of rules and regulations that govern how devices on a network access the network's medium. 
- MAC protocols are used in both wired and wireless networks, and they are a key component of the Internet of Things (IoT). 
- The most commonly used MAC protocol is the Carrier Sense Multiple Access with Collision Detection (CSMA/CD). CSMA/CD is a protocol that allows devices to share a single medium by sensing when other devices are transmitting and then waiting for a certain amount of time before attempting to transmit. 
- CSMA/CD is a contention-based protocol, meaning that devices must compete for access to the medium. 
- Another commonly used MAC protocol is the Carrier Sense Multiple Access with Collision Avoidance (CSMA/CA). CSMA/CA is a protocol that allows devices to share a single medium by sensing when other devices are transmitting and then waiting for a certain amount of time before attempting to transmit. 
- CSMA/CA is a contention-based protocol, meaning that devices must compete for access to the medium. 
- CSMA/CA is used in wireless networks and is designed to reduce collisions by using a technique called "collision avoidance". 
- Another MAC protocol used in wireless networks is the Distributed Coordination Function (DCF). DCF is a contention-based protocol that allows devices to share a single medium by sensing when other devices are transmitting and then waiting for a certain amount of time before attempting to transmit. 
- DCF is designed to reduce collisions by using a technique called "collision avoidance". 
- Finally, MAC protocols can also be used in ad-hoc networks. Ad-hoc networks are networks that are created on the fly and do not have an established infrastructure. 
- In ad-hoc networks, the MAC protocol used is the Random Access Protocol (RAP). RAP is a contention-based protocol that allows devices to share a single medium by sensing when other devices are transmitting and then waiting for a certain amount of time before attempting to transmit. 
- RAP is designed to reduce collisions by using a technique called "collision avoidance".
