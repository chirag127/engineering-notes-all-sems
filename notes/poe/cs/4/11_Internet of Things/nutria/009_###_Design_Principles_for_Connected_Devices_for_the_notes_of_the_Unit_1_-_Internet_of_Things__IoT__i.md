
### Design Principles for Connected Devices for the notes of the Unit 1 - Internet of Things (IoT) in the subject of Internet of Things

The Design Principles for Connected Devices are a set of guidelines that should be followed when designing products that are intended to be used in the Internet of Things (IoT). These principles help ensure that the product is secure, reliable, and easy to use.

1. Security: Security should be a top priority when designing products for the IoT. It is important to ensure that the product is secure from malicious attacks, and that the data it collects is protected from unauthorized access.

2. Reliability: Products for the IoT should be reliable and able to operate without interruption. This includes ensuring that the product is able to withstand environmental conditions such as temperature and humidity.

3. Usability: Products should be designed with the user in mind. This includes making sure that the product is easy to set up and use, as well as providing clear instructions and documentation.

4. Interoperability: Products should be designed to work with other products in the IoT. This includes making sure that the product is compatible with other products, and that it can exchange data with them.

5. Scalability: Products should be designed to be scalable, so that they can be easily upgraded or expanded as the needs of the user change.

6. Efficiency: Products should be designed to be as energy-efficient as possible. This includes making sure that the product uses as little energy as possible, and that it is designed to minimize the amount of energy it consumes.

7. Cost: Products should be designed to be cost-effective. This includes making sure that the product is affordable and that it provides value for the money.

Mnemonics: 

The Design Principles for Connected Devices can be remembered with the acronym "S.R.U.I.S.E.C." which stands for Security, Reliability, Usability, Interoperability, Scalability, Efficiency, and Cost.
