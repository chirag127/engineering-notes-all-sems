
### Wireless Medium Access Issues for the Notes of the Unit 4 - Network & Communication Aspects in IoT in the Subject of Internet of Things

- Wireless medium access is a process of controlling the access to a shared medium in a wireless network. 
- Common medium access techniques used in wireless networks include Carrier Sense Multiple Access with Collision Avoidance (CSMA/CA), Carrier Sense Multiple Access with Collision Detection (CSMA/CD), and Time Division Multiple Access (TDMA). 
- CSMA/CA is the most commonly used technique in wireless networks and is based on the principle of “listen before talk”, meaning that a node must listen to the medium before transmitting. 
- CSMA/CD is used in wired networks and is based on the principle of “listen while talk”, meaning that a node can transmit while listening to the medium. 
- TDMA is used in wireless networks and is based on the principle of “time-slicing”, meaning that a node is allocated a certain amount of time to transmit. 
- Wireless medium access techniques are used to ensure that only one node is transmitting at a given time and that collisions are avoided. 
- Advantages of using wireless medium access techniques include improved network performance, increased efficiency, and improved reliability. 
- Disadvantages of using wireless medium access techniques include increased complexity, increased power consumption, and increased cost. 
- Examples of applications that use wireless medium access techniques include wireless sensor networks, cellular networks, and wireless local area networks.
