### Digital sensors for the notes of the Unit 2 - Hardware for IoT in the subject of Internet of Things

- Digital sensors provide an output in digital form (on/off or high/low) which can be easily interpreted by microcontrollers or microprocessors.
- Some common types of digital sensors are:

- Touch sensors: Detect touch or proximity and output a digital signal. Examples: Pushbuttons, capacitive touch sensors.
- Image sensors: Convert incoming light into digital images. Examples: CMOS and CCD image sensors used in cameras.
- Magnetic sensors: Detect presence/absence or changes in magnetic fields and output a digital signal. Examples: Reed switches, hall effect sensors.
- Motion sensors: Detect motion and provide a digital output. Examples: PIR sensors, accelerometers.

Advantages:

- Digital output is easy to interface with digital systems.
- Less noise prone compared to analog sensors.
- Inexpensive and widely available.

Disadvantages:

- Limited in the amount of information they can provide (only on/off).
- Require additional processing to extract meaningful information.

Applications:

- Used in touch interfaces, image capturing devices, security systems, motion activated devices, etc.

Mnemonics:

- DTS for Digital Touch Sensors
- DIS for Digital Image Sensors
- DMS for Digital Motion Sensors
- DMA for Digital Magnetic sensors
