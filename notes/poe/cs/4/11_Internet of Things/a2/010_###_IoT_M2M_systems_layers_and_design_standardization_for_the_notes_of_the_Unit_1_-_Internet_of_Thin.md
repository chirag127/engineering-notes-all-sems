### IoT/M2M systems layers and design standardization

The IoT system can be divided into five layers:

1. Perception layer: Consists of sensors and actuators that can sense and collect data from the physical world and trigger actions. E.g. Temperature sensors, motion sensors, etc.

2. Network layer: Consists of communication networks like Wi-Fi, cellular, Bluetooth, etc. that connect the perception layer to the upper layers. The data from the perception layer is transmitted using appropriate network technologies.

3. Processing layer: Consists of edge devices like gateways that collect and aggregate data from the network layer and perform processing and filtering before sending data to the cloud. Some actions can also be taken at this layer.

4. Application layer: Consists of cloud-based applications and services that store, process and analyze the data from the processing layer. Dashboards and APIs are provided to trigger actions and present information.

5. Business layer: Consists of the stakeholders and organizations that utilize the information and functionality from the application layer to provide value-added products and services to the users.

To enable interoperability between different products and systems, certain design standards have been developed like:

- IoTivity: An open source software framework for building IoT applications.
- OneM2M: A global standard for M2M communication and device integration.
- MQTT: A machine-to-machine connectivity protocol.
- DDS: A data-centric connectivity protocol.
- CoAP: A web transfer protocol for constrained nodes and constrained networks.

Advantages of standardization include:

- Interoperability between devices and systems from different vendors.
- Avoid vendor lock-in situations.
- Faster innovation due to focus on application instead of connectivity.
- Large-scale deployments and global IoT implementations.
