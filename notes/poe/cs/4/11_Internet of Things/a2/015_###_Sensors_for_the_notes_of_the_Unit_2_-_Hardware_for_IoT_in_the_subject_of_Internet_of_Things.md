### Sensors for the notes of the Unit 2 - Hardware for IoT in the subject of Internet of Things

- Sensors are devices that detect and respond to some type of input from the physical environment. They convert the physical quantity into signals which can be measured or recorded.
- Following are the commonly used sensors for IoT devices:

1. Temperature Sensor: Measures ambient temperature and converts it into an electrical signal. Examples: Thermistor, Thermocouple, RTD, etc.
Advantages: Inexpensive, Small size.
Disadvantages: Accuracy can be affected by self-heating.
Applications: Smart thermostats, HVAC systems, Refrigerators, etc.

2. Humidity Sensor: Measures amount of moisture in air and converts it into an electrical signal. Examples: Capacitive, Resistive, Thermal, etc.
Advantages: Inexpensive, Wide range of measurement.
Disadvantages: Can be affected by temperature changes.
Applications: Smart greenhouses, Dehumidifiers, Air purifiers, etc.

3. Pressure Sensor: Measures atmospheric or fluid pressure and converts it into an electrical signal. Examples: Piezoelectric, Capacitive, Piezoresistive, etc.
Advantages: High accuracy and sensitivity.
Disadvantages: Requires complex circuitry.
Applications: Altimeters, Flow meters, Spirometers, etc.

4. Light Sensor: Measures ambient light intensity and converts it into an electrical signal. Examples: Photodiode, Phototransistor, etc.
Advantages: Inexpensive, Small size.
Disadvantages: Can be affected by temperature and requires calibration.
Applications: Lighting systems, Mobile phones, Smart mirrors, etc.
