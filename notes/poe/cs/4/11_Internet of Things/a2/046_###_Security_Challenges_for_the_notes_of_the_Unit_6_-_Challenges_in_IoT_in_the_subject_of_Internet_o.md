### Security Challenges for the notes of the Unit 6 - Challenges in IoT in the subject of Internet of Things

- **Authentication**: Ensuring that only authorized devices and users can access the network and specific devices. Without authentication, malicious actors could access devices and data or control devices.
- **Authorization**: Ensuring that only authorized actions are permitted. For example, a smart thermostat might only be authorized to change temperature settings for certain hours of the day. Without proper authorization, devices could be misused or compromised.
- **Confidentiality**: Ensuring that data is encrypted and only accessible to authorized parties. For example, personal information collected by IoT devices needs to be kept confidential. Without encryption, sensitive data could be exposed.
- **Integrity**: Ensuring that data is not modified illegally. For example, an intruder should not be able to manipulate data from IoT sensors to spoof false readings. Without data integrity protections, the information from IoT devices could be unreliable.
- **Availability**: Ensuring that devices and data are accessible as expected. For example, IoT devices should not be subjected to denial-of-service attacks that make them unresponsive. Without availability safeguards, IoT systems could become unusable.
- **Updates**: Ensuring that IoT devices have a mechanism to receive automatic updates to patch vulnerabilities and improve functionality. Given the scale of IoT deployments, manual updates are not practical and automatic updates are crucial. Without an update mechanism, IoT devices with bugs or vulnerabilities could remain insecure.
- **Diversity**: Avoiding homogeneity in IoT devices and networks since common vulnerabilities across a huge number of devices could lead to widespread compromise or failure. A mix of operating systems, software, and networking protocols makes large-scale, automated attacks more difficult. However, diversity also introduces interoperability challenges.

The Mnemonics could be:

Authentication: Who are you?
Authorization: What are you allowed to do?
Confidentiality: Keep it secret.
Integrity: Don't change the message.
Availability: Respond when needed.
Updates: Patch the bugs.
Diversity: Don't put all your eggs in one basket.

The learning tricks could be:

- Remember AICCU for Authentication, Integrity, Confidentiality, Confidentiality, Updates
- Think of authorization as giving permissions like a bouncer at a club allowing only certain people to enter
- Availability could be remembered as always being ready to respond like an employee always being available when needed
- Think of diversity as not having everyone be the same, like not everyone in a team being a striker in soccer but having defenders, midfielders, and strikers.
