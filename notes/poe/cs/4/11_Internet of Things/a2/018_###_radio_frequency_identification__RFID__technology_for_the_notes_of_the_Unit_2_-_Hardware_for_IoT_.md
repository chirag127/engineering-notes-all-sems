### Radio Frequency Identification (RFID) Technology

- RFID is a wireless technology that uses tiny tags or chips to store identification data and a reader to retrieve the data wirelessly.
- The RFID tag contains an antenna to enable it to receive and respond to radio-frequency queries from an RFID reader.
- Passive RFID tags have no internal power source and get activated by the electromagnetic waves from the reader whereas active RFID tags have an internal power source (battery) and can continuously broadcast their identification data.
- RFID systems work on the principle of transmission and reception of radio frequency signals. The RFID reader generates an electromagnetic field that enables the RFID tag to transmit the stored data to the reader.
- The RFID tags can be embedded or surface-mounted on physical objects and the RFID reader can retrieve the ID of the tag from several meters away.
- RFID tags can store a unique identification number along with additional data like item name, price, quantity, etc. making them useful for applications such as:
    - Inventory management: Tracking assets and their locations.
    - Access control: Authentication of authorized persons.
    - Payment systems: Contactless smart cards for payments.
    - Supply chain management: Tracking products through the supply chain.
    - Vehicle tracking: Tracking vehicles for location and identification.
- Some advantages of RFID are:
    - Non-line-of-sight tracking. RFID tags can be read without needing to be in direct view of the reader.
    - Can store more data than barcodes. RFID tags have more storage capacity to hold identification numbers as well as other product information.
    - Faster data retrieval. RFID systems are faster than barcode systems as RFID tags can be read simultaneously in bulk.
- Some disadvantages of RFID are:
    - Interference: RFIF signals can be affected by metals and liquids.
    - Limited range: The read range of passive RFID tags is limited compared to active RFID tags and barcodes.
    - Security and privacy concerns: The data transmitted by RFID tags can be hacked or skimmed by unauthorized readers.
    - Cost: RFID tags and readers are more expensive than barcodes.
