### Design Principles for Connected Devices

The following are the key design principles for connected devices in IoT:

1. Keep it Simple: The devices should be simple in design and functionality. They should perform specific tasks and not be overloaded with complex features. This makes them easy to use, less prone to errors and vulnerabilities, and cost-effective to manufacture and maintain.

2. Modularity: The system should be designed in a modular fashion. The components should be loosely coupled and highly cohesive. This makes it easy to update, replace, and scale individual modules without impacting the entire system. It also increases reusability of modules.

3. Standardization: There should be standardization of communication protocols, data formats, and interfaces. This interoperability makes it possible for devices and systems from different manufacturers to connect and work together. Some of the standard protocols for IoT include HTTP, CoAP, MQTT, AMQP, etc.

4. Security: Security should be an integral part of the system design. This includes both authentication and authorization of devices and data protection. Vulnerabilities in one device can compromise the entire system. Hence, all layers of the system should be secured - from devices to networks to cloud.

5. Scalability: The system should be designed to scale efficiently as the number of connected devices grows. This includes scaling of networks, servers, data storage, and processing capabilities. A modular design and cloud-based infrastructure help in scaling the system.

6. Reliability: The system should be robust and fault-tolerant. This means that the system should continue to function even when there are device failures or network issues. Redundancy of devices and connections along with fault detection and self-healing capabilities can enhance the reliability of the system.

7. Energy Efficiency: Most IoT devices operate on batteries and energy efficiency is crucial. The devices should be designed to consume minimal power and optimized to the essential functionalities. They can also have energy harvesting capabilities to recharge batteries. Low-power protocols can be used for communication.

8. Usability: The system should be easy to use for the end-users. This includes simple onboarding of devices, intuitive interfaces, and automated configurations and management. The data from the system should be presented in a useful and understandable format. Usability is key to the adoption of IoT solutions.
