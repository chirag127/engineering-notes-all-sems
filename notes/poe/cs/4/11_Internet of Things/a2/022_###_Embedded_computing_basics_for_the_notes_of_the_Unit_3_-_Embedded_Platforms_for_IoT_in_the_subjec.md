### Embedded computing basics

- Embedded systems are dedicated computer systems that are embedded inside electrical and electronic devices to control and monitor their functions. They are designed to perform specific tasks and are constrained by limited resources such as memory, processing power, and energy.
- Some key characteristics of embedded systems are:
    - Embedded in the device it controls
    - Real-time and reactive to events
    - Constrained by limited resources
- Examples of embedded systems:
    - Microcontrollers in appliances, vehicles, industrial machines, etc.
    - System on chip (SoC) in smartphones, routers, etc.
- Important components of an embedded system:
    - Processor: Microcontroller or microprocessor for executing program instructions
    - Memory: Stores program instructions and data
    - Peripherals: Connects the processor to external world like timers, interrupts, I/O ports, analog-to-digital converters, etc.
    - Software: Embedded firmware or operating system and application software
- Key considerations while designing an embedded system:
    - Precise task specification and requirements analysis
    - Selecting appropriate hardware components based on requirements and constraints
    - Efficient software design and programming
    - Testing and verification of functionality
    - Performance optimization within given constraints
- Advantages:
    - They are optimized for specific tasks leading to efficiency.
    - They offer more reliability due to focused functionality and testing.
    - They are present in many smart devices and systems enabling automation and enhanced experiences.
- Disadvantages:
    - They are less flexible to changes as they are tailored to specific tasks.
    - They have limited resources leading to challenges in complex applications.
    - They require specialized knowledge to design and develop.
