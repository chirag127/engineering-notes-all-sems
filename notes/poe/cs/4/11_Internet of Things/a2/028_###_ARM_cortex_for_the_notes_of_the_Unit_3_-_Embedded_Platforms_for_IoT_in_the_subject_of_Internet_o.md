### ARM Cortex

- ARM Cortex is a family of 32-bit RISC ARM processor cores designed by ARM Holdings.
- ARM Cortex cores are widely used in embedded systems, IoT devices, and mobile phones.
- Popular ARM Cortex cores:

- Cortex-A Series: Applications processors focusing on high performance. Example: Cortex-A53, Cortex-A72.
- Cortex-R Series: Real-time processors focusing on high predictability and reliability. Example: Cortex-R5, Cortex-R7.
- Cortex-M Series: Microcontroller processors focusing on low cost and low power. Example: Cortex-M0, Cortex-M3, Cortex-M4.

**Advantages:**

- Low power consumption.
- High performance.
- Widely supported by software and tools.
- Cost effective.

**Disadvantages:**

- Less advanced processor architectures compared to x86 and some other RISC processors.
- Limited to 32-bit computing (except for newer Cortex-A75 and Cortex-A76 which have 64-bit extensions).

**Applications:** IoT devices, mobile phones, smart watches, drones, robotic systems, etc.

**Mnemonics:**

- A is for Applications, R is for Real-time, M is for Microcontrollers.
- Higher number means higher performance (but also higher power consumption and cost).

**Tips:** Study the specifications of different ARM Cortex cores and their use cases to understand them better. Programming and experimenting on ARM Cortex boards can help gain hands-on experience.
