## Unit 1 - Internet of Things (IoT)

The Internet of Things (IoT) refers to the interconnection of everyday physical objects and appliances via the internet. These devices are equipped with sensors, software, and network connections that allow them to send and receive data. IoT enables these devices to be remotely monitored and controlled.

Some key points about IoT:

- IoT devices have sensors and software that allow them to collect and exchange data.
- The data is transmitted over the internet, allowing devices to be connected and exchange information.
- IoT enables remote monitoring and control of devices. For example, a smart thermostat can be controlled via an app to adjust the temperature from afar.
- IoT devices have machine-to-machine communication, allowing them to automatically trigger actions and control each other without human intervention. For example, a sensor could detect a broken window and alert the security system to take action.
- IoT has a wide range of applications in homes, businesses, wearables, transportation, and healthcare. Smart homes, smart cities, and industrial IoT are major application areas.

Some mnemonics/learning tricks for IoT:

- Think of IoT as an 'internet of interactions' - devices interacting with each other over the internet.
- Remember the key parts of IoT devices: sensors to collect data, software to process data, network connections to exchange data.
- Picture IoT as a web of connected devices that can monitor, communicate, and control things around you.

The advantages of IoT include efficiency, convenience, and improved automation. However, there are also disadvantages like security/privacy issues, technical challenges, and increased complexity.
