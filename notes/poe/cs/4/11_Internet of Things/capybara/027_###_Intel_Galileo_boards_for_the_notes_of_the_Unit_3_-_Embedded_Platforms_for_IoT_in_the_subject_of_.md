### Intel Galileo boards for the notes of the Unit 3 - Embedded Platforms for IoT in the subject of Internet of Things

The Intel Galileo boards are a series of development boards designed to enable rapid prototyping and experimentation in the Internet of Things (IoT) space. They are based on Intel's Quark processors and come equipped with a range of sensors, inputs, and outputs that make them ideal for building IoT applications.

Here are some key features and benefits of the Intel Galileo boards:

- **Powerful processor:** The Galileo boards are powered by Intel's Quark processor, which provides ample processing power for most IoT applications. This processor is designed to be low-power, which makes it ideal for battery-powered applications.

- **Integrated sensors:** The Galileo boards come equipped with a range of sensors, including temperature, humidity, and light sensors. These sensors make it easy to collect data from the environment and use it to build intelligent IoT applications.

- **Easy to program:** The Galileo boards can be programmed using a range of programming languages, including C++, Python, and JavaScript. This makes it easy to find a language that suits your needs and experience level.

- **Wide range of inputs and outputs:** The Galileo boards come equipped with a range of inputs and outputs, including digital and analog inputs, PWM outputs, and I2C and SPI interfaces. These inputs and outputs make it easy to connect to a range of sensors and other devices.

- **Easy to connect to the cloud:** The Galileo boards can be easily connected to the cloud using a range of protocols, including MQTT and HTTP. This makes it easy to store and analyze data in the cloud, and to build powerful IoT applications that leverage cloud computing.

Mnemonics and learning tricks:

- One useful mnemonic for remembering the key features of the Intel Galileo boards is "P.I.E." This stands for "Powerful processor," "Integrated sensors," and "Easy to program." It's a simple way to remember the key benefits of these boards and how they can be used in IoT applications.

- Another useful learning trick is to start with a simple project, such as reading data from a temperature sensor, and then build on that project by adding more sensors and outputs.
