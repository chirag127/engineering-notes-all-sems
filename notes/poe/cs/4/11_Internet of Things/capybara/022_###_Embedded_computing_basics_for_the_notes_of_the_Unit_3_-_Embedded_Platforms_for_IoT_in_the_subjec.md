### Embedded Computing Basics for the Notes of Unit 3 - Embedded Platforms for IoT in the Subject of Internet of Things

Embedded computing is a specialized form of computer programming that is concerned with the design and development of small-scale computing systems. These systems are often used in industrial, scientific, and medical applications where they perform specific tasks and functions. In this article, we will explore some of the basics of embedded computing that are essential for the notes of Unit 3 - Embedded Platforms for IoT in the subject of Internet of Things.

#### What is Embedded Computing?

Embedded computing is the use of specialized computer systems that are designed to perform specific tasks and functions. These systems are typically found in electronic devices such as mobile phones, tablets, and IoT devices. Unlike general-purpose computers, embedded systems are designed to perform a limited range of functions and are optimized for specific applications.

#### Features of Embedded Computing Systems

The following are some of the key features of embedded computing systems:

- They are designed to perform specific tasks and functions.
- They are optimized for power consumption, size, and cost.
- They often have limited resources (e.g., memory, processing power, and storage).
- They are often programmed in low-level languages such as C and Assembly.
- They may not have a user interface or may have a limited user interface.

#### Embedded Computing Platforms

There are several platforms that are commonly used for embedded computing, including:

- Microcontrollers: These are small, low-power devices that are used to control simple systems. They typically have a limited amount of memory and processing power.
- System on a Chip (SoC): These are complete computer systems that are integrated onto a single chip. They typically include a processor, memory, and other components such as a graphics processor or network interface.
- Field-Programmable Gate Arrays (FPGAs): These are programmable digital circuits that can be configured to perform specific functions. They are often used in applications that require high-speed processing or custom logic.

#### Advantages of Embedded Computing

The following are some of the advantages of embedded computing:

- Low power consumption: Embedded systems are designed to consume minimal power, making them ideal for battery-powered devices.
- Small size: Embedded systems are often small in size, making them easy to integrate into other devices.
- Low cost: Embedded systems are typically less expensive than general-purpose computers.
- Customizability: Embedded systems can be customized for specific applications, providing greater flexibility and control.

#### Applications of Embedded Computing

Embedded computing is used in a wide range of applications, including:

- Consumer electronics: Mobile phones, tablets, and smart home devices are all examples of embedded systems.
- Industrial control systems: Embedded systems are often used to control manufacturing equipment and other industrial processes.
- Medical devices: Embedded systems are used in medical devices such as pacemakers, insulin pumps, and blood glucose monitors.
- Automotive systems: Embedded systems are used in automotive applications such as engine management, anti-lock braking systems, and airbag control.

#### Mnemonics and Learning Tricks

Unfortunately, there are no easy mnemonics or learning tricks for embedded computing basics. However, it is important to understand the key features and platforms of embedded systems, as well as their advantages and applications. By focusing on these key concepts, you can gain a better understanding of how embedded computing works and how it can be used in various applications.
