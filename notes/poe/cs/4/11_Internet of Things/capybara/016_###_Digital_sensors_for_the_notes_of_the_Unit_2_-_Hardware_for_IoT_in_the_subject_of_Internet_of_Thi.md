### Digital Sensors for the notes of the Unit 2 - Hardware for IoT in the subject of Internet of Things

Digital sensors are an essential component of the hardware used in IoT devices. These sensors can detect and measure physical parameters such as temperature, humidity, pressure, light intensity, and many others. They convert these physical parameters into digital signals that can be processed by the microcontroller or processor of the IoT device.

In this section, we will discuss some of the most commonly used digital sensors in IoT devices and their working principles.

#### 1. Temperature Sensors
- Temperature sensors are used to measure the temperature of the surrounding environment.
- The most commonly used temperature sensors are Thermistors, RTDs (Resistance Temperature Detectors), and Thermocouples.
- Thermistors are made of semiconductor materials that change their resistance in response to temperature changes.
- RTDs are made of a metal wire that changes its resistance in response to temperature changes.
- Thermocouples use two different metals that generate a voltage in response to temperature changes.

#### 2. Humidity Sensors
- Humidity sensors are used to measure the relative humidity of the surrounding environment.
- These sensors can be capacitive or resistive.
- Capacitive humidity sensors measure the change in capacitance caused by the absorption or release of water vapor.
- Resistive humidity sensors measure the change in resistance caused by the absorption or release of water vapor.

#### 3. Pressure Sensors
- Pressure sensors are used to measure the pressure of fluids or gases.
- The most commonly used pressure sensors are Piezoelectric, Strain Gauge, and Capacitive sensors.
- Piezoelectric sensors generate a voltage proportional to the applied pressure.
- Strain Gauge sensors measure the change in resistance caused by the deformation of a metal wire due to the applied pressure.
- Capacitive sensors measure the change in capacitance caused by the deformation of a diaphragm due to the applied pressure.

#### 4. Light Sensors
- Light sensors are used to measure the intensity of light.
- The most commonly used light sensors are Photodiodes, Phototransistors, and Photoresistors.
- Photodiodes generate a current proportional to the intensity of light.
- Phototransistors amplify the current generated by a photodiode.
- Photoresistors change their resistance in response to the intensity of light.

#### 5. Accelerometer Sensors
- Accelerometer sensors are used to measure the acceleration of an object.
- These sensors can be capacitive or piezoelectric.
- Capacitive accelerometers measure the change in capacitance caused by the acceleration of a mass attached to a diaphragm.
- Piezoelectric accelerometers generate a voltage proportional to the acceleration of a mass attached to a piezoelectric crystal.

Mnemonics and learning tricks:
- For temperature sensors, remember the acronym TRT (Thermistors, RTDs, Thermocouples).
- For humidity sensors, remember the acronym CR (Capacitive, Resistive).
- For pressure sensors, remember the acronym PSC (Piezoelectric, Strain Gauge, Capacitive).
- For light sensors, remember the acronym PPP (Photodiodes, Phototransistors, Photoresistors).
- For accelerometer sensors, remember the acronym CPA (Capacitive, Piezoelectric, Acceleration).
