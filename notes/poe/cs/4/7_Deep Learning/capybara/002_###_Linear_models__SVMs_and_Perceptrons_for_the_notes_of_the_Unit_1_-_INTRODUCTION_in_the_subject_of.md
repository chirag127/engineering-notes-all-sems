### Linear models (SVMs and Perceptrons for the notes of the Unit 1 - INTRODUCTION in the subject of Deep Learning

Linear models are a fundamental concept in machine learning, and they form the basis for many advanced algorithms. In this unit, we will be discussing two popular types of linear models, Support Vector Machines (SVMs) and Perceptrons.

#### Support Vector Machines (SVMs)

SVMs are a type of binary classifier that separates two classes of data by finding the hyperplane that maximizes the margin between the two classes. The margin is the distance between the hyperplane and the closest data points in each class.

Some key concepts to remember about SVMs include:

- The hyperplane is defined by a weight vector w and a bias term b.
- The decision boundary is given by w^T*x + b = 0, where x is the input data.
- SVMs aim to maximize the margin between the decision boundary and the closest data points in each class.
- SVMs can be extended to non-linear problems using kernel functions.

Some advantages of SVMs include:

- They are effective in high-dimensional spaces.
- They are memory efficient, as they only need to store support vectors.
- They work well with unstructured and semi-structured data like text and images.

#### Perceptrons

Perceptrons are the simplest type of neural network, consisting of a single layer of output units that take a linear combination of the input features and apply a threshold function to make a prediction.

Some key concepts to remember about Perceptrons include:

- The output of a Perceptron is a binary value (0 or 1).
- The threshold function is used to determine whether the input falls above or below a certain threshold.
- Perceptrons can be used for binary classification problems.

Some advantages of Perceptrons include:

- They are simple and fast to train.
- They can work well for problems with linearly separable data.

In conclusion, understanding linear models is essential for understanding many advanced machine learning algorithms. SVMs and Perceptrons are two important examples of linear models that are widely used in practice. Remembering the key concepts and advantages of each can help you apply them effectively to solve real-world problems.
