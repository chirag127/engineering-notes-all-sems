### Computational & Artificial Neuroscience for the notes of the Unit 4 - OPTIMIZATION AND GENERALIZATION in the subject of Deep Learning

- Computational neuroscience is the study of brain function in terms of the information processing properties of the structures that make up the nervous system. 
- It is an interdisciplinary field that links neuroscience, computer science, psychology, physics, mathematics, and medicine. 
- The goal is to understand the fundamental computational principles underlying perception, learning, memory, and action.
- Artificial neural networks are inspired by biological neural networks and are used to solve complex pattern recognition and classification problems in machine learning. 
- They are made up of interconnected nodes that operate like neurons firing and connections that operate like synapses. 
- As the network is exposed to large amounts of data, it learns by adjusting the strengths of the connections (weights) between nodes in an iterative process. 
- This is similar to how synaptic connections between neurons in the brain are strengthened or weakened over time based on experience.

Some key points to remember -
- Both computational neuroscience and artificial neural networks are inspired by biology.
- Computational neuroscience focuses on understanding biological brains.
- Artificial neural networks are machine learning models inspired by biology.
- Artificial neural networks learn features and patterns in data to solve machine learning problems.
- The learning process in artificial neural networks is analogous to biological synaptic plasticity.

Advantages - Help understand intelligence and brain function. Useful for machine learning and pattern recognition problems.
Disadvantages - It is difficult to fully understand the complex dynamics of biological brains. Artificial neural networks are black boxes that can be hard to interpret.

Examples - Convolutional neural networks for image classification. Recurrent neural networks for sequence modeling.
Applications - Image recognition, natural language processing, robotics, data mining, etc.
