### History of Deep Learning

- 1950s: The idea of neural networks was first proposed in the 1950s inspired by the human brain. However, limited computational power was a major constraint in training these networks.
- 1980s: Increased computing power enabled training of neural networks with multiple layers, called deep neural networks. However, overfitting was still a major challenge.
- 1998: The concept of MNIST database of handwritten digits and convolutional neural networks was introduced.
- 2006: Geoffrey Hinton proposes a more efficient way to train neural networks called "deep belief networks"
- 2011: IBM's Watson defeats human champions at Jeopardy! demonstrating the power of machine learning and natural language processing.
- 2012: Deep Learning and neural networks beat state-of-the-art in image and speech recognition (Microsoft and Google)
- 2015: Major breakthroughs in deep learning lead to systems that can summarize text, translate between languages, generate artificial images and more. DeepMind's AlphaGo defeats Lee Sedol in the game of Go demonstrating the power of deep reinforcement learning.
- Recent years: Deep Learning achieves state-of-the-art results in various domains such as computer vision, speech recognition, natural language processing, etc. Deep Learning is being adopted by many large tech companies to power various applications and services.

Some key points to remember:

- Increasing computing power and large amounts of data have enabled the success of deep learning.
- Convolutional neural networks and recurrent neural networks are two popular architectures for deep learning.
- Deep learning has achieved massive breakthroughs in various domains and continues to push the boundaries of what machines are capable of.
