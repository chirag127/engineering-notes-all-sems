### Neural networks as universal function approximates

- Neural networks with at least one hidden layer can approximate any function arbitrarily well given enough neurons in the hidden layer. This is known as the universal approximation theorem.
- This means that neural networks are capable of learning complex patterns in data and modeling nonlinear relationships. This powerful property is what enables neural networks to be applied to a wide variety of tasks like classification, regression, etc.
- However, having more neurons does not necessarily lead to better performance. There is a trade-off between having more capacity to learn complex relationships and risk of overfitting the training data. Regularization methods are used to mitigate overfitting.

Advantages:
- Ability to learn complex nonlinear relationships and patterns
- Wide applicability to numerous tasks like classification, regression, etc.

Disadvantages:
- Risk of overfitting with more complex networks
- Requirement of large amounts of data to learn parameters for complex networks
- Computationally expensive to train complex networks

Examples: Convolutional Neural Networks, Recurrent Neural Networks, etc. can all be viewed as special types of neural networks which are universal function approximators.

Applications: Computer Vision, Natural Language Processing, Speech Recognition, etc.
