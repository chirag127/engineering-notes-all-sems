### Architectures for the notes of the Unit 3 - DIMENSIONALITY REDUCTION in the subject of Deep Learning

Dimensionality reduction is a crucial technique in deep learning that helps to reduce the number of features in a dataset while retaining as much information as possible. This technique is used to improve the efficiency and effectiveness of machine learning algorithms. There are various architectures and techniques used in dimensionality reduction, some of which are discussed below:

1. Principal Component Analysis (PCA): PCA is a linear method used for dimensionality reduction. It is used to transform a set of correlated variables into a smaller set of uncorrelated variables called principal components. PCA works by finding the eigenvectors and eigenvalues of the covariance matrix of the dataset.

2. Autoencoders: Autoencoders are neural networks used for non-linear dimensionality reduction. Autoencoders consist of an encoder and a decoder. The encoder reduces the data to a lower-dimensional representation, while the decoder attempts to reconstruct the original data from the lower-dimensional representation.

3. t-SNE: t-SNE is a non-linear dimensionality reduction technique used for visualizing high-dimensional data. t-SNE works by minimizing the divergence between the similarity of the points in the high-dimensional space and the similarity of the points in the low-dimensional space.

4. UMAP: Uniform Manifold Approximation and Projection (UMAP) is a non-linear dimensionality reduction technique that is similar to t-SNE. UMAP is faster and more scalable than t-SNE and can be used with very large datasets.

5. Isomap: Isomap is a non-linear dimensionality reduction technique that works by preserving the geodesic distances between the data points. Isomap is often used for visualizing high-dimensional data in a lower-dimensional space.

6. Locally Linear Embedding (LLE): LLE is a non-linear dimensionality reduction technique that works by preserving the local structure of the data. LLE is often used for manifold learning, where the data is assumed to lie on a low-dimensional manifold embedded in a high-dimensional space.

Mnemonics and learning tricks for these architectures can vary and may not be easy to remember. It is recommended to study and practice these techniques regularly to become proficient in dimensionality reduction. Additionally, understanding the advantages and disadvantages of each architecture can help in choosing the appropriate method for a specific problem.
