
### Convolutional Networks for the notes of the Unit 2 - DEEP NETWORKS in the subject of Deep Learning

- Convolutional Networks (also known as ConvNets or CNNs) are a type of deep neural network used for image recognition and classification. They are composed of multiple layers of neurons, each of which performs a convolution operation over the input data. The convolutional layers are followed by one or more fully-connected layers, which are used to classify the input data.

- Convolutional networks can be used to identify objects in images, such as faces and animals, as well as to classify images into different categories, such as cats and dogs. They are also used for image segmentation, where the goal is to identify different regions in an image.

- Convolutional networks have several advantages over traditional neural networks. They are more efficient at processing data, as they can learn a set of weights that can be applied to all input data. This reduces the number of parameters that need to be learned. They are also more robust to changes in the input data, as the convolutional layers are able to detect features in the data that are invariant to translation, rotation, and scaling.

- A common mnemonic for remembering the layers of a convolutional network is "Input-Convolution-Pooling-Fully Connected". This refers to the input layer, the convolutional layers, the pooling layers, and the fully-connected layers, respectively.

- Convolutional networks are widely used in computer vision, natural language processing, and other areas of artificial intelligence. They are also used in many applications, such as image classification, object detection, and image segmentation.
