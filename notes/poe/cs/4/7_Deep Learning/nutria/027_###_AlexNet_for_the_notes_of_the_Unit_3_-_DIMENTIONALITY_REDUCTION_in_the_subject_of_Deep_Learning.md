
### AlexNet for the notes of the Unit 3 - DIMENTIONALITY REDUCTION in the subject of Deep Learning

- AlexNet is a convolutional neural network (CNN) developed by Alex Krizhevsky, Ilya Sutskever, and Geoffrey Hinton in 2012. 
- It is a deep learning model that won the 2012 ImageNet Large Scale Visual Recognition Competition (ILSVRC) with a top-5 error rate of 15.3%, compared to 26.2% achieved by the runner up. 
- AlexNet is composed of 5 convolutional layers, 3 fully connected layers, and a final output layer. 
- Each convolutional layer is followed by a max-pooling layer and a ReLU activation function. 
- The first two convolutional layers have a stride of 4 and the rest have a stride of 1. 
- The first two convolutional layers also have a kernel size of 11×11 and the rest have a kernel size of 3×3. 
- The fully connected layers have 4096 neurons each. 
- The output layer has 1000 neurons and a softmax activation function. 
- AlexNet is trained with stochastic gradient descent with a learning rate of 0.01 and momentum of 0.9. 
- AlexNet uses data augmentation techniques such as random cropping, flipping, and adjusting the brightness and contrast of images. 
- AlexNet is widely used for image classification tasks and is a popular choice for transfer learning. 
- It has also been used for object detection, segmentation, and video classification tasks. 
- AlexNet has been used to develop applications such as image recognition for autonomous vehicles and medical image analysis. 
- As a mnemonic for AlexNet, it is useful to remember that AlexNet is an acronym for "Alex Krizhevsky Neural Network".
