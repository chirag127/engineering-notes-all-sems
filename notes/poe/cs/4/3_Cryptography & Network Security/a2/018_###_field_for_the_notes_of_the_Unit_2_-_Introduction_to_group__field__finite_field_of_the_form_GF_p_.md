### Unit 2 - Introduction to group, field, finite field of the form GF(p), modular arithmetic, prime and relative prime numbers

Group: A set G together with an associative binary operation on G is called a group if there exists an identity element and every element has an inverse.
Field: A field is a set where addition, subtraction, multiplication and division are defined and satisfy certain properties. Every field has an additive identity (0) and a multiplicative identity (1).
Finite field of the form GF(p): A field with a finite number of elements is called a finite field. The simplest example is the field with p elements denoted by GF(p) where p is a prime number. The elements of GF(p) are {0, 1, 2, ..., p-1} and the operations are done modulo p.
Modular arithmetic: In modular arithmetic, numbers "wrap around" upon reaching a certain value called the modulus. Only the remainder is important in modular arithmetic. It is used in computing checksums, in music, and in cryptography.
Prime and relative prime numbers: A prime number is a positive integer greater than 1 that is only divisible by 1 and itself. Two numbers are relatively prime if their greatest common divisor is 1. The numbers must be mutually prime to each other. Relative primality is important in cryptography for sharing secrets and generating keys.
Extended Euclidean Algorithm: The Extended Euclidean Algorithm finds the coefficients of Bézout's identity (a linear combination of two numbers that equals gcd(a, b)). It can be used to find the multiplicative inverse of a number modulo m which is important in cryptography.
