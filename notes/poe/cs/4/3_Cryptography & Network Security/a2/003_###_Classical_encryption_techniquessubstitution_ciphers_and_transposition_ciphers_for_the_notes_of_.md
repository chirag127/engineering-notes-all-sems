### Classical encryption techniques

- Substitution ciphers: In substitution ciphers, each letter of the plaintext is replaced by another letter or symbol. The security depends on the strength of the cipher alphabet (the mapping of plaintext letters to cipher letters). Examples: Caesar cipher, one-time pad.
- Transposition ciphers: In transposition ciphers, the positions of the letters in the plaintext are shifted according to a systematic rule. The security depends on the strength of the rule. Examples: Rail fence cipher, route cipher.

### Substitution ciphers

- In substitution ciphers, each letter of the plaintext is replaced by another letter or symbol.
- The cipher alphabet (the mapping of plaintext letters to cipher letters) must be kept secret.
- The security depends on the strength of the cipher alphabet. If the cipher alphabet is a shifted version of the plaintext alphabet (e.g. Caesar cipher), it is easy to crack by analyzing letter frequencies.
- One-time pad is a perfect cipher if the key is truly random and used only once. However, it requires a key as long as the plaintext, making it impractical.

### Transposition ciphers

- In transposition ciphers, the positions of the letters in the plaintext are shifted according to a systematic rule.
- The security depends on the strength of the rule (or key). If the rule is simple (e.g. spiral cipher), it is easy to crack by analyzing the pattern.
- Examples: Rail fence cipher, route cipher.
