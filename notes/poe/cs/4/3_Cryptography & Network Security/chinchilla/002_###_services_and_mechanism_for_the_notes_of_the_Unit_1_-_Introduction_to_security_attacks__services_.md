Cryptography is the practice of securing information by converting it into an unreadable format using encryption algorithms. This Unit introduces the fundamental concepts of cryptography and network security. It covers various aspects such as confidentiality, integrity, authentication, and non-repudiation. The following are the topics covered in this Unit:

1. Security Attacks: This section covers various types of security attacks such as active attacks, passive attacks, insider attacks, and outsider attacks. It also covers the different phases of a security attack, such as reconnaissance, scanning, gaining access, maintaining access, and covering tracks.

2. Security Services: This section covers the different types of security services provided by cryptography such as confidentiality, integrity, authentication, and non-repudiation.

3. Classical Encryption Techniques: This section covers two types of classical encryption techniques: substitution ciphers and transposition ciphers. Substitution ciphers replace each plaintext character with a different ciphertext character, while transposition ciphers rearrange the plaintext characters.

4. Cryptanalysis: This section covers the process of breaking a cryptographic system using different techniques such as brute force, frequency analysis, and known plaintext attack.

5. Steganography: This section covers the technique of hiding information within other information so that it is not visible to the naked eye.

6. Stream and Block Ciphers: This section covers the two types of symmetric key encryption algorithms: stream ciphers and block ciphers. Stream ciphers encrypt one bit or byte at a time, while block ciphers encrypt a block of plaintext at a time. 

7. Modern Block Ciphers: This section covers the principles of block ciphers, Shannon's theory of confusion and diffusion, the Fiestal structure, and the Data Encryption Standard (DES). DES is a widely used block cipher that uses a 56-bit key. It also covers the strength of DES, differential cryptanalysis, and the different modes of operation for block ciphers such as ECB, CBC, CFB, and OFB.

8. Triple DES: This section covers the concept of Triple DES, which is a modification of the DES algorithm. Triple DES uses three keys to encrypt the plaintext, making it more secure than the DES algorithm.

In conclusion, this unit provides a comprehensive introduction to cryptography and network security. It covers various topics such as security attacks, security services, classical encryption techniques, cryptanalysis, steganography, stream and block ciphers, modern block ciphers, and Triple DES. Understanding these concepts is essential for securing information in today's digital world.
