
### Classical encryption techniquessubstitution ciphers and transposition ciphers

Substitution ciphers are a type of encryption technique in which characters or units of plaintext are replaced by other characters or units to produce ciphertext. The units may be single letters, pairs of letters, triplets of letters, mixtures of the above, and so forth. This technique is also known as monoalphabetic substitution.

Transposition ciphers are a type of encryption technique in which the position of the characters or units of plaintext are rearranged to produce ciphertext. The units may be single letters, pairs of letters, triplets of letters, mixtures of the above, and so forth.

Cryptanalysis is the study of techniques for obtaining the meaning of encrypted information, without having access to the secret information that is normally required to do so. It is also known as codebreaking.

Steganography is the practice of concealing a file, message, image, or video within another file, message, image, or video.

Stream and block ciphers are two types of encryption algorithms. Stream ciphers encrypt data one bit or byte at a time, while block ciphers take a number of bits or bytes and encrypt them as a single unit.

Modern Block Ciphers: Block ciphers principles, Shannon’s theory of confusion and diffusion, fiestal structure, Data encryption standard(DES), Strength of DES, Idea of differential cryptanalysis, block cipher modes of operations, Triple DES 

Modern block ciphers are based on principles developed by Claude Shannon in 1949. The principles are known as Shannon’s theory of confusion and diffusion. The idea is to make the relationship between the plaintext and the ciphertext as complex and unpredictable as possible.

The Feistal structure is a design structure used in modern block ciphers. It is named after Horst Feistal, who developed the structure in 1973. The structure consists of a series of rounds, each of which performs a substitution followed by a permutation.

The Data Encryption Standard (DES) is a block cipher developed by IBM in the 1970s. It uses a 56-bit key and operates on 64-bit blocks of data. DES is considered to be a weak cipher, as it can be broken with relative ease using modern computing power.

The strength of DES lies in its key length. A longer key length makes it more difficult for an attacker to guess the key. As computing power increases, the key length must be increased in order to maintain the same level of security.

The idea of differential cryptanalysis was developed by Eli Biham and Adi Shamir in the early 1990s. It is a technique used to attack block ciphers. It is based on the observation that if two ciphertexts are generated using the same key, the difference between the two ciphertexts can be used to derive information about the key.

Block cipher modes of operations are methods of using block ciphers to encrypt data of arbitrary length. The most common modes are ECB (Electronic Codebook), CBC (Cipher Block Chaining), CFB (Cipher Feedback), OFB (Output Feedback), and CTR (Counter).

Triple DES (3DES) is a variant of the DES algorithm. It uses three 56-bit keys and operates on 64-bit blocks of data. It is considered to be more secure than DES, as it increases the key length to 168 bits.
