
### Field for the Notes of the Unit 2 - Introduction to Group, Field, Finite Field of the Form GF(p), Modular Arithmetic, Prime and Relative Prime Numbers, Extended Euclidean Algorithm, Advanced Encryption Standard (AES) Encryption and Decryption, Fermat’s and Euler’s Theorem, Primarily Testing, Chinese Remainder Theorem, Discrete Logarithmic Problem, Principals of Public Key Crypto Systems, RSA Algorithm, Security of RSA in the Subject of Cryptography & Network Security

* Group: A set of elements with an operation defined on them that satisfies certain axioms.
* Field: A set of elements with two operations defined on them that satisfies certain axioms.
* Finite Field of the Form GF(p): A field with a finite number of elements, where p is a prime number.
* Modular Arithmetic: A system of arithmetic for integers, where numbers "wrap around" after they reach a certain value.
* Prime and Relative Prime Numbers: Prime numbers are numbers that are only divisible by 1 and themselves, while relative prime numbers are numbers that do not have any common factors other than 1.
* Extended Euclidean Algorithm: An algorithm used to find the greatest common divisor of two numbers, as well as the coefficients of the linear combination of the two numbers.
* Advanced Encryption Standard (AES) Encryption and Decryption: A symmetric-key encryption algorithm used to protect sensitive information.
* Fermat's and Euler's Theorem: Theorems which relate prime numbers and the number of elements in a finite field.
* Primality Testing: A method used to determine if a number is prime or not.
* Chinese Remainder Theorem: A theorem which allows for the calculation of the remainder from a large number of divisions.
* Discrete Logarithmic Problem: A problem which involves finding the logarithm of a given number with respect to a given base.
* Principals of Public Key Crypto Systems: A system of cryptography which uses two keys, a public key and a private key, to encrypt and decrypt messages.
* RSA Algorithm: An algorithm used to encrypt and decrypt messages using the principal of public key cryptography.
* Security of RSA: The security of RSA depends on the difficulty of factoring large numbers.
