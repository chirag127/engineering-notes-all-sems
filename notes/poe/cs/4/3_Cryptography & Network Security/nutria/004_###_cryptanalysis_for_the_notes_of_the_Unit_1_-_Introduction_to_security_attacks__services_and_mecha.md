Cryptanalysis is the science of breaking codes and ciphers, and is an essential part of cryptography. Cryptanalysis is used to discover weaknesses in cryptographic systems, and to develop methods for defeating them. Cryptanalysis can be used to attack both classical and modern encryption techniques.

#### Classical Encryption Techniques

Classical encryption techniques include substitution ciphers and transposition ciphers. In a substitution cipher, each letter of the plaintext is replaced with another letter or symbol. In a transposition cipher, the letters of the plaintext are rearranged, but not replaced.

#### Steganography

Steganography is the practice of hiding information in plain sight. It is used to conceal messages in digital images, audio files, and other forms of digital media.

#### Stream and Block Ciphers

Stream ciphers encrypt data one bit or byte at a time, while block ciphers encrypt data in blocks of bits or bytes. Stream ciphers are faster and more efficient than block ciphers, but are also less secure.

#### Modern Block Ciphers

Modern block ciphers are based on the principles of Shannon’s theory of confusion and diffusion. They use a Feistal structure, which is a combination of substitution and transposition ciphers. The most widely used modern block cipher is the Data Encryption Standard (DES), which was developed in the 1970s. DES is a symmetric key algorithm that uses a 56-bit key for encryption. DES has been replaced by the more secure Triple DES, which uses a 168-bit key.

Differential cryptanalysis is a technique used to attack block ciphers. It is based on the idea that the difference between two plaintexts should not affect the difference between the corresponding ciphertexts. Block cipher modes of operations are used to extend the block cipher to encrypt longer messages.
