#### Switching techniques and multiplexing in Computer Networks

Switching is the process of forwarding packets from one network device to another. There are three types of switching techniques: circuit switching, packet switching, and message switching.

Multiplexing is a technique that allows multiple signals to share a single communication channel. There are two types of multiplexing: frequency division multiplexing (FDM) and time division multiplexing (TDM).

Let's take a closer look at each of these techniques:

### Switching Techniques

1. Circuit Switching: In this technique, a dedicated communication path is established between two devices for the duration of the communication. The path remains reserved for the two devices until the communication is complete. Circuit switching is used in traditional telephone systems. It ensures that there is no interference or congestion during the communication.

2. Packet Switching: In this technique, data is divided into small packets and sent over the network. Each packet contains the destination address, source address, and data. Packets are sent from one device to another based on the destination address. Packets may take different routes to reach their destination. Packet switching is used in the Internet and is more efficient than circuit switching.

3. Message Switching: In this technique, messages are divided into smaller units called packets. The packets are stored in intermediate nodes until the entire message has been received. The message is then forwarded to the destination. This technique is rarely used today.

### Multiplexing Techniques

1. Frequency Division Multiplexing (FDM): In this technique, multiple signals are combined into a single signal by assigning each signal a different frequency range. Each signal is modulated onto a different carrier frequency and then combined into a single signal. The signals can be separated at the receiving end by demodulating the signal and filtering out the desired frequency range.

2. Time Division Multiplexing (TDM): In this technique, multiple signals are interleaved in time. Each signal is given a time slot during which it can transmit data. The time slots are repeated periodically. The signals can be separated at the receiving end by demultiplexing the time slots.

#### Mnemonics and Learning Tricks

Unfortunately, there are no easy mnemonics or learning tricks for switching techniques and multiplexing. The best way to learn these concepts is to understand the differences between the techniques and practice applying them in different scenarios. It is important to understand when to use each technique and the advantages and disadvantages of each.
