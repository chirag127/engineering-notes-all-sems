#### Categories of networks in computer networks

The major categories of networks in computer networks are:

1. Local Area Networks (LANs): LANs connect computers and devices in a limited geographical area, such as an office building, a group of office buildings, or a home. Ethernet and Wi-Fi are the most popular LAN technologies.
Advantages: Fast speed, inexpensive, easy to setup and maintain.
Disadvantages: Limited geographical reach.

2. Wide Area Networks (WANs): WANs connect LANs and other networks together, enabling users and devices in one location to access resources in other locations. The Internet is the largest WAN. Technologies like fiber optic cables, satellites, and telephone lines are used to establish WAN connections.
Advantages: Cover large geographical areas, connect multiple LANs.
Disadvantages: Typically more expensive than LANs, can be slower.

3. Metropolitan Area Networks (MANs): MANs connect users and devices in a metropolitan area and lie between LANs and WANs in scale. They are often used by organizations to connect multiple LANs in a city.
Advantages: Larger reach than LANs but less expensive than WANs.
Disadvantages: Technical expertise and costs required to set up and maintain.

4. Wireless Networks: Wireless networks use radio frequencies or microwaves instead of physical cables to connect devices. Wi-Fi and mobile networks are common types of wireless networks.
Advantages: Mobility, reduced costs of additional infrastructure.
Disadvantages: Potential security and interference issues, limited range.
