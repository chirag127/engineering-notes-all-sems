#### Channel allocation in medium access control

- Channel allocation refers to the process of assigning channels to different nodes in a network for communication.
- In medium access control, channels need to be allocated efficiently among multiple nodes to avoid collisions and maximize throughput.
- Some key ways of channel allocation in MAC are:

1. Fixed channel allocation: Channels are preassigned to nodes. Eg: In FDMA, each node is allocated a dedicated frequency band. Advantage is no collisions. Disadvantage is inflexibility and underutilization of channels if traffic is uneven across nodes.
2. Dynamic channel allocation: Channels are allocated on demand depending on traffic. Eg: In CDMA, codes are allocated on demand. This is more efficient but requires complex coordination mechanisms.
3. Hybrid channel allocation: Combines fixed and dynamic allocations. Some channels are preassigned and remaining channels are allocated dynamically.

**Mnemonics**:
- Think `FDMA`: Nodes get `dedicated lanes` (frequency bands)
- Think `CDMA`: Nodes get `access codes on demand`
- Hybrid: Gets best of both worlds
