#### Network topology design in Computer Networks

- **Bus topology:** All devices are connected to a central cable called bus/backbone. Data is transmitted across the bus and received by all devices, addressed devices accept the data.
**Mnemonic:** Think of a bus transporting data and devices waiting at stops to get on/off the bus.
**Advantages:** inexpensive, simple.
**Disadvantages:** heavy traffic can slow down the network, difficult to troubleshoot faults.

- **Star topology:** All devices connect to a central switch. Data is sent to the switch which routes it to the addressed device.
**Mnemonic:** Think of a star shape with devices at the end of each ray connected to the central switch.
**Advantages:** easy to troubleshoot, scalable.
**Disadvantages:** dependent on central switch, expensive.

- **Ring topology:** Devices are connected in a closed loop. Data is passed along the ring in one direction and received by addressed devices.
**Mnemonic:** Think of a ring passing on data in one direction.
**Advantages:** inexpensive, simple.
**Disadvantages:** heavy traffic or faults can take down the entire network.

- **Tree topology:** Consists of a root node and hierarchical layers of switches. Resembles an upside-down tree.
**Mnemonic:** Think of a tree with the root at the top and hierarchical branches below.
**Advantages:** robust, scalable.
**Disadvantages:** expensive, complex.
