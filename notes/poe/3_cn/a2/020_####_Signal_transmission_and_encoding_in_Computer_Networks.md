#### Signal transmission and encoding in Computer Networks

- Signals are transmitted over a physical medium in the form of electromagnetic waves or electrical signals in a computer network. The encoding is done to convert the data into a signal that can be transmitted over the physical medium.
- The most common types of signal encoding are:
- Analog encoding - The signal is encoded as a continuous wave where the amplitude or frequency is varied to map the signal. It is less immune to noise but can achieve high data rates. Example: Analog modem
- Digital encoding - The signal is encoded as a series of pulses where each pulse represents a binary digit (0 or 1). It is more noise resistant but lower data rates. Example: Digital Subscriber Line (DSL)
- Baseband transmission - The encoded signal is directly transmitted over a network medium. It requires a dedicated transmission medium and offers high data rates. Example: Ethernet
- Broadband transmission - The encoded signal is modulated to a higher frequency band before transmission. It can share the medium with other signals and achieve high data rates. Example: Cable modem, DSL

Some key points to remember:
- Analog encoding can achieve high data rates but is less noise resistant
- Digital encoding is more noise resistant but offers lower data rates
- Baseband transmission requires a dedicated medium but can achieve high data rates
- Broadband transmission can share the medium but may be susceptible to interference

 Mnemonics:
- Think of analog encoding as a smooth wave (continuous changes) but noisy (less resistant)
- Think of digital encoding as pulses (discrete changes) that are robust (more resistant to noise)
- Think of baseband as a dedicated highway lane (sole use of medium) resulting in fast speeds (high data rates)
- Think of broadband as a multi-lane highway (shared use of medium) that can be traffic-prone (susceptible to interference)
