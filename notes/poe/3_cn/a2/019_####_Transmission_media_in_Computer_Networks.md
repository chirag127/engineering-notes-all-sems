#### Transmission media in Computer Networks

The transmission medium refers to the physical path between the transmitter and the receiver in a data transmission system. The properties of the medium determine the rate of data transmission and the distance over which data can be transmitted reliably.

The most common types of transmission media are:

- Twisted pair wire: Twisted pair wire consists of two insulated copper wires twisted together. It is commonly used for telephone lines and local area networks. The twists in the wires reduce crosstalk and noise.
- Coaxial cable: Coaxial cable has an inner conductor surrounded by an insulating layer and an outer shield conductor. It is commonly used for cable television networks. The shielding makes coaxial cable less prone to interference.
- Fiber optic cable: Fiber optic cable uses glass or plastic fibers to transmit data as light pulses. It has low attenuation and high bandwidth, allowing for high speed and long distance data transmission. However, fiber optic cable is more expensive than copper cable.
- Wireless: Wireless refers to transmission via radio waves or microwaves. Wireless networks are often cheaper and easier to set up but they are more prone to interference and have lower bandwidth than wired networks. Cellular and Wi-Fi networks are common examples of wireless networks.

Some mnemonics to remember the types of transmission media:

- Twisted pair: Two **twin** wires **twisted** together
- Coaxial cable: One central conductor, surrounded by an **insulating layer**, then a **shielding layer**
- Fiber optic: Uses pulses of **light** through **fibers**
- Wireless: Uses **radio waves** or **microwaves** with no physical cable
