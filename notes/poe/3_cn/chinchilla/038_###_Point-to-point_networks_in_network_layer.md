### Point-to-point networks in network layer

Point-to-point network is a type of communication network where two devices or nodes are directly connected to each other through a dedicated communication link. In the network layer, point-to-point networks are used to establish a connection-oriented communication between two nodes in a packet-switched network.

#### Characteristics of point-to-point networks

- Two endpoints: A point-to-point network contains only two endpoints or nodes, which are connected through a dedicated communication link.
- Dedicated link: The communication link between the two nodes is dedicated, which means that the entire bandwidth of the link is available for communication between the two nodes.
- Full-duplex communication: Point-to-point networks support full-duplex communication, which means that both nodes can transmit and receive data simultaneously.
- Connection-oriented: Point-to-point networks provide a connection-oriented communication where a logical connection is established between the two nodes before data transmission can start.

#### Advantages of point-to-point networks

- Efficient use of bandwidth: Point-to-point networks provide a dedicated communication link between two nodes, which means that the entire bandwidth of the link is available for communication between the two nodes. This makes point-to-point networks more efficient in terms of bandwidth utilization compared to other types of networks.
- Secure communication: Since point-to-point networks provide a dedicated communication link between two nodes, it is more secure compared to other types of networks that use shared communication links.
- High reliability: Point-to-point networks are more reliable compared to other types of networks since they provide a dedicated communication link between two nodes, which means that there are fewer chances of network congestion or failure.

#### Disadvantages of point-to-point networks

- Limited scalability: Point-to-point networks are limited in terms of scalability since they can only connect two nodes. If there are more than two nodes that need to communicate with each other, multiple point-to-point links need to be established, which can be complex and expensive.
- Higher cost: Point-to-point networks require a dedicated communication link between two nodes, which can be costly compared to other types of networks that use shared communication links.

#### Examples of point-to-point networks

- Leased lines: Leased lines are a type of point-to-point network where a dedicated communication link is established between two nodes over a long distance. Leased lines are commonly used by businesses for high-speed data communication between their branch offices.
- Dial-up connections: Dial-up connections are a type of point-to-point network where a connection is established between two nodes over a telephone line. Dial-up connections are commonly used for remote access to a network.
- Virtual private networks (VPNs): VPNs are a type of point-to-point network where a secure connection is established between two nodes over a public network, such as the internet. VPNs are commonly used by businesses to provide remote access to their employees.

#### Applications of point-to-point networks

- High-speed data communication: Point-to-point networks are commonly used for high-speed data communication between two nodes over a long distance.
- Remote access: Point-to-point networks are commonly used for remote access to a network, such as dial-up connections or VPNs.
- Video conferencing: Point-to-point networks are commonly used for video conferencing between two locations over a long distance. 

#### Learning trick
One simple way to remember the characteristics of point-to-point networks is to think of it as a "one-to-one" communication, where only two nodes are involved and they have a dedicated link between them.
