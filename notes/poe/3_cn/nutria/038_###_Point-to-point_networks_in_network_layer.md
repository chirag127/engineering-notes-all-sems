
### Point-to-point networks in network layer 

Point-to-point networks are computer networks that are composed of two or more nodes connected by a single physical link. They are usually used to connect two or more computers or networks together, such as in a home or office network.

A point-to-point network is a type of network topology that consists of a single physical link connecting two nodes. This type of network is often used to connect two computers or networks together, such as in a home or office network. It is also commonly used to connect two or more computers or networks together in a wide area network (WAN).

One of the advantages of a point-to-point network is that it is relatively simple to set up and maintain. It also provides a direct connection between the two nodes, meaning that data can be transferred quickly and securely. Furthermore, it is relatively inexpensive compared to other types of networks.

However, one of the disadvantages of a point-to-point network is that it is limited in terms of scalability. As the number of nodes increases, the complexity of the network increases, making it more difficult to manage. Additionally, point-to-point networks are often more vulnerable to security threats due to the direct connection between the two nodes.
