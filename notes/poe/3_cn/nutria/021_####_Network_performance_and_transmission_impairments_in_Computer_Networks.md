
#### Network Performance and Transmission Impairments in Computer Networks

* Network performance is a measure of how well a network is performing, and is typically measured in terms of throughput, latency, and packet loss.
* Transmission impairments are any factors that can degrade the performance of a network, such as noise, interference, and signal attenuation.
* Noise is any unwanted signal that can interfere with the transmission of data. Examples of noise include static, crosstalk, and thermal noise.
* Interference occurs when two or more signals overlap, resulting in a distorted signal. Common sources of interference include other wireless networks, electrical devices, and even the sun.
* Signal attenuation is the weakening of a signal as it travels through a medium, such as a cable or a wireless channel. This is caused by factors such as distance, obstacles, and the environment.
* Error detection and correction techniques can be used to reduce the impact of transmission impairments. Examples include forward error correction, automatic repeat request, and cyclic redundancy check.
* Quality of Service (QoS) is a set of techniques used to prioritize certain types of network traffic. This can help to reduce the impact of transmission impairments and improve the overall performance of a network.
* Mnemonics can be used to help remember the different types of transmission impairments. For example, the acronym "NICE" can be used to remember Noise, Interference, Signal Attenuation, and Error detection and correction techniques.
