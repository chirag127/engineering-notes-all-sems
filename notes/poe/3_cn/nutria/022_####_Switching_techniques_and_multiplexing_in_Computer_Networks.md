

#### Switching techniques and multiplexing in Computer Networks

- **Switching** is the process of routing data from one node to another in a network. It is used to connect multiple devices together, allowing them to communicate with each other. 
- There are two main types of switching techniques: **circuit switching** and **packet switching**. 
- **Circuit switching** is a technique that establishes a dedicated physical connection between two nodes for the duration of a communication session. It is commonly used for telephone calls, as it guarantees a constant connection between the two nodes. 
- **Packet switching** is a technique that breaks data into small packets and sends them to their destination through a network of routers. It is more efficient than circuit switching, as it can route packets around congested routes. 
- **Multiplexing** is the process of combining multiple data streams into one signal. It is commonly used in networks to reduce the amount of bandwidth being used. There are two main types of multiplexing: **frequency division multiplexing** and **time division multiplexing**. 
- **Frequency division multiplexing** is a technique that combines multiple signals into one signal by assigning each signal a different frequency. It is commonly used in radio communications. 
- **Time division multiplexing** is a technique that combines multiple signals into one signal by assigning each signal a different time slot. It is commonly used in telephone systems. 
- Mnemonics for remembering switching techniques and multiplexing: 
    - **Circuit Switching**: C for Connection 
    - **Packet Switching**: P for Packets 
    - **Frequency Division Multiplexing**: F for Frequency 
    - **Time Division Multiplexing**: T for Time
