
### Forwarding and Delivery in Network Layer

The Network Layer is responsible for forwarding and delivering data packets from the source to the destination. It is responsible for routing the packets through the most efficient path, as well as ensuring that the packets are delivered correctly.

* **Routing**: The process of finding the most efficient path from the source to the destination is known as routing. The Network Layer must decide which path to take, taking into account factors such as latency, throughput, cost, and reliability.

* **Forwarding**: The process of forwarding data packets from the source to the destination is known as forwarding. The Network Layer must decide which router to send the packet to, taking into account factors such as the destination address, the current network topology, and the current traffic load.

* **Reliability**: The Network Layer must ensure that the data packets are delivered correctly. It must detect and discard corrupted packets, as well as ensure that all packets arrive at the destination in the correct order.

* **Mnemonics**: To help remember the functions of the Network Layer, it is helpful to remember the acronym “FDR”, which stands for “Forwarding, Delivery, and Reliability”.
