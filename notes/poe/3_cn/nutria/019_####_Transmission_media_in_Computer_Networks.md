
#### Transmission Media in Computer Networks

Transmission media are the physical pathways that allow for the transmission of a signal from one point to another. Common types of computer network transmission media include twisted-pair cables, coaxial cables, optical fibers, and radio waves.

##### Twisted-Pair Cables
Twisted-pair cables are the most common type of transmission media used in computer networks. They consist of two insulated copper wires twisted together. The twists help reduce the amount of interference from external sources, such as electrical fields and electromagnetic radiation.

##### Coaxial Cables
Coaxial cables are similar to twisted-pair cables, but they use a single copper wire surrounded by an insulating material. They are used mainly for cable television and high-speed internet connections.

##### Optical Fibers
Optical fibers are made of glass or plastic and are used to transmit data over long distances. They are capable of transmitting data at very high speeds and are also more resistant to interference than twisted-pair cables.

##### Radio Waves
Radio waves are electromagnetic waves that can be used to transmit data over long distances. They are used mainly for wireless networking and for long-distance communication.
