
### Multiplexing in transport layer

- Multiplexing is a communication process that allows multiple signals to be transmitted over a single physical medium. 
- In the transport layer, multiplexing allows multiple applications to share a single physical connection. 
- Multiplexing is done using a process called segmentation, which divides the data into smaller chunks and then combines them together. 
- Multiplexing is used to increase the efficiency of data transmission by allowing multiple applications to use the same connection. 
- Multiplexing is also used to reduce the amount of data that needs to be sent, as only the data that is necessary is sent. 
- Multiplexing is also used to increase the reliability of data transmission, as the data is divided into smaller chunks and sent separately. 
- Multiplexing can be implemented in various ways, such as time-division multiplexing (TDM), frequency-division multiplexing (FDM), and statistical multiplexing (SM). 
- Advantages of multiplexing include increased efficiency, reduced data transmission, and improved reliability. 
- Disadvantages of multiplexing include increased complexity, increased cost, and increased latency. 
- Examples of multiplexing include Ethernet, Wi-Fi, and cellular networks. 
- Applications of multiplexing include voice and video communication, data transmission, and streaming media. 

Mnemonic: 
FDM - Frequency Division Multiplexing 
TDM - Time Division Multiplexing 
SM - Statistical Multiplexing
