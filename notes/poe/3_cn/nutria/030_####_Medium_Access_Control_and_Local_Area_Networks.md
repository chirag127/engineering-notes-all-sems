
### Medium Access Control and Local Area Networks

* Medium Access Control (MAC) is a sub-layer of the Data Link Layer of the OSI model responsible for controlling how devices in a network gain access to a shared medium. 
* MAC addresses are used by the MAC sub-layer to identify a device on a network.
* Local Area Networks (LANs) are computer networks that are limited to a small geographic area, such as a home, office, or building. 
* LANs typically use Ethernet or Wi-Fi technology to link the computers in the network. 
* The MAC sub-layer is responsible for controlling how devices in a LAN gain access to the shared medium. 
* MAC protocols, such as Carrier Sense Multiple Access with Collision Detection (CSMA/CD) and Carrier Sense Multiple Access with Collision Avoidance (CSMA/CA), are used to control how devices in a LAN gain access to the shared medium. 
* CSMA/CD is a MAC protocol that is used in Ethernet networks. 
* CSMA/CA is a MAC protocol that is used in Wi-Fi networks. 
* The MAC sub-layer is also responsible for controlling the flow of data on the network. 
* Flow control protocols, such as the Sliding Window Protocol, are used to control the flow of data on a network. 
* Mnemonics and learning tricks can be helpful for understanding and remembering how the MAC sub-layer works and how different MAC protocols are used in different types of networks. For example, the mnemonic "CSC" can be used to remember the three main MAC protocols: CSMA/CD, CSMA/CA, and the Sliding Window Protocol.
