## Unit 7 - NoSQL Databases

NoSQL databases have been gaining popularity in recent years due to their ability to handle large volumes of unstructured data. This unit covers the basics of NoSQL databases, their advantages and disadvantages, and their applications.

### What are NoSQL Databases?

NoSQL databases are non-relational databases that store and retrieve data in a flexible schema. Unlike relational databases, which have a fixed schema, NoSQL databases can store data in various formats, including key-value pairs, document-based, column-oriented, and graph-based.

### Advantages of NoSQL Databases

- Better scalability: NoSQL databases can handle large volumes of unstructured data with ease, making them ideal for big data applications.
- High availability: NoSQL databases can replicate data across multiple servers, ensuring that data is always available even if one server goes down.
- Flexibility: NoSQL databases can store data in various formats, making them ideal for storing unstructured data such as social media posts, images, videos, and sensor data.
- Cost-effective: NoSQL databases can be deployed on commodity hardware, making them more cost-effective than traditional relational databases.

### Disadvantages of NoSQL Databases

- Lack of ACID compliance: NoSQL databases sacrifice ACID compliance for scalability and availability, making them less suitable for applications that require strong consistency and transactional support.
- Limited query support: NoSQL databases have limited support for complex queries, making it challenging to perform ad-hoc analysis on the data.
- No standardization: NoSQL databases lack standardization, making it challenging to switch between different NoSQL databases without significant effort.

### Types of NoSQL Databases

There are four main types of NoSQL databases:

- Key-value store: Stores data as a collection of key-value pairs.
- Document-based: Stores data as a document, typically in JSON or XML format.
- Column-oriented: Stores data in columns rather than rows, making it ideal for data warehousing and analytics.
- Graph-based: Stores data in nodes and edges, making it ideal for representing complex relationships between entities.

### Applications of NoSQL Databases

NoSQL databases are ideal for applications that require high scalability, flexibility, and availability, such as:

- Social media platforms: NoSQL databases can store large volumes of unstructured data such as tweets, posts, and images.
- Internet of Things (IoT): NoSQL databases can store sensor data from millions of devices.
- E-commerce: NoSQL databases can store product catalogs and customer data.
- Big data analytics: NoSQL databases can store massive amounts of data for real-time analytics and machine learning.

Overall, NoSQL databases offer a flexible and scalable alternative to traditional relational databases for applications that require handling large amounts of unstructured data.
