### Low Level Design for the notes of the Unit 3 - Software Design in the subject of Software Engineering

Low Level Design (LLD) is the second stage of the software design process. It is the process of detailing the design of the software system at a lower level of abstraction. This stage of design involves creating detailed design documents that specify how the software will be implemented.

In this stage, the design is broken down into modules, and each module is further decomposed into sub-modules. The LLD documents describe the functionality of each module and the interfaces between them. The LLD documents also specify the data structures and algorithms to be used for each module.

#### Advantages of Low Level Design

- Provides a detailed view of the system design.
- Helps in identifying the potential problems and drawbacks of the system.
- Helps in identifying the modules that can be reused in future projects.
- Helps in estimating the time and cost of the project.
- Helps in understanding the system better.

#### Disadvantages of Low Level Design

- Time-consuming process.
- Requires a high level of expertise.
- Changes made at this stage can be costly and time-consuming.

#### Example of Low Level Design

Let's consider an example of a banking application. The LLD documents for this application will detail the design of the various modules such as login, account creation, money transfer, and so on. The LLD documents will specify the data structures to be used, the algorithms to be implemented, and the interfaces between the modules.

#### Applications of Low Level Design

Low Level Design is used in various fields such as software engineering, electrical engineering, and mechanical engineering. It is used in the design of complex systems such as aircraft, automobiles, and software systems.
