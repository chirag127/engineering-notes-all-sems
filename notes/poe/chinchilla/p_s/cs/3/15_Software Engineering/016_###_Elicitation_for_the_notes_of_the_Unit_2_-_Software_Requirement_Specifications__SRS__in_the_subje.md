### Elicitation for the notes of the Unit 2 - Software Requirement Specifications (SRS) in the subject of Software Engineering.

Elicitation is the process of gathering requirements from stakeholders for software development. It is a crucial step in Software Requirement Specifications (SRS) as it helps in understanding the requirements of the software that is being developed.

The following points explain the process of elicitation:

1. Identification of Stakeholders: The first step in elicitation is to identify the stakeholders who will be involved in the development process. Stakeholders can be anyone who is affected by the software, such as users, developers, and managers.

2. Planning Elicitation: Once stakeholders are identified, the next step is planning the elicitation process. This involves selecting the appropriate techniques for requirements gathering, scheduling meetings, and identifying the necessary resources.

3. Conducting Interviews: Interviews are one of the most common techniques used for elicitation. They involve asking questions to stakeholders to understand their requirements. Open-ended questions are usually used to allow stakeholders to express their thoughts freely.

4. Focus Groups: Focus groups involve bringing together a group of stakeholders to discuss their requirements. This technique is useful when a group of stakeholders has similar requirements or when there is a need to resolve conflicting requirements.

5. Prototyping: Prototyping involves creating a working model of the software to gather requirements. This technique is useful when stakeholders have difficulty expressing their requirements in words.

6. Observation: Observation involves watching stakeholders perform their tasks to understand their requirements. This technique is useful when stakeholders are unable to articulate their requirements.

7. Document Analysis: Document analysis involves reviewing existing documents, such as user manuals and specifications, to gather requirements. This technique is useful when stakeholders are unable to provide requirements directly.

Advantages of Elicitation:

- Elicitation helps in understanding the requirements of the software accurately.
- It helps in identifying the stakeholders who will be affected by the software.
- It helps in resolving conflicting requirements.
- It helps in identifying the necessary resources for the development process.

Disadvantages of Elicitation:

- Elicitation can be time-consuming and costly.
- Stakeholders may have different opinions on what they want, leading to conflicting requirements.
- Some stakeholders may not be able to articulate their requirements clearly.

Examples of Elicitation:

- A software development team conducting interviews with users to understand their requirements for a new mobile app.
- A focus group of managers discussing their requirements for a new project management software.
- A software development team creating a prototype of a new software to gather requirements from stakeholders.

Applications of Elicitation:

- Elicitation is used in various fields, such as software development, business analysis, and project management.
- It is useful in developing software, products, and services that meet the needs of stakeholders.

In conclusion, elicitation is a crucial step in Software Requirement Specifications (SRS) as it helps in understanding the requirements of the software accurately. It involves identifying stakeholders, planning the elicitation process, and using various techniques, such as interviews, focus groups, and prototyping, to gather requirements. Elicitation has its advantages and disadvantages, but it is a useful technique in developing software, products, and services that meet the needs of stakeholders.
