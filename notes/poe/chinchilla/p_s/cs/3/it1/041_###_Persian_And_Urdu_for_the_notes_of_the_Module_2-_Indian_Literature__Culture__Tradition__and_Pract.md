### Persian And Urdu

Persian and Urdu are two languages that have had a significant impact on Indian literature, culture, tradition, and practices. Here are some important points to keep in mind about these languages:

- Persian was introduced to India during the reign of the Mughal Empire. It was the language of the court and was used for official purposes. Many Indian rulers, including Akbar, were patrons of Persian literature and poetry.
- Urdu, on the other hand, is a language that was born out of the fusion of Persian, Arabic, and Hindi. It originated in the 13th century in North India and was used by the Mughals as a language of communication between different regions of the country. Urdu gradually became popular as a language of poetry and literature.
- Both Persian and Urdu have had a major influence on Indian literature. Persian literature, including works by poets such as Rumi and Hafez, has been translated into many Indian languages. Urdu poetry, on the other hand, is an integral part of Indian culture and has produced renowned poets such as Mirza Ghalib and Allama Iqbal.
- Persian and Urdu have also had an impact on Indian music. Many classical Indian musicians have been influenced by Persian music, and many Indian film songs have lyrics in Urdu.
- Persian and Urdu have also contributed to Indian cuisine. Many Persian and Mughlai dishes, such as biryani and kebabs, are popular in India.
- However, the use of Persian and Urdu has also been a point of controversy in India. Some argue that the promotion of these languages over Indian languages has contributed to a loss of national identity, while others argue that they are an integral part of Indian culture and should be celebrated.

In conclusion, Persian and Urdu have played an important role in shaping Indian literature, culture, tradition, and practices. While they have had their share of controversy, they remain an integral part of Indian heritage.
