### Buddhist And Jain Literature in Pali, Prakrit And Sanskrit

Buddhism and Jainism, both originated in India, have their own unique literature in different languages such as Pali, Prakrit and Sanskrit. Let's dive deeper into the literature of these two religions.

#### Buddhist Literature
- Buddhist literature is mainly divided into two categories - canonical and non-canonical.
- Canonical literature is known as Tripitaka or Three Baskets which includes Vinaya Pitaka, Sutta Pitaka and Abhidhamma Pitaka.
- Vinaya Pitaka contains rules and regulations for Buddhist monks and nuns, Sutta Pitaka contains Buddha's teachings and Abhidhamma Pitaka contains philosophical and psychological analysis of Buddha's teachings.
- Non-canonical literature is called commentaries, texts on monastic discipline, and other works.
- Pali is the primary language of Buddhist literature, and most of the canonical literature is written in Pali.

#### Jain Literature
- Jain literature is also divided into two main categories - Agamas and non-Agamas.
- Agamas are the primary texts and contain the teachings of the Tirthankaras, the spiritual leaders of Jainism.
- Non-Agamas include texts on Jain philosophy, grammar, mathematics, and science.
- Jain literature is mostly written in Prakrit, a language used in ancient India.

#### Sanskrit Literature
- Both Buddhism and Jainism have contributed significantly to Sanskrit literature.
- Buddhist Sanskrit literature includes texts on philosophy, logic, and meditation.
- Jain Sanskrit literature includes texts on grammar, philosophy, and ethics.
- Sanskrit was considered a prestigious language for writing in ancient India, and both religions used it to communicate with a wider audience.

In conclusion, the literature of Buddhism and Jainism in Pali, Prakrit, and Sanskrit provides valuable insights into the teachings, philosophy, and practices of these two religions. Understanding this literature can help us gain a deeper appreciation of Indian culture, tradition, and practices.
