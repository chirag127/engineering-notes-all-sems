### Harappan Script

The Harappan script is one of the oldest writing systems in the world, used by the Harappan civilization in the Indus Valley around 2600-1900 BCE. Here are some important things to know about the Harappan script:

- The Harappan script is also known as the Indus script and remains largely undeciphered. Scholars have yet to agree on the language it represents, though some believe it may have been a Dravidian language.
- The script is made up of around 400 signs, many of which are pictographic, representing objects like animals or tools. Others are abstract symbols, possibly representing concepts or sounds.
- The script was written from right to left in a series of rows, with some signs facing left and others right. It was likely written on perishable materials like wood or cloth, which is why few examples of the script have survived.
- The Harappan civilization was highly advanced, with a complex system of writing, trade, and urban planning. The script was used on seals, which were likely used to mark goods or documents, but little else is known about its usage or purpose.
- Despite its mystery, the Harappan script is an important part of India's cultural heritage and a reminder of the rich history and diversity of the subcontinent. Its legacy can be seen in the modern-day Indian scripts that evolved from it, like Brahmi and Devanagari.

While the Harappan script remains largely undeciphered, its legacy continues to fascinate scholars and enthusiasts alike. By studying the script and the civilization that created it, we can gain a better understanding of India's rich cultural heritage and the complex history of the subcontinent.
