### Challenges faced by Women

Women in India have been facing various challenges for centuries. Despite the progress made towards gender equality, women still face discrimination, harassment, and violence in various forms. Some of the challenges faced by women in India are:

1. Gender Inequality: Women in India are often discriminated against based on their gender. They are paid less than men for the same work, and their education and employment opportunities are limited.

2. Violence against Women: Violence against women is a major problem in India. Women face various forms of violence, such as domestic violence, sexual harassment, rape, and acid attacks. The lack of justice for victims of violence is another challenge.

3. Child Marriage: Child marriage is a common practice in some parts of India, where girls as young as 10 years old are married off to older men. This practice deprives girls of their childhood and education, and also puts them at risk of domestic violence and sexual abuse.

4. Female Infanticide and Foeticide: The preference for male children in Indian society has led to the practice of female infanticide and foeticide. This practice has resulted in a skewed sex ratio in the country, and has deprived many girls of their right to life.

5. Lack of Access to Healthcare: Women in India often face a lack of access to healthcare, especially in rural areas. This leads to higher maternal mortality rates and poor health outcomes for women.

6. Limited Political Representation: Women in India are underrepresented in politics. Despite the reservation of seats for women in local government bodies, women are still underrepresented in state and national politics.

7. Limited Access to Property Rights: Women in India often face a lack of access to property rights. This makes it difficult for them to own and inherit property, and also puts them at risk of being dispossessed of their property.

These challenges faced by women in India are deeply rooted in the cultural and societal norms of the country. Addressing these challenges requires a multi-pronged approach that involves legal reforms, social awareness, and cultural change.
