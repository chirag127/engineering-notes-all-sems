### Six Systems of Indian Philosophy

Indian philosophy is a vast and diverse subject that has been studied for centuries. It is believed that there are six classical systems of Indian philosophy, also known as 'Shad-darsanas.' These six systems are considered to be the foundational systems of Indian philosophy, and they offer different perspectives on the nature of reality, the self, and the ultimate goal of life. 

The six systems of Indian philosophy are:

1. Samkhya
2. Yoga
3. Nyaya
4. Vaisheshika
5. Mimamsa
6. Vedanta

#### 1. Samkhya

The Samkhya system of Indian philosophy is one of the oldest systems and is attributed to the sage Kapila. According to this system, there are two fundamental elements in the universe - Purusha (consciousness) and Prakriti (nature). The ultimate goal of life, according to Samkhya, is to attain liberation by realizing the difference between Purusha and Prakriti.

#### 2. Yoga

The Yoga system of Indian philosophy is attributed to the sage Patanjali. The goal of Yoga is to achieve a state of union with the ultimate reality, which is called Samadhi. The system emphasizes the practice of meditation, self-discipline, and ethical behavior, among other things.

#### 3. Nyaya

The Nyaya system of Indian philosophy is concerned with logic and epistemology. The system aims to establish a methodology for gaining knowledge and understanding the nature of reality. Nyaya emphasizes the use of reason and logical arguments to arrive at the truth.

#### 4. Vaisheshika

The Vaisheshika system of Indian philosophy is concerned with ontology and the nature of the universe. The system posits that there are nine fundamental substances in the universe, and all other things are combinations or modifications of these substances. The ultimate goal of life, according to Vaisheshika, is to attain liberation by understanding the nature of the universe.

#### 5. Mimamsa

The Mimamsa system of Indian philosophy is concerned with the interpretation of the Vedas, which are the oldest sacred texts of Hinduism. The system aims to establish the correct interpretation of the Vedas and the proper performance of Vedic rituals. Mimamsa emphasizes the importance of karma and the performance of one's duties.

#### 6. Vedanta

The Vedanta system of Indian philosophy is the most prominent and widely studied system of Indian philosophy. It is based on the Vedas and is attributed to the sage Vyasa. The system posits that there is only one ultimate reality, known as Brahman, and that all other things are manifestations of Brahman. The ultimate goal of life, according to Vedanta, is to attain liberation by realizing one's identity with Brahman.

In conclusion, the six systems of Indian philosophy offer different perspectives on the nature of reality, the self, and the ultimate goal of life. Each system has its own unique insights and methods for achieving liberation. Studying these systems can provide a deeper understanding of Indian philosophy and its impact on Indian culture and society.
