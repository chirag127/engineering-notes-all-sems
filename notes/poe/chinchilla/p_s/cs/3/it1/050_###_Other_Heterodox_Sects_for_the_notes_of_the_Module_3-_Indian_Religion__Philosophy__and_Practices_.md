### Other Heterodox Sects

Apart from Buddhism and Jainism, there were other heterodox sects in India. Let's take a closer look at them:

1. Lokayata or Carvaka Philosophy
   - Also known as the materialist philosophy, it rejected the existence of God, the soul, and the afterlife.
   - It believed that everything in the universe is made up of four elements - earth, water, fire, and air.
   - According to this philosophy, pleasure is the ultimate goal of life, and one should strive to maximize it.
   - They believed that knowledge could only be gained through direct perception and not through inference or testimony.
   - The Carvaka philosophy was criticized by other schools of thought for its extreme materialism and hedonism.

2. Ajivikas
   - Founded by Makkhali Gosala, the Ajivikas believed in the concept of fate and predestination.
   - According to this philosophy, all living beings are bound by their karma, and their destiny is predetermined.
   - The Ajivikas believed that the universe is eternal and cyclical and goes through cycles of creation and destruction.
   - They also believed in the concept of austerities and self-mortification to attain liberation from the cycle of birth and death.
   - The Ajivikas were criticized for their fatalism and extreme asceticism.

3. Samkhya Philosophy
   - The Samkhya philosophy is one of the oldest schools of Indian philosophy and believed in the duality of nature.
   - According to this philosophy, the universe is composed of two fundamental principles - purusha (consciousness) and prakriti (matter).
   - The Samkhya philosophy believed in the concept of reincarnation and liberation, and the ultimate goal of life was to attain liberation from the cycle of birth and death.
   - The Samkhya philosophy was criticized for its dualism and lack of a supreme being.

4. Yoga Philosophy
   - The Yoga philosophy is one of the six schools of Indian philosophy and believed in the concept of the union of the individual soul with the universal soul.
   - According to this philosophy, the ultimate goal of life was to attain self-realization and liberation from the cycle of birth and death.
   - The Yoga philosophy believed in the practice of meditation, breathing exercises, and physical postures to achieve self-realization.
   - The Yoga philosophy was criticized for its emphasis on individualism and lack of a supreme being.

In conclusion, these heterodox sects played an important role in shaping Indian philosophy and religion. While they were criticized for their extreme views and lack of a supreme being, they contributed to the diversity and richness of Indian thought.
