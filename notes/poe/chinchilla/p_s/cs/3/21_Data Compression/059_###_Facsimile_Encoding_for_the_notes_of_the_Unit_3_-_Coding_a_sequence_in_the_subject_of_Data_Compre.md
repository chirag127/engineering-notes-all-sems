### Facsimile Encoding for the notes of the Unit 3 - Coding a sequence in the subject of Data Compression

Facsimile encoding is a type of data compression technique that is used to transmit images over telecommunication channels. It is also known as fax encoding because it was originally used for transmitting facsimile messages. The facsimile encoding technique is based on run-length encoding, which is a lossless data compression technique that reduces the size of data by identifying and encoding sequences of repeated symbols or values.

Facsimile encoding works by scanning an image and converting it into a binary format. The binary data is then compressed using run-length encoding to create a compressed image that can be transmitted over telecommunication channels. The compressed image is then decompressed at the receiving end to recreate the original image.

Advantages of Facsimile Encoding:
- It is a lossless data compression technique that preserves the original quality of the image.
- It is simple and easy to implement.
- It is widely used for transmitting documents and images over telecommunication channels.

Disadvantages of Facsimile Encoding:
- It is not suitable for compressing complex images or photographs.
- It can result in large file sizes for high-resolution images.
- It is not as efficient as other compression techniques such as JPEG or PNG.

Example of Facsimile Encoding:
Consider an image of a barcode that contains a sequence of black and white bars. The facsimile encoding technique can be used to compress the image by identifying the sequences of repeated bars and encoding them as a single value. For example, a sequence of 10 black bars can be encoded as "10B" instead of "BBBBBBBBBB". This reduces the size of the data and makes it easier to transmit over telecommunication channels.

Applications of Facsimile Encoding:
- It is commonly used in fax machines for transmitting documents and images.
- It is used in the telecommunication industry for transmitting images over telephone lines.
- It is used in the medical industry for transmitting medical images such as X-rays and MRI scans.
