### Inverse Filtering for the notes of the Unit 3 - IMAGE RESTORATION in the subject of Image Processing

In image processing, image restoration refers to the process of restoring a degraded image to its original state. One of the methods used for image restoration is inverse filtering.

Inverse filtering is a technique that involves the estimation of the original image from the degraded image by using the inverse of the degradation function. It is a frequency domain technique that works by restoring the high-frequency components of the image that were lost during the degradation process.

#### Steps involved in Inverse Filtering

The following are the steps involved in inverse filtering:

1. Convert the image and the point spread function to the frequency domain using the Fourier Transform.
2. Divide the Fourier transform of the degraded image by the Fourier transform of the point spread function.
3. Apply the inverse Fourier Transform to obtain the restored image.

#### Advantages of Inverse Filtering

The following are the advantages of inverse filtering:

1. It is a simple and effective method for image restoration.
2. It can restore images that have been degraded by a linear and shift-invariant system.

#### Disadvantages of Inverse Filtering

The following are the disadvantages of inverse filtering:

1. It is sensitive to noise and can amplify the noise in the image.
2. It requires the knowledge of the point spread function, which is not always easy to obtain.

#### Applications of Inverse Filtering

The following are the applications of inverse filtering:

1. It is used in medical image processing to restore degraded images of X-rays, MRI scans, etc.
2. It is used in the restoration of old and damaged photographs.
3. It is used in the restoration of satellite images and other remote sensing images.

#### Conclusion

Inverse filtering is a technique that is widely used in image restoration. It is a simple and effective method that can restore images that have been degraded by a linear and shift-invariant system. However, it is sensitive to noise and requires the knowledge of the point spread function, which is not always easy to obtain.
