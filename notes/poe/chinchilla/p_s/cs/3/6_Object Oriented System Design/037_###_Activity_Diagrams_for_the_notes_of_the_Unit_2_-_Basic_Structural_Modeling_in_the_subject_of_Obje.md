### Activity Diagrams for the notes of the Unit 2 - Basic Structural Modeling in the subject of Object Oriented System Design

Activity diagrams are a type of behavioral modeling diagram that represents the flow of activities in a system. They are used to model business processes or workflows and are commonly used in software development to illustrate the steps in a use case or scenario. In this section, we will discuss the basics of activity diagrams, their symbols, and how they can be used in system design.

#### Symbols in Activity Diagrams

Activity diagrams use a variety of symbols to represent different elements of a system. Some of the commonly used symbols are:

- Rounded rectangles: Represent activities or tasks
- Diamonds: Represent decision points or branching paths
- Arrows: Represent the flow of activities or transitions between tasks
- Swimlanes: Represent the roles or responsibilities of different actors in the system

#### Advantages of Activity Diagrams

Activity diagrams have several advantages in system design, including:

- Easy to understand: Activity diagrams are simple to read and understand, even for non-technical stakeholders.
- Visual representation: They provide a visual representation of a system's workflow or business process, making it easier to identify potential bottlenecks or inefficiencies.
- Clarity: Activity diagrams can help clarify complex systems by breaking them down into smaller, more manageable parts.
- Communication: They can serve as a communication tool between developers, stakeholders, and end-users.

#### Disadvantages of Activity Diagrams

While activity diagrams have many advantages, they also have some disadvantages, including:

- Limited scope: Activity diagrams are limited in scope and are not suitable for modeling complex systems.
- Ambiguity: They can be ambiguous in some cases, making it difficult to understand the flow of activities.
- Time-consuming: Creating activity diagrams can be time-consuming, especially for large systems.

#### Examples of Activity Diagrams

Here are some examples of activity diagrams in action:

- A use case scenario for an online shopping cart
- A workflow for a customer service call center
- A business process for employee onboarding

#### Applications of Activity Diagrams

Activity diagrams can be used in various applications, including:

- Business process modeling
- Software development
- Project management
- Quality assurance

In conclusion, activity diagrams are an essential tool in system design and modeling. They provide a visual representation of a system's workflow or business process, making it easier to understand and identify potential bottlenecks or inefficiencies. When used correctly, activity diagrams can help improve communication, streamline workflows, and ensure project success.
