### Temporal Parameters of Real Time Workload

Real-time systems are designed to respond to events in a timely manner. The performance of these systems is affected by various factors such as processing time, response time, and deadline. These factors are referred to as temporal parameters of real-time workload. In this section, we will discuss the various temporal parameters that affect the workload of real-time systems.

#### Processing Time

Processing time refers to the amount of time required to complete a task. In real-time systems, processing time is crucial as it directly affects the response time of the system. The shorter the processing time, the faster the response time. The processing time can be divided into two types: deterministic and non-deterministic processing time.

Deterministic processing time is the processing time that is known beforehand. For example, in a system that processes a fixed number of operations, the processing time for each operation can be calculated beforehand. On the other hand, non-deterministic processing time is the processing time that cannot be predicted beforehand. For example, in a system that processes user input, the processing time for each input cannot be predicted beforehand.

#### Response Time

Response time refers to the time required for a system to respond to an event. In real-time systems, response time is critical as it determines the system's ability to meet deadlines. The shorter the response time, the better the system's performance. The response time can be divided into two types: maximum response time and average response time.

Maximum response time is the maximum amount of time that a system takes to respond to an event. This is important as it determines the worst-case scenario for the system. Average response time, on the other hand, is the average amount of time that a system takes to respond to an event. This is important as it determines the overall performance of the system.

#### Deadline

Deadline refers to the time by which a task must be completed. In real-time systems, meeting deadlines is critical as it determines the system's ability to perform its tasks on time. The deadline can be divided into two types: hard deadline and soft deadline.

A hard deadline is a deadline that must be met under all circumstances. Failure to meet a hard deadline can result in catastrophic consequences. For example, in a medical monitoring system, failure to meet a hard deadline can result in loss of life. A soft deadline, on the other hand, is a deadline that can be missed occasionally without catastrophic consequences. For example, in a stock trading system, missing a soft deadline may result in a loss of profit, but it will not result in loss of life.

#### Advantages of Real-time Systems

Real-time systems have several advantages such as:

- They are designed to respond to events in a timely manner.
- They can be used in critical applications such as medical monitoring systems and nuclear power plants.
- They can improve the overall efficiency of a system.

#### Disadvantages of Real-time Systems

Real-time systems also have several disadvantages such as:

- They are more complex to design and implement.
- They require more processing power and memory.
- They are more expensive to develop and maintain.

#### Examples of Real-time Systems

Real-time systems are used in various applications such as:

- Medical monitoring systems
- Nuclear power plants
- Flight control systems
- Stock trading systems
- Automotive control systems

In conclusion, real-time systems are designed to respond to events in a timely manner. The temporal parameters such as processing time, response time, and deadline are critical in determining the performance of these systems. Real-time systems have several advantages and disadvantages, and are used in various applications such as medical monitoring systems and automotive control systems.
