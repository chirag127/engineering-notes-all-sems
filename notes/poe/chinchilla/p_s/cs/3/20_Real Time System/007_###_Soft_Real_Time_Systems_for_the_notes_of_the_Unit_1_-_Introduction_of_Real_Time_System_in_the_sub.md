### Soft Real Time Systems

Soft Real Time Systems are those systems in which the correctness of the system is affected by the timeliness of the response, but the response can still be useful even if it is delayed. These systems are characterized by having deadlines that are not strict and can be missed occasionally without causing significant harm.

#### Characteristics of Soft Real Time Systems

- Soft real-time systems have lower priority than hard real-time systems.
- They have a certain degree of fault tolerance and can tolerate occasional missed deadlines.
- Soft real-time systems have a lower cost than hard real-time systems since they do not have to meet strict timing constraints.
- They are used in applications where the response time is important but not critical.

#### Advantages of Soft Real Time Systems

- Soft real-time systems are less expensive than hard real-time systems because they do not have to meet strict timing requirements.
- They are more flexible because they can tolerate occasional missed deadlines without causing any significant harm.
- Soft real-time systems have a greater range of applications than hard real-time systems since they are not limited to applications that require strict timing constraints.

#### Disadvantages of Soft Real Time Systems

- Soft real-time systems cannot guarantee that a response will always be timely, which can be a problem in certain applications.
- They are not suitable for applications that require strict timing constraints, such as those in the aerospace and defense industries.
- Soft real-time systems may not be suitable for applications where the consequences of a missed deadline are severe.

#### Examples of Soft Real Time Systems

- Multimedia streaming applications, such as video conferencing and online gaming, are examples of soft real-time systems.
- Interactive applications, such as web browsing and email, are also soft real-time systems.
- Industrial control systems, such as those used in manufacturing and process control, can also be classified as soft real-time systems.

In conclusion, soft real-time systems are those systems in which the correctness of the system is affected by the timeliness of the response, but the response can still be useful even if it is delayed. They are characterized by having deadlines that are not strict and can be missed occasionally without causing significant harm. They are less expensive and more flexible than hard real-time systems but are not suitable for applications that require strict timing constraints. Examples of soft real-time systems include multimedia streaming applications, interactive applications, and industrial control systems.
