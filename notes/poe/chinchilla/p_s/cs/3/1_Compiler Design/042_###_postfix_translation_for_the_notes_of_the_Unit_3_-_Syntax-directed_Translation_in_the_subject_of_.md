Postfix translation is a type of syntax-directed translation that converts an infix expression to a postfix expression. It is also known as Reverse Polish Notation (RPN) and is widely used in many computer programs, calculators, and compilers.

The postfix notation is a way of writing expressions in which the operator is placed after the operands. For example, in the expression 3 + 4, the operator + is placed between the operands 3 and 4. In postfix notation, the expression will be written as 3 4 +. The advantage of postfix notation is that it eliminates the need for parentheses and operator precedence rules, making it easier to evaluate expressions.
