### Various Philosophical Doctrines in India

India has a rich history of philosophy, with various doctrines developed and propagated by great thinkers over the centuries. Here are some of the major philosophical doctrines in India:

1. **Samkhya philosophy**: Samkhya is one of the oldest philosophical systems in India. It states that the universe is composed of two fundamental categories - Purusha (consciousness) and Prakriti (matter). The goal of life is to attain liberation by separating the Purusha from Prakriti through discrimination.

2. **Yoga philosophy**: Yoga is a spiritual discipline that aims to unite the individual self with the universal Self. According to yoga philosophy, the mind is the root cause of suffering, and it can be controlled through meditation and other yogic practices. The ultimate goal is to attain Moksha or liberation.

3. **Nyaya philosophy**: Nyaya is a school of logic that aims to establish the validity of knowledge. It states that there are four sources of knowledge - perception, inference, testimony, and comparison. The goal of Nyaya is to provide a systematic method for arriving at the truth.

4. **Vaisheshika philosophy**: Vaisheshika is a system of atomism that was developed around the same time as Nyaya. It holds that the universe is composed of atoms and their qualities. The goal of Vaisheshika is to understand the nature of reality through a systematic analysis of the physical world.

5. **Mimamsa philosophy**: Mimamsa is a school of Vedic interpretation that focuses on ritual and ethical conduct. It holds that the Vedas are the ultimate authority on dharma (righteousness), and the goal of life is to perform one's duties in accordance with the Vedas.

6. **Vedanta philosophy**: Vedanta is a school of thought that focuses on the Upanishads, the final section of the Vedas. It holds that the ultimate reality is Brahman, a non-dual, infinite, and eternal consciousness. The goal of Vedanta is to realize one's true nature as identical to Brahman.

Overall, these philosophical doctrines have had a profound impact on Indian culture and society, shaping everything from religious practices to ethical principles. Studying these doctrines can provide a deeper understanding of Indian philosophy and its contributions to the world.
