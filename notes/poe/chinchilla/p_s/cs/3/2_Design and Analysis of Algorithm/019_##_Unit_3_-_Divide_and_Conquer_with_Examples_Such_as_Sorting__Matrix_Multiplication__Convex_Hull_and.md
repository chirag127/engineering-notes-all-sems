### Divide and Conquer
Divide and conquer is a problem-solving technique that involves breaking down a problem into smaller sub-problems that are easier to solve. The sub-problems are solved independently, and their solutions are then combined to solve the original problem. The key steps involved in the divide and conquer approach are:

- Divide the problem into smaller sub-problems
- Conquer the sub-problems by solving them recursively
- Combine the solutions of the sub-problems to solve the original problem

Examples of divide and conquer algorithms include:

- Sorting algorithms such as Merge Sort and Quick Sort
- Matrix multiplication algorithms such as Strassen’s algorithm
- Convex hull algorithms such as Graham’s scan

### Greedy Methods
Greedy methods are problem-solving techniques that involve making locally optimal choices at each step in the hope of finding a global optimum solution. Greedy algorithms are often used when the problem can be broken down into a set of smaller sub-problems that can be solved independently. The key steps involved in the greedy approach are:

- Define a set of candidate solutions
- Evaluate each candidate solution based on some objective function
- Select the locally optimal solution at each step
- Repeat until the global optimum solution is found

Examples of greedy algorithms include:

- Optimal reliability allocation
- Knapsack problem
- Minimum spanning trees – Prim’s and Kruskal’s algorithms
- Single source shortest paths – Dijkstra’s and Bellman-Ford algorithms
