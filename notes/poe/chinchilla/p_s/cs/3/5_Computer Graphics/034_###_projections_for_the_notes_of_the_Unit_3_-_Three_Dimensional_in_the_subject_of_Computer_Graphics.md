### Projections

In computer graphics, projections are a way to represent 3D objects onto a 2D plane. It is important to understand projections because they are used in many applications, including video game development, architecture, and product design.

There are two main types of projections: parallel and perspective. Let's take a closer look at each:

#### Parallel Projections

Parallel projections maintain the same scale and proportions of the 3D object in the 2D plane. In other words, parallel lines in the 3D object will remain parallel in the 2D projection.

There are two types of parallel projections:

- Orthographic Projection: This type of projection is commonly used in architecture and engineering. It shows the object as if it were viewed from an infinite distance, with all the lines of sight parallel to each other.

- Isometric Projection: This type of projection is used in video game development and product design. It shows the object at a 45-degree angle, with all three axes equally foreshortened.

#### Perspective Projections

Perspective projections are used to create a more realistic representation of the 3D object. The scale and proportions of the object are not maintained in the 2D projection, as objects that are further away appear smaller.

There are two types of perspective projections:

- One-point Perspective: This type of projection is used to show objects as if they were viewed from a single point in space. It is commonly used in art and illustrations.

- Two-point Perspective: This type of projection is used to show objects as if they were viewed from two points in space. It is commonly used in architecture and product design.

Projections are an important concept in computer graphics, and understanding the different types can help you create more accurate and realistic 2D representations of 3D objects.
