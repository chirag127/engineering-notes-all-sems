### Material Requirement Processing

Material Requirement Processing (MRP) is a computer-based inventory management system that assists organizations in planning and controlling inventory levels. The system generates production schedules and purchase orders based on the demand forecast and inventory levels.

The MRP system helps businesses to manage and maintain optimal inventory levels and reduce the cost of holding inventory. The system also helps in improving customer service by ensuring that the products are available when needed.

#### How MRP works

1. Bill of Materials (BOM): A bill of materials is a list of all the raw materials, sub-assemblies, and parts required to manufacture a product. The BOM is created by the engineering department and is used in the MRP system.

2. Inventory Records: The MRP system maintains inventory records for every item in the BOM. The inventory records include the current inventory level, the lead time for ordering, and the safety stock level.

3. Master Production Schedule (MPS): The MPS is a plan that specifies the quantity and timing of the products to be manufactured. The MPS is created by the production department and is used in the MRP system.

4. MRP Calculation: The MRP system uses the BOM, inventory records, and MPS to calculate the quantity and timing of the materials to be ordered. The system generates purchase orders for the materials and production schedules for the products.

#### Advantages of MRP

1. Improved Inventory Management: MRP helps in maintaining optimal inventory levels, reducing the cost of holding inventory, and improving customer service.

2. Efficient Production Planning: MRP helps in generating production schedules based on the demand forecast and inventory levels.

3. Cost Reduction: MRP helps in reducing the cost of holding inventory, ordering materials, and managing production.

#### Disadvantages of MRP

1. Complexity: MRP is a complex system that requires a significant amount of time and resources to implement and maintain.

2. Dependence on Data Accuracy: MRP requires accurate data on inventory levels, lead times, and demand forecasts. Any errors in data can lead to inefficiencies and stockouts.

#### Example

Suppose a company manufactures bicycles. The BOM for a bicycle includes raw materials such as steel, rubber, and plastic, as well as sub-assemblies such as wheels and brakes. The MPS specifies the quantity and timing of the bicycles to be manufactured. The MRP system uses the BOM, inventory records, and MPS to calculate the quantity and timing of the materials to be ordered. The system generates purchase orders for the materials and production schedules for the bicycles.

#### Applications

MRP is widely used in manufacturing industries such as automotive, aerospace, and electronics to manage inventory levels and production schedules. The system can also be used in service industries such as healthcare and hospitality to manage the inventory of supplies and equipment.
