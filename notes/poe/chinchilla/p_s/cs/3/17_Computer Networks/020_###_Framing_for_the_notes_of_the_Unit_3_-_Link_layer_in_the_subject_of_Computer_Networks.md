### Framing for the notes of the Unit 3 - Link layer in the subject of Computer Networks

The link layer is the second layer of the OSI model that is responsible for transmitting data between adjacent network nodes. In this unit, we will focus on the framing process that takes place at the link layer. Framing is the process of adding a header and trailer to the data at the sender's end and removing them at the receiver's end.

In this unit, we will cover the following topics related to framing:

1. Introduction to Framing: 
   - Definition of Framing
   - Purpose of Framing
   - Importance of Framing in Networking

2. Types of Framing: 
   - Character-Oriented Framing
   - Bit-Oriented Framing
   - Byte-Oriented Framing

3. Character-Oriented Framing: 
   - Definition of Character-Oriented Framing
   - ASCII and EBCDIC Character Sets
   - Methods of Character-Oriented Framing
   - Advantages and Disadvantages of Character-Oriented Framing

4. Bit-Oriented Framing: 
   - Definition of Bit-Oriented Framing
   - Methods of Bit-Oriented Framing
   - Advantages and Disadvantages of Bit-Oriented Framing

5. Byte-Oriented Framing: 
   - Definition of Byte-Oriented Framing
   - Methods of Byte-Oriented Framing
   - Advantages and Disadvantages of Byte-Oriented Framing

6. Frame Synchronization: 
   - Definition of Frame Synchronization
   - Bit Stuffing Technique
   - Byte Stuffing Technique
   - Comparison between Bit Stuffing and Byte Stuffing Techniques

7. Error Control: 
   - Types of Errors in Data Transmission
   - Error Detection Techniques 
   - Error Correction Techniques

8. Flow Control: 
   - Definition of Flow Control
   - Methods of Flow Control
   - Advantages and Disadvantages of Flow Control

In conclusion, framing is a critical process in data transmission at the link layer. Understanding the different types of framing, frame synchronization techniques, error control, and flow control methods is essential for building efficient and reliable communication networks.
