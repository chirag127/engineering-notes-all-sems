### Signal Transmission and Encoding

The physical layer of computer networks deals with the physical transmission of data over a communication channel. In this unit, we will study the signal transmission and encoding techniques used to transmit data over a communication channel.

#### Signal Transmission

Signal transmission refers to the process of transmitting data from one point to another through a communication channel. The communication channel can be wired or wireless. The physical layer is responsible for the transmission of bits over the channel. The three main aspects of signal transmission are:

1. Data Rate: Data rate refers to the number of bits transmitted per second. It is also called the transmission rate or bit rate. Data rate is measured in bits per second (bps).

2. Bandwidth: Bandwidth refers to the range of frequencies that can be transmitted over the channel. It is measured in Hertz (Hz). The higher the bandwidth, the higher the data rate that can be achieved.

3. Transmission Medium: The transmission medium refers to the physical medium used to transmit data. It can be a wired medium such as a copper cable or a wireless medium such as radio waves.

#### Encoding Techniques

Encoding techniques are used to convert digital data into analog signals that can be transmitted over the channel. The two main encoding techniques are:

1. Digital-to-Analog Conversion (DAC): In DAC, digital data is converted into analog signals. The two main DAC techniques are amplitude shift keying (ASK), frequency shift keying (FSK), and phase shift keying (PSK).

2. Analog-to-Digital Conversion (ADC): In ADC, analog signals are converted into digital data. The two main ADC techniques are pulse code modulation (PCM) and delta modulation (DM).

#### Advantages and Disadvantages

The advantages and disadvantages of different encoding techniques are:

1. Amplitude Shift Keying (ASK)
    - Advantages: Simple and easy to implement.
    - Disadvantages: Limited data rate and susceptible to noise.

2. Frequency Shift Keying (FSK)
    - Advantages: Higher data rate than ASK.
    - Disadvantages: Requires a wider bandwidth and susceptible to noise.

3. Phase Shift Keying (PSK)
    - Advantages: Higher data rate and less susceptible to noise than ASK and FSK.
    - Disadvantages: Complex and requires a high signal-to-noise ratio.

4. Pulse Code Modulation (PCM)
    - Advantages: High-quality signal and less susceptible to noise.
    - Disadvantages: Requires a high data rate and complex.

5. Delta Modulation (DM)
    - Advantages: Simple and requires a low data rate.
    - Disadvantages: Susceptible to noise and produces lower quality signals.

#### Applications

The different encoding techniques have different applications. Some common applications are:

1. Amplitude Shift Keying (ASK) is used in radio frequency identification (RFID) systems.

2. Frequency Shift Keying (FSK) is used in wireless communication systems such as cordless phones and wireless LANs.

3. Phase Shift Keying (PSK) is used in digital subscriber line (DSL) systems.

4. Pulse Code Modulation (PCM) is used in digital audio and video systems.

5. Delta Modulation (DM) is used in low data rate applications such as voice transmission over the internet.

In conclusion, signal transmission and encoding play a crucial role in the physical layer of computer networks. The selection of an appropriate encoding technique depends on the specific requirements of the application. Understanding these techniques is essential for designing efficient and reliable communication systems.
