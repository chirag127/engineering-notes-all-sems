### Transmission Media

Transmission media refers to the physical means through which data travels from one device to another in a computer network. The physical layer of the network model is responsible for transmitting data over the transmission media.

There are two types of transmission media:

1. Guided Transmission Media
2. Unguided Transmission Media

#### 1. Guided Transmission Media

Guided transmission media, also known as wired media, is a type of transmission media that uses a physical path to transmit data from one device to another. The guided transmission media can be further classified into the following categories:

##### a. Twisted Pair Cable

A twisted pair cable is a type of guided transmission media that consists of two insulated copper wires twisted around each other. This type of cable is commonly used in telephone networks and Ethernet networks. Twisted pair cables are of two types:

- Unshielded Twisted Pair (UTP)
- Shielded Twisted Pair (STP)

UTP cables are widely used in computer networks, whereas STP cables are used in industrial environments where electromagnetic interference is a concern.

##### b. Coaxial Cable

A coaxial cable is a type of guided transmission media that consists of a central conductor, an insulating layer, a metallic shield, and an outer cover. Coaxial cables are commonly used in cable television networks and Ethernet networks.

##### c. Fiber Optic Cable

A fiber optic cable is a type of guided transmission media that uses light to transmit data. It consists of a core made of glass or plastic fibers, an insulating layer, and an outer cover. Fiber optic cables are widely used in long-distance networks due to their high bandwidth and low signal attenuation.

#### 2. Unguided Transmission Media

Unguided transmission media, also known as wireless media, is a type of transmission media that uses air or space as a medium to transmit data from one device to another. The unguided transmission media can be further classified into the following categories:

##### a. Radio Waves

Radio waves are a type of unguided transmission media that are commonly used in wireless networks. They are used to transmit data over short distances, such as in Bluetooth and Wi-Fi networks.

##### b. Microwaves

Microwaves are a type of unguided transmission media that are used to transmit data over long distances, such as in satellite communication and cellular networks.

##### c. Infrared Waves

Infrared waves are a type of unguided transmission media that are used to transmit data over short distances, such as in remote controls and infrared communication devices.

Transmission media has its own advantages and disadvantages. Guided media is more reliable and provides a higher quality signal, but it is more expensive and difficult to install. Unguided media is more flexible and easier to install, but it is more susceptible to interference and signal degradation.

In conclusion, understanding the different types of transmission media is essential in designing and implementing computer networks. Knowing the advantages and disadvantages of each type of media can help network engineers choose the appropriate transmission media for their network requirements.
