### Switching techniques and multiplexing for the notes of the Unit 2 - Physical Layer in the subject of Computer Networks

Switching and multiplexing techniques are essential for efficient and effective data communication in computer networks. In this topic, we will discuss the different types of switching and multiplexing techniques used in computer networks.

#### Switching Techniques

Switching is the process of moving data from one point to another in a network. There are three types of switching techniques used in computer networks:

1. Circuit Switching: In circuit switching, a dedicated communication path is established between the sender and receiver. The path remains active for the entire duration of the communication. The circuit switching technique is commonly used in telephone networks.

2. Message Switching: In message switching, the message is divided into packets and each packet is sent individually to the destination. Each packet may take a different path to reach the destination. Message switching is commonly used in email and file transfer applications.

3. Packet Switching: In packet switching, the message is divided into small packets and each packet is sent individually to the destination. Each packet may take a different path to reach the destination. Packet switching is commonly used in internet networks.

#### Multiplexing Techniques

Multiplexing is the process of combining multiple data streams into a single data stream for transmission over a single communication channel. There are three types of multiplexing techniques used in computer networks:

1. Frequency Division Multiplexing (FDM): In FDM, different data streams are assigned different frequencies and then combined for transmission over a single communication channel. FDM is commonly used in analog systems such as radio and television broadcasting.

2. Time Division Multiplexing (TDM): In TDM, different data streams are assigned different time slots and then combined for transmission over a single communication channel. TDM is commonly used in digital systems such as telephone networks.

3. Statistical Multiplexing: In statistical multiplexing, data streams are combined based on their statistical characteristics. Statistical multiplexing allows for more efficient use of bandwidth by dynamically allocating bandwidth to different data streams based on their needs.

#### Advantages and Disadvantages

Switching and multiplexing techniques have their advantages and disadvantages. Some of the advantages of switching and multiplexing techniques include:

- Efficient use of communication channels
- Increased data transmission speeds
- Increased network reliability
- Better scalability

However, some of the disadvantages of switching and multiplexing techniques include:

- Increased complexity
- Increased cost
- Increased latency

#### Example Applications

Switching and multiplexing techniques are used in a variety of applications such as:

- Telephone networks
- Internet networks
- Cable TV networks
- Satellite communication systems
- Wireless communication systems

In conclusion, switching and multiplexing techniques play a crucial role in ensuring efficient and effective data communication in computer networks. Understanding these techniques is essential for network designers and administrators to create and maintain robust and reliable networks.
