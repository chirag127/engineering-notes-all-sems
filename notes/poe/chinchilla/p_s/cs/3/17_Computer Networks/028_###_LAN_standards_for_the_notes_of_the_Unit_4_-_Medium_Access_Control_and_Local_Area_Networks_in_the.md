### LAN standards for the notes of the Unit 4 - Medium Access Control and Local Area Networks in the subject of Computer Networks

Local Area Networks (LAN) provide a way for computers and other network devices to communicate with each other within a small geographic area such as a building, a campus, or a group of nearby buildings. To ensure interoperability and compatibility among different network devices, various LAN standards have been developed over the years. In this unit, we will study some of the most widely used LAN standards and their characteristics.

Here are the LAN standards that we will cover in this unit:

1. Ethernet
2. Token Ring
3. FDDI
4. Wi-Fi

#### Ethernet

Ethernet is the most widely used LAN standard in the world. It was developed by Xerox Corporation in the 1970s and later standardized by the Institute of Electrical and Electronics Engineers (IEEE). Ethernet defines the physical and data link layer specifications for wired Ethernet networks. It uses a CSMA/CD (Carrier Sense Multiple Access with Collision Detection) protocol to control access to the network channel.

Advantages:
- Widely used and supported standard
- High speed and low cost
- Easy to install and configure

Disadvantages:
- Susceptible to collisions and other network congestion issues
- Limited distance coverage without repeaters or switches

#### Token Ring

Token Ring is another LAN standard that was popular in the 1980s and 1990s. It was developed by IBM and uses a token passing mechanism to control access to the network channel. In Token Ring, each device is connected in a logical ring topology, and a token is passed around the ring to determine which device can transmit data.

Advantages:
- Fair access to the network channel
- No collisions or other congestion issues
- Better performance than Ethernet under heavy load

Disadvantages:
- Less widely used and supported than Ethernet
- Higher cost and complexity
- Limited speed and distance coverage

#### FDDI

Fiber Distributed Data Interface (FDDI) is a LAN standard that uses fiber optic cables for high-speed data transmission. It was developed in the 1980s and standardized by the American National Standards Institute (ANSI). FDDI uses a token passing mechanism similar to Token Ring to control access to the network channel.

Advantages:
- High speed and reliable data transmission
- Long distance coverage
- Immune to electromagnetic interference

Disadvantages:
- Higher cost and complexity than Ethernet
- Limited adoption and support
- Requires specialized hardware and cabling

#### Wi-Fi

Wi-Fi is a wireless LAN standard that uses radio waves to transmit data between devices. It was developed by the Wi-Fi Alliance and is based on the IEEE 802.11 standard. Wi-Fi is widely used in homes, offices, and public spaces to provide wireless internet access.

Advantages:
- Wireless and mobile connectivity
- Easy to install and use
- Good speed and coverage for small areas

Disadvantages:
- Susceptible to interference and security threats
- Limited distance coverage compared to wired LANs
- Slower speed and higher latency than wired LANs

In summary, LAN standards play a crucial role in defining the physical and data link layer specifications for local area networks. Understanding the characteristics and trade-offs of different LAN standards can help network engineers and administrators choose the right technology for their specific needs.
