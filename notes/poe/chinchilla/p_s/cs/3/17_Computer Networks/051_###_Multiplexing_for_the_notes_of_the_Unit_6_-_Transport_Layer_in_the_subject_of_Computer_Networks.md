### Multiplexing for the notes of the Unit 6 - Transport Layer in the subject of Computer Networks

Multiplexing is the process of combining multiple data streams into a single stream for transmission over a shared medium. In the context of computer networks, multiplexing refers to the technique used by the transport layer to enable multiple applications to share the same network connection.

#### Types of Multiplexing

There are two types of multiplexing techniques used by the transport layer:

1. **Frequency Division Multiplexing (FDM)** - In this technique, the available bandwidth of a communication channel is divided into multiple non-overlapping frequency bands. Each band is then allocated to a specific user or application for transmission. FDM is commonly used in analog communication systems such as radio and television broadcasting.

2. **Time Division Multiplexing (TDM)** - In this technique, the available bandwidth of a communication channel is divided into multiple time slots. Each time slot is then allocated to a specific user or application for transmission. TDM is commonly used in digital communication systems such as voice and data networks.

#### Advantages of Multiplexing

- Efficient utilization of available bandwidth by allowing multiple applications to share the same network connection.
- Cost-effective as it eliminates the need for separate network connections for each application.
- Simplifies network management by reducing the number of physical connections required.

#### Disadvantages of Multiplexing

- Increased complexity of the network architecture.
- Higher risk of data collisions and errors due to shared network connections.

#### Example of Multiplexing

The most common example of multiplexing is the use of the Transmission Control Protocol (TCP) and User Datagram Protocol (UDP) in the Internet Protocol (IP) suite. TCP and UDP are transport layer protocols that enable multiple applications to share the same network connection.

#### Applications of Multiplexing

- Multiplexing is commonly used in voice and data networks to enable multiple users to share the same network connection.
- It is also used in television broadcasting to transmit multiple channels over a single frequency band.
- Multiplexing is used in satellite communication systems to transmit multiple signals over a single satellite link.

In conclusion, multiplexing is a crucial technique used by the transport layer to enable multiple applications to share the same network connection. It allows efficient utilization of available bandwidth and reduces the cost and complexity of network management. However, it also increases the risk of data collisions and errors due to shared network connections.
