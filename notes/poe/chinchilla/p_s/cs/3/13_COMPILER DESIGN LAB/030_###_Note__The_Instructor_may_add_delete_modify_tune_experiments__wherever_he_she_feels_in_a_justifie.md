### Note: The Instructor may add/delete/modify/tune experiments, wherever he/she feels in a justified manner.

In scientific research, experiments are a crucial tool to test hypotheses and theories. However, experiments can be complex and require careful planning and execution. Therefore, it is common for instructors to modify, add, delete or tune experiments in order to better align them with the learning objectives of a course. Here are some key points to keep in mind regarding this practice:

- Modifying experiments can help students better understand the concepts being taught. For example, an instructor may add a step to an experiment to highlight a particular aspect of the theory being studied.
- Deleting experiments may be necessary if the experiment is not relevant to the course or if it is too difficult for students to complete. However, it is important that any deletions are carefully considered to ensure that the overall learning objectives of the course are not compromised.
- Tuning experiments involves making adjustments to the procedure, materials or equipment to ensure that the experiment is more effective. For example, an instructor may adjust the timing of an experiment to ensure that students have enough time to complete it or may modify the equipment to make it more user-friendly.
- Instructors should always justify any modifications, additions, deletions or tuning of experiments so that students understand the reasoning behind these decisions.
- It is also important for instructors to communicate any changes to experiments clearly and in advance so that students are prepared and understand what is expected of them.
- Finally, it is important to note that experiments are not set in stone and can always be improved upon. By continuously evaluating and adjusting experiments, instructors can ensure that students are getting the most out of their learning experience.

In conclusion, the practice of modifying, adding, deleting or tuning experiments is an important tool for instructors to help students grasp complex concepts. As long as any changes are justified and communicated clearly to students, this practice can greatly enhance the learning experience.
