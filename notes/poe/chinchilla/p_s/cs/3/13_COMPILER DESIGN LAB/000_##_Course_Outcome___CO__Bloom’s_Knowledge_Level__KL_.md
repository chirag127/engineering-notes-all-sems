## Course Outcome (CO) Bloom’s Knowledge Level (KL)

In education, the Bloom's Taxonomy is a framework used to categorize educational goals into different levels of complexity and specificity. It is a tool that helps educators to design learning objectives that are appropriate for the level of knowledge that they want their students to acquire. The Bloom's Taxonomy has six levels of cognitive learning, which are arranged in a hierarchical order. The levels are: 

1. Remembering: This is the lowest level of cognitive learning, which involves the ability to recall information from memory. This includes remembering facts, definitions, or concepts. 

2. Understanding: This level of cognitive learning involves comprehension of the meaning of information. It includes interpreting information, summarizing, or paraphrasing information in one's own words. 

3. Applying: This level of cognitive learning involves the ability to apply knowledge to solve problems or complete tasks. It includes using information in a new or different way to solve a problem, or applying knowledge to a new situation. 

4. Analyzing: This level of cognitive learning involves breaking down information into component parts to better understand it. It includes identifying patterns, relationships, or connections between different pieces of information. 

5. Evaluating: This level of cognitive learning involves making judgments about the quality, value, or significance of information. It includes assessing the accuracy of information, evaluating sources, or making decisions based on information. 

6. Creating: This level of cognitive learning involves the ability to create something new or original using knowledge, skills, or ideas. It includes designing, creating, or inventing something new based on what has been learned. 

When designing learning objectives, educators should consider the level of cognitive learning they want their students to achieve. By using Bloom's Taxonomy, educators can create learning objectives that are specific, measurable, achievable, relevant, and time-bound (SMART). 

In summary, the Bloom's Taxonomy is a useful tool for educators to design learning objectives that are appropriate for the level of cognitive learning they want their students to achieve. By using this framework, educators can create learning objectives that are specific, measurable, achievable, relevant, and time-bound.
