### Membership functions for the notes of the Unit 4 - Fuzzy Logic –II (Fuzzy Membership, Rules)

1. Triangular Membership Function:
- It is a piecewise linear function defined by three parameters - a, b and c.
- The function value increases linearly from 0 to 1 as the variable increases from a to b and then decreases linearly from 1 to 0 as the variable increases from b to c.
- The point b is the peak or maximum point of the membership function.
- It is commonly used as it is simple to understand and implement.

2. Trapezoidal Membership Function:
- It is defined by four parameters - a, b, c and d.
- The function value stays at 0 from a to b, then increases linearly from 0 to 1 from b to c, stays at 1 from c to d and then decreases from 1 to 0 from d to infinity.
- The range from b to c is where the fuzzy set has non-zero membership values.
- It generalizes the triangular function and can model concepts more flexibly.

3. Gaussian Membership Function:
- It is a bell-shaped curve defined by two parameters - mean (m) and standard deviation (s).
- The function value is maximum at the mean and decreases as a Gaussian distribution on either side of the mean.
- It is smooth, differentiable and models natural concepts well but is computationally more intensive than triangular or trapezoidal functions.
