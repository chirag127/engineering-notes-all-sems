### Relationships of Higher Degree

- Relationships of degree higher than one are possible in ER models.
- A relationship of degree two is called a binary relationship. Relationships of degree three or higher are called ternary or n-ary relationships respectively.
- Higher degree relationships are used to capture more complex associations among entities.
- Ternary or 3-ary relationship: A relationship that involves three entity types.
- N-ary relationship: A relationship that involves more than two entity types.
- Examples:
  - Marriage relationship involving husband, wife and child.
  - Transaction relationship involving account, agent and branch.
- Representation using Crow's foot notation: The degree of a relationship is shown by the number of crow's feet at the end of the relationship.
- Advantages: Can capture more complex relationships, provides a compact representation.
- Disadvantages: Diagrams can get cluttered, cognitive complexity increases.
- Applications: Used when multiple entity types are strongly related and form a single unit.
