## Unit 4 - Distortion criteria

The following points discuss the distortion criteria for evaluating lossy data compression techniques:

1. **Signal-to-noise ratio (SNR)** - The ratio of the signal power to the noise power. A higher SNR indicates less distortion. It is calculated as the ratio of the squared amplitude of the original signal to the squared error (difference between original and reconstructed signal).
2. **Peak signal-to-noise ratio (PSNR)** - The ratio between the maximum possible power of a signal and the power of distorting noise that affects the fidelity of its representation. It is usually expressed in decibels (dB). A higher PSNR indicates less distortion. It is calculated as the ratio of the maximum possible power of the signal and the power of the error (noise).
3. **Mean squared error (MSE)** - The average of the squared differences between the original and compressed signals. A lower MSE indicates less distortion. It is calculated as the average of the squared errors (differences) between the original and reconstructed signal.
4. **Mean absolute error (MAE)** - The average of the absolute differences between the original and compressed signals. A lower MAE indicates less distortion. It is calculated as the average of the absolute errors (differences) between the original and reconstructed signal.

The above criteria can be used to evaluate and compare the performance of different lossy data compression techniques based on the amount of distortion in the reconstructed signal. They give a quantitative measure of how well the original signal is approximated in the reconstruction.
