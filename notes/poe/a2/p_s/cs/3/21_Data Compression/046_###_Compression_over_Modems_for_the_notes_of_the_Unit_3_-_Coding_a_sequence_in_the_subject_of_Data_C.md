### Compression over Modems

- Modems are devices that convert digital data to analog signals and vice versa. They are used to transmit data over analog communication channels like telephone lines.
- Since analog channels have limited bandwidth, it is important to compress the data before transmission over modems to increase the throughput and reduce transmission time.
- Some of the popular compression techniques used over modems are:
    - V.42bis - It is a data compression standard for modems that can provide a compression ratio of up to 10:1. It uses Huffman coding on the header and predicts the next character using past characters.
    - MNP-5 - It is a compression protocol from Microcom Networking Protocol standards with a compression ratio of 2:1 to 5:1. It uses a variant of Lempel-Ziv compression algorithm.
    - V.44 - It is a modem compression standard that can provide compression ratios of up to 20:1. It uses a more advanced version of the Lempel-Ziv algorithm along with other techniques like context modeling and dictionary modeling.
- The compression technique used depends on the modem standards and compatibility. The higher the compression ratio, the more is the reduction in transmission time but it also depends on the overhead introduced by the compression algorithm.
- Advantages: Reduced transmission time, increased throughput.
- Disadvantages: Added cost and complexity of modems, reduced compatibility.
- Applications: Remote access over dial-up connections, fax transmission.
