### A brief introduction to information theory for the notes of the Unit 1 - Compression Techniques in the subject of Data Compression

1. Information theory is a branch of applied mathematics and electrical engineering involving the quantification of information. It was developed by Claude Shannon to find fundamental limits on signal processing and communication operations such as data compression.
2. The main idea in information theory is that information can be quantified using mathematical techniques and is inherently related to entropy and probability. The amount of information generated by a random variable can be measured by its Shannon entropy in bits.
3. The key results in information theory are:
- The entropy of a random variable quantifies the average amount of information generated by it.
- The mutual information between two random variables quantifies the average amount of information that one variable "tells" about the other.
- The channel coding theorem which establishes that for a given channel there is a limit on how much information can be communicated over the channel with arbitrarily small probability of error.
4. Information theory has broad applications in many areas such as data compression, cryptography, neurobiology, perception, linguistics, statistics, and physics. Application of fundamental ideas from information theory have led to the development of highly efficient data compression algorithms and error-correcting codes that enable modern technologies such as mobile phones and hard disk drives.
