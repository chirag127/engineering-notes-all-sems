### Models for the notes of the Unit 4 - Distortion criteria in the subject of Data Compression

1. Rate Distortion Theory - It provides a theoretical bound on the amount of compression possible. It states that the minimum bit rate required to represent a source to within a given distortion D is R(D), and it gives the mathematical expression to calculate R(D). It provides a fundamental limit on compression.
2. Mean Squared Error (MSE) - It is a measure of the average squared difference between the original and compressed data. A lower MSE indicates lesser distortion. It is given by MSE = ∑(xi - yi)2/N where xi and yi are the original and compressed data values respectively and N is the total number of data values.
3. Peak Signal to Noise Ratio (PSNR) - It is a measure of the peak error. A higher PSNR indicates lesser distortion. It is given by PSNR = 20log10(MAX/sqrt(MSE)) where MAX is the maximum possible pixel value of the image. PSNR is commonly used as a measure of quality of compression in images and videos.
4. Subjective models - They involve subjective tests, evaluations and ratings by humans to assess the perceived distortion in compressed data. The results are more accurate but time-consuming and expensive. Mean Opinion Score (MOS) is a commonly used subjective model where users rate the quality on a scale of 1 to 5.

The models help in determining and comparing the amount of distortion incurred by different compression algorithms and techniques. They are useful in optimizing and improving the compression algorithms to achieve maximum compression with acceptable distortion levels based on application requirements.
