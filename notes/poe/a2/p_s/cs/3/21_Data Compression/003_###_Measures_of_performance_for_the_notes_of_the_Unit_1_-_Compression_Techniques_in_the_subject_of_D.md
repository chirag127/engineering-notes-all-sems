### Measures of performance for the notes of the Unit 1 - Compression Techniques in the subject of Data Compression:

1. Compression Ratio - It is the ratio of the uncompressed size of the data to the compressed size of the data. It is calculated as:
Compression Ratio = Original Size / Compressed Size

A higher compression ratio indicates better compression.

2. Bit Rate - It is the number of bits required to represent a unit of information (like a character or a pixel) in the compressed data. A lower bit rate indicates better compression.

3. Speed - It is the rate at which the compression and decompression algorithm can convert raw data into compressed data and back. A faster speed is always preferred. However, achieving high speed may reduce compression ratio or increase bit rate. So, it is a trade-off.

4. Complexity - It is the amount of time and space required to execute the compression and decompression algorithms. Lower complexity algorithms are preferred as they are easier to implement and require less resources. However, they may not achieve high compression ratio or low bit rate. So, it is again a trade-off.

Thus, there is no single measure of performance that can evaluate a compression technique. Multiple measures have to be considered together based on the requirements and a suitable compression technique has to be chosen.
