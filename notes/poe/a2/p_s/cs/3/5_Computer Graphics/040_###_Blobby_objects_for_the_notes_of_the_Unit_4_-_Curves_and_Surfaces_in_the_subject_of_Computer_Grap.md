### Physical Layer in Computer Networks

The physical layer is the first and lowest layer in the OSI model. It is responsible for the actual physical connectivity between the devices. The main functions of the physical layer are:

1. Bit transmission - The physical layer converts the data received from the upper layers into signals and transmits them over the physical medium. It also receives and converts the signals back into bits.
2. Physical topology - The physical layer specifies the actual layout of the network and the physical interconnections between the devices. It defines the type of cabling, connectors, and interfaces used.
3. Signal encoding - The physical layer encodes the bits into signals suitable for transmission over the medium. It uses techniques like modulation and demodulation.
4. Physical medium - The physical layer specifies the physical medium to be used for transmission like twisted-pair copper wires, coaxial cables, fiber optic cables, wireless frequencies, etc. The characteristics of the medium determine the type of signal encoding and transmission techniques to be used.
5. Synchronization - The physical layer provides synchronization of the transmitted bits by using techniques like clock recovery. This is necessary to ensure proper reception and decoding of the transmitted bits.

Some key concepts and components of the physical layer are:
- Bit rate - Measured in bits per second (bps), it refers to the speed of data transmission. Higher bit rate leads to faster data transmission.
- Bandwidth - Measured in Hertz (Hz), it refers to the capacity of the medium to carry signals. Higher bandwidth means more data can be transmitted simultaneously.
- Latency - Refers to the time taken for the signals to travel from source to destination. Lower latency is desirable for applications like video calling which require quick transmission of data.
- Attenuation - The loss of signal strength as it travels through the medium. The amount of attenuation depends on the medium material and frequency of signals.
- Noise - Unwanted electrical or electromagnetic signals that degrade the main signal. Noise leads to errors in data transmission which need to be detected and corrected.
- Repeaters - Devices used to boost the strength of signals and regenerate the waveforms after a certain distance. They help in extending the length of the network.
- Transceivers - Devices that combine the functionality of transmitters and receivers. They are used to send and receive signals over the physical medium.

Advantages:
- Provides the actual physical connectivity between devices
- Determines the physical medium, speed of transmission, etc. based on requirements

Disadvantages:
- Susceptible to signal degradation and noise which can lead to errors
- Limited by the characteristics and bandwidth of the physical medium

Applications:
- Used in all types of networks like LAN, WAN, Internet, etc. to provide the basic physical connectivity
- Necessary for transmitting data, voice and video signals over various mediums
