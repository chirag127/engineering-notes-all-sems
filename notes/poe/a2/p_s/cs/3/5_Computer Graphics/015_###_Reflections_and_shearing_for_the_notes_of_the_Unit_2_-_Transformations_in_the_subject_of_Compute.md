### Organization of the Internet

The Internet is a massive network that connects millions of computers and other devices from all over the world. It is designed to be decentralized and fault-tolerant, with no single point of control or failure. The key aspects of its organization are:

- Physical infrastructure: The Internet is built on a physical infrastructure of cables, satellites, telephone lines, and wireless signals that carry data between devices.
- Internet service providers: ISPs operate the physical infrastructure that connects devices to the Internet. Most homes and businesses access the Internet through an ISP.
- Routers: Routers are networking devices that direct data packets across the Internet based on address information. They route data between ISPs and across the infrastructure.
- Protocols: A set of protocols known as TCP/IP define how data is formatted and addressed so that it can be reliably routed between any two devices on the Internet.
- Domain name system: The domain name system matches easy-to-remember domain names to numerical IP addresses so that people can access websites and other resources without having to know or remember the IP addresses.

The Internet is a globally distributed network, with no single point of control or governance. This decentralized structure makes the Internet very resilient but also difficult to regulate. Various organizations work to establish open standards and policies to maintain the Internet's global interoperability.

### Reflections and shearing for the notes of the Unit 2 - Transformations in Computer Graphics

Reflection is a transformation that flips a shape across a line of reflection. It can be done across:

- X-axis: Reflects across the x-axis. Coordinates are negated either above (y>0) or below (y<0) the axis.
- Y-axis: Reflects across the y-axis. Coordinates are negated either left (x<0) or right (x>0) of the axis.
- Arbitrary line: Reflects across any arbitrary line defined by slope (m) and y-intercept (b). Coordinates are negated relative to the line.

Shearing is a transformation that slants a shape by displacing each point proportional to its distance from a line called the shear line or axis. Shearing can be done in the x- or y-directions:

- X-shear: Each point is offset in the x-direction proportional to its y-coordinate. Useful for simulating 3D effects or page slants.
- Y-shear: Each point is offset in the y-direction proportional to its x-coordinate. Also useful for simulating 3D effects.
