### Ambient light

- Ambient light is a global illumination light that illuminates all objects in a scene equally.
- It simulates indirect lighting that comes from all directions and is scattered/reflected by the environment.
- It adds flat, uniform lighting to all surfaces in a 3D scene and is useful when other direct/point lights are not present.
- The intensity of ambient light can be specified using RGB triplets (ranging 0-255) or floating point values (0.0-1.0).
- Higher ambient light intensity makes a scene look more lit up while lower intensity can make a scene look dimly lit or dark.
- Ambient light is combined with direct/point lights using addition and affects the final color of surfaces/pixels in a 3D graphics pipeline.
- Some advantages of ambient light are:
    - Adds base level of illumination to a scene.
    - Brings out details on surfaces that are in shadow.
    - Makes a 3D scene look more realistic and less dull when only direct lights are used.
- Some disadvantages are:
    - Can wash out details/shadows on surfaces if intensity is too high.
    - Does not reflect actual light sources/direction in a real scene.
    - Requires tuning ambient light intensity for desired effect which can be tricky.
- Ambient light finds applications in 3D graphics for games, animation, architecture visualization, etc. to provide basic scene lighting when only direct lights are not sufficient or suitable.
