### Specular reflection
- Specular reflection is the mirror-like reflection of light from a surface.
- It occurs when light reflects off a smooth surface at the same angle as the incident ray.
- The resulting reflected ray has the same color as the incident ray.
- The intensity of the reflected light depends on the material's specular reflectance.
- To simulate specular reflection in computer graphics, a specular highlight is added to 3D models.
- The specular highlight is the bright spot on the model that looks like a highlight on a shiny surface.
- The size, intensity, and location of the specular highlight depends on:
    - The material's specular exponent or shininess which controls the size of the highlight
    - The angle between the viewing direction and the reflection direction
    - The intensity of the light source
- Specular reflection can make 3D models look more realistic by simulating shiny appearance of objects like glossy plastics or metals.
- However, it adds additional computations to the rendering process.
- Examples of specular reflection:
    - Reflection of light on a glossy sphere
    - Glare on a shiny car or floor
    - twinkling of stars on a lake surface
