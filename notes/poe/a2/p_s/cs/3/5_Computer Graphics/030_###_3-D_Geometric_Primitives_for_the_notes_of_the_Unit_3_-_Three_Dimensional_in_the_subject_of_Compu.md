### 3-D Geometric Primitives for the notes of the Unit 3 - Three Dimensional in the subject of Computer Graphics

- Point: A point indicates a location in space and has no size. It is represented by its X, Y and Z coordinates.
- Line: A line is an infinite extent with no thickness. It is represented by two point locations.
- Ray: A ray is a semi-infinite line with an origin and a direction. It is often used to model vision and lighting.
- Segment: A line segment is a finite line with two endpoints.
- Triangle: A triangle is a 3-sided polygon represented by 3 points. It is often used as a primitive to model curved surfaces.
- Quadrilaterals: A quadrilateral is a 4-sided polygon. Cubes, rectangles, etc. are examples of quadrilaterals.
- Polygons: Polygons are 2-dimensional shapes with straight lines as sides. Triangles, quadrilaterals, pentagons, etc. are examples of polygons.
- Meshes: Meshes are networks of polygons that are used to represent the surfaces of 3-D objects.
- Curves: Curves are 1-dimensional shapes that can be described parametrically or algebraically. Circles, ellipses, spirals, etc. are examples of curves.
- Surfaces: Surfaces are 2-dimensional shapes that can be described parametrically or algebraically. Spheres, cylinders, cones, etc. are examples of surfaces.
- Solids: Solids are 3-dimensional shapes that can be described parametrically or algebraically. Cubes, spheres, cones, etc. are examples of solids.
