### Spheres

- A sphere is a perfectly round three-dimensional shape. In computer graphics, spheres are commonly used to model objects like balls, planets, etc.
- The mathematical equation to represent a sphere is:
(x-h)^2 + (y-k)^2 + (z-l)^2 = r^2
where (h, k, l) represents the center of the sphere and r represents the radius of the sphere.
- To render a sphere, we need to sample points on its surface and connect them to form polygons. The sampling can be done using techniques like latitude-longitude sampling, spherical harmonics, etc.
- Once we have the surface points, we can use polygon mesh creation algorithms to connect the points and form triangular meshes that approximate the sphere. These meshes can then be rendered using rendering techniques like shading, texturing, etc.
- Some pros of using spheres are that they are simple to define and sample and they resemble real-world objects. Some cons are that a large number of polygons may be required to get an accurate approximation of a sphere leading to more processing overhead.
- Spheres find applications in various areas like modeling planets and celestial bodies, collision detection, simulations, etc.
