#### Signal transmission and encoding in Computer Networks.

1. Signal transmission refers to the process of transmitting digital data over a physical medium in the form of electric signals. The medium could be copper wires, optical fibers, wireless radio waves, etc. The transmitted signals are in the form of different physical quantities like voltage, current, light intensity, etc. depending on the medium used.
2. For the signals to be transmitted and received correctly without any loss or distortion, they must be encoded into a signal format suitable for the transmission medium. This is done by using a signal encoding technique called line coding. Some common line coding techniques are:
- Unipolar encoding: Uses only one voltage level, either high or low. Eg: Non-Return-to-Zero (NRZ)
- Polar encoding: Uses two voltage levels, positive and negative. Eg: Return-to-Zero (RZ)
- Multi-level encoding: Uses more than two voltage levels. Eg: Pulse Amplitude Modulation (PAM)
3. The encoding technique used depends on the transmission medium and the bandwidth requirements. The received encoded signals are then decoded back to the original data for further processing. This decoding is done using a method compatible with the encoding technique. Proper encoding and decoding is essential for accurate data transmission with no loss of information.
