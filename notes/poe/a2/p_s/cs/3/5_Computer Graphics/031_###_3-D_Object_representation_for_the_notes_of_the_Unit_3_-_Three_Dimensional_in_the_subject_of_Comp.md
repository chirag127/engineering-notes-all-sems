### 3-D Object Representation

The 3-D objects can be represented in the following ways for Computer Graphics:

1. Wireframe Model:
- It is the simplest type of 3-D representation.
- It represents the edges and vertices of the object.
- No shading or texture mapping is done.
- Easy to construct but hard to visualize the complete object.
- Useful for rendering objects with lesser processing power.

2. Surface Model:
- It represents the surface of the 3-D object.
- The surfaces can be plane, curved or meshed.
- Additional attributes like color, texture, shading, etc can be added.
- More realistic than wireframe but computing power required is more.
- Examples: Polygon mesh, NURBS surfaces, subdivision surfaces, etc.

3. Solid Model:
- It represents the 3-D object as a solid figure.
- The internal structures and attributes can also be represented.
- Most realistic but most complex to construct and render.
- Examples: Constructive Solid Geometry (CSG), Boundary Representation (B-rep), etc.

The representation to be used depends on the level of realism required and the computational resources available. Surface and solid models provide more realistic graphics but require higher processing power and resources. Wireframe models are simple but lack realism.
