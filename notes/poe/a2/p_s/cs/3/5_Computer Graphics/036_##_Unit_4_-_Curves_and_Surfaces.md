## Unit 4 - Curves and Surfaces

### Curves

- A curve is a one-dimensional geometrical object that has a tangent line at every point.
- The curvature of a curve describes how quickly a curve bends. The curvature depends on the second derivative of the curve.
- Some common types of curves:
    - Line - has zero curvature
    - Circle - has constant curvature
    - Parabola - has increasing then decreasing curvature
    - Ellipse - has varying curvature
    - Hyperbola - has varying curvature
- Curves can be represented in parametric or non-parametric forms. Parametric curves are defined by x(t) and y(t) where t is the parameter. Non-parametric curves are defined by an implicit equation F(x, y) = 0.
- The arc length of a curve describes the distance along the curve. It can be calculated by integrating the square root of (1 + f'(x)^2) dx from a to b, where f(x) is the function defining the curve.

### Surfaces

- A surface is a two-dimensional geometrical object that has a tangent plane at every point.
- The curvature of a surface describes how quickly the surface bends in multiple directions. The curvature depends on partial derivatives of second order of the surface.
- Some common types of surfaces:
    - Plane - has zero curvature
    - Sphere - has constant positive curvature
    - Saddle surface - has varying curvature with positive and negative regions
    - Ellipsoid - has varying curvature
- Surfaces can be represented in parametric or implicit forms similar to curves. Parametric surfaces are defined by x(u, v) and y(u, v) where (u, v) are parameters. Implicit surfaces are defined by an implicit equation F(x, y, z) = 0.
- The surface area can be calculated by integrating over the surface. The exact method depends on the type of surface and parametrization or implicit form.
