### 3-D Clipping

- 3-D Clipping is a process of cutting away parts of a 3-D scene that do not contribute to the final rendered image.
- It removes unwanted portions of a 3-D scene to save computational effort and increase efficiency.
- The goal is to show only the visible surfaces from the view frustum and exclude everything else.
- The two main types of 3-D clipping are:
	- Frustum clipping: Clipping objects outside the view frustum. Objects completely outside the frustum are clipped.
	- Plane clipping: Clipping objects in front of or behind a clipping plane. Objects on the opposite side of a clipping plane are clipped.
- Advantages:
	- Increases rendering speed and efficiency.
	- Prevents unnecessary processing of hidden surfaces and objects.
	- Enables optimization techniques like view frustum culling.
- Disadvantages:
	- Increases computational complexity of the rendering pipeline.
	- Can result in clipping artifacts if not implemented properly.
- Applications: 3-D computer graphics, video games, 3-D modelling software, etc.
