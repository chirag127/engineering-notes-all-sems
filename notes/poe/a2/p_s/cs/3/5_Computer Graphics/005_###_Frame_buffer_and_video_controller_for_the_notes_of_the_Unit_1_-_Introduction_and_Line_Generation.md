 Here is the content in markdown format for the topic ### Frame buffer and video controller for the notes of the Unit 1 - Introduction and Line Generation in the subject of Computer Graphics:

**Frame Buffer:**
- The frame buffer is a memory area in the graphics card that stores the pixel data of the image/scene that is to be displayed on the screen.
- It contains the color and intensity information for every pixel on the screen.
- The size of the frame buffer determines the resolution/display quality of the image or scene. Larger frame buffers allow for higher resolution and quality display.
- The pixel data in the frame buffer is continuously scanned and sent to the display via the video controller.

**Video Controller:**
- The video controller is the component that scans the frame buffer and converts the pixel data into a video signal to display the image/scene on the screen.
- It scans the frame buffer line by line, reading the pixel data sequentially and converting it into an analog RGB video signal.
- The scanning rate or refresh rate determines how frequently the frame buffer is scanned and refreshed on the display. Standard refresh rates are 60Hz and 70Hz. Higher refresh rates result in smoother display of motion.
- The video controller also synchronizes the scanning of the frame buffer with the refresh rate of the display to ensure proper display of the pixel data.

The frame buffer and video controller work in coordination to display the 3D graphics on the screen. The CPU and graphics processor render the 3D scene into the frame buffer and the video controller scans and converts the frame buffer data into a display signal. This process repeats continuously to display an animated 3D scene.
