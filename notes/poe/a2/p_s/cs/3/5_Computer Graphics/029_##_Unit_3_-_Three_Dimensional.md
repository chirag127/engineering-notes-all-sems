## Unit 3 - Three Dimensional

- Three dimensional (3D) refers to the extension of concepts from two-dimensional (2D) planes to include the third dimension, depth or height.
- This allows for the modeling and representation of physical 3D objects and spaces.
- Some key concepts in 3D:

-- Volume: The three-dimensional analog of area in 2D. Volume describes the total space that an object occupies.
-- Surface: The boundary between two media, e.g. the surface of a 3D solid. Surfaces have area.
-- Solid: A three-dimensional shape with a distinct volume. Solids have both volume and surface area.
-- Plane: A flat 2D surface extending infinitely in two dimensions. Three non-parallel planes intersect to form a 3D volume.
-- Point: A zero-dimensional location in space. Three non-collinear points determine a unique plane and four non-coplanar points determine a unique 3D space.

- Examples of 3D shapes: cube, sphere, cylinder, cone, pyramid.
- Applications of 3D: 3D printing, 3D computer graphics, 3D modeling, 3D scanner, Robotics, Architecture, Product design, Virtual reality, etc.
- Advantages: Provides a more natural and intuitive way to represent the real world. Allows for more perspectives and a deeper understanding of shapes and concepts.
- Disadvantages: Can be more computationally intensive to work with 3D data. Additional skills required to interpret and understand 3D information.
