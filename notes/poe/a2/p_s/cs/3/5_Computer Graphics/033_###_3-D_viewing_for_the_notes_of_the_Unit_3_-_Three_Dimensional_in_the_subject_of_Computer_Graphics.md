# 3-D Viewing

### Introduction
- 3-D viewing is the technique of displaying 3-D objects/models on a 2-D display screen.
- It gives an illusion of depth to the objects viewed on the screen.
- It allows users to view the 3-D scenes from different angles as if viewing the real 3-D world.

### Methods
- There are mainly 3 methods for 3-D viewing:
1. Stereoscopy: Uses stereo pairs of images for left and right eye respectively to create depth perception. Requires stereo display and viewing devices.
2. Motion Parallax: Uses relative motion between the viewer and the scene to create depth perception. Requires tracking viewer's motion and updating the view accordingly.
3. Depth Cues: Uses monocular depth cues like relative size, occlusion, shadows, etc. to create depth perception. Does not require any special display or tracking devices.

### Advantages and Disadvantages
- Advantages: Engaging visualization; Gain insights into 3-D structures and relationships; Useful for design applications, medical imaging, etc.
- Disadvantages: Additional computing resources required; Lack of physiologic depth cues can cause visual fatigue or discomfort; Limited field of view.

### Examples and Applications
- Examples: Stereoscopic displays; Head-mounted displays; Motion parallax in mobile devices; Depth cues in video games and movies.
- Applications: Computer-aided design; Virtual reality; Robotics; Medical imaging; Education; Entertainment; Architecture, etc.
