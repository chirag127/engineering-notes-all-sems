### Intensity Attenuation for the notes of the Unit 5 - Hidden Lines and Surfaces in the subject of Computer Graphics

- Intensity attenuation is the reduction in intensity of a ray of light as it travels through a medium or gets reflected/refracted from a surface.
- The amount of attenuation depends on the properties of the medium like absorption coefficient, scattering coefficient, etc.
- In computer graphics, intensity attenuation is used to simulate realistic shading effects and to model light interactions with various materials/media.
- The basic equation used is:

I = I0e^(-kd*d)

Where I is the final intensity, I0 is the initial intensity, kd is the attenuation/extinction coefficient and d is the distance travelled.
- This equation can be modified to incorporate scattering effects and other factors as well.
- Proper modelling of intensity attenuation leads to high quality and photorealistic computer graphics images/animations.
