### Categories of networks in computer networks

1. Local Area Network (LAN): A LAN is a network that is confined to a relatively small area. It is made up of computers and other devices dispersed over a relatively limited area, connected by a communications link that is not generally accessible to the public. Typically, the computers and other devices are located within a building or campus.

2. Metropolitan Area Network (MAN): A MAN is a network that covers an entire city or a large campus. It uses high-speed backbone technologies like fiber optics to interconnect local area networks in a city. A MAN is larger than a LAN but smaller than a WAN.

3. Wide Area Network (WAN): A WAN is a geographically dispersed telecommunications network. A WAN interconnects multiple LANs and MANs, and can cover a wide area, such as a state, country or the whole world. The typical example of a WAN is the Internet. WANs are built on top of existing physical networks, using techniques such as packet switching, to create "virtual" networks that can span large geographical areas.

- Limited bandwidth.

- Internet
- Enterprise networks spanning multiple office locations
- Banking networks
- Research networks

### Basic transformation for the notes of the Unit 2 - Transformations in the subject of Computer Graphics

1. Translation: Translation is shifting an object's location in the 2D or 3D space. It does not change the object's size or orientation. The translation of an object can be specified with a translation vector.

2. Rotation: Rotation is turning an object around a fixed point. The rotation of an object can be specified using an angle of rotation. The axis of rotation can be defined using a rotation axis and the center of rotation is the point around which the rotation occurs.

3. Scaling: Scaling is resizing an object by a scale factor. The scale factor can be different in x, y and z directions resulting in non-uniform scaling. Uniform scaling means the same scale factor is used in all directions.
