## Estimation of Project Metrics

- Project metrics are quantitative measures used to estimate various aspects of a software project.
- Some of the commonly estimated project metrics are:

1. Size metrics:
- Estimation of the size of the software to be developed in terms of number of modules, number of files, number of classes, number of function points, etc.
- Example: Estimating that the project will have 50 modules and 100 classes.

2. Effort metrics:
- Estimation of the effort required to complete the project in terms of person-months or person-hours.
- Example: Estimating that the project will take 5 engineers working for 6 months to complete.

3. Cost metrics:
- Estimation of the cost required to complete the project in terms of total cost or cost per unit.
- Example: Estimating that the project will cost $200,000 to complete.

4. Schedule metrics:
- Estimation of the schedule required to complete the project in terms of timelines for major tasks or total time to completion.
- Example: Estimating that requirements analysis will take 1 month, design will take 2 months and coding and testing will take 4 months.

- Accurate estimation of project metrics is important for proper planning and management of software projects.
- However, it is difficult to estimate project metrics accurately due to the inherent uncertainty in software projects. Estimates are often padded to account for risks and uncertainty.
- Estimation techniques like expert judgment, algorithmic models, and parametric models are commonly used to estimate project metrics. But they still result in errors.
- Proper tracking and monitoring of projects and taking corrective actions is necessary as the project progresses to meet the estimated project metrics.
