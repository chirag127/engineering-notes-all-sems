## Identifying Requirements from Problem Statements

1. Understand the problem statement thoroughly - Read the problem statement multiple times to understand the actual problem that needs to be solved. Identify the key points and components of the problem. Some important points to identify are:
- Who is the user of the system?
- What exactly is the problem? What pain point does it address?
- What is the scope of the system? What all functions/features are required?
- Any constraints or non-functional requirements specified?

2. List down the requirements - After understanding the problem statement, list down the important requirements to solve the problem. Some tips for listing requirements:
- Use simple and clear language
- Write in imperative tone (what the system should do)
- Group related requirements together
- Leave requirements at an abstract level, avoid implementation details

3. Analyze and refine requirements - The initial listed requirements would be quite broad and vague. Analyze them in detail and refine them:
- Remove duplicates or overlapping requirements
- Break down complex requirements into smaller sub-requirements
- Check for consistency and completeness of requirements
- Specify measurable conditions/criteria wherever possible
- Note down important non-functional requirements like performance, security, etc.

The refined list of requirements can then be used to design and develop the actual software solution. Requirements are important to build the right product that satisfies the user needs. Hence, sufficient time should be spent on properly analyzing and refining the requirements.

Advantages:
- Helps build the right product that meets user needs
- Acts as a reference during design and development
- Used for testing and verification

Disadvantages:
- Difficult to identify all requirements
- Requirements can be misunderstood or misinterpreted
- Requirements can change over time leading to rework

Applications: Requirement analysis is the first step in any software engineering project. The requirements identified drive all the subsequent design and development steps.
