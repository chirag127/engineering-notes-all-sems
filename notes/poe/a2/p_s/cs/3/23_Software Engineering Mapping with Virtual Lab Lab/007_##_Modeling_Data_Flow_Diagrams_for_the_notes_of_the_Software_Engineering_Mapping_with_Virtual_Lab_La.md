## Modeling Data Flow Diagrams

A Data Flow Diagram (DFD) is a graphical representation of the flow of data through an information system. It models the flow of data into and out of processes and stores the data.

- DFDs are used to visualize the transfer of data between processes and data stores. They show the flow of data and the transformations applied as data moves through a system.
- DFDs are often used as a preliminary step to create an overview of the system and identify major transformations and stores of data.
- The main components of a DFD are:
    - Processes: Processes are actions that transform the input data into output data. They are represented by circles or bubbles.
    - Data flows: Data flows are the pathways along which data moves between processes, data stores, and the external entities. They are represented by arrows.
    - Data stores: Data stores are the repositories of data that are accessed by the processes. They are represented by two parallel lines.
    - External entities: External entities are objects outside the system that interact with it by sending or receiving data. They are represented by squares.
- The levels of detail in a DFD are:
    - Context diagram: Shows the entire system as a single process and its interactions with the external environment.
    - Level 1 DFD: Breaks down the single process in the context diagram into sub-processes and shows data flows between them.
    - Level 2 DFD: Takes each process in Level 1 and breaks it down into further details.
- The advantages of using DFDs are:
    - They provide a clear understanding of the system and its scope.
    - They facilitate identifying the inputs and outputs of a system.
    - They help to find deficiencies and redundancies in the system.
    - They are easy to understand and provide a good communication medium between analysts and users.
- The disadvantages of using DFDs are:
    - They do not show the sequence of events and processes. The relationships are static and do not represent any time-ordering.
    - The processes and data flows can become complex as more levels of detail are added. This can make the diagrams hard to understand and maintain.
    - The semantics are not precise and different people can interpret the diagrams in different ways.
