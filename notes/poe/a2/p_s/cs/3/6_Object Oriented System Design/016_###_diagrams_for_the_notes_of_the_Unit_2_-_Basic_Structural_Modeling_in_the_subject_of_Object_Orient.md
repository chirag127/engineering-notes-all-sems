### Class Diagrams

- Class diagrams are the main diagrams used for static modeling of the system.
- They show the classes in the system and the relationships between them.
- Each class is represented by a box containing the class name. Relationships between classes are represented by lines connecting the classes.
- The various relationships that can exist between classes are:
-- Association: A relationship between two classes that indicates that instances of one class may know about, and use or be associated with, instances of the other class.
-- Aggregation: A special type of association that denotes a whole-part relationship between the aggregate (whole) and the constituent part. The part can exist independently of the whole.
-- Composition: A special type of aggregation with a strong life cycle dependency between the whole and the parts. The parts cannot exist independent of the whole.
-- Inheritance: The relationship between a general class (superclass) and a more specific class (subclass) that inherits characteristics from the superclass. The subclass can be substituted for the superclass.

> Note: Class diagrams are static models, representing structures rather than behaviors. Sequence diagrams and collaboration diagrams are used to model the dynamic aspects of the system.

- Advantages: Provide an overview of the system and show the static structure of the system. Help in communication between stakeholders and defining components.
- Disadvantages: Can become complex for large systems. Only show a partial view of the system.
- Applications: Used in the initial stages of design to identify classes and relationships. Then can be used as reference diagrams in later stages.
