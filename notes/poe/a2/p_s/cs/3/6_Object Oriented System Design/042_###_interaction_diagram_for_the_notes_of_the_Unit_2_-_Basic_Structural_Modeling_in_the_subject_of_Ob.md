### Interaction Diagram

- Interaction diagrams are used to visualize the dynamic behavior of a system. They depict the sequence of messages exchanged between different objects over time.
- The main elements of an interaction diagram are:
  - Objects: Represented by rectangles. They contain the object name and type.
  - Messages: Represented by arrows. They show the message name and direction (sending vs receiving object).
  - Lifelines: Represented by bars. They show the existence period of an object over time.
- The most common types of interaction diagrams are:
  - Sequence diagrams: Focus on the sequence of messages. Objects are arranged along the vertical axis and messages are shown in the order they occur.
  - Communication diagrams: Focus on object relationships. Objects are arranged in a free form and messages are shown with links between sending and receiving objects.
  - Timing diagrams: Focus on time. The vertical axis represents time and objects/messages are placed accordingly. They are useful to show concurrency and performance.
- Advantages:
  - Helps understand the dynamic aspects of a system.
  - Detects errors and inconsistencies early.
  - Useful for documentation and communication.
- Disadvantages:
  - Might be difficult to read for complex systems with many objects/messages.
  - Does not show conditional logic or looping constructs.
- Applications:
  - Analysis and design of the dynamic behavior of a system.
  - Specification of the ordering of messages.
