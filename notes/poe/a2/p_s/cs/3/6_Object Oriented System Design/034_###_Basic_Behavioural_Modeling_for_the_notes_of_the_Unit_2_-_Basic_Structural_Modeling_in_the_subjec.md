### Basic Behavioural Modeling

Behavioural modeling focuses on the dynamics of a system. It captures the behaviour of a system over time by showing the sequences of operations and interactions.

Following are the key aspects of behavioural modeling -

1. State Diagrams - Used to represent the states of an object and the transitions between those states. They show the dynamic behaviour of a system.
2. Sequence Diagrams - Used to represent the sequence of messages exchanged between objects for a particular scenario. They show the order of execution of methods and interactions between objects.
3. Collaboration Diagrams - Similar to sequence diagrams but focus on the relationships and interactions between objects. They show the structure of the interactions.
4. Activity Diagrams - Used to represent the flow of control or workflow in a system. They show the sequential and concurrent activities, decision points, etc.
5. Use Case Diagrams - Used to capture the functionality of a system from an end user's perspective. They show the interactions between the system and external actors.

Advantages -
- Help understand the dynamics and runtime behaviour of a system.
- Aid in validating logic and sequences.
- Useful for visualizing and communicating behaviour.

Disadvantages -
- Can get complex for large systems.
- May be subjective and open to interpretations.
- Difficult to keep updated if the system changes.

Applications - Requirements analysis, design modeling, documentation, etc.
