### Sequence Diagrams for the notes of the Unit 2 - Basic Structural Modeling in the subject of Object Oriented System Design

Sequence diagrams are interaction diagrams that detail how operations are carried out. They are organized according to time. They are used to show how objects interact with each other to carry out a use case.

Sequence diagrams are made up of the following:

- Objects: Represented as rectangles with the object name inside.
- Lifelines: Under each object. A dashed line that represents the existence of the object over a period of time.
- Messages: Represented as arrows from the sender to the receiver. The message name is written on the arrow.

Sequence diagrams can be used to:

- Model the logic of complex use cases and scenarios.
- Understand the runtime behavior of a system.
- See potential concurrency problems between objects.

Advantages:

- Easy to understand as they are organized chronologically.
- Show how objects interact over time and the order of interactions.
- Can be used as a communication tool between analysts and stakeholders.

Disadvantages:

- Do not show conditional logic or alternative paths.
- Only shows the interactions between a small number of objects, so they do not scale well to large systems.
- The objects and messages shown are dependent on the scenario being modeled. Different scenarios may show different objects and messages.

Examples and applications:

- Sequence diagrams are typically created as part of use case modeling to visualize a particular scenario.
- They are often used to model key scenarios in the user interfaces of interactive systems.
- They can be used to model the logic behind business processes.
- They are a dynamic modeling technique commonly used in object-oriented analysis and design.
