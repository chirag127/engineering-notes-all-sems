### Activity Diagrams

- Activity diagrams are used to model the dynamic aspects of a system. They depict the flow of control from activity to activity.
- An activity diagram consists of activities, actions and control flows.
- Activities are depicted as rounded rectangles. They represent the actions that can be performed.
- Actions are depicted as rounded rectangles with double borders. They represent atomic pieces of work in the system.
- Control flows are depicted as labelled arrows. They show the order in which activities and actions are performed.
- Decisions are modelled using diamonds. Forks and joins are used to depict concurrency.
- The initial and final states are depicted using unlabelled rounded rectangles.
- Advantages:
    - Used to model workflow and business processes.
    - Help understand the dynamics of a system.
    - Can be mapped to use cases and software requirements.
- Disadvantages:
    - Does not show the object interactions.
    - Complex designs can become cluttered and unreadable.
- Applications:
    - Modelling business processes.
    - Analysing workflow.
    - Specifying system logic.
