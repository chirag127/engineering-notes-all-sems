### Collaboration Diagrams

Collaboration diagrams, also known as Communication Diagrams, are used to show the relationships and interactions between objects in a system. They are closely related to sequence diagrams but focus on object relationships rather than the time-ordering of messages.

- Collaboration diagrams show objects as simplistic shapes with their names inside and links between these objects representing relationships or interactions.
- The links between objects indicate communication and usually show the name of the method being called and the parameters being passed.
- Collaboration diagrams are often considered more readable than sequence diagrams as they focus on the structural aspect rather than the sequence of messages.
- However, they can be more difficult to construct as the links between objects are not always straightforward to determine.

**Advantages:**

- Focus on relationships and interactions between objects rather than time ordering.
- Often considered more intuitive and easy to understand than sequence diagrams.
- Useful for documenting and communicating how a system is structured.

**Disadvantages:**

- Can be more difficult to create than sequence diagrams as determining interactions is not always straightforward.
- Do not explicitly show sequence of events which can be important in some systems.
- Limited support in some modeling tools compared to sequence diagrams.

**Examples and Applications:**

- Collaboration diagrams are commonly used to model the interactions between objects in a system.
- They are often used as part of the object-oriented analysis and design process to show the structural relationships between components.
- Systems with complex interactions between objects are suitable candidates for collaboration diagram modeling.
- They can be useful as a complement to sequence diagrams to show both the structural and sequential aspects of a system.
