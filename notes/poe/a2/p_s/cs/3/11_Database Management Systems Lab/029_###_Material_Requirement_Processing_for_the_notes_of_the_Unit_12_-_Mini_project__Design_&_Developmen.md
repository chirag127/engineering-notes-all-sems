### Material Requirement Processing

Material Requirement Planning (MRP) is a computer-based inventory management system designed to assist production managers in scheduling and placing orders for dependent demand items. MRP helps to maintain low inventory levels while ensuring that materials are available when needed for production or procurement.

The major inputs to an MRP system are:

- Master production schedule (MPS): The overall level of manufacturing output in units of finished products.
- Bill of materials (BOM): A listing of all subassemblies, components, and raw materials that go into a parent assembly or final product.
- Inventory status files: The level of inventory of all items in the BOM.

The outputs of an MRP system are planned orders - releases for purchasing, work orders for manufacturing, or schedule revisions. The key steps in MRP processing are:

1. Exploding the bill of materials to determine gross requirements.
2. Netting the gross requirements against on-hand inventory and scheduled receipts to determine net requirements.
3. Scheduling the release of planned orders to meet the net requirements while respecting capacity constraints.

Advantages of MRP:

- Helps in maintaining low inventory levels while ensuring availability of materials.
- Provides detailed scheduling information to assist in procurement and shop floor control.
- Detects potential material shortages.

Disadvantages of MRP:

- The system can become complex as the number of items and levels in the BOM increase.
- Lead times and demand patterns must be estimated which can introduce errors.
- MRP does not handle capacity constraints well.

Applications of MRP:

- Manufacturing companies to plan and schedule materials and production.
- Any business or process where materials and components are assembled or combined to produce finished goods.
