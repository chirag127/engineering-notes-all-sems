### Inventory Control System

An inventory control system is used to track and maintain inventory levels, orders, and sales. It ensures that a business has the right products in the right quantities to meet customer demand.

Some key points about an inventory control system:

- Keeps track of inventory: The system records inventory items, quantities, and locations. It updates inventory counts as items are received, sold, or moved.
- Manages orders: The system can automatically generate purchase orders when inventory reaches a reorder point. It keeps track of order status and updates inventory when orders are received.
- Supports sales: The system can check inventory availability of a product when a sale is made. It then updates inventory to account for the sale.
- Provides reports: The system generates reports on inventory value, turnover, and margins. It can also report on sales, purchases, and other metrics to help with planning and decision making.
- Integrates with accounting: An inventory control system works together with an accounting system to value inventory and track costs. It shares data with the accounting system to keep totals in sync.

Some potential components or tables in an inventory control system database include:

- Products: Lists item names, IDs, descriptions, categories, prices, cost prices, etc.
- Inventory: Tracks product quantities on hand, allocated, on order, minimum/maximum stock levels, locations, etc.
- Purchase Orders: Lists order details including products, quantities, prices, order dates, delivery dates, statuses, and suppliers.
- Sales: Records individual sales or invoices including products, quantities, prices, dates, and customer information.
