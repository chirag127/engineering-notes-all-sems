1. Knowledge Representation (KR) - The goal of knowledge representation is to represent knowledge in a computer in a way that enables efficient processing by automated reasoning systems. Following are few approaches to represent knowledge:

- Logic-based representations - Prolog language uses Horn clauses, logical rules to represent knowledge. Allows automatic reasoning & inferencing but scales poorly with increasing data.
- Frame-based representations - Real-world objects & concepts are represented in terms of frames which are data structures containing attributes & their values. Facilitates modeling hierarchical & taxonomic knowledge but inefficient in handling uncertainties & ambiguities.
- Semantic networks - Knowledge is represented as nodes connected by directed/undirected edges for relationships. Supports graphical visualization & navigation but lacks formal, structured semantics.
- Conceptual graphs - Knowledge is represented using nodes for concepts & edges for relations with well-defined semantics. Captures contextual & structured knowledge but limited in expressing modalities & probabilities.

2. Applications of Knowledge Representation - KR techniques are applied in various AI applications such as:

- Expert systems - To encode expertise & infer conclusions/decisions in a specific domain.
- Question answering systems - To represent knowledge & retrieve relevant information to user queries.
- Robotics - To represent information about actions, sequences of actions & effects of actions to achieve goals.
- Computer-aided instruction - To model learning material & provide adaptive instruction & feedback to students.
- Information retrieval - To represent information needs & match user queries to relevant documents.
