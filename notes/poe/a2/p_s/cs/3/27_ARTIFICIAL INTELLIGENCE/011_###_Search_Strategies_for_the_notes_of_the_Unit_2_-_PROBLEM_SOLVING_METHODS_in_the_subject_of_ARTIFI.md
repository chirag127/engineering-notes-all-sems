### Search Strategies for the notes of the Unit 2 - PROBLEM SOLVING METHODS in the subject of ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE KCS

1. Breadth First Search (BFS):
- Searches shallowly through the problem space by exploring all siblings of a node before moving to the next level.
- Advantage: Guarantees the shortest path.
- Disadvantage: May explore a large number of unnecessary nodes.
- Example: Finding the shortest path in a maze or graph.

2. Depth First Search (DFS):
- Searches deeply through the problem space by exploring one branch as far as possible before backtracking.
- Advantage: May find a solution quickly without exploring the entire problem space.
- Disadvantage: Does not guarantee the shortest path and may miss optimal solutions.
- Example: Finding a solution path in a maze or graph.

3. Hill Climbing:
- A basic local search strategy that starts with an initial solution and iteratively moves to better neighboring solutions.
- Advantage: Simple and efficient.
- Disadvantage: May get stuck in local optima and miss the global optimum.
- Example: Finding the highest point of a hill (local optimum may be a lower hilltop).
