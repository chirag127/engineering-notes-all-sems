## Typical Intelligent Agents

The following are some of the typical intelligent agents:

1. Expert Systems: These are computer programs that contain encoded knowledge and inferences procedures to solve problems that normally require human expertise. They are designed to perform tasks like technical diagnosis, advising users, and aiding in decision making. Some examples are:
    - MYCIN: Used for identifying bacteria that causes severe infections and recommending antibiotics.
    - XCON: Used for configuring computers and networks by novice users.
    - R1/XCON: Used for helping users configure VAX computers.

2. Fuzzy Systems: These are systems that can make decisions based on uncertain or imprecise information. They are designed to mimic human decision making and are useful for applications such as:
    - Controlling devices (ex: antilock braking systems)
    - Pattern recognition
    - Forecasting and planning
    - Robotics

3. Neural Networks: These are algorithms inspired by the structure and functioning of the human brain. They are made up of interconnected nodes that must be trained on large amounts of data to detect patterns and learn how to perform tasks like classification and prediction. Some applications of neural networks are:
    - Image recognition
    - Speech recognition
    - Predicting stock market trends
    - Detecting spam emails
    - Robotics
