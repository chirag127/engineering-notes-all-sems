### Game Playing

- Game playing is a common AI problem where an AI system learns to play strategic games (like chess, checkers, Go, etc.) at a superhuman level.
- The AI agent has to learn successful strategies and sequences of actions (moves) that lead to the goal state (winning the game).
- The AI system has to evaluate board positions and select the optimal move that maximizes the expected outcome (winning chances).
- Some of the techniques used are:
    - Minimax algorithm with alpha-beta pruning: Evaluates all possible sequences of moves and picks the one that maximizes the minimum outcome. Alpha-beta pruning improves efficiency.
    - Heuristic evaluation function: Gives an approximate assessment of the position without searching the entire game tree. Helps cut down the search space.
    - Opening books and endgame tables: Pre-computed sequences of optimal moves for common opening and endgame positions to save computation.
    - Machine Learning: Recently, ML techniques like reinforcement learning are used where the system learns successful strategies by trial-and-error using feedback from outcomes of games.
- Examples: IBM's Deep Blue system that beat the chess champion in 1997. Recent systems that beat top players in the games of Go, poker, DOTA 2, Starcraft, etc.
- Applications: Besides entertainment, game playing research leads to developments in AI, machine learning, and reasoning under uncertainty that can be applied to real-world problems.
