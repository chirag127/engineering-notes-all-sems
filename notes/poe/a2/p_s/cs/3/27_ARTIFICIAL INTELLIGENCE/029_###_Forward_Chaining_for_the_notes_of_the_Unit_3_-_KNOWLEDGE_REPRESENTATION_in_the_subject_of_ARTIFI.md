### Forward Chaining

- Forward chaining is a inference method used in expert systems and automated reasoning.
- In forward chaining, the inference engine tries to resolve a goal by matching it against the condition (IF) parts of the rules and finding rules whose conditions are satisfied.
- It then draws inferences by the conclusions (THEN) parts of the matching rules and continues this process until a solution is found or no more rules can be applied.
- The process is called *forward chaining* because it moves forward from the conditions to the conclusions.
- For example, to determine if a person has diabetes, the system would first check if the person is overweight (rule 1), then check for family history (rule 2), then check for high blood sugar (rule 3) and so on until a conclusion can be reached.
- Advantages: Guaranteed to find a solution if one exists; Useful for diagnosis and design problems.
- Disadvantages: May take a long time to find a solution; May go down unnecessary paths; May provide more information than needed.
- Applications: Expert systems, automated reasoning, diagnosis systems, design systems, etc.
