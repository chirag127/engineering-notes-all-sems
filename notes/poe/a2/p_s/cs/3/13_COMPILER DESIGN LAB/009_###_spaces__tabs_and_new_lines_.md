### Spaces, Tabs and New Lines

- Spaces: Spaces are used to separate words and make the text more readable. They are represented by " " in the text. Using spaces appropriately improves the readability and formatting of the text. For example, to separate function and variable names from the parentheses: myFunction(), to separate mathematical operators from operands: a + b, etc.
- Tabs: Tabs are used to indent the text and structure it in a hierarchical manner. They are represented by "\t" in the text. Using tabs improves the formatting and visibility of the structured text. For example, to indent lines of code in a block or to show nested headings, lists, etc.
- New Lines: New lines are used to separate blocks of text and indicate the end of a line. They are represented by "\n" in the text. Using new lines appropriately makes the text easy to read and follow. For example, to separate paragraphs, indicate the end of a statement, separate items in a list, etc.

Advantages:
- Improves readability and visibility of the text.
- Enhances the formatting of the text.
- Facilitates proper structuring of the text.

Disadvantages:
- Excessive use of spaces, tabs and new lines can make the text messy and difficult to read.
- Inconsistent and improper use of spaces, tabs and new lines can disrupt the formatting and structure of the text.

Examples and Applications:
- Formatting code in programming languages.
- Writing documents, articles, essays, etc.
- Structuring content in markdown or other formats.
