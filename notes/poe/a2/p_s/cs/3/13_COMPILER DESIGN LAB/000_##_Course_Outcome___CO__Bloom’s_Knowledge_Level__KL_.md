## Course Outcome ( CO) Bloom’s Knowledge Level (KL)

Bloom's taxonomy is a classification of learning objectives within education proposed in 1956 by Benjamin Bloom. It aimed to classify educational goals into categories or levels. The original Bloom's taxonomy consisted of six levels arranged from the simplest behavior to the most complex:

1. Remembering - Recalling or recognizing terms, facts, concepts, ideas, materials, patterns, sequences, methods, principles, etc. (KL-1)
2. Understanding - Constructing meaning from different types of functions be they written or graphic messages or activities (KL-2)
3. Applying - Carrying out or using a procedure in a given situation (KL-3)
4. Analyzing - Breaking down material into its constituent parts, examining and trying to understand the relationships between parts and the overall structure or purpose (KL-4)
5. Evaluating - Making judgments about the value of ideas, works, solutions, methods, materials, etc. (KL-5)
6. Creating - Putting elements together to form a coherent or functional whole; reorganizing elements into a new pattern or structure (KL-6)

The revised Bloom's taxonomy (by Anderson & Krathwohl, 2001) changed the levels to:

1. Remembering
2. Understanding
3. Applying
4. Analyzing
5. Evaluating
6. Creating

Course Outcomes (COs) using Bloom's taxonomy help in clearly defining and distinguishing between the learning levels and aid the students in understanding the depth and specificity of the topics to be covered. The COs are measurable through assessments and the KL helps determine the difficulty level of assessments.
