### Fairs and Festivals

- Fairs and festivals are an important part of Indian culture and traditions. They are celebrated throughout the year in different regions of the country.
- Some of the popular fairs and festivals of India are:
    - Kumbh Mela: It is one of the largest religious gatherings in the world that is held every 12 years at four holy locations - Prayagraj, Haridwar, Ujjain and Nashik. Devotees gather to take a holy dip in the rivers and worship.
    - Pushkar Fair: It is one of the largest camel fairs held in Pushkar, Rajasthan. Apart from camel trading, it features cultural events, competitions and religious celebrations.
    - Hornbill Festival: It is a popular festival held every year in the first week of December in Kohima, Nagaland. It showcases the richness of Naga heritage through music, dance, food, sports, art, handicrafts, and customs.
    - Onam: It is a harvest festival celebrated in Kerala to honour King Mahabali. It is a 10-day long festival featuring boat races, tiger dances, elaborate feasts, and decorative floors with floral rangoli.
    - Dusshera: It signifies the victory of good over evil. Giant effigies of demon king Ravana are burnt, signifying the destruction of evil. It is celebrated with parades and stage performances of Ramlila.
    - Diwali: It is one of the major festivals of India celebrated as the festival of lights. Diyas and lights illuminate houses and streets. People worship Goddess Lakshmi and exchange gifts and sweets.
    - Holi: It is a popular spring festival of colours celebrated primarily in North India. People play with coloured powder and water, symbolizing the triumph of good over evil.

The festivals promote cultural unity and preserve Indian traditions while also providing entertainment and strengthening community bonding. They attract many tourists and boost the economy of the regions where they are celebrated.
