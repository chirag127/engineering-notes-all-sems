### Hindi Literature

- Hindi literature refers to literary works written in Hindi language, particularly those from the 15th century onwards.
- It includes compositions in various genres such as poetry, novels, short stories, dramas, and puranas.
- Hindi literature flourished under various literary traditions such as Bhakti movement, medieval Hindi literature, and modern Hindi literature.
- Prominent figures include Kabir, Tulsidas, Surdas, Munshi Premchand, Jaishankar Prasad, Sumitranandan Pant, Mahadevi Varma, and Harivansh Rai Bachchan.

**Poetry:**
- Leading poetic forms include Kabir's dohas, Tulsidas's vinaya patrika, and Jayadeva's Geet Govinda.
- Other forms are chhands, kanda, holi, and chaitti.
- The themes in Hindi poetry include love, valour, devotion, and pride in one's language.

**Prose:**
- Important prose works include the Panchatantra tales, biography of religious figures, travelogues, and essays on science, philosophy, society, and culture.
- Munshi Premchand incorporated realism in his short stories and novels such as Godan and Kafan which reflected the struggles of the common people.
- Modern fiction includes works of writers such as Yashpal, Nagarjun, and Upendra Nath 'Ashk'.

**Drama:**
- Bhavabhuti's 8th century plays set standards for laterSanskrit and Hindi plays.
- Jaishankar Prasad incorporated Indian traditions in his plays such as Skanda Gupta and Bharat Durdasha.
- Important contemporary playwrights include Vijay Tendulkar, Badal Sircar, and Mahesh Dattani.
