### Buddhism

- Buddhism was founded in ancient India by Siddhartha Gautama, who is known as the Buddha, meaning "enlightened one."
- The Buddha was an ascetic who meditated on the nature of life and taught the Four Noble Truths, the Eightfold Path, and the concepts of karma and rebirth.
- The Four Noble Truths state that (1) life is suffering, (2) the cause of suffering is desire, (3) the end of desire leads to the end of suffering, and (4) the Eightfold Path can end desire.
- The Eightfold Path includes guidelines for moral behavior, meditation, and wisdom to achieve release from suffering.
- Karma is the idea that good and bad deeds produce merit and demerit, which determine how a person will be reborn after death. Through following the Eightfold Path, one can achieve release from this cycle of rebirth.
- Buddhism spread throughout ancient India and Southeast Asia, evolving into many different regional forms. The two major branches are Theravada, focused on individual liberation, and Mahayana, which emphasizes liberation for all beings.
- Core beliefs in Buddhism include the impermanence of existence, the cycle of rebirth, and the path to liberation from suffering by overcoming desire and attachment. Buddhism is a nontheistic religion and Buddha is viewed as a teacher, not a god.
- Meditation, following the precepts of moral behavior, and acts of devotion are common practices in Buddhism. Monasticism is important in Theravada Buddhism, while Mahayana Buddhism includes lay practitioners. There are over 500 million Buddhists worldwide today.
