### Trade in Ancient India

- Trade has been an important activity in India since ancient times. The Indus Valley civilization was a major trading center. Various objects like precious beads, stones, shells, etc. excavated from Indus Valley sites indicate trade with other civilizations like Mesopotamia and Egypt.
- During the Vedic period, trade was carried on through barter system as money had not come into use. Goods were exchanged for goods. Cows and horses were regarded as units of value to measure the price of articles. Trade was both internal as well as external.
- Internal trade was carried on through rivers as well as land routes. Merchants were highly organized and lived in settlements. Occupational specialization emerged.
- External trade was carried on through land routes as well as sea routes. The land routes included the silk route through which trade with Central Asia and China took place. The sea routes included trade with Southeast Asia as well as the Roman empire. Spices, cotton fabrics, precious stones, etc. were exported and gold, silver, etc. were imported.
- The Mauryan empire saw growth in trade and commerce. The Mauryan emperor Ashoka promoted trade for the spread of Buddhism to other countries. The main centers of trade were Taxila, Ujjain, Kalinga, etc.
- The Gupta period saw further progress in trade and commerce. The main exports were cotton and silk fabrics, spices, indigo dye, etc. The main imports were gold, silver, olive oil, etc. The trade was regulated by the state. The guilds played an important role in the regulation of production and exchange.
- In summary, trade has been an integral part of the Indian economy since ancient times. There was a wide network of trade with other countries as well as within the country. Various factors like rivers, ports, guilds, etc. facilitated the growth of trade and commerce in Ancient India.
