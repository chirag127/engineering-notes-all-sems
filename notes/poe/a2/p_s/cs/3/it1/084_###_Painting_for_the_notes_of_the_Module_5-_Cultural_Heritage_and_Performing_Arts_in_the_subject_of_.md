### Painting

- Painting is one of the oldest art forms in India with a history spanning more than 2000 years. The paintings depict a wide range of themes and subjects.
- The pictorial traditions encompass prehistoric rock paintings, ancient murals, miniature paintings, and modern and contemporary art.
- Indian paintings can be broadly classified as:

1. **Murals:** Paintings done on the walls and ceilings of the structures, especially temples and palaces. Eg: The Ajanta Caves and Kailasanatha temple at Ellora contains magnificent examples of fresco secular paintings.

2. **Miniature Paintings:** Small-sized intricately painted illustrations, usually meant for personal viewing. Done in a variety of styles; prominent ones being the Pahari and Rajput schools. The Mughal paintings are characterized by their delicate brushwork, the use of paintings as illustrations of courtly narratives, and their synthesis of Indian and Persian styles.

3. **Modern Paintings:** These incorporated Western artistic styles and techniques along with traditional Indian themes and subjects. Key figures include Raja Ravi Varma, Amrita Sher-Gil, Jamini Roy, and M.F. Husain.

Some key characteristics of Indian paintings are:

- Abundance of rich colors.
- Two-dimensional and do not provide the illusion of depth.
- Importance of lines and intricate patterns.
- Religious, mythological and literary themes and subjects.
- Strong storytelling emphasis with the help of facial expressions and hand gestures.

The diverse traditions of Indian painting have played a key role in the art history of South and Southeast Asia and influenced later traditions of Asian art. The artists, techniques, subjects and styles offer a vivid visual chronicle of the cultural, religious and social changes in the Indian subcontinent over several millennia.
