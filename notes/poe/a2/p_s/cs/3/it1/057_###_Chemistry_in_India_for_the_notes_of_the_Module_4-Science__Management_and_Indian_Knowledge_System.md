## Chemistry in Ancient India

- The Indus Valley Civilization (3300 BCE - 1300 BCE) had some knowledge of basic chemistry like manufacture of bricks, beads, seals, and sanitation and water systems.
- The Vedas (composed 1500 BCE - 500 BCE) contain some of the earliest known chemical uses like production of pigments for paintings, tanning leather, distillation of perfumes, extraction of metals, and manufacture of dyes for clothes.
- The Ancient Indian medicinal system of Āyurveda (originating in the Vedic period) included chemistry for metallic preparations, herbal extracts, and mineral medicines. Rasaśāstra is the branch of Āyurveda dealing with these metallic and mineral preparations.
- The Arthashastra by Chanakya (4th century BCE) refers to the state supervision of weights and measures, metals, and other commodities to standardize production.

## Chemistry Advancements in Medieval India

- In the Gupta period (320 to 550 CE), many advancements were made like the production of zinc and distillation techniques producing perfumes and alcoholic drinks.
- The Rasarnava (11th century) systematically compiled and synthesized processes from Rasaśāstra tradition. It describes extraction and purification processes for minerals and metals at length.
- The Rasendramangalam (13th century) includes additional processes and recipes for substances like mercuric and arsenic compounds.
- The Ānandakanda (15th century) compiled and described new chemical processes, especially those involving metallic salts and alkalis.
