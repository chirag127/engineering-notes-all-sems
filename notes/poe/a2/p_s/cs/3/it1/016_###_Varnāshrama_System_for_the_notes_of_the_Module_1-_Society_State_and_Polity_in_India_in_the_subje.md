### Varnāshrama System
- The Varnāshrama system refers to the socio-economic class system and the stages of life (ashramas) in ancient India.
- It consisted of four varnas or socio-economic classes - Brahmins (priests and teachers), Kshatriyas (warriors and rulers), Vaishyas (traders and merchants), and Shudras (labourers and servants).
- The four ashramas or stages of life were - Brahmacharya (student), Grihastha (householder), Vanaprastha (retired), and Sannyasa (renunciation).
- The varna of a person was determined by birth. The ashrama was based on a person's age and life achievements.
- The system aimed to create a stable social order by dividing people into classes based on their duties and occupations. It facilitated social harmony and prevented clashes by restricting inter-caste marriages and interactions.
- However, the system later became rigid and hierarchical, leading to discrimination and unequal treatment of the lower varnas and women. It failed to accommodate changes in economy and society. Hence, it is criticized as being inherently unequal and obsolete in modern times.
- The varnāshrama system provides insights into the ancient Indian concept of an ideal society with clearly demarcated social classes and stages of regulated life. Though outdated, some of its principles such as fulfilling one's duties can still be applied in modern contexts.
