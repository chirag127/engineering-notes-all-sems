### Music

- Indian classical music is one of the oldest musical traditions in the world. It has its roots in ancient vedic chanting and tribal music. It is based on ragas (melodic modes) and talas (rhythmic patterns).
- The two main traditions of Indian classical music are Hindustani music (North India) and Carnatic music (South India). Both these systems are fundamentally based on melody and rhythm, and they use tals - rhythmic cycles of beats.
- Hindustani music places more emphasis on improvisation while Carnatic music focuses on the compositions. Hindustani music is mainly melodic while Carnatic music uses percussion and rhythmic aspects.
- Some of the popular instruments used in Indian classical music are tabla, sitar, sarod, veena, flute, etc. Tabla is a popular percussion instrument used to keep rhythm. Sitar and Sarod are plucked string instruments. Veena is a string instrument played with a bow. Flute is a wind instrument.
- Gharanas are the systematic style of learning music under the guidance of a teacher which has been followed traditionally. Some well known gharanas of Hindustani music are Jaipur-Atrauli gharana, Agra gharana, etc.
- Some famous artists of Indian classical music are Pt. Ravi Shankar, Ustad Amjad Ali Khan, MS Subbulakshmi, etc. Indian classical music has gained popularity all over the world due to artists like Ravi Shankar.
- The future of Indian classical music looks bright with many young artists innovating and experimenting with the tradition while staying true to its roots. At the same time, fusion music combining elements of Indian classical with other genres is also gaining popularity.
