### Coins

- Coins are one of the important artifacts that provide valuable insights into the economic, political and cultural history of a civilization.
- The study of coins is known as 'Numismatics'. Numismatics plays an important role in reconstructing the past.
- The origin of coins in India can be traced back to the 6th century BCE. The coins of this period were punch-marked coins called Purana.
- The Mauryan empire under Chandragupta Maurya introduced the first round coins in the 4th century BCE in silver and copper. The symbols of sun, horse and elephant were used on these coins.
- The Kushan kings introduced gold coins in addition to silver and copper coins in the 1st century CE. These coins featured images of the kings and deities.
- The Gupta empire which ruled over large parts of India from 320 to 550 CE introduced the first gold coins in India. The coins featured images of the kings performing religious rituals or victories in battle.
- The medieval period saw the introduction of bilingual coins by various dynasties. This period also saw a decline in the metal content of coins leading to debasement of coins.
- Mughal coins featured the names and titles of the Mughal emperors in Arabic script. Portraits of the emperors were also prominently displayed on the coins.
- During the British rule, coins featured images of British monarchs. After independence, coins in India featured the symbols of the Indian union and the value of the coin in Hindi and English.
