### Medicine in India

- India has a long history of indigenous medical practices. The Ayurvedic system of medicine is one of the oldest medical systems still practiced today. It is based on the idea of balance in bodily systems and uses a comprehensive holistic approach.
- Susruta Samhita is one of the foundational texts of Ayurveda written by the scholar Susruta in the 6th century BCE. It describes advanced surgical techniques and classifies human surgery in 8 categories. Susruta is considered as the 'Father of Surgery' for his seminal contributions to the field.
- Other important texts include Charaka Samhita which focuses on internal medicine and healthy living. These ancient texts describe medicinal uses of plants and minerals and methods for treating diseases and ailments. Many modern medicines are derived from indigenous Indian plants and herbs.
- Unani medicine was introduced in India by the Arabs and Persians. It is based on the teachings of Greek physician Hippocrates and Roman physician Galen. It uses medicines from plants, animals and minerals. It focuses on removing the cause of the disease through regulation of six essential elements of the body - air, earth, water, fire, minerals and vegetables.
- Siddha medicine is a traditional Tamil system of medicine. It is based on the principles of the three humors viz. Vatham, Pitham and Kapham which correspond to air, fire and water. It uses herbs, metals, shells and other materials for treatment. The Siddhars developed this system and it involves alchemy and spiritual practices as well for treatment.
- In modern India, both Western medicine and traditional Indian systems of medicine are practiced. There have been efforts to integrate modern and traditional medicine to develop new treatment approaches and make healthcare more comprehensive and accessible. The Ministry of AYUSH promotes research and development in traditional Indian medicine systems.
