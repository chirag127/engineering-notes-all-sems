## Module 4-Science, Management and Indian Knowledge System

1. Indian Knowledge System - Ancient Indian knowledge system refers to the accumulation of knowledge developed in ancient India before the modern scientific framework came into existence. It includes knowledge systems of mathematics, astronomy, architecture, metallurgy, medicine, agriculture, etc. Few key aspects of ancient Indian knowledge system are:
- Emphasis on observation, reasoning and logic. Various deductive and inductive logical reasoning methods were developed.
- Holistic approach - Interconnections between various subjects was emphasised. For ex. - Astronomy and architecture were connected for Vastu Shastra.
- Oral tradition of Gurukul education - Knowledge was passed down through generations via oral recitations and memorization.
- Practical applications - Knowledge was applied to solve practical issues in various fields. For ex. - Calculus used in astronomical calculations, metallurgy used to produce high quality steels, etc.

2. Indian Management Thought - References to management thoughts are found in ancient Indian scriptures and texts like Vedas, Upanishads, Ramayana, Mahabharata, Arthashastra, etc. Some key aspects are:
- Importance of leadership and governance - concepts of Praja (citizens), Rajan (ruler), Dandaniti (justice and punishment)
- Importance of planning and organization - Arthashastra discusses necessity of strategies, rules, procedures, policies for efficient management of economy and society.
- People centric approach - reflecting concern for happiness and welfare of people. Concepts of ethics, values, and social responsibilities of businesses.
- Flexibility and adaptability - Ancient Indian management thought taught the need to adapt principles based on prevailing conditions.
