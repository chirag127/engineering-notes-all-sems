# Jainism

- Jainism is an ancient Indian religion that emphasizes complete non-violence and asceticism.
- Followers of Jainism are called Jains, and they follow the teachings of the 24 Tirthankaras. The first Tirthankara was Rishabha, and the 24th and most important was Mahavira (599 BCE - 527 BCE).
- The core beliefs of Jainism are:

1. Non-violence - Ahimsa: Jains minimize harm to all living beings including themselves. They practice non-violence through vegetarianism and non-harming all forms of life.
2. Non-attachment - Aparigraha: Jains minimize attachment to possessions and people. They live an ascetic life devoid of desire and greed.
3. Multiple realities - Anekantavada: Jains believe that the ultimate truth has multiple aspects and that no single view can capture the whole picture. They emphasize relativism and manifold perspectives.
4. Karma and rebirth - Jains believe in karma and rebirth. Liberation from rebirth is achieved by destroying all karma through ascetic practices and non-attachment.
5. Ascetic practices - Jains follow rigorous ascetic practices like fasting, meditation, and minimizing consumption to burn off karma and achieve liberation.

- The primary texts of Jainism are the Agamas which include the 12 Angas and 14 Purvas. The most important Jain practices are the 'Three Jewels': Right perception, Right knowledge, and Right conduct.
- Jainism influenced other Indian philosophies and religions and emphasized extreme non-violence, asceticism and relativism. Today, there are about 4-5 million Jains worldwide with most living in India.
