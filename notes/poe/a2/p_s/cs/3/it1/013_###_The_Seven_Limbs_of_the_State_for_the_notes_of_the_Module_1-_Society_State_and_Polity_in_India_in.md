##### The Seven Limbs of the State

According to Hindu political thought, the state or government has seven limbs or elements:

1. The King: The head of the state who wields supreme authority. He is responsible for the protection and welfare of his subjects.
2. Ministers: They are appointed by the King to aid and advise him in governance. They are responsible for the functioning of various governmental departments.
3. Territory: The land inhabited by the people over which the King has sovereignty.
4. Subjects: The people inhabiting the territory of the state.
5. Treasury: The revenue collected in the form of taxes to meet the expenses in running the state machinery.
6. Army: The armed forces responsible for the security, defense and expansion of the state.
7. Allies: Other neighboring states with which the state has friendly relations and alliances.

The proper functioning of all the seven limbs is necessary for the welfare of the subjects and stability of the state. The absence or malfunctioning of any of the elements weakens the state. The King and ministers should work in tandem to ensure that the territory, subjects, treasury, army and allies are maintained properly. In this way, the Hindu theory conceives of the state as an organic whole with interdependent parts working towards a common end.

The concept of seven limbs provides a comprehensive framework to understand the institution of the state, the duties of the ruler and other functionaries and the objectives of governance in Hindu political thinking.
