### Shankaracharya

- Shankaracharya, also known as Adi Shankaracharya or simply, Shankara, was an 8th century CE Indian philosopher and theologian who consolidated the doctrine of Advaita Vedanta.
- He was born in a Brahmin family in present-day Kerala. He became a Sanyasi (ascetic) at a young age and travelled across India debating opponents and establishing mathas (monasteries).
- Shankara's teachings interpret the Vedanta teachings to identify Brahman (the impersonal Absolute) with the individual self (Atman), the ultimate truth and the path of religious knowledge was the only way to moksha (liberation), while rejecting the ritualism and pantheism of other Hindu schools.
- His main teachings, collectively known as Advaita Vedanta, are captured in his commentaries on the Prasthanatrayi - the Bhagavad Gita, the Upanishads, and the Brahma Sutras.
- His prominent disciples propagated his monastic order in different parts of India and established the Dasanami Sampradaya, which forms the umbrella order of several spiritual lineages.
- Shankara is regarded by followers as an incarnation of Shiva and a jagadguru (world teacher), who founded the of the sacred order of monks called Dashanami Sampradaya. He is credited with unifying and establishing the core ideas of Hindu philosophy.
- Shankara remains an influential figure in Indian thought, as the philosopher to harmonize contrasting strands of thought in various Indian traditions. His influence is strongest within smarta tradition of Hinduism. His teachings are followed by various schools and sects within Hinduism such as Advaita, Dashanami Sampradaya, and Vishishtadvaita.
