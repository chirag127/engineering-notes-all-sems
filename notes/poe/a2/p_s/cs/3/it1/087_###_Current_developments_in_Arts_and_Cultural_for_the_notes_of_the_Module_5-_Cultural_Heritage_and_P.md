## Current developments in Arts and Cultural

- Revival of folk arts and crafts: There is a growing recognition of the importance of folk arts and crafts in India. Many state governments and private organisations are working towards the revival and preservation of traditional folk arts and crafts. For example, 'Paramparik Karigar' is a national level organisation working for the welfare of folk artists and artisans. Many e-commerce portals are also promoting the sales of handicrafts and handloom products.
- Growth of new art forms: Contemporary art forms like installation art, digital art, conceptual art, etc. are gaining more acceptance and patronage. Many new art galleries and cultural spaces are promoting contemporary art. Street art and graffiti art are also getting more popular in Indian cities. Several art festivals feature new experimental art forms.
- Increased private patronage: Earlier, most of the patronage for arts came from royal courts and governments. Now, private companies and business tycoons are also supporting art and culture through art foundations, cultural centres, and other initiatives. For example, the Kiran Nadar Museum of Art has one of the largest collections of modern and contemporary art in India. Many new museums and cultural spaces are being privately funded.
- Cultural collaborations: There is increasing collaboration between artists, art institutions, and governments across the world. Many international arts festivals invite Indian artists to participate and showcase their works. Similarly, many Indian cultural festivals now have international collaborations and invite artists from other countries. Such cultural exchanges help in the cross-pollination of ideas and expose audiences to diverse art forms from around the world.
- Access through digital platforms: The Internet and digital platforms are making arts and culture more accessible to common people. Many museums and art galleries have online collections and virtual tours. Performances of dance, music, and theatre are streamed live online. People can learn art forms through online videos and tutorials. However, digital access may not be feasible for everyone due to the digital divide in the country. More efforts are needed to make arts and culture accessible to underprivileged sections.
