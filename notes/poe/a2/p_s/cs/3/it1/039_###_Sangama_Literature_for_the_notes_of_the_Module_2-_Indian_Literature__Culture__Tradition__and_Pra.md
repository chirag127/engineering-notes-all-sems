### Sangama Literature

- Sangama literature refers to the ancient Tamil literature produced in the region ruled by the Sangam dynasty roughly between 300 BCE and 300 CE.
- Sangama literature consists of texts such as the Eight Anthologies (Ettuttokai) and Ten Idylls (Pathupattu) which include some of the earliest known Tamil poetry.
- The Eight Anthologies cover a range of themes from love to war and include around 4,000 poems in total attributed to around 470 poets. The poems are composed in strict metrical forms and are noted for their vivid metaphors, imagery, and philosophical content.
- The Ten Idylls are longer poetic works focussed on themes of love and romance following the moods (thinai) of the Tamil landscape. They provide insights into the society, culture, and beliefs of ancient Tamilakam and include celebrated works such as the Silappadhikaram ('The Story of an Anklet') and Manimegalai.
- Sangama literature is highly influential in modern Tamil literature and provides a window into the cultural and linguistic heritage of the Tamil people during the ancient period. The sophisticated poetic forms and imagery have enduring artistic appeal while the works also provide historical information about the societies and polities of the time.
- The key points to remember are:
1. Sangama literature refers to ancient Tamil texts from 300 BCE-300 CE.
2. It consists of the Eight Anthologies (Ettuttokai) and Ten Idylls (Pathupattu).
3. The poems cover themes of love, war, philosophy and provide insights into ancient Tamil culture and society.
4. Sangama literature features sophisticated poetry and imagery and is highly influential on modern Tamil literature.
