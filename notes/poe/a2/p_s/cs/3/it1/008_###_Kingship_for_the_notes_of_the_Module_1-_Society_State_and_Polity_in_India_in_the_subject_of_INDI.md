### Kingship

- Kingship refers to a system of government headed by a king. In ancient and medieval India, most regions were ruled by kings and kingdoms.
- The king was the supreme ruler and head of the state. He held considerable powers and authority over his subjects. His powers were considered to be divinely sanctioned.
- The king's main duties were to protect his subjects, maintain law and order, dispense justice, and oversee economic development. He was the chief law-giver and judge who could enact and interpret laws.
- The powers and style of functioning of kings varied across kingdoms and time periods. Some kings were autocratic while others were guided by assemblies and ministers.
- Important characteristics of kingship in India included hereditary succession, grants of land and titles to subordinates, patronage of art/culture, and performance of royal rituals/ceremonies to uphold dharma.
- The decline of kingship started with the rise of British colonial rule and subsequent democratic institutions in independent India. However, symbolic monarchies still exist in some princely states.

The key points to remember about kingship in India are:
1. Kings were the supreme rulers with divine sanction and considerable powers
2. Main duties were maintaining order, dispensing justice, economic development
3. Powers and functioning styles varied across time and regions
4. Important features included hereditary rule, land/title grants, patronage, rituals
5. Decline started with colonial rule and democracy, though symbolic monarchies remain
