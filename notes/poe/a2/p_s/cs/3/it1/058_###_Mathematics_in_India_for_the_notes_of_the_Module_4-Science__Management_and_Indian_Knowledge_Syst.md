### Mathematics in India

- Indian mathematics emerged in the Indian subcontinent from 1200 BCE until the end of the 18th century.
- The Indus Valley civilization and the efforts of ancient Indian mathematicians were behind the beginnings of Indian mathematics.
- The earliest extant mathematical documents from India are the Sulba Sutras ('rule of chords' in Sanskrit), dated to 800 BCE to 500 BCE.
- The Sutras contain examples of simple geometry, such as calculating areas and volumes of simple shapes, as well as more advanced geometry, including approximations for pi.
- Indian mathematicians made contributions to the theory of zero, negative numbers, arithmetic, and algebra.
- In the 5th century CE, Aryabhata formulated the first known descriptions of a decimal number system in the Aryabhatiya.
- Brahmagupta's book Brahmasphutasiddhanta from 628 CE included the first description of the use of zero as a number, rules for manipulating both positive and negative numbers, and rules for manipulating fractions, quadratic equations, and sums of power series.
- By the 12th century, Indian mathematicians were solving cubic equations and quartic equations.
- The Kerala School of Astronomy and Mathematics from the late 14th century to 16th century developed and expanded the use of infinite series approximations for pi and trigonometric functions.
- Advantages: Introduced the concept of zero and negative numbers, made progress in arithmetic and algebra, and developed trigonometric series approximation.
- Disadvantages: The decimal number system and mathematical notation were not adopted in India, limiting further advancement.
- Applications: The zero and place value system were transmitted to the Middle East and Europe and contributed to significant developments in mathematics outside India.
