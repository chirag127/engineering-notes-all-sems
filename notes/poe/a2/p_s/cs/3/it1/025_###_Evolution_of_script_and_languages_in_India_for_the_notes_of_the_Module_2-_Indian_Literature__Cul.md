### Evolution of Scripts in India

- The Indus script is the earliest known writing system developed by the Indus Valley Civilization around 3300 BCE. The script contains 400 distinct signs and is still undeciphered.
- The Brahmi script was developed in the 6th century BCE and was the first script used to write Sanskrit and Middle Indo-Aryan languages. It evolved into many scripts used in South and Southeast Asia.
- The Gupta script was developed during the Gupta Empire around the 4th century CE. It was derived from Brahmi and was the primary script used to write Sanskrit.
- The Nagari script was developed in the 7th century CE and evolved into Devanagari, the primary script used to write Hindi, Marathi, and Nepali.
- Other scripts developed from Brahmi include Tamil Brahmi, Kannada, Telugu, Malayalam, Odia, Bengali, and Tibetan.

### Evolution of Languages in India

- Sanskrit is the earliest known language of the Indo-European family and is recorded in the Vedas composed around 1500 BCE. Classical Sanskrit was refined around the 4th century BCE.
- The Middle Indo-Aryan languages like Pali and Ardhamagadhi developed from Sanskrit and were spoken during the 2nd millennium BCE. Pali was the language of early Buddhist scriptures.
- The modern Indo-Aryan languages like Hindi, Bengali, Marathi, Punjabi, etc. developed from Middle Indo-Aryan languages and are the most widely spoken languages in India today.
- The Dravidian languages like Tamil, Kannada, Telugu, and Malayalam originate from the Dravidian family of languages. Tamil Brahmi inscriptions date back to the 3rd century BCE.
- Other language families of India include Austroasiatic (e.g. Munda, Santali) and Tibeto-Burman (e.g. Meitei, Tangkhul). India has a rich linguistic diversity with 22 major languages and 720 dialects.
