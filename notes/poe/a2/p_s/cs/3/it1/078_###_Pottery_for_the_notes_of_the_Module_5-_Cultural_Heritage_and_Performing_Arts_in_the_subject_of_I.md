### Pottery

- Pottery is one of the oldest crafts known to humans and dates back to prehistoric times. The earliest pottery fragments found are from around 29,000 BC.
- Clay is the primary raw material used for making pottery. Clay is shaped into objects by moulding or throwing techniques on a potter's wheel and then fired in a kiln to harden it.
- Firing the clay causes chemical and physical changes that convert it into ceramic material. This is what gives pottery its strength, durability and resistance to water.
- Based on firing temperature, pottery can be earthenware, stoneware or porcelain. Earthenware is fired at lower temperatures while stoneware and porcelain require higher firing temperatures.
- Pottery is shaped by hand or using moulds and can have various patterns and decorations applied using techniques like painting, stamping or glazing. Glazes contain metallic salts that produce colours when fired.
- Important types of Indian pottery include Harappan pottery, terracotta, khurja pottery, blue pottery, meenakari, kagzi pottery, etc. Each has unique styles, techniques, colours and motifs.
- Pottery has applications in storage, cooking, religious ceremonies, decoration, etc. It is an important part of India's cultural heritage with each region having its own unique pottery tradition.
