Kautilya's Arthashastra is an ancient Indian treatise on statecraft, economic policy and military strategy which identifies its author by the name Kautilya, who is traditionally identified with Vishnugupta, who authored the work in the 4th century BCE. The text, which is in Sanskrit, is composed of 15 books which cover various topics such as the role of the king, the machinery of the state, taxation, economy, foreign policy, and army in great detail.

- Kautilya suggests that the king should be an autocrat and he should concentrate all powers into his own hands. He should exercise his royal prerogatives to collect taxes, administer justice, maintain law and order, and safeguard the security of the state.
- The text emphasizes that the happiness of the common people should be the chief concern of the king. The king should protect the people, create conditions for welfare and prosperity, promote economic activities, and wage defensive wars to protect his people and territory.
- The Arthashastra focuses on the principles of governance and military strategy. It covers a wide range of topics including politics, welfare economics, administrative organization and fiscal policies. Its overriding aim appears to have been the security and prosperity of the state and the achievement of a balanced and harmonious development of all the elements of the body politic.
- It is considered as one of the most important works in the field of political science and economics in ancient India. It is a valuable historical document that provides a detailed account of the Mauryan administration and also gives us an idea about the prevailing social and economic conditions during that period.

The key points in the Arthashastra regarding state governance can be summarized as:

1. The happiness of the king depends on the happiness of his people.
2. The administration of the kingdom must be regulated by law and order (danda).
3. Protecting the country is the primary duty of the king. He should always be ready for action.
4. Espionage is an imperative tool of good governance. The king must know what is happening in all parts of his kingdom.
5. An efficient and honest bureaucracy is essential for good administration.
6. A balance should be maintained between severity and leniency while imposing punishments.
7. The king should be ever vigilant, wise, courageous and adept in practicing the duties of kingship.
