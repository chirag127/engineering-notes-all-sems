## Module 2- Indian Literature, Culture, Tradition, and Practices

- Indian literature includes some of the oldest and longest epic poems in the world such as the Ramayana and Mahabharata. The Ramayana contains 24,000 shlokas and 500 sargas (chapters), while the Mahabharata contains 100,000 shlokas and 18 parvas (sections). These epics provide great insight into ancient Indian culture, tradition, and practices.
- The Vedas are some of the oldest religious texts which include the Rigveda, Yajurveda, Samaveda and Atharvaveda. The Vedas contain hymns, prayers, and rituals which formed the basis of ancient Indian religion and practices. The Upanishads and other philosophical texts explored metaphysical questions about the nature of existence and reality.
- Ancient Indian culture emphasized Dharma (ethics and duties), Karma (action and deeds), and Moksha (liberation from the cycle of life and death). The caste system divided people based on their occupations and ensured duties were carried out smoothly. The concept of reincarnation, Yoga, and Ayurveda also emerged from ancient Indian culture.
- Ancient Indian tradition involved worship of natural elements and forces called the Panchamahabhutas as well as various deities in the Vedic pantheon. Fire sacrifices, rituals, invocations, and festivals were an important part of tradition. The symbols of Om, Swastika, and Banyan tree were also significant. Many art forms like dance, drama, music, and architecture flourished.
- In terms of practices, greeting elders by touching feet, wearing bindi or tilak on forehead, and celebrating festivals with colors like Holi are still observed today. Applying turmeric and vermilion holds religious significance. Decorating homes with Rangoli, and following vegetarian dietary practices are also popular practices that originate from ancient India.
