### Society in Ancient India

- Ancient Indian society was divided into four Varnas - Brahmins (priests and teachers), Kshatriyas (warriors and rulers), Vaishyas (traders and merchants), and Shudras (labourers and servants). This varna system was based on occupation and contributed to a stable social structure.
- The varna system led to the development of Jatis or castes based on birth. People were born into a caste and social mobility was restricted. There were strict rules regarding marriage, interaction, and occupations based on Jati.
- Family and marriage were considered sacred. Most marriages were arranged by families. Women faced discrimination and were expected to be devoted to family and domestic work.
- Religion and philosophy played an important role in society. Most people followed either Hinduism, Jainism or Buddhism. The Vedas and Upanishads shaped cultural and social norms. Pilgrimages and religious festivals were common.
- Villages were mostly self-sufficient and agriculture was the main occupation. Most people were peasants who worked in farms and produced food grains and other crops. Artisans and traders also lived in villages and nearby towns.
- Society was highly stratified with distinct hierarchies based on gender, caste, and wealth. However, guilds and religious institutions provided some social security and cohesion. Family and kinship bonds were strong. Regional diversities also existed in culture, language, and practices.
