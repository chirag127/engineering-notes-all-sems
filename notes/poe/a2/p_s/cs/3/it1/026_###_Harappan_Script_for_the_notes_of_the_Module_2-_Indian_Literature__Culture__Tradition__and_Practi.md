### Harappan Script

- The Harappan script is the writing system developed by the Harappan civilization. It was used in the Indus Valley Civilization from 3300 BCE to 1900 BCE.
- The Harappan script consists of 400 basic signs which include numerals and non-numerical symbols including abstract symbols as well as representations of humans and animals.
- The script is yet to be deciphered as the brevity of inscription and signs pose major challenges in its interpretation. However, statistical analysis of the script suggests that it follows the pattern of a structured language.
- Some scholars believe that the Harappan script is logo-syllabic while others consider it to be a proto-Dravidian language. However, there is no concrete evidence to conclusively prove any of the claims.
- The discovery of Harappan seals with scripts and engraved images show the significance of writing during the civilization for purposes such as stamping ownership, identification, communication, etc.
- Despite several attempts, the Harappan script remains undeciphered due to the lack of bilingual texts and short length of inscriptions. Deciphering the script could provide valuable insight into the language, society, and culture of the Harappan Civilization.
