The Vedas are the oldest scriptures of Hinduism and are considered to be revealed texts by the seers (rishis) of the Vedic religion. There are four Vedas:

1. Rig Veda: Contains hymns recited by the hotar priests during the yajna sacrifices. It includes verses dedicated to Agni (fire god) and Indra (rain/war god).
2. Yajur Veda: Contains mantras recited by the adhvaryu priests during the yajna sacrifices.
3. Sama Veda: Contains melodies meant to be sung by the udgatr priests during the yajna sacrifices.
4. Atharva Veda: Contains spells and charms for medicinal and practical purposes.

The Vedas are composed in Vedic Sanskrit and contain various philosophical ideas like Brahman (ultimate reality), Atman (individual soul), samsara (cycle of life and death), karma (law of cause and effect), etc. The Vedas are considered apaurusheya or authorless and are regarded as the foundational scriptures of Sanatana Dharma.

The key points to note about The Vedas are:

1. They are the oldest scriptures of Hinduism composed in Vedic Sanskrit.
2. There are 4 Vedas: Rig, Yajur, Sama and Atharva Veda.
3. The Vedas contain hymns, mantras, melodies and spells used during yajna sacrifices.
4. The Vedas reflect the philosophy and beliefs of the Vedic religion like Brahman, Atman, samsara, karma, etc.
5. The Vedas are considered apaurusheya or authorless and fundamental to Sanatana Dharma.
