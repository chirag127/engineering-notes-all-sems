### Buddhist And Jain Literature in Pali,Prakrit And Sanskrit

- Buddha taught in Magadhi Prakrit which was spoken by common people. His teachings were compiled in Pali Canon by Buddhist monks. Pali Canon includes Vinaya Pitaka (rules for monks), Sutta Pitaka (Buddha's discourses), and Abhidhamma Pitaka (metaphysical teachings).
- Prominent Buddhist texts in Pali are Dhammapada (ethical teachings), Therigatha (verses by female monks), and Jataka tales (previous lives of Buddha).
- Jain literature was composed in Ardhamagadhi Prakrit and Sanskrit. Prominent Jain texts are Tattvartha Sutra by Umaswati (Jain metaphysics), Kalpa Sutra (biographies of Jain tirthankaras), and Samavayanga Sutra (conduct of monks).
- Jain texts deal with rigorous self-discipline, severe asceticism, and harmlessness towards all life forms. They glorify tirthankaras who have attained liberation (moksha) through destruction of karma by extreme self-effort.
- Advantages: The teachings of Buddha and Mahavira reached common people as they were composed in Prakrit. They provide valuable insights into various philosophical ideas and everyday life during those times.
- Disadvantages: Since Prakrit and Sanskrit are not widely studied today, it requires efforts to read, understand, and translate these texts. Also, Pali Canon and Jain scriptures are vast, making it difficult to study entire literature.
- Examples: Excerpts from Dhammapada and stories from Jataka tales are popular and provide life lessons. Verses from Therigatha describe lives of early Buddhist nuns. Stories of tirthankaras like Rishabha and Mahavira inspire Jains.
- Applications: These texts form basis of Buddhist and Jain philosophies which stress on moral conduct, non-violence, and liberation from suffering. Their teachings are relevant even today to cultivate qualities like compassion and inner peace.
