Metallurgy is the process of extracting metals from their ores and refining them for use. Metallurgy in ancient India was quite advanced and evidence of metal extraction and processing is available from the Harappan sites. Some of the major developments in metallurgy in ancient India are:

- Copper: Copper was the first metal to be extensively used in India. It was extracted from copper ore found in Khetri mines in Rajasthan. The Harappans knew the art of making tools and vessels from bronze, an alloy of copper and tin.
- Iron: The Iron pillar of Delhi is a testimony to the high quality of iron produced in ancient India. Wootz steel was produced in South India around 600 BCE by heating iron ore with charcoal and quenching it in water to produce small chunks of high carbon steel. Wootz steel was exported to the Middle East and Europe.
- Brass: Brass is an alloy of copper and zinc. Evidence suggests that the blending of copper and zinc to produce brass was done around the beginning of the Christian era in India. A Buddhist monument at Sultanganj was made of brass in the 7th century CE.
- Steel: The crucible steel making process was quite advanced in ancient India. It produced very high quality steel comparable to modern steel and was exported to Europe and the Middle East. The Delhi and Daulatabad cannons were made using crucible steel.

Advantages of advanced metallurgy:
- Better tools and weapons could be produced leading to advancement in agriculture and military strength.
- More intricate jewellery and artefacts could be produced showcasing excellent craftsmanship.
- The iron pillar of Delhi has not rusted significantly due to the superior quality of iron used.

Thus, India had a very advanced metallurgical tradition since ancient times as evidenced by the high quality iron, steel and brass produced. This contributed significantly to the economic and technological development of Indian civilization.
