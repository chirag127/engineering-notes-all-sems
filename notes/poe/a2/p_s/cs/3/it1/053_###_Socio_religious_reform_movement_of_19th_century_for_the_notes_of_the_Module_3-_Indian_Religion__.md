### Socio religious reform movement of 19th century

- The 19th century witnessed several socio-religious reform movements in India as a reaction to some malpractices and rigidity that had crept into the Indian society and as a result of Western education and influence.
- The reformers stressed on importance of rational thinking, opposed blind faith and prejudices. They condemned the caste system and position of women in the society. Main reform movements were:
- Brahmo Samaj founded by Raja Ram Mohan Roy in 1828. It promoted monotheism and condemned idolatry, polytheism and the caste system. It fought against Sati system and child marriage.
- Prarthana Samaj was started in 1867 in Bombay by Dr Atmaram Pandurang. The main objective was to make people abandon superstitious beliefs and follow the Vedas.
- Arya Samaj was founded by Dayanand Saraswati in 1875. It was based on the infallibility of the Vedas. It rejected idolatry and the caste system. It preached monotheism and stressed the importance of the Vedas.
- Other reform movements include Satya Shodhak Samaj by Jyotirao Phule, Sri Narayana Dharma Paripalana movement in Kerala by Chattampi Swamikal which fought against untouchability and unreasonable social customs.
- The reform movements helped in strengthening the foundations of social progress and national awakening in India in the 19th and 20th centuries. They removed many social evils, promoted education and instilled the scientific temper among the people.
