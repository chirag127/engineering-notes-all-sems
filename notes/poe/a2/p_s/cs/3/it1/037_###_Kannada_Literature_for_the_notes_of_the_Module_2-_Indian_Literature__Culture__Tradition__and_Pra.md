### Kannada Literature

- Kannada literature refers to the body of literature created in the Kannada language, one of the Dravidian languages spoken mainly in the state of Karnataka in southern India.
- The earliest extant prose work in Kannada is Kavirajamarga by King Amoghavarsha I, written around AD 850.
- The earliest extant literary works in Kannada are Jain poetry from around the 9th century, written by early Brahmin writers, under the patronage of the Western Ganga dynasty of Talakad.
- The Vijayanagara and Keladi rulers, also supported Kannada literature as did the Mysore Kingdom, with its capital at Mysore.
- Noted Kannada writers include the early Jaina writers Jinasena and Adikavi Pampa; the 12th-century Hoysala court poets Rudrabhatta and Raghavanka; Haradatta Suri; Lakkana Dandesa; Linganna; Arebhashe; saint poets Purandaradasa and Kanakadasa of the Bhakti movement; Kuvempu; and Gopalakrishna Adiga.
- The Kannada literature influenced other literatures, as seen in the Jain epic 'Pampa Bharata' by Adikavi Pampa, based on the Ramayana; and 'Yashodhara Charite' by Ratnakarvarni, based on the 'Yasodhara-Kumara Campu'.
- The Vijayanagara emperor Krishnadevaraya's 'Amuktamalyada' is considered a literary masterpiece. 'Kadambari' by Banabhatta is the earliest biography in Kannada and proto-Kannada.
- The earliest comprehensive Kannada grammar is Shabdhamanidarpana by Keshiraja in 1260 CE. Chamarasa's Prabhulinga Lile (1430 CE) and Sriranga Deva's Yatharthadeepika are landmark later compositions.
