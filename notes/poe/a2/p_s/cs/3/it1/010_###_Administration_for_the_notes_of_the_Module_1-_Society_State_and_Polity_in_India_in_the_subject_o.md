### Administration

- The administration of a state involves maintaining law and order, collecting taxes, providing essential services, etc. The nature of administration has evolved over time in India:
- Village administration: Village assemblies called panchayats were responsible for managing village affairs. There were village officials like the headman (mukhiya or sarpanch), accountant (patwari), and watchman (chaukidar) to help administer the village.
- Feudal administration: Under feudal systems, lords and noblemen were granted territories which they administered in a hierarchical manner. For example, officers like amils and faujdars were appointed to administer districts and military contingents respectively.
- Centralised administration: The Chola, Maurya, and Mughal empires had centralised bureaucracies with officials like ministers, governors, tax collectors, judges, etc. appointed by the ruler to administer the empire. The Mughals introduced features like the mansabdar system and jagirdari system to administer their empire.
- Modern administration: The modern Indian administration is based on the colonial legacy and the Constitution. There is a clearly defined separation of powers between the executive, legislature, and judiciary. The bureaucracy follows a hierarchical system with defined roles and responsibilities for officials at the central, state, and local levels. However, there are challenges like corruption and inefficiency that the system faces.
