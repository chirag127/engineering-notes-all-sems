The Mahabharata is one of the two major Sanskrit epics of ancient India, the other being the Ramayana. It comprises over 100,000 shlokas or over 200,000 individual verse lines in 18 Parvas or sections. It is the longest epic poem in the world.

The core story of the Mahabharata revolves around the power struggles between the Pandavas and the Kauravas - two groups of royal cousins in the Kuru kingdom. The Pandavas, represented by Yudhisthir, Bhima, Arjuna, Nakula and Sahadeva, are the protagonists. The Kauravas are led by the eldest Duryodhan - who seeks to disrobe Draupadi in public, attempts to kill Bhima and hampers the Pandavas at several stages. The epic finally culminates at the Kurukshetra War - where the Pandavas defeat the Kauravas, but lose most of their warriors and relatives.

The Mahabharata is more than just an epic war story. It contains philosophical and devotional material, such as in the Bhagavad Gita, and Bhishma's instructions on statecraft in the Shanti Parva. It elaborates different theories of values, contains sections on cosmology and geography, and attaches significance to diverse Vedic practices and rituals.

Some key points about the Mahabharata:

- It is attributed to the sage Vyasa, who appears as a character in the epic.
- The core story revolves around the power struggles between the Pandavas and Kauravas.
- The Bhagavad Gita is a highly influential philosophical dialogue within the Mahabharata, reflecting on dharma and karma.
- It elaborates on values, philosophy and Vedic practices, containing over 100,000 individual verse lines and 18 Parvas or sections.
- The Kurukshetra War leads to massive destruction and the loss of lives of most protagonists. Only the Pandavas and two others survive.
- Different regional versions of the epic developed over time, including Tamil and Kannada versions. The Javanese Wayang style presents the epic through shadow theatre.
- The Mahabharata has inspired numerous later works, including plays, films, and other adaptations across the cultures of Asia.
