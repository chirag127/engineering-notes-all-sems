Mystical Theory:
- According to mystical theorists, the origin of the state lies in the supernatural. The state came into existence due to divine intervention.
- They believed that the king ruled by divine right and he was responsible to God. The subjects owed him absolute obedience as he was the representative of God on earth.
- In Indian tradition, the concept of divine origin of state is present in the Vedas. The Sanskrit term 'Raja' meaning king is derived from 'Rajan' which means one who illuminates. The king was regarded as a delegate of God (Devata).
- The concept of divine right of kings is also present in the Puranas and the Manu Smriti which emphasize that the king is a great god in human form. The duty of the subjects is unconditional allegiance and service to the king. Disobedience to the king is disobedience to God.
- The mystical theory has been used to strengthen the legitimacy and authority of the ruler. It discourages any questioning of the powers and authority of the ruler as it has divine sanction. However, this theory disregards the human element in the development of the state. It overlooks the historical process of struggle and conflict through which political institutions have evolved.

Advantages:
- It strengthens the legitimacy and authority of the ruler.
- It ensures obedience and allegiance of subjects to the ruler.

Disadvantages:
- It excludes the human element in the origin of the state.
- It overlooks the historical evolution of the state through conflict and struggles.
- It provides an unrealistic view about the origin of the state.

Examples: Divine origin of state as per Vedas and Puranas in Indian tradition. Divine right of kings in medieval Europe.
