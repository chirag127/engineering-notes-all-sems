### Sculptures

- Sculpture is a form of visual art that uses any three-dimensional medium to represent the human figure or other subject matter.
- In India, sculptures were made from various materials such as stone, bronze, terracotta, wood, etc.
- Indian sculpture has a long tradition and history dating back to the Indus Valley civilization.
- Some of the prominent sculptures in India are:

1. Mauryan Empire (322 BCE - 185 BCE): Key sculptures include the Lion Capital of Ashoka at Sarnath which has four Asiatic lions back to back. The 80 cm high sculpture was erected by Emperor Ashoka.
2. Gupta Empire (4th century CE - 6th century CE): Key sculptures include the Dashavatara Temple in Deogarh which has Vishnu sculptures and the Udayagiri and Khandagiri Caves which have Jain tirthankara sculptures.
3. Pallava Dynasty (6th century CE - 9th century CE): The shore temple in Mahabalipuram has intricate sculptures and reliefs depicting various deities and mythological scenes. The sculptures are known for their architectural grandeur and delicate carvings.
4. Chola Dynasty (9th century CE - 13th century CE): The Brihadeeswarar Temple in Thanjavur has sculptures of deities, dancers, and musicians embellishing its walls and towers. The Nataraja statue is a famous Chola sculpture depicting the cosmic dance of Shiva.

- The sculptures represent the artistic skills, religious beliefs, and cultural heritage of the respective eras. They provide insights into the society, culture, and mythology of ancient India.
- The sculptures are an important part of India's cultural heritage and attract tourists and art enthusiasts from around the world.
