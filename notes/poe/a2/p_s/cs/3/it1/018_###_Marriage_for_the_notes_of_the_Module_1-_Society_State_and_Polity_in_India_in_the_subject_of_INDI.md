#### Marriage

- Marriage is a socially sanctioned union between two individuals that establishes certain rights and obligations between the spouses. It is an institution that is prevalent in all societies and cultures.
- In India, marriage is not just a personal affair but a social obligation. Traditionally, marriages were arranged by the parents after considering social status, caste, religion, etc. of the families. However, love marriages have become more common in recent times.
- There are a variety of marriage practices prevalent in India such as monogamy, polygamy, arranged marriage, self-choice marriage, etc. The appropriate marriage practice depends on social norms, caste, and religion of a region or community.
- Solemnization of marriage - According to Hindu traditions, marriage is a sanskara (sacrament) and not a contract. The rituals and ceremonies are elaborate and differ based on region and caste. Some key rituals common to all Hindu marriages are - Kanyadaan, Panigrahana, Saptapadi, Mangalya dharana, Sindoor daan, etc.
- Functions and significance of marriage - Marriage serves many important functions in society such as procreation and raising children, regulation of sexual behavior, division of labor, mutual dependence, solidarity, and companionship. Marriage stabilizes the relationship between men and women and ensures social acceptance of sexual relationships and legitimate children.
- Cross-cousin marriages - Marriages with father's sister's daughter or mother's brother's daughter are prevalent among many tribal and peasant communities in South India. These marriages help in retaining property within the family and strengthening kinship bonds. However, cross-cousin marriages are prohibited among other groups as they are considered incestuous.
- Child marriages - Traditionally, child marriages were prevalent in many parts of India. Though now prohibited by law, the practice still persists in some rural and backward areas. Child marriages are harmful as they deny children their childhood, opportunities for education, and consent to choose their life partners.
