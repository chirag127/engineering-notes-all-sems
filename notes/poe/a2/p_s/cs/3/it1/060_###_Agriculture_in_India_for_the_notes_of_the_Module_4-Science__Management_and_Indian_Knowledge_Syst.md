## Agriculture in India

- India is an agriculture dominant country with about 60% of the population dependent on agriculture and contributes about 15% to the GDP.
- The Indian agricultural scenario is characterized by small and marginal land holdings, primitive technology, low productivity, and dependence on monsoon.
- The major crops produced in India are rice, wheat, millets, pulses, tea, coffee, sugarcane, oilseeds, etc.
   - Rice and wheat are the primary food crops.
   - Millets like jowar, bajra, and ragi are drought-resistant and grown in dry regions.
   - Pulses like tur, gram, and lentils are rich in proteins.
   - Cash crops like cotton, jute, sugarcane, and oilseeds are commercial crops.
   - Tea and coffee are plantation crops mainly grown in hilly areas.
- The Green Revolution in the 1960s led to the adoption of high-yielding variety seeds, fertilizers, pesticides, and increased use of irrigation which boosted the production of wheat and rice.
- However, the green revolution has also led to lowered genetic diversity, soil degradation, and over-reliance on external inputs.
- Sustainable agricultural practices like organic farming, agroforestry, and integrated farming need to be promoted to address these issues.
- Government schemes like PM-KISAN, e-NAM, and agricultural subsidies help support farmers by providing income support, access to markets, and subsidized inputs.
- More investment in agriculture, farmer welfare, and modernization of practices can help strengthen India's food security and boost the agricultural economy.
