## Pre-Vedic Religion

- Harappan civilization had flourished in the Indus Valley from 3300 BCE to 1300 BCE. The Harappans worshipped a Mother Goddess and a male horned deity.
- There was a prevalence of fertility symbols and worship of sacred trees, animals and natural objects.
- Burial of the dead with goods and utensils indicates a belief in an afterlife.
- The decline of the Harappan civilization led to the development of new religious ideas and practices, which ultimately evolved into the Vedic religion.

## Vedic Religion

- The Vedas are the foundational scriptures of Hinduism and were composed between 1500 BCE to 600 BCE.
- The early Vedic religion was primarily centered around worship of the elements and forces of nature through yajnas or sacrifices. Agni (fire), Indra (rain and thunder) and Soma (intoxicating plant juice) were the most important deities.
- The later Vedic period saw the rise in prominence of Brahmins (priests), the Varna system based on birth, and the ideas of samsara (cycle of rebirth) and moksha (liberation). The concepts of karma and dharma also emerged in this period.
- Upanishads constituted the philosophical portions of the Vedas and expounded ideas such as Brahman, Atman and the path of knowledge for attaining moksha. This shift from ritualism to philosophy marked the transition from the Vedic religion to Hinduism.
