### Dance

- Dance is a performing art form consisting of purposefully selected sequences of human movement. Dance can be categorized and described by its choreography, by its repertoire of movements, or by its historical period or place of origin.
- Dance may also be regarded as a form of nonverbal communication or expression, and is often accompanied by music and may or may not be performance focused.
- Dance holds great relevance in Indian culture and traditions. The Natya Shastra, an ancient Indian treatise on the performing arts, initially crafted by sage Bharata Muni between 200 BCE and 200 CE, is the foundational treatise on performing arts in India, including dance.
- Some well-known forms of Indian classical dance include:

- Bharatanatyam from Tamil Nadu - characterized by intricate footwork, sculpturesque poses, and expressions of emotions.
- Kathak from Uttar Pradesh - characterized by fast rhythmic footwork, spins, and storytelling through hand gestures and facial expressions.
- Odissi from Odisha - characterized by the predominance of Tribhangi (literally "three parts break"), in which the body is shifted into three parts to form beautiful curves.
- Kuchipudi from Andhra Pradesh - characterized by a mix of graceful movements and sharp, angular bends and turns.
- Manipuri from Manipur - characterized by slow, soft, and fluid movements, with an emphasis on devotion and spirituality.

- Each classical dance form represent a unique school with its own design vocabulary and technique. The differences arise from unique cultural influences and traditions of the regions from where they originate. In addition to classical Indian dance, there are many folk dance forms that vary on the basis of local traditions of different states and regions in India.

- Advantages:
- Preserves Indian culture and traditions.
- Requires discipline and skill.
- Provides artistic expression and physical fitness.
- Applications: Performances, celebrations, religious ceremonies, etc.
