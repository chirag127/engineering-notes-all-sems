## Drama

- Drama is a performing art form consisting of a story enacted on stage for an audience. It has dialogue and action and is often combined with music and dance.
- Indian drama has its origins in religious ritual and festival performances of Sanskrit plays. The major types of traditional Indian drama are:

**Natya:** Dance-drama typically performed in temples or courts, based on Hindu mythological stories. Includes classical Indian dance forms.

**Ramlila:** Drama depicting the life of Lord Rama, staged annually around Dussehra festival. Includes song, dance and dialogue.

**Nautanki:** Colorful folk theatre popular in North India, often presenting dramatized versions of local legends. Uses vivid costumes and makeup and loud musical accompaniment.

**Tamasha:** Lively traditional Maharashtrian folk theatre using music, songs and satirical comedy to enact fairy tales and local stories.

- Traditional Indian drama has influenced and shaped modern drama in the country. Modern Indian theatre explores social and political issues, uses both traditional and Western forms, and can include multimedia, physical theatre, etc. Well-known playwrights include Girish Karnad and Vijay Tendulkar.

- Key points:
- Origins in religious festivals and Sanskrit plays
- Variety of regional drama forms with song, dance, music, and dialogue
- Both traditional and modern Indian theatre address contemporary issues
- Influenced by both Indian and Western theatrical forms
