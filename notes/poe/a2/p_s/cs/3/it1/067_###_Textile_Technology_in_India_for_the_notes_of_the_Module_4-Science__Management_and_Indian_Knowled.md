### Textile Technology in India

- India has a long history of textile production and technology. Indian textiles were a key driver of the industrial revolution in Britain.
- The Indus Valley civilization and Mohenjo-Daro possessed great textile technology. Cotton was spun and woven into cloth using spindle and looms. Evidence of dyed fabric has also been found.
- During Gupta and post-Gupta period, textile technology and trade flourished. Gurjara-Pratiharas and Palas patronized textile production. Fine muslin and silk fabrics were produced.
- In South India, textile production was prominent in Coromandel and Malabar coasts. South produced heavier and wider clothes. Many historic indigenous techniques like Kattari, Pattedu khadi, and kasuti embroidery were popular.
- Mughal period saw growth in cotton textile production. New techniques like kalabattun were adopted. Finely woven jamdani and kinkhwab with ornate embellishments were produced.
- Handloom sector dominated textile production in India until the industrial revolution when mechanical mills were introduced by British. This led to a decline of indigenous techniques and artisans.
- However, handloom sector still employs many people in India. Government supports & preserves this sector through schemes and GI tags for historic indigenous textiles. Many artisanal clusters actively work on traditional techniques.

Advantages:
- Preservation of indigenous knowledge and art forms.
- Employment generation in rural areas.
- Production of high-quality niche fabrics.

Disadvantages:
- Unable to meet mass market demand due to low production.
- Threat from power loom and mill sector.
- Lack of technological upgradation and innovation.
