### Water Management in India

- Water management in India has been practiced since ancient times. Many traditional water harvesting structures are still in use today.
- Some of the traditional structures are:
- - Tanks: Tanks were man-made reservoirs used for irrigation and drinking water supply. Earthen bunds were used to store rainwater.
- - Wells: Wells were an important source of groundwater. They used the principle of hydrostatic pressure to tap groundwater.
- - Canals: Canals were built to divert water from rivers/lakes to agricultural fields. Canal irrigation was highly efficient and the canals were well maintained.
- Ancient texts like Arthashastra by Kautilya mention rules and regulations for construction and maintenance of water structures.
- The Indus Valley Civilization was also highly skilled in water management. They had a sophisticated system of drains and reservoirs.
- In modern India, water management is handled by government bodies like Central Water Commission, state irrigation departments, etc. However, lack of maintenance of traditional structures and over-exploitation of groundwater has led to water scarcity in many parts.
- Some key steps needed are:
- - Restoration of traditional structures like tanks, wells, etc.
- - Rainwater harvesting to be mandatory for all new constructions.
- - Efficient irrigation methods and drip irrigation to be promoted.
- - Groundwater recharge and regulation of groundwater use.
- - Reduction in water leakage and efficient usage of available water.
- Following the traditional practices and incorporating modern technologies can help achieve sustainable water management in India.
