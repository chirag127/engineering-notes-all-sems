### State in Ancient India

- The concept of state emerged in the Vedic period when tribal chieftains started consolidating their power through military strength and ritual authority.
- The Vedic king (Rajan) was initially a tribal chieftain but later acquired divine sanctity and supreme authority. The concept of divine right of kings i.e king getting his authority from the gods became popular.
- The Rigveda refers to a tribal structure headed by a chieftain called Vispati or Vrisha who led tribal wars. The later Vedic period witnessed the rise of powerful kingdoms headed by Rajan and Samrat.
- The Upanishads elevated the status of kings to gods on earth or Kshatriyas. The Sukraniti and Manudharma-Shastra laid emphasis on absolute monarchy and divine right theory of kings.
- The famous empires in ancient India such as the Mauryan empire and the Gupta empire had highly centralized administrations with the emperor enjoying supreme power. The concept of personal rule of the monarch i.e. ruler takes all decisions himself became popular.
- Evidences of local self-government institutions like Sangha, Gana, and Puga were found from the 6th century BCE which gradually declined with the rise of large empires. These institutions assisted chieftains in administration.
- Some characteristics of ancient Indian state were divine monarchies, growth of large territorial empires, prevalence of socialism, and gradual decline of local institutions. The epics and scriptures greatly contributed to strengthening the concept of divine monarchy.
