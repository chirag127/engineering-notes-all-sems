### Āshrama or the Stages of Life

According to Hindu traditions, an individual's life was divided into four Āshramas or stages:

1. Brahmacharya Āshrama - The student stage: In this first stage of life spanning approximately 25 years, a person undergoes education and training to prepare for adult life. Celibacy and service to teachers are expected during this period.

2. Grihastha Āshrama - The householder stage: This is the longest stage where one gets married, raises a family and becomes a productive member of society. Householder duties and earning a livelihood are the main focus here.

3. Vānaprastha Āshrama - The forest dweller stage: In this penultimate stage starting around age 50, one starts withdrawing from worldly affairs, gives up marital life and lives a life of austerity and meditation. One prepares for the final stage of renunciation.

4. Sannyāsa Āshrama - The renouncer stage: In the final stage, one renounces all worldly attachments and devotes oneself completely to achieving liberation or Moksha through meditation and asceticism. One becomes a wandering ascetic with no home or possessions.

The four stages were designed to allow an individual to fulfil his/her debts or duties to oneself, family, society and God respectively. Successful progression through the stages was believed to lead to the ultimate spiritual goal of liberation. However, in practice not all individuals went through all the stages. The Āshrama system reflected the emphasis on dharma or righteous duty in Hindu traditions.
