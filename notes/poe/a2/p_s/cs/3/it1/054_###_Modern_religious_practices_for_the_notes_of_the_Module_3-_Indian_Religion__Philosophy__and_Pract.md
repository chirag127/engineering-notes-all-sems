### Modern religious practices

- Reform movements: Several reform movements emerged in the 19th and 20th centuries to remove social evils and make Hinduism more rational and egalitarian. Some important movements were Brahmo Samaj, Arya Samaj, Prarthana Samaj and Ramakrishna Mission. These movements rejected practices like idol worship, caste distinctions and excessive rituals. They promoted widow remarriage, inter-caste marriage and women education.
- Neo-Hinduism: Neo-Hinduism refers to a religious reform and reconstruction within Hinduism that developed in response to western colonialism during the 19th century. Neo-Vedanta, a modern interpretation of selected aspects of Vedanta philosophy, gained popularity among Western audiences interested in Indian religions. It was used as a defense against Christian missionary critiques of Hinduism. Notable proponents of Neo-Vedanta include Ram Mohan Roy, Swami Vivekananda and Sarvepalli Radhakrishnan.
- New religious movements: Several new religious movements emerged in the 20th century such as Brahma Kumaris, ISKCON, Osho movement, etc. They blend traditional Hindu beliefs with modern values and practices and have followers across the world. These movements focused on meditation, yoga and spiritual wellbeing. They popularized Hindu practices like vegetarianism, simple living and mantra chanting in the west.
- Influence of Hindu nationalism: The rise of Hindu nationalism in the 20th century shaped the modern practice of Hinduism to a large extent. Hindu nationalist organizations like RSS and VHP promoted a militant brand of Hindutva that identifies India as a culturally Hindu nation and Indian civilization as fundamentally Hindu civilization. They campaigned to build Ram temple in Ayodhya and ban cow slaughter. This has influenced the beliefs and practices of many Hindus.
