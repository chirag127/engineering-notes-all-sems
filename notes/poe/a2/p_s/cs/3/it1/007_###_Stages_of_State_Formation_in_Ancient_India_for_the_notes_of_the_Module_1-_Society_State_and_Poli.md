### Stages of State Formation in Ancient India

The process of state formation in Ancient India went through following stages:

1. Tribal stage - In the initial stage, people lived in small tribal communities. There was no central authority and decisions were taken collectively in tribal councils called 'Sabhas' and 'Samiti'.
2. Chiefdom stage - As population increased and settlements grew into villages, tribes started electing chiefs for coordinating activities. The chief derived power from the tribe and did not have absolute power. This was the chiefdom stage.
3. Kingdom stage - Powerful chiefs started turning into monarchs and hereditary rulers. They acquired control over resources and established administrative systems. This led to the rise of kingdoms of varying sizes. This was the kingdom stage of state formation.
4. Empire stage - Some kings were able to defeat others and bring large territories under their control through wars and alliances. This led to the rise of large empires like Mauryan empire and Gupta empire. The emperor had supreme authority and an elaborate administrative system was established. This was the empire stage of state formation.

The stages were not strictly sequential and multiple stages existed simultaneously at different places. State formation was driven by factors like growth in population, control over resources, wars, and alliances. The emergence of authoritative structures led to loss of autonomy for tribes and local communities. However, village assemblies continued to play an important role even under kingdoms and empires.
