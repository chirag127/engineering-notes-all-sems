## Indian Cinema

Indian cinema is one of the largest and most diverse film industries in the world. It consists of films produced in many of the nation's regional languages. As per the Central Board of Film Certification of India, around 1000 films in various languages of India are produced annually. Indian cinema comprises major film producing regions:

-   Hindi cinema or Bollywood based in Mumbai which produces Hindi language films
-   Tamil cinema based in Chennai which produces Tamil language films
-   Telugu cinema based in Hyderabad which produces Telugu language films
-   Bengali cinema based in Kolkata which produces Bengali language films
-   Malayalam cinema based in Kochi which produces Malayalam language films
-   Kannada cinema based in Bangalore which produces Kannada language films
-   Marathi cinema based in Pune which produces Marathi language films

Themes in Indian cinema are highly melodramatic and song-and-dance numbers are common in films of the major regional traditions. Themes such as religion, poverty, social issues, family drama, comedy, romance etc. are popular in Indian films. Indian films have gained recognition worldwide and have been screened at major film festivals around the world. They have won many prestigious awards including Academy Awards and Golden Globes. Popular stars and directors transcend regional boundaries and work in many languages. World cinema has influenced Indian cinema in terms of cinematography and plot themes.

The evolution of Indian cinema began with the Bengali film industry in Tollygunge, Calcutta, in 1899. The first full-length feature was the Bengali film Jamai Shashthi, made in 1931. The first Indian sound film was Alam Ara (1931). In the early 1900s, parallel cinema emphasizing authenticity and realism sprouted as an alternative to the mainstream commercial cinema aimed at the masses. The Indian government extended financial support and recognition to films deemed artistically valuable. The "Apu" trilogy (1955???1959), directed by Satyajit Ray and written by Ray and Bibhutibhushan Bandopadhyay, is renowned in world cinema for its artistic excellence. The Hindi film industry produced the cult classics Mother India (1957) and Sholay (1975). Directors such as Guru Dutt, Raj Kapoor, Bimal Roy, Satyajit Ray and Ritwik Ghatak shaped the golden age of Indian cinema.
