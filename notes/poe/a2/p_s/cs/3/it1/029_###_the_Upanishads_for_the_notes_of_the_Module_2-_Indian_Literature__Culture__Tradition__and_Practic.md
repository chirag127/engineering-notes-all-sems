The Upanishads are a collection of ancient Indian spiritual texts that contain some of the central philosophical concepts and ideas of Hinduism, some of which are shared with religious traditions like Buddhism and Jainism. The Upanishads are considered to contain revealed truths or transcendent knowledge and are largely about the nature of ultimate reality (brahman) and the human being.

The Upanishads date from roughly 800 to 200 BCE, and are typically embedded as part of the concluding portions of the Brahmanas and Aranyakas.

Key ideas and teachings of the Upanishads include:

- Brahman: The Upanishads conceptualize brahman as the eternal, infinite, omnipresent spiritual source of the universe. Brahman is the unchanging reality amidst and beyond the world, which is constantly changing. Brahman is regarded as the essence of an individual's soul or atman.
- Atman: The Upanishads develop the concept of atman or self, which they describe as brahman embodied in the individual. The quest for liberation or Self-knowledge is knowing one's "soul" or atman to be identical with brahman.
- Karma and Rebirth: The Upanishads assume the concepts of karma and rebirth, and that liberation from the cycles of rebirth is achievable through knowledge and meditation on the identity of atman and brahman.
- Moksha: The ultimate goal of Upanishadic philosophy is liberation or freedom (moksha) from the cycle of death and rebirth by realizing the identity of atman and brahman. This is achieved through spiritual knowledge and understanding, not through ritual acts or sacrifices.
- Non-duality: The Upanishads teach the ultimate identity of all Reality; that atman and brahman are one and the same. This is expressed through statements such as "Thou art that" (tat tvam asi). This concept of nondualism has been very influential in Indian thought.

The Upanishads have inspired many later philosophical traditions, and their ideas have influenced disciples and scholars such as Adi Shankara, Ramanuja, and Madhvacharya. They continue to be widely read and studied in Hinduism.
