Evolutionary Theory:
- According to this theory, society evolves from simple to complex form. The driving force for this evolution is the increasing division of labour and consequent specialization.
- The development of agriculture, the rise of private property and accumulation of surplus gave way to the formation of social classes and a state to regulate the society. Thus, society evolves through stages: savagery, barbarism and finally civilization.
- Key proponents: Nineteenth century evolutionists like Herbert Spencer and Lewis Henry Morgan.
- Critics argue that this theory builds on assumptions of cultural superiority and linear progress which are not reflective of how societies evolve. There is no single path of development and evolution can happen through multiple trajectories.
- Advantages: Provides a broad sweep of historical changes; focuses on the impact of material factors and economic changes in societal transformation.
- Disadvantages: Premised on ethnocentric and culturally biased ideas of progress being achievable only through western ideals.
- Examples: The transition from primitive tribal societies to agrarian civilizations to industrial societies as evidence of evolution. The caste system in India as arising out of division of labour.
- Applications: Understanding broad patterns of historical societal changes. However, it does not sufficiently recognize agency, cultural differences and alternative paths of development. Hence, the theory has limited applicability.
