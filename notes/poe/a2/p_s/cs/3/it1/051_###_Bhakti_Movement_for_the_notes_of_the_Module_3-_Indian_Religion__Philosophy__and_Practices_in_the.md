### Bhakti Movement

- The Bhakti movement was a revivalist movement in medieval India (roughly between the 7th and 17th centuries CE) that emphasized religious devotion and love of a personal god or goddess.
- It emerged in South India in the 7th-9th centuries and spread northwards, reaching its peak between the 15th and 17th centuries.
- The movement was a rejection of the rigid hierarchical notions of the caste system and the ritualistic emphasis of the Brahmanic temple worship.
- Instead, it championed an intensely personal and emotional devotion to a chosen deity, giving status to all people regardless of gender, caste or social position. Anyone could choose their ishta-devata or 'cherished deity' and worship through singing, chanting, meditation and other practices.
- Important Bhakti saints and poets include the Alvars and Nayanars of South India, and figures such as Ramananda, Kabir, Mirabai, Tulsidas, Surdas, Meera and Tukaram from North India.
- The movement led to a rich devotional literature of songs, poems and stories in regional languages, as well as temple-building by royal patrons. It had a major impact on Indian religion, crossing boundaries between Hinduism, Sikhism and Islam.
- The major themes of Bhakti were: complete surrender to and love for the deity; seeing all beings as equal before God; rejection of rituals and priests; and the idea that spiritual liberation could be attained in this life by following the path of Bhakti.
