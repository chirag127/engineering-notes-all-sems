### India’s Dominance up to Pre-colonial Times

- India was one of the leading economies of the world up to the 18th century. It contributed to almost 25% of the world GDP.
- India was a major exporter of textiles, spices, indigo dye, sugar, etc. Indian textiles, especially cotton textiles, were highly sought after in Europe, Middle East and South East Asia.
- The Indian subcontinent was a major centre of trade and commerce. Goods were traded within the country as well as with other countries through land and sea routes. Major trade routes included the Silk Route connecting India with Central Asia and the Spice Route connecting India with Middle East and Europe via sea.
- The Indus Valley Civilization, dating back to 3000 BCE, had a planned urban civilization with advanced engineering skills. They had trade links with Mesopotamia.
- The Mauryan empire under Ashoka in 3rd century BCE further expanded trade, communication and economic activities across South and South East Asia.
- The Gupta empire between 4th to 6th centuries CE saw developments in science, technology, engineering, art, literature, mathematics, astronomy, religion and philosophy. This is considered as the Golden Age of India.
- The Pallava, Chola and Vijayanagara empires between 6th century CE to 16th century CE were major naval powers that dominated sea trade in the Indian Ocean region. They had trade links with South East Asia, Middle East, Greece and Rome.
- India was a major centre of manufacturing and production of cotton and silk textiles, steel, etc. using advanced techniques. This led to flourishing trade of finished goods within India and with other countries.
- The decimal number system, zero, trigonometry, algebra and calculus originated in India. These mathematical concepts were later adopted by the Arabs and Europeans.
- Ayurveda and Siddha were traditional Indian medicinal systems that were holistic in nature. Many medicines and surgical procedures were derived from these systems.
- The Indian society was characterized by efficient administration, agricultural surplus, commercialization and urbanization up to the pre-colonial times. However, the colonial rule disrupted this dominance.
