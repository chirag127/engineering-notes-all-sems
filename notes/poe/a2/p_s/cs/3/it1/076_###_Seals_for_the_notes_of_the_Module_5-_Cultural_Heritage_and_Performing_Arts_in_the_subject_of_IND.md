### Seals for the notes of the Module 5- Cultural Heritage and Performing Arts in the subject of INDIAN TRADITIONS, CULTURAL AND SOCIETY

- Seals were engraved stones used to create an impression on clay or ink. They were used as a form of signature to authenticate documents or goods.
- The Harappan civilization used engraved seals for trade and administration. The seals were usually made of steatite and had animal motifs or symbols. The unicorn is a prominent motif found on many seals. The seals provide insights into the possible religious beliefs and trade networks of the Harappan civilization.
- The Mauryan empire used seals to authenticate edicts and documents. The edicts of Ashoka have been found inscribed on rocks and pillars along with an impression of the royal seal. The seals had symbols of Indian royalty like the elephant, horse, and the mythical lion.
- Temples and monasteries used seals to authenticate records and documents. The seals had the emblem or symbol of the institution and the name of the head of the institution. For example, seals from the Nalanda university had the name of the head of the institution and the emblem of a Dharma Chakra.
- Advantages: Authenticated documents and goods, Prevented forgery, Provided identification
- Disadvantages: Could be copied by skilled engravers diminishing effectiveness
- Applications: Trade, Administration, Record keeping
