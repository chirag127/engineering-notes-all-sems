Slavery refers to the condition of a person over whom any or all of the powers attaching to the right of ownership are exercised. In other words, slaves were treated as the property of their owners and had to work for them without consent and often under threat of punishment.

In ancient India, slavery was prevalent and slaves were mainly prisoners of war, debtors unable to repay loans, and children sold by poor parents. The slaves had limited rights and were treated as the property of their masters. They had to work in the households or fields of their owners without wages.

The rights of the slaves were limited under the Hindu law. The masters had the power to transfer or sell their slaves. However, the killing or torturing of slaves was prohibited. The slaves could earn and accumulate property but that would belong to the master. They could not marry without the permission of their owner and their children would also become slaves.

The practice of slavery declined over time. The spread of Jainism and Buddhism emphasizing equality and non-violence contributed to the decline of slavery. The large-scale demand for slaves during the Muslim rule again revived slavery to some extent in India. Slavery was finally abolished in India during British rule in the 19th century.

The key points to note about slavery in ancient India are:

- Slaves had limited rights and were treated as the property of masters
- Sources of slaves were mainly war prisoners, debtors, and children sold by poor parents
- Slaves had to work without wages and could not marry/earn property without master's permission
- Slavery declined with spread of Jainism/Buddhism but revived during Muslim rule
- Slavery was finally abolished in 19th century under the British rule
