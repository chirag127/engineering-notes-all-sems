### Six Systems of Indian Philosophy
The six systems or schools of Indian philosophy are:

1. Samkhya: Dualism; matter and spirit are eternal and distinct. Liberation is achieved by separation of soul from matter.
2. Yoga: Goal is liberation of the soul through concentration and meditation. Follows Samkhya metaphysics but believes in God.
3. Nyaya: Emphasizes knowledge and logic as the path to liberation. Proposes steps to obtain valid knowledge and challenge wrong beliefs.
4. Vaisheshika: Proposes atomism and views all objects in the universe as resulting from the aggregation of atoms. Liberation is achieved through correct understanding of the nature of the self and the world.
5. Mimamsa: Centered on the proper understanding and performance of Vedic ritual as the path to liberation. Later emphasised understanding dharma as key to liberation.
6. Vedanta: Considered the culmination of Upanishadic thought. Proposes that brahman or ultimate reality is the basis of the world and the self, and liberation is achieved by correct understanding of the identity of brahman and atman.

The key characteristics and areas of difference between the six systems were:
- Metaphysical concepts like the nature of reality and existence of God
- Epistemology or the theory of knowledge and its validity
- Soteriology or the nature of liberation and the path to achieve it
- Ethics or the basis for moral principles and values

The systems originated from roughly the 7th century BCE to the 5th century CE and fed into each other, leading to both synergies and debates. They represent the sophisticated philosophical tradition of ancient and classical India.
