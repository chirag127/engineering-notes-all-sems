## Module 1- Society State and Polity in India

- Indian society is diverse and complex with many ethnic, linguistic, regional, economic and religious groups. The diverse nature of Indian society has a bearing on its political structure and processes.
- The Indian state is a sovereign, secular, democratic republic that derives its sovereignty from the people. The Constitution of India establishes certain institutions and principles that shape the polity of India - parliamentary system of government, separation of powers, fundamental rights, directive principles of state policy, etc.
- The Preamble to the Constitution expresses the aspirations of the people of India - justice (social, economic and political), liberty (of thought, expression, belief, faith and worship), equality (of status and opportunity) and fraternity (assuring the dignity of the individual).
- The Constitution has established a parliamentary system of government at the Centre and in the states. The Prime Minister is the head of the government and the President is the head of the state. The President is elected by an electoral college comprising elected members of Parliament and state legislatures. The President can exercise executive and judicial powers and is the supreme commander of the defence forces.
- The legislature comprises the Lok Sabha (House of the People) and the Rajya Sabha (Council of States). The Lok Sabha is directly elected by the people for a term of 5 years. The Rajya Sabha has up to 250 members indirectly elected by the state legislatures for 6 years.Together, the two houses make laws, control the budget, and provide a forum for deliberation on public issues.
- The Supreme Court is the highest judicial authority in India and has the power to declare laws passed by Parliament as unconstitutional if they contravene the provisions of the Constitution. Below the Supreme Court are High Courts at the state level and subordinate courts at the district and lower levels. The doctrine of judicial review empowers the courts to determine the conformity of laws and executive actions to the Constitution.
