### Sufi Movement

- The Sufi movement was a mystical movement within Islam that emphasized the personal relationship between the devotee and God. It originated in medieval Persia and Iraq in the 8th to 10th centuries CE.
- The Sufis sought to attain a direct, intuitive experience of the sacred through emotional devotion, rather than focusing on rigorous ritual or legalistic aspects of Islam. They believed that such an experience of the divine was available to all, regardless of gender, religious background, or ethnicity.
- Key Sufi practices included dhikr (the remembrance or recitation of the names of God), sama (divine listening or audition), and the veneration of Sufi saints and shrines. Poetry, music, and dance were important means of cultivating an ecstatic abandonment and union with the divine.
- Important themes in Sufi thought included fana (the annihilation of the self in the divine), baqa (subsistence or immortality in the divine), tawhid (the oneness of existence), and ishq (divine love). Prominent Sufi orders included the Chishti, Qadiri, Naqshbandi, and Shadhili orders.
- The Sufi tradition was influential across the Islamic world and also attracted followers from other faiths in South Asia. Its emphasis on transcending outward differences and cultivating inner spirituality appealed to many. The Sufi shrines and festivals of South Asia are still popular today, though some practices are controversial with Islamic orthodoxy.
