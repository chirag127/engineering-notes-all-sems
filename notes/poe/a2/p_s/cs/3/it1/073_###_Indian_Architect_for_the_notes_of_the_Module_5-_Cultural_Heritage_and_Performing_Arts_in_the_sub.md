Indian Architecture refers to the style of architecture which has developed in India over the centuries and displays distinctive characteristics and styles. It has evolved into a blend of aesthetics, construction techniques, craftsmanship, artistry and ornamentation with strong cultural roots but also reflects foreign influences adapted for Indian sensibilities.

Some key points about Indian Architecture:

- Indian architecture is rooted in Vastu Shastra, which means "science of construction" in Sanskrit. It is an ancient collection of texts that lay down the principles of architecture and design. It provides guidelines for town planning, spatial layout of cities and appropriate locations and structures for temples.
- Temple architecture dominated and is highly evolved. Elaborate stone temple complexes were built starting around the 4th century CE. Notable examples include the Khajuraho Group of Monuments and the Sun Temple at Konark. The temple plans became increasingly complex with halls, courtyards and towering superstructures called Gopurams.
- Stupas were initially small hemispherical structures containing Buddha's relics but evolved into larger elongated domed structures with railings and gateways. Notable examples include the Great Stupa at Sanchi and the Amaravati Stupa.
- Forts, palaces and havelis (mansions) demonstrate distinctive periods in architectural styles influenced by Rajput, Mughal and colonial British styles. Notable examples include palaces in Udaipur and Jaipur, Hampi ruins and the havelis in Shekhawati region.
- The use of local materials and regional differences resulted in distinctive styles in various parts of India - eg. the style of Tamil Nadu temples, Orissan style of Odisha and the Indo-Islamic style in northern India. The modern Indian architecture blends traditional elements with more contemporary styles.

The key advantages of Indian Architecture are its artistic grandeur, utilization of natural resources and sustainability. However, many ancient structures have been lost or are under threat due to lack of preservation. Indian Architecture has had a profound influence on architectural styles around the world and continues to evolve while retaining its rich cultural roots.
