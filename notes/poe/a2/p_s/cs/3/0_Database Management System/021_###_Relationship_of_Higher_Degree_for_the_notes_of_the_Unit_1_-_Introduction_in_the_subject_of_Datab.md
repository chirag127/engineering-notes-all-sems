**Relationship of Higher Degree**:

- When a relation can be connected to more than one entity of the same type, it results in a relationship of higher degree.
- For example, one employee can report to more than one manager. This is a relationship of higher degree between employee and manager entity sets.
- Represented using a 'crow's foot' at the many end of the relationship.
- Degree of relationship refers to the number of entity sets that can be associated with one member of the other entity set.
- Higher degree relationships lead to creation of more tables and more complex database design.
- Examples:
    - One department can have multiple employees (Department -> Employee).
    - One employee can have multiple skills (Employee -> Skill).
    - One project can have multiple programmers (Project -> Programmer).

Advantages: Accurately represents real-world relationships.
Disadvantages: Can make database design more complex with more tables and joins.

Applications: Used when an entity can be associated with multiple instances of another entity. Commonly seen in employee-project allocation, medical patient-doctor allocation, etc.
