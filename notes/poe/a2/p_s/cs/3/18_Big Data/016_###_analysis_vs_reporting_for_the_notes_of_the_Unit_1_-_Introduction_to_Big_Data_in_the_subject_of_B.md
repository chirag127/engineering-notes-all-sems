### Analysis vs Reporting

- Analysis refers to the process of inspecting and transforming data to extract useful information and derive insights from it. It involves applying statistical techniques and data mining methods to understand trends and patterns in the data that can be used to make business decisions.
- Reporting refers to the process of organizing and presenting data in a summarized format for easy understanding. It involves creating data visualizations, charts, and reports to convey insights and information to stakeholders.
- Analysis is thus focused on discovering insights while reporting is focused on communication. Both are important steps in data processing and are often used together.
- Analysis can lead to reporting and vice versa. For example, analysis can reveal key metrics and trends which can then be represented in reports. Reports can also highlight anomalies or areas that require deeper analysis.
- Some advantages of analysis are obtaining hidden patterns, finding causal relationships, predicting outcomes, and automated decision making. Some advantages of reporting are easy understanding, spotting deviations, and informing business actions.
- Examples of analysis techniques include clustering, regression, and association rule learning. Examples of reporting techniques include data visualization, dashboards, and static/interactive reports.
- Both analysis and reporting use tools and techniques to extract value from data. They are complementary processes that can be applied sequentially or iteratively in a data processing pipeline to gain insights and enable data-driven decisions.
