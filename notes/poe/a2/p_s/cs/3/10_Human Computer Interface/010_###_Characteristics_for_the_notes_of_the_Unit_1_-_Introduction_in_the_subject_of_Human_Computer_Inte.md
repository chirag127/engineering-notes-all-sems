### Introduction to Software Engineering

1. Software Engineering is an engineering discipline that is concerned with all aspects of software production.
2. It applies engineering principles to software development.
3. It involves a systematic team-based approach to develop, operate, maintain, and retire software.
4. The goal is to produce software that is reliable, robust, and efficient.
5. Software Engineering processes include specification, design, development, testing, and maintenance.
6. Requirements analysis is conducted to determine what the software should do.
7. Software design outlines the software structure, identifies modules, and determines interfaces.
8. Implementation involves coding in a programming language.
9. Software testing verifies and validates that the software meets specifications and identifies and removes bugs.
10. Maintenance continues to improve the software after it is deployed.

### Characteristics for the notes of the Unit 1 - Introduction in the subject of Human Computer Interface

1. Human Computer Interface (HCI) is the study of how humans interact with computers and to what extent computers are or are not developed for successful interaction with human beings.
2. The goal of HCI is to design interfaces that allow effective, efficient and satisfying interaction between humans and computers.
3. HCI involves studying and designing interfaces to make them easy to use, useful and enjoyable for the user.
4. HCI principles include consistency, clarity, feedback, error handling, simplicity, accessibility and flexibility.
5. Areas of HCI application include software, web design, mobile computing and technical writing.
6. Benefits of easy to use interfaces include increased user productivity, satisfaction and safety.
7. Examples of HCI include graphical user interfaces, touch interfaces and voice user interfaces.
