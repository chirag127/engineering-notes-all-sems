### Screen planning and purpose for the notes of the Unit 3 - Screen Designing in the subject of Human Computer Interface

 Screen planning is an important step in the screen designing process for software or websites. It involves the following steps:

1. Identifying the screens and their purpose: Firstly, all the screens required for the software or website are identified along with their main purpose and function. This could include login screens, home screens, detail screens, edit screens, etc. The purpose of each screen is defined clearly.
2. Grouping related screens: Then, related screens are grouped together in a flow. For example, all the screens related to user management are grouped together. The flow of screens and navigation between screens is defined.
3. Prioritizing important screens: The most important screens are then prioritized. These are the key screens on which the user would spend the maximum time or screens that are critical for the software or website functionality.
4. Defining relationships between screens: The relationship between screens, including the navigational paths and data flow, is defined in the screen planning process. This ensures consistency and coherence between the screens.
5. Estimating approximate layouts: The approximate layouts of the screens are estimated in terms of sections and key elements. The rough positioning of elements is defined. This acts as a starting point for the subsequent screen designing process.

Screen planning is important as it provides a blueprint for the screen designing process and ensures that all screens are designed consistently and coherently to provide the best user experience. It forms the basis for the actual designing of the screens.

### Similarity and Differences from Conventional Engineering Processes

The screen designing process for software or websites has some similarities as well as differences from conventional engineering design processes:

Similarities:

- Both involve planning and defining requirements or specifications initially.
- Both are iterative processes, involving testing and refining the designs.
- Both aim to solve a problem within constraints.

Differences:

- Screen designing focuses on the layout and UI design, whereas conventional engineering design focuses more on technical specifications.
- The medium of design is software and screens, rather than physical systems and components.
- The parameters to optimize are user experience metrics like usability and accessibility rather than just technical metrics.
- The design constraints revolve more around interface guidelines, branding, and user needs rather than just technical feasibility and costs.
- The pace of change is faster, requiring designs to be flexible and scalable.

Thus, while there are some similarities in the overall process, screen designing has distinct differences in terms of the nature of the design and the parameters that are optimized. The differences are due to the focus on user experience and software interfaces rather than physical systems.
