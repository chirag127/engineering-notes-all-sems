The Spiral Model is an iterative software development process. It is a combination of iterative development process model and sequential linear development model with very high emphasis on risk analysis. The Spiral Model of the Systems Development Life Cycle organizes the software development effort into distinct phases including planning, risk analysis, engineering and evaluation.

The Spiral Model has the following phases:

1. Planning - Requirements are gathered and risk is assessed. A plan for conducting the next iteration is established.
2. Risk Analysis - Identify and resolve potential risks, uncertainties and issues of the project.
3. Engineering - Software is designed, developed and tested.
4. Evaluation - The output of the phase is evaluated for performance and risks.

The iterations continue in a spiral path until the system meets the requirements. Each iteration involves the following:

- A portion of the requirements is implemented.
- The model makes a periodic assessment of risk and evaluation.
- The next phase starts only if the risk is acceptable, otherwise the process is repeated again with alternate plans.

Advantages:
- Highly flexible - Risks can be assessed and solutions developed.
- Real problems can be tackled in parallel.
- Usable products are developed early.

Disadvantages:
- Can be costly and time-consuming.
- Difficult to determine when to stop iterating.
- Needs proper risk management for success.

Applications: Large, expensive and complicated systems.
