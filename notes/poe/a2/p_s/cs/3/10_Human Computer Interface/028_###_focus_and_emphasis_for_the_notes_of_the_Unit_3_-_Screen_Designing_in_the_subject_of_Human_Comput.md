### Prototype Model in SDLC

The Prototype Model is a software development model. It is an iterative approach where prototypes are rapidly developed, tested and modified. A prototype is a preliminary working model of the software to allow users to evaluate developers' understanding of requirements and provide feedback.

 Key points about Prototype Model:

-   Requirements are not understood well in the initial stages. Prototyping helps to understand the requirements better.
-   Prototypes are developed quickly and cheaply. They are not perfect but help uncover important information.
-   User feedback on prototypes is incorporated. Prototypes evolve through iterative refinement and modification.
-   Risks are handled early as important requirements and design issues are discovered early.
-   Saves time and money in the long run as changes are incorporated early but needs extra effort to build prototypes.
-   Works well for projects where requirements are not well defined or volatile.

 Steps in Prototype Model:

1.  Identify basic requirements and objectives
2.  Develop initial prototype
3.  Review and evaluate prototype
4.  Refine prototype by incorporating feedback
5.  Repeat steps 3 and 4 until the prototype is accepted
6.  Use the final prototype as the basis for development

 Advantages:

-   User involvement is high as requirements can be visualised early.
-   Ambiguous or misunderstood requirements can be clarified.
-   Risks can be identified early and clarified.
-   Development process is flexible.

 Disadvantages:

-   May cost more if many revisions are made.
-   Insufficient refinement may lead to customer expectation of a perfect final system.
-   May be unsuitable for small or well-defined projects.

 Applications:

-   When requirements are not well understood.
-   For complex and mission-critical systems where user involvement is high.
-   As a risk reduction strategy by uncovering risks early.
