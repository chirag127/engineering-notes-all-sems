### Characteristics

1. The interaction between humans and computers - HCI involves studying and designing the interaction between humans (users) and computers. It focuses on making this interaction efficient, effective and satisfying.
2. Multidisciplinary field - HCI draws on knowledge and skills from various disciplines including computer science, design, psychology, sociology, anthropology, engineering, etc. This multidisciplinary nature is key to understanding and designing interactions between the human and the computer.
3. Users and tasks - HCI is centered around users, tasks and the contexts in which interaction with computers take place. The designs and interactions must be tailored to the users, tasks and environments.
4. Usability - A key goal of HCI is to make the interaction with computers and devices useful, usable and accessible. Usability focuses on making the interface easy to learn, efficient to use, minimally frustrating and satisfying.
5. Context-dependent - The interactions between humans and computers are highly context-dependent. The variability in users, tasks, environments and technologies mean that there is no 'one size fits all' solution. The context must be carefully analyzed to design suitable interactions.
6. Iterative design - HCI uses an iterative design process that involves evaluating and redesigning interactions based on feedback and learning. This is necessary due to the complex and variable nature of HCI where the optimal solution is often arrived at through multiple iterations.
