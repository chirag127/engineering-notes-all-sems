### Human interaction speeds

The speed at which a human can interact with a system is an important design consideration. Some key human interaction speeds to keep in mind are:

- Reaction time: The time taken by a human to react to a stimulus. Typical reaction times are 0.2-0.3 seconds. Systems should not demand responses faster than this.
- Movement time: The time taken to physically interact with a device (e.g. by pressing a button). This is typically around 0.1-0.2 seconds for simple interactions.
- Perception time: The time taken to perceive and interpret information. This depends a lot on the complexity of the information and the task, but even simple perception can take around 0.5 seconds.
- Learning time: The time taken for a user to learn how to interact with a system. More intuitive, familiar interfaces have shorter learning times.
- Memory time: The time taken to recall information from memory. This depends heavily on the nature of the information and can vary from milliseconds to minutes.

Keeping these speeds in mind during design and providing appropriate feedback and tolerances can help create interfaces that feel responsive and natural. It's important not to overwhelm the human with interactions that must be completed faster than they are physically and cognitively capable of.

Advantages: Interfaces designed with human interaction speeds in mind tend to feel more responsive and easier to use.
Disadvantages: Accommodating human interaction speeds may require more complex engineering and design.
Applications: Consideration of human interaction speeds is important for the design of all interactive systems.
