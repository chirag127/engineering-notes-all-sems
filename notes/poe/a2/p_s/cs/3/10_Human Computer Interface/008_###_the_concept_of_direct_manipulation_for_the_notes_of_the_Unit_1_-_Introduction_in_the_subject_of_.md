### The concept of direct manipulation

- Direct manipulation is a design principle where users interact with digital objects on a computer screen through physical actions that correspond to the physical world.
- It provides a natural mapping between the user's action and the system response. The user manipulates an object on the screen as if it were a physical object, and sees the immediate results of their actions.
- Some key characteristics of direct manipulation interfaces are:

1. Continuous representation of the object of interest. The user is directly manipulating the object of interest rather than issuing commands to invoke actions on it.
2. Physical actions that correspond to the physical world. The input actions and metaphors are drawn from the physical rather than symbolic world.
3. Rapid, incremental, and reversible actions with immediate feedback. The user can explore possibilities and undo or reverse actions to reach the desired state.
4. Simplicity of learning and remembering. The interface is easy to learn and remember as it draws on familiar physical interactions and feedback is immediate.

- Examples of direct manipulation interfaces are drag and drop interfaces, resizing windows, moving objects, selecting and manipulating objects on the screen, etc.
- Advantages: Natural and easy to learn, intuitive, clarity between user actions and system responses.
- Disadvantages: Can be inefficient for complex tasks, limitations of input/output devices can constrain the metaphor.
- Applications: Graphical user interfaces, computer aided design systems, interactive simulations, etc.
