### Design Structure Charts

Design Structure Charts (DSCs) are a hierarchical decomposition technique that depicts the decomposition of a system into subsystems and components. They are used to represent the structural relationships between the components of a software system.

* DSCs consist of boxes that represent components and lines that show relationships between components.
* The components at the top of a DSC are more general, and components lower down are more specific.
* DSCs can be used to show functional, resource, or module views of a system.
* DSCs are easy to understand and can be combined with data flow diagrams to provide a complete structural model of a system.
* The main advantages of DSCs are that they are easy to draw and understand, that they show clear relationships between components, and that they can be represented at various levels of detail.

The main symbols used in DSCs are:

* Rectangles - represent system components
* Lines - show relationships between components
* Arrowheads - indicate the direction of the relationship
* Labels - describe the type of relationship, e.g., "uses", "contains"

Examples of DSCs and their applications can be shown with the help of diagrams to make the concepts more clear. DSCs can be useful in the initial design stages of software engineering to get an overview of the major system components and how they interact. They are easy to understand and can be combined with other techniques like data flow diagrams to provide a complete picture of the software system.
