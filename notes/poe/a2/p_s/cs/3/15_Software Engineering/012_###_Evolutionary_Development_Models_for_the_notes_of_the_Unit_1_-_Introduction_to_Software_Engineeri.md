### Evolutionary Development Models

- Evolutionary development models emphasize incremental, iterative approaches to software development. The software is developed in small incremental steps or builds. Each increment provides additional functionality to the software.
- These models are preferred when requirements are not well understood or are likely to change. They are flexible and accommodate changing requirements.
- The different increments or builds are released in iterations. Each iteration includes requirement analysis, design, coding and testing activities. The customer can give feedback on each increment which is then incorporated into the next iteration.
- Examples of evolutionary development models are:
    - Incremental model - divides the software into increments and delivers them in phases. Each phase includes requirements, design, coding and testing.
    - Iterative model - uses iterations of the waterfall model. Each iteration results in incremental releases of the software with additional functionality.
    - Agile models - focus on adaptability to changing requirements. Use of sprints to implement requirements in iterative cycles. Example - Scrum model.
- Advantages:
    - Customer involvement and satisfaction is high due to incremental releases and feedback.
    - Flexible and can accommodate changes easily.
    - Risks are mitigated since small portions are implemented at a time.
- Disadvantages:
    - More time and effort required as some work may be redone in each iteration.
    - Difficult to estimate time and cost precisely due to lack of detailed plans for the complete software.
    - May not be suitable for projects with firm requirements and specifications.
