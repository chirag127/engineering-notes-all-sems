### Data Flow Diagrams

- Data Flow Diagrams (DFDs) are a graphical representation of the flow of data through an information system. They show the flow of data from external entities into the system, showed how the data moved from one process to another, as well as its logical storage.
- DFDs can be used to provide an overview of the system and identify major transformations or processes, as well as the data flows between these processes.
- The main components of a DFD are:
  - External entities: Represent sources or destinations of data. They are depicted as squares on the edge of the diagram.
  - Processes: Represent transformations or manipulations of data. They are depicted as circles or rounded rectangles.
  - Data stores: Represent permanent data storage. They are depicted as open rectangles.
  - Data flows: Represent the flow of data between components. They are depicted as arrows.
- The processes in a DFD can be decomposed into more detailed, lower-level DFDs to provide additional details. This results in a hierarchical set of DFDs with different levels of details.
- Advantages of DFDs:
  - Provide an overview of the system and its major components and interactions.
  - Are easy to understand and create.
  - Aid in identifying processes and data flows.
- Disadvantages of DFDs:
  - Do not show sequence of processes or feedback loops.
  - Do not show conditional logic or timing.
  - Can become complex if taken to lower levels.
- DFDs are commonly used in the requirements analysis and design phases of software engineering to model and specify the requirements of a system. They can be a useful communication tool between stakeholders and developers.
