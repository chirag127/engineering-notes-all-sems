### Basic Concept of Software Design

1. Modularization - Dividing the system into modules (self-contained portions of code) that have specific functions. This makes the system easier to understand, develop and maintain.
2. Abstraction - Hiding the complexity of lower level modules and exposing only the necessary interfaces to the higher level modules. This reduces complexity and emphasizes on what the module does rather than how it does it.
3. Layering - Arranging modules in hierarchical layers where each layer provides services to the layer above it and serves as a client to the layer below it. This enforces modularization and abstraction and also improves portability.
4. Coupling - The degree of dependency between modules. Lower coupling leads to more independent and reusable modules.
5. Cohesion - The degree to which the elements within a module belong together. Higher cohesion leads to modules that have a single well-defined purpose.

Advantages of good software design -
- Increased understandability and maintainability.
- Increased reusability of modules.
- Localization of errors and effects of changes.
- Adaptability to change.

Examples of software design -
- Layered architecture (Presentation layer, Business logic layer, Data access layer).
- Microkernel architecture.
- Event-driven architecture.
- Pipe and filter architecture.

Applications of software design -
- Widely used in developing large and complex software systems.
- The concepts are language and platform independent.
- Provides a systematic approach to software development leading to high quality software.
