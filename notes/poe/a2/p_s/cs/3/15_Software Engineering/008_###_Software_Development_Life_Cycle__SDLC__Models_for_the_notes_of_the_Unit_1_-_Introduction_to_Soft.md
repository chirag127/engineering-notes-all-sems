# Software Development Life Cycle (SDLC) Models

The Software Development Life Cycle (SDLC) is a process used by the software industry to design, develop and test high quality softwares. The SDLC aims to produce a high-quality software that meets or exceeds customer expectations, reaches completion within times and cost estimates.

There are several SDLC models developed to achieve the steps involved in the SDLC systematically. Some of the popular SDLC models are:

1. **Waterfall Model**: The waterfall model is a sequential design process, often used in software development processes, in which progress is seen as flowing steadily downwards (like a waterfall) through the phases of conception, initiation, analysis, design, construction, testing, production/implementation and maintenance.

    - Advantages: Simple and easy to understand. Emphasis on planning. Supports strict management and control.
    - Disadvantages: Difficult to accomodate changes. Initial stage contains high amount of uncertainty. Lacks iterative approach.

2. **Iterative and Incremental Model**: The iterative and incremental model focuses on iterative development and incremental releases of the software. Requirements and solutions evolve through each iterative cycle.

    - Advantages: Accomodates changes easily. Risks are addressed early. Customer can see the progress in increments.
    - Disadvantages: More complex to manage. Require good planning and tracking. Higher investment of time required as compared to waterfall model.

3. **Spiral Model**: The spiral model incorporates elements of both design and prototyping-in-stages, in an effort to combine advantages of top-down and bottom-up concepts.

    - Advantages: High amount of risk management. Good for large and expensive projects. System can be added to at any point in the lifecycle.
    - Disadvantages: Can be a costly model to follow due to the extra involvement at each stage. High expertise required to evaluate risks and implications.
