### Top-Down and Bottom-Up Design

- Top-Down Design: In top-down design, the system is designed from the top level downwards. The high-level modules are identified first and then each module is elaborated in a hierarchical manner. The main advantage is that it provides an overview of the system and its components very early in the design process. However, there is a risk of missing out on lower-level details and making inaccurate assumptions about lower-level modules during high-level design.
- Bottom-Up Design: In bottom-up design, the lower-level modules are designed first and then integrated to form higher-level modules. The main advantage is that lower-level details are taken care of first. However, there is a risk of losing sight of the complete system and how the lower-level modules fit into the overall system.
- For large and complex systems, a combination of top-down and bottom-up design is often used. The high-level system structure is identified using top-down design and then critical lower-level components are designed in detail using bottom-up design. This helps to get the benefits of both approaches and cope with their limitations.

Advantages of Top-Down Design:
- Provides an overview of the system and its components early on.
- Decomposition is done on the basis of system functionality.
- Higher-level modules are designed before lower-level modules leading to better planning.

Disadvantages of Top-Down Design:
- There is a risk of making inaccurate assumptions about lower-level modules during high-level design.
- Important implementation details may be missed out.

Advantages of Bottom-Up Design:
- Lower-level details are taken care of first.
-The design is based on well-understood components.

Disadvantages of Bottom-Up Design:
- There is a risk of losing sight of the whole system and how components fit into the system.
- Requirements may change during the design of lower-level components leading to extra work.
