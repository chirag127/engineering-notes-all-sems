### Requirement Engineering Process for Software Requirement Specifications (SRS)

1.  Elicitation - This is the first step where the requirements are gathered from the customer, users, stakeholders, etc. This can be done via interviews, questionnaires, observing users, etc. The gathered requirements are noted down in natural language.
2.  Analysis - The collected requirements are then analyzed for consistency, completeness, clarity, feasibility, etc. Ambiguities and conflicts are resolved. Requirements are categorized into functional and non-functional requirements.
3.  Specification - The analyzed requirements are documented in a standard format resulting in the Software Requirement Specification (SRS) document. The SRS acts as a communication medium between the customer and the developer. It should be complete, consistent, feasible, and unambiguous.
4.  Validation - The SRS is validated by taking feedback from the customer and stakeholders. Their reviews and feedback are incorporated. This step assures that the SRS has captured the actual needs.
5.  Management - The requirements and SRS are properly managed and controlled. Changes are incorporated and the SRS is updated accordingly. Version control is employed for traceability.

Advantages:
-   Captures actual customer needs and expectations.
-   Reduces rework by providing a proper design and development baseline.
-   Acts as a communication medium between the customer and developer.
-   Supports estimation and risk management.

Disadvantages:
-   Tedious and time-consuming process.
-   Difficult to elicit all requirements. Some may be missed or omitted.
-   Requirements are prone to changes which necessitate updates and maintenance of SRS.
