### Prototype Model

The Prototype Model is a software development model in which a prototype is built, tested and reconstructed until an acceptable prototype is achieved from which the complete system can be developed.

- The prototype is developed based on the currently known requirements.
- The requirements are then validated through user evaluation of the prototype.
- Requirements are modified as needed and a new prototype is developed.
- This process continues until the prototype is acceptable.
- The final system is then developed from the prototype.

Advantages:

- User involvement is high.
- Risk handling is good.
- Requirements can be clarified.
- Acceptance criteria can be defined.

Disadvantages:

- Insufficient analysis may lead to redesigning and reimplementation.
- Cost-benefit ratio is difficult to evaluate.
- The scope of changes may become uncontrollable leading to "scope creep".
- The prototype developed late in the process may not be structured well for conversion into the final system.

Applications:

- The prototype model is useful for systems that are not well understood by the user such as a web-based information system or a pilot project.
- It is also useful for developing systems with interactive user interfaces to obtain early user feedback.

This model works best when requirements are not well understood or the technology to be used is not known. It focuses on user involvement and risk handling. The prototype is not an end in itself but just a means towards building the final system.
