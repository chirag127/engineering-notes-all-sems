### Categories of Maintenance

The following are the major categories of software maintenance:

1. Corrective Maintenance: It refers to the correction of discovered software faults or errors. This is the most frequent maintenance activity. It involves fixing bugs or errors in the software.
2. Adaptive Maintenance: It refers to the modification of the software to adapt to changes in its external environment. For example, changes in operating system, hardware, or other interfaces. This maintenance is required to keep the software operational in its changed environment.
3. Perfective Maintenance: It refers to the modification of the software to improve its performance, maintainability, or other non-functional attributes. It is done to refine the software and make it more efficient and effective. For example, modifying the code to make it more readable, restructuring the database, etc.
4. Preventive Maintenance: It refers to the modification of the software to detect and eliminate potential faults or errors before they become problems. The goal is to prevent the occurrence of errors. For example, extra sanity checks or exception handling.

The above categories are not mutually exclusive and a maintenance task may involve activities from multiple categories. For example, a change in external environment may require corrective as well as adaptive maintenance.

The maintenance activities require modification of the software as well as the associated documentation. The documentation must be updated to keep it consistent with the current version of the software. Proper maintenance is essential to retain the long term value of the software and to enable its continued safe and effective use.
