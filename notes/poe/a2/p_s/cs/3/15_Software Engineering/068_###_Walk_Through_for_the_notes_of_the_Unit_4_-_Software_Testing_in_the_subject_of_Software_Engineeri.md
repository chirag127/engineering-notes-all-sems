### Walk Through for the notes of the Unit 4 - Software Testing in the subject of Software Engineering

1. Introduction to Software Testing
- Software Testing is a process of evaluating a software product to identify differences between expected and actual outcomes.
- It helps to identify bugs, defects or other issues in the software.
- It is conducted to provide stakeholders with information about the quality of the software product and risk of its failures.
- It provides an objective, independent view of the software to allow the business to appreciate and understand the risks of software implementation.

2. Why is Software Testing Required?
- To identify bugs and defects in the software which if undetected can lead to potential failures or underperformance of the software.
- To verify and validate that the software meets the required specifications and expectations.
- To ensure high quality, reliable and defect-free software.
- To provide concrete information to stakeholders about the quality and functionality of the software.

3. Software Testing Approaches
- White Box Testing: Tests are designed based on the internal structure of the software. Also known as Glass Box Testing or Clear Box Testing.
- Black Box Testing: Tests are designed based on the functionality of the software without considering the internal structure. Also known as Behavioral Testing or Functional Testing.
- Grey Box Testing: Combination of Black Box and White Box Testing methods. The tester has limited knowledge of the internal structure of the software.
