### Reverse Engineering

Reverse engineering is the process of analyzing a system to identify its components and their interrelationships and create representations of the system in another form or at a higher level of abstraction.

- It is often done to recreate or duplicate a system when its blueprints or schematics are not available.
- In software engineering, reverse engineering is the process of analyzing a software system to identify its components and their interrelationships and to create representations of the system in another form or at a higher level of abstraction.
- It is a process of extracting knowledge or design information from anything man-made.
- The goal is to gain understanding, documentation, replication, or the ability to interface to/with the thing.

Advantages:
- Allows understanding of how a system works internally.
- Allows recreation or improvement of a system.
- Allows integration with or connection to a system.

Disadvantages:
- Can be time consuming and difficult depending on system complexity.
- May require specialized tools or expertise.
- May be illegal if used to copy or steal intellectual property.

Applications:
- Software documentation - Automatically generate class diagrams/ER diagrams from code.
- Computer forensics - Recover data/programs from damaged storage media.
- Interoperability - Interface to/connect systems/components.
- Migration - Move system to new platform/language.
- Security auditing - Check for vulnerabilities or malware.
