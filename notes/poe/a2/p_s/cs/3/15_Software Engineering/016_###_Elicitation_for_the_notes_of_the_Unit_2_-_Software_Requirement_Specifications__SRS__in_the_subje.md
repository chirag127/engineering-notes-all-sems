### Elicitation for the notes of the Unit 2 - Software Requirement Specifications (SRS) in the subject of Software Engineering

- Elicitation is the process of gathering requirements from stakeholders such as users, customers, operators etc. This is the first and most important step in requirements engineering process.
- Techniques used for elicitation are:
- Interviews - One-on-one meetings with stakeholders to understand their needs. Can be structured, semi-structured or unstructured.
- Questionnaires - Written set of questions sent to stakeholders to understand their needs. Can be open-ended or closed-ended.
- Observation - Observing stakeholders performing their tasks to understand their needs and requirements.
- Brainstorming - Group meetings to come up with creative ideas and requirements.
- prototyping - Creating prototypes of the system to understand the feedback and elicit requirements from stakeholders.
- Requirements workshops - Interactive meetings with stakeholders to define and elaborate requirements.
- Storyboarding - Using a sequence of drawings or images to show the flow of a process and elicit requirements.

Advantages of elicitation:
- Involves stakeholders and considers their needs.
- May reveal hidden or implicit requirements.
- Builds trust and consensus between stakeholders and analysts.

Disadvantages of elicitation:
- Time consuming and expensive process.
- Difficult to elicit requirements from all stakeholders.
- May introduce biases if not performed properly.
- Stakeholders may not be able to articulate some requirements clearly.

Examples of elicitation in projects:
- Interviews and questionnaires to understand needs of users of a software application.
- Observation of technicians performing tasks to elicit requirements for a new monitoring system.
- Prototyping the interface of a website and eliciting feedback from customers.
- Storyboarding the process of ordering and delivery of products to elicit requirements for an e-commerce website.
