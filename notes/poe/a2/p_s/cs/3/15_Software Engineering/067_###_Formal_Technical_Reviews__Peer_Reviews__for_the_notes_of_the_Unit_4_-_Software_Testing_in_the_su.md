### Formal Technical Reviews (Peer Reviews)

Formal Technical Reviews, also known as Peer Reviews, are a systematic technique for detecting and eliminating defects early in the software development lifecycle. They involve a small group of developers examining the work product systematically, step-by-step.

The major steps in a formal technical review are:

1. Planning: Review activities are planned, review schedule and tasks are decided. Roles and responsibilities of reviewers and author are defined.

2. Overview: The author briefly describes the review work product, highlights key points. The reviewers get a quick understanding of what is being reviewed.

3. Preparation: Reviewers study the review work product in detail, formally identify defects and queries.

4. Meeting: Face-to-face meeting, review work product is examined systematically. Reviewers report identified defects and queries, author provides responses and resolves queries.

5. Rework: Author carries out rework based on review feedback.

The advantages of formal technical reviews are:

- Early detection of defects which reduces cost of fixing defects later.
- Identifies issues in requirements, design or code.
- Promotes consistent quality standards across a project.
- Educational value, reviewers gain insight into work product.
- Acts as a checkpoint, completion criteria for a work product to proceed to next phase.
