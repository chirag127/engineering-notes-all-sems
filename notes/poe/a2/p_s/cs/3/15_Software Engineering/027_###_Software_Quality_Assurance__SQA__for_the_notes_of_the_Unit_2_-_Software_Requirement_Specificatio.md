### Software Quality Assurance (SQA)

- **SQA** refers to the planned and systematic set of activities to ensure that software processes and products conform to requirements, standards, and procedures.
- It involves reviewing and auditing software artifacts and processes to identify issues and ensure compliance with applicable regulations, standards, and best practices.
- The key SQA activities are:
  - Reviews: Informal reviews, technical reviews, inspection
  - Audits: Process audits, product audits
  - Testing: Unit testing, integration testing, system testing, acceptance testing
  - Measurement and analysis: Collecting metrics on software quality, size, complexity, etc. and analyzing data to evaluate quality and identify improvement opportunities.
- The benefits of SQA are:
  - Higher quality software delivered on time and within budget.
  - Identifying and fixing defects early in the software lifecycle.
  - Improved productivity and reduced rework.
  - Compliance with standards and regulations.
- The challenges with SQA are:
  - Requires time, effort, and resources which add to project costs.
  - Difficult to achieve a balance between risk mitigation and cost-effectiveness.
  - Subjective nature of reviews and audits can lead to inconsistencies.
  - Measuring software quality is difficult and no single metric is sufficient.
- Some best practices for effective SQA:
  - Involve SQA early in the software lifecycle.
  - Define and follow a well-structured SQA process.
  - Provide adequate training to SQA team.
  - Use multiple techniques together for comprehensive coverage.
  - Track quality-related metrics and use data for informed decisions.
  - Foster a quality-focused culture and get buy-in from all stakeholders.
