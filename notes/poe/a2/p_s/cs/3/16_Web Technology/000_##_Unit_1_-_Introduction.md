## Unit 1 - Introduction

1. What is AI?
- AI or Artificial Intelligence is the simulation of human intelligence processes by machines, especially computer systems.
- These processes include learning, reasoning, and self-correction.
- The ultimate goal of AI is to create general artificial intelligence - a machine that can perform any intellectual task that a human being can. However, we are not there yet.
- Today, AI systems are narrow in scope and are designed to perform specific, limited tasks, like driving a car, translating between languages, or detecting objects in images.

2. Brief History of AI
- The concept of AI was first proposed in the 1950s. The early goals of AI research were to create programs that could solve problems in mathematical logic and other complex tasks that were simple for humans but had been considered extremely difficult for machines.
- In the 1960s, the US Department of Defense took interest in AI and funded research, but after unrealistic expectations were not met, funding was cut. This led to a period of reduced funding known as the "AI winter" in the 1970s and 1980s.
- In the 1990s and 2000s, advances in computing power and the emergence of the Internet led to new successes and renewed funding for AI research. Machine learning and deep learning achieved significant results in pattern recognition, speech recognition, and other complex tasks.
- Today, advanced AI systems with neural networks and massive amounts of data are achieving human-level performance on more tasks like vision, planning, and decision making. Self-driving cars, automated assistants, and AI that can create artificial "works of art" are examples of modern AI.
