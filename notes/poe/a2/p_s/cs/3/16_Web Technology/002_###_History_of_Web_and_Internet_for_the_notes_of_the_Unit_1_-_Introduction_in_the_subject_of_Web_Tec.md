### History of Web and Internet

* In 1960s, the US government funded research projects to create robust and fault-tolerant computer networks. This led to the development of ARPANET (Advanced Research Projects Agency Network) in 1969 which connected a few major universities and research centers.
* In 1970s, research on packet switching and TCP/IP protocols took place to allow multiple different networks to connect and form an 'internet'. The US government turned control of the ARPANET over to researchers in the early 1980s.
* In 1980s, the ARPANET was decommissioned and replaced by NSFNET and CSNET. The TCP/IP protocol was standardized and adopted, leading to the development of a massive 'internet'. The number of networks and users on the internet started growing rapidly.
* In 1989, Tim Berners-Lee invented the World Wide Web while at CERN. He created the first web browser and web server and the HTML and HTTP standards.
* In 1990s, the NSFNET was decommissioned, the internet commercialized, and the World Wide Web popularized with the first web browsers like Mosaic and Internet Explorer. The growth of the internet and web exploded in this decade, allowing various web services, ecommerce, and more to develop.
* In 2000s and beyond, the internet and web have become a huge part of society, connecting billions of users and enabling new innovations in social media, smartphones, Internet of Things, and more. The 'mobile-first' and 'cloud-first' approaches have continued the rapid pace of progress.
