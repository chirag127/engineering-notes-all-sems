### Classify them as weak and strong classes for the notes of the Unit 1 - Introduction of Software Engineering Lab in the subject of Software Engineering Lab:

1. Weak classes:
- Classes which are not tightly integrated and independent of each other are weak classes.
- Changes in one class do not affect other classes.
- Loose coupling between classes.
- Less interdependencies between classes.
- Easy to maintain and modify.

2. Strong classes:
- Classes which are tightly coupled and highly dependent on each other are strong classes.
- Changes in one class affects other classes.
- Tight coupling between classes.
- High interdependencies between classes.
- Difficult to maintain and modify.

Advantages of weak classes:
- Increased modularity.
- Increased reusability.
- Isolated from impact of changes.
- Easy to parallelize.

Disadvantages of strong classes:
- Rigid design.
- Difficult to reuse components.
- Changes lead to cascading effects.
- Parallelization is difficult.

Examples of weak classes:
- Classes implementing a printer interface.
- Decorator classes.

Examples of strong classes:
- Composite classes.
- Classes in a singleton pattern.

Applications:
- Weak classes are preferred in software architectures to make the system more robust, flexible and maintainable.
- Strong classes are used when high performance, efficiency and resource optimization is required.
