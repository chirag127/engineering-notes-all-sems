Software Quality Frameworks (SQF) in Software Requirements Specification (SRS) refer to the standards, models or guidelines used to ensure high quality software is developed as per the requirements. Some of the key SQFs used in SRS are:

1. ISO/IEC 25000:2014 - SQuaRE - Software Quality Requirements and Evaluation series of standards. It provides a comprehensive model for software quality assurance including requirements, architecture and test specifications.
2. ISO/IEC 9126 - Software product quality model with 6 characteristics - Functionality, Reliability, Usability, Efficiency, Maintainability and Portability. Requirements can be specified and evaluated against these characteristics.
3. McCall's Quality Model - Defines 3 quality factors - Product transition, Product revision and Product operational. Requirements can be aligned to these factors to ensure product quality.
4. Dromey's Quality Model - Focuses on properties like Correctness, Reliability, Efficiency, Integrity and Usability. Requirements can specify conditions to achieve these properties.

Using SQFs in SRS helps in the following ways:

- Provide a structured approach to capture quality aspects of the software.
- Enable evaluation of requirements against quality factors and characteristics.
- Improve the completeness and consistency of requirements.
- Serve as a reference to verify quality of the final software product.
- Aid in risk management as the quality risks can be identified early.
