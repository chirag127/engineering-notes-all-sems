### Verification and Validation in SRS

Verification and validation are important aspects of software requirements specification (SRS).

- **Verification** refers to the set of activities that ensure that the software that has been built is traceable to customer requirements. It is the process of evaluating software to determine whether the products of a given development phase satisfy the conditions imposed at the start of that phase. In other words, verification ensures that the software meets the specified requirements.
- **Validation** refers to the process of evaluating software during or at the end of the development process to determine whether it satisfies specified requirements. Validation ensures that the software meets the user needs and intended uses.

Validation is more subjective than verification. Verification is generally an technical evaluation, while validation typically involves testing the software with a group of users to assess whether it is acceptable.

Both verification and validation should be described in an SRS to ensure high quality software. The SRS should specify how verification and validations will be carried out for each feature or component, the criteria that will be used to evaluate the software, who will carry out the verification and validation, and the schedule for these activities.

Advantages of verification and validation:
- Finds errors and bugs early
- Reduces rework and associated costs
- Produces high quality software that meets user needs
- Increases user acceptance of the software

Disadvantages:
- Can be time-consuming and costly
- Difficult to determine optimal levels of verification and validation
- Verification and validation criteria can be difficult to define
