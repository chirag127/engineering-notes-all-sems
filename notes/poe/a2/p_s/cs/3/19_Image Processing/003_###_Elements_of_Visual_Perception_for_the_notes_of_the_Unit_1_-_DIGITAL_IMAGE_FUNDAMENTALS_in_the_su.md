### Decision Tables in Software Requirement Specification (SRS)

- Decision tables are a compact way to model complicated logic that depends on multiple conditions and choices. They are often used in software requirement specifications (SRS) to clearly and concisely capture complex logic and decisions.
- A decision table consists of conditions listed in rows, with possible actions or choices listed in columns. Each cell in the table specifies the action or choice that is taken for the particular combination of conditions.
- Decision tables are useful for:
- Capturing complex conditional logic
- Clearly specifying multiple alternative flows in a system
- Avoiding ambiguity in specifications
- Serving as input for automated code or test case generation
- Example of a simple decision table:

Condition   Choice 1   Choice 2
 sunny      go to park go hiking
 rain       stay home  go museum

- The columns represent choices or actions and the rows represent conditions. The cell at the intersection of the satisfied conditions specifies the chosen action.
- Advantages:
- Unambiguous - Clear specification of choices for all combinations of conditions
- Compact - Can capture complex logic in a small space
- Analyst and stakeholder friendly - Easy to read and review
- Disadvantages:
- May not be suitable for highly complex logic with many conditions and choices
- Prone to errors if not designed properly
- Verification of correctness can be difficult for large tables
