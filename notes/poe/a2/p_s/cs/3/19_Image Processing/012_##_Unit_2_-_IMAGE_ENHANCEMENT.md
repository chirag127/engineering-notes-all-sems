## Unit 2 - IMAGE ENHANCEMENT

Image enhancement is a process of adjusting an image so that the results are more suitable for a specific application. It improves the quality of an image for human perception or computer processing. The main objective is to improve the interpretability or perception of information in images for human viewers, or to provide 'better' input for other automated image processing techniques.

The important points to learn about image enhancement are:

1. Improving the contrast: Contrast enhancement is one of the most commonly used techniques. It increases the dynamic range of intensities in the image so that the foreground details are more easily perceived. Methods like histogram equalization, gamma correction, etc. are used to improve contrast.

2. Noise removal: Noise refers to the unwanted signal which degrades the quality of images. Noise can arise due to various sources like sensor errors, transmission errors, etc. Noise removal techniques like median filtering, wiener filtering, etc. are used to remove noise and produce a cleaner image.

3. Sharpening: Sharpening enhances the high-frequency components in the image and gives the impression of a sharper image. Techniques like unsharp masking, high-boost filtering, etc. are used to sharpen the images and highlight the edges.

4. Spatial filtering: Spatial filters are used to remove unwanted spatial frequencies from an image. Low pass filters are used to remove high frequency noise and smoothen the image. High pass filters are used to enhance the high frequency components like edges in an image. Ideal low pass and high pass filters cannot be realized physically, so approximations are used.

The techniques mentioned above have their own advantages and disadvantages and are used depending on the application. Detailed examples and codes can be included to understand the implementation of these techniques. Image enhancement has a wide range of applications in fields like medical imaging, satellite imaging, etc. to improve the interpretability and perception of images.
