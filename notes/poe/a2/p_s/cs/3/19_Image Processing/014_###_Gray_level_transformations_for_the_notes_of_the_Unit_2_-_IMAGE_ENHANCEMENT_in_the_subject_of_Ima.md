### Gray level transformations
Gray level transformations are a class of simple point operations on the intensity values (gray levels) of the pixels in an image. These transformations are used to enhance the contrast and change the dynamic range of an image, thereby improving its visual quality. Some of the commonly used gray level transformations are:

1. Linear transformations: These include changes in brightness and contrast. Changes in brightness move all the intensity values by a constant amount, while changes in contrast expand or compress the intensity range of the image.
2. Histogram equalization: This normalization technique redistributes the intensity values uniformly and compresses or spreads out the intensity range to obtain a uniform histogram. This often improves the contrast in an image and brings out details.
3. Gamma correction: This nonlinear power law transformation is used to control the overall brightness of an image and alter the intensity contrast of an image. A gamma value of one leaves the image unchanged.

Advantages of gray level transformations:
- Simple and easy to implement.
- Can significantly improve the contrast and brightness of an image thereby enhancing details and features.

Disadvantages:
- May produce artifacts or degrade image quality if parameters are not chosen properly.
- Do not add any new information to the image, just remap the intensity values.

Applications: Image enhancement, preprocessing for segmentation and feature extraction, etc.
