### MPEG (Motion Picture Experts Group)

- MPEG is a working group under ISO and IEC that sets standards for audio and video compression and transmission.
- The MPEG standards are numbered with MPEG-1, MPEG-2, MPEG-4, etc. The most popular and widely used standards are MPEG-1, MPEG-2 and MPEG-4.
- The MPEG standards achieve high compression ratios by storing only the changes from one frame to the next, instead of each entire frame. This technique is known as Inter-Frame Compression or Temporal Compression.
- The MPEG standards also use spatial compression techniques like DCT to compress the individual frames.
- The key features of MPEG are:

1. It uses both spatial and temporal compression.
2. It applies different amounts of compression to different parts of the video based on the complexity of the image and the effect on picture quality. Areas that change a lot from frame to frame (like action sequences) are given less compression than areas with minor changes (like still backgrounds).
3. The data is transmitted in a layered hierarchy so that the stream can be truncated at any point to meet bandwidth requirements while still maintaining reasonable picture quality.
4. The compression is lossy, so some picture detail is lost, but the resulting images are usually of acceptable quality for the intended applications.

- Some applications of MPEG are:
- Digital television and streaming of movies and videos over the internet. MPEG-2 is used in digital TV and MPEG-4 is used for streaming videos.
- DVDs use MPEG-2 format for video compression.
- Some image and video editing software use MPEG formats as their native file format.
