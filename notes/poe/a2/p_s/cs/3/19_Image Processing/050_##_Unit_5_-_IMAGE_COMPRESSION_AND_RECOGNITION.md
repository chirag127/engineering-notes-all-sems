### Design Strategies in Software Design

1. Divide and Conquer: Breaking down a complex problem into multiple smaller sub-problems that are similar in nature, solving them independently and combining them to get the final solution.
For example, Merge sort algorithm which divides the array into halves, sorts them recursively and merges the sorted halves.
Advantages: Deals with complexity, similar sub-problems can be reused.
Disadvantages: Overhead of division and merging, sub-problems dependencies.

2. Dynamic Programming: Breaking down a complex problem into overlapping sub-problems and storing their results to avoid repeated work. The results of sub-problems are used to build up the final solution.
For example, Fibonacci number calculation using memoization or Tabulation which stores the results of sub-problems and uses them to calculate the final Fibonacci number.
Advantages: Optimized solution, avoids repeated work.
Disadvantages: Required additional space to store the results, not suitable for problems with sub-problems that are not overlapping.

3. Greedy Approach: Making a locally optimal choice at each stage with the hope of finding a global optimum.
For example, Activity selection problem which selects the next activity with the earliest ending time.
Advantages: Simple and efficient.
Disadvantages: Does not always lead to an optimal solution, getting stuck in local optima.
