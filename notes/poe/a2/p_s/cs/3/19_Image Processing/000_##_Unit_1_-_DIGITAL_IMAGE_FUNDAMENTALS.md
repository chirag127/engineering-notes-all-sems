## Unit 1 - DIGITAL IMAGE FUNDAMENTALS

1. Pixel - Pixel is the smallest individual element of an image. Pixels are arranged in a grid to form an image. The number of pixels determine the resolution of an image. More the number of pixels, higher the resolution and greater the detail.

2. Bit Depth - Bit depth refers to the number of bits used to represent the color of a single pixel. Higher bit depth means a pixel can represent a wider range of colors leading to better color accuracy and quality. Most common bit depths are 8-bit (256 colors), 24-bit (16.7 million colors) and 32-bit (billions of colors).

3. Resolution - Resolution refers to the number of pixels in an image. It determines the level of detail and sharpness in an image. Higher resolution means more pixels and greater level of detail. Resolution is measured in pixels per inch (PPI) or dots per inch (DPI).

4. Color Models - Color models are mathematical representations of colors. They specify how colors can be represented and stored digitally. The most common color models are RGB, CMYK and HSV.

- RGB (Red, Green, Blue) - Used for displaying images on screens. Combines 3 primary colors - red, green and blue to represent a wide range of colors.
- CMYK (Cyan, Magenta, Yellow, Key/Black) - Used for printing images. Combines 4 colors - cyan, magenta, yellow and black to represent a wide range of colors.
- HSV (Hue, Saturation, Value) - Represents color in terms of hue, saturation and brightness. Useful for applications where color needs to be adjusted easily.
