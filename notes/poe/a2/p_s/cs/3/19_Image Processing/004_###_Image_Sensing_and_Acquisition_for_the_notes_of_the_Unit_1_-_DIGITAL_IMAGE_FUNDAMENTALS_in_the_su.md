### Image Sensing and Acquisition

- An image is acquired by an imaging sensor which converts the incident light into electrical signals. The imaging sensor is the input device for an imaging system.
- The most common types of imaging sensors are:

1. Charge-Coupled Device (CCD) - It is an analog device which converts incident light into electrical charge. The electrical charge is then converted into voltage and digitized. CCDs provide high quality images with low noise but they consume more power and are more expensive.
2. Complementary Metal-Oxide Semiconductor (CMOS) - It is a digital image sensor which contains an array of photodiodes and CMOS transistors. Each photodiode converts the incident light into an electrical signal which is then processed by the CMOS transistors to produce an image. CMOS sensors are cheaper, consume less power but can have higher noise than CCDs.

- The acquisition process involves:

1. Light from the scene is focused onto the image sensor using lenses.
2. The imaging sensor converts the incident light into electrical signals.
3. The electrical signals are then digitized and processed to produce a digital image.
4. The digital image is stored and can be displayed, transmitted, analyzed, etc.

- Important parameters for acquisition are: Color Filter Array (CFA), resolution, bit depth, frame rate, sensitivity, etc.
- CFA is used in color imaging to capture color information. Common CFAs are RGB Bayer filter, CMY, etc.
- Resolution determines the image size and level of detail. Higher resolution leads to higher quality but larger images.
- Bit depth determines the intensity resolution. Higher bit depth leads to higher dynamic range.
- Frame rate determines how fast images can be captured. Higher frame rate leads to capturing motion smoothly.
- Sensitivity determines how well the sensor responds to light. Higher sensitivity allows capturing images in low light.
