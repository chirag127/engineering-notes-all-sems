1. Self-Driving Car: A self-driving car is an autonomous vehicle capable of sensing its environment and navigating roads without human input. Self-driving cars use a variety of techniques including computer vision, lidar, GPS, and inertial measurement units to perceive and react to the world around them.

2. How Self-Driving Cars Work: Self-driving cars use a variety of sensors and machine learning algorithms to navigate autonomously. The key steps involved are:

- Perception: The car uses sensors like radar, lidar, cameras to detect surrounding objects like other cars, traffic lights, pedestrians, cyclists, road markings, etc.
- Localization: The car uses GPS, inertial measurement units and map data to determine its current location and orientation.
- Planning: The car plans a path to the destination while obeying traffic rules and avoiding obstacles. It uses machine learning and optimization algorithms to determine steering and speed controls.
- Control: The car executes the steering and speed controls to maneuver safely to the destination.

3. Challenges: There are many challenges involved in developing fully autonomous self-driving cars:

- Handling all possible driving scenarios: It is difficult to train the car to handle all possible driving scenarios that can be encountered in the real world. Machine learning models would require massive amounts of data to learn how to handle all scenarios.
- Bad weather conditions: Self-driving cars struggle with perceiving the environment in bad weather conditions like heavy rain, snow, fog, etc. The sensors can be impaired by such conditions leading to unsafe driving.
- Machine learning biases: The machine learning models can reflect biases in data or design that can lead to unsafe behaviors. Eliminating such biases is difficult.
- System errors: There is a possibility of system errors or software bugs that can cause the self-driving system to behave unexpectedly leading to dangerous situations. Guaranteeing 100% error-free and safe software is challenging.
