### Bayesian networks

Bayesian networks are graphical models that use Bayesian probabilities to represent relationships between variables. They are a way of encoding probabilistic dependencies between variables in a graphical model.

- Nodes in the graph represent random variables, and edges represent conditional dependencies.
- Nodes with no edges between them are conditionally independent.
- Dependencies are encoded using conditional probabilities, relating a child variable to its parent variables.
- Probabilities are updated using Bayes' theorem as evidence arrives.
- Used for:

- Probabilistic reasoning and inference.
- Causal modeling.
- Decision making under uncertainty.

Advantages:

- Handle uncertainty and missing data naturally.
- Compactly encode complex relationships.
- Support efficient inference algorithms.

Disadvantages:

- Require large amounts of data to estimate probabilities accurately.
- Scalability can be an issue for very large networks.
- Assumptions of conditional independence can be violated.

Examples:

- Diagnosing the cause of medical symptoms.
- Speech and handwriting recognition.
- Computer vision and image analysis.
