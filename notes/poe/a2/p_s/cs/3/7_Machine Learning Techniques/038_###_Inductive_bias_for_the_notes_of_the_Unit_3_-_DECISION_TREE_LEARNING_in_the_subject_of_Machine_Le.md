### Inductive bias for the notes of the Unit 3 - DECISION TREE LEARNING in the subject of Machine Learning Techniques

- Inductive bias refers to the assumptions a learning algorithm makes to simplify the learning process.
- For decision trees, the inductive bias is that the target variable can be predicted based on a small number of simple logical conditions on the input features.
- This leads to decision trees that are easy to understand and interpret, but may not have the best accuracy.
- Some key inductive biases of decision tree learning are:

1. The target variable depends on a small number of input variables
2. The dependencies between the input variables and the target variable are locally homogeneous (can be captured by simple logical conditions)
3. The learned hypothesis takes the form of a tree-like structure (a series of logical conditions/tests)

- These inductive biases allow decision trees to learn hypotheses even with little data and their interpretable nature makes them a popular choice for many applications despite potential lower accuracy relative to other ML algorithms with different inductive biases.
- The key is to understand the pros and cons of different inductive biases and choose an algorithm suitable for your problem and goals.
