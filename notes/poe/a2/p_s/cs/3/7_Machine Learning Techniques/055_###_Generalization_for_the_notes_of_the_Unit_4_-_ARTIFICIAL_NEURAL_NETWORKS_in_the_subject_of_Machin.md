### Generalization for the notes of the Unit 4 - ARTIFICIAL NEURAL NETWORKS

1. Artificial Neural Networks (ANNs) are inspired by biological neural networks (in the brain). They are made up of interconnected nodes that must be trained on large amounts of data to learn how to perform a task.
2. The key components of an ANN are:
- Input layer: Receives input data
- Hidden layers: Processes the input data
- Output layer: Produces the output
- Connections between layers: Assign weights to connections that can be adjusted during training to produce the desired output
3. Training an ANN involves forward propagation to get outputs from inputs and backpropagation to adjust the weights and reduce error. This is done iteratively until the network achieves an acceptable level of accuracy.
4. ANNs are suited to tasks like classification and regression on complex, nonlinear data. They have achieved state-of-the-art results on tasks such as image classification, speech recognition, machine translation, etc.
5. Some disadvantages of ANNs are that they are opaque (black box), training can be time-consuming, and they may overfit the training data. Various techniques can be used to improve performance and reduce overfitting such as regularization, dropout, etc.
