## Unit 5 - REINFORCEMENT LEARNING
Reinforcement Learning is a type of Machine Learning wherein an agent learns by interacting with the environment. The agent performs actions and gets rewards in return or penalties in the form of negative rewards. The agent learns the optimal policy of actions to maximize the rewards over time through trial-and-error using feedback from the environment.

Key Points:
1. Agent - The learner or decision-maker.
2. Environment - The system or world the agent interacts with.
3. State - The agent's current situation or context.
4. Action - The agent's possible moves or decisions.
5. Reward - Feedback from the environment indicating the consequences of the action. Positive rewards are good and negative rewards are bad.
6. Policy - The agent's strategy or way of selecting actions based on states. The goal is to determine the optimal policy to maximize rewards.
7. Value - The long-term accumulation of rewards from a particular state or state-action pair. The goal is to determine the state-value function or action-value function.

Steps:
1. Initialize the policy and value function arbitrarily.
2. Collect experience by interacting with the environment following the current policy.
3. Update the policy and/or value function based on the collected experience to improve the learning.
4. Repeat steps #2 and #3 until the desired solution is reached.

Advantages:
- Agent learns to achieve complex goals without being explicitly programmed how to achieve them.
- Can handle problems like control, planning, and decision-making under uncertainty.
Disadvantages:
- Requires massive amounts of interaction with the environment.
- Convergence to optimal solutions not guaranteed.
- Curse of Dimensionality - may not scale to large, high-dimensional problems.

Applications:
- Robotics
- Game Playing
- Traffic Control
- Stock Trading
- etc.
