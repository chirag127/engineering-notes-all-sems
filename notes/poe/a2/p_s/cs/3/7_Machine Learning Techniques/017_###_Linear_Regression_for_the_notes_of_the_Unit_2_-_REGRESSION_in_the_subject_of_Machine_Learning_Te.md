### Linear Regression

- Linear regression is a statistical technique for analyzing the relationship between two continuous variables using a straight line.
- The goal is to find a "best fit" line through the data that minimizes the error (distance) between the observed data and the fitted line.
- The form of the model is: Y = a + bX, where a is the intercept and b is the slope.
- The coefficients a and b are estimated using the least squares method - minimizing the sum of squared errors (difference between actual and predicted values).
- Assumptions:
    - Linear relationship between the variables
    - Error terms are normally distributed
    - Error terms have constant variance (homoscedasticity)
    - Error terms are uncorrelated
- Uses:
    - Prediction - estimate a continuous value based on other variables.
    - Causation - understand effects of one variable on another (after establishing correlation).
    - Fitted line can be used to describe the relationship and trends in the data.
- Advantages:
    - Simple and easy to implement
    - Interpretable results
- Disadvantages:
    - Error terms may not satisfy assumptions
    - Cannot capture non-linear relationships
- Example: Predict housing prices based on size of the house. Application: Real estate price prediction.
