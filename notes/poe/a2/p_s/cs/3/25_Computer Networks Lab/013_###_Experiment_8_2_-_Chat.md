### Experiment 8.2 - Chat

- Chat is a way of communicating with another person over the internet. It allows people to exchange messages in real-time without the need for email.
- To start a chat, two or more people must be online at the same time and use a chat service or app. The most common ways to chat are:
    - Instant messaging (IM) apps like WhatsApp, Messenger, WeChat, etc.
    - Chat websites like Telegram, Skype, Slack, etc.
- How chat works:
    - Each chat user connects to the chat service.
    - The chat service then facilitates the real-time exchange of text messages between the users.
    - As one user sends a message, it is received by the chat service and instantly delivered to the other user(s).
    - This creates a conversational experience that feels like an ongoing dialogue.
- Key features of chat:
    - Real-time messaging: Chat allows for quick back-and-forth messaging with low latency. Messages are delivered and displayed very fast.
    - Presence indication: Chat lets you see which of your contacts are online and available to chat. This allows you to connect with the right people at the right time.
    - History: The conversation history is preserved, so you can scroll back through previous messages. This context can be helpful in following the discussion or picking the conversation back up after being away.
    - Multi-person chat: Many chat services support creating group chats with multiple participants. This allows collaboration and sharing among more than two people.
- Advantages of chat:
    - Convenience and ease of use. Chat is an easy way to quickly send and receive short messages.
    - Feeling of connection. The real-time, conversational nature of chat allows people to stay in touch and feel close, even if they are physically apart.
    - Low cost. Most chat services are free to use.
- Disadvantages of chat:
    - Informal communication. The brevity of chat messages can make conversations feel casual and lacking in depth. Important discussions may be better suited to other communication channels.
    - Distractions and interruptions. The real-time messaging of chat means people expect quick responses, which can lead to distractions and fractured attention.
- Applications of chat:
    - Personal communication. Staying in touch with friends and family.
    - Business communication. Enabling collaboration and quick conversations between team members.
    - Customer service. Providing real-time support to users via chat.
