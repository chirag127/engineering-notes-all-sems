### 3D Mice

- 3D mice are input devices used to provide 3D input to the computer. They allow the user to control the cursor/object in 3D space.
- They are equipped with rotational sensors to track rotation and translate rotational movements into 3D space as input. Some 3D mice also have additional buttons and scroll wheels like regular 2D mice.
- The core components of a 3D mouse are:
  - Trackball: To sense rotational movements along X, Y and Z axes.
  - Sensors: To detect the rotation of trackball and convert into electrical signals.
  - Encoder: To encode the electrical signals into digital data understandable by the computer.
- Types of 3D mice:
  - Trackball based: Have a trackball to sense rotations. Example: 3Dconnexion SpaceNavigator
  - Gyroscopic: Have gyroscopes to sense angular velocity. Example: 3D Systems Touch X haptic device
- Advantages:
  - Intuitive to use. Mimics hand movements in 3D space.
  - Precise and accurate 3D input device.
  - Compatible with 3D modeling and design applications.
- Disadvantages:
  - expensive compared to regular 2D mice.
  - Require training to get accustomed to.
  - Limited to rotational movements only. Linear movements can't be tracked.
- Applications: 3D modeling software, Computer-aided design, Virtual reality environments, etc.
