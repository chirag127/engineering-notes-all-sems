### Case Study: Mixing System Control Methods

- Mixing multiple 3D interaction techniques can provide more intuitive and efficient interfaces for certain applications.
- For example, combining gestural input with tangible input can allow users to quickly manipulate objects at a high level (using gestures) and then perform finer-grained manipulation (using physical controls).
- As another example, combining tangible input with head tracking allows users to select objects or modes using physical controls and then direct their attention/gaze to manipulate the selected objects.
- Care must be taken when combining techniques to choose techniques that are complementary (not redundant or conflicting) and to provide clear affordances and mappings between actions/inputs and effects.
- For complex systems with many features or modes, mixing techniques may be necessary to support efficient access to all functions, but the increased complexity of the interaction must be balanced with the potential gains in performance or intuition.
- Examples of real-world systems that combine multiple 3D interaction techniques include surgical simulators that use gestures, tangible controls, and gaze/head tracking and assembly/design systems that combine tangible objects, gestural input, and motion tracking.
- Advantages: Increased expressiveness and efficiency; intuitive divisions of function between techniques
- Disadvantages: Potential for complexity and ambiguity; non-intuitive or complex mappings required
- Applications: Systems with diverse/complex functions; systems requiring both high-level and fine-grained 3D interaction
