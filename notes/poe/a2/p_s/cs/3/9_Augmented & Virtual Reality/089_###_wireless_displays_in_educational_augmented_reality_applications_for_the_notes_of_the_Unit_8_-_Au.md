## Wireless Displays in Educational Augmented Reality Applications

- Wireless displays eliminate the need for cables to connect the augmented reality device to the display, allowing for a tether-free and immersive AR experience. This makes them ideal for educational AR applications where students can freely move around and interact with the AR content.
- Some examples of wireless display technologies used in AR include:
    - Wi-Fi: Devices can transmit AR content to displays over a local Wi-Fi network. This allows for a range of around 30-100 feet indoors.
    - Miracast: Uses Wi-Fi direct to transmit AR content between devices with lower latency but shorter range than Wi-Fi.
    - Bluetooth: Can be used for short-range transmission of AR content up to 30 feet but may face bandwidth limitations impacting graphics quality.
- Advantages:
    - Tether-free and immersive experience allowing free movement.
    - Easy to set up using existing wireless networks and technologies.
- Disadvantages:
    - Limited range and potential connectivity issues.
    - Higher latency which can reduce immersion and usability.
    - Require line of sight between devices which can be challenging in educational settings.
- Examples of educational AR applications using wireless displays:
    - Students can view annotations and graphics overlaid on physical objects through AR smart glasses or headsets.
    - Students can see a shared AR display of a 3D model projected onto a table surface, allowing collaborative learning.
    - AR content can be transmitted from a teacher's device to student devices, allowing the teacher to control and modify the content in real-time.
