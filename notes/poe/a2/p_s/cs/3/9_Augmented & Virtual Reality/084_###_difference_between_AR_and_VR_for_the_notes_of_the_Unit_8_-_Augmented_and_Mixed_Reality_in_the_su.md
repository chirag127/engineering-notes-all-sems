AR (Augmented Reality) and VR (Virtual Reality) are two popular technologies that are often confused with each other. Though they both enhance the user experience through digital information, they work in different ways:

- VR replaces the real world with a simulated one. The user is immersed in a completely artificial digital environment. The user cannot see the real world around them. Examples include Oculus Quest, HTC Vive, etc.
- AR superimposes digital information on the real world via devices like smartphones, smart glasses, etc. The user can still see the real world around them but with additional digital information layered on top of it. Examples include Pokémon Go, IKEA Place, etc.

Following are some key differences between AR and VR:

- Immersion: VR provides full immersion into a simulated environment cutting off the physical world. AR provides partial immersion where digital content is blended with the physical world.
- Devices: VR requires headsets and other tracking devices. AR can use smartphones, tablets or smart glasses.
- Applications: VR is suitable for gaming, entertainment and simulations. AR enhances the real world and is used for education, business, navigation, etc.
- Challenges: VR faces challenges like simulator sickness, isolated experience, etc. AR faces challenges like accurate object recognition and tracking in complex environments.

In summary, AR and VR are two related yet distinct technologies with different capabilities and applications. Both have the potential to greatly impact various industries and our day to day lives. The choice between AR and VR depends on the specific use case and requirements of the application. For an immersive digital experience isolating the user from the physical world, VR is suitable. For enhancing the real world, AR is more appropriate.
