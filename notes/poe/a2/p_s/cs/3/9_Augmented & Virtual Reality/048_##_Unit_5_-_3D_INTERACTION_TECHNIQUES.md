## Unit 5 - 3D INTERACTION TECHNIQUES

1. Selection techniques
- Ray casting: Selecting objects by firing a ray from the user's viewpoint and testing intersection with all objects. Simple but inefficient for complex scenes.
- Proximity selection: Objects within a bounding volume of the cursor are candidates for selection.
- Brushing: 2D brush strokes specify a selection region (e.g. circle/rectangle) in 3D. Useful for selecting obscured objects.

2. Navigation techniques
- Walk-through: Simulating walking through a virtual environment. Most natural but can cause disorientation/motion sickness.
- Fly-through: Like walking but with free movement in any direction. Less natural but avoids the problems of walking.
- Point-and-go: Click on the ground/floor to (instantly) move to that point. Easy but unnatural.
- Teleportation: Click on the ground to jump to that location. Can be useful for an overview or to quickly move between distant locations.

3. System interfaces
- WIMP: Standard desktop metaphor of windows, icons, menus, pointing device. Limited for 3D interaction due to reliance on 2D input/output devices.
- Immersive interfaces: Head-mounted displays, data gloves, and other devices to provide an immersive virtual environment experience. Removes the desktop metaphor but can be expensive and cumbersome.
