### User Centered Wayfinding Support

User centered wayfinding support refers to designing virtual environments or augmented reality experiences in a way that helps users navigate and find their way easily. Some key points to keep in mind for user centered wayfinding support are:

1. Provide familiar cues: Use familiar cues from the real world like paths, signs, landmarks, etc. to help users navigate. This makes the experience intuitive and easy to understand.

2. Use simple and consistent design: Use a simple and consistent design for pathways, signs, etc. Complex or inconsistent designs can confuse users and make wayfinding difficult.

3. Include maps: Include maps, preferably with the user's current location highlighted. This gives users an overview of the entire virtual environment and helps them understand where they are placed and where they need to go.

4. Use audio cues: Use audio cues in addition to visual cues to help users with wayfinding. For example, ambient sounds can help convey a sense of direction or specific sounds can be used to highlight important landmarks or destinations.

5. Provide a grid structure: Organize the virtual environment or AR experience with a grid structure. This gives users a systematic way to understand the layout, find targets, and determine directions.

6. Include possible interactions: Include interactions that reveal the user's location, paths to a destination, etc. when needed. This could be through highlighting routes, signs that point to targets, or labels that reveal the user's current position.

7. Account for areas of interest: Highlight important areas of interest, destinations, objects, etc. to give users reference points to help with navigation. This provides a sense of context that aids wayfinding.
