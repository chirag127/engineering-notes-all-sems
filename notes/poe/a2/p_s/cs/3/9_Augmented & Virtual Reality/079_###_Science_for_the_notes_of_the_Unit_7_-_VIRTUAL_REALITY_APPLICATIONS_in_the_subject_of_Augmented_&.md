### Science for the notes of the Unit 7 - VIRTUAL REALITY APPLICATIONS

- Virtual Reality(VR) is the creation of immersive digital experiences using head-mounted displays(HMDs) or multi-projected environments.
- The science behind VR involves technologies like stereoscopy, head tracking, and motion tracking to create the illusion of presence in a simulated 3D world.
- Stereoscopy is used to present separate images to the user's left and right eyes to create depth perception. The lenses or displays in the HMDs present these images and the brain fuses them into a single 3D perception.
- Head tracking and motion tracking are used to sense the user's movements and adjust the view accordingly. This allows the user to look around the virtual environment and navigate through it. Inertial Measurement Units(IMUs) and optical tracking systems are commonly used for motion tracking.
- The applications of VR in various fields involve the use of the above concepts in immersive simulations. Some key applications are:

1. Education - VR can be used to create interactive educational simulations and experiences for topics like astronomy, biology, architecture, etc. This enhances learning and retention.
2. Entertainment - VR is used in gaming, sports viewing, cinematic experiences, etc. VR headsets are commercially available for entertainment and gaming.
3. Healthcare - VR is used for rehabilitation, pain distraction, surgical simulations, exposure therapy, etc. It shows promising results for treating conditions like PTSD, anxiety, phobias, etc.
4. Engineering - VR is used to visualize and interact with prototypes and models. It is extensively used in product design, architecture, and robotics.
5. Social Connection - VR can connect people through virtual meetings, dates, conferences, and remote collaboration. It allows people to interact in shared virtual spaces.

The advantages of VR include immersiveness, real-time feedback, and safe simulation of dangerous situations. The disadvantages include potential negative health effects of prolonged use, cost, and technical challenges. VR is an emerging and active area of research with many possibilities for the future.
