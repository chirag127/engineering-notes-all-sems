1. Marker-based AR:
- Uses visual markers (like QR codes) to trigger and locate AR content
- The camera detects the marker and overlays the AR content on top of it
- Advantage: Inexpensive, easy to develop. Disadvantage: Require physical markers in the real world.
- Examples: AR business cards, product catalogues, etc.

2. Markerless AR:
- Does not require physical markers and uses other cues to locate and orient AR content
- Examples: Image recognition (detect objects, scenes), SLAM (reconstruct 3D world), GPS (locate user position)
- Advantages: No physical markers required. Disadvantages: More complex to develop, limited tracking accuracy.

3. Projection-based AR:
- Projects AR content onto physical surfaces using projectors
- The projected content appears to be an inherent part of the physical environment.
- Allows augmentation of non-flat and irregularly shaped physical objects or surfaces.
- Examples: Projection mapping, spatial augmented reality.
