### Hardware Controls

The hardware components that are used to control and interact with Augmented and Virtual Reality systems are:

1.  Head-Mounted Display (HMD) - It is a display device worn on the head that presents AR/VR content. It tracks the user's movements and adjusts the view accordingly. Examples are Oculus Rift, HTC Vive, Microsoft HoloLens, etc.
2.  Handheld Controllers - They are input devices held in the hands to interact with the AR/VR system. They track the user's hand movements and gestures to control objects in the AR/VR environment. Examples are controllers for Oculus Rift, HTC Vive, etc.
3.  Motion Trackers - They are devices that track the position and orientation of the user and input devices. This allows the system to adjust the view and controls based on the user's movements. Examples are exterior cameras, inertial measurement units, etc.
4.  Biosensors - They are devices that track physiological signals from the user like heartbeat, breath, eye movements, etc. This allows the system to sense the user's emotions, reactions and adjust the experience accordingly. Examples are EEG, ECG, eye trackers, etc.

The choice of hardware components depends on the requirements and applications of the AR/VR system. The hardware tracks the user's movements and inputs and the software processes them to provide an immersive AR/VR experience. More advanced hardware leads to a more immersive and engaging experience.

The advantages of these hardware components are:

1.  Provide a natural and intuitive way to interact with AR/VR systems through movements and gestures.
2.  Enable adjustment of view and controls based on user's movements leading to an immersive experience.
3.  Capture physiological signals to sense user's emotions and reactions.

The disadvantages are:

1.  Additional devices leading to extra cost and complexity.
2.  Latency in tracking and adjusting the view which can reduce immersion.
3.  Inaccurate tracking and input can lead to a poor user experience.
