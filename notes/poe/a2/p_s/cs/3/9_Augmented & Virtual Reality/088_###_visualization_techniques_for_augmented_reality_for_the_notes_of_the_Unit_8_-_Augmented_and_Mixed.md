#### 1. Marker-based Augmented Reality
- In this technique, the camera detects visual markers or patterns in the real world to trigger the display of augmented reality content.
- The markers are usually 2D images, QR codes or AR tags which are recognized by the camera and their position and orientation is tracked to augment the 3D graphics on them.
- Examples: AR business cards, product packaging, etc.
- Advantages: Inexpensive, easy to implement.
- Disadvantages: Limited to the marker and requires the marker to be present in the field of view to display the AR content.

#### 2. Markerless Augmented Reality
- In this technique, the camera detects and tracks natural features in the real world like planes, surfaces or 3D objects to overlay the augmented reality content.
- Feature detection and tracking algorithms are used to recognize and determine the pose of real-world elements to position and render the 3D graphics.
- Examples: Augmented navigation, interior designing apps, etc.
- Advantages: No need for markers and can augment on any real world object or surface.
- Disadvantages: Computationally expensive, complex to implement and may face difficulties with featureless uniform textures or in poor lighting conditions.

- Advantages: Can work without visual features and in all environments.
