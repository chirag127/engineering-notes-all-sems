### Direct Human Input
Direct human input devices track the user's movements and gestures to allow interaction with the virtual environment. These include:

- Hand controllers: Gloves or wands with sensors to track hand movements. Allow natural hand gestures to interact with virtual objects.
- Data gloves: Tight-fitting gloves with sensors to capture finger bend and joint positions. Allow tracking of complex hand poses and gestures.
- Body trackers: Sensors/cameras to track body movement and pose. Allow natural body motions to control and interact in the virtual world.
- Motion trackers: Use cameras/sensors to track position/orientation of user's head, limbs or other body parts. Allow hands-free navigation and interaction in the virtual environment through body gestures and movements.
- Eye trackers: Track eye gaze, blinks, and other eye movements. Allow hands-free selection and control through eye gestures.
- Voice recognition: Recognize voice commands to allow hands-free control and interaction.

Advantages:
- Natural and intuitive to use.
- Allow complex gestures and interactions.

Disadvantages:
- Require tight fit or attachments to body which can be uncomfortable for long use.
- Latency in tracking and recognizing inputs can reduce effectiveness.
- Require robust tracking which can be difficult to achieve.

Applications:
- Virtual reality games and experiences.
- Design and modeling software.
- Training and education software.
- Accessibility tools for disabled users.
