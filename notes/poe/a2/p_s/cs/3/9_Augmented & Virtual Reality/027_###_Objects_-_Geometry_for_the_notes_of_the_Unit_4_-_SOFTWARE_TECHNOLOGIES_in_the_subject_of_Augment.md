## Objects - Geometry

- Objects are the fundamental building blocks of any 3D environment. They are the representations of real-world entities in the virtual world.
- Every object has a geometry which describes its shape and form. The geometry of an object is defined by points, lines, and polygons that enclose a volume of space.
- The geometry data can be represented in various formats such as:
	- **Vertex data**: Contains the positions of each vertex of an object.
	- **Index data**: Contains the indices to the vertex data that define each polygon of the object's surface.
	- **Normal data**: Contains unit vectors perpendicular to each polygon, defining its surface orientation.
	- **Texture coordinates**: Contains texture map coordinates for each vertex of an object.

- The most common types of 3D geometries are:
	- **Polygonal meshes**: Made up of planar polygons (triangles or quads) that approximate the surface shape.
	- **NURBS curves/surfaces**: Based on polynomial formulas to define smooth curved surfaces.
	- **Subdivision surfaces**: Created by repeatedly subdividing planar polygons into smaller polygons until a smooth surface is formed.

- The geometry of an object determines its appearance, motion, and interaction in a 3D scene. Efficient representation and rendering of complex geometries is an important aspect of 3D graphics and AR/VR applications.

- Examples of object geometries: Sphere, cube, cylinder, cone, torus, etc.
- Applications: 3D modeling software, 3D printers, AR/VR apps, 3D games, etc.
