### Visual Displays for 3D User Interfaces

The following are the popular visual display technologies used for 3D User Interfaces:

1. Stereoscopic Displays:
- They present different images to the left and right eyes, creating an illusion of depth.
- Requires the user to wear specialized glasses or head-mounted displays.
- Examples: Active shutter glasses, polarized glasses, autostereoscopic displays.
- Advantages: Inexpensive, realistic depth perception.
- Disadvantages: Limited field of view, causes eye strain, requires additional hardware.

2. Volumetric Displays:
- Create 3D visualizations in actual 3D space, not just on a 2D surface.
- Allow users to see objects from all sides without additional devices.
- Examples: Laser-based, swept-volume, static volume, light field displays.
- Advantages: Do not require additional devices, provide realistic spatial cues.
- Disadvantages: Expensive, complex hardware, limited display size and resolution.

3. Holographic Displays:
- Project light field data using laser, providing light field cues.
- Do not require additional devices and provide a realistic spatial impression.
- Still mostly in research and prototypes.
- Advantages: Do not require additional devices, wide field of view, full parallax.
- Disadvantages: Very expensive, high computational requirements, low resolution.
