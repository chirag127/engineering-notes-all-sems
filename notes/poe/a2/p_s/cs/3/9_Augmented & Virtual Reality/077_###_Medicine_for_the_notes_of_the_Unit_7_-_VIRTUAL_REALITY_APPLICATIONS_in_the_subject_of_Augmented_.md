1. Virtual Surgery Training - VR is used to train surgeons through simulated virtual surgeries. This allows them to practice complex surgeries repeatedly without any risk to patients. They can learn the procedures and techniques effectively.
2. Phobia Treatment - VR is used to treat phobias and anxiety disorders through exposure therapy. The patients are exposed to the things they fear in a controlled virtual environment which helps them become desensitized to the fear stimuli.
3. Pain Distraction - VR can distract patients from pain by immersing them in a virtual experience. This helps reduce the perception and discomfort of pain. It is often used for treating burn patients or during painful medical procedures.
4. Rehabilitation - VR enables patients to do repetitive exercise routines in an engaging way through virtual simulations. This is useful for physical rehabilitation and treating conditions like stroke, Parkinson's disease, etc. The patients can set their own pace and gradually increase difficulty.
5. Telehealth - VR can be combined with sensors and tracking technologies to enable remote consultations between patients and doctors. The doctors can evaluate the patient's condition through virtual visits as if they are in the same room. This provides access to care for people in remote locations.

**Advantages:** Provides safe, controlled, and repeatable environments for training, treatment, and rehabilitation. Enables remote access to healthcare. Can make medical procedures and treatments more engaging and effective.
**Disadvantages:** Requires expensive VR equipment and development of specialized software. May cause negative side effects like nausea or dizziness in some patients. Data privacy and security concerns with telehealth. Reliance on technology can be an issue if there are technical problems.
