### Symbolic Input Techniques

- Symbolic input techniques involve the use of symbolic gestures, postures or props as input to interact with and control the system.
- Some examples are:

#### Gesture Recognition
- Hand gestures are commonly used and recognized using computer vision and machine learning techniques.
- The position and motion of the hands and fingers are tracked and classified into predefined gestures that map to specific inputs or commands.
- Eg. Swiping hand horizontally can be mapped to pan camera left or right, making an 'OK' sign can select an object, etc.

#### Posture or Pose Recognition
- Full body postures or poses can also be used as input.
- The position and orientation of the user's body is tracked and classified into different poses that represent inputs.
- Eg. Standing with arms raised can activate a menu, leaning forward can zoom in, etc.

#### Tangible User Interfaces
- Physical props or artifacts are used as input devices.
- The props are augmented with sensors and recognition markers to sense manipulation of physical artifacts and map them to digital inputs.
- Eg. Physical sliders, dials, sticks, cubes, cards, etc. can be used to provide input in an intuitive way by mimicking physical manipulation.

Advantages:
- Intuitive and easy to learn
- Do not require precise tracking
Disadvantages:
- Limited input bandwidth
- Restricted to predefined gestures/postures/props
- Prone to misclassification errors

Applications: Gaming, Presentations, etc.
