 Here are the design guidelines for 3D travel tasks for the notes of Unit 5 - 3D Interaction Techniques in Augmented & Virtual Reality:

1. Provide contextual cues: Provide sufficient contextual cues to the user about the 3D environment and the task at hand. This helps the user understand the scenario and navigate & interact effectively. Some examples are:
- Arrow markers or highlight indicators to indicate direction of travel or target location.
- Outline or wireframe of objects to provide shape cues.
- Semantic labels or textual instructions.

2. Ensure perceived stability: The 3D environment should provide a sense of stability and avoid conditions that lead to simulator sickness like lags, jitter, etc. Some ways to achieve this are:
- Maintain consistent frame rates.
- Avoid rapid movements, spins or acceleration/deceleration.
- Provide horizon references.
- Include ground planes or reference grids.

3. Choose appropriate navigation metaphor: The navigation metaphor should be intuitive and easy to learn. Some options are:
- Walking in place.
- Pointing or steering.
- Selection from a menu.
Choose based on the desired immersiveness of the experience and user preferences.

4. Keep interfaces simple: The interfaces for interaction should be simple and minimalistic. Complex interfaces can distract the user and be difficult to use. Some tips are:
- Use simple shapes and minimal colors/textures.
- Only include essential and relevant controls/information.
- Provide affordances and feedback on interactions.
