
### Feasibility Study in Software Requirement Specification (SRS)

A feasibility study is a process that assesses the practicality of a proposed software project and provides a detailed report of its potential risks and rewards. It helps stakeholders to assess the potential of a project before investing time and resources into its development.

The feasibility study should be conducted before the software requirements specification (SRS) is created. The SRS is a document that outlines the project's scope, objectives, and requirements. It is used to define the system's functional and non-functional requirements.

The feasibility study should include an analysis of the project's: 

* Marketability 
* Technical feasibility 
* Economic feasibility 
* Operational feasibility 
* Schedule feasibility 
* Legal feasibility

The marketability analysis should include an assessment of the project's potential customers and market size. The technical feasibility analysis should assess the project's technical requirements and the availability of the necessary resources. The economic feasibility analysis should assess the project's cost-benefit analysis. The operational feasibility analysis should assess the project's potential operational risks and rewards. The schedule feasibility analysis should assess the project's timeline and resource availability. Finally, the legal feasibility analysis should assess the project's potential legal risks.

Once the feasibility study is complete, the SRS should be created. The SRS should include detailed descriptions of the system's functions, features, and requirements. It should also include diagrams, tables, and other visuals to help the stakeholders understand the project. 

The feasibility study and SRS should be regularly reviewed and updated as the project progresses. This will help ensure that the project remains within the scope and budget of the stakeholders.
