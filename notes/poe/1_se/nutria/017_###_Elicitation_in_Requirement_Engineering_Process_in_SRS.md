
### Elicitation in Requirement Engineering Process in SRS

* Elicitation is the process of gathering requirements from stakeholders in order to create a system specification. It is an important part of the software requirements specification (SRS) process.
* The goal of elicitation is to identify, analyze, and document the needs and expectations of stakeholders in order to create a system that meets their requirements.
* Elicitation techniques include interviews, surveys, workshops, document analysis, and observation.
* Interviews are the most common elicitation technique and involve asking stakeholders questions to uncover their needs and expectations.
* Surveys are another elicitation technique that involve asking stakeholders a series of questions to uncover their needs and expectations.
* Workshops are a collaborative elicitation technique that involve stakeholders working together to identify and analyze their needs and expectations.
* Document analysis involves examining existing documentation such as user manuals, existing systems, and other relevant documents to uncover stakeholders’ needs and expectations.
* Observation is an elicitation technique that involves observing stakeholders in their natural environment to identify their needs and expectations.
* Mnemonics are a useful tool for remembering the steps of the elicitation process. The acronym “GATHER” can be used to remember the steps of the elicitation process: 
  * **G**ather information 
  * **A**nalyze the information 
  * **T**ranslate the information 
  * **H**arvest the information 
  * **E**valuate the information 
* It is important to remember that the elicitation process is iterative and should be repeated as needed to ensure that all stakeholders’ needs and expectations are met.
