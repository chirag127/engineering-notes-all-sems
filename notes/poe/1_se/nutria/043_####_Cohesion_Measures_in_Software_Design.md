
### Cohesion Measures in Software Design

Cohesion measures are used to measure the degree of coupling between software modules. They are used to identify the degree of relatedness between the components of a software system. Cohesion measures can be used to assess the modularity of a software system, identify potential problems, and optimize the design of a system.

There are four main types of cohesion measures: 

1. **Functional Cohesion**: This measure assesses the degree of relatedness between the functions of a module. A module with high functional cohesion contains functions that are related to each other and serve a single purpose. 

2. **Logical Cohesion**: This measure assesses the degree of relatedness between the data elements of a module. A module with high logical cohesion contains data elements that are related to each other and serve a single purpose. 

3. **Temporal Cohesion**: This measure assesses the degree of relatedness between the tasks of a module. A module with high temporal cohesion contains tasks that are related to each other and serve a single purpose. 

4. **Communicational Cohesion**: This measure assesses the degree of relatedness between the messages of a module. A module with high communicational cohesion contains messages that are related to each other and serve a single purpose. 

Mnemonics and learning tricks for these cohesion measures can be helpful for remembering and understanding the different types of cohesion. For example, the acronym **FLTC** can be used to remember the four types of cohesion: Functional, Logical, Temporal, and Communicational. 

In software design, it is important to consider the degree of cohesion between the components of a system. High cohesion ensures that the system is well-structured, maintainable, and extensible. Low cohesion, on the other hand, can lead to a system that is difficult to maintain and extend. Thus, cohesion measures can be used to assess the modularity of a system and identify potential problems.
