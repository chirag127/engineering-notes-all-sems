### Prototype Model in SDLC

The Prototype Model is a software development model in which a prototype (an early incomplete version of the software) is first built, then it is refined to get the final complete software.

The key steps in the Prototype Model are:

1. Requirements Analysis - Requirements of the software product are gathered from the customer.
2. Quick Design - A quick design of the prototype is made.
3. Building Prototype - The prototype is built quickly using some tools and techniques.
4. Customer Evaluation - The prototype is evaluated by the customer and user and their feedback is gathered.
5. Refining Prototype - The prototype is refined and enhanced based on the customer feedback. More prototype revisions may be developed.
6. Development of Complete Software - The complete software is developed based on the refined prototype.

Advantages:

- Customer involvement is high. The customer can see the prototype and provide feedback early in the process. This avoids misunderstandings and reduces risks.
- Problems can be detected earlier as the prototype provides an early vision of the software.
- Flexibility is high. Changes can be incorporated at the prototype stage, so less rework is required later.

Disadvantages:

- Extra time and cost may be required to build prototypes.
- The prototype and the final software may differ considerably, leading to extra work.
- The customer may expect the prototype to be the final software and may not be willing to accept a product that is different from the prototype.

Applications: When requirements are not clear, for new technology areas, for user interfaces, and for high risk projects.

Mnemonics:
Quick and dirty,
Evaluate and refine.
