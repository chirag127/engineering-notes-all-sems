### Similarity and Differences from Conventional Engineering Processes

Conventional engineering processes like waterfall model follow a sequential approach where each phase must be completed before moving on to the next one. Some key similarities and differences between conventional engineering processes and agile methodology are:

**Similarities:**
- Clearly defined requirements and goals. Agile also starts with a vision or roadmap of the final product.
- Team formation and project planning. As with conventional methods, agile also involves identification and assignment of team members and planning the schedule, budget, etc.

**Differences:**
- Static vs dynamic requirements. Conventional methods assume requirements are well-defined upfront whereas agile methods acknowledge that requirements evolve over time and embrace change.
- Sequential vs iterative approach. Conventional methods follow a sequential lifecycle (analyze, design, develop, test, deploy) while agile methods take an iterative approach, rapidly cycling through phases to adapt to changes.
- Heavy documentation vs working software. Conventional methods rely heavily on documentation while agile prioritizes delivering working software over comprehensive documentation. Documentation is produced as needed.

Some key advantages of the agile approach are faster delivery, higher quality, and increased customer satisfaction. However, it may not be suitable for smaller projects or those with strict constraints on budget, resources, or timelines. The choice between conventional and agile methods depends on various factors specific to a project and organization.
