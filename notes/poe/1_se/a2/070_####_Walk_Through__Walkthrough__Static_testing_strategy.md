#### Walk Through (Walkthrough) Static testing strategy

Walkthrough is an informal method of static testing where the developers and testers step through the code or document together, with one person explaining the logic and verifying expected behaviors, while others review and provide feedback.

**Advantages:**

- Easy to perform.
- Inexpensive.
- Quick feedback.
- Finds logical defects and gaps in functionality or requirements.

**Disadvantages:**

- Dependent on the individuals involved and their focus/ thoroughness.
- Does not provide objective measurements or statistics.
- May miss defects if reviewers are not familiar with the system or are not meticulous.

**Steps:**

1. Schedule a meeting with developers, testers, and requirements/business analysts.
2. Review use cases/requirements and talk through the flow of events/logic.
3. Step through the code/document line by line (or section by section) and explain the logic.
4. Discuss exceptions/alternate flows and verify that these are properly handled.
5. Capture issues/Questions/feedback and assign responsibility for resolution.
6. Repeat as needed.

**Mnemonics:**

- Think of a "walkthrough" like walking through a house and exploring how it's built and how all the pieces fit together. You're looking for any issues, gaps, or needed improvements.

**Examples:** Walkthroughs are commonly done for requirements specs, design docs, and code. They are often an informal precursor to more rigorous inspections/reviews.

**Applications:** Walkthroughs are lightweight and inexpensive, so they can be applied early and often in the SDLC to identify and resolve issues earlier before they grow into larger problems. They are a good way to spread knowledge and buy-in about a system/feature among stakeholders.
