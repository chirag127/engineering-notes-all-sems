### Software Quality Frameworks (SQF) in SRS

The Software Requirements Specification (SRS) document specifies the requirements for a software system. It is important that the requirements are complete, consistent, unambiguous, verifiable, and meet the quality standards. Some of the commonly used Software Quality Frameworks (SQF) that can be incorporated in the SRS are:

- **McCall's Quality Model**: It focuses on three quality factors - correctness, reliability, and efficiency. The SRS can specify requirements related to accuracy, fault tolerance, resource utilization, etc. to address these quality factors.
- **ISO 9126**: It defines six quality characteristics - functionality, reliability, usability, efficiency, maintainability, and portability. The SRS can include requirements pertaining to these characteristics such as functional suitability, accuracy, fault tolerance, time behavior, compatibility, etc.
- **IEEE 1061**: It provides a checklist of more than 50 software quality factors grouped under characteristics such as functionality, reliability, usability, efficiency, maintainability, and portability. The SRS can enumerate requirements to satisfy the relevant quality factors from this checklist.

**Mnemonics**:

- Correctness, Reliability, Efficiency = McCall's Quality Model
- Functionality, Reliability, Usability, Efficiency, Maintainability, Portability = ISO 9126
- 50+ Factors under 6 Characteristics = IEEE 1061

**Advantages**: Incorporating SQF in SRS leads to high-quality software that meets stakeholders' needs.
**Disadvantages**: It may lead to an SRS with too many requirements making it complicated to use. The SQF may not be universally applicable to all software.
**Examples**: The system shall have an accuracy of 99% (Correctness as per McCall's Model). The system shall have a fault tolerance of less than 5 faults per million transactions (Reliability as per ISO 9126).
