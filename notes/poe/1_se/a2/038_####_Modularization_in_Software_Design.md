#### Modularization in Software Design

Modularization refers to the process of breaking down a software system into multiple modules (independent and self-contained units) that can be designed, developed and tested separately. Some key points about modularization are:

-   It increases reusability of code as modules can be reused in other applications.
-   It enhances maintainability as modules can be maintained or modified independently, reducing impact on other modules.
-   It enables parallel development of modules by different teams.
-   It facilitates distributed development and integration.
-   It enhances understandability and manageability of the software system by breaking it down into manageable parts.

Some tips for effective modularization:

-   Identify functional units in the system and define modules around them.
-   Define clear interfaces/contracts between modules specifying services required and provided.
-   Minimize interdependencies between modules.
-   Keep modules cohesive and focused on specific functions.
-   Keep modules small and lightweight for ease of understanding.

Advantages of modularization:

-   Reduced complexity.
-   Increased reusability.
-   Better maintainability.
-   Parallel and distributed development.
-   Fault isolation.

Disadvantages of modularization:

-   Added overhead due to increased interfaces.
-   Difficult to determine right granularity of modules.
-   Intermodule communication can affect performance.
-   Dependencies between modules can lead to cascading effects.
