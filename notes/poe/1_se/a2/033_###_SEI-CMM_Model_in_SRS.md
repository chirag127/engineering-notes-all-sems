### SEI-CMM Model in SRS

The Software Engineering Institute's Capability Maturity Model (SEI-CMM) is a framework that describes five levels of organizational maturity in process improvement. It can be applied to the software requirements specification (SRS) development process to evaluate and improve the SRS process.

The five maturity levels in increasing order are:

1. Initial - The SRS process is ad hoc, chaotic and random. Success depends on luck and heroics.
2. Repeatable - The SRS process is repeatable but may not be defined. Basic project management practices are established to achieve consistent results.
3. Defined - The SRS process is defined and documented. Standard processes and procedures are established and subject to periodic improvement.
4. Managed - The SRS process is quantitatively managed and controlled. Performance is measured and corrective actions taken when necessary.
5. Optimizing - The SRS process is improved continuously by exploiting new technologies and feedback to meet relevant quality and process performance objectives.

**Mnemonics:**

- I RRDeMO - Initial, Repeatable, Defined, Managed, Optimizing
- Process Maturity Mountain - Climbing the 5 levels

**Advantages:** Provides a systematic process improvement approach. Identifies weaknesses and suggests improvements.

**Disadvantages:** Difficult to assess maturity level. Heavyweight model that requires significant time and resources.

**Applications:** Widely used by organizations to evaluate and improve their software development processes including SRS.
