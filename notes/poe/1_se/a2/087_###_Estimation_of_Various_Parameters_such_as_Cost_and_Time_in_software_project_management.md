### Estimation of Various Parameters such as Cost and Time in software project management

-   **Estimation techniques:** There are various estimation techniques used to estimate cost and time parameters for software projects such as:
    -   **Expert judgment:** Experts estimate the parameters based on their experience. Useful for small projects but prone to bias.
    -   **Analogous estimation:** Estimates are based on historical data from similar past projects. Adjustments are made for differences in scale or complexity. Prone to errors if projects are not truly similar.
    -   **Parametric estimation:** Estimates are calculated using statistical techniques and project metrics like lines of code. Requires historical data and is more quantitative but can be inaccurate if metrics are miscalculated.
-   **Three-point estimation:** A weighted average of three estimates is calculated:
    -   **Optimistic (o):** Best case scenario.
    -   **Most likely (m):** Realistic scenario.
    -   **Pessimistic (p):** Worst case scenario.
    -   Estimates are calculated as (o + 4m + p) / 6. Useful as it considers variability and risk but still depends on the accuracy of individual estimates.
-   **Estimation checklists:** Lists of standard tasks and estimates of time/cost required for each. Useful as a starting point but individual project details must be considered for accurate estimates.
-   **Learning curve considerations:** As team gains experience, estimates may decrease. The law of diminishing returns applies - efficiency gains decrease over time. Historical data must account for learning curves to produce accurate estimates for new projects.
-   **Risks and uncertainty:** Software project estimates are uncertain and prone to risks/unknowns. Sensitivity analysis determines impact of variables on estimates. Risk management techniques like risk mitigation and contingency allowance can handle uncertainty. Buffer/contingency time/cost is added to estimates to account for risks.

**Mnemonics:**

-   **PERT:** Program Evaluation and Review Technique. Uses three-point estimation.
-   **COCOMO:** Constructive Cost Model. Uses parametric estimation based on lines of code.

**Advantages:** Quantitative, considers risks, uses expert knowledge.
**Disadvantages:** Prone to bias/inaccuracy, requires historical data which may not exist for new types of projects.
**Applications:** Producing project schedules/budgets, resource allocation, monitoring/control.
