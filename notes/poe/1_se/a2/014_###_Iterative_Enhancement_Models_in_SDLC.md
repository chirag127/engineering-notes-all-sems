### Iterative Enhancement Models in SDLC

Iterative enhancement models involve gradually building software prototypes and evolving them into the complete system. This process is repeated in a series of cycles where each cycle involves adding or modifying a set of functionality to each prototype. Some popular iterative models are:

- Rapid Application Development (RAD): Focuses on building prototypes and then incrementally enhancing them. The entire process is people-centric and emphasizes user involvement. Usually consists of 4 stages: Business modeling, Data modeling, Process modeling, and Construction. Advantages include fast development and user satisfaction. Disadvantages include lack of scalability and documentation.
- Incremental Model: Involves breaking down the entire software into increments or subsets. Each increment is thoroughly tested before the next increment is built. This model combines the elements of the waterfall model with the iterative philosophy of prototyping. It has advantages of early user feedback and risk mitigation but can be more time-consuming.
- Spiral Model: Combines the idea of iterative development with the systematic, controlled aspects of the waterfall model. It comprises of 4 quadrants: Planning, Risk Analysis, Engineering and Evaluation. Each quadrant feeds into the next, representing increasing levels of detail. This model is ideal for large, expensive, and complicated projects. However, it can be a costly model to implement.

Some mnemonics or learning tricks for these models could be:

- RAD: Rapidly build prototypes, users involved
- Incremental: Break into increments, test then build
- Spiral: Plan -> Risk analyze -> Engineer -> Evaluate in cycles
