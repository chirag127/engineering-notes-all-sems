#### Design Structure Charts in Software Design

Design Structure Charts (DSCs) are a formalism for representing and analyzing systems and software architectures. They are a hierarchical and graphical way to represent components of a system and their interactions.

Advantages of DSCs:

- They are easy to understand as they use graphical representations.
- They show the decomposition of a system into subsystems and components.
- They explicitly show the interactions between components.
- They support analysis of architectures, for example, to find dependencies or detect potential structural problems.

Components in a DSC are represented as boxes and their interactions as arrows. The following are some key aspects of DSCs:

- Components can be refined into subcomponents to represent a hierarchy.
- Interactions can be asynchronous (dotted arrow) or synchronous (solid arrow).
- Multiple inputs or outputs are indicated by forked arrows.
- Constraints and non-determinism are indicated by dashed arrows.

Mnemonics to remember:

- Boxes are components. Arrows are interactions.
- Solid arrows are synchronous. Dotted arrows are asynchronous.
- Forked arrows show multiple inputs/outputs.
- Dashed arrows show constraints/non-determinism.

Advantages of DSCs:

- They explicitly show components and connections between them, making dependencies clear.
- They support architectural analysis, e.g., finding cycles that could lead to deadlock.
- They are easy to understand as they use a simple graphical notation.
- They can be mapped to/from other notations, e.g., UML component diagrams.

Disadvantages of DSCs:

- They do not scale well to very large systems as diagrams can become complex.
- They provide a static view and do not show dynamic aspects well, e.g., sequencing/timing.
- There are no widely-used tools to support DSCs, though they can be drawn using general-purpose tools.
