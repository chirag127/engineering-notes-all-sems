#### Formal Technical Reviews (Peer Reviews) Static testing strategy

- Formal Technical Reviews (FTRs), also known as Peer Reviews, are a structured static testing technique where a team of qualified personnel examines the suitability of a work product (e.g., requirements specification, design document, code) and identifies potential issues.
- Led by a Moderator, team members systematically go through the work product, line by line, to find and record defects/issues.
- The process is highly methodical and rigorous. Typically, multiple passes are made by different subsets of team members to identify increasingly subtle issues.
- Advantages: Can uncover a wide range of issues; facilitates knowledge transfer and team learning; can be less expensive than other techniques for small, localized work products.
- Disadvantages: Can be time-consuming and tedious; success depends on expertise and diligence of reviewers; may miss integration/system-level issues; work products must be suitable for line-by-line review.
- Mnemonics: PAY-PAL - Participants, Assumptions, Your understanding, Peer reviews, Analysis, Lead.
- Examples: Code inspections, Design reviews, Document reviews.
- Applications: Requirements reviews, Architecture reviews, Test case reviews.
