### Spiral Model in SDLC

The spiral model is a software development process combining elements of both design and prototyping-in-stages, in an effort to combine advantages of top-down and bottom-up concepts.

* Points:

1. The spiral model consists of four phases: Planning, Risk Analysis, Engineering and Evaluation.

2. Software is developed in a series of incremental releases. Each release is a spiral through the phases.

3. The spiral model is an iterative model. Each iteration involves an increment of product release.

4. Risk handling is one of the key points of this model. Risks are identified and resolved throughout each phase of each iteration.

5. The spiral model allows for incremental releases of the product, which can be very useful in getting customer feedback along the way.

* Mnemonics/Learning Tricks:

PLRE Model - Planning, Risk analysis, Engineering and Evaluation.
Taking risks inloops.
Releasing in chunks.

* Advantages:

- High amount of risk analysis hence, avoidance of risks.
- Software is released in increments so can get customer feedback.
- Good for large and expensive projects.
- Can accommodate changes to requirements.

* Disadvantages:

- Can be a costly model to follow.
- Needs very precise initial requirements.
- Needs high level of risk management.
