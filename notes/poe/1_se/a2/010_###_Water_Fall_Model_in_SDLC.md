### Water Fall Model in SDLC

The Waterfall model is a sequential design process, often used in software development processes, in which progress is seen as flowing steadily downwards (like a waterfall) through the phases of conception, initiation, analysis, design, construction, testing, production/implementation and maintenance.

The waterfall model:

-   Is a sequential model. Each phase must be completed before the next phase can begin.
-   Emphasizes that distinct stages occur in sequence and that outputs of one stage become inputs to the next stage.
-   Requirements are fully collected before design begins.
-   No iterations between development phases are expected.

Advantages:

-   Simple and easy to understand.
-   Stages and workflows are well defined and easy to manage due to rigidity.
-   Works well for smaller projects where requirements are very well understood.

Disadvantages:

-   Rigid model and difficult to accommodate changes later in the process.
-   In practice, requirements change and there can be a need for iteration. The waterfall model does not support iteration well.
-   Risks and uncertainties are not accounted for. Problems may emerge after a stage is completed.
-   Can lead to products/systems that do not meet customer requirements well.

Mnemonics:

-   Imagine a 'falling' waterfall to remember the sequential phases of the model - Conception, Initiation, Analysis, Design, Construction, Testing, Implementation, Maintenance.
-   'Can't Integrate? Try Again!' - to emphasize that each phase must be completed before the next phase. Iteration is difficult in this model.

Applications:

-   Projects where requirements are well understood and stable.
-   Projects following a structured approach with milestone stages.
-   Simple, small projects with fixed timelines and budgets.

Examples of use: Early software development processes, construction/engineering projects with well-defined requirements.
