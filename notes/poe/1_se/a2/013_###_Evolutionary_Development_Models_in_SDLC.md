### Evolutionary Development Models in SDLC

The evolutionary development models in Software Development Life Cycle (SDLC) are:

1. Incremental Model: In this model, the software is developed in increments or parts. Each increment adds some functionality to the software. The key advantage is that it produces a working product quickly and risks can be mitigated as each increment is tested. The disadvantages are that it is difficult to estimate costs and timelines and integration of increments could be challenging.
Mnemonic: Develop software in parts to get working product early and mitigate risks.

2. Spiral Model: This model combines iterative development process model with systematic, controlled aspects of the waterfall model. The software is developed in iterative phases called spirals. Each spiral involves planning, risk analysis, engineering and evaluation. It allows for incremental releases of the product, as well as periodic review and adaptation to changes. The key disadvantage is that it could be costly to implement.
Mnemonic: Combination of iterative and waterfall models with risk-analysis in each spiral for incremental releases.

3. Agile Model: Agile methods like Extreme Programming (XP), Scrum, etc. involve iterative and incremental development using self-organizing teams. The key features are adapting to changes, early delivery, continuous testing and integration. The advantages are that it is flexible and delivers working software early. The disadvantages are that it could be difficult to estimate costs and timelines and it may not be suitable for large, mission-critical projects.
Mnemonic: Flexible, iterative approach delivering working software early through self-organizing teams.
