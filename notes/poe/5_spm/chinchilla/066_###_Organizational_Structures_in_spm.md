### Organizational Structures in SPM

Organizational structure refers to the way in which an organization is structured and how its various departments, functions, and people are organized. In project management, organizational structure plays a critical role in determining how projects are executed and how resources are managed. There are various types of organizational structures that can be used in project management, each with its own advantages and disadvantages.

#### Functional Organizational Structure

In a functional organizational structure, employees are grouped according to their specialized skills and functions, such as accounting, marketing, human resources, etc. This structure is hierarchical, with each employee reporting to a department head who reports to the CEO. Projects are managed within the functional departments, and project managers are responsible for coordinating activities within their department. This structure is best suited for organizations that have a stable environment and routine projects.

#### Project-Based Organizational Structure

In a project-based organizational structure, employees are organized around specific projects rather than functional departments. Project teams are formed and are responsible for executing the project from start to finish. Project managers have full authority over the project and have direct access to resources. This structure is best suited for organizations that have a high level of project activity and require flexibility in managing resources.

#### Matrix Organizational Structure

In a matrix organizational structure, employees are organized around both functional departments and project teams. This structure combines the strengths of both functional and project-based structures. Project managers have access to resources from various functional departments, and employees can work on multiple projects simultaneously. However, this structure can lead to conflicts over priorities and resources.

#### Composite Organizational Structure

In a composite organizational structure, employees are organized around both functional departments and projects, but the project manager has more authority than in a matrix structure. Project managers have full authority over the project, and functional managers provide resources as needed. This structure is best suited for organizations that have a mix of routine and non-routine projects and require flexibility in managing resources.

Mnemonics: 

- "FPPC" - Functional, Project-Based, Matrix, Composite. This can help you remember the four types of organizational structures in SPM.

Learning Tricks:

- To remember the differences between the various types of organizational structures, create a chart or table that lists the advantages and disadvantages of each structure. This can help you quickly recall the key points during an exam or when managing a project.
