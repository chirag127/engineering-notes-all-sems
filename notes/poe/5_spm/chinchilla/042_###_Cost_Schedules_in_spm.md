### Cost Schedules in SPM

Cost schedules in Strategic Performance Management (SPM) refer to the graphical representation of the relationship between the total cost and the level of output or production. Cost schedules are essential in SPM because they help organizations determine the most efficient production levels and make informed decisions that maximize profits.

#### Types of Cost Schedules

There are two main types of cost schedules in SPM, namely:

1. Total Cost Schedule: This schedule shows the total cost of production at different levels of output. It is obtained by adding up the fixed costs and variable costs of production.

2. Average Cost Schedule: This schedule shows the average cost per unit of output at different levels of production. It is obtained by dividing the total cost by the level of output.

#### Learning Tricks and Mnemonics

Some learning tricks and mnemonics that can help in remembering cost schedules in SPM include:

- Total cost is the sum of fixed costs and variable costs. (TFC + TVC = TC)
- Average cost is total cost divided by the level of output. (AC = TC/Q)

#### Advantages of Cost Schedules

- Cost schedules help organizations determine the most efficient production levels that maximize profits.
- They help organizations identify the break-even point, which is the level of production at which the total cost equals the total revenue.
- Cost schedules enable organizations to determine the price at which they should sell their products to maximize profits.

#### Disadvantages of Cost Schedules

- Cost schedules assume that all inputs are variable, which may not be the case in reality.
- They do not consider external factors such as changes in market demand and government regulations that may affect production costs and revenues.

#### Applications of Cost Schedules

Cost schedules are commonly used in SPM to:

- Determine the most efficient production levels that maximize profits.
- Identify the break-even point and the price at which products should be sold to maximize profits.
- Compare the costs of different production methods and make informed decisions on which method to adopt.
