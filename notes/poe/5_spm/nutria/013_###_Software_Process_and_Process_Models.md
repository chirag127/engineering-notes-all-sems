
### Software Process and Process Models

Software Process and Process Models are a set of activities that are used to create, maintain, and deliver software products. They provide a framework for understanding the development life cycle and help developers to plan, organize, and control their work. 

The most commonly used process models are the Waterfall Model, the Iterative Model, and the Agile Model. 

#### Waterfall Model
The Waterfall Model is a sequential process model that follows a linear progression. It is the most traditional and widely used process model. It involves a series of steps that are performed in order, with each step building on the results of the previous step. The steps include Requirements Gathering, Design, Implementation, Testing, Integration and Deployment.

#### Iterative Model
The Iterative Model is an incremental process model that involves multiple cycles of development. It starts with the initial development of a software product and then follows a series of iterative cycles to refine and improve the product. Each cycle includes the same basic steps as the Waterfall Model, but with more focus on testing and feedback.

#### Agile Model
The Agile Model is an adaptive process model that focuses on collaboration, flexibility, and responsiveness. It is based on the idea of iterative and incremental development, but with an emphasis on collaboration and customer feedback. The Agile Model is designed to be more flexible and responsive to changing requirements and customer needs.

Mnemonics:
- WATERFALL: Requirements Gathering, Design, Implementation, Testing, Integration and Deployment
- ITERATIVE: Iterative cycles to refine and improve the product
- AGILE: Adaptive process model that focuses on Collaboration, Flexibility, and Responsiveness
