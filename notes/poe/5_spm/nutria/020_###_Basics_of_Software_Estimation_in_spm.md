
### Basics of Software Estimation in SPM
Software Project Management (SPM) is the application of knowledge, skills, tools, and techniques to project activities to meet the project requirements. Software estimation is a key part of SPM. Software estimation is the process of predicting the most realistic amount of effort required to develop or maintain software based on incomplete, uncertain and noisy input. 

The following are some of the key concepts related to software estimation:

* **Goals:** Estimation should be based on the project's goals and objectives. Estimation should also consider the project's constraints such as cost, schedule, and quality.

* **Scope:** Estimation should take into account the scope of the project, which includes the features, functions, and components that will be included in the software.

* **Risk:** Estimation should include the identification and assessment of risks that may impact the project.

* **Effort:** Estimation should include an estimate of the effort required to complete the project, including the time and resources required.

* **Tools:** Estimation should include the use of appropriate tools and techniques such as expert judgment, historical data, analogy, and simulation.

* **Accuracy:** Estimation should strive for accuracy by using appropriate methods and techniques, and by validating the estimates with stakeholders.

* **Mnemonics:** Estimation can be aided by the use of mnemonics such as COCOMO (Cost, Organizational, Complexity, Objectives, and Measured) and FPA (Function Point Analysis).

* **Learning Tricks:** Estimation can be aided by learning tricks such as breaking down a project into small tasks and estimating each task separately. This can help to reduce the uncertainty of the overall estimate.
