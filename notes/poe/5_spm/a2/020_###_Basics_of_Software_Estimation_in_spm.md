### Basics of Software Estimation in spm

Software estimation is the process of predicting the effort required to develop a software product. It is one of the most challenging and critical tasks in software project management. Accurate estimation is important for creating realistic schedules and budgets. Some key points to keep in mind for software estimation:

- Requirements analysis: The first step is to understand the requirements of the software product. This forms the basis of estimation as the effort and time taken would depend on the scope and complexity of requirements.
- Choose an estimation technique: Some popular techniques are expert judgment, analogy, bottom-up, top-down, etc. Expert judgment and analogy are informal while the others are more formal quantitative techniques. Choose a technique suitable for the project and team.
- Breakdown into smaller components: It is easier to estimate smaller modules or tasks rather than estimate the entire project at once. Breakdown the software into components and estimate each component separately.
- Consider risks and uncertainties: Software development is prone to risks and uncertainties which can affect the estimates. It is important to consider such factors while estimating and add a buffer to the estimates to account for risks.
- Get input from the team: Estimation should not be done by a single person. Getting input from the entire team and arriving at a consensus leads to better estimates. Individual team members can provide estimates for modules they are responsible for.
- Track progress and update: The initial estimates would be rough and will change as the project progresses. It is important to track the actual progress and update the estimates periodically. This helps in managing schedules and budgets effectively.

Some tips for easy learning -

- Think of the software as lego blocks - first estimate individual blocks, then assemble to get the total estimate.
- For each component, estimate - best case, worst case and likely case. The likely case is the final estimate.
- Add buffer of 25-30% to the likely case estimate to account for risks.
- Get the team to estimate anonymously at first to avoid bias, then discuss and arrive at a consensus.
