### The Oldham-Hackman Job Characteristic Model in spm

The Oldham-Hackman Job Characteristic Model states that there are five core job characteristics that lead to critical psychological states in the individual, which in turn lead to work-related outcomes. The five core job characteristics are:

1. Skill Variety: The degree to which a job requires a variety of different activities and skills to carry out the work. Having a variety of skills leads to the psychological state of experienced meaningfulness.

2. Task Identity: The degree to which the job involves identifiable outcomes or products. Having a complete piece of work from start to finish leads to experienced responsibility.

3. Task Significance: The degree to which the job affects the lives or work of other people. Having a significant impact on others leads to knowledge of the actual results of one's work.

4. Autonomy: The degree to which the job provides freedom, independence, and discretion to the individual. Having autonomy leads to experienced responsibility for work outcomes.

5. Feedback: The degree to which individuals receive direct information about their performance. Having feedback leads to knowledge of results as it allows individuals to assess how well they are performing.

The critical psychological states then lead to the following outcomes:

- Higher internal work motivation
- Higher job satisfaction
- Lower absenteeism and turnover

The strength of the Oldham-Hackman Job Characteristic Model is that it identifies specific job characteristics that can be designed into jobs to improve motivation and other work-related outcomes. However, the model does not consider individual differences in reactions to the job characteristics. What leads to positive outcomes for one person may not for another. The model also takes a universalistic approach, assuming the same job characteristics will lead to the same outcomes for all people. In reality, cultural differences may influence reactions to the characteristics.
