### Network Planning Models in spm

The following are the key network planning models used in spm:

1. Traffic Requirements Model: This model focuses on estimating the traffic requirements of a network based on factors like calling rates, average call duration, etc. The traffic requirements are then used to determine the capacity requirements of the network.

Advantage: Helps determine capacity requirements and budgeting for network expansion.
Disadvantage: Does not consider the topology and components of the network.

2. Transmission Facilities Model: This model focuses on estimating the requirements of transmission equipment and facilities like fiber cables, repeaters, etc. based on the traffic requirements and network topology.

Advantage: Helps determine equipment and facilities budgeting for network expansion.
Disadvantage: Does not consider the impact of equipment and facilities on network performance.

3. Switching Facilities Model: This model focuses on estimating the requirements of switching equipment like switches and routers based on the network topology and traffic requirements.

Advantage: Helps determine equipment budgeting for network expansion.
Disadvantage: Does not consider the impact of switching equipment on network delay and throughput.

4. Delay and Throughput Model: This model analyzes the delay, throughput and performance of a network based on the topology, components and traffic requirements. It helps in identifying potential bottleneck areas in the network.

Advantage: Provides performance metrics to evaluate different design options.
Disadvantage: Does not directly provide budget estimates.

The planning models can be used in combination to analyze various aspects of a network and arrive at an optimum design solution based on requirements and constraints. They help in systematic network planning and management.
