### PERT Technique in spm

The PERT Technique (Program Evaluation and Review Technique) is a statistical tool used to analyze and represent the uncertainty in the completion times of project activities. It deals with probabilistic time estimates for each activity rather than deterministic time estimates as used in CPM.

The key steps in PERT are:

1. Identify the specific activities and milestones of the project
2. Determine the optimistic time (O), most likely time (M), and pessimistic time (P) for each activity
3. Calculate the expected time (TE) and variance (VAR) for each activity using the following formulas:

TE = (O + 4M + P) / 6
VAR = (P - O) / 6

4. Draw the network diagram and determine the critical path and project duration
5. Determine the probability of finishing the project within a specified time using the Beta Distribution for calculating probabilities

The advantages of PERT over CPM are:

- It considers uncertainty in activity completion times through minimum, maximum, and expected times
- It provides a probabilistic measure of achieving completion within a specified time
- It accounts for both optimism bias and pessimism in estimates through the three time estimates for each activity

The main disadvantages are:

- It requires additional effort to obtain the three time estimates for each activity
- It involves more complex calculations than CPM to determine expected times and probabilities
