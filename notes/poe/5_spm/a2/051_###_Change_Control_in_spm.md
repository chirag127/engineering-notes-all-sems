### Change Control in spm

- Change control refers to the process of managing changes to configuration items (CIs), including software components. It aims to minimize disruption of services through properly evaluating, approving, and controlling changes.
- In spm, change control involves the following steps:

1. Change request - A change request is raised by stakeholders when a change is needed. It should describe the change, reason for change, and impact analysis.
2. Change evaluation - The change request is evaluated by the change management board (CMB) for approval. Impact assessment and risk analysis are done. Alternative solutions may be proposed.
3. Change approval - If the CMB approves the change, it is formally authorized to proceed. Resources, timelines, and schedules would be defined.
4. Change implementation - The authorized change is implemented. Dev and testing are done as needed. rollback plans should be in place in case of issues.
5. Change review - After implementation, a post-implementation review is done to assess success of change, issues encountered, and lessons learned. Necessary adjustments are made.

Advantages:
- Ensures changes are properly evaluated to minimize disruptions and risks.
- Brings alignment between changes and business/IT strategies.
- Provides transparency on the status of changes.

Disadvantages:
- Can introduce delays in implementing changes due to the evaluation process.
- Additional costs are incurred in managing the change control process.
