### Risk Evaluation in spm

- Risk evaluation is the process of assessing the likelihood and consequences of adverse events that could negatively impact project objectives.
- In spm, risk evaluation is done by analyzing the risk events/scenarios identified in the risk identification process and assessing:
-- Likelihood: The probability or frequency of occurrence of a risk event. Likelihood can be rated subjectively (low, medium, high) or objectively using quantitative analysis.
-- Consequences: The impact (positive or negative) of risk events if they occur. Consequences can be quantified in terms of cost, time, quality, etc.
- Based on likelihood and consequences, risk events are prioritized by risk score/impact to determine which risks need attention or risk response planning. Higher risk score risks should be mitigated or managed first.
- Some tips for effective risk evaluation:
-- Focus on major risks, don't get bogged down by minor risks.
-- Use a standard scale/rating system for likelihood and consequences to facilitate comparisons.
-- Consider both optimistic and pessimistic scenarios in evaluations.
-- Get input from experts and those who will be affected by the risks.
-- Document the risk evaluation approach and results.

- Mnemonics:
-- LIMPET: Likelihood, Impact, Manageability, Perceived confidence, Exposure time, Trends (for factors to consider in risk evaluation)
-- Crossed fingers: Consequences, Reach, Ownership, Severity, Frequency, Effort, Nature (of risk), Dependency (on tasks/events)
