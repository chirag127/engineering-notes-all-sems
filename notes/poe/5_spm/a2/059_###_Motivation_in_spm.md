### Motivation in spm

- SPM or smoothened particle hydrodynamics is a computational fluid dynamics technique used to simulate the motion of fluids.
- The key motivation behind using SPM is its ability to handle large deformations and ruptures in fluids with ease as compared to other CFD techniques like finite volume method or finite element method.
- SPM represents fluids as particles with properties like position, velocity, density, etc. and the interactions between particles are modeled using kernel functions. This particle nature makes SPM handle discontinuities and ruptures naturally without the need for special treatments.
- SPM is also relatively easy to implement and the calculations are less complex when compared to other CFD techniques. This makes SPM a attractive choice for simulating highly nonlinear fluid flows.
- However, SPM has some disadvantages like numerical dissipation and lack of conservation of angular momentum. It also requires more computational power when simulating high Reynolds number flows due to the large number of particles required.
- Some mnemonics to remember the key motivation of SPM:
› Particles can handle ruptures - representing fluids using particles helps handle discontinuities
› Less complex - calculations are simpler than other CFD techniques
› Handle large deformations - SPM can simulate large fluid deformations and movements easily
