### Setting Objectives in spm

- Clearly define the objectives of your study in spm. The objectives should be:
- Specific - Focus on a specific area of interest within the broad field of study. For example, if studying consumer behaviour, focus on a specific aspect such as factors influencing impulse purchasing.
- Measurable - Include specific variables or factors that can be measured or assessed to determine if the objectives have been achieved. For example, measure the effect of pricing discounts on impulse purchases.
- Achievable - Ensure the objectives are realistic and can be achieved within the required time frame and with the available resources. A limited scope may be necessary for undergraduate research projects or in cases where limited time or resources are available.
- Relevant - Ensure the objectives are relevant to existing gaps in knowledge or areas needing further investigation. Align the objectives with current trends or issues in the field of study. For example, study how consumer behaviour is influenced by mobile and digital technologies.
- Time-bound - Set specific timelines for achieving different aspects of the objectives. This helps provide structure to the study and ensures timely completion of the project.

Advantages of clearly defined objectives:
- Provide focus and guidance to the study.
- Enable assessment of progress and achievement.
- Help determine if deviations or changes are needed.
- Inform readers of the purpose and direction of the study.

Disadvantages of unclear or poorly defined objectives:
- Lack of focus can lead to an unfocused study that does not address relevant issues.
- Difficult to determine if objectives have been achieved if they are not measurable.
- Objectives that are too broad, not achievable or not relevant can lead to an ineffective study.
- Lack of timelines can result in delays or rushed work toward the end of the project.
