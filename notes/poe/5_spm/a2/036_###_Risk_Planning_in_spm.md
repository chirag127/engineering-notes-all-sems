### Risk Planning in spm

Risk planning is an important aspect of project management. Some key points to remember about risk planning in spm are:

1. Identify potential risks: First step is to identify the potential risks that can impact the project objectives. These risks can be internal or external to the project. Brainstorming techniques like asking 'what if' questions can be used to identify risks.

2. Analyze risks: Next, analyze the impacts and probabilities of the identified risks. Assess the likelihood of risks occurring and their impacts on cost, time and scope. High impact and high probability risks should be prioritized.

3. Response planning: Determine appropriate responses to the major risks. For risks with high impact, consider risk mitigation actions to reduce the impact. For risks with high probability, consider risk avoidance or risk transference options if possible.

4. Reserve time and budget: Allocate contingency reserve in the project schedule and budget to handle the impact of risks. The amount of reserve should be proportional to the risks and the criticality of tasks.

5. Monitoring and control: Constantly monitor the risk environment and triggers to ensure response plans are implemented promptly if required. The risk register should be updated throughout the project lifecycle.

Mnemonics:

- RISK - Record, Identify, Score, Know responses
- PRIORITIZE - Probability, Risk Impact, Options, Ranking, Implementation, Timing, Information, Zero tolerance

Advantages of risk planning:

- Prepares the project team to handle risks and uncertainties
- Increases the likelihood of achieving project objectives
- Prevents small risks from escalating into major issues
