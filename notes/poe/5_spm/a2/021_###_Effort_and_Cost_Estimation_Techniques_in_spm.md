### Effort and Cost Estimation Techniques in spm

#### Expert Judgment
- Experts are consulted to estimate effort and cost based on their experience with similar projects.
- Easy and quick but prone to bias and inaccuracies.
- Useful as a starting point but should be combined with other techniques.

#### Analogy/Comparative Estimation
- Estimate is based on actual effort/cost of a previous, similar project.
- Adjustments are made for differences between projects.
- More accurate than expert judgment but still prone to errors if similarities are not properly considered.

#### Bottom-up Estimation
- Estimate is derived by estimating and summing the effort/cost for individual tasks.
- More time-consuming but more accurate than expert judgment or analogy.
- Accuracy depends on decomposition of the project and accuracy of task-level estimates.

#### Parametric Modeling
- Estimate is derived using a mathematical model that relates effort/cost to project parameters.
- Requires historical data to determine model parameters.
- Can be more accurate than other techniques if a suitable model is found.
- Examples:
- Effort = Size * Complexity
- Cost = Effort * Rate
