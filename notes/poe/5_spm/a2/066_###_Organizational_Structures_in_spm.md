### Organizational Structures in spm

The following are the major organizational structures in spm:

1. Functional Structure:
- Grouping of positions based on specialty/function (marketing, finance, production, etc.).
- Common in smaller organizations or those producing standardized goods/services.
- Allows for specialization and efficient use of resources.
- Can lead to departmental silos and lack of communication across functions.

Mnemonic: Grouped by FUNCTION, best for STANDARDIZATION.

2. Divisional Structure:
- Grouping of positions/divisions by product, geographic region, or customer.
- Enables decentralized decision making and responsiveness to local needs.
- Requires strong coordination and control to avoid inefficiencies.
- Examples: product division (e.g. mobile phones, TVs), regional division (e.g. North America, Europe), customer division (e.g. businesses, consumers).

Mnemonic: Grouped by DIVISION, allows DECENTRALIZATION.

3. Matrix Structure:
- Combination of functional and divisional structures.
- Employees report to both functional and product/project managers.
- Facilitates knowledge sharing across functions/divisions.
- Can lead to conflict and confusion over priorities and authority.
- Common in large, complex organizations or companies pursuing multiple strategic initiatives.

Mnemonic: COMBINES functions and divisions, enables coordination but can cause CONFUSION.
