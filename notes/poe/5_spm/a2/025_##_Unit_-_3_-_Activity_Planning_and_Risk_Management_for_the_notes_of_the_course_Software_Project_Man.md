## Unit - 3 - Activity Planning and Risk Management

### Activity Planning
- Activity planning involves breaking down the project into smaller tasks or activities and estimating the required effort and duration for each activity.
- It helps in allocating resources and scheduling the activities properly to complete the project on time.
- The main outputs of activity planning are activity lists, activity attributes, and project schedule.
- Activity lists contain the list of activities required to complete the project. Activity attributes define the properties of each activity like duration, effort, resource requirements, precedence relationships, etc. The project schedule shows the start and finish dates of each activity and the project completion date.
- Some techniques used for activity planning are decomposition, rolling wave planning, and expert judgment.

### Risk Management
- Risk management is the process of identifying, analyzing and responding to risk factors throughout the life of a project and in the best interests of its objectives.
- It involves risk identification, risk analysis, risk response planning, and risk monitoring and control.
- Risk identification identifies the risks that may affect the project and documents their characteristics. Risk analysis evaluates the likelihood and consequences of each risk. Risk response planning determines appropriate ways to deal with significant risks. Risk monitoring and control monitors residual risks and identifies new risks and executes risk response plans.
- Quantitative risk analysis uses numerical estimates of risk and quantitative risk management techniques like decision trees, Monte Carlo analysis, and modeling and simulation. Qualitative risk analysis uses expert judgment to assess the risks and prioritize them based on their probability of occurrence and impact.
