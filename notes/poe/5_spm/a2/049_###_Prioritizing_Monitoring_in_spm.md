### Prioritizing Monitoring in spm

The key points to keep in mind for prioritizing monitoring in spm are:

1. Critical business processes and functions: The critical business processes and functions which are essential for the organization should be monitored closely. For example, core revenue generating processes, critical infrastructure components, etc. Any failures or issues in these critical areas can have a significant impact on the business.

2. Regulatory and compliance requirements: There are various regulatory and compliance requirements which mandate close monitoring of specific metrics and KPIs. These requirements should be closely monitored to avoid any legal or financial penalties. For example, monitoring response times for customer requests to comply with service level agreements.

3. Risk areas: The areas which pose a higher risk to the business in terms of costs, reputation, etc. should be closely monitored. For example, monitoring security vulnerabilities and threats to mitigate the risk of cyber attacks which can be costly to deal with. Monitoring customer feedback and reviews to address any dissatisfaction early and avoid potential reputation loss.

4. Performance indicators: The key performance indicators which give an overview of the health and performance of the important business areas should be closely tracked. This includes metrics like revenue, profitability, operational efficiency, quality, etc. Monitoring these indicators helps in taking corrective actions early if the performance is deviating from goals.

5. Automate as much as possible: Wherever feasible, monitoring activities should be automated to minimize manual efforts and the possibility of human errors. This frees up resources to focus on key areas which require manual intervention. Automation also enables monitoring at higher frequencies and scalable volumes which would not be possible manually. However, the automation logic and algorithms should be thoroughly tested before full-scale deployment.

The above points help in prioritizing the monitoring activities and focusing the efforts on the most important areas to gain visibility and take necessary actions promptly. A good monitoring strategy balances close monitoring of critical areas with optimizing resources by not over-monitoring low priority areas. The specific focus areas can vary based on the organization and industry.
