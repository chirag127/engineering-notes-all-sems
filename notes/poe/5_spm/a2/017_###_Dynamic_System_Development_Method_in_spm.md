### Dynamic System Development Method in spm

The Dynamic System Development Method (DSDM) is an agile project delivery framework, initially used for software development projects. Key features of DSDM are:

- Focus on the business need
- Iterative and incremental approach
- Active user involvement
- Rapid delivery
- Built-in quality

Some key principles of DSDM are:

- Focus on the business need
- Deliver on time
- Collaborate
- Never compromise quality
- Build incrementally from firm foundations
- Develop iteratively
- Communicate continuously and clearly

The DSDM lifecycle has 5 phases:

1. Feasibility Study - Examine the business case and risks. Decide if the project is feasible and should proceed.
2. Business Study - Further analysis of requirements and finalize the Business Case. Plan the project in detail.
3. Functional Model Iteration - Build and get approval for the system requirements and design.
4. Design and Build Iteration - Develop and test components and integrate the system.
5. Implementation - Install the system and capture lessons learned.

Advantages of DSDM:

- Focus on business needs and delivering value
- High user involvement leading to better solutions
- Reduced risk of project failure through the agile, iterative approach
- Flexibility to embrace change

Disadvantages of DSDM:

- Can be difficult to estimate costs and timescales accurately
- Requires highly skilled practitioners
- User engagement is critical but can be difficult to achieve
- Tight control and governance is required which can impact team autonomy

In summary, DSDM is a proven agile method that focuses on early delivery of real business benefits. It promotes teamwork, iterative development and continuous testing and integration. DSDM suits projects where requirements are rapidly changing or not fully understood and provides a controlled environment for agile project management.
