#### Hadoop 2.0 New Features - NameNode High Availability

Hadoop 2.0 is a major upgrade of the Hadoop Distributed File System (HDFS) that introduces several new features, including NameNode high availability. This feature is critical for large-scale Hadoop deployments, where the NameNode acts as a single point of failure. In this section, we will discuss the new features of Hadoop 2.0 related to NameNode high availability.

1. **Introduction to NameNode High Availability**
   - In Hadoop 1.x, the NameNode was a single point of failure. If the NameNode failed, the entire Hadoop cluster would become unavailable until the NameNode was restarted.
   - In Hadoop 2.0, NameNode high availability is introduced to eliminate this single point of failure.
   - The basic idea of NameNode high availability is to have two NameNodes running in the cluster at the same time. One NameNode is the active node, while the other is the standby node.

2. **How NameNode High Availability Works**
   - In NameNode high availability, the two NameNodes share a namespace and block pool.
   - The active NameNode manages the namespace and block pool, while the standby NameNode maintains a copy of the namespace and block pool.
   - The standby NameNode keeps itself synchronized with the active NameNode by constantly applying the edit logs generated by the active NameNode.
   - If the active NameNode fails, the standby NameNode takes over as the active NameNode, and the Hadoop cluster continues to operate.

3. **Advantages of NameNode High Availability**
   - NameNode high availability provides continuous availability of the Hadoop cluster, even if the active NameNode fails.
   - It eliminates the downtime associated with restarting the NameNode.
   - It improves the reliability of the Hadoop cluster by ensuring that there is always a NameNode available to manage the namespace and block pool.

4. **Disadvantages of NameNode High Availability**
   - NameNode high availability requires more hardware resources than a single NameNode configuration.
   - It introduces additional complexity to the Hadoop cluster, which can make it more difficult to manage.

5. **Configuration of NameNode High Availability**
   - NameNode high availability is configured by setting up two NameNodes and configuring them to work together.
   - The configuration involves setting up the NameNode, JournalNode, and ZooKeeper quorum, which are the components that enable failover and synchronization between the two NameNodes.
   - The configuration process is complex and requires a thorough understanding of the Hadoop cluster architecture.

In conclusion, NameNode high availability is a critical feature of Hadoop 2.0 that provides continuous availability of the Hadoop cluster. While it introduces additional complexity to the Hadoop cluster, the benefits of NameNode high availability outweigh the disadvantages. Understanding the configuration and operation of NameNode high availability is essential for large-scale Hadoop deployments.
