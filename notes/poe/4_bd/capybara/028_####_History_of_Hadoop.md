#### History of Hadoop

Hadoop is an open-source software framework that is used for distributed storage and processing of large data sets across clusters of computers. It was initially developed by Doug Cutting and Mike Cafarella in 2005, and it was named after a toy elephant that Cutting’s son owned. Here are some important events in the history of Hadoop:

1. In 2006, Hadoop was made available as an Apache open-source project. This made it easier for developers all over the world to contribute to its development.

2. In 2008, Hadoop 0.18 was released with significant improvements in terms of scalability, reliability, and performance.

3. In 2009, Hadoop 0.20 was released with more features and bug fixes. This version of Hadoop became more popular among enterprises.

4. In 2011, Hadoop 0.22 was released with support for HDFS (Hadoop Distributed File System) federation, which improved the scalability of Hadoop clusters.

5. In 2012, Hadoop 1.0 was released with improved stability, and it became the first stable version of Hadoop.

6. In 2013, Hadoop 2.0 was released with significant improvements in terms of scalability and reliability. This version of Hadoop introduced YARN (Yet Another Resource Negotiator), which separated the resource management and job scheduling functions of Hadoop.

7. In 2014, Hadoop 2.5 was released with support for Hadoop on Docker containers.

8. In 2015, Hadoop 2.7 was released with support for data encryption, which improved the security of Hadoop clusters.

9. In 2017, Hadoop 3.0 was released with support for erasure coding, which reduced the storage overhead of Hadoop clusters.

Mnemonics and Learning Tricks:

- Hadoop is named after a toy elephant, so you can remember that it is used for handling large data sets.

- The release of Hadoop 1.0 in 2012 can be remembered as a milestone in the development of Hadoop.

- YARN, which was introduced in Hadoop 2.0, can be remembered as a system that separates the management and scheduling of resources.

- The release of Hadoop 3.0 in 2017 can be remembered as a significant update that added support for erasure coding.

In conclusion, Hadoop has come a long way since its inception in 2005. It has evolved from a small open-source project to a widely-used framework for distributed storage and processing of big data. The history of Hadoop is a testament to the power of open-source development and collaboration.
