## Unit 7 - NoSQL Databases

In this unit, we will be discussing NoSQL databases, which are non-relational databases that offer flexible data models and scalability. NoSQL databases are increasingly popular in the industry due to their ability to handle large amounts of unstructured and semi-structured data.

### Key Concepts

- What are NoSQL databases and how do they differ from relational databases?
- Types of NoSQL databases: document-oriented, key-value, column-family, and graph.
- Data modeling in NoSQL databases.
- Querying and indexing in NoSQL databases.
- Advantages and disadvantages of using NoSQL databases.

### NoSQL Databases vs. Relational Databases

- NoSQL databases are designed to handle unstructured and semi-structured data, while relational databases are designed for structured data.
- NoSQL databases are horizontally scalable, meaning they can handle large amounts of data by adding more servers, while relational databases are vertically scalable, meaning they require more powerful hardware to handle large amounts of data.
- NoSQL databases offer flexible data models, which can be updated without changing the entire schema, while relational databases require strict adherence to a pre-defined schema.

### Types of NoSQL Databases

#### Document-Oriented Databases

- Store data in documents, which can be nested and contain different data types.
- Examples include MongoDB and Couchbase.

#### Key-Value Databases

- Store data in key-value pairs, where each key is associated with a value.
- Examples include Redis and Riak.

#### Column-Family Databases

- Store data in columns instead of rows, allowing for efficient storage and retrieval of large amounts of data.
- Examples include Apache Cassandra and HBase.

#### Graph Databases

- Store data in nodes and edges, allowing for efficient querying and analysis of relationships between data.
- Examples include Neo4j and OrientDB.

### Data Modeling in NoSQL Databases

- NoSQL databases offer flexible data models, which can be updated without changing the entire schema.
- Data modeling in NoSQL databases involves identifying the data entities and relationships, and designing a schema that reflects these relationships.

### Querying and Indexing in NoSQL Databases

- NoSQL databases use different query languages, such as MongoDB's query language and Cassandra's CQL.
- Indexing is important in NoSQL databases to improve query performance.

### Advantages and Disadvantages of NoSQL Databases

#### Advantages

- Flexible data models allow for easy scalability and updates.
- Horizontally scalable, allowing for handling of large amounts of data.
- Can handle unstructured and semi-structured data.
- Lower cost compared to relational databases.

#### Disadvantages

- Lack of ACID transactions can lead to data inconsistencies.
- No standard query language across all NoSQL databases.
- Lack of widespread adoption can lead to limited community support and resources.

### Applications of NoSQL Databases

- Big data and analytics.
- Content management systems.
- Social media platforms.
- Internet of Things (IoT) applications. 

Overall, NoSQL databases offer a flexible and scalable alternative to relational databases, but require careful consideration of data modeling and indexing to ensure optimal performance.
