### Challenges of Conventional Systems for the Notes of the Unit 1 - Introduction to Big Data in the Subject of Big Data

Big data has become an essential part of almost every industry. With the increasing amount of data being generated every day, there is a need for efficient and effective systems to manage, store, and analyze this data. Conventional systems, however, face several challenges when it comes to handling big data. In this unit, we will explore the challenges that conventional systems face when dealing with big data. 

Here are some of the challenges of conventional systems for the notes of the Unit 1 - Introduction to Big Data in the subject of Big Data:

- **Storage Capacity**: Conventional systems have limited storage capacity, and they cannot handle the vast amounts of data that big data generates. As a result, they often require expensive upgrades to handle the increasing amount of data.

- **Processing Speed**: Conventional systems are not designed to process large amounts of data quickly. They often take a long time to process data, which can slow down the decision-making process.

- **Scalability**: Conventional systems are not easily scalable, which means they cannot handle the sudden increase in data volume. As a result, they require significant investments to scale up the system.

- **Data Integration**: Conventional systems often use different formats and structures to store data, making it difficult to integrate and analyze data from different sources.

- **Data Security**: Conventional systems often lack the necessary security measures to protect sensitive data. They are vulnerable to cyber-attacks, which can result in data breaches and loss of data.

- **Cost**: Conventional systems are often expensive to acquire, maintain, and upgrade. The cost of upgrading the system to handle big data can be prohibitive for many organizations.

To overcome these challenges, many organizations are turning to big data solutions that are designed specifically to handle the challenges of big data. These solutions offer advantages such as:

- **Scalability**: Big data solutions are designed to scale up or down as per the requirements of the organization. They can easily handle the sudden increase of data volume.

- **Processing Speed**: Big data solutions are designed to process large amounts of data quickly, which can speed up the decision-making process.

- **Data Integration**: Big data solutions are designed to integrate and analyze data from different sources, making it easier to gain insights from the data.

- **Data Security**: Big data solutions offer advanced security measures to protect sensitive data from cyber-attacks.

- **Cost-effectiveness**: Big data solutions are often cost-effective compared to conventional systems. They offer a range of pricing options, making them accessible to organizations of all sizes.

In conclusion, conventional systems face several challenges when it comes to handling big data. These challenges include limited storage capacity, slow processing speed, scalability issues, data integration issues, data security concerns, and high costs. To overcome these challenges, organizations are turning to big data solutions that are designed specifically to handle the challenges of big data. These solutions offer several advantages, including scalability, processing speed, data integration, data security, and cost-effectiveness.
