### Definition for the notes of the Unit 1 - Introduction of Real Time System in the subject of Real Time System

Real-time systems refer to those computer systems that are designed to respond to external events or stimuli within a specified time frame. These systems have stringent timing requirements and must produce the desired output within a specified time interval. In other words, a real-time system is a computer system that must satisfy both functional and temporal requirements.

Real-time systems can be classified into two categories: hard real-time systems and soft real-time systems. Hard real-time systems must produce the correct output within a specified time interval, failing which the system may fail catastrophically. Soft real-time systems, on the other hand, can tolerate occasional missed deadlines without catastrophic consequences.

Some of the key features of real-time systems are:

- Predictability: Real-time systems should be predictable in their behavior, i.e., their response times should be consistent and deterministic.
- Responsiveness: Real-time systems should respond to external events or stimuli within a specified time frame.
- Reliability: Real-time systems should be reliable and should produce the correct output within a specified time interval.
- Safety: Real-time systems that control critical systems must be designed to ensure safety of human life and property.

Real-time systems find applications in a wide range of domains such as avionics, automotive systems, medical devices, control systems, robotics, and telecommunications. Some of the advantages of real-time systems are:

- Improved efficiency: Real-time systems can improve the efficiency of systems by reducing response times and improving throughput.
- Increased safety: Real-time systems can improve safety in critical systems by ensuring that the system produces the correct output within a specified time interval.
- Better control: Real-time systems can provide better control in complex systems by responding to external events or stimuli within a specified time frame.

However, real-time systems also have some disadvantages such as:

- Increased complexity: Real-time systems can be more complex to design and implement due to their stringent timing requirements.
- Higher cost: Real-time systems can be more expensive to design and implement due to the need for specialized hardware and software.
- Limited flexibility: Real-time systems may have limited flexibility due to their strict timing requirements.

In conclusion, real-time systems are computer systems that must produce the correct output within a specified time interval. They have stringent timing requirements and must satisfy both functional and temporal requirements. Real-time systems find applications in a wide range of domains and can provide several advantages such as improved efficiency, increased safety, and better control. However, they also have some disadvantages such as increased complexity, higher cost, and limited flexibility.
