### Intensity Attenuation for the notes of the Unit 5 - Hidden Lines and Surfaces in the subject of Computer Graphics

In computer graphics, intensity attenuation is an important concept that deals with the decrease in intensity or brightness of light as it travels through a medium or interacts with a surface. This effect is particularly important in rendering realistic images of objects in 3D space, as it can greatly enhance the sense of depth and realism in a scene.

Here are some key points to keep in mind when studying intensity attenuation in computer graphics:

- Intensity attenuation is a function of distance: The amount of light that reaches a surface decreases as the distance between the light source and the surface increases. This is due to the fact that light spreads out as it travels, so the same amount of light is distributed over a larger area as the distance increases.
- The inverse square law: Intensity attenuation is often modeled using the inverse square law, which states that the intensity of light decreases in proportion to the square of the distance from the source. This means that if the distance between the light source and a surface is doubled, the intensity of the light will be reduced to one-fourth of its original value.
- Other factors can also affect intensity attenuation: In addition to distance, other factors such as the angle of incidence, surface reflectivity, and absorption by the medium can also affect the amount of light that reaches a surface.
- Different rendering techniques can be used to simulate intensity attenuation: In computer graphics, various techniques can be used to simulate intensity attenuation, such as ray tracing, radiosity, and photon mapping. These techniques can simulate the way that light interacts with surfaces and can create realistic images of objects in 3D space.

- Enhanced realism: By simulating the way that light interacts with surfaces, intensity attenuation can greatly enhance the sense of depth and realism in a scene.
- Better lighting effects: By modeling the way that light spreads out and is absorbed by different surfaces, intensity attenuation can create more realistic lighting effects such as shadows and reflections.

Some disadvantages of using intensity attenuation in computer graphics include:

- Increased computational complexity: Simulating intensity attenuation can be computationally expensive, especially when using techniques such as ray tracing that require tracing the path of light through a scene.
- More difficult to control: Intensity attenuation can be more difficult to control than simpler lighting models, as it depends on a variety of factors such as distance, angle of incidence, and surface properties.
