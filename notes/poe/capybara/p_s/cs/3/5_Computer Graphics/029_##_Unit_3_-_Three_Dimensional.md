## Unit 3 - Three Dimensional

In this unit, we will learn about three-dimensional objects and how to represent them on a two-dimensional plane. Three-dimensional geometry is an essential part of mathematics and is used in various fields like engineering, architecture, computer graphics, and physics. 

### Topics Covered:

1. Introduction to Three-Dimensional Geometry
2. Coordinates in 3-Dimensional Space
3. Distance Formula in 3-Dimensional Space
4. Section Formula in 3-Dimensional Space
5. Direction Cosines and Direction Ratios
6. Angle between Two Lines
7. Equation of a Plane
8. Intersection of Two Planes
9. Angle between Two Planes
10. Distance of a Point from a Plane
11. The Equation of a Straight Line in 3-Dimensional Space
12. The Intersection of a Line and a Plane

### Key Concepts:

1. Three-dimensional objects are objects that have three dimensions, namely length, width, and height or depth.
2. The coordinates of a point in three-dimensional space are represented by three numbers (x, y, z) called the Cartesian coordinates.
3. The distance between two points in three-dimensional space is calculated using the distance formula.
4. The section formula is used to find the coordinates of a point that divides a line segment joining two given points in a given ratio.
5. The direction cosines and direction ratios of a line passing through two points are used to find the angle between two lines.
6. The equation of a plane is obtained by using the coordinates of three non-collinear points in 3D space.
7. The intersection of two planes is a straight line.
8. The angle between two planes is the angle between their normal vectors.
9. The distance of a point from a plane is the perpendicular distance between the point and the plane.
10. The equation of a straight line in three dimensions is obtained by using the direction ratios of the line and the coordinates of a point on the line.
11. The intersection of a line and a plane is a point that satisfies both the equation of the line and the equation of the plane.

### Advantages of Three-Dimensional Geometry:

1. It is used in various fields like engineering, architecture, computer graphics, and physics to represent and analyze 3D objects.
2. It helps in the design and construction of buildings, machines, and other structures.
3. It is used in computer graphics to create realistic 3D models of objects and environments.

### Disadvantages of Three-Dimensional Geometry:

1. It can be challenging to visualize and work with three-dimensional objects on a two-dimensional plane.
2. It requires a good understanding of mathematical concepts like vectors and matrices.

### Examples and Applications:

1. Three-dimensional geometry is used in architecture to design and construct buildings and structures.
2. It is used in engineering to design and analyze machines and other structures.
3. In computer graphics, it is used to create realistic 3D models of objects and environments.
4. It is used in physics to study the motion of objects in three-dimensional space.

In conclusion, three-dimensional geometry is an essential part of mathematics and has various applications in different fields. Understanding the key concepts and formulas in this unit is crucial for anyone interested in pursuing a career in engineering, architecture, computer graphics, or physics.
