### Warn Model for the Notes of the Unit 5 - Hidden Lines and Surfaces in the Subject of Computer Graphics

In computer graphics, hidden lines and surfaces refer to the parts of an object that are not visible to the viewer. In order to create a realistic and accurate image, it is important to be able to determine which parts of an object are visible and which are hidden. This is where the Warn model comes in.

The Warn model is a method used in computer graphics to identify hidden lines and surfaces. It is a mathematical algorithm that uses the properties of 3D objects to determine which surfaces are hidden and which are visible. Here are some important points to keep in mind when studying the Warn model:

1. The Warn model is based on the principle of occlusion. This means that if one surface is in front of another, it will hide the surfaces behind it.

2. The model uses a process called backface culling to determine which surfaces are visible. Backface culling involves analyzing the direction of the surface normals to determine whether they are facing towards or away from the viewer.

3. The Warn model also uses a process called depth buffering to determine which surfaces are visible. Depth buffering involves assigning a depth value to each pixel on the screen and comparing these values to determine which surfaces are in front and which are behind.

4. The Warn model is useful for creating realistic and accurate images of 3D objects. By identifying hidden lines and surfaces, it allows for more realistic lighting and shading effects.

5. However, there are some limitations to the Warn model. For example, it may not be able to handle complex objects with overlapping surfaces or transparent materials.
