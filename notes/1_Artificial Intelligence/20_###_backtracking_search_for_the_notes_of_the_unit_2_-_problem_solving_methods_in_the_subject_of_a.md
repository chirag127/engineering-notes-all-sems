### Backtracking Search for the notes of the Unit 2 - Problem Solving Methods in the subject of Artificial Intelligence
Backtracking search is a depth-first search algorithm used for finding all (or some) solutions to a problem that incrementally builds candidates to the solutions, and abandons a candidate ("backtracks") as soon as it determines that the candidate cannot possibly be completed to a valid solution. Backtracking search can be applied to problems that can be expressed in terms of decisions and constraints. It is often used in constraint satisfaction problems, optimization problems, and decision problems. The algorithm starts with an empty solution and builds it incrementally, checking the constraints at each step to ensure that the solution remains feasible. If the current solution is not feasible, the algorithm backtracks to the previous step and tries a different decision. The algorithm continues until a feasible solution is found or all possibilities have been exhausted. The time complexity of backtracking search is exponential in the worst-case, but it can be very efficient in practice for problems with small solution spaces or problems with a large amount of symmetry.
