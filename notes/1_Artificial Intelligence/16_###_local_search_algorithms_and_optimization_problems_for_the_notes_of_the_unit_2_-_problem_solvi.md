### Local Search Algorithms and Optimization Problems for the notes of the Unit 2 - Problem Solving Methods in the subject of Artificial Intelligence
Local Search Algorithms are heuristic optimization methods that iteratively improve a solution by making small changes to it. They are commonly used to solve optimization problems, which involve finding the best solution from a set of candidate solutions.

Optimization Problems can be divided into two categories: continuous and discrete. Continuous optimization problems involve finding the global minimum or maximum of a continuous function, while discrete optimization problems involve finding the best solution from a finite set of candidate solutions.

Examples of Local Search Algorithms include Hill Climbing, Simulated Annealing, and Genetic Algorithms. Hill Climbing is a greedy algorithm that iteratively selects the best neighboring solution and moves to it. Simulated Annealing is a probabilistic algorithm that allows for moves that worsen the solution, with a probability that decreases over time. Genetic Algorithms are inspired by the process of natural selection and use mechanisms such as mutation and crossover to generate new solutions.

Applications of Local Search Algorithms and Optimization Problems include scheduling, routing, and resource allocation problems in fields such as operations research, logistics, and computer science.
