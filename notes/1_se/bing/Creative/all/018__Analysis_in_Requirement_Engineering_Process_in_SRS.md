### Analysis in Requirement Engineering Process in SRS

- Analysis is the second stage of the requirement engineering process, after elicitation. In this stage, the requirements gathered from the stakeholders are analyzed and validated to ensure that they are clear, consistent, complete, correct, and feasible .
- Analysis involves the following activities :
  - **Modeling the requirements**: This involves creating graphical or textual representations of the requirements, such as use cases, data flow diagrams, entity-relationship diagrams, state transition diagrams, etc. Modeling helps to visualize the system behavior, structure, and interactions, and to identify any gaps, ambiguities, or conflicts in the requirements.
  - **Prioritizing the requirements**: This involves assigning different levels of importance to the requirements, such as high, medium, or low, based on the stakeholder needs, business value, technical feasibility, and risk. Prioritizing helps to focus on the most critical and essential requirements, and to manage the scope and schedule of the project.
  - **Verifying the requirements**: This involves checking the requirements for quality attributes, such as completeness, correctness, clarity, consistency, testability, and traceability. Verifying helps to ensure that the requirements are well-defined, unambiguous, accurate, and conform to the standards and regulations.
  - **Validating the requirements**: This involves confirming that the requirements meet the stakeholder expectations and the system objectives, and that they are feasible to implement within the given constraints. Validating helps to ensure that the requirements are aligned with the customer needs and the project vision, and that they are realistic and achievable.
- Analysis is an iterative and collaborative process that involves constant communication and feedback among the stakeholders, analysts, and developers. Analysis helps to create a software requirements specification (SRS) document, which is a formal and comprehensive description of the software to be developed, including its functionality, interfaces, constraints, quality attributes, and validation criteria .
- A good SRS document serves as a single source of truth and a blueprint for the software development process. It helps to keep the project team and the stakeholders on the same page, to ensure that each requirement is met, and to make vital decisions on the product lifecycle. A good SRS document also follows the four Ds principle: define the product's purpose, describe what you are building, detail the requirements, and deliver it for approval.
- Some of the benefits of using a dedicated requirements management tool to create an SRS document are :
  - It allows you to easily create, organize, link, trace, and manage the requirements in a centralized and structured way.
  - It enables you to collaborate and communicate with the stakeholders and the project team in real-time, and to capture and address their feedback and changes.
  - It supports various formats and standards for the requirements, such as text, diagrams, tables, images, etc., and allows you to export and import the requirements to and from different tools and platforms.
  - It helps you to ensure the quality and consistency of the requirements, and to perform verification and validation activities, such as reviews, inspections, tests, etc.
  - It provides you with various features and functions to analyze, prioritize, and report on the requirements, such as filters, queries, metrics, dashboards, etc.
  - It integrates with other tools and systems, such as design, development, testing, configuration management, etc., to facilitate the traceability and alignment of the requirements throughout the software development lifecycle.
