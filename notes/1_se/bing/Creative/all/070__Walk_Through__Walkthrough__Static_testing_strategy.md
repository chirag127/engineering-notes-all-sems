#### Walk Through (Walkthrough) Static testing strategy

- A walk through is a type of static testing technique that involves a group of people reviewing a document or a piece of code to find defects, errors, or inconsistencies.
- The walk through is usually led by the author or the owner of the document or code, who explains the logic, purpose, and functionality of the work product to the reviewers.
- The reviewers can ask questions, make comments, or suggest improvements during the walk through session, but they do not make any changes to the document or code themselves.
- The walk through is a less formal and less structured technique than an inspection, which requires more preparation, documentation, and follow-up activities.
- The walk through is typically used in the early stages of the software development life cycle, when the work products are still incomplete or evolving, and the main objective is to get feedback and improve the quality of the work products.
- The walk through can also be used as a training or knowledge transfer tool, to familiarize the reviewers with the work products or the system under development.
- The walk through can be applied to various types of work products, such as requirements specifications, design documents, test cases, test plans, user manuals, code modules, etc.
- The walk through can have various benefits, such as:
  - Finding and fixing defects early, which can reduce the cost and effort of rework and testing later.
  - Improving the clarity, consistency, and completeness of the work products, which can enhance the communication and collaboration among the stakeholders.
  - Increasing the confidence and satisfaction of the author or the owner of the work products, who can get constructive feedback and validation from the reviewers.
  - Enhancing the skills and knowledge of the reviewers, who can learn from the author or the owner of the work products, and from each other.
  - Promoting a culture of quality and teamwork, which can improve the overall performance and productivity of the project team.
- The walk through can also have some challenges, such as:
  - Requiring time and resources from the participants, which can affect their availability and commitment to other tasks.
  - Depending on the expertise and experience of the reviewers, who may not have sufficient knowledge or background to provide effective feedback or identify defects.
  - Depending on the attitude and behavior of the participants, who may not be cooperative, respectful, or constructive during the walk through session.
  - Depending on the quality and completeness of the work products, which may not be ready or suitable for a walk through session.
- A possible mnemonic to remember the steps of a walk through is: **PAPER**.
  - **P**repare: The author or the owner of the work products selects the reviewers, distributes the work products, and schedules the walk through session.
  - **A**nalyze: The reviewers analyze the work products individually, and identify any defects, errors, or inconsistencies.
  - **P**resent: The author or the owner of the work products presents the logic, purpose, and functionality of the work products to the reviewers, and answers any questions or clarifies any doubts.
  - **E**valuate: The reviewers evaluate the work products, and provide feedback, comments, or suggestions to the author or the owner of the work products.
  - **R**eport: The author or the owner of the work products records the results and outcomes of the walk through session, and reports any defects, errors, or inconsistencies to the relevant stakeholders.
