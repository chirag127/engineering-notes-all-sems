### Architectural Design in Software Design

- Architectural design is the process of defining the high-level structure and behavior of a software system, as well as the principles and guidelines for its development and evolution.
- Architectural design is concerned with finding the right balance between functional and non-functional requirements, such as performance, reliability, security, modifiability, etc.
- Architectural design involves identifying and organizing the main components of a software system, their interfaces, interactions, dependencies, and configurations.
- Architectural design also involves choosing the appropriate architectural styles, patterns, and frameworks that best suit the system's goals and constraints.
- Architectural design is usually documented using graphical notations, such as UML diagrams, or textual descriptions, such as architecture description languages (ADLs).
- Architectural design is an iterative and collaborative process that requires constant communication and feedback among stakeholders, such as developers, users, customers, managers, etc.
- Architectural design is influenced by various factors, such as the system's domain, scope, size, complexity, quality attributes, technologies, standards, etc.
- Architectural design is a crucial activity in software engineering, as it affects the system's feasibility, quality, cost, maintainability, and evolution.

Some mnemonics and learning tricks for architectural design in software design are:

- A mnemonic for the four main activities of architectural design is **DICE**: Define, Identify, Choose, and Evaluate.
- A mnemonic for the four main views of a software architecture is **4+1**: Logical, Development, Process, Physical, and Scenarios.
- A mnemonic for the six main types of architectural styles is **PACMAN**: Pipe and Filter, Abstract Data Types, Client-Server, Main Program and Subroutine, Artificial Neural Network, and N-Tier.
- A learning trick for remembering the benefits of architectural design is to think of the acronym **FAME**: Facilitates communication, Allows reuse, Mitigates risks, and Enables analysis.
