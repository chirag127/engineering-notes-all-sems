### Software Quality Attributes

Software quality attributes are the non-functional requirements that describe how well a software system performs its functional requirements. They are also known as quality factors, quality characteristics, or quality criteria. Software quality attributes are usually measured by metrics, such as reliability, availability, performance, security, usability, maintainability, etc.

Some of the common software quality attributes are:

- **Reliability**: The ability of a software system to function correctly and consistently under normal and abnormal conditions. Reliability is often measured by the frequency and severity of failures, the mean time to failure (MTTF), the mean time to repair (MTTR), or the availability ratio (MTTF / (MTTF + MTTR)).
- **Availability**: The ability of a software system to be operational and accessible when needed by the users or other systems. Availability is often measured by the percentage of time that the system is up and running, the downtime, or the uptime ratio (uptime / (uptime + downtime)).
- **Performance**: The ability of a software system to meet the timeliness and throughput requirements of the users or other systems. Performance is often measured by the response time, the latency, the throughput, the utilization, or the efficiency of the system.
- **Security**: The ability of a software system to protect itself and its data from unauthorized access, modification, or destruction. Security is often measured by the confidentiality, integrity, availability, authentication, authorization, or non-repudiation of the system.
- **Usability**: The ability of a software system to be easy to learn, use, and understand by the users. Usability is often measured by the user satisfaction, the user feedback, the user errors, the learnability, the efficiency, or the effectiveness of the system.
- **Maintainability**: The ability of a software system to be easily modified, updated, or repaired by the developers or maintainers. Maintainability is often measured by the modularity, the cohesion, the coupling, the complexity, the readability, or the testability of the system.

A mnemonic to remember these six quality attributes is **RAPSUM** (Reliability, Availability, Performance, Security, Usability, Maintainability).

There are many other software quality attributes, such as scalability, portability, reusability, interoperability, compatibility, etc. However, these are not as widely used or standardized as the ones mentioned above.

Software quality attributes are important for designing, developing, testing, and evaluating software systems. They help to define the quality goals and expectations of the stakeholders, and to guide the trade-offs and decisions that affect the quality of the system. Software quality attributes are also used to compare and contrast different software systems or alternatives, and to identify the strengths and weaknesses of each system. Software quality attributes are often prioritized and balanced according to the needs and preferences of the stakeholders, the context and domain of the system, and the available resources and constraints.
