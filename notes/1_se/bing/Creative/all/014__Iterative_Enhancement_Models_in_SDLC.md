### Iterative Enhancement Models in SDLC

- Iterative enhancement models are software development life cycle (SDLC) approaches that use repeated cycles of development and testing to deliver software incrementally.
- In iterative enhancement models, the software requirements are not fully specified at the beginning, but are refined and clarified through each iteration.
- Iterative enhancement models can be classified into two types: simple iterative development and agile development.
- Simple iterative development is a model where the software is developed in parallel by different teams working on different components, and then integrated and tested at the end of each iteration.
- Agile development is a model where the software is developed in small and frequent releases, with continuous feedback and collaboration from the customers and stakeholders.
- Iterative enhancement models have some advantages over the traditional waterfall model, such as:
  - They can accommodate changing requirements and user feedback more easily.
  - They can deliver working software faster and more frequently.
  - They can reduce the risk of failure and rework by detecting and fixing errors early.
  - They can improve the quality and performance of the software by applying best practices and testing techniques.
- Iterative enhancement models also have some disadvantages, such as:
  - They require more coordination and communication among the development teams and the customers.
  - They may not be suitable for complex and large-scale projects that need a clear and stable vision.
  - They may not be compatible with some organizational cultures and processes that prefer a more structured and planned approach.
  - They may increase the cost and effort of maintaining and documenting the software.
- Iterative enhancement models are most often used in the following scenarios:
  - When the requirements of the system are not clear or may change over time.
  - When there is a time to market constraint or a need for early and frequent delivery.
  - When a new technology is being used and learned by the development team.
  - When there are some high-risk features and goals that may change in the future.
- A mnemonic to remember the iterative enhancement models is **I**ncremental **R**elease **A**nd **T**esting (IRAT).
- A learning trick to understand the iterative enhancement models is to compare them with building a house. In the waterfall model, you would design and build the whole house at once, and then inspect and fix any problems at the end. In the simple iterative development model, you would design and build different parts of the house in parallel, such as the foundation, the walls, the roof, etc., and then assemble and test them at the end of each iteration. In the agile development model, you would design and build a small and functional part of the house, such as a room, a bathroom, a kitchen, etc., and then deliver and get feedback from the customer at the end of each iteration.
