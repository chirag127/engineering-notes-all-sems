### Prototype Model in SDLC

- The prototype model is a software development life cycle (SDLC) model in which a prototype is built, tested, and then reworked as necessary until an acceptable prototype is finally achieved from which the complete system or product can be developed.
- A prototype is a working replica of a product or system that has to be engineered. It offers a small scale facsimile of the end product and is used for obtaining customer feedback.
- This model is used when the customers do not know the exact project requirements beforehand. In this model, the customer can see the product early in the life cycle and provide feedback for improvement.
- The prototype model has the following phases :

  - Requirement Identification: In this phase, the product requirements are identified and clarified in detail. This is done by interviewing the future users and other stakeholders of the product.
  - Design Stage: In this phase, a first-round design is created for the new system. The design should include the basic functionality and features as desired by the customer.
  - Build the Initial Prototype: In this phase, an initial prototype of the target software is built from the design. The prototype may not have all the product components or may not be perfect or accurate. The prototype is tailored as per the feedback given by the users and based on that the second prototype is built.
  - Review of the Prototype: In this phase, the prototype is presented to the customer or other stakeholders for evaluation and feedback. The feedback is collected in an organized way so that they can be used for further system enhancements.
  - Iteration and Enhancement of Prototype: In this phase, the prototype is refined and improved based on the feedback and factors such as time, workforce, and budget. Also, the technical feasibility of actual implementation is checked. The iteration and enhancement process continues until the customer approves the prototype and finds the working model to be satisfactory.

- The prototype model has the following types:

  - Rapid Throwaway Prototyping: In this type, the developers can explore the ideas and get customer feedback for each of them. The prototype need not be a final one, and so it can be further iterated to develop new versions of the final product.
  - Evolutionary Prototyping: In this type, the prototype developed initially is incrementally refined on the basis of customer feedback until the final one gets accepted. It provides a better way which can save time and effort.
  - Incremental Prototyping: In this type, the final product is broken into different small pieces of prototypes and developed individually. In the end, the different prototypes are merged into a single final product in their predefined order. It reduces the complexity of the development process and the time interval between the project's beginning and final delivery.
  - Extreme Prototyping: In this type, the prototype is developed using a web development framework and then refined using HTML, JavaScript, and XML. It is mainly used for web applications.

- The prototype model has the following advantages :

  - It helps in reducing the risk of failure by involving the customer in the development process.
  - It helps in improving the quality of the product by incorporating the customer feedback and suggestions.
  - It helps in saving time and cost by avoiding unnecessary features and design faults.
  - It helps in increasing the user satisfaction and acceptance by delivering a working model that meets their expectations.

- The prototype model has the following disadvantages :

  - It may lead to confusion and misunderstanding between the customer and the developer if the prototype is not well documented and communicated.
  - It may lead to incomplete or inadequate analysis and design if the prototype is considered as the final product.
  - It may lead to poor performance and maintainability if the prototype is not properly refined and tested.
  - It may lead to scope creep and unrealistic expectations if the customer demands too many changes or features in the prototype.

- The prototype model is suitable for projects that have unclear or changing requirements, high user involvement, and fast feedback. It is not suitable for projects that have well-defined and stable requirements, low user involvement, and slow feedback.
- A mnemonic to remember the phases of the prototype model is: **R**equirement **I**dentification, **D**esign, **B**uild, **R**eview, **I**teration.
- An example of a prototype model is a mockup of a website or an app that is shown to the customer for feedback and improvement before launching the final version.
