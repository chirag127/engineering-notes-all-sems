### Design Strategies in Software Design

Design strategies in software design are the approaches that are taken to design a software system. They help to define the structure, behavior, and interactions of the software components and modules. They also help to align the software design with the business goals and user needs.

There are many types of design strategies in software design, but some of the most common ones are:

- **Top-Down Design**: This strategy starts with a high-level view of the system and gradually breaks it down into smaller, more manageable components. Each component is then designed and implemented separately, and then integrated with the rest of the system. This strategy helps to focus on the overall functionality and architecture of the system, and to divide the problem into subproblems. However, it also requires a clear and complete understanding of the system requirements and specifications, and it may delay the testing and feedback until the later stages of development. A mnemonic to remember this strategy is: **T**ake a **B**ig **P**icture and **S**plit it into **S**maller **P**arts.

- **Bottom-Up Design**: This strategy starts with individual components and builds the system up, piece by piece. Each component is designed and tested independently, and then combined with other components to form larger units. This strategy helps to reuse existing components and to test and debug the system incrementally. However, it also requires a lot of coordination and integration among the components, and it may lose sight of the overall system goals and functionality. A mnemonic to remember this strategy is: **B**uild **S**mall **P**arts and **C**ombine them into a **B**ig **P**icture.

- **Structured Design**: This strategy is based on the principle of modularity, which means dividing the system into independent and well-defined modules that communicate with each other through interfaces. Each module has a single and specific function, and hides its internal details from other modules. This strategy helps to improve the readability, maintainability, and reliability of the system, and to reduce the complexity and coupling among the modules. However, it also requires a careful design of the interfaces and the data flow among the modules, and it may not be suitable for some systems that require dynamic and flexible behavior. A mnemonic to remember this strategy is: **S**eparate **C**oncerns and **E**ncapsulate **D**etails.

- **Function-Oriented Design**: This strategy is based on the principle of decomposition, which means breaking down the system into functions that perform specific tasks or operations. Each function is then designed and implemented as a module, and the modules are organized into a hierarchy of levels. The higher-level modules call the lower-level modules to perform subtasks, and the lower-level modules return the results to the higher-level modules. This strategy helps to focus on the processes and actions of the system, and to reduce the redundancy and duplication of code. However, it also requires a lot of data passing and parameter passing among the modules, and it may not capture the state and behavior of the system entities well. A mnemonic to remember this strategy is: **F**ocus on **W**hat the **S**ystem **D**oes.

- **Object-Oriented Design**: This strategy is based on the principle of abstraction, which means identifying the system entities and their attributes and behaviors. Each entity is then designed and implemented as an object, and the objects are organized into classes and hierarchies. The objects communicate with each other through messages, and the classes inherit the properties and methods of their parent classes. This strategy helps to model the system as a collection of interacting entities, and to encapsulate the data and behavior of each entity. However, it also requires a careful design of the classes and their relationships, and it may introduce some overhead and complexity due to inheritance and polymorphism. A mnemonic to remember this strategy is: **O**rganize **E**ntities and **M**essages.
