### Constructive Cost Models (COCOMO) in software project management

- COCOMO stands for Constructive Cost Model, which is a regression model based on the number of lines of code (LOC) for software projects.
- It was proposed by Barry Boehm in 1981 and is based on the study of 63 projects, which makes it one of the best-documented models.
- It is used to estimate the effort, cost, and schedule of software projects, based on various parameters and assumptions.
- It consists of a hierarchy of three increasingly detailed and accurate forms: Basic COCOMO, Intermediate COCOMO, and Detailed COCOMO.
- Basic COCOMO is good for quick, early, rough order of magnitude estimates of software costs, but its accuracy is limited due to its lack of factors to account for difference in project attributes (Cost Drivers).
- Intermediate COCOMO incorporates a set of 15 cost drivers that can affect the effort and productivity of software development.
- Detailed COCOMO further refines the estimates by breaking down the software project into different phases and modules, and applying different effort multipliers for each of them.
- COCOMO II is the successor of COCOMO 81 and is claimed to be better suited for estimating modern software development projects; providing support for more recent software development processes and was tuned using a larger database of 161 projects.
- The advantages of COCOMO are that it is simple, easy to use, widely applicable, and well validated by empirical data.
- The disadvantages of COCOMO are that it is based on historical data, which may not reflect the current trends and technologies, it assumes a linear relationship between size and effort, which may not hold for very large or complex projects, and it relies on the accuracy of LOC as a measure of size, which may vary depending on the programming language, style, and quality.
- Some mnemonics and learning tricks for COCOMO are:

  - COCOMO sounds like cocoa, which is a hot drink that can warm you up in cold weather. Similarly, COCOMO can help you estimate the cost and effort of software projects, which can be useful in planning and budgeting.
  - The three forms of COCOMO are Basic, Intermediate, and Detailed, which can be remembered by the acronym BID. You can also think of bidding for a software project, which requires you to estimate the cost and effort using COCOMO.
  - The 15 cost drivers of Intermediate COCOMO can be grouped into four categories: Product, Platform, Personnel, and Project, which can be remembered by the acronym PPPP. You can also think of the four Ps of marketing, which are Product, Price, Place, and Promotion, and relate them to the cost drivers of software development.
