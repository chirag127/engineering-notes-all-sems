### IEEE Standards for SRS

- A software requirements specification (SRS) is a description of a software system to be developed. It specifies the functional and non-functional requirements, as well as the format and structure of the document.
- IEEE stands for the Institute of Electrical and Electronics Engineers, which is a professional organization that publishes standards for software engineering and other fields.
- IEEE has published several standards for SRS, such as IEEE Std 830-1998, which was replaced by ISO/IEC/IEEE 29148:2011, with an update in 2018. These standards provide guidance and recommendations for writing a good SRS, as well as examples of SRS outlines and templates.
- The main purpose of IEEE standards for SRS is to ensure that the SRS is clear, complete, consistent, traceable, verifiable, modifiable, and reusable. The standards also help to avoid ambiguity, incompleteness, inconsistency, and misunderstanding between the stakeholders of the software project.
- The IEEE standards for SRS cover the following topics:
  - The process of developing and managing software requirements, including elicitation, analysis, validation, verification, and change control.
  - The content and qualities of a good software requirement, including its attributes, characteristics, and types.
  - The format and structure of an SRS document, including its introduction, overall description, specific requirements, appendices, and references.
  - The organization and presentation of functional and non-functional requirements, using different criteria such as mode, user class, object, feature, stimulus, functional hierarchy, or combinations of these.
  - The compliance with other IEEE standards and international standards, such as IEEE/EIA 12207, which is a common framework for software lifecycle processes, and IEEE 15288, which is a common framework for system lifecycle processes.

- A possible mnemonic to remember the main topics of IEEE standards for SRS is **PACFOC**, which stands for **P**rocess, **A**ttributes, **C**ontent, **F**ormat, **O**rganization, and **C**ompliance.
