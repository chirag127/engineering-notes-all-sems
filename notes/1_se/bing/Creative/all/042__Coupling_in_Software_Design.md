#### Coupling in Software Design

- Coupling is the degree of interdependence between software modules; a measure of how closely connected two routines or modules are; the strength of the relationships between modules.
- Coupling is usually contrasted with cohesion, which is the degree to which elements within a module work together to fulfill a single, well-defined purpose.
- Low coupling often correlates with high cohesion, and vice versa. Low coupling and high cohesion make a system easier to maintain and improve, while high coupling and low cohesion make a system difficult to change and test.
- Coupling metrics help development teams determine the complexity of their architecture based on the dependencies between classes, modules and methods.
- There are different types of coupling, such as data coupling, stamp coupling, control coupling, common coupling, content coupling, and external coupling .
- Data coupling: If the dependency between the modules is based on the fact that they communicate by passing only data, then the modules are said to be data coupled. In data coupling, the components are independent of each other and communicate through data. Module communications don’t contain tramp data.
- Stamp coupling: If the dependency between the modules is based on the fact that they communicate by passing a composite data structure, such as a record or a class object, then the modules are said to be stamp coupled. In stamp coupling, the components share a common data structure and use only a part of it. Stamp coupling is less desirable than data coupling, as it exposes more information than necessary.
- Control coupling: If the dependency between the modules is based on the fact that they communicate by passing a control flag, such as a boolean or an enum, then the modules are said to be control coupled. In control coupling, the components influence each other’s execution logic. Control coupling is less desirable than stamp coupling, as it creates a tight dependency between the modules.
- Common coupling: If the dependency between the modules is based on the fact that they communicate by accessing a shared global data, then the modules are said to be common coupled. In common coupling, the components have read and write access to the same global data. Common coupling is less desirable than control coupling, as it increases the risk of side effects and makes the modules difficult to reuse.
- Content coupling: If the dependency between the modules is based on the fact that they communicate by directly accessing or modifying the content of another module, such as a variable or a statement, then the modules are said to be content coupled. In content coupling, the components violate the principle of encapsulation and have high dependency on each other. Content coupling is the worst type of coupling, as it makes the modules impossible to change or test independently.
- External coupling: If the dependency between the modules is based on the fact that they communicate by using an external resource, such as a file, a database, or a network, then the modules are said to be external coupled. In external coupling, the components depend on the format and availability of the external resource. External coupling is inevitable in some cases, but it should be minimized and isolated as much as possible.

- A good software design should aim for low coupling, which means that the modules should be independent and communicate through simple and well-defined interfaces  .
