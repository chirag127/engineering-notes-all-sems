### Software as an Evolutionary Entity

- Software is an evolutionary entity because it changes over time in response to changing requirements, environments, technologies, and user feedback.
- Software evolution is the process of developing, maintaining, and improving software systems throughout their life cycle.
- Software evolution can be classified into two types: planned evolution and unplanned evolution.
  - Planned evolution is the deliberate modification of software systems to add new features, improve performance, fix bugs, or adapt to new platforms or standards. Planned evolution is usually driven by user or market demands, and follows a structured process of analysis, design, implementation, testing, and deployment.
  - Unplanned evolution is the accidental or unintended modification of software systems due to errors, hacks, patches, workarounds, or external factors. Unplanned evolution is usually driven by necessity or urgency, and does not follow a systematic process or adhere to quality standards.
- Software evolution can be modeled using different approaches, such as:
  - The Lehman's laws of software evolution, which describe the empirical observations and generalizations about the behavior and characteristics of evolving software systems. Some of the laws are:
    - Law of continuing change: A software system must be continually adapted or it becomes progressively less satisfactory.
    - Law of increasing complexity: As a software system evolves, its complexity increases unless work is done to maintain or reduce it.
    - Law of self-regulation: A software system's evolution process is self-regulating with the distribution of product and process measures close to normal.
    - Law of conservation of organizational stability: The average effective global activity rate in an evolving software system is invariant over the product's lifetime.
    - Law of conservation of familiarity: The incremental change in each release of an evolving software system is approximately constant.
    - Law of continuing growth: The functional content of an evolving software system must be continually increased to maintain user satisfaction.
    - Law of declining quality: The quality of an evolving software system will appear to be declining unless it is rigorously maintained and adapted to operational environment changes.
    - Law of feedback system: The evolution process of a software system is a multi-level, multi-loop, multi-agent feedback system and must be treated as such to achieve significant improvement over any reasonable base.
  - The staged model of software evolution, which describes the phases and transitions of software systems over time. The phases are:
    - Initial development: The software system is created from scratch or based on existing components or frameworks. The system is tested and deployed for the first time.
    - Evolution: The software system is modified and updated to meet changing requirements, environments, technologies, and user feedback. The system is tested and deployed repeatedly.
    - Servicing: The software system is maintained and supported to fix bugs, improve performance, or enhance security. The system is tested and deployed occasionally.
    - Phase-out: The software system is gradually replaced by a newer or better system, or becomes obsolete or irrelevant. The system is tested and deployed rarely or never.
    - Close-down: The software system is no longer used or maintained. The system is archived or deleted.
  - The spiral model of software evolution, which describes the iterative and incremental development of software systems. The model consists of four quadrants that represent the activities and risks involved in each cycle of evolution. The quadrants are:
    - Planning: The objectives, alternatives, and constraints of the software system are defined and analyzed. The risks are identified and evaluated.
    - Risk analysis: The feasible alternatives and their associated risks are explored and resolved. The risks are mitigated or avoided.
    - Engineering: The software system is developed or modified according to the chosen alternative. The system is tested and verified.
    - Evaluation: The software system is reviewed and evaluated by the stakeholders. The feedback and lessons learned are used to plan the next cycle of evolution.

- Software evolution can be influenced by various factors, such as:
  - Technical factors: The characteristics and quality of the software system, such as functionality, performance, reliability, usability, maintainability, portability, etc.
  - Organizational factors: The structure and culture of the organization that develops, maintains, or uses the software system, such as goals, strategies, policies, processes, resources, roles, responsibilities, communication, collaboration, etc.
  - Environmental factors: The external conditions and events that affect the software system, such as market trends, user needs, technological changes, legal regulations, social norms, etc.
