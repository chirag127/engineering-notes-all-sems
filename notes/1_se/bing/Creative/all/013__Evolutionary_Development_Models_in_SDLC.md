### Evolutionary Development Models in SDLC

- Evolutionary development models are a combination of iterative and incremental models of software development life cycle (SDLC) .
- In evolutionary development models, the development team does not capture all the requirements upfront, but starts with some of the core or riskier modules .
- The core modules of the software are first developed, which are refined into an increasing level of capabilities called iterations .
- Each iteration is developed through a mini waterfall model, and at the end of an iteration, a tested, integrated executable system is developed and deployed at the customer site . This is also called a minor release of the software product .
- The iterations are short and fixed, usually between 2 and 6 weeks, and development takes many iterations (10-15) for a single release, also called an increment .
- The requirements, plan, estimates, and solution evolve or are refined over the course of the iterations, rather than fully defined and frozen in a major upfront specification effort .
- The development team responds to the user feedback and changes the product, plan, or process accordingly . Therefore, the software product evolves with time .
- The evolutionary development models are suitable for large projects where the modules can be easily identified and implemented incrementally .
- The evolutionary development models are also suitable for projects where the customer wants to start using the core features instead of waiting for the full software .
- The evolutionary development models are also suitable for projects where the technology is new and requires time to learn .
- The evolutionary development models are also suitable for projects where the requirements are unclear or changing .
- The evolutionary development models are consistent with the pattern of unpredictable discovery and change in new product development .

#### Advantages of Evolutionary Development Models

- The user gets a chance to experiment with the partially developed system and provide feedback .
- The development team can reduce the risk and uncertainty by focusing on the core or critical modules first .
- The development team can deliver the software faster and in smaller chunks, which increases the customer's confidence and satisfaction .
- The development team can adapt to the changing requirements and user needs .
- The development team can improve the quality and performance of the software by testing and integrating each iteration .

#### Disadvantages of Evolutionary Development Models

- The development team may face difficulties in dividing the problem into several versions that would be acceptable to the customer .
- The development team may face challenges in managing the complexity and dependencies of the software as it evolves .
- The development team may need to rework or redesign the software as the requirements change .
- The development team may need to communicate and coordinate more frequently with the customer and stakeholders .
- The development team may need to have more skills and experience in the technology and domain .

#### Examples of Evolutionary Development Models

- Prototyping Model: A prototype is a working model of the software that is built to demonstrate some of the features and functionalities of the final product . The prototype is used to elicit feedback from the user and refine the requirements and design . The prototype can be either discarded or evolved into the final product .
- Spiral Model: A spiral model is a risk-driven model that combines the features of the waterfall model and the prototyping model . The spiral model consists of four phases: planning, risk analysis, engineering, and evaluation . The development team repeats these phases in a circular manner until the software is completed .
- Agile Model: An agile model is a value-driven model that emphasizes collaboration, communication, feedback, and adaptation . The agile model consists of several frameworks and methodologies, such as Scrum, Kanban, XP, etc . The agile model follows an iterative and incremental approach to deliver the software in short cycles called sprints .
