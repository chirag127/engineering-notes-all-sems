### Similarity and Differences from Conventional Engineering Processes

- Conventional engineering processes are the methods of designing, developing, testing, and deploying software or hardware systems that follow a predefined set of steps, such as the waterfall model, the spiral model, or the V-model.
- Similarity and differences from conventional engineering processes are the ways of comparing and contrasting the characteristics, advantages, disadvantages, and applications of different engineering processes, such as agile, lean, or DevOps.
- Some of the common criteria for similarity and differences from conventional engineering processes are:

  - Flexibility: the ability to adapt to changing requirements, feedback, or environment.
  - Efficiency: the ability to deliver high-quality products or services with minimal waste, cost, or time.
  - Collaboration: the degree of communication, coordination, and cooperation among the stakeholders, such as developers, customers, users, or managers.
  - Innovation: the ability to generate new ideas, solutions, or features that meet the needs or expectations of the customers or users.
  - Risk: the potential for failure, error, or loss that may affect the project, product, or service.

- Some of the examples of similarity and differences from conventional engineering processes are:

  - Agile vs. Waterfall: Agile is a flexible, iterative, and collaborative engineering process that delivers working software or hardware in short cycles, called sprints, and incorporates feedback from the customers or users. Waterfall is a rigid, sequential, and linear engineering process that follows a predefined set of steps, such as analysis, design, implementation, testing, and deployment, and delivers the final product or service at the end of the project. Agile is more efficient, innovative, and responsive to changes than waterfall, but also more complex, uncertain, and risky.
  - Lean vs. Spiral: Lean is an efficient, incremental, and customer-centric engineering process that focuses on eliminating waste, maximizing value, and delivering products or services that satisfy the customers or users. Spiral is a risk-driven, evolutionary, and prototyping engineering process that follows a cyclic approach of planning, analysis, design, implementation, testing, and evaluation, and incorporates risk management and customer feedback at each stage. Lean is more streamlined, simple, and fast than spiral, but also more prone to over-optimization, under-specification, and customer dissatisfaction.
  - DevOps vs. V-model: DevOps is an innovative, integrated, and automated engineering process that combines the development and operations teams and practices, and uses tools and techniques such as continuous integration, continuous delivery, continuous testing, and continuous monitoring, to deliver reliable, secure, and scalable products or services. V-model is a verification-driven, hierarchical, and structured engineering process that follows a parallel approach of specification, design, implementation, testing, and validation, and ensures that each phase is completed and verified before moving to the next phase. DevOps is more collaborative, adaptive, and productive than V-model, but also more challenging, demanding, and dependent on technology.
