### Software Quality Frameworks (SQF) in SRS

- Software Quality Frameworks (SQF) are the procedures, techniques, and tools that are used to ensure that a software product or service meets the requirements defined in the Software Requirements Specification (SRS) document.
- SQF are based on the quality characteristics of a good SRS document, such as completeness, correctness, consistency, clarity, verifiability, modifiability, traceability, and usability.
- SQF can be applied at different stages of the software development life cycle, such as planning, analysis, design, implementation, testing, and maintenance.
- SQF can be classified into two main categories: software quality assurance (SQA) and software quality control (SQC).
  - Software quality assurance (SQA) is the process of ensuring that the software development process follows the established standards, guidelines, and best practices. SQA activities include reviews, audits, inspections, walkthroughs, and testing.
  - Software quality control (SQC) is the process of verifying that the software product or service conforms to the specified requirements. SQC activities include testing, debugging, and defect management.
- Some examples of SQF are:
  - ISO/IEC 25010:2011, which defines a set of quality attributes and sub-attributes for software products and services, such as functional suitability, performance efficiency, compatibility, usability, reliability, security, maintainability, and portability.
  - IEEE 730-2014, which provides guidelines for developing and maintaining a software quality assurance plan (SQAP) that covers the software life cycle processes, such as management, documentation, standards, reviews, audits, testing, problem reporting, and corrective actions.
  - CMMI (Capability Maturity Model Integration), which is a framework for improving the processes and capabilities of software development organizations, based on five maturity levels, such as initial, managed, defined, quantitatively managed, and optimizing.
- Some benefits of using SQF are:
  - They help to improve the quality of the software product or service, by reducing defects, errors, and failures, and increasing customer satisfaction and loyalty.
  - They help to reduce the cost and time of software development, by preventing rework, waste, and delays, and enhancing productivity and efficiency.
  - They help to comply with the legal and regulatory requirements, by following the industry standards and best practices, and avoiding penalties and lawsuits.
