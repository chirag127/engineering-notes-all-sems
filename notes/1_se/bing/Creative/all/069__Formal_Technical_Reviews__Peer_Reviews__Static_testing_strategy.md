#### Formal Technical Reviews (Peer Reviews) Static testing strategy

- Formal Technical Reviews (FTR) or Peer Reviews are a type of static testing technique that involves a structured and documented analysis of software artifacts by a team of reviewers.
- FTRs aim to find defects, issues, and ambiguities in the software documents, such as requirements, design, code, test cases, etc. before they are executed or implemented.
- FTRs also help to improve the quality of the software, verify its compliance with standards and specifications, and enhance the knowledge and skills of the reviewers.
- FTRs are usually conducted by a moderator, an author, a scribe, and one or more reviewers. The moderator is responsible for planning, organizing, and facilitating the review process. The author is the creator of the software artifact under review and is responsible for fixing the defects found. The scribe is the person who records the defects and comments during the review meeting. The reviewers are the peers or technical experts who examine the software artifact and provide feedback and suggestions.
- FTRs follow a defined process that consists of six steps: planning, overview, preparation, inspection, rework, and follow-up. In the planning step, the moderator selects the software artifact to be reviewed, the review team, the review criteria, and the schedule. In the overview step, the moderator gives a brief introduction of the software artifact and the review objectives to the review team. In the preparation step, the reviewers study the software artifact individually and identify potential defects and questions. In the inspection step, the review team meets and discusses the software artifact, the defects, and the possible improvements. In the rework step, the author corrects the defects and verifies the changes. In the follow-up step, the moderator checks the rework, closes the defects, and documents the review results and metrics.
- FTRs can be classified into four types: informal reviews, walkthroughs, technical reviews, and inspections. Informal reviews are the simplest and least formal type of FTRs, where the software artifact is reviewed by one or more reviewers without a formal process or documentation. Walkthroughs are a type of FTRs where the author leads the review team through the software artifact and explains its logic and functionality. Technical reviews are a type of FTRs where the review team consists of technical experts who evaluate the technical aspects of the software artifact, such as design, architecture, algorithms, etc. Inspections are the most formal and rigorous type of FTRs, where the review team follows a strict process and uses checklists and tools to detect defects and measure quality.
- FTRs have several benefits, such as:
  - They help to detect and remove defects early in the software development life cycle, which reduces the cost and effort of fixing them later.
  - They improve the quality and reliability of the software by ensuring its conformity with standards and specifications.
  - They enhance the communication and collaboration among the software team members and stakeholders by sharing information and feedback.
  - They increase the knowledge and skills of the reviewers by exposing them to different perspectives and approaches.
  - They provide a basis for quality assurance and improvement by collecting and analyzing review data and metrics.
