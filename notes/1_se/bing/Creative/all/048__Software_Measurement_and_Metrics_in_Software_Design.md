### Software Measurement and Metrics in Software Design

- Software measurement is the process of quantifying or counting the attributes of a software product or process. Software metrics are the values obtained by applying software measurement techniques. Software measurement and metrics are important for software engineering because they help to assess the quality, performance, reliability, usability, and cost-effectiveness of software products and processes.  
- Software measurement and metrics can be classified into three types: product metrics, process metrics, and project metrics.  
  - Product metrics are used to evaluate the state of the software product, such as its size, complexity, functionality, maintainability, and quality. Product metrics can be further divided into static metrics and dynamic metrics. Static metrics are obtained by analyzing the source code or design documents of the software, such as lines of code (LOC), cyclomatic complexity, cohesion, and coupling. Dynamic metrics are obtained by executing the software and observing its behavior, such as response time, throughput, availability, and defect density.   
  - Process metrics are used to measure the characteristics of the software development process, such as its efficiency, effectiveness, productivity, and maturity. Process metrics can help to identify, monitor, and improve the software development methods, tools, and techniques. Process metrics can also help to predict the outcomes and quality of the software products. Some examples of process metrics are defect detection rate, defect removal efficiency, defect leakage, and process capability.   
  - Project metrics are used to measure the status and progress of the software development project, such as its scope, schedule, budget, and resources. Project metrics can help to plan, control, and manage the software development activities and deliverables. Project metrics can also help to communicate the project information and expectations to the stakeholders. Some examples of project metrics are effort, duration, cost, schedule variance, earned value, and customer satisfaction.   
- Software measurement and metrics can be applied at different phases of the software development life cycle, such as planning, analysis, design, implementation, testing, deployment, and maintenance. Software measurement and metrics can help to achieve various objectives, such as:   
  - Increase the return on investment (ROI) of the software development project by optimizing the use of resources and reducing the risks and costs.
  - Identify the areas of improvement and opportunities for innovation in the software product and process by analyzing the strengths and weaknesses.
  - Manage the workloads and expectations of the software development team and stakeholders by setting realistic and measurable goals and milestones.
  - Reduce the overtime and stress of the software development team by monitoring and controlling the project progress and quality.
  - Enhance the quality and performance of the software product and process by applying best practices and standards and implementing corrective and preventive actions.
- Software measurement and metrics should have some desirable characteristics, such as:   
  - Quantitative: They should be expressed in numerical values that can be compared and analyzed.
  - Understandable: They should be clearly defined and explained and easy to comprehend and interpret.
  - Applicable: They should be relevant and suitable for the software product and process that are being measured and the purpose and context of the measurement.
  - Repeatable: They should be consistent and reliable and produce the same results when applied under the same conditions.
  - Economical: They should be cost-effective and efficient and not consume too much time and resources.
  - Language-independent: They should not depend on any specific programming language or technology and be applicable to different types of software products and processes.

- Some mnemonics and learning tricks for software measurement and metrics in software design are: 
  - To remember the three types of software metrics, use the acronym PPP: Product, Process, and Project.
  - To remember the difference between static and dynamic metrics, use the analogy of a car: Static metrics are like inspecting the car's parts and features, while dynamic metrics are like driving the car and testing its performance.
  - To remember the four functions of software metrics, use the acronym POCI: Planning, Organizing, Controlling, and Improving.
