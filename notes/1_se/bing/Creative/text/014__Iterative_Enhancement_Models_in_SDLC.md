### Iterative Enhancement Models in SDLC

- Iterative enhancement models are software development life cycle (SDLC) approaches that involve developing software in small and manageable increments, rather than in one complete and final version .
- Each increment adds some functionality or enhancement to the previous version of the software, and is tested and reviewed before moving on to the next iteration .
- Iterative enhancement models do not require a full specification of requirements at the beginning of the project, but allow for requirements to be refined and modified as the software evolves .
- Iterative enhancement models are suitable for projects that have unclear or changing requirements, time to market constraints, new or unfamiliar technologies, or high-risk features .
- Iterative enhancement models can be classified into two types: simple iterative development and agile development.
  - Simple iterative development follows a linear sequence of phases, such as specification, design, implementation, and testing, for each increment.
  - Agile development follows an adaptive and collaborative approach, where increments are delivered in short and frequent cycles, and feedback from customers and stakeholders is incorporated into the development process.
