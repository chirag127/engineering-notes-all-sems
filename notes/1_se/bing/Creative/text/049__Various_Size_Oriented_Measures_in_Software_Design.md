#### Various Size Oriented Measures in Software Design

- Size oriented measures are based on the assumption that the size of the software product is the primary determinant of its cost, effort, and quality.
- Size oriented measures can be classified into two categories: direct measures and indirect measures.
- Direct measures are those that can be obtained by examining the software product itself, such as lines of code (LOC), number of statements, number of modules, etc.
- Indirect measures are those that are derived from the direct measures, such as productivity, defect density, complexity, etc.
- Some of the advantages of size oriented measures are:
  - They are easy to collect and automate.
  - They are widely used and understood by the software industry.
  - They can be used for benchmarking and comparison across projects and organizations.
- Some of the disadvantages of size oriented measures are:
  - They are dependent on the programming language, coding style, and level of abstraction used.
  - They do not capture the functionality, quality, or usability of the software product.
  - They may encourage undesirable behaviors, such as padding, over-designing, or under-testing the software product.
