### Elicitation in Requirement Engineering Process in SRS

Elicitation is the process of gathering, analyzing, and validating the requirements for a software system from various sources, such as customers, users, stakeholders, and domain experts. Elicitation is one of the most important and challenging activities in the requirement engineering process, as it involves understanding the needs and expectations of different parties, resolving conflicts, and ensuring completeness and consistency of the requirements.

The main steps of the elicitation process are:

- Identify the sources of requirements, such as people, documents, or systems, and establish a communication plan with them.
- Prepare for elicitation, by defining the scope, objectives, and techniques of the elicitation session, and preparing the necessary materials, such as questionnaires, prototypes, or scenarios.
- Conduct elicitation, by applying the selected techniques, such as interviews, workshops, observation, or surveys, to collect the requirements from the sources, and documenting the results.
- Confirm elicitation, by reviewing, verifying, and validating the elicited requirements, and resolving any issues, ambiguities, or inconsistencies.
- Manage elicitation, by tracking, controlling, and reporting the progress and quality of the elicitation process, and managing the changes and risks.

The outcome of the elicitation process is a set of requirements that are documented in a Software Requirements Specification (SRS) document. The SRS is a formal description of the software system, its functions, interfaces, design, and user experience. The SRS serves as a contract between the developers and the customers, and as a basis for the design, implementation, testing, and maintenance of the software system. The SRS should be clear, complete, consistent, traceable, verifiable, and modifiable.
