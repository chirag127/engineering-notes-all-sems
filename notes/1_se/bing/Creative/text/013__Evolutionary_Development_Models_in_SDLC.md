### Evolutionary Development Models in SDLC

- Evolutionary development models are a combination of iterative and incremental models of software development life cycle (SDLC) .
- In evolutionary development models, the development team does not capture all the requirements upfront, but starts with some of the core or riskier modules .
- The core modules of the software are first developed and refined into an increasing level of capabilities called iterations  .
- Each iteration is developed through a mini waterfall model, and at the end of an iteration, a tested, integrated, and executable system is developed and deployed at the customer site  . This is also called a minor release of the software product .
- The iterations are short and fixed, usually between 2 and 6 weeks, and development takes many iterations (10-15) for a single release, also called an increment .
- The requirements, plan, estimates, and solution evolve or are refined over the course of the iterations, rather than fully defined and frozen in a major upfront specification effort .
- The development team responds to the user feedback and changes the product, plan, or process accordingly  .
- The final product may have new functionalities, enhanced functionalities, or changed functionalities compared to the initial product .
- Evolutionary development models are suitable for large projects where the modules can be easily identified and implemented incrementally .
- Evolutionary development models are also suitable for projects where the customer wants to start using the core features instead of waiting for the full software .
- Evolutionary development models are consistent with the pattern of unpredictable discovery and change in new product development .
- Evolutionary development models are also used in object-oriented software development because the system can be easily partitioned into units in terms of objects .

Advantages of Evolutionary Development Models:

- They allow the user to experiment with the partially developed system and provide feedback .
- They reduce the risk of failure because the core modules get tested thoroughly .
- They increase the customer's confidence and satisfaction as they constantly get quantifiable goods or services from the beginning of the project .
- They allow for changing requirements and design as the project progresses .

Disadvantages of Evolutionary Development Models:

- They require more management and coordination efforts as the project involves multiple iterations and releases .
- They may result in scope creep or loss of focus as the requirements and design keep changing .
- They may not be suitable for projects where the problem is hard to divide into smaller versions that would be acceptable to the customer .
- They may not be suitable for projects where the technology is new and requires time to learn .
