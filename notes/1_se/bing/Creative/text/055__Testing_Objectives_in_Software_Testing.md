### Testing Objectives in Software Testing

- Testing objectives are the goals and expectations that guide the testing process and ensure its effectiveness and quality.
- Some of the common testing objectives are:

  - To evaluate the work products such as requirements, design, user stories, and code, and verify that they are clear, consistent, and complete.
  - To verify the fulfillment of all specified requirements and ensure that the software meets the customer's needs and expectations.
  - To validate if the test object is complete and works as per the expectation of the users and the stakeholders, and check its functionality, usability, performance, security, and reliability .
  - To build confidence in the quality level of the test object and prevent bugs, reduce development costs, and improve customer satisfaction .
  - To find and report defects as early as possible, and ensure that they are fixed and retested .
  - To measure and improve the quality of the software development process and adhere to the standards and best practices.
  - To provide information and feedback to the developers, managers, and customers about the status and progress of the testing activities.

- Testing objectives may vary with the type of testing technique and the software development life cycle model, depending on the scope, purpose, and context of the testing.
- Testing objectives should be SMART: Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, and Time-bound. They should also be aligned with the project objectives and the stakeholder's expectations.
