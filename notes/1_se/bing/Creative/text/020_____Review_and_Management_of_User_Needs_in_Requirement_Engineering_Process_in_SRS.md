### Review and Management of User Needs in Requirement Engineering Process in SRS

- Requirement engineering is the process of eliciting, analyzing, specifying, validating, and managing the user needs for a software system.
- User needs are the expectations, preferences, goals, and constraints of the stakeholders who will use or interact with the software system.
- Review and management of user needs are essential activities in requirement engineering to ensure that the software system meets the user needs and satisfies the quality attributes such as functionality, usability, reliability, performance, and security.
- Review of user needs involves checking the completeness, consistency, correctness, clarity, and feasibility of the user needs, as well as resolving any conflicts or ambiguities among them.
- Management of user needs involves prioritizing, tracing, and controlling the changes of the user needs throughout the software development lifecycle, as well as documenting and communicating them to the relevant stakeholders.
- Some of the techniques and tools for review and management of user needs are:

  - Requirement inspection: A formal technique that involves a team of reviewers who examine the user needs for defects and provide feedback for improvement.
  - Requirement validation: A technique that involves verifying the user needs with the actual or potential users of the software system, such as through prototyping, demonstration, or testing.
  - Requirement prioritization: A technique that involves ranking the user needs according to their importance, urgency, or value for the software system, such as using the MoSCoW method or the Analytic Hierarchy Process (AHP).
  - Requirement traceability: A technique that involves establishing and maintaining the links between the user needs and other artifacts of the software system, such as design, code, test cases, or documentation, to ensure consistency and completeness.
  - Requirement change management: A technique that involves identifying, assessing, approving, and implementing the changes of the user needs, as well as tracking and documenting their impact and rationale, to ensure quality and stability.
  - Requirement management tools: Software tools that support the review and management of user needs, such as by providing features for elicitation, analysis, specification, validation, prioritization, traceability, and change management of user needs, as well as for collaboration and communication among stakeholders. Examples of such tools are IBM Rational DOORS, Jama Connect, and Jira.
