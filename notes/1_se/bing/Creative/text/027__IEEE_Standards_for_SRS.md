### IEEE Standards for SRS

A software requirements specification (SRS) is a description of a software system to be developed. It is modeled after business requirements specification (CONOPS).

The IEEE Computer Society publishes several standards for software engineering, including IEEE Std 830-1998, "IEEE Recommended Practice for Software Requirements Specifications". This standard has been replaced by ISO/IEC/IEEE 29148:2011, with an update in 2018. 

The IEEE standards provide guidance and recommended practices for specifying software requirements. They are not complete tutorials on requirements development, but they do contain some useful information. The standards also provide several suggestions of how to organize functional and nonfunctional requirements in an SRS document. 

The IEEE standards are aimed at specifying requirements of software to be developed but also can be applied to assist in the selection of in-house and commercial software products. They also provide guidelines for compliance with IEEE/EIA 12207.1-1997, a common framework for software lifecycle processes. 

The IEEE standards are voluntary and can be tailored and customized to suit the nature and scale of different projects. They are not meant to be prescriptive or rigid, but rather to offer suggestions and examples of good software requirements specification practices. 

The IEEE standards are intended to help software engineers, developers, customers, users, and other stakeholders communicate and agree on the software requirements and avoid ambiguity, inconsistency, incompleteness, and errors.
