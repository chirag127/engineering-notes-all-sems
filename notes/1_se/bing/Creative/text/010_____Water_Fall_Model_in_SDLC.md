### Waterfall Model in SDLC

- The waterfall model is a linear, sequential approach to the software development lifecycle (SDLC) that is popular in software engineering and product development .
- The waterfall model uses a logical progression of SDLC steps for a project, similar to the direction water flows over the edge of a cliff.
- The waterfall model divides the software development process into separate phases, such as requirements analysis, design, implementation, testing, deployment and maintenance.
- The waterfall model assumes that each phase is complete and correct before moving to the next phase, and that there is no need for feedback or changes between phases.
- The waterfall model has some advantages, such as simplicity, clarity, documentation and verification.
- The waterfall model also has some disadvantages, such as rigidity, inflexibility, delay and risk of failure.
- The waterfall model is suitable for projects that have clear and stable requirements, well-defined technologies and methods, and experienced and disciplined teams.
- The waterfall model is not suitable for projects that have changing or uncertain requirements, complex or novel technologies and methods, and dynamic or inexperienced teams.
