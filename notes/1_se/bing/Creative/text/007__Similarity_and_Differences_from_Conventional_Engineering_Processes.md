### Similarity and Differences from Conventional Engineering Processes

Conventional engineering processes are the methods and techniques used to design, build, and test physical products, such as machines, buildings, cars, etc. Software engineering processes are the methods and techniques used to design, build, and test software products, such as applications, websites, systems, etc.

Some of the similarities and differences between conventional engineering processes and software engineering processes are:

- Similarities:
  - Both are based on scientific and mathematical principles and require in-depth knowledge of their field .
  - Both involve creativity, problem-solving, and innovation to meet the needs and requirements of the customers and users.
  - Both follow a systematic and iterative process of planning, designing, implementing, testing, and improving the product.
  - Both aim to ensure the quality, reliability, and efficiency of the product.

- Differences:
  - Conventional engineering processes deal with physical and tangible products, while software engineering processes deal with abstract and intangible products.
  - Conventional engineering processes have higher construction and development costs, while software engineering processes have lower construction and development costs.
  - Conventional engineering processes usually apply only known and tested principles to meet product requirements, while software engineering processes can involve the application of new and untested elements in software projects.
  - Conventional engineering processes require more effort to change old designs, while software engineering processes require more effort to build new designs and features.
  - Conventional engineering processes emphasize more on mass production, while software engineering processes emphasize more on quality.
