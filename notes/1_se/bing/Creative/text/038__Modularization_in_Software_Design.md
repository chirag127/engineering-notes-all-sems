#### Modularization in Software Design

- Modularization is a technique to divide a software system into multiple discrete and independent modules, which are expected to be capable of carrying out task (s) independently .
- Modularization aims to improve the readability, maintainability, and reusability of software by breaking it down into smaller and simpler pieces.
- Modularization is closely related to structured programming and object-oriented programming, which also seek to facilitate the construction of large software programs and systems by decomposition.
- A module is a unique and addressable component of the software that has a well-defined interface and implementation .
- A module interface expresses the elements that are provided and required by the module, such as functions, data types, constants, etc.
- A module implementation contains the working code that corresponds to the elements declared in the interface.
- Modules may work as basic constructs for the entire software, or they may be further composed of submodules.
- The process of breaking down a software into multiple independent modules is called modularization.
- Effective modular design can be achieved if the partitioned modules are separately solvable, modifiable, and compilable.
- Separately solvable means that each module can be understood and tested independently of other modules.
- Separately modifiable means that changes in one module do not affect other modules, or affect them minimally.
- Separately compilable means that each module can be compiled and linked independently of other modules, or with minimal dependencies.
- A key factor for effective modular design is functional independence, which means that each module performs only one aspect of the desired functionality, with minimal interaction with other modules.
- Functional independence is a sign of high modularity, which leads to better software design and quality.
- Functional independence can be measured using two criteria: cohesion and coupling.
- Cohesion is a measure of the strength of the relationship between the elements within a module, such as functions, data, etc .
- High cohesion means that the elements within a module are closely related and work together to achieve a single purpose .
- Low cohesion means that the elements within a module are loosely related and perform different or unrelated tasks .
- Cohesion can be classified into seven types, from high to low: functional, sequential, communicational, procedural, temporal, logical, and coincidental.
- Coupling is a measure of the strength of the relationship between the modules, such as the degree of interdependence, communication, and data sharing .
- Low coupling means that the modules are independent and have minimal interaction and dependency on each other .
- High coupling means that the modules are dependent and have frequent interaction and dependency on each other .
- Coupling can be classified into six types, from low to high: data, stamp, control, common, content, and external.
- The goal of modular design is to achieve high cohesion and low coupling, which leads to better software quality, maintainability, and reusability .
