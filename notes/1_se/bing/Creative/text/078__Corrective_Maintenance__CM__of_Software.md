#### Corrective Maintenance (CM) of Software

- Corrective maintenance (CM) of software is the process of fixing errors or defects in a software system after it has been delivered or deployed.
- CM can be classified into four types: emergency, urgent, routine, and preventive.
- Emergency CM is performed when a critical error or defect causes a system failure or a severe loss of functionality. Emergency CM requires immediate action and may involve bypassing or disabling some features or components of the system.
- Urgent CM is performed when a non-critical error or defect affects the performance or usability of the system. Urgent CM requires prompt action and may involve modifying or replacing some features or components of the system.
- Routine CM is performed when a minor error or defect does not affect the functionality or performance of the system, but may cause inconvenience or dissatisfaction to the users. Routine CM requires scheduled action and may involve correcting or enhancing some features or components of the system.
- Preventive CM is performed when a potential error or defect is identified before it causes any problem or failure in the system. Preventive CM requires proactive action and may involve improving or updating some features or components of the system.
- CM can be triggered by various sources, such as user feedback, testing results, error reports, change requests, or system monitoring.
- CM can be performed by various agents, such as developers, testers, maintainers, or users.
- CM can be measured by various metrics, such as error rate, defect density, mean time to repair, availability, reliability, or customer satisfaction.
- CM can be influenced by various factors, such as software quality, complexity, architecture, documentation, standards, tools, processes, or resources.
- CM can be improved by various practices, such as defect prevention, error detection, fault diagnosis, fault correction, fault verification, fault removal, or fault prevention.
