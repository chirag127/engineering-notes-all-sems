### Requirement Engineering Process in SRS

Requirement engineering is the process of eliciting, analyzing, specifying, and validating the software requirements for a system. It is the first and crucial phase of the software development lifecycle, as it lays the foundation for the design, implementation, testing, and maintenance of the software product. The goal of requirement engineering is to produce a Software Requirements Specification (SRS) document that captures the complete and consistent description of what the system is expected to do and how it will perform under various scenarios .

The requirement engineering process typically consists of the following activities :

- **Requirement elicitation**: This is the process of gathering the software requirements from various sources, such as stakeholders, users, domain experts, existing systems, standards, regulations, etc. The purpose of this activity is to identify the needs, expectations, and constraints of the system, as well as the problems and opportunities that the system will address. Requirement elicitation techniques include interviews, questionnaires, surveys, brainstorming, observation, prototyping, etc.
- **Requirement analysis**: This is the process of analyzing the elicited requirements to resolve any conflicts, ambiguities, inconsistencies, or gaps. The purpose of this activity is to ensure that the requirements are clear, complete, correct, feasible, relevant, and testable. Requirement analysis techniques include modeling, prioritization, negotiation, validation, verification, etc.
- **Requirement specification**: This is the process of documenting the analyzed requirements in a formal and structured way, using a standard notation or language. The purpose of this activity is to communicate the requirements to the developers, testers, and other stakeholders, and to serve as a reference for the subsequent phases of the software development lifecycle. Requirement specification techniques include natural language, structured language, graphical language, mathematical language, etc.
- **Requirement validation**: This is the process of checking the quality and accuracy of the specified requirements, and ensuring that they meet the needs and expectations of the stakeholders and users. The purpose of this activity is to confirm that the requirements are correct, consistent, complete, feasible, verifiable, and traceable. Requirement validation techniques include reviews, inspections, walkthroughs, prototyping, testing, etc.

The output of the requirement engineering process is the Software Requirements Specification (SRS) document, which is the basis for the design, development, testing, and maintenance of the software system. The SRS document should include the following elements :

- **Business drivers**: This section describes the reasons the customer is looking to build the system, including the problems with the current system and the opportunities the new system will provide.
- **Business model**: This section describes the business model of the customer that the system has to support, including the organizational, business context, main business functions, and process flow diagrams.
- **Business/functional and system requirements**: This section typically consists of requirements that are organized in a hierarchical structure. The business/ functional requirements are at the top level and describe the goals and objectives of the system, while the system requirements are at the lower level and describe the specific features and functions of the system.
- **Business and system use cases**: This section consists of a Unified Modeling Language (UML) use case diagram depicting the key external entities that will be interacting with the system and the different use cases that they will have to perform. Each use case should also have a detailed description of the actors, preconditions, postconditions, main flow, and alternative flows.
- **Technical requirements**: This section lists the non-functional requirements that define the technical environment where the software needs to operate and the technical restrictions under which it needs to operate. These include the hardware, software, network, security, performance, reliability, availability, scalability, maintainability, etc. requirements of the system.
- **System qualities**: This section describes the non-functional requirements that define the quality attributes of the system, such as usability, user interface, accessibility, user satisfaction, etc.
- **Constraints and assumptions**: This section includes any constraints that the customer has imposed on the system design, such as budget, schedule, standards, regulations, etc. It also includes the requirements engineering team’s assumptions about what is expected to happen during the project, such as the availability of resources, the stability of requirements, the cooperation of stakeholders, etc.
- **Acceptance criteria**: This section details the conditions that must be met for the customer to accept the final system, such as the expected functionality, performance, quality, etc. of the system.
