### Analysis in Requirement Engineering Process in SRS

- Analysis is the second phase of the requirement engineering process in software engineering, after elicitation.
- Analysis involves refining, structuring, prioritizing, and validating the elicited requirements to ensure that they are clear, consistent, complete, and feasible.
- Analysis also involves resolving any conflicts or ambiguities among the stakeholders' needs and expectations, and identifying any gaps or missing requirements.
- Analysis can be performed using various techniques, such as:
  - Modeling: using graphical or textual representations to describe the system's behavior, structure, and data.
  - Prototyping: creating a simplified or partial version of the system to demonstrate or test some of its features or functionality.
  - Scenarios: using examples or stories to illustrate how the system will be used in different situations or contexts.
  - Use cases: specifying the interactions between the system and its users or other systems, in terms of goals, actors, steps, and outcomes.
  - Decision tables: using a tabular format to show the possible combinations of conditions and actions for a given situation or problem.
  - Data flow diagrams: showing the flow of data and information among the system's components or processes.
  - Entity-relationship diagrams: showing the relationships and attributes of the data entities or objects in the system.
  - State transition diagrams: showing the states and transitions of the system or its components in response to events or inputs.
- Analysis results in a set of refined and validated requirements that are documented in a software requirements specification (SRS) document, which serves as the basis for the design and development of the system.
