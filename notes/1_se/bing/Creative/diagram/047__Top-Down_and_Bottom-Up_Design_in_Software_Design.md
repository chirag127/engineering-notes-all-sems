Top-down and bottom-up design are two strategies of software design that can be used to decompose a complex system into smaller and manageable components. In top-down design, the system is first divided into high-level modules or subsystems, and then each module is further refined into lower-level components until the desired level of detail is reached. In bottom-up design, the system is built from the basic or atomic components, and then these components are combined or integrated to form higher-level modules or subsystems until the complete system is formed.
