Software Risk Analysis and Management in software project management is a process of identifying, assessing, and mitigating the potential risks that may affect the success of a software project. It involves the following steps:

- Risk identification: This is the process of finding out the possible sources of risk, such as technical, operational, organizational, or external factors. Risk identification can be done by using various techniques, such as brainstorming, checklists, interviews, surveys, or historical data.
- Risk analysis: This is the process of estimating the probability and impact of each identified risk on the project objectives, such as scope, time, cost, and quality. Risk analysis can be done by using qualitative or quantitative methods, such as risk matrices, risk scoring, or risk modeling.
- Risk evaluation: This is the process of prioritizing the risks based on their probability and impact, and determining the acceptable level of risk for the project. Risk evaluation can be done by using criteria, such as risk exposure, risk tolerance, or risk appetite.
- Risk mitigation: This is the process of developing and implementing strategies to reduce or eliminate the negative effects of the risks, or to exploit the positive effects of the opportunities. Risk mitigation can be done by using various techniques, such as risk avoidance, risk transfer, risk reduction, or risk acceptance.
- Risk monitoring and control: This is the process of tracking and reviewing the status of the risks and the effectiveness of the risk mitigation strategies, and making adjustments as needed. Risk monitoring and control can be done by using various tools, such as risk registers, risk reports, or risk audits.
