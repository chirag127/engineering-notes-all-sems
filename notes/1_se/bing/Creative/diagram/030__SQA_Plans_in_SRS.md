A Software Quality Assurance Plan (SQAP) is a document that describes the procedures, techniques, and tools that are used to ensure that a software product or service meets the requirements defined in the Software Requirement Specification (SRS). The SQAP also defines the roles and responsibilities of the SQA team, the areas that need to be reviewed and audited, and the SQA work products.

The SQA Plan Document is the main output of the SQA Management process. It contains the following sections:

- Purpose: The objective and scope of the SQAP.
- Reference: The documents and standards that are used as inputs for the SQAP.
- Software Configuration Management: The methods and tools for managing the software configuration items, such as source code, documents, data, etc.
- Problem Reporting and Corrective Action: The procedures and tools for reporting, tracking, and resolving software problems and defects.
- Tools, Technologies, and Methodologies: The software tools, technologies, and methodologies that are used for developing, testing, and maintaining the software product or service.
- Code Control: The standards and guidelines for coding, naming, commenting, and documenting the software source code.
- Records: Collection, Maintenance, and Retention: The types and formats of the records that are generated and maintained during the software development process, such as plans, reports, test cases, etc.
- Testing Methodology: The testing strategy, techniques, and tools that are used for verifying and validating the software product or service.

The SQA Audit and Review is the main output of the SQA Activities process. It involves conducting formal and informal audits and reviews of the software processes and products to ensure compliance with the SQA standards and requirements. The audits and reviews can be performed at various stages of the software development life cycle, such as planning, design, coding, testing, etc.

The SQA Metrics and Reports is the main output of the SQA Standards process. It involves collecting, analyzing, and reporting the data and information related to the software quality, such as defects, errors, performance, reliability, etc. The metrics and reports can be used for measuring and improving the software quality, as well as for communicating the software quality status to the stakeholders.
