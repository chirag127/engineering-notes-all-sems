Software characteristics are the attributes or properties of software that affect its quality, performance, functionality, and usability. Software characteristics are classified into six major components, according to GeeksforGeeks:

- Functionality: It refers to the degree of performance of the software against its intended purpose. Functionality refers to the set of features and capabilities that a software program or system provides to its users.
- Reliability: It refers to the ability of the software to provide desired functionality under the given conditions. Reliability is a characteristic of software that refers to its ability to perform its intended functions correctly and consistently over time.
- Efficiency: It refers to the ability of the software to use system resources in the most effective and efficient manner. Efficiency is a characteristic of software that refers to its ability to deliver the required functionality with optimal performance, speed, and resource utilization.
- Usability: It refers to the ease with which the software can be used by its intended users. Usability is a characteristic of software that refers to its user-friendliness, learnability, accessibility, and satisfaction.
- Maintainability: It refers to the ease with which the software can be modified or corrected to meet changing requirements or fix defects. Maintainability is a characteristic of software that refers to its modularity, readability, testability, and adaptability.
- Portability: It refers to the ability of the software to run on different platforms or environments. Portability is a characteristic of software that refers to its compatibility, interoperability, reusability, and transferability.
