Software as an Evolutionary Entity is a term that refers to the idea that software systems are not static, but change over time due to various factors, such as user feedback, changing requirements, new technologies, and maintenance activities. Software evolution is the process of developing, maintaining, and updating software systems to keep them consistent, reliable, and useful throughout their life cycle. Software evolution processes are the methods and practices that guide and manage software evolution.

The diagram shows that a software system has multiple versions, each of which is produced by an evolution process that takes into account the evolution factors that affect the software system. Evolution factors are the external and internal forces that drive software change, such as user needs, market trends, technological advances, legal regulations, organizational policies, and software quality. Evolution processes are the activities and techniques that enable software change, such as requirements analysis, design, implementation, testing, deployment, and maintenance. Evolution processes also involve feedback loops that allow the software system to adapt to the evolution factors and improve its performance and functionality.

The diagram also implies that software evolution is not a linear or sequential process, but a dynamic and iterative one, where each version of the software system and its evolution process can influence and be influenced by the previous and subsequent versions and their evolution processes. Software evolution is also a co-evolutionary process, where the software system and its evolution process co-evolve with the evolution factors and their changes. Software evolution is therefore a complex and adaptive phenomenon that requires constant monitoring, evaluation, and improvement.
