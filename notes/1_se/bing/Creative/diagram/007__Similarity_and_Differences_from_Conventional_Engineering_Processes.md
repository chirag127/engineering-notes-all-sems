Similarity and Differences from Conventional Engineering Processes
---
Conventional engineering processes are those that involve building physical products or systems, such as cars, machines, buildings, etc. Software engineering processes are those that involve creating software applications or systems, such as websites, games, databases, etc.

Both types of engineering processes share some similarities, such as:

- They require in-depth knowledge of their field .
- They follow a series of steps or stages to design, develop, test, and improve their products or systems .
- They can use automation tools to assist or speed up their work .
- They emphasize on quality, reliability, and efficiency of their products or systems  .

However, they also have some differences, such as:

- Conventional engineering processes are mainly based on science, mathematics, and empirical knowledge, while software engineering processes are mainly based on computer science, information technology, and discrete mathematics .
- Conventional engineering processes have high construction and development costs, while software engineering processes have low construction and development costs .
- Conventional engineering processes usually apply only known and tested principles to meet product requirements, while software engineering processes can involve the application of new and untested elements in software projects .
- Conventional engineering processes require more development efforts to change old designs, while software engineering processes require more development efforts to build new designs and features .
- Conventional engineering processes emphasize on mass production, while software engineering processes emphasize on customization and innovation .
