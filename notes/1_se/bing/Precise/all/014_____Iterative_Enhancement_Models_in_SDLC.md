### Iterative Enhancement Models in SDLC

Iterative Enhancement Model is a software development life cycle (SDLC) model that focuses on the delivery of an application in a series of increments. Each increment builds upon the previous versions of the software, adding new functionality and improving the existing features until the final system is complete.

The key features of the Iterative Enhancement Model are:

1. The development process is divided into small, manageable increments.
2. Each increment delivers a working version of the software, with additional functionality added in each iteration.
3. The requirements for each increment are well-defined and prioritized.
4. Feedback from users and stakeholders is incorporated into the development process, allowing for changes and improvements to be made in subsequent iterations.
5. Testing is performed at the end of each iteration to ensure that the software meets the requirements and is of high quality.

Advantages of the Iterative Enhancement Model include:

- Early delivery of working software to users and stakeholders.
- The ability to incorporate feedback and make changes to the software in a timely manner.
- Improved risk management, as risks can be identified and addressed in each iteration.
- Increased flexibility, as changes to requirements can be accommodated more easily.

Disadvantages of the Iterative Enhancement Model include:

- The need for careful planning and management of each iteration.
- The potential for scope creep, as new requirements may be added during the development process.
- The potential for increased costs and development time, as changes and improvements may need to be made in each iteration.

A mnemonic to remember the key features of the Iterative Enhancement Model is **D-R-F-T**:
- **D**evelopment process is divided into small, manageable increments.
- **R**equirements for each increment are well-defined and prioritized.
- **F**eedback from users and stakeholders is incorporated into the development process.
- **T**esting is performed at the end of each iteration.

In summary, the Iterative Enhancement Model is a flexible and adaptable approach to software development that allows for the delivery of working software in a series of increments, with feedback and improvements incorporated into the process. It is well-suited to projects where requirements may change or evolve over time, and where early delivery of working software is a priority. However, careful planning and management of each iteration is essential to ensure the success of the project.
