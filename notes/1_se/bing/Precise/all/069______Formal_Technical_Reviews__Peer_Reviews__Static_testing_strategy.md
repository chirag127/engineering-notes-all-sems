#### Formal Technical Reviews (Peer Reviews) Static testing strategy

Formal Technical Reviews, also known as Peer Reviews, are a type of static testing strategy. Static testing involves reviewing and analyzing software artifacts, such as requirements, design documents, and code, without executing the software. The goal of Formal Technical Reviews is to identify and resolve issues early in the development process, before the software is released.

Some key points to remember about Formal Technical Reviews are:

1. They are a type of static testing strategy, which means they involve reviewing and analyzing software artifacts without executing the software.
2. They are also known as Peer Reviews.
3. The goal is to identify and resolve issues early in the development process, before the software is released.
4. They can be applied to various software artifacts, such as requirements, design documents, and code.
5. They involve a team of reviewers, who work together to identify issues and suggest improvements.

One mnemonic to remember the key points of Formal Technical Reviews is "SPRINT":
- **S**tatic testing strategy
- **P**eer Reviews
- **R**esolve issues early
- **I**dentify issues
- **N**on-execution of software
- **T**eam of reviewers

Advantages of using Formal Technical Reviews include:
- Early identification and resolution of issues, which can save time and money in the long run.
- Improved quality of the software, as issues are identified and resolved before the software is released.
- Increased collaboration and knowledge sharing among team members, as they work together to review and improve the software.

Disadvantages of using Formal Technical Reviews include:
- They can be time-consuming, as they involve a team of reviewers working together to review and analyze the software artifacts.
- They require a certain level of expertise and knowledge from the reviewers, which may not always be available.
- There is a potential for conflicts and disagreements among the reviewers, which can slow down the review process.

In conclusion, Formal Technical Reviews are a valuable static testing strategy that can help improve the quality of software by identifying and resolving issues early in the development process. However, they do require time and expertise, and there is a potential for conflicts among the reviewers. As with any testing strategy, it is important to weigh the advantages and disadvantages and determine if Formal Technical Reviews are the right approach for a given project.
