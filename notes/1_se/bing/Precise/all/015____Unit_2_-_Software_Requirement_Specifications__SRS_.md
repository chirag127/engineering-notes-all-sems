## Unit 2 - Software Requirement Specifications (SRS)

Software Requirement Specifications (SRS) is a document that describes the requirements of a software system. It is a comprehensive description of the intended purpose and environment for software under development. The SRS fully describes what the software will do and how it will be expected to perform.

1. **Purpose**: The purpose of the SRS is to establish and document the requirements of the software system. It serves as a reference for developers, testers, and stakeholders to ensure that the software meets the needs of its users.

2. **Audience**: The SRS is intended for a variety of audiences, including developers, testers, project managers, and stakeholders. It is important that the SRS is written in a clear and concise manner so that it can be easily understood by all parties involved.

3. **Structure**: The SRS is typically structured into several sections, including an introduction, overall description, specific requirements, and appendices. The introduction provides an overview of the software system and its purpose. The overall description provides a high-level view of the system and its functionality. The specific requirements section details the functional and non-functional requirements of the system. The appendices may include additional information such as diagrams, data definitions, and references.

4. **Functional Requirements**: Functional requirements describe the features and capabilities of the software system. They specify what the system should do and how it should behave. Examples of functional requirements include user authentication, data input and output, and data processing.

5. **Non-Functional Requirements**: Non-functional requirements describe the characteristics of the software system that are not directly related to its functionality. They specify how well the system should perform and the constraints it must operate within. Examples of non-functional requirements include performance, scalability, and security.

6. **Importance**: The SRS is an important document as it serves as a contract between the development team and the stakeholders. It ensures that all parties have a clear understanding of the requirements of the software system and helps to prevent misunderstandings and miscommunications.

7. **Mnemonic**: A helpful mnemonic to remember the key elements of an SRS is **PANIC** - Purpose, Audience, Non-functional requirements, Introduction, and Content.

In summary, the Software Requirement Specifications (SRS) is a crucial document that describes the requirements of a software system. It serves as a reference for developers, testers, and stakeholders to ensure that the software meets the needs of its users. It is important that the SRS is written in a clear and concise manner and is structured into several sections, including an introduction, overall description, specific requirements, and appendices. The SRS should detail both the functional and non-functional requirements of the system and serves as a contract between the development team and the stakeholders. A helpful mnemonic to remember the key elements of an SRS is **PANIC** - Purpose, Audience, Non-functional requirements, Introduction, and Content.
