#### Walk Through (Walkthrough) Static testing strategy

A Walk Through (Walkthrough) is a type of static testing strategy that involves a group of people, including the author of the code or document, reviewing the code or document to identify any defects or issues. The goal of a Walk Through is to improve the quality of the code or document by finding and fixing defects early in the development process.

Here are some key points to remember about Walk Throughs:

1. A Walk Through is a type of peer review, where the author of the code or document presents their work to a group of their peers for review and feedback.
2. The group of reviewers should include individuals with different areas of expertise, such as developers, testers, and business analysts, to ensure a thorough review.
3. The author should prepare for the Walk Through by creating an agenda and providing any necessary background information to the reviewers.
4. During the Walk Through, the author should present their work and the reviewers should ask questions and provide feedback.
5. The goal of the Walk Through is to identify defects and issues, not to fix them during the meeting. Any defects or issues identified should be recorded and addressed after the meeting.
6. Walk Throughs can be time-consuming, so it is important to use them judiciously and only for critical or complex code or documents.

One mnemonic to remember the key points of a Walk Through is **PAGFDR**:
- **P**eer review
- **A**uthor presents work
- **G**roup of reviewers with different expertise
- **F**eedback and questions from reviewers
- **D**efects and issues identified, not fixed during meeting
- **R**ecorded and addressed after meeting

Walk Throughs can be a valuable tool for improving the quality of code or documents by identifying defects early in the development process. However, they can be time-consuming, so it is important to use them judiciously and only for critical or complex code or documents.
