### Spiral Model in SDLC

- Spiral Model is a type of software development life cycle (SDLC) model that combines the features of the waterfall model and the prototyping model.
- It is based on the idea of iterative and incremental development, where the software is developed in cycles, each cycle consisting of four phases: planning, risk analysis, engineering, and evaluation.
- The spiral model is suitable for large, complex, and high-risk projects, where the requirements are not clear or stable, and where the customer feedback is important.
- The spiral model has the following advantages:
  - It allows for early detection and resolution of risks and issues, which can reduce the cost and time of development.
  - It provides flexibility and adaptability to changing requirements and customer needs, as the software can be modified and improved in each cycle.
  - It ensures high quality and customer satisfaction, as the software is tested and evaluated in each cycle, and the customer can provide feedback and suggestions.
- The spiral model has the following disadvantages:
  - It can be expensive and time-consuming, as each cycle involves a lot of planning, analysis, design, testing, and evaluation activities.
  - It requires a lot of expertise and experience in risk management and project management, as the success of the project depends on the identification and mitigation of risks and issues.
  - It can be difficult to estimate the cost and time of the project, as the scope and complexity of the project may change in each cycle.
- A mnemonic to remember the four phases of the spiral model is **PARE** (Planning, Analysis, Engineering, Evaluation).
