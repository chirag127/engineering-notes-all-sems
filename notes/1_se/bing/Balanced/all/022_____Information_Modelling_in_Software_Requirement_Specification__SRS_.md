### Information Modelling in Software Requirement Specification (SRS)

- Information modelling is the process of creating a logical representation of the data and information that will be used by the software system.
- Information modelling helps to define the data structures, relationships, constraints, and operations that are relevant to the software requirements.
- Information modelling can be done using various techniques and notations, such as entity-relationship diagrams (ERDs), class diagrams, data flow diagrams (DFDs), data dictionaries, etc.
- Information modelling can provide the following benefits for the software requirement specification (SRS):
  - It can help to identify and eliminate ambiguities, inconsistencies, and redundancies in the requirements.
  - It can help to communicate the requirements clearly and precisely to the stakeholders and developers.
  - It can help to validate and verify the requirements against the business and user needs.
  - It can help to facilitate the design, implementation, testing, and maintenance of the software system.
- Information modelling can be done at different levels of abstraction and detail, depending on the scope and complexity of the software system.
- Information modelling can be divided into two main phases: conceptual modelling and logical modelling.
  - Conceptual modelling is the process of creating a high-level overview of the information domain, without considering the implementation details or the physical representation of the data.
  - Logical modelling is the process of refining and formalizing the conceptual model, by specifying the data types, attributes, keys, relationships, constraints, and operations of the data entities.
- A possible mnemonic to remember the steps of information modelling is: **C**reate **C**onceptual **M**odel, **R**efine **L**ogical **M**odel, **V**alidate and **V**erify **M**odel, **C**ommunicate and **D**ocument **M**odel. (CCM-RLM-VVM-CDM)
