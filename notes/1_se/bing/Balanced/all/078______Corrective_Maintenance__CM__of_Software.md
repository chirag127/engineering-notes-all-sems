#### Corrective Maintenance (CM) of Software

- Corrective maintenance is the process of fixing errors or faults in software applications that affect its functionality, performance, or quality .
- Corrective maintenance can be triggered by user reports, system alerts, testing results, or audits.
- Corrective maintenance can involve repairing or replacing software components, modifying design, logic, or code, updating documentation, or applying patches .
- Corrective maintenance can be classified into two types: emergency and planned.
  - Emergency corrective maintenance is performed when a software failure causes a critical impact on the system or the users, and requires immediate action to restore normal operation.
  - Planned corrective maintenance is performed when a software error is detected but does not cause a severe disruption, and can be scheduled for a convenient time to avoid affecting the system availability or performance.
- Corrective maintenance can have several benefits, such as improving software reliability, usability, security, and compliance, reducing operational costs and risks, and enhancing user satisfaction and loyalty .
- Corrective maintenance can also have some challenges, such as increasing software complexity, requiring more resources and time, introducing new errors or dependencies, and affecting software evolution and maintenance .
- Corrective maintenance is an essential part of software maintenance, but it should not be the only one. Other types of software maintenance include preventive, perfective, and adaptive maintenance, which aim to prevent errors, improve functionality, and adapt to changing requirements or environments, respectively .
