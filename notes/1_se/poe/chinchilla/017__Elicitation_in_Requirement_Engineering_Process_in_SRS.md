### Elicitation in Requirement Engineering Process in SRS

Elicitation is a crucial step in the requirement engineering process for Software Requirement Specifications (SRS). It involves the gathering of information from stakeholders to determine the features and functionalities of a software product. The following are the key points to consider for Elicitation in Requirement Engineering Process in SRS:

1. Identify Stakeholders: The first step in the elicitation process is to identify the stakeholders who will be involved in providing the requirements for the software product. Stakeholders can be internal or external to the organization.

2. Understand Business Needs: It is essential to understand the business needs of the organization before eliciting the requirements. This helps in aligning the requirements with the overall goals of the organization.

3. Use Appropriate Techniques: There are several techniques that can be used for eliciting requirements, such as interviews, surveys, focus groups, and workshops. The appropriate technique should be selected based on the type of requirements that need to be elicited.

4. Document Requirements: It is imperative to document the requirements accurately to ensure that they are well-defined, complete, and consistent. This documentation serves as a reference for the development team and helps in managing changes to the requirements.

5. Validate Requirements: Once the requirements have been documented, they should be validated to ensure that they meet the needs of the stakeholders. Validation can be done through reviews, walkthroughs, and prototypes.

6. Manage Changes: Requirements are subject to change throughout the software development lifecycle. It is essential to have a process in place to manage changes to the requirements and ensure that they are communicated to all stakeholders.

7. Involve the Right People: The right people should be involved in the elicitation process, including subject matter experts, users, and developers. This helps in ensuring that the requirements are accurate and complete.

8. Consider Non-Functional Requirements: Non-functional requirements, such as performance, security, and usability, should also be considered during the elicitation process. These requirements can have a significant impact on the success of the software product.

In conclusion, Elicitation is a crucial step in the requirement engineering process for Software Requirement Specifications (SRS). It involves the gathering of information from stakeholders to determine the features and functionalities of a software product. The above key points should be considered for effective elicitation in the SRS process.
