#### Cohesion Measures in Software Design

- Coupling refers to the degree of interdependence between software modules. High cohesion refers to strong relationship between the tasks within a module. The following are some measures of cohesion:

1. Communicational Cohesion: Modules are cohesive if they operate on the same data.
2. Sequential Cohesion: Modules are cohesive if the output of one module is the input to the next module.
3. Functional Cohesion: Modules are cohesive if they perform subsets of a single task.
4. Procedural Cohesion: Modules are cohesive if they perform a series of steps to achieve a goal.

High cohesion leads to several benefits such as:

- Increased understandability - Logically related components are grouped together
- Increased maintainability - Changes are localized to specific modules
- Increased reusability - Cohesive modules can be reused
- Increased reliability - Errors are localized to specific modules

To achieve high cohesion:

- Focus on a single purpose or task per module
- Group logically or functionally related components together
- Minimize dependency between modules
- Apply the Single Responsibility Principle
