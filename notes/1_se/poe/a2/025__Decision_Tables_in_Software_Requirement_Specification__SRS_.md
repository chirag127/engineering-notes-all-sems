### Decision Tables in Software Requirement Specification (SRS)

- Decision tables are a useful way to specify conditional logic in software requirements.
- They show the combination of conditions and the corresponding outcomes in a tabular format.
- Each column in a decision table represents a condition or set of conditions. Each row shows a unique combination of conditions and the associated outcome.
- Decision tables are useful to specify complicated business rules or complex logic. They provide a comprehensive view of various scenarios and their expected outputs.
- While writing decision tables in SRS, follow these guidelines:

- Clearly state the purpose and usage of the decision table.
- Identify all input conditions and list them in the first column. Ensure there are no duplication or overlap between conditions.
- List all possible combinations of conditions in separate rows. The number of rows depends on the number of conditions.
- Specify the corresponding outcome for each row. The outcomes can be actions, outputs or transitions.
- Ensure there is an outcome specified for every possible combination of conditions. No rows should be left empty.
- Provide a default outcome for invalid or undefined combinations of conditions.
- Use consistent and unambiguous terms while specifying conditions and outcomes.
- Keep the decision table simple and easy to understand. Break complex tables into multiple smaller tables if required.
