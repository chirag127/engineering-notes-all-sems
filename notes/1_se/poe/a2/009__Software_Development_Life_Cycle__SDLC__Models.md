### Software Development Life Cycle (SDLC) Models

1. Waterfall Model: Sequential design process. stages: Requirements, Design, Implementation, Testing, Deployment. One stage must be completed before moving to next stage.
2. Agile Model: Iterative and incremental. Developed parts of software are delivered in short iterations. Focuses on customer collaboration. Examples: Scrum, Extreme Programming.
3. V-Shaped Model: Phases are mapped to corresponding test phases. Testing performed in parallel to development. Emphasis on testing.
4. Spiral Model: Software developed in iterative cycles. Each cycle involves planning, risk analysis, engineering and evaluation. Useful for large, expensive and complicated software projects. Risks are mitigated.
5. DevOps Model: Focuses on collaboration and communication between software developers and other IT professionals. Goals: faster release cycles, increased deployment frequency and more stable operating environments.
6. Rapid Application Development (RAD) Model: Focuses on building software in a very short period of time while compromising on functionality and software quality. Prototyping and iterative development for speed.

The models highlight key software development phases and differ in terms of rigidity, speed and functionality. The appropriate model is chosen based on requirements and constraints of the software project.
