### Elicitation in Requirement Engineering Process in SRS

1. Requirement elicitation is the process of gathering requirements from stakeholders for a system to be developed or modified.
2. Elicitation techniques help stakeholders articulate their needs, desires, and constraints regarding the system.
3. Some common elicitation techniques are:
- Interviews: One-on-one meetings with stakeholders to understand their requirements.
- Focus groups: Facilitated group meetings with stakeholders to discuss high-level requirements.
- Observation: Observing stakeholders to understand their tasks and requirements.
- Surveys: Questionnaires to a large number of stakeholders to gather requirements.
- Prototyping: Developing prototypes and getting feedback from stakeholders.
- Documentation analysis: Analyzing existing documents to elicit requirements.
4. Elicited requirements are then analyzed and organized into the SRS (Software Requirements Specification) document. The SRS acts as a contract between stakeholders and developers.
5. Challenges in requirement elicitation include stakeholders not knowing what they need, misunderstanding stakeholder needs, and complex or vague requirements. Requirement engineers need to apply appropriate elicitation techniques and handle these challenges to gather the right set of requirements.
