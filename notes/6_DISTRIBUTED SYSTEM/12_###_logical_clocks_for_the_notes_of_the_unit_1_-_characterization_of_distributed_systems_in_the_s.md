### Logical clocks for the notes of the Unit 1 - Characterization of Distributed Systems in the subject of DISTRIBUTED SYSTEM
Logical clocks are a method for assigning timestamps to events in a distributed system. They are used to maintain causality, which is the relationship between events that determine the order in which they occur. Logical clocks are used to assign timestamps to messages in a distributed system, which helps to order events and determine causality. They work by assigning a unique timestamp to each event, based on the timestamps of previous events. There are two main types of logical clocks: Lamport clocks and Vector clocks. Lamport clocks use a simple counter to assign timestamps, while Vector clocks use a vector of counters to keep track of causality between multiple processes. Logical clocks are important in distributed systems because they help to ensure consistency and avoid conflicts when multiple processes are updating the same data.
