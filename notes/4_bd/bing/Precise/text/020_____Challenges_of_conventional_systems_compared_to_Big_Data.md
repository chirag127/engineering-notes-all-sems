### Challenges of conventional systems compared to Big Data

1. **Scalability**: Conventional systems are not designed to handle the large volume of data that Big Data systems can handle. As the volume of data grows, conventional systems struggle to scale and process the data efficiently.

2. **Data Variety**: Big Data systems are designed to handle a wide variety of data types, including structured, semi-structured, and unstructured data. Conventional systems, on the other hand, are typically designed to handle structured data and may struggle to process and analyze data that does not fit into a predefined schema.

3. **Real-time Processing**: Big Data systems are designed to process data in real-time, allowing for faster decision making and more timely insights. Conventional systems, on the other hand, may require batch processing, which can result in delays and outdated information.

4. **Cost**: Big Data systems can be more cost-effective than conventional systems, as they are designed to run on commodity hardware and can scale horizontally by adding more machines to the cluster. Conventional systems, on the other hand, may require expensive, specialized hardware and may not scale as easily.

5. **Flexibility**: Big Data systems are designed to be flexible and can adapt to changing data and processing requirements. Conventional systems, on the other hand, may be more rigid and may require significant effort to modify or update.

These are some of the challenges that conventional systems face when compared to Big Data systems. Big Data systems are designed to handle large volumes of data, process data in real-time, and adapt to changing requirements, making them a more suitable choice for many modern data processing needs.
