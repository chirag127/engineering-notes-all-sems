### Pig

- A pig (Sus domesticus) is an omnivorous, domesticated, even-toed, hoofed mammal that belongs to the family Suidae and the genus Sus. 
- It is considered a subspecies of the wild boar (Sus scrofa) or a distinct species. 
- Pigs have a large head with a long snout that is strengthened by a special prenasal bone and a disk of cartilage at the tip.  
- The snout is used to dig into the soil to find food and is a very acute sense organ.  
- Pigs have 44 teeth, with the canine teeth forming tusks in males that grow continuously and are sharpened by constantly being ground against each other.  
- Pigs have four hoofed toes on each foot, with the two larger central toes bearing most of the weight, but the outer two also being used in soft ground.  
- Pigs have a sparse hair covering on their skin, except for some woolly-coated breeds.  
- Pigs have both apocrine and eccrine sweat glands, but they do not use them for cooling.  
- Pigs lose heat by wallowing in mud or water via evaporative cooling, which may also serve other functions such as protection from sunburn, ecto-parasite control, and scent-marking.  
- Pigs are one of four known mammalian species that possess mutations in the nicotinic acetylcholine receptor that protect against snake venom. 
- Pigs are farmed primarily for the production of meat, called pork, but their bones, hide, and bristles are also used in products.  
- Pigs, especially miniature breeds, are kept as pets.  
- A group of pigs is called a passel, a team, or a sounder.  
- The pig's head-plus-body length ranges from 0.9 to 1.8 m (3 to 6 ft), and adult pigs typically weigh between 50 and 350 kg (110 and 770 lb), with well-fed individuals even exceeding this range.  
- The size and weight of pigs largely depends on their breed.  
- The pig's core body temperature is about 39 °C (102 °F), and its thermoneutral zone is 16 to 22 °C (61 to 72 °F).
