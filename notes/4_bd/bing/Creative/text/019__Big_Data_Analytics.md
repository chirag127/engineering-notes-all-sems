### Big Data Analytics

- Big data analytics is the use of advanced analytic techniques against very large, diverse big data sets that include structured, semi-structured and unstructured data, from different sources, and in different sizes from terabytes to zettabytes .
- Big data analytics can help businesses discover market trends, insights, and patterns that can improve their decision-making, efficiency, and profitability .
- Big data analytics can also help businesses understand customer needs, identify potential risks, create new products and services, and optimize their operations.
- Big data analytics can be applied to various industries, such as health care, education, finance, retail, manufacturing, and more .
- Big data analytics uses four data analysis methods: descriptive, diagnostic, predictive, and prescriptive.
  - Descriptive analytics summarizes what has happened in the past using historical data.
  - Diagnostic analytics explains why something has happened in the past using statistical techniques.
  - Predictive analytics forecasts what might happen in the future using machine learning and artificial intelligence.
  - Prescriptive analytics suggests what actions should be taken in the future using optimization and simulation.
- Big data analytics requires both innovative technologies and analytical skills to collect, process, and derive insights from big data.
- Big data is characterized by the five V's: volume, velocity, variety, variability, and value.
  - Volume refers to the amount of data generated and stored.
  - Velocity refers to the speed at which data is generated and processed.
  - Variety refers to the diversity of data types and sources.
  - Variability refers to the inconsistency and unpredictability of data quality and meaning.
  - Value refers to the usefulness and relevance of data for business purposes.
- Big data analytics relies on various tools and systems, such as business intelligence (BI) tools, data warehouses, data lakes, cloud computing, Hadoop, Spark, and more .
