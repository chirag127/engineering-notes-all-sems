#### Stages and Tasks in Spark

- Stages and tasks are the basic units of execution in Spark.
- A stage in Spark represents a set of parallel tasks that operate on a subset of the data. The tasks within a stage are executed on different executor nodes in the cluster, and the data is partitioned into smaller chunks called partitions, which are processed by the tasks in parallel .
- A task in Spark is a single unit of work or execution that is sent to a Spark executor. Each task maps to a single core and works on a single partition of data .
- Stages and tasks are created based on the operations performed on the data. There are two types of operations in Spark: transformations and actions.
- Transformations are operations that create a new RDD from an existing one, such as map, filter, join, etc. Transformations are lazy, meaning they are not executed until an action is invoked on the RDD.
- Actions are operations that trigger a computation on the RDD and return a value to the driver program, such as count, collect, save, etc. Actions are eager, meaning they are executed immediately when called.
- When an action is invoked on an RDD, a Spark job is created. A job is a parallel computation consisting of multiple tasks that gets spawned in response to a Spark action .
- A job is divided into stages based on the shuffle boundary. Shuffle is a process of redistributing the data across the cluster, such as when a groupBy or sortBy operation is performed. Shuffle creates a dependency between the stages, as the output of one stage becomes the input of the next stage.
- There are mainly two types of stages in Spark: ShuffleMapStage and ResultStage. The ShuffleMapStage is an intermediate stage that prepares data for subsequent stages, such as by sorting or aggregating the data. The ResultStage is the final stage that computes the result of the Spark action, such as by counting or collecting the data.
- The number of tasks in a stage depends on the number of partitions in the RDD. The number of partitions in an RDD can be specified by the user or determined by Spark based on the size of the data and the configuration of the cluster.
- The execution of stages and tasks can be monitored using the Spark UI, which provides a graphical representation of the DAG (directed acyclic graph) of the job, the status and duration of each stage and task, the input and output data size, the shuffle statistics, and the resource utilization of each executor.
