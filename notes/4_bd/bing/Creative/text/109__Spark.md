### Spark

Spark is a word that has multiple meanings and uses in English. Depending on the context, it can be a noun or a verb, and it can refer to different phenomena related to fire, electricity, or feelings. Here are some of the main definitions and examples of spark:

- As a noun, spark can mean:
  - A small particle of a burning substance thrown out by a fire, alight in ashes, or produced by striking together two hard surfaces such as stone or metal  . For example: _Sparks were flying out of the bonfire and blowing everywhere._
  - A small flash of light produced by a sudden disruptive electrical discharge through the air  . For example: _Angry sparks were flashing in her eyes._
  - An electrical discharge serving to ignite the explosive mixture in an internal combustion engine . For example: _The ignition sparks as soon as the gas is turned on._
  - A trace of a specified quality or intense feeling . For example: _There was a spark of light in the dark room._
  - A sense of liveliness and excitement . For example: _There was a spark between them at their first meeting._
  - A lively young fellow. For example: _He was a spark of a boy, always cheerful and energetic._
- As a verb, spark can mean:
  - To emit sparks of fire or electricity . For example: _The wires sparked when they touched each other._
  - To produce sparks at the point where an electric circuit is interrupted. For example: _The switch sparked as he turned it on._
  - To ignite or set fire to something . For example: _The explosion sparked a fire in the building._
  - To provide the stimulus for something, especially an argument or a fight . For example: _The question sparked a lively discussion among the students._
  - To stir to activity or incite something . For example: _The coach sparked her team to victory with an inspiring speech._
  - To woo or court someone. For example: _He went a sparking among the rosy country girls._
