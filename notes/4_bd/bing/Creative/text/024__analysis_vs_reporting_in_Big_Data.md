### Analysis vs Reporting in Big Data

- Analysis and reporting are both important processes for data-informed business practices, but they have different purposes and outcomes.
- Reporting is the process of gathering data from various sources and presenting it in visual forms, such as charts, tables, and dashboards. Reporting tells what is happening with the data, such as trends, patterns, and changes. Reporting is useful for monitoring performance, tracking progress, and communicating results.
- Analysis is the process of interpreting data and giving it context, such as why something is happening, what are the causes and effects, and what are the implications and recommendations. Analysis is useful for exploring data, finding insights, and solving problems.
- Some of the key differences between analysis and reporting are:

  - Reporting is descriptive, while analysis is prescriptive. Reporting describes the data, while analysis prescribes actions based on the data.
  - Reporting is objective, while analysis is subjective. Reporting presents the data as it is, while analysis interprets the data and draws conclusions.
  - Reporting is repeatable, while analysis is exploratory. Reporting follows a fixed format and frequency, while analysis varies depending on the question and the data.
  - Reporting is retrospective, while analysis is prospective. Reporting looks at the past and present data, while analysis looks at the future and potential data.
  - Reporting is quantitative, while analysis is qualitative. Reporting relies on numbers and metrics, while analysis relies on logic and reasoning.
  - Reporting is passive, while analysis is active. Reporting provides information, while analysis provides insights.
