### Drivers for Big Data

Big data is the term used to describe the massive amounts of data that are generated and collected from various sources, such as social media, sensors, mobile devices, web logs, etc. Big data has the potential to provide valuable insights and solutions for various business and societal problems, but it also poses significant challenges in terms of storage, processing, analysis, and visualization.

There are several factors that have contributed to the emergence and growth of big data in the recent years. These factors are known as the drivers for big data, and they include:

- **The digitization of society**: With the widespread adoption of digital technologies, such as smartphones, tablets, laptops, etc., people are constantly creating and consuming data through various applications and platforms. According to some estimates, 60% of the data in the world was generated within the last two years , which shows the rapid pace of data creation and digitization.
- **The drop in technology costs**: The costs of data storage and processing have decreased significantly over the years, making it more affordable and accessible for small and large organizations to handle big data. For example, the cost of computer memory has dropped from $411,000 per gigabyte in 1980 to $0.02 per gigabyte in 2019. Moreover, the development of open source software frameworks, such as Apache Hadoop, Spark, etc., has enabled distributed and parallel processing of big data at scale.
- **Connectivity through cloud computing**: Cloud computing is the delivery of computing services, such as storage, processing, networking, etc., over the internet, rather than on local servers or devices. Cloud computing enables organizations to quickly scale up or down their IT infrastructure and pay only for what they use. This makes it easier and cheaper to store and process big data in remote and distributed systems, without having to invest in large and expensive hardware.
- **Increased knowledge about data science**: Data science is the interdisciplinary field that combines mathematics, statistics, computer science, and domain knowledge to extract meaningful insights from data. Data science has become more popular and accessible in recent years, thanks to the availability of online courses, books, blogs, podcasts, etc. that teach the concepts, techniques, and tools of data science. Data science has also become more in demand, as organizations realize the value and potential of big data for improving their decision making, performance, and innovation.
- **Social media applications**: Social media platforms, such as Facebook, Twitter, Instagram, etc., are among the major sources and consumers of big data. Social media users generate and share a variety of data, such as text, images, videos, audio, etc., on a daily basis, creating a rich and diverse dataset that can be analyzed for various purposes, such as sentiment analysis, trend detection, recommendation systems, etc. Social media platforms also use big data to enhance their user experience, engagement, and monetization, by providing personalized content, ads, and features.
- **The rise of Internet-of-Things (IoT)**: IoT is the network of physical objects, such as devices, vehicles, appliances, etc., that are embedded with sensors, software, and connectivity, enabling them to collect and exchange data with other objects and systems. IoT has enabled the generation and collection of big data from various domains, such as smart homes, smart cities, smart health, smart agriculture, etc., creating new opportunities and challenges for data analysis and management.
