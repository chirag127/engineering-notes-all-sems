### Challenges of conventional systems compared to Big Data

- Conventional systems are designed to handle structured and relational data, which have predefined schemas and fixed formats. Big Data, on the other hand, consists of structured, semi-structured, and unstructured data, which have diverse sources, types, and formats. Conventional systems cannot efficiently process and analyze such heterogeneous and complex data .
- Conventional systems are limited by the size and volume of the data they can store and process. Big Data is characterized by its massive scale, which can range from petabytes to zettabytes or more. Conventional systems cannot scale up to meet the storage and processing demands of Big Data, which requires distributed and parallel computing architectures .
- Conventional systems are not designed to handle the velocity and veracity of Big Data. Big Data is generated at a high speed and needs to be processed in real time or near real time. Conventional systems cannot cope with the streaming and dynamic nature of Big Data, which requires fast and flexible data ingestion and analysis methods . Big Data is also prone to errors, inconsistencies, and noise, which can affect its quality and reliability. Conventional systems cannot ensure the accuracy and validity of Big Data, which requires robust and scalable data cleansing and integration techniques .
- Conventional systems are not able to provide the same level of insights and benefits as Big Data. Big Data can reveal hidden patterns, trends, and correlations that are not possible to discover with traditional data. Big Data can also enable more accurate predictions, better decision making, and improved efficiency and performance for businesses and organizations. Conventional systems cannot leverage the full potential of Big Data, which requires advanced and innovative data analytics and visualization tools .
