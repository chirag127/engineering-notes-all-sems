### Zookeeper

A zookeeper is a person who works in a zoo and is responsible for the care and management of the animals. Some of the duties of a zookeeper are:

- Feeding the animals according to their dietary needs and schedules.
- Cleaning the enclosures and providing fresh water and bedding for the animals.
- Monitoring the health and behavior of the animals and reporting any signs of illness or injury to the veterinarian.
- Enriching the environment of the animals to stimulate their natural behaviors and prevent boredom and stress.
- Educating the public about the animals and their conservation status and needs.
- Assisting with the breeding and research programs of the zoo.
- Handling and restraining the animals when necessary for medical or safety reasons.
- Following the safety and ethical protocols of the zoo and the animal welfare laws and regulations.

To become a zookeeper, one usually needs to have:

- A high school diploma or equivalent.
- A bachelor's degree in zoology, biology, animal science, wildlife management, or a related field.
- Some experience working with animals, such as volunteering or interning at a zoo, wildlife sanctuary, animal shelter, or veterinary clinic.
- A passion for animals and a willingness to learn and work hard.
- Physical stamina, strength, and agility to handle the demands of the job.
- Good communication, teamwork, and problem-solving skills.
- A valid driver's license and a clean background check.

The average salary of a zookeeper in the United States is about $30,000 per year, but it may vary depending on the location, experience, and qualifications of the zookeeper. The job outlook for zookeepers is expected to grow by 4% from 2019 to 2029, which is about as fast as the average for all occupations. However, the competition for zookeeper positions is high, as there are more applicants than available jobs. Therefore, zookeepers may need to relocate or work part-time or seasonal jobs to find employment opportunities. Zookeepers may also advance their careers by obtaining additional education, certifications, or specializations in specific areas of animal care or management.
