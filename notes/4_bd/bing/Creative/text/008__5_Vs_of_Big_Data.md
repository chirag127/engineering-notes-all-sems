### 5 Vs of Big Data

Big data is a term that refers to the large and complex datasets that are generated from various sources and require special techniques and technologies to process, analyze and derive value from them. Big data has five main characteristics, also known as the 5 Vs, that describe its properties and challenges. They are:

- **Volume**: This refers to the amount or size of data that is collected and stored. Big data typically involves terabytes, petabytes or even exabytes of data. The volume of data determines whether it can be considered as big data or not, and also affects the storage, processing and analysis methods that are needed. For example, in 2016, the estimated global mobile traffic was 6.2 exabytes (6.2 billion GB) per month. By 2020, we will have almost 40000 exabytes of data.

- **Velocity**: This refers to the speed or rate at which data is generated and processed. Big data often involves real-time or near-real-time data streams that need to be captured and analyzed quickly. The velocity of data affects the potential of data to provide timely and relevant insights and actions. For example, there are more than 3.5 billion searches per day on Google. Facebook users are increasing by 22% (approx.) year by year.

- **Variety**: This refers to the diversity or heterogeneity of data types and sources. Big data can be structured, semi-structured or unstructured, and can come from different domains, such as text, images, videos, audio, sensor data, social media, web logs, etc. The variety of data poses challenges for data integration, quality, consistency and analysis. For example, text, pictures and videos are examples of unstructured data that cannot be stored in the form of rows and columns in a relational database.

- **Veracity**: This refers to the quality, accuracy, reliability and trustworthiness of data. Big data can be noisy, incomplete, inconsistent, ambiguous, erroneous or fraudulent, and can affect the validity and usefulness of the data analysis and results. The veracity of data depends on the data sources, collection methods, processing techniques and analysis models. For example, data in bulk could create confusion, whereas less amount of data could convey half or incomplete information.

- **Value**: This refers to the usefulness, relevance, benefit and impact of data. Big data can provide valuable insights and opportunities for organizations, businesses, governments and individuals, if it is properly processed, analyzed and interpreted. The value of data depends on the data quality, context, objectives, methods and outcomes. For example, big data can provide companies with valuable insights into their customers that can then be used to refine marketing techniques to increase customer engagement and conversion rates.
