# Big Data

Big data is a term that refers to the large, diverse, and fast-growing sets of information that are generated by various sources and require special techniques and technologies to store, process, and analyze. Big data can be used to discover patterns, trends, and insights that can help businesses, governments, and individuals make better decisions and solve complex problems. Some of the characteristics and applications of big data are:

- **Volume**: Big data involves huge amounts of data, ranging from terabytes to petabytes and beyond, that exceed the capacity of traditional data storage and processing systems. For example, according to Oracle, Twitter generates about 12 terabytes of data per day, and Facebook ingests 500 terabytes of new data every day.
- **Velocity**: Big data is generated and collected at high speed, often in real time or near real time, and requires fast processing and analysis to extract value. For example, according to Oracle, smart meters can collect data every 15 minutes, and sensors on jet engines can generate 10 terabytes of data per 30 minutes of flight.
- **Variety**: Big data comes in different formats and types, from structured data that can be easily organized and stored in databases or spreadsheets, to unstructured data that is more complex and diverse, such as text, audio, video, images, social media posts, web pages, etc. For example, according to Investopedia, unstructured data accounts for 80% of the world's data.
- **Value**: Big data has the potential to provide valuable insights and solutions for various domains and challenges, such as customer behavior, market trends, fraud detection, risk management, health care, education, security, etc. However, big data also poses challenges, such as data quality, data privacy, data security, data integration, data analysis, data visualization, etc. Therefore, big data requires specialized skills, tools, and methods to extract value and ensure trustworthiness.
- **Veracity**: Big data also involves the accuracy, reliability, and validity of the data, which can affect the quality and usefulness of the analysis and outcomes. Big data can be affected by noise, errors, biases, inconsistencies, incompleteness, etc. Therefore, big data requires techniques and measures to ensure data veracity and reduce uncertainty.
