### Pig

A pig is a mammal that belongs to the order Artiodactyla, which means it has an even number of toes on each foot. Pigs are also known as hogs or swine, and they are raised for their meat, skin, and other products. Pigs are found and raised all over the world, and they have a long history of domestication and interaction with humans. Here are some facts about pigs:

- Pigs have the intelligence of a human toddler and are ranked as the fifth most intelligent animal in the world. They can learn their names, come when they are called, and even play video games better than some primates.
- Pigs have a keen sense of smell and can detect odors up to seven miles away and 20 feet underground. They use their snouts for digging, rooting, and communicating with other pigs.
- Pigs do not have many sweat glands, so they do not sweat much. They cool themselves by wallowing in mud or water, which also protects their skin from sunburn and parasites.
- Pigs are omnivorous, which means they eat both plants and animals. They will eat almost anything, including fruits, vegetables, grains, nuts, seeds, insects, worms, eggs, and even feces. Pigs need a balanced diet and plenty of water to stay healthy.
- Pigs are social animals and live in groups called herds or sounders. They form bonds with each other and have a hierarchy based on size, age, and sex. Pigs communicate with each other using a variety of sounds, such as grunts, squeals, snorts, and oinks.
- Pigs are sexually mature at about six months of age, and they can breed throughout the year. The gestation period of a pig is about 114 days, and the average litter size is 10 to 12 piglets. The mother pig, called a sow, nurses her piglets for about three to four weeks, and then weans them off her milk.
- Pigs can live up to 20 years in captivity, but their lifespan is much shorter in the wild or on farms. The average lifespan of a pig is about six to 10 years. Pigs are susceptible to various diseases and parasites, such as swine flu, foot-and-mouth disease, mange, and worms.
- Pigs are one of the most widely consumed animals in the world, and they provide valuable products to humans, such as pork, bacon, ham, sausage, lard, leather, glue, fertilizer, and medicine. Pigs are also used for scientific research, such as organ transplantation, gene therapy, and stem cell therapy.
- Pigs are not native to North America, but they were introduced by European settlers in the 16th century. Today, there are about 75 million pigs in the United States, and they are raised in large-scale farms or factory farms. Pigs are also kept as pets by some people, especially miniature breeds, such as pot-bellied pigs, teacup pigs, and micro pigs.
- Pigs are related to other animals, such as hippos, whales, deer, camels, and giraffes. They belong to the suborder Suina, which includes two families: Suidae (pigs) and Tayassuidae (peccaries). There are about 16 species of pigs in the world, and some of the most common ones are the domestic pig, the wild boar, the warthog, and the pygmy hog .
