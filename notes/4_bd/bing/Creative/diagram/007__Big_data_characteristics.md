Big data is a term that refers to data sets that are too large, complex, or diverse to be handled by conventional data processing methods. Big data has five main characteristics, often known as the 5 Vs: volume, velocity, variety, value, and veracity.

- Volume: This refers to the amount or size of data that is generated and stored. Big data can range from terabytes to zettabytes of data, depending on the source and application. For example, a single MRI scan can result in 20,000 images, and an autonomous vehicle can generate 11 terabytes of data each day.
- Velocity: This refers to the speed or rate at which data is generated, collected, processed, and analyzed. Big data can be produced in real-time or near real-time, requiring fast and efficient processing methods. For example, Google processes 3.5 billion search queries per day, and Twitter users post 3,000 tweets every second.
- Variety: This refers to the diversity or heterogeneity of data types and sources. Big data can be structured, semi-structured, or unstructured, and can come from various sources such as text, audio, video, images, sensors, social media, etc. For example, Instagram users post 54,000 photos each minute, and a smart city can have thousands of sensors collecting data on traffic, weather, pollution, etc.
- Value: This refers to the usefulness or significance of data for decision making, problem solving, or innovation. Big data can provide valuable insights and opportunities for businesses, organizations, and individuals, if analyzed properly and effectively. For example, big data can help optimize marketing strategies, improve customer satisfaction, enhance healthcare outcomes, or discover new scientific phenomena.
- Veracity: This refers to the quality or reliability of data, especially in terms of accuracy, completeness, consistency, and validity. Big data can be noisy, incomplete, inconsistent, or erroneous, depending on the source and method of collection. For example, big data can be affected by human errors, biases, fraud, or malicious attacks.
