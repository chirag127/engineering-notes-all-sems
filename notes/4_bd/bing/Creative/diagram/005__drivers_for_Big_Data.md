The drivers for Big Data are the factors that have contributed to the emergence and growth of Big Data as a field of study and practice. They are the business needs and technology innovations that have enabled organizations to collect, store, process, and analyze massive amounts of diverse and complex data.

The drivers for Big Data can be categorized into six main groups:

- The digitization of society: The increasing use of digital devices and applications by consumers and businesses has generated a huge amount of data that can be used for various purposes, such as personalization, recommendation, optimization, etc.
- The plummeting of technology costs: The decreasing costs of data storage and processing, as well as the availability of open source software frameworks, have made Big Data more accessible and affordable for organizations of different sizes and domains.
- Connectivity through cloud computing: The use of cloud computing platforms and services has enabled organizations to scale up or down their IT infrastructure and data resources according to their needs and pay only for what they use, reducing the upfront investment and operational costs of Big Data.
- Increased knowledge about data science: The development and dissemination of data science methods and techniques, such as statistics, machine learning, artificial intelligence, etc., have provided organizations with new ways of extracting insights and value from Big Data.
- Social media applications: The popularity and diversity of social media platforms and applications, such as Facebook, Twitter, Instagram, YouTube, etc., have created new sources and types of data that can be used for various purposes, such as sentiment analysis, trend detection, influencer identification, etc.
- The upcoming Internet-of-Things (IoT): The emergence and proliferation of IoT devices and systems, such as smart homes, smart cities, smart cars, smart wearables, etc., have created new opportunities and challenges for Big Data, as they generate large volumes of real-time and streaming data that require fast and efficient processing and analysis.
