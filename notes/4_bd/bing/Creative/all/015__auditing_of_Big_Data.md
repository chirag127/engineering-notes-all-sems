#### Auditing of Big Data

- Big data refers to the large and complex data sets that are generated and processed at high speed and variety from multiple sources, such as social media, sensors, web logs, etc.  
- Big data can provide opportunities for organizations to innovate and expand their market share by developing new products or making better decisions using advanced analytics techniques.  
- Big data also poses significant challenges and risks for organizations, such as data quality, security, privacy, compliance, governance, and ethics.   
- Auditing of big data involves providing assurance and advisory services to help the organization address the risks and challenges associated with big data, and ensure the alignment of big data initiatives with the strategic objectives and stakeholder expectations.  
- Auditing of big data requires auditors to have a good understanding of the big data concepts, components, and technologies, such as data sources, data storage, data processing, data analysis, and data visualization.   
- Auditing of big data also requires auditors to apply appropriate audit techniques and tools, such as data profiling, data sampling, data mining, data analytics, and data visualization, to assess the effectiveness and efficiency of the big data processes and controls.   
- Auditing of big data may involve the following steps:   
  - Define the audit objectives and scope, based on the risk assessment and stakeholder expectations.
  - Identify the big data components and processes, such as data sources, data storage, data processing, data analysis, and data visualization, and map them to the audit objectives and scope.
  - Evaluate the big data governance and management, such as policies, procedures, roles, responsibilities, and performance indicators, and assess their adequacy and effectiveness.
  - Assess the big data quality and integrity, such as completeness, accuracy, validity, consistency, and timeliness, and identify any data quality issues or gaps.
  - Review the big data security and privacy, such as access controls, encryption, anonymization, and compliance with relevant laws and regulations, and identify any security or privacy breaches or risks.
  - Test the big data processes and controls, such as data collection, data transformation, data analysis, and data reporting, and verify their accuracy, reliability, and efficiency.
  - Analyze the big data outputs and outcomes, such as reports, dashboards, insights, and recommendations, and evaluate their relevance, usefulness, and impact.
  - Report the audit findings and recommendations, such as strengths, weaknesses, opportunities, and threats, and communicate them to the relevant stakeholders.

- A possible mnemonic to remember the steps of auditing of big data is **DIE STAR** (Define, Identify, Evaluate, Assess, Review, Test, Analyze, Report).
