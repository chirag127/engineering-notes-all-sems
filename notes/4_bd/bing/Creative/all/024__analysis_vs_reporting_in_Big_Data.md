### Analysis vs Reporting in Big Data

- Analysis and reporting are two essential tasks in big data that help to extract value and insights from large and complex datasets.
- Analysis is the process of exploring, transforming, modeling, and interpreting data to answer specific questions or test hypotheses. Analysis can be descriptive, predictive, or prescriptive, depending on the goal and the type of data.
- Reporting is the process of presenting and communicating the results of analysis to stakeholders, such as managers, customers, or regulators. Reporting can be done in various formats, such as dashboards, charts, tables, or narratives, depending on the audience and the purpose.
- Analysis and reporting are interrelated and complementary, but they have different objectives, methods, and skills. Some of the main differences are:

  - Analysis aims to discover patterns, trends, correlations, or causal relationships in data, while reporting aims to summarize, explain, or visualize the findings of analysis.
  - Analysis requires more technical and analytical skills, such as programming, statistics, machine learning, or data mining, while reporting requires more business and communication skills, such as domain knowledge, storytelling, or data visualization.
  - Analysis involves more creativity and experimentation, as there is no predefined or fixed way to analyze data, while reporting involves more standardization and consistency, as there are best practices and guidelines to follow for effective reporting.
  - Analysis is more iterative and dynamic, as it can change based on new data, questions, or feedback, while reporting is more static and final, as it delivers a clear and concise message to the audience.

- A simple mnemonic to remember the difference between analysis and reporting is:

  - Analysis: **A**sk questions, **N**avigate data, **A**pply methods, **L**earn insights, **Y**ield recommendations, **S**eek feedback, **I**mprove models, **S**olve problems.
  - Reporting: **R**eview findings, **E**valuate audience, **P**repare format, **O**rganize content, **R**efine message, **T**ell story, **I**llustrate data, **N**otify stakeholders, **G**et action.
