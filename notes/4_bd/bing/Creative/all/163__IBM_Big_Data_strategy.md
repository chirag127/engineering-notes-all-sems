### IBM Big Data strategy

- IBM Big Data strategy is the company's approach to offer solutions to store, manage, and analyze the huge amounts of data generated daily and equip large and small companies to make informed business decisions.
- IBM Big Data strategy is based on four dimensions: volume, variety, velocity, and veracity of data. These dimensions represent the challenges and opportunities of big data.
- IBM Big Data strategy is aligned with the company's Smarter Planet initiative, which seeks to highlight how government and business leaders can capture the potential of smarter systems to achieve economic and sustainable growth and societal progress.
- IBM Big Data strategy consists of six steps to design and implement a data strategy that makes sense of data, aligns to business strategy, and builds solutions that span the entire organization. The six steps are:
  - Understand your business objectives: Connect your data strategy with the business strategy by meeting with C-suite and business stakeholders and clarifying and agreeing on the priorities and metrics.
  - Assess your current state: Unpack pain points to reveal blockers and gaps by conducting a data maturity assessment and identifying the current and desired capabilities and outcomes.
  - Map out data strategy framework: Define your data's target state by outlining the vision, mission, principles, and goals of your data strategy and aligning them with the business objectives and stakeholder expectations.
  - Prioritize data initiatives: Focus on the most impactful and feasible data projects by applying a prioritization matrix and creating a roadmap and a business case for each initiative.
  - Execute data initiatives: Deliver data solutions that meet business needs by following an agile and iterative approach, leveraging best practices and standards, and measuring and communicating the value and impact of each solution.
  - Govern and evolve data strategy: Ensure data quality, security, and compliance by establishing a data governance framework and a data culture, and continuously monitoring and improving the data strategy and its outcomes.
