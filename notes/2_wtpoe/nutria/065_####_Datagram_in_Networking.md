

### Datagram in Networking

* A datagram is a self-contained, independent piece of data carrying enough information to be routed from the source to the destination without relying on earlier exchanges between the source and destination. 
* It is a type of packet switching technology used in computer networks, where data is sent in the form of packets or datagrams over the network.
* Datagrams are used in connectionless protocols, such as UDP, where each packet is routed independently and can be received out of order. 
* Each packet contains the source and destination IP addresses, as well as the data being sent.
* Datagrams are also used in connection-oriented protocols, such as TCP, where each packet is routed independently and can be received out of order. 
* In connection-oriented protocols, the datagrams are used to establish and maintain the connection between the source and the destination.
* Datagrams can be used for real-time applications, such as streaming audio and video, where data needs to be sent quickly and reliably. 
* Datagrams are also used for other applications, such as file transfers, where data needs to be sent reliably but does not need to be sent quickly.
* Advantages of datagrams include their simplicity, efficiency, and reliability. They are also able to handle large amounts of data quickly and reliably. 
* Disadvantages of datagrams include their vulnerability to packet loss and the need for additional protocols to ensure reliable delivery. 
* Examples of applications that use datagrams include streaming audio and video, file transfers, and voice over IP (VoIP).
