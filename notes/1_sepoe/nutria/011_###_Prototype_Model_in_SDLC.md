
### Prototype Model in SDLC

- The Prototype Model in SDLC is an iterative software development process that involves creating a prototype of the product, testing it, and then making changes and improvements based on the feedback. 
- The Prototype Model is used when the requirements of the software are not clearly defined and the user needs to be involved in the development process. 
- The model is based on the idea of prototyping, which is creating a working version of the product and then making changes and improvements based on the feedback from the users. 
- This model is useful for understanding the user's requirements and for developing a product that meets those requirements.
- The Prototype Model consists of four stages:
  1. Requirements Gathering: This is the first stage of the process where the user's requirements are gathered and analyzed. 
  2. Prototype Development: In this stage, a prototype of the product is created. This prototype can be a paper prototype, an interactive prototype, or a combination of both. 
  3. Testing: Once the prototype is created, it is tested by the users. This helps to identify any problems or issues with the product. 
  4. Refinement: After the testing phase, the product is refined based on the feedback from the users.
- Advantages of the Prototype Model: 
  - It helps to identify the user's requirements early on in the development process. 
  - It allows for rapid development of the product. 
  - It can help to reduce the risk of failure. 
  - It helps to reduce the cost of development. 
- Disadvantages of the Prototype Model: 
  - It can be difficult to create a prototype that accurately reflects the user's requirements. 
  - The prototype may not be able to handle large amounts of data. 
  - It can be difficult to make changes to the prototype once it has been developed. 
  - It can be difficult to determine when the prototype is ready for testing.
