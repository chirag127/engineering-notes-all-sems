### Elicitation in Requirement Engineering Process in SRS

Elicitation is the process of gathering requirements from stakeholders to develop a Software Requirements Specification (SRS). The SRS is a document that describes the software system to be developed, including its functional and non-functional requirements, constraints, and performance criteria. Elicitation is a crucial step in the requirement engineering process, as it lays the foundation for the entire software development process.

#### Steps involved in Elicitation:

1. Identifying stakeholders: This step involves identifying all the stakeholders who will be involved in the software development process, including end-users, customers, developers, project managers, and other relevant parties.

2. Planning and preparing for elicitation: This step involves planning the elicitation process, including the selection of appropriate elicitation techniques, scheduling meetings with stakeholders, and preparing the necessary documents and tools.

3. Conducting elicitation sessions: This step involves conducting the actual elicitation sessions, which can be done using various techniques such as interviews, surveys, focus groups, and brainstorming sessions.

4. Documenting requirements: This step involves documenting the requirements that have been gathered during the elicitation sessions in a clear and concise manner.

5. Validating requirements: This step involves reviewing the documented requirements with the stakeholders to ensure that they meet their expectations and are feasible to implement.

6. Managing requirements: This step involves managing the requirements throughout the software development process, including tracking changes and updates, and ensuring that they are properly implemented and tested.

#### Mnemonics and learning tricks:

- MIME (Most Important and Most Expected): This mnemonic can be used to prioritize the requirements gathered during elicitation sessions. Stakeholders should be asked to identify the most important and most expected requirements, which can then be given priority during the development process.

- SMART (Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, Time-bound): This mnemonic can be used to ensure that the requirements gathered during elicitation sessions are well-defined and can be easily measured and achieved.

- PECS (Problem, Environment, Constraints, Stakeholders): This mnemonic can be used as a checklist to ensure that all aspects of the software system are covered during the elicitation process.

- GROW (Goal, Reality, Options, Way Forward): This mnemonic can be used during elicitation sessions to help stakeholders identify their goals and requirements for the software system, and to explore different options for achieving those goals.

#### Advantages of Elicitation:

- Elicitation helps to ensure that the software system meets the needs of its stakeholders.

- Elicitation allows for early identification of potential issues or problems with the software system, which can be addressed before they become major obstacles.

- Elicitation promotes collaboration and communication between stakeholders and developers, which helps to build trust and ensure a more successful software development process.

#### Disadvantages of Elicitation:

- Elicitation can be time-consuming and expensive, especially if multiple elicitation techniques are used.

- Elicitation can be difficult if stakeholders have conflicting or unclear requirements or if they are not actively engaged in the process.

- Elicitation can lead to scope creep if requirements are not properly managed and controlled throughout the software development process.

#### Examples of Elicitation Techniques:

- Interviews: One-on-one interviews with stakeholders can be used to gather detailed information about their requirements and expectations for the software system.

- Surveys: Surveys can be used to gather information from a large number of stakeholders in a relatively short amount of time.

- Focus groups: Focus groups can be used to gather feedback and ideas from a group of stakeholders in a structured and collaborative environment.

- Prototyping: Prototyping can be used to create a working model of the software system, which can be used to gather feedback and refine requirements.

#### Applications of Elicitation:

- Elicitation is used in a wide range of software development projects, including the development of web applications, mobile apps, and enterprise software systems.

- Elicitation can also be used in other industries, such as healthcare, finance, and government, to gather requirements for software systems that support their operations and services.

In conclusion, elicitation is a crucial step in the requirement engineering process, and it is essential to ensure that the software system meets the needs of its stakeholders. By using appropriate elicitation techniques and managing requirements effectively, software developers can build successful software systems that meet the expectations of their stakeholders.
