### Categories of Maintenance of Software

Software maintenance is the process of modifying and improving an existing software system after delivery to ensure its continued effectiveness and relevance. There are three main categories of software maintenance, each with its own set of goals and objectives. These categories are:

1. Corrective Maintenance: This type of maintenance is concerned with fixing errors or defects in the software system. Corrective maintenance is typically performed in response to user complaints or bug reports. The goal of corrective maintenance is to restore the correct functioning of the software system. 

Mnemonic: "Corrective maintenance corrects errors."

2. Adaptive Maintenance: This type of maintenance is concerned with modifying the software system to accommodate changes in the environment in which it operates. The environment may include changes to hardware, operating systems, or other software systems that the software system interacts with. The goal of adaptive maintenance is to ensure that the software system remains effective and efficient in the face of changing circumstances.

Mnemonic: "Adaptive maintenance adapts to changes."

3. Perfective Maintenance: This type of maintenance is concerned with improving the software system's performance, functionality, or other non-functional requirements. Perfective maintenance may involve adding new features or capabilities to the software system or improving its usability or performance. The goal of perfective maintenance is to increase the value of the software system to its users.

Mnemonic: "Perfective maintenance perfects the software."

Each of these categories of software maintenance is important for ensuring the continued effectiveness and relevance of a software system. By performing the appropriate type of maintenance, software developers can ensure that their software systems continue to meet the needs of their users and remain competitive in their respective markets.
