#### Formal Technical Reviews (Peer Reviews) Static testing strategy

Formal Technical Reviews (FTRs), also known as Peer Reviews, are a type of static testing strategy that involves a team of reviewers examining a software product for defects, errors, and other issues before it is released to end-users. FTRs are considered a highly effective method of ensuring software quality, as they allow for early detection and correction of defects, thus reducing the cost and time required for software development.

Here are some key points to keep in mind about FTRs:

1. FTRs are conducted by a team of reviewers who have expertise in different areas of the software development process.

2. The purpose of FTRs is to identify defects and errors in the software product before it is released to end-users.

3. FTRs can be conducted at different stages of the software development process, including requirements gathering, design, coding, and testing.

4. FTRs can be done manually or using specialized tools that automate the review process.

5. FTRs can be conducted in different formats, including walkthroughs, inspections, and peer reviews.

6. Walkthroughs involve a team of reviewers going through the software product and discussing any issues they find.

7. Inspections involve a formal review process where a team of reviewers examines the software product in detail and records any defects or issues found.

8. Peer reviews involve a team of reviewers examining the software product independently and then coming together to discuss any issues they found.

9. FTRs can be time-consuming and require a significant amount of effort, but they are highly effective in improving software quality and reducing development costs.

Mnemonics and Learning Tricks:

- Remember the acronym FTRs as "Find and Rectify" to help recall its purpose of identifying defects and errors in software products before release.

- Use the phrase "Teamwork makes the dream work" to remember that FTRs are conducted by a team of reviewers with different expertise to ensure software quality.

- Remember the different formats of FTRs by using the acronym WIP, which stands for Walkthroughs, Inspections, and Peer Reviews.

- Use the phrase "Early detection saves time and money" to remember the benefits of FTRs in reducing development costs and improving software quality.

Overall, FTRs are an essential part of the software development process and should be conducted regularly to ensure high-quality software products.
