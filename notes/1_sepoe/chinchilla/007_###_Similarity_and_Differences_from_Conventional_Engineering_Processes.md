### Similarity and Differences from Conventional Engineering Processes

Similarity:
- Both conventional engineering processes and non-conventional engineering processes aim to develop and design systems or products that meet specific requirements and standards.
- Both processes require a team of experts with diverse knowledge and skills to work together to achieve the desired outcome.
- Both processes involve using tools and techniques to analyze, design, and test the system or product.

Differences:
- Non-conventional engineering processes often involve innovative and unconventional approaches that challenge traditional engineering methods and practices.
- Conventional engineering processes follow a linear and sequential approach, where each stage of the process is completed before moving on to the next stage. Non-conventional engineering processes, on the other hand, often adopt an iterative and flexible approach, allowing for changes and improvements to be made throughout the process.
- Non-conventional engineering processes often prioritize user-centered design, focusing on understanding the needs and preferences of the end-users and incorporating their feedback into the design process. Conventional engineering processes may prioritize technical specifications and functionality over user experience.
- Non-conventional engineering processes often involve rapid prototyping, where early versions of the product or system are quickly built and tested to gather feedback and make improvements. Conventional engineering processes may involve more extensive testing and quality assurance procedures before the final product is released.
- Non-conventional engineering processes may involve interdisciplinary teams, including designers, engineers, scientists, and social scientists. Conventional engineering processes may involve a narrower range of expertise.

- "NCDIF" can be used to remember the differences between non-conventional and conventional engineering processes:

  - C - Conventional (linear)
  - D - Design for users (non-conventional)
  - I - Iterative (non-conventional)
  - F - Flexible (non-conventional)
