### Software Re- Engineering (SR) of Software

Software Re-Engineering (SR) is the examination and alteration of a system to reconstitute it in a new form and the subsequent implementation of the new form.

-   It focuses on reorganizing, restructuring, and reimplementing a software system to improve maintainability and other quality attributes when the existing design and implementation becomes hard to change or understand due to decaying structure, increasing errors, or other issues.
-   The goal is to transform a software system to reduce complexity and improve quality, extend functionality, or adapt to a changed environment.
-   The steps involved are:
    -   Understanding the existing system through reverse engineering techniques like source code analysis, runtime analysis, etc. to extract design and requirements information.
    -   Redesigning the system at a higher level of abstraction to achieve desired quality attributes and functionalities. This may involve re-engineering components/subsystems or reorganizing interactions.
    -   Implementing the redesign by updating, replacing, or rewriting components.
-   Mnemonics/learning tricks: Think of SR as " Software Revival" or " Software Remodeling". The crux is to understand, redesign, and reimplement for better quality and adaptability.
-   Advantages: Improves software quality, reduces complexity and cost of changes, enables adaptation to new requirements or environments.
-   Disadvantages: Requires significant time and effort, can be risky if the original system is poorly understood, limited success for large legacy systems.
-   Examples: Replacing a nested switch-case control structure with object-oriented design, database reorganization, re-implementing a monolithic system as service-oriented architecture, etc.
-   Applications: bring legacy systems up-to-date, integrate components from different systems, reuse or extend the lifetime of software, etc.
