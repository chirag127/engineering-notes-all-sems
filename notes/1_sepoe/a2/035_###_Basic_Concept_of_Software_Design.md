### Basic Concept of Software Design

1. Modularity - The software is divided into modules which are independent and interact with each other through interfaces. This makes the software easier to understand, modify, reuse and maintain.

 Mnemonic - Break down the problem into parts, solve the parts independently, then join the parts.

2. Abstraction - Complex details are hidden and only essential features are exposed. This reduces complexity and focuses on key concepts.

 Mnemonic - Hide the mess, show the best.

3. Hierarchy - The system is organized as a hierarchy of layers where each layer interacts only with adjacent layers. This reduces complexity and enforces modular design.

 Mnemonic - Top to bottom, keep it tight.

4. Loose Coupling - Modules are loosely coupled where they interact through interfaces and have minimal dependencies. This makes the system more robust, flexible and reusable.

 Mnemonic - Loosely hold, flexibility untold.

5. Separation of Concerns - The system is separated into distinct sections that address separate concerns like presentation, business logic, data access, etc. This increases reusability, maintainability and comprehension.

 Mnemonic - One job one section, no confusion no affliction.
