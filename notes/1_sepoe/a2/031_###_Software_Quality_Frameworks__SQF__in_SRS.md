### Software Quality Frameworks (SQF) in SRS

Software Quality Frameworks (SQF) are used in Software Requirements Specification (SRS) to ensure high quality software is developed. Some of the key SQFs used in SRS are:

1. ISO 9126 Quality Model: It defines 6 characteristics of software quality - Functionality, Reliability, Usability, Efficiency, Maintainability, and Portability. These characteristics can be used in SRS to specify quality requirements and constraints for the software to be developed.

2. McCall's Quality Model: It defines 3 dimensions of software quality - Product transition, Product revision, and Product operations. These dimensions can be used in SRS to specify quality requirements from evolutionary, adaptability, and operational perspectives respectively.

3. Dromey's Quality Model: It classifies software quality into 2 categories - External quality (as perceived by users) and Internal quality (as perceived by developers). External quality has characteristics like correctness, understandability, and usability while Internal quality has characteristics like testability, maintainability, and structure. These can be used in SRS to specify user-centric and developer-centric quality requirements.

Advantages of using SQFs in SRS:
- It ensures a comprehensive set of quality aspects are considered for the software.
- It acts as a reference to verify if all necessary quality requirements are specified.
- It makes the quality expectations and constraints more clear, measurable, and testable.

Disadvantages of using SQFs in SRS:
- It can make the SRS lengthy and complex with too many quality-related details.
- The SQF may not cover all quality aspects relevant for the specific software being developed.
- The quality characteristics and dimensions in SQFs can be interpreted in different ways leading to inconsistencies.
