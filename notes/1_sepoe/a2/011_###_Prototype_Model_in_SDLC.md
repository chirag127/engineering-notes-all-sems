### Prototype Model in SDLC

The Prototype Model is a software development model. In this model, instead of freezing the requirements before a design or coding can proceed, a prototype is built to understand the requirements.

* Requirements are collected from the customer.
* A prototype is developed that includes some key features of the final system.
* The prototype is reviewed by the customer and feedback is collected.
* The prototype is refined and enhanced based on the feedback.
* This process continues until the customer is satisfied with the prototype.
* The final system is developed based on the prototype.

Advantages:

* Customer involvement is high. The customer can get a feel of the final system and provide feedback early on.
* Risks are reduced as issues can be identified early on.
* Developers get a better understanding of requirements through the iterative process.

Disadvantages:

* Extra time and effort required to develop prototypes.
* The customer may have difficulty visualizing the complete system from prototypes and may be unable to evaluate interfaces and usability issues.
* The prototype and the final system may differ significantly leading to additional costs.

Applications: When requirements are not well-defined or the system is very complex, the prototype model is useful. Examples include user interfaces and safety critical systems.

 Mnemonics:

* Quickly build something to learn and refine.
* Customer sees and Says - refine and enhance.
* Risks reduced, Requirements cleared.

Learning tricks:

* Compare building a prototype to drafting a rough structure before constructing a building. It lets you visualize and refine the design early on.
* Relate the iterative nature of prototyping to an artist's techniques - multiple iterations of a sketch before creating the final painting.
* Prototyping enables progressive elaboration of requirements through feedback. Link this to the concept of Agile development.
