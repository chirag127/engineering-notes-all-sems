### Feasibility Study in Software Requirement Specification (SRS)

A feasibility study is conducted to determine if a project is practical and worthwhile. It considers things like costs, benefits, and risks to determine if a project should proceed.

In Software Requirement Specification (SRS), a feasibility study helps to assess if a software project is viable and worthwhile by evaluating factors such as:

- Technical Feasibility - Evaluates if the technology needed to develop the software is available or can be developed. Consider things like programming languages, tools, infrastructure, etc. required and if the organization has the technical capability to build the software.
- Economic Feasibility - Evaluates if the software can be developed and maintained at an acceptable cost. Consider costs of development, resources, maintenance, risks, and potential gains/savings to determine if it's economically practical.
- Legal Feasibility - Evaluates if the software can be developed while complying with legal requirements and regulations. Consider applicable laws and ensure the SRS and software comply with them.
- Operational Feasibility - Evaluates if the software can be integrated into operations. Consider the impact on internal processes/resources and how it will affect users. Ensure it's operationally practical.

 Mnemonics/Tricks:
 - TELO: Technical, Economic, Legal, Operational feasibility
 - CA$H: Costs, Acceptability, Savings/Gains, Hazards (risks) for economic feasibility

In detail, the feasibility study in an SRS would:

- Describe the objectives and scope of the feasibility study.
- Evaluate technical feasibility by analyzing the technology, infrastructure, and skills required and if available or developable.
- Evaluate economic feasibility by estimating costs and benefits and determining if the software can be developed and maintained cost-effectively.
- Evaluate legal feasibility by identifying applicable laws and ensuring the SRS and software comply with them.
- Evaluate operational feasibility by assessing the impact on operations and users and ensuring integration is practical.
- Provide a recommendation on if the project should proceed based on the feasibility study.
- Note any constraints, assumptions, or risks that could affect feasibility.

The feasibility study helps determine if a software project is worthwhile to pursue further by evaluating practical and viable factors early on. Conducting a comprehensive feasibility analysis as part of the SRS can help avoid issues later and results in a more successful project.
