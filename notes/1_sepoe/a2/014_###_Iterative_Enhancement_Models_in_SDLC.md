### Iterative Enhancement Models in SDLC

Iterative enhancement models involve incremental releases of the software. The software is developed in iterations and at the end of each iteration, a release of the software is made available. Some of the popular iterative models are:

1. Spiral Model: It is a risk-driven iterative model. The spiral cycle has four quadrants: Planning, Risk Analysis, Engineering and Evaluation. Multiple spirals/iterations may be required based on the complexity of the project and risk involved. Each iteration results in a release to the customer.
Advantages: High quality software is produced, risk management is emphasized.
Disadvantages: Can be a costly model to follow.

2. Incremental Model: Requirements are broken down into increments/phases. Each increment delivers a release to the customer after every iteration. The next increment is planned based on the customer feedback from the previous release.
Advantages: Customer can provide early feedback, flexible model.
Disadvantages: Difficult to estimate costs and timelines for the complete project.

Mnemonics/Learning tricks:
- Spiral model: Imagine software development as spiral staircase where after each revolution, you climb up a step (release a version).
- Incremental model: Imagine building a house brick-by-brick (increment-by-increment) where you can get feedback after building each brick and then proceed to the next.

The iterative models are useful when requirements are not well-defined initially or for large, expensive and complicated projects where incremental releases provide tangible benefits to the customer and risk management is critical.
