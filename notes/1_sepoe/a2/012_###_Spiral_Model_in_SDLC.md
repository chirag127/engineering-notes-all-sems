### Spiral Model in SDLC

The Spiral Model is an iterative software development model. It is a combination of prototype model and waterfall model. In Spiral model, each phase of development passes through a spiral, with activities representing increments of the product development. The Spiral Model usually consists of the following phases:

1. Planning - The project is planned in detail. Requirements are analyzed and risks are assessed.
2. Risk Analysis - The risks associated with the project are identified and assessed. Alternate solutions are evaluated.
3. Engineering - The product is designed and developed. Development activities like coding and testing are performed in an iterative manner.
4. Evaluation - The product is evaluated against the requirements and risks are reviewed. Customer evaluations are conducted and feedback is collected.
5. Evolution - Based on the feedback, the product is evolved by incorporating changes to the requirements, design and functionalities. The spiral then starts again with the next cycle of development.

Advantages:
- High amount of risk mitigation. As risks are identified early, they can be avoided or minimized.
- Strong approval and documentation control. Each phase of the lifecycle must be signed off before proceeding to the next phase.
- Early involvement of customer. Continuous customer feedback helps in building the right product.
- Enables incremental releases of the product. Partial functionality can be released for customers early on.

Disadvantages:
- Can be expensive to implement as each phase of the lifecycle involves repeated iterations.
- Success of the project is highly dependent on risk analysis. If some risks are not identified early on, they can be fatal to the project.
- Not suitable for smaller projects where costs need to be minimized.

Mnemonic: Imagine a spiral staircase going upwards. Each step is a phase - Plan, Risk, Engineer, Evaluate, Evolve. You keep spiraling up the staircase with each iteration of the phases.

Learning trick: Associate each phase of the spiral model with climbing up the steps of a spiral staircase. This makes the phases easier to remember in sequence.
