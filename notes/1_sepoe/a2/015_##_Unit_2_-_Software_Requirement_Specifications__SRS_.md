## Unit 2 - Software Requirement Specifications (SRS)

- SRS stands for Software Requirement Specification. It is a formal document that captures all the requirements for a software project.
- It acts as a blueprint for the development team to build the software.
- SRS includes both functional and non-functional requirements.
- Functional requirements specify the functions or capabilities the system must have, e.g. login, payment, etc.
- Non-functional requirements specify the constraints on the system, e.g. performance, security, reliability, etc.
- SRS is usually created by the client or product owner and developers.
- It helps to avoid misunderstandings and development of unwanted features.
- SRS should be unambiguous, verifiable, modifiable and traceable.

**Mnemonics:**

- Think of SRS as a **S**oftware **R**equirement **S**pecification which acts as the **B**lueprint for developers to build the **S**oftware.

**Benefits:**

- Reduces ambiguity and misunderstanding.
- Forces detailed thinking about the requirements.
- Establishes a baseline for project planning and management.
- Facilitates reviews and feedback.
- Supports testing and quality assurance.

**Disadvantages:**

- Can be time-consuming and expensive to create.
- The requirements may change over time leading to inconsistencies.
- The users may not be able to clearly articulate their needs.
- Developers may not follow the SRS leading to feature creep or scope creep.
