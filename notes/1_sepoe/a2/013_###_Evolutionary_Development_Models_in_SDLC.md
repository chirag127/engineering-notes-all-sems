### Evolutionary Development Models in SDLC

The evolutionary development models in Software Development Life Cycle (SDLC) are:

1. Incremental Model: In this model, the software is developed in increments or parts. Each increment adds functionality to the software. After each increment, testing is performed. This process continues till the complete software is developed.

Advantages:
- Risks are mitigated as development and testing is done in parts.
- The customer can see the software take shape gradually and can provide feedback.

Disadvantages:
- Integration of increments may be difficult.
- The overall architecture of the software may be difficult to design as requirements keep changing.

2. Spiral Model: This model emphasizes risk management. The phases in the spiral are:

- Planning: Determine objectives, risks, alternatives, constraints
- Risk Analysis: Identify and resolve risks
- Engineering: Development and testing
- Evaluation: Evaluate alternatives and determine next phase

The spiral is repeated until the final product is ready. It is suitable for large, expensive and complicated projects.

Advantages:
- High amount of risk management.
- Each phase evaluates risks and alternatives before proceeding to the next phase.

Disadvantages:
- Can be costly to use.
- The outcome is not always predictable due to the emergent nature of the process.

Mnemonics:
- Incremental - Think parts and increments
- Spiral - Think loop and cycles
