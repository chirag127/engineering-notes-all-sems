### Analysis in Requirement Engineering Process in SRS

The analysis phase in Requirement Engineering Process for Software Requirements Specification (SRS) involves the following steps:

1. Identifying the stakeholders - The stakeholders including end users, customers, developers, testers, managers, etc. are identified. Their needs, expectations, and requirements are determined.
2. Understanding the problem - The problem is thoroughly understood. The root cause of the problem is determined. The impacts and effects of the problem are analyzed.
3. Studying the current system - The current system is studied in detail. Its features, functionalities, interfaces, and workings are understood. Its pros and cons are identified.
4. Specifying user requirements - The needs and expectations of the end users and stakeholders are specified. Their inputs are taken and a set of user requirements are determined.
5. Specifying functional requirements - The functional requirements or capabilities of the proposed system are specified. What the system must do to address the user requirements is determined.
6. Specifying non-functional requirements - The non-functional requirements or constraints such as performance, security, reliability, etc. are specified. These define the quality parameters for the system.

Advantages of thorough analysis:
- Accurate understanding of the problem and requirements
- Complete and comprehensive SRS
- Higher chances of satisfying stakeholders
- Lesser rework and changes later

Disadvantages of inadequate analysis:
- Incomplete understanding leading to incorrect SRS
- Dissatisfied stakeholders due to missed requirements
- Increased cost and effort due to rework
