#### Alpha and Beta Testing of Products  software testing strategy

Alpha and Beta testing are two important software testing strategies performed before releasing the software product to the actual customers or public.

Alpha Testing:
- Performed in-house by the QA team.
- The software product is tested in a lab environment.
- Tested for functionality, usability, and compatibility.
- Led by the QA team.
- Iterative process where bugs and feedback are reported to the development team who fix the issues and release a new build.
- Repeated until the product meets the quality standards.

Advantages:
- Inexpensive as done in-house.
- Test environment can be controlled.
- Test cases can be exhaustive as the QA team has full access.

Disadvantages:
- There is a risk of biases as the testers and developers belong to the same organization.
- The lab setup may not exactly replicate the real user conditions leading to some issues left unidentified.

Beta Testing:
- Performed by a select group of customers in a real-world environment.
- Monitored by the QA team.
- Tested for user experience, performance, and usability.
- Feedback and bugs are reported by the customers and fixed by the development team.
- Number of beta testers and testing time varied based on the type of software and company.

Advantages:
- Tested in real-world conditions leading to more accurate results.
- Receiving feedback from actual customers helps in improving the product.
- Acts as a trial before the full-scale launch.

Disadvantages:
- Can be more expensive than alpha testing.
- Lack of control over how beta testers use the software.
- Legal issues if the beta testers are not properly contracted.
