### Requirement Engineering Process in SRS

The requirement engineering process for Software Requirements Specification (SRS) involves the following steps:

1.  **Elicitation**: This is the process of gathering requirements from the customer, users and other stakeholders. Techniques used are interviews, questionnaires, observing users, prototyping, etc. The goal is to capture the right requirements and avoid incorrect or missing requirements.

2.  **Analysis**: The collected requirements are analyzed for consistency, completeness, clarity, conciseness, correctness, unambiguousness, verifiability and modifiability. The analysis may require additional elicitation to resolve issues or gaps.

3.  **Specification**: The analyzed and validated requirements are organized and structured into the SRS document. The SRS should be complete, consistent, unambiguous, verifiable and modifiable. It should specify functional and non-functional requirements, constraints, assumptions and dependencies.

4.  **Validation**: The SRS is validated by stakeholders to ensure it meets their needs and is an accurate representation of the system requirements. Feedback is incorporated and the SRS is revised as needed.

5.  **Management**: Requirements are managed and tracked throughout the software development lifecycle. As changes occur, the SRS is updated and existing requirements are traced to design, code and test artifacts.

**Mnemonics**: A mnemonic for remembering the requirement engineering process steps is **EASIV** for **E**licitation, **A**nalysis, **S**pecification, **I**mplementation, **V**alidation.

**Advantages**: The requirement engineering process leads to high quality requirements that reduce defects, miscommunications and rework. It ensures stakeholders and developers have a shared understanding of the system being built.

**Disadvantages**: The process can be time-consuming and expensive, especially for large or complex systems. It requires skill and experience to execute effectively. If not done well, incorrect requirements may be captured or missed, leading to inappropriate solutions.
