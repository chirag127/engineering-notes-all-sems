### Design Strategies in Software Design

There are several design strategies followed in software design:

1. Divide and Conquer: The problem is divided into smaller subproblems until they become simple enough to be solved directly. The solutions to the subproblems are then combined to solve the original problem.
**Mnemonic:** Break down the problem into small parts. Solving the small parts leads to solving the whole.
**Example:** Binary search, Merge sort, etc.

2. Decomposition: The problem is divided into modules/objects with specific responsibilities/functions. The modules/objects are designed in a hierarchical manner with higher-level modules invoking lower-level modules to perform tasks.
**Mnemonic:** Break down into modules with specific functions. The whole is achieved via the parts working together.
**Example:** Function calls, Object-oriented programming, etc.

3. Abstraction: Only the essential details are represented, ignoring the unnecessary details. This makes the system simpler to understand and work with.
**Mnemonic:** Hide unnecessary details and show only what is important.
**Example:** Abstract classes, Interfaces, etc.

4. Pattern reuse: Using solutions to common problems that have been developed and tested previously. This makes the design more efficient and robust.
**Mnemonic:** Why reinvent the wheel? Use existing proven solutions.
**Example:** Design patterns like Singleton, Factory, Observer, etc.

The strategies can be used individually or in combination based on the problem and requirements. Using the appropriate strategies leads to a clean, efficient, and maintainable software design.
