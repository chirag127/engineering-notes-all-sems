### SQA Plans in SRS

The Software Quality Assurance (SQA) plans in Software Requirements Specification (SRS) document specify the following:

1. Reviews and audits - Planned reviews and audits to be conducted on the SRS as well as at various phases of software development lifecycle (SDLC) to identify defects and ensure quality. E.g. Technical review, Management review, Inspection, Walkthrough, etc.
2. Testing - Planned testing approaches, objectives, activities, and schedules to systematically validate and verify the software and ensure quality. E.g. Unit testing, Integration testing, System testing, Acceptance testing, Regression testing, etc.
3. Configuration management - Plans for managing multiple versions of software and software-related artifacts. E.g. Version control, baseline management, configuration auditing, status accounting, etc.
4. Nonconformance tracking - Plans for logging and tracking defects or issues found in reviews, audits, testing, etc. and managing their resolution. E.g. Defect reporting and tracking system.
5. Corrective action - Plans for analyzing the root cause of issues or nonconformances and taking appropriate actions to eliminate the cause of problems and prevent recurrence.
6. Quality control - Plans for conducting activities to monitor and control the quality of processes and products and take corrective actions to meet the quality objectives. E.g. Collecting metrics, measuring quality factors, etc.

Advantages:
- Ensures quality is built into the software systematically through planning and control.
- Identifies and resolves issues early to reduce rework and associated costs.
- Fosters confidence in the software and satisfies user expectations.

Disadvantages:
- Incurs additional costs and effort for planning and conducting SQA activities.
- Can be time-consuming if not properly planned and managed.

Examples:
- Review checklists, metrics to capture, testing approaches and schedules, bug tracking templates, etc.

Applications:
- Any software development project where achieving and demonstrating quality is important.
