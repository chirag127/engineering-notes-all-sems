#### Formal Technical Reviews (Peer Reviews) Static testing strategy

- Formal technical reviews, also known as peer reviews, are a systematic technique for detecting and eliminating defects early in the software development lifecycle.
- In a formal technical review, a group of reviewers examine software work products line by line to find and remove defects before the work products move into the next phase of development.
- The key steps in a formal technical review are:

1. Planning - Selecting the work products to be reviewed, identifying participants, and planning the review.
2. Overview - Having the author of a work product briefly present an overview of the product to reviewers to orient them to its content and purpose.
3. Preparation - Having reviewers individually examine the work product and identify potential defects.
4. Meeting - Having a group meeting in which reviewers present defects found in preparation and reach consensus on the existence and severity of defects.
5. Rework - Having the author address the defects found in the review and produce a revised work product.
6. Follow-up - Monitoring the resolution of defects and the quality of the reworked product.

- Advantages: Can find and remove a high percentage of defects early, increases development productivity, spreads knowledge among team members, good learning experience for new team members.
- Disadvantages: Can be time-consuming and expensive, requires significant preparation time, success depends on careful planning and competent leadership.
- Mnemonics: Please (Plan, Overview, Prepare, Meeting, Rework) Do (Defect detection and removal) Formal (Technical reviews) Peer (Reviews)
- To learn: Understand each step thoroughly, practice conducting mock reviews, understand pros and cons, know applications in software development lifecycle.
