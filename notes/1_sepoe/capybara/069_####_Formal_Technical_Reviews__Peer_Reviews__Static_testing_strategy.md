#### Formal Technical Reviews (Peer Reviews) Static testing strategy

Formal Technical Reviews, also known as Peer Reviews, is a software testing strategy that involves reviewing software artifacts like requirements, design documents, code, and test plans to identify defects and quality issues. These reviews are conducted by a group of peers who have expertise in the domain and technology used in the software development process. 

Here are some important points to remember about Formal Technical Reviews:

- Formal Technical Reviews are conducted during the early stages of the software development life cycle to identify defects and issues before they propagate to later stages, where they can be expensive and time-consuming to fix.

- The key objective of Formal Technical Reviews is to improve the quality of the software product by ensuring that it meets the specified requirements, is designed well, and is maintainable and extensible.

- Formal Technical Reviews can be conducted in different ways, such as walkthroughs, inspections, and peer reviews. The choice of the review type depends on the type of software artifact being reviewed and the goals of the review.

- Walkthroughs are informal reviews where the author of the artifact presents it to a group of peers who provide feedback and suggestions. Inspections are more formal and structured reviews where the artifact is examined in detail using a checklist or set of predefined criteria. Peer reviews are a hybrid of walkthroughs and inspections, where the author presents the artifact, and the peers provide feedback and suggestions using a predefined set of guidelines or standards.

- To conduct a successful Formal Technical Review, it is essential to have a well-defined process and a set of guidelines or standards that all participants follow. The process should include steps for planning, preparation, review, and follow-up.

- Some advantages of Formal Technical Reviews include improved software quality, reduced development time and cost, increased collaboration and knowledge sharing among team members, and enhanced team morale and motivation.

- Some disadvantages of Formal Technical Reviews include the cost and time involved in conducting them, the potential for conflicts and disagreements among team members, and the need for expertise and training to conduct them effectively.

- Mnemonics and learning tricks for Formal Technical Reviews can be helpful in remembering the key aspects of the process. For example, the acronym FTR can stand for Formal Technical Review, and the mnemonic "STOP" can stand for the four steps in the review process: Set up, Test, Observe, and Proceed.

In conclusion, Formal Technical Reviews are an effective software testing strategy that can help improve the quality of software products by identifying defects and issues early in the development process. By following a well-defined process and using a set of guidelines and standards, teams can conduct successful reviews and achieve their goals of delivering high-quality software products.
