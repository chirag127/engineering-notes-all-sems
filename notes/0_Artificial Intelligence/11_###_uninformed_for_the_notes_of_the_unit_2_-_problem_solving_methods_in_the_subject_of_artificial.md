### Uninformed for the notes of the Unit 2 - Problem Solving Methods in the subject of Artificial Intelligence
Uninformed Search is a problem-solving strategy where the search is performed without knowledge of the state of the problem or the cost of actions. This method is also known as blind search. The main idea is to explore all possible states of the problem until a solution is found or it is proven that no solution exists. The search is performed in a systematic way, usually with a queue data structure, where the next state to be explored is the one that was added first. The main uninformed search algorithms are Breadth-First Search (BFS) and Depth-First Search (DFS). BFS explores all the states at a given depth before moving on to the next level, while DFS explores as far as possible along a single branch before backtracking. Uninformed search is mainly used in problems where the cost of actions is not known or does not matter, such as finding a path between two points in a maze.
