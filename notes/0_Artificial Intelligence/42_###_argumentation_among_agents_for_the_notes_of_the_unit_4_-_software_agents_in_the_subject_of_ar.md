### Argumentation among Agents for the notes of the Unit 4 - Software Agents in the subject of Artificial Intelligence
Argumentation among agents refers to the process of exchanging arguments and counter-arguments between multiple intelligent agents in order to reach a conclusion or make a decision. This process is based on the principles of formal argumentation, which involves the analysis and evaluation of arguments and counter-arguments. 

In the context of software agents, argumentation can be used to resolve conflicts, make decisions, and negotiate with other agents. For example, two software agents may be negotiating over the allocation of resources, and they may use argumentation to reach a mutually acceptable solution. 

In order to facilitate argumentation among agents, a number of techniques and frameworks have been developed. These include argumentation frameworks, such as the Dung framework, and argumentation-based negotiation protocols, such as the Contract Net Protocol. 

Overall, argumentation among agents is an important aspect of software agent design and development, as it enables agents to make decisions and negotiate with each other in a rational and structured manner.
