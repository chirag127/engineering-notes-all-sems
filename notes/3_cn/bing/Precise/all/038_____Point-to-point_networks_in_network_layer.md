### Point-to-point networks in network layer

- Point-to-point networks are a type of network topology in which each connection is a direct link between two endpoints.
- In the network layer, point-to-point networks are used to establish direct connections between two devices, such as two routers or a router and a host.
- Point-to-point networks are commonly used in Wide Area Networks (WANs) to connect remote locations.
- Point-to-point networks can be implemented using various technologies, such as leased lines, Frame Relay, or Asynchronous Transfer Mode (ATM).
- In a point-to-point network, data is transmitted directly from one endpoint to the other, without the need for intermediate devices such as switches or hubs.
- Point-to-point networks are generally considered to be more reliable and secure than other types of networks, as there are fewer points of failure.
- However, point-to-point networks can be more expensive to implement and maintain, as each connection requires a dedicated link.
- A mnemonic to remember the characteristics of point-to-point networks is "Direct, WAN, Various, No intermediate, Reliable, Expensive" (DWVNR-E).
