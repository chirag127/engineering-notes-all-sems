#### LAN standards in local area network

- A local area network (LAN) is a data communication network connecting various terminals or computers within a building or limited geographical area. The connection among the devices could be wired or wireless.
- LAN standards are the specifications and protocols that define the physical and logical aspects of how devices communicate and share resources on a LAN. LAN standards are developed and maintained by various organizations, such as the Institute of Electrical and Electronics Engineers (IEEE), the American National Standards Institute (ANSI), and the International Organization for Standardization (ISO).
- The most widely used and recognized LAN standards are the IEEE 802 family of standards, which cover different types of LAN technologies, such as Ethernet, Token Ring, Wireless LAN, and others . Each IEEE 802 standard has a number and a letter that indicates the type of LAN technology and the focus group that developed it. For example, IEEE 802.3 is the standard for Ethernet, and IEEE 802.11 is the standard for Wireless LAN.
- Some of the main features and characteristics of the IEEE 802 LAN standards are:

  - They use a common logical link control (LLC) sublayer that provides a uniform interface between the upper layers of the network stack and the different media access control (MAC) sublayers of the various LAN technologies.
  - They support different types of network topologies, such as bus, star, ring, and mesh, depending on the LAN technology and the physical medium used .
  - They define different methods of media access control, such as contention-based (e.g., CSMA/CD for Ethernet), token-based (e.g., Token Ring), and polling-based (e.g., FDDI), to coordinate the transmission of frames among the devices on the LAN .
  - They specify different frame formats, addressing schemes, error detection and correction mechanisms, and other parameters for each LAN technology .
  - They support different data rates, ranging from 1 Mbps to 10 Gbps or more, depending on the LAN technology and the physical medium used .
  - They enable interoperability and compatibility among devices from different vendors that comply with the same LAN standard .
