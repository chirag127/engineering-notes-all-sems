# Introductory Concepts of Computer Networks

- A computer network is a system that connects numerous independent computers in order to share information (data) and resources.
- Computer networks help you to connect with multiple computers together to send and receive information.
- Computer networks follow protocols, which define how communications are sent and received.
- Computer networks consist of essential components, such as end devices, media, protocols, and networking devices.
- End devices are devices that send or receive the data in the network, such as computers, printers, and smartphones.
- Media provides connectivity between the end devices, such as cables, fiber optics, or wireless signals .
- Protocols enable communication between two or more end devices, such as TCP/IP, HTTP, and FTP.
- Networking devices are devices that facilitate the transmission and routing of data in the network, such as switches, routers, and wireless access points .
- Computer networks can be classified based on different criteria, such as topology, size, architecture, and functionality.
- Topology refers to the physical or logical arrangement of the network nodes and links, such as bus, ring, star, and mesh.
- Size refers to the geographical area covered by the network, such as local area network (LAN), metropolitan area network (MAN), and wide area network (WAN).
- Architecture refers to the design and structure of the network, such as peer-to-peer (P2P), client-server, and hybrid.
- Functionality refers to the purpose and scope of the network, such as intranet, extranet, and internet.
