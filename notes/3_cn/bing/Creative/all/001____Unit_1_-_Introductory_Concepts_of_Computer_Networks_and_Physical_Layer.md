## Unit 1 - Introductory Concepts of Computer Networks and Physical Layer

- A computer network is a collection of devices that can communicate with each other using a common set of rules or protocols.
- The main components of a computer network are nodes, links, and switches.
  - Nodes are the devices that send and receive data, such as computers, printers, routers, etc.
  - Links are the physical or wireless connections that carry data between nodes, such as cables, fiber optics, radio waves, etc.
  - Switches are the devices that forward data to the appropriate destination based on the address or label of the data, such as hubs, bridges, routers, etc.
- The main benefits of computer networks are sharing resources, data, and services, enhancing reliability and performance, and enabling distributed applications and collaboration.
- The main challenges of computer networks are scalability, security, management, and quality of service.
- A computer network can be classified based on its size, topology, architecture, and protocol.
  - Size refers to the geographical area covered by the network, such as local area network (LAN), metropolitan area network (MAN), wide area network (WAN), etc.
  - Topology refers to the physical or logical arrangement of nodes and links in the network, such as bus, ring, star, mesh, tree, etc.
  - Architecture refers to the design principles and layers of abstraction used to organize the network functions and protocols, such as peer-to-peer, client-server, OSI model, TCP/IP model, etc.
  - Protocol refers to the set of rules and formats used to exchange data between nodes, such as Ethernet, IP, TCP, UDP, HTTP, etc.
- The physical layer is the lowest layer of the network architecture that deals with the transmission and reception of raw bits over a physical medium.
- The main functions of the physical layer are encoding, modulation, multiplexing, and transmission.
  - Encoding is the process of converting bits into signals that can be transmitted over the medium, such as NRZ, RZ, Manchester, etc.
  - Modulation is the process of varying the properties of a carrier signal (such as amplitude, frequency, or phase) according to the data signal, such as ASK, FSK, PSK, QAM, etc.
  - Multiplexing is the process of combining multiple data signals into one composite signal that can be transmitted over a single medium, such as TDM, FDM, WDM, CDMA, etc.
  - Transmission is the process of sending and receiving signals over the medium, such as electrical, optical, or wireless.
- The main performance metrics of the physical layer are bandwidth, bit rate, latency, and error rate.
  - Bandwidth is the range of frequencies that can be transmitted over the medium, measured in Hertz (Hz).
  - Bit rate is the number of bits that can be transmitted over the medium per unit time, measured in bits per second (bps).
  - Latency is the time it takes for a bit to travel from the sender to the receiver, measured in seconds or milliseconds.
  - Error rate is the probability that a bit is corrupted or lost during transmission, measured in percentage or bit error rate (BER).
- The main challenges of the physical layer are noise, attenuation, interference, and distortion.
  - Noise is the unwanted or random signals that are added to the data signal during transmission, such as thermal noise, shot noise, etc.
  - Attenuation is the loss of signal strength or power as it travels over the medium, due to the resistance, capacitance, or inductance of the medium.
  - Interference is the unwanted or overlapping signals that are caused by other sources or devices in the same or adjacent frequency bands, such as crosstalk, jamming, etc.
  - Distortion is the alteration or deviation of the signal shape or waveform due to the non-linear or frequency-dependent characteristics of the medium or the devices, such as delay distortion, amplitude distortion, etc.
- The main techniques to overcome the challenges of the physical layer are amplification, equalization, filtering, error detection, and error correction.
  - Amplification is the process of increasing the signal strength or power to compensate for the attenuation, using devices such as amplifiers or repeaters.
  - Equalization is the process of adjusting the signal frequency or phase to compensate for the distortion, using devices such as equalizers or adaptive filters.
  - Filtering is the process of removing the noise or interference from the signal, using devices such as filters
