### Quality of service in transport layer

- Quality of service (QoS) refers to the ability of a network to provide different levels of service to different types of traffic, such as voice, video, or data.
- QoS in transport layer is mainly concerned with the end-to-end performance of a communication session, such as delay, jitter, bandwidth, reliability, and error rate.
- QoS in transport layer can be achieved by using different protocols, mechanisms, and techniques, such as:
  - TCP and UDP: TCP provides reliable, ordered, and error-free delivery of data, but may incur high overhead and delay. UDP provides fast and low-overhead delivery of data, but does not guarantee reliability, order, or error correction. Depending on the application, either TCP or UDP can be used to provide different QoS levels.
  - Congestion control and flow control: Congestion control is the process of adjusting the sending rate of data packets in response to network congestion, to avoid packet loss and reduce delay. Flow control is the process of regulating the amount of data that a sender can transmit to a receiver, to avoid overwhelming the receiver's buffer. Both congestion control and flow control can improve QoS by preventing network congestion and ensuring smooth data transmission.
  - Stream Control Transmission Protocol (SCTP): SCTP is a transport layer protocol that provides reliable, ordered, and error-free delivery of data, similar to TCP, but also supports multiple streams within a single connection, and allows partial reliability and unordered delivery of data, similar to UDP. SCTP can provide QoS by allowing different types of data to be transmitted in different streams, with different reliability and order requirements.
  - Real-time Transport Protocol (RTP): RTP is a transport layer protocol that provides end-to-end delivery of real-time data, such as audio and video, over IP networks. RTP can provide QoS by using timestamps, sequence numbers, and payload types to synchronize and identify the data packets, and by using RTP Control Protocol (RTCP) to monitor the network conditions and provide feedback to the sender and receiver.
  - Resource Reservation Protocol (RSVP): RSVP is a signaling protocol that allows the sender and receiver of a data stream to request and reserve network resources, such as bandwidth and buffer space, along the path of the data stream, to ensure QoS. RSVP can provide QoS by using admission control, traffic specification, and traffic policing to allocate and manage the network resources for the data stream.
