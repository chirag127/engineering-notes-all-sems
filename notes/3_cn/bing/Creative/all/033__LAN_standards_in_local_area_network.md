#### LAN standards in local area network

- LAN standards are the specifications and protocols that define how devices communicate and share data over a local area network (LAN).
- LAN standards are developed and maintained by various organizations, such as IEEE, ISO, ANSI, and EIA/TIA.
- LAN standards cover different aspects of LAN design and operation, such as physical layer, data link layer, network layer, media access control, network topology, transmission speed, and cabling.
- Some of the common LAN standards are:

  - Ethernet: The most widely used LAN standard, based on the IEEE 802.3 series of specifications. Ethernet uses a bus or star topology, and supports speeds from 10 Mbps to 10 Gbps. Ethernet uses CSMA/CD (Carrier Sense Multiple Access with Collision Detection) as the media access control method, and twisted-pair, coaxial, or fiber-optic cables as the transmission media.
  - Token Ring: A LAN standard based on the IEEE 802.5 specification. Token Ring uses a ring topology, and supports speeds of 4 Mbps or 16 Mbps. Token Ring uses a token-passing scheme as the media access control method, and shielded twisted-pair or fiber-optic cables as the transmission media.
  - FDDI: A LAN standard based on the ANSI X3T9.5 specification. FDDI stands for Fiber Distributed Data Interface, and uses a dual ring topology, and supports speeds of 100 Mbps. FDDI uses a token-passing scheme as the media access control method, and fiber-optic cables as the transmission media.
  - Wireless LAN: A LAN standard based on the IEEE 802.11 series of specifications. Wireless LAN uses radio waves or infrared signals as the transmission media, and supports speeds from 1 Mbps to 600 Mbps. Wireless LAN uses CSMA/CA (Carrier Sense Multiple Access with Collision Avoidance) as the media access control method, and various network topologies, such as ad hoc, infrastructure, or mesh.

- A mnemonic to remember the four common LAN standards is: **E**very **T**eacher **F**ears **W**ireless (Ethernet, Token Ring, FDDI, Wireless LAN).
