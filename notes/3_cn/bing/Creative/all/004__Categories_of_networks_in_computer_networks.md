#### Categories of networks in computer networks

A computer network is a collection of devices that are connected by communication channels to share data and resources. There are different ways to categorize computer networks based on their size, scope, topology, architecture, and purpose. Some of the common categories of networks are:

- **Personal Area Network (PAN)**: A PAN is a network that connects devices within a small range, such as a few meters. A PAN is typically used for personal or individual purposes, such as connecting a smartphone, a laptop, a printer, or a wearable device. A PAN can be wired or wireless, and can use technologies such as Bluetooth, infrared, or USB.

- **Local Area Network (LAN)**: A LAN is a network that connects devices within a limited area, such as a home, an office, a building, or a campus. A LAN is typically used for private or organizational purposes, such as sharing files, printers, or internet access. A LAN can be wired or wireless, and can use technologies such as Ethernet, Wi-Fi, or fiber optic.

- **Metropolitan Area Network (MAN)**: A MAN is a network that connects devices within a large area, such as a city or a region. A MAN is typically used for public or governmental purposes, such as providing broadband services, cable TV, or traffic control. A MAN can be wired or wireless, and can use technologies such as DSL, cable, or microwave.

- **Wide Area Network (WAN)**: A WAN is a network that connects devices across a wide geographical area, such as a country or a continent. A WAN is typically used for global or inter-organizational purposes, such as connecting different LANs, MANs, or other WANs. A WAN can be wired or wireless, and can use technologies such as telephone lines, satellite, or cellular.

- **Wireless Local Area Network (WLAN)**: A WLAN is a network that connects devices using wireless communication, such as radio waves or infrared. A WLAN is a type of LAN that does not require cables or wires to connect the devices. A WLAN can use technologies such as Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, or ZigBee.

- **Wireless Personal Area Network (WPAN)**: A WPAN is a network that connects devices using wireless communication, such as radio waves or infrared. A WPAN is a type of PAN that does not require cables or wires to connect the devices. A WPAN can use technologies such as Bluetooth, infrared, or NFC.

- **Wireless Metropolitan Area Network (WMAN)**: A WMAN is a network that connects devices using wireless communication, such as radio waves or microwave. A WMAN is a type of MAN that does not require cables or wires to connect the devices. A WMAN can use technologies such as WiMAX, LTE, or 5G.

- **Wireless Wide Area Network (WWAN)**: A WWAN is a network that connects devices using wireless communication, such as radio waves or satellite. A WWAN is a type of WAN that does not require cables or wires to connect the devices. A WWAN can use technologies such as cellular, satellite, or GPS.

- **Virtual Private Network (VPN)**: A VPN is a network that connects devices using a secure and encrypted tunnel over a public network, such as the internet. A VPN is typically used for remote or confidential purposes, such as accessing a private network from a different location, or protecting the data from eavesdropping or hacking. A VPN can use technologies such as SSL, IPSec, or PPTP.

- **Peer-to-Peer Network (P2P)**: A P2P network is a network that connects devices directly without using a central server or authority. A P2P network is typically used for distributed or collaborative purposes, such as sharing files, resources, or computing power. A P2P network can use technologies such as BitTorrent, Gnutella, or Napster.

- **Client-Server Network**: A client-server network is a network that connects devices using a central server or authority that provides services or resources to the clients. A client-server network is typically used for centralized or hierarchical purposes, such as storing data, processing requests, or managing access. A client-server network can use technologies such as HTTP, FTP, or SMTP.

- **Mesh Network**: A mesh network is a network that connects devices using multiple paths or links. A mesh network is typically used for resilient or redundant purposes, such as avoiding single points of failure, increasing bandwidth, or improving performance. A mesh network can use technologies such as ZigBee, Z-Wave, or Wi-Fi Direct.
