### Introductory Concepts of Computer Networks

- A computer network is a group of computers that are connected to each other and follow similar usage protocols for the purpose of sharing information and resources.
- A computer network can be classified by its size, topology, architecture, protocols, and purpose. Some common types of computer networks are:
  - LAN (local area network): A network that connects computers over a short distance, such as in an office building, school, or hospital. LANs are typically privately owned and managed.
  - WAN (wide area network): A network that connects computers over a large area, such as across regions or continents. The internet is the largest WAN, connecting billions of computers worldwide. WANs are typically collectively or distributedly owned and managed.
  - WLAN (wireless local area network): A network that connects computers wirelessly, using radio waves or infrared signals. WLANs are a subset of LANs that do not require cables or wires.
  - MAN (metropolitan area network): A network that connects computers within a city or a metropolitan area. MANs are typically owned and managed by cities or government entities.
  - PAN (personal area network): A network that serves one person, such as connecting a smartphone and a laptop. PANs are typically owned and managed by individuals.
  - SAN (storage area network): A network that provides access to block-level storage, which is shared network or cloud storage that appears as a local storage drive to the user. SANs are typically used by enterprises or organizations that need high-performance and reliable data storage.
  - CAN (campus area network): A network that connects computers within a campus, such as a university or a business park. CANs are larger than LANs but smaller than WANs. CANs are typically owned and managed by the campus authorities.
- A computer network consists of essential components, such as:
  - End devices: The devices that send or receive data in the network, such as computers, printers, scanners, etc.
  - Media: The physical or logical means that provide connectivity between the end devices, such as cables, fiber optics, wireless signals, etc.
  - Protocols: The rules and standards that enable communication between the end devices, such as TCP/IP, HTTP, FTP, etc.
  - Networking devices: The devices that facilitate the transmission and routing of data in the network, such as routers, switches, hubs, bridges, etc.
- A computer network follows a layered architecture, which divides the network functionality into discrete levels or layers. Each layer performs a specific task and communicates with the adjacent layers. The most widely used network architecture is the TCP/IP model, which consists of four layers: application, transport, internet, and network access. Another common network architecture is the OSI model, which consists of seven layers: application, presentation, session, transport, network, data link, and physical.
- A computer network can have different topologies, which describe the physical or logical arrangement of the network components. Some common network topologies are:
  - Bus: A network topology in which all the devices are connected to a single cable or backbone. This topology is simple and cheap, but it has low performance and reliability.
  - Star: A network topology in which all the devices are connected to a central device, such as a hub or a switch. This topology is easy to install and manage, but it depends on the central device for communication.
  - Ring: A network topology in which all the devices are connected in a circular fashion, forming a closed loop. This topology is simple and reliable, but it has high latency and low scalability.
  - Mesh: A network topology in which every device is connected to every other device. This topology is robust and fault-tolerant, but it is complex and expensive.
  - Tree: A network topology in which the devices are arranged in a hierarchical structure, with a root node at the top and leaf nodes at the bottom. This topology is scalable and efficient, but it can have high maintenance and cabling costs.
  - Hybrid: A network topology that combines two or more of the above topologies. This topology is flexible and adaptable, but it can be difficult to design and implement.
- A computer network can have different modes of communication, such as:
  - Simplex: A mode of communication in which data can flow in only one direction, such as from a sender to a receiver. This mode is simple and fast, but it does not allow feedback or interaction.
  - Half-duplex: A mode of communication in which data can flow in both directions, but not at the same time.
