#### ARP
- ARP stands for **Address Resolution Protocol**   .
- It is a **communication protocol** that is used for **discovering the link layer address**, such as a **MAC address**, associated with a given **internet layer address**, typically an **IPv4 address**   .
- It is a **critical function** in the **Internet protocol suite**   .
- It was defined in **1982** by **RFC 826**, which is **Internet Standard STD 37**.
- It is a **procedure** that connects an **ever-changing IP address** to a **fixed physical machine address** in a **local area network (LAN)** .
- It is essentially a **translation** of **32-bit addresses** to **48-bit addresses** and vice versa.
- It works by **sending a broadcast message** to all the devices in the LAN, asking for the MAC address of the device that has the desired IP address.
- The device that has the desired IP address **replies** with its MAC address, and the sender **updates** its ARP cache with the new entry.
- The sender can then **communicate** with the device using its MAC address.
- ARP can be **divided** into two types: **static ARP** and **dynamic ARP**.
- Static ARP is when the **IP-MAC mapping** is **manually entered** and **stored** in the ARP cache.
- Dynamic ARP is when the **IP-MAC mapping** is **automatically discovered** and **updated** in the ARP cache.
- Static ARP is **more secure** but **less scalable** than dynamic ARP.
- Dynamic ARP is **more vulnerable** to **ARP spoofing** attacks, where an attacker **sends fake ARP messages** to trick the sender into **updating** its ARP cache with the **wrong MAC address**.
- ARP spoofing can lead to **man-in-the-middle** attacks, where the attacker can **intercept**, **modify**, or **drop** the packets between the sender and the receiver.
- To **prevent** ARP spoofing, some **countermeasures** are: **ARP monitoring**, **ARP filtering**, **ARP authentication**, and **ARP encryption**.
