#### Flow control in link layer in Computer Networks

Flow control is a technique that allows two stations working at different speeds to communicate with each other. It is a set of procedures that tells the sender how much data it can transmit before it overwhelms the receiver. The receiving device has a limited amount of memory and processing power to store and process the incoming data. It needs a mechanism to inform the sender to stop or slow down the transmission temporarily before it reaches its limit. Flow control is a design issue at the data link layer, which is responsible for reliable and efficient delivery of frames between adjacent nodes.

There are two main approaches to flow control: feedback-based and rate-based.

- Feedback-based flow control: In this approach, the sender transmits data to the receiver, and the receiver sends feedback to the sender to allow or restrict the transmission of more data. The sender transmits data only after receiving an acknowledgment from the receiver. This means that the sender adapts its transmission rate to the receiver's processing rate. Examples of feedback-based flow control are stop-and-wait and sliding window.

- Rate-based flow control: In this approach, the sender transmits data at a fixed or variable rate without waiting for feedback from the receiver. The sender adjusts its transmission rate based on some built-in mechanism in the protocol that limits the overall rate at which data is transmitted. This means that the sender does not depend on the receiver's processing rate. Examples of rate-based flow control are leaky bucket and token bucket.

Some techniques of flow control in the data link layer are:

- Stop-and-wait flow control: This is the simplest form of flow control. In this technique, the sender sends one frame at a time and waits for an acknowledgment from the receiver before sending the next frame. The receiver sends an acknowledgment for each frame it receives and processes. The sender can send only one frame at a time, and the receiver can receive only one frame at a time. This technique is inefficient if the propagation delay is much longer than the transmission delay, as the sender has to wait for a long time before sending the next frame.

- Sliding window flow control: This is an improvement over the stop-and-wait technique. In this technique, the sender can send multiple frames at a time without waiting for acknowledgments, and the receiver can receive and process multiple frames at a time. The sender and the receiver maintain a window of frames that can be sent or received at any time. The window size is determined by the available buffer space at the receiver and the bandwidth-delay product of the link. The sender slides its window forward as it receives acknowledgments from the receiver, and the receiver slides its window forward as it sends acknowledgments to the sender. This technique increases the efficiency and throughput of the data transmission.

- Ethernet flow control: This is a mechanism for temporarily stopping the transmission of data on Ethernet family computer networks. The goal of this mechanism is to avoid packet loss in the presence of network congestion. The sender and the receiver use a special media access control (MAC) frame called a pause frame to signal each other to suspend or resume the transmission. The pause frame contains a pause time, which is the duration of the suspension requested by the sender or the receiver. The pause frame can be sent to a unique or a multicast address, depending on whether the flow control is applied to a single link or a group of links. The pause frame is not forwarded by the switches, but is acted upon only within the switch. This mechanism is applicable only to stations configured for full-duplex operation.
