#### Protocols and standards in networks architecture in Computer Networks

- A **protocol** is a set of rules and procedures that govern the communication between two or more devices in a network. Protocols define the format, timing, sequencing, and error control of data exchange. Protocols also specify how devices identify and locate each other, how they initiate and terminate a communication session, and how they manage the flow and congestion of data packets. Examples of protocols are TCP, IP, HTTP, SMTP, etc.
- A **standard** is a set of rules for data communication that are agreed upon by official organizations or industry groups. Standards ensure the compatibility and interoperability of devices and applications from different vendors and platforms. Standards also facilitate the development and innovation of new technologies and services. Examples of standards are IEEE 802.11, ISO/OSI, ANSI X3T9.5, etc.
- Protocols and standards are essential for the design and operation of computer networks. They provide a common framework and language for network devices and applications to communicate with each other. They also enable the modularization and abstraction of network functions and services, which simplifies the network architecture and management.
- Protocols and standards are organized into different **layers** according to their functions and scope. Each layer performs a specific task and relies on the services of the layer below it, while providing services to the layer above it. This is known as the **layered approach** or the **layered model** of network architecture. The most widely used layered models are the **TCP/IP model** and the **OSI model**. The TCP/IP model has four layers: application, transport, internet, and network access. The OSI model has seven layers: application, presentation, session, transport, network, data link, and physical.
- Protocols and standards can be classified into different **types** based on their purpose, scope, and origin. Some common types are:
  - **Network communication protocols**: These are protocols that define how data is transmitted and routed across networks. They include protocols for addressing, encapsulation, fragmentation, multiplexing, error detection, and correction. Examples are IP, TCP, UDP, ICMP, ARP, etc.
  - **Network management protocols**: These are protocols that monitor and control the network performance, configuration, and security. They include protocols for discovery, reporting, logging, auditing, and troubleshooting. Examples are SNMP, DHCP, DNS, NTP, etc.
  - **Network security protocols**: These are protocols that protect the network data and devices from unauthorized access, modification, or disruption. They include protocols for encryption, authentication, authorization, integrity, and non-repudiation. Examples are SSL, TLS, IPSec, Kerberos, etc.
  - **Network application protocols**: These are protocols that enable the exchange of information and services between network applications. They include protocols for web browsing, email, file transfer, remote access, etc. Examples are HTTP, SMTP, FTP, SSH, etc.
  - **De facto standards**: These are standards that are widely adopted and used by the industry or the public, but are not officially approved or endorsed by any organization. They are often established by market leaders, popular products, or common practices. Examples are Ethernet, Wi-Fi, USB, etc.
  - **De jure standards**: These are standards that are formally approved and published by recognized organizations or authorities, such as IEEE, ISO, ANSI, etc. They are often the result of consensus, collaboration, and standardization among various stakeholders. Examples are OSI, IPv6, FDDI, etc.
