## Unit 1 - Introductory Concepts of Computer Networks and Physical Layer

- A computer network is a group of interconnected computing devices that can send or receive data.
- A computing device can be any device that can run a program, such as a computer, tablet, phone, or smart sensor.
- Computer networks can be classified by their size, topology, and protocols .
- The size of a network refers to the number and geographic distribution of the devices in the network.
- The topology of a network refers to the arrangement and connection of the devices in the network.
- The protocols of a network refer to the rules and standards that govern the communication between the devices in the network.
- Some common types of networks are:
  - Local area network (LAN): a network that covers a limited area like a house or school.
  - Wide area network (WAN): a network that extends over a large geographic area and is composed of many LANs.
  - Data center network (DCN): a network used in data centers where data must be exchanged with very little delay.
- The Internet is the world's largest network, which is a WAN that connects millions of LANs and DCNs using the Internet protocols.
- The physical layer is the lowest layer of the OSI network model, which defines the hardware, cabling, wiring, frequencies, pulses, and signals used to transmit and receive data over a physical medium .
- The physical layer is responsible for converting the data bits from the data link layer into electrical, optical, or electromagnetic signals that can be sent over the physical medium.
- The physical layer also defines the types of encoding, modulation, multiplexing, and synchronization used to represent the data bits in the signals.
- The physical layer provides its services to the data link layer, which is responsible for reading and writing data from and onto the line, and detecting and correcting link errors.
- Some common physical layer devices are:
  - Network interface card (NIC): a device that connects a computing device to a network and converts data bits into signals and vice versa.
  - Hub: a device that connects multiple devices in a network and broadcasts the signals received from one device to all other devices.
  - Repeater: a device that amplifies and regenerates the signals received from one device and forwards them to another device.
  - Cable: a medium that carries the signals between devices, such as twisted pair, coaxial, or fiber optic cable.
  - Antenna: a device that converts electrical signals into electromagnetic waves and vice versa, and enables wireless communication.
- Some common physical layer standards are:
  - Ethernet: a family of standards that define the physical and data link layer specifications for LANs, such as the cable types, data rates, and frame formats.
  - Wi-Fi: a family of standards that define the physical and data link layer specifications for wireless LANs, such as the frequency bands, modulation schemes, and security protocols.
  - Bluetooth: a standard that defines the physical and data link layer specifications for short-range wireless communication between devices, such as the frequency hopping, power control, and encryption techniques.
  - 5G: a standard that defines the physical and data link layer specifications for mobile broadband communication, such as the frequency ranges, beamforming, and network slicing techniques.
