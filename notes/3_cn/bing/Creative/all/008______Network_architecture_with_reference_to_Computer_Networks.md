#### Network architecture with reference to Computer Networks

- Network architecture is the design of a computer network    .
- It is a framework for the specification of a network's physical components and their functional organization and configuration, its operational principles and procedures, as well as communication protocols used   .
- Network architecture components include hardware, software, transmission media (wired or wireless), network topology, and communications protocols  .
- Network architecture can be classified into two types: client-server and peer-to-peer .
  - Client-server architecture: A client-server architecture consists of one or more servers that provide services to multiple clients. The clients request services from the servers and the servers respond to the requests. The servers are usually more powerful and reliable than the clients. Examples of client-server architecture are web browsing, email, and online gaming .
  - Peer-to-peer architecture: A peer-to-peer architecture consists of a network of nodes that can act as both clients and servers. The nodes communicate directly with each other without relying on a central server. The nodes are usually equal in terms of power and responsibility. Examples of peer-to-peer architecture are file sharing, video conferencing, and blockchain .
- Network architecture can also be classified into different layers according to the functions and protocols involved. The most common layering model is the OSI (Open Systems Interconnection) model, which consists of seven layers: physical, data link, network, transport, session, presentation, and application   .
  - Physical layer: The physical layer is responsible for transmitting and receiving raw bits over a physical medium, such as copper wire, optical fiber, or radio waves. It defines the characteristics of the medium, such as voltage levels, modulation schemes, and connectors   .
  - Data link layer: The data link layer is responsible for organizing bits into frames and ensuring reliable and error-free transmission over a single link. It defines the addressing, framing, error detection, and flow control mechanisms. Examples of data link layer protocols are Ethernet, Wi-Fi, and PPP   .
  - Network layer: The network layer is responsible for routing packets across multiple links and networks. It defines the addressing, routing, forwarding, and congestion control mechanisms. Examples of network layer protocols are IP, ICMP, and ARP   .
  - Transport layer: The transport layer is responsible for providing end-to-end communication between applications. It defines the port numbers, segmentation, reassembly, reliability, and flow control mechanisms. Examples of transport layer protocols are TCP, UDP, and SCTP   .
  - Session layer: The session layer is responsible for establishing, maintaining, and terminating sessions between applications. It defines the synchronization, checkpointing, and recovery mechanisms. Examples of session layer protocols are RPC, NFS, and SQL  .
  - Presentation layer: The presentation layer is responsible for translating, encrypting, and compressing data between applications. It defines the data formats, encryption schemes, and compression algorithms. Examples of presentation layer protocols are SSL, TLS, and JPEG  .
  - Application layer: The application layer is responsible for providing specific services to users. It defines the user interfaces, commands, and messages. Examples of application layer protocols are HTTP, FTP, and SMTP   .
- A mnemonic to remember the OSI layers from bottom to top is "Please Do Not Throw Sausage Pizza Away" or "All People Seem To Need Data Processing" .
