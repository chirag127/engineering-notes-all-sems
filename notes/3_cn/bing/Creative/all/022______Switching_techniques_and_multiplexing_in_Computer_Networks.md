#### Switching techniques and multiplexing in Computer Networks

Switching techniques are methods of connecting multiple devices in a network and transferring data between them. There are three main types of switching techniques: circuit switching, message switching, and packet switching.

- Circuit switching: In circuit switching, two nodes communicate with each other over a dedicated communication path. The path is established before the data transmission and remains active until the communication is over. The advantage of circuit switching is that it provides a guaranteed and continuous connection between the nodes. The disadvantage is that it wastes bandwidth if the connection is idle or the data rate is low.
- Message switching: In message switching, the whole message is treated as a data unit. The message is stored and forwarded by intermediate nodes until it reaches the destination. The advantage of message switching is that it does not require a dedicated path and can avoid congestion by buffering the messages. The disadvantage is that it introduces delay and overhead due to the storage and forwarding process.
- Packet switching: The packet switching technique is derived from message switching where the message is broken down into smaller chunks called packets. The packets are transmitted independently and may take different routes to reach the destination. The advantage of packet switching is that it can utilize the bandwidth efficiently and adapt to dynamic network conditions. The disadvantage is that it may cause packet loss, duplication, or reordering due to the network errors or congestion.

Multiplexing is a technique of combining multiple signals into one signal over a shared medium. Multiplexing allows the simultaneous transmission of multiple signals across a single data link. There are different types of multiplexing techniques, such as frequency division multiplexing (FDM), time division multiplexing (TDM), wavelength division multiplexing (WDM), and code division multiplexing (CDM).

- Frequency division multiplexing: In FDM, the bandwidth of the channel is divided into several frequency bands, and each signal is modulated by a different carrier frequency. The modulated signals are then combined and transmitted over the channel. The advantage of FDM is that it is simple and robust. The disadvantage is that it may cause interference and crosstalk between the signals.
- Time division multiplexing: In TDM, the time of the channel is divided into several time slots, and each signal is assigned a different time slot. The signals are then transmitted one after another over the channel. The advantage of TDM is that it can avoid interference and crosstalk between the signals. The disadvantage is that it may cause synchronization and framing issues.
- Wavelength division multiplexing: In WDM, the wavelength of the light is divided into several bands, and each signal is modulated by a different wavelength. The modulated signals are then combined and transmitted over the optical fiber. The advantage of WDM is that it can increase the capacity and bandwidth of the optical fiber. The disadvantage is that it may require expensive and complex devices.
- Code division multiplexing: In CDM, each signal is encoded by a different code, and the encoded signals are then combined and transmitted over the channel. The advantage of CDM is that it can provide security and privacy for the signals. The disadvantage is that it may require complex encoding and decoding algorithms.

Statistical multiplexing is a communication link sharing technique, which is used in packet switching. The shared linking is variable in statistical multiplexing, whereas it is fixed in TDM or FDM. This is a strategic application for maximizing the utilization of bandwidth. This can increase the efficiency of network, as well.
