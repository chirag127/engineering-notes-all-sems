#### Network devices in Computer Networks

Network devices are hardware components that connect computers and other devices in a network. They enable data transmission and communication among different nodes in a network. Some of the common network devices are:

- **Router**: A router is a device that forwards packets between different networks based on their destination addresses. A router operates at the network layer of the OSI model and can connect networks with different protocols and architectures. A router can also perform functions such as filtering, security, and quality of service. A router has a routing table that stores the information about the best routes to reach different networks.

- **Switch**: A switch is a device that connects multiple devices in a local area network (LAN) and forwards frames based on their destination MAC addresses. A switch operates at the data link layer of the OSI model and can create separate collision domains for each port. A switch can also perform functions such as VLANs, spanning tree, and port mirroring. A switch has a MAC address table that stores the information about the MAC addresses of the devices connected to each port.

- **Hub**: A hub is a device that connects multiple devices in a LAN and broadcasts frames to all the ports. A hub operates at the physical layer of the OSI model and creates a single collision domain for all the devices. A hub does not perform any filtering or forwarding functions and is considered a passive device. A hub has no memory or intelligence and does not store any information about the devices connected to it.

- **Repeater**: A repeater is a device that amplifies and regenerates signals in a network. A repeater operates at the physical layer of the OSI model and can extend the range of a network segment. A repeater does not perform any filtering or forwarding functions and is considered a passive device. A repeater has no memory or intelligence and does not store any information about the network.

- **Bridge**: A bridge is a device that connects two or more LAN segments and forwards frames based on their destination MAC addresses. A bridge operates at the data link layer of the OSI model and can create separate collision domains for each segment. A bridge can also perform functions such as filtering, security, and traffic shaping. A bridge has a MAC address table that stores the information about the MAC addresses of the devices connected to each segment.

- **Modem**: A modem is a device that modulates and demodulates signals in a network. A modem operates at the physical layer of the OSI model and can convert analog signals to digital signals and vice versa. A modem can also perform functions such as compression, encryption, and error correction. A modem can connect a computer to a telephone line, a cable network, or a wireless network.

- **Firewall**: A firewall is a device that monitors and controls the incoming and outgoing traffic in a network. A firewall operates at the network layer or the transport layer of the OSI model and can apply rules and policies to the packets based on their source and destination addresses, ports, and protocols. A firewall can also perform functions such as logging, auditing, and authentication. A firewall can protect a network from unauthorized access, attacks, and malware.

- **Access point**: An access point is a device that connects wireless devices to a wired network. An access point operates at the data link layer of the OSI model and can create a wireless local area network (WLAN) using radio waves. An access point can also perform functions such as encryption, authentication, and roaming. An access point can provide wireless access to a network for devices such as laptops, smartphones, and tablets.
