#### Network performance and transmission impairments in Computer Networks

- Network performance is the measure of how well a computer network can deliver data and services to its users. It can be evaluated by various metrics, such as throughput, delay, jitter, packet loss, availability, reliability, etc.
- Transmission impairments are the factors that degrade the quality of a signal as it travels through a transmission medium. They can cause errors, distortions, or losses in the data transmission. Some common transmission impairments are noise, attenuation, and distortion.
- Noise is any unwanted signal that interferes with the original signal. It can be caused by various sources, such as thermal noise, shot noise, impulse noise, crosstalk, etc. Noise can affect both analog and digital signals, but it is more difficult to remove from analog signals. Noise can reduce the signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) and increase the bit error rate (BER) of the transmission.
- Attenuation is the decrease in signal strength as it travels through a transmission medium. It can be caused by various factors, such as distance, resistance, absorption, scattering, reflection, etc. Attenuation can affect both analog and digital signals, but it is more noticeable in analog signals. Attenuation can be compensated by using amplifiers or repeaters along the transmission path.
- Distortion is the change in shape or form of a signal as it travels through a transmission medium. It can be caused by various factors, such as delay, frequency response, phase shift, etc. Distortion can affect both analog and digital signals, but it is more significant in analog signals. Distortion can be reduced by using equalizers or filters along the transmission path.

- A mnemonic to remember the three types of transmission impairments is **NAD** (Noise, Attenuation, Distortion).
- A learning trick to understand the difference between noise and distortion is to imagine a radio station. Noise is like the static or hissing sound that you hear when the radio signal is weak or interfered by other signals. Distortion is like the change in pitch or quality of the sound that you hear when the radio signal is delayed or filtered by the transmission medium.
