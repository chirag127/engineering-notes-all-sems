#### Transmission media in Computer Networks

- Transmission media in computer networks are the physical channels that carry data from one device to another using electromagnetic signals .
- Transmission media can be classified into two main types: guided media and unguided media.
- Guided media are those that provide a physical path for the signals, such as wires, cables, or optical fibers. Examples of guided media are twisted pair, coaxial cable, and fiber optic cable .
- Unguided media are those that do not require a physical path for the signals, such as air, water, or vacuum. Examples of unguided media are radio waves, microwaves, infrared, and visible light .
- The choice of transmission media depends on several factors, such as bandwidth, cost, distance, reliability, security, and interference .
- Bandwidth is the amount of data that can be transmitted per unit time over a medium. Higher bandwidth means faster data transmission and higher quality of service.
- Cost is the amount of money required to install, maintain, and operate a medium. Lower cost means more affordability and accessibility.
- Distance is the maximum length of a medium that can support data transmission without significant loss or distortion. Longer distance means more coverage and scalability.
- Reliability is the degree of consistency and accuracy of data transmission over a medium. Higher reliability means less errors and retransmissions.
- Security is the level of protection of data transmission from unauthorized access, modification, or interception. Higher security means more confidentiality and integrity.
- Interference is the unwanted noise or signals that affect the quality of data transmission over a medium. Lower interference means more clarity and stability.

- A mnemonic to remember the types of transmission media is **GURU**: Guided, Unguided, Radio, and Ultraviolet.
- A mnemonic to remember the factors that affect the choice of transmission media is **BC DRIS**: Bandwidth, Cost, Distance, Reliability, Interference, and Security.
