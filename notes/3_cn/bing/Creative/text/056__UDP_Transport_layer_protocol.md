#### UDP Transport layer protocol

- UDP stands for User Datagram Protocol and it is one of the core communication protocols of the Internet protocol suite used to send messages (transported as datagrams in packets) to other hosts on an Internet Protocol (IP) network.
- UDP is a simple, connectionless and unreliable transport layer protocol that does not require prior communication to set up communication channels or data paths .
- UDP provides checksums for data integrity, and port numbers for addressing different functions at the source and destination of the datagram. It has no handshaking dialogues, and thus exposes the user's program to any unreliability of the underlying network; there is no guarantee of delivery, ordering, or duplicate protection.
- UDP is suitable for purposes where error checking and correction are either not necessary or are performed in the application; UDP avoids the overhead of such processing in the protocol stack. Time-sensitive applications often use UDP because dropping packets is preferable to waiting for packets delayed due to retransmission, which may not be an option in a real-time system.
- UDP is also used for simple query-response protocols such as the Domain Name System or the Network Time Protocol, for datagrams suitable for modeling other protocols such as IP tunneling or remote procedure call and the Network File System, and for bootstrapping or other purposes without a full protocol stack, such as the DHCP and Trivial File Transfer Protocol .
- UDP header is an 8-bytes fixed and simple header, consisting of four fields: source port, destination port, length, and checksum . The source and destination port numbers are each 16 bits long, and identify the port number of the source and destination applications. The length field is 16 bits long, and specifies the length of the UDP datagram, including the header and the data. The checksum field is 16 bits long, and is the 16-bit one’s complement of the one’s complement sum of the UDP header, the pseudo-header of information from the IP header, and the data, padded with zero octets at the end (if necessary) to make a multiple of two octets . The checksum calculation is not mandatory in UDP, but it is recommended for error detection.
- UDP does not provide any error control or flow control mechanisms, and relies on IP and ICMP for error reporting. UDP also does not provide any sequencing or reordering of the datagrams, and leaves it to the application layer to handle any out-of-order or missing packets.
- UDP is more efficient than TCP in terms of both latency and bandwidth, as it does not incur the overhead of establishing and maintaining connections, acknowledging and retransmitting packets, and managing congestion control . UDP is also suitable for multicasting, as it supports packet switching and allows multiple recipients to receive the same datagram.
- Some of the applications that use UDP as a transport layer protocol are: NTP (Network Time Protocol), DNS (Domain Name Service), BOOTP, DHCP, NNP (Network News Protocol), RTP (Real-time Transport Protocol), SNMP (Simple Network Management Protocol), and VoIP (Voice over IP) .
