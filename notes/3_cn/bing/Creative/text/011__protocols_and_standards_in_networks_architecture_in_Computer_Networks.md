#### Protocols and Standards in Networks Architecture in Computer Networks

- A **protocol** is a set of rules and procedures that govern the communication between devices in a network. Protocols specify how data is formatted, transmitted, received, and acknowledged. Protocols also define the functions and responsibilities of different network layers and devices. Protocols enable interoperability and compatibility among different network components and applications.
- A **standard** is a formal document that establishes the specifications and guidelines for a protocol or a technology. Standards are developed and approved by recognized organizations, such as IEEE, ISO, ANSI, etc. Standards ensure that products and services from different vendors and manufacturers can work together in a network. Standards also promote innovation and competition in the market.
- There are two types of standards: **de facto** and **de jure**. De facto standards are those that have not been officially approved by any organization, but have become widely adopted and accepted by the industry or the public due to their popularity or convenience. For example, Apple and Google have their own de facto standards for their products and services. De jure standards are those that have been officially approved and endorsed by a recognized organization, such as ANSI, ISO, IEEE, etc. These standards are mandatory and authoritative for the domains they cover. For example, TCP/IP, SMTP, DNS, etc. are de jure standards for data communication protocols.
- Protocols and standards are essential for the design, implementation, and operation of computer networks. They provide a common framework and language for network devices and applications to communicate and exchange information. They also facilitate the integration and interoperability of different network technologies and architectures. Protocols and standards also enable the development and improvement of network performance, security, and reliability.
