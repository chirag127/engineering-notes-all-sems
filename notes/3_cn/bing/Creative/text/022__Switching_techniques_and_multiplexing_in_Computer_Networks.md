#### Switching techniques and multiplexing in Computer Networks

Switching techniques and multiplexing are two methods that are used to share the communication resources among multiple users or devices in a network. Switching techniques determine how the data is transferred from one node to another over a network, while multiplexing techniques determine how multiple signals are combined into one signal over a shared medium.

- Switching techniques can be classified into three types: circuit switching, message switching, and packet switching.
  - Circuit switching: In circuit switching, two nodes communicate with each other over a dedicated communication path. The path is established before the data transmission and remains active until the communication is over. The path consists of a series of physical links between intermediate nodes, such as switches or routers. Circuit switching is suitable for real-time applications that require a constant and reliable connection, such as voice or video calls. However, circuit switching is inefficient in terms of bandwidth utilization, as the dedicated path cannot be used by other nodes during the communication. 
  - Message switching: In message switching, the whole message is treated as a data unit. The message is stored at each intermediate node and forwarded to the next node when the link is available. The message may take different routes to reach the destination, depending on the network conditions. Message switching does not require a dedicated path or a reservation of bandwidth. However, message switching introduces delays and overheads due to the store-and-forward process. Message switching is suitable for applications that can tolerate delays and do not require a fixed order of delivery, such as email or file transfer. 
  - Packet switching: The packet switching technique is derived from message switching, where the message is broken down into smaller chunks called packets. Each packet has a header that contains the source and destination addresses, the sequence number, and other information. The packets are transmitted independently over the network and may take different routes to reach the destination. The packets are reassembled at the destination node based on the sequence number. Packet switching does not require a dedicated path or a reservation of bandwidth. However, packet switching may introduce delays, errors, or losses due to the network congestion or failures. Packet switching is suitable for applications that can tolerate some degree of unreliability and variability, such as web browsing or streaming. 

- Multiplexing techniques can be classified into four types: frequency-division multiplexing (FDM), time-division multiplexing (TDM), wavelength-division multiplexing (WDM), and statistical multiplexing.
  - Frequency-division multiplexing (FDM): Bandwidth on a communications link is divided into subchannels of different frequency widths, each carrying a signal in parallel with the other signals. Analog radio transmissions commonly use FDM to multiplex signals across radio waves. Analog cable TV also uses FDM, sending multiple channels down the same strands of coaxial cable. An offshoot of FDM is orthogonal frequency-division multiplexing (OFDM), which transmits subchannel frequencies closer together, while enabling them to overlap and still remain separate. 
  - Time-division multiplexing (TDM): Bandwidth on a communications link is divided into time slots, each carrying a signal in turn with the other signals. Digital telephony and cellular networks use TDM to multiplex signals across wires or wireless channels. TDM can be synchronous or asynchronous, depending on whether the time slots are fixed or variable. An offshoot of TDM is code-division multiplexing (CDM), which assigns a unique code to each signal and allows them to share the same bandwidth simultaneously. 
  - Wavelength-division multiplexing (WDM): Bandwidth on a fiber optic cable is divided into subchannels of different wavelengths, each carrying a signal in parallel with the other signals. WDM is used to increase the capacity of fiber optic networks by transmitting multiple signals over the same fiber. WDM can be coarse or dense, depending on the number and spacing of the wavelengths. An offshoot of WDM is space-division multiplexing (SDM), which uses multiple spatial modes or cores within a single fiber to multiplex signals. 
  - Statistical multiplexing: Statistical multiplexing is a communication link sharing technique, which is used in packet switching. The shared linking is variable in statistical multiplexing, whereas it is fixed in TDM or FDM. This is a strategic application for maximizing the utilization of bandwidth. This can increase the efficiency of network, as well.
