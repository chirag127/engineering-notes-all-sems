## Unit 2 - Link layer in Computer Networks and Medium Access Control and Local Area Networks

- The link layer is the lowest layer in the Internet protocol suite, the networking architecture of the Internet. 
- The link layer is the group of methods and communications protocols confined to the link that a host is physically connected to. 
- The link layer provides the functional and procedural means to transfer data between network entities and may also provide the means to detect and possibly correct errors that can occur in the physical layer. 
- The link layer is concerned with local delivery of frames between nodes on the same level of the network. Data-link frames, as these protocol data units are called, do not cross the boundaries of a local area network. 
- The link layer is sometimes described as a combination of the OSI's data link layer (layer 2) and physical layer (layer 1). 
- The data link layer, or layer 2, is the second layer of the seven-layer OSI model of computer networking. This layer is the protocol layer that transfers data between nodes on a network segment across the physical layer. 
- The data link layer is divided into two sublayers: the media access control (MAC) sublayer and the logical link control (LLC) sublayer. 
- The MAC sublayer is responsible for controlling how devices access the shared medium, such as Ethernet, Wi-Fi, or Token Ring. The MAC sublayer defines various protocols for multiple access resolution, such as ALOHA, CSMA, CSMA/CA, and CSMA/CD.  
- The LLC sublayer is responsible for providing reliable and error-free communication between network devices. The LLC sublayer defines various protocols for flow control, error control, and multiplexing, such as IEEE 802.2, HDLC, and PPP. 
- The local area network (LAN) is a type of network that connects devices within a limited geographic area, such as a building, a campus, or a home. LANs typically use wired or wireless technologies to provide high-speed and low-cost communication. 
- The LANs can be classified into different types based on their topology, such as bus, ring, star, tree, or mesh. The topology defines how the devices are physically or logically connected to each other. 
- The LANs can also be classified into different types based on their technology, such as Ethernet, Wi-Fi, Token Ring, or FDDI. The technology defines the standards and protocols for data transmission and media access control.
