#### Organization of the Internet

- The Internet is a global network of interconnected computers and devices that communicate using standardized protocols and procedures.
- The Internet is composed of many smaller networks, called internets, that are connected by routers and gateways.
- The Internet is organized at four levels: hardware, access, navigation, and community.
  - Hardware: The physical devices and infrastructure that enable data transmission and processing, such as computers, servers, cables, satellites, etc.
  - Access: The providers and services that allow users and organizations to connect to the Internet, such as Internet service providers (ISPs), broadband, dial-up, etc.
  - Navigation: The tools and systems that help users find and access information and resources on the Internet, such as domain names, IP addresses, web browsers, search engines, etc.
  - Community: The sites and platforms that host and facilitate online interactions and content creation, such as social media, blogs, forums, wikis, etc.
- The Internet is governed by a variety of organizations and entities that have different roles and responsibilities, such as:
  - Internet Society (ISOC): A non-profit organization that promotes the development, evolution, and use of the Internet for the benefit of all people.
  - Internet Corporation for Assigned Names and Numbers (ICANN): A non-profit organization that coordinates the global domain name system (DNS) and IP address allocation.
  - Internet Engineering Task Force (IETF): A voluntary group of network engineers and experts that develops and maintains the technical standards and protocols of the Internet.
  - World Wide Web Consortium (W3C): An international consortium of members that develops and maintains the standards and guidelines for the web.
  - Internet Governance Forum (IGF): A multi-stakeholder platform that facilitates dialogue and policy-making on Internet-related issues.
