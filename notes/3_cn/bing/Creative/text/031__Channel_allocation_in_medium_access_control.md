#### Channel allocation in medium access control

- Channel allocation is the process of assigning different frequency channels to different users or devices in a wireless network to avoid interference and improve performance.
- Medium access control (MAC) is the protocol that coordinates the access to the shared wireless channel among multiple users or devices.
- Channel allocation and medium access control are closely related, as the channel allocation scheme affects the MAC performance and vice versa.
- There are different types of channel allocation schemes, such as fixed, dynamic, and hybrid.
  - Fixed channel allocation assigns a fixed number of channels to each user or device, regardless of their traffic demand or channel condition. This scheme is simple and avoids collisions, but it may waste bandwidth or cause congestion.
  - Dynamic channel allocation assigns channels to users or devices on demand, based on their traffic demand or channel condition. This scheme is more flexible and efficient, but it may incur more overhead or complexity.
  - Hybrid channel allocation combines fixed and dynamic channel allocation, such as dividing the channel into fixed and variable parts, or using fixed channels for control and dynamic channels for data.
- There are different types of medium access control protocols, such as contention-based, reservation-based, and hybrid.
  - Contention-based MAC protocols allow users or devices to compete for the channel access, such as using random backoff or carrier sensing. This protocol is simple and decentralized, but it may cause collisions or unfairness.
  - Reservation-based MAC protocols allow users or devices to reserve the channel access in advance, such as using polling or slot allocation. This protocol is more reliable and fair, but it may waste bandwidth or cause delay.
  - Hybrid MAC protocols combine contention-based and reservation-based MAC protocols, such as using contention for reservation and reservation for data, or using contention in some slots and reservation in others.
