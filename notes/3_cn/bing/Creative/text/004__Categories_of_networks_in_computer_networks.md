#### Categories of networks in computer networks

A computer network is a system of interconnected devices that share information, data and resources among each other. Depending on the size, scope, design and purpose of the network, there are different types of networks. Some of the common categories of networks are:

- **Personal Area Network (PAN)**: A PAN is the smallest and simplest type of network. It connects devices within the range of an individual, usually within 10 meters. Most PANs are wireless and use technologies like Bluetooth or infrared. Examples of PANs are USB, computer, phone, tablet, printer, etc. 
- **Local Area Network (LAN)**: A LAN is a network that connects devices within a limited area, such as a home, school, office or building. A LAN usually consists of two or more computers connected to a server. LANs use technologies like Ethernet or Wi-Fi. Examples of LANs are networking in a home, school, library, laboratory, college, office, etc.  
- **Wide Area Network (WAN)**: A WAN is a network that connects devices over a large geographical distance, such as across cities, countries or continents. A WAN can also be a collection of LANs that communicate with each other. The most common example of a WAN is the Internet. WANs use technologies like routers, switches, fiber-optic cables or satellites.  
- **Wireless Local Area Network (WLAN)**: A WLAN is a type of LAN that uses wireless technology, such as Wi-Fi, to connect devices. A WLAN does not require physical cables or wires to communicate. The most common example of a WLAN is Wi-Fi. WLANs use technologies like access points, wireless routers or wireless adapters.  
- **Campus Area Network (CAN)**: A CAN is a type of network that covers a larger area than a LAN, but smaller than a WAN. A CAN is usually used in places like a school or college campus. A CAN connects several buildings within the campus. Examples of CANs are networks that cover schools, colleges, buildings, etc.  
- **Metropolitan Area Network (MAN)**: A MAN is a type of network that covers a larger area than a LAN, but smaller than a WAN. A MAN is usually used in places like a city or a town. A MAN connects several LANs within the city or town. Examples of MANs are networks that cover cities, towns, airports, etc.  
- **Storage Area Network (SAN)**: A SAN is a type of network that connects storage devices, such as hard disks, tape drives or optical drives, to servers or other devices. A SAN provides high-speed data transfer and access to the storage devices. A SAN is usually used in data centers, enterprises or organizations that require large amounts of storage. Examples of SANs are networks that store databases, backups, archives, etc.  
- **System-Area Network (SAN)**: A SAN is a type of network that connects high-performance computers, such as supercomputers or clusters, to each other. A SAN provides low-latency and high-bandwidth communication between the computers. A SAN is usually used for parallel processing, distributed computing or scientific applications. Examples of SANs are networks that perform simulations, calculations, modeling, etc.  
- **Passive Optical Local Area Network (POLAN)**: A POLAN is a type of LAN that uses optical fiber technology, such as fiber to the home (FTTH) or fiber to the building (FTTB), to connect devices. A POLAN does not require active components, such as switches or routers, to communicate. A POLAN is usually used for providing high-speed Internet, voice and video services to the devices. Examples of POLANs are networks that cover homes, buildings, hotels, etc.  
- **Enterprise Private Network (EPN)**: An EPN is a type of network that connects the devices and locations of a single organization or company. An EPN is usually owned and managed by the organization or company. An EPN can use technologies like LAN, WAN, WLAN, VPN, etc. Examples of EPNs are networks that cover offices, branches, headquarters, etc.
