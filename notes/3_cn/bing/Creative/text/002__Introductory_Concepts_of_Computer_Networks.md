### Introductory Concepts of Computer Networks

- A computer network is a system that connects numerous independent computers in order to share information (data) and resources .
- Computer networks enable communication for every business, entertainment, and research purpose. The internet, online search, email, audio and video sharing, online commerce, live-streaming, and social networks all exist because of computer networks.
- Computer networks follow protocols, which define how communications are sent and received. Protocols enable communication between two or more end devices .
- Computer networks use media, such as cables, fiber optics, or wireless signals, to connect nodes, such as computers, routers, and switches. The media provides connectivity between the end devices.
- Computer networks use networking devices, such as routers, switches, access points, and cables, to facilitate data transmission and routing. Networking devices can also provide security, management, and quality of service functions.
- Computer networks can be classified based on their size, scope, topology, architecture, and purpose. Some common types of computer networks are LAN (local area network), WLAN (wireless local area network), WAN (wide area network), MAN (metropolitan area network), PAN (personal area network), SAN (storage area network), and CAN (campus area network) .
- Computer networks can also be classified based on their transmission methods, such as circuit switching, packet switching, or message switching. Circuit switching establishes a dedicated connection between two nodes, packet switching divides data into small units called packets and sends them over a shared network, and message switching stores and forwards data as a whole over a network.
- Computer networks can also be classified based on their design approaches, such as layered architecture, peer-to-peer architecture, or client-server architecture. Layered architecture divides the network functionality into hierarchical levels, such as the OSI model or the TCP/IP model, peer-to-peer architecture allows nodes to communicate directly without a central server, and client-server architecture relies on a central server to provide services to clients.
- Computer networks can also be classified based on their applications, such as the internet, intranet, extranet, VPN (virtual private network), or VoIP (voice over internet protocol). The internet is the global network of networks that connects billions of computers worldwide, intranet is a private network that uses internet technologies within an organization, extranet is a private network that allows authorized external users to access some of the intranet resources, VPN is a secure network that uses encryption and tunneling to connect remote users or sites over a public network, and VoIP is a technology that allows voice communication over the internet.
