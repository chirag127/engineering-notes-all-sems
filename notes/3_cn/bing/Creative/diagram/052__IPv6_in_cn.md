IPv6 is the latest version of the Internet Protocol, which assigns unique addresses to devices and networks on the internet. IPv6 has a much larger address space than IPv4, allowing for more devices and users to connect to the internet. China is one of the countries that is actively promoting the deployment and adoption of IPv6, as it faces a shortage of IPv4 addresses and a growing demand for internet services.

According to the Notice on Accelerating the Large-scale Deployment and Application of IPv6 issued by China's Central Cyberspace Affairs Commission and Cyberspace Administration in July 2021, China has set a goal of running a single-stack IPv6 network by 2030, and has ordered various sectors and industries to upgrade their infrastructure and applications to support IPv6. China also aims to have 700 million active IPv6 users by 2023, and 800 million by 2025, as well as 200 million and 400 million Internet of Things devices using IPv6 by the same years, respectively .

A single-stack IPv6 network means that only IPv6 is used as the default protocol for communication, and IPv4 is not needed or supported. This would simplify the network architecture and management, and avoid the performance and security issues caused by the transition mechanisms that enable IPv4 and IPv6 to coexist. However, achieving a single-stack IPv6 network also requires a high level of coordination and cooperation among various stakeholders, such as network operators, service providers, content providers, device manufacturers, and end users.
