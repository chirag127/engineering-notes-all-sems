#### Medium Access Control and Local Area Networks

Medium access control (MAC) is a sublayer of the data link layer that regulates the access of multiple devices to a shared medium, such as a wireless channel or a wired network. MAC protocols are designed to avoid or resolve collisions, which occur when two or more devices transmit data at the same time on the same medium.

Local area networks (LANs) are networks that connect devices within a limited geographic area, such as a home, office, or campus. LANs can use different types of media, such as twisted-pair cables, coaxial cables, or wireless signals. LANs can also use different types of MAC protocols, such as contention-based or token-passing.
