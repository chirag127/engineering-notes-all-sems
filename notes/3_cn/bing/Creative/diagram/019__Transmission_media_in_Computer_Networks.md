Transmission media in computer networks are the physical channels that carry data signals from one node to another. There are two types of transmission media: guided and unguided. Guided media use physical wires or cables to transmit data, while unguided media use wireless signals or waves to transmit data.

The diagram above shows some examples of transmission media in computer networks. The computers are connected to different types of network devices, such as routers, switches, and hubs, which in turn are connected to different types of transmission media, such as telephone lines, coaxial cables, and optical fibers. These transmission media have different characteristics, such as bandwidth, speed, noise immunity, cost, and distance. For example, optical fibers have high bandwidth and speed, but are more expensive and require more careful installation than coaxial cables. Telephone lines have low bandwidth and speed, but are widely available and cheap. Coaxial cables have moderate bandwidth and speed, but are susceptible to interference and attenuation.
