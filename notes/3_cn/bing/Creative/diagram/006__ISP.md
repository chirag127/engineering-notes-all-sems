An ISP (Internet Service Provider) is a company that provides internet access and related services to customers. ISPs can be classified into different categories based on the type of services they offer, such as access providers, hosting providers, transit providers, virtual providers, etc.

The diagram shows the following components:

- Customers: These are the end-users who pay the ISP for internet access and related services. Customers can connect to the ISP through various methods, such as telephone lines, cable, wi-fi, or fiber optics.
- Access Providers: These are the ISPs that provide the physical connection and network infrastructure for customers to access the internet. Access providers can also offer other services, such as email, web hosting, security, etc.
- Core Network: This is the backbone network of the ISP that connects the access providers to the transit providers, hosting providers, and peers. The core network is usually composed of high-speed routers and switches that can handle large volumes of traffic.
- Transit Providers: These are the ISPs that provide connectivity between different ISPs. Transit providers charge other ISPs for carrying their traffic across their network. Transit providers can also offer other services, such as peering, caching, content delivery, etc.
- Hosting Providers: These are the ISPs that provide web hosting, cloud computing, and other online services for customers and other ISPs. Hosting providers usually have data centers that store and process data for various applications and websites.
- Peers: These are the other ISPs that exchange traffic with the ISP through peering agreements. Peering is a voluntary arrangement between ISPs to share traffic without paying fees. Peering can improve network performance, reduce costs, and increase redundancy.
