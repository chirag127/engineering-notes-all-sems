#### LAN standards in local area network

Local Area Networks (LANs) are networks that connect devices within a limited area, such as a home, office, or campus. There are several LAN standards that define the various aspects of the network, including the physical and data link layers. In this section, we will discuss the most common LAN standards used in local area networks.

1. Ethernet:
   - Ethernet is the most widely used LAN standard.
   - It defines the physical and data link layers of the network.
   - Ethernet uses a bus or star topology and supports speeds of up to 10 Gbps.
   - It uses Carrier Sense Multiple Access with Collision Detection (CSMA/CD) as a media access control method.

2. Wi-Fi:
   - Wi-Fi is a wireless LAN standard that uses radio waves to transmit data.
   - It operates in the unlicensed 2.4 GHz and 5 GHz frequency bands.
   - Wi-Fi supports speeds of up to 10 Gbps.
   - It uses Carrier Sense Multiple Access with Collision Avoidance (CSMA/CA) as a media access control method.

3. Token Ring:
   - Token Ring is a LAN standard that uses a token-passing mechanism to avoid collisions.
   - It operates at speeds of up to 16 Mbps.
   - Token Ring uses a ring topology and supports up to 260 devices on a single network.

4. FDDI:
   - FDDI (Fiber Distributed Data Interface) is a LAN standard that uses fiber-optic cables to transmit data.
   - It operates at speeds of up to 100 Mbps.
   - FDDI uses a dual ring topology and supports up to 500 devices on a single network.

5. ATM:
   - Asynchronous Transfer Mode (ATM) is a LAN standard that uses fixed-size cells to transmit data.
   - It operates at speeds of up to 622 Mbps.
   - ATM uses a point-to-point topology and supports up to 65,535 virtual connections on a single network.

In conclusion, understanding the various LAN standards used in local area networks is essential for network administrators and IT professionals. Each LAN standard has its own advantages and disadvantages, and choosing the right standard for a particular network depends on various factors such as the network size, topology, and speed requirements.
