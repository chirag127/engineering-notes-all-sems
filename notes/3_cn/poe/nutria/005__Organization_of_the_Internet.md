
#### Organization of the Internet

1. The Internet is a global network of computers that are connected to each other via the Transmission Control Protocol/Internet Protocol (TCP/IP). 
2. This network is composed of millions of individual networks that are connected together. 
3. The Internet is organized into a hierarchy of networks, with the smallest networks being linked to larger networks. 
4. At the top of the hierarchy is the Internet backbone, which is composed of the largest networks. 
5. These networks are connected to each other via high-speed connections, such as fiber-optic cables. 
6. The Internet backbone is responsible for routing data from one network to another. 
7. At the next level down is the regional networks, which are connected to the backbone via high-speed connections. 
8. These regional networks are responsible for providing access to the Internet for local users. 
9. At the lowest level of the hierarchy are the individual networks, which are responsible for providing access to the Internet for local users. 
10. These networks are connected to the regional networks via lower-speed connections, such as dial-up or DSL.
