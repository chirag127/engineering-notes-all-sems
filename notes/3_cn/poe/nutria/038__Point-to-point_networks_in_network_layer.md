
### Point-to-point networks in network layer

1. Point-to-point networks are a type of computer network topology in which two nodes are connected directly to each other through a dedicated physical connection.

2. This type of network is typically used to connect two geographically distant locations, or to establish a high-speed, low-latency connection between two computers.

3. Point-to-point networks are typically used in wide area networks (WANs), where they are used to connect two locations that are far apart.

4. Point-to-point networks are typically used in applications such as voice over IP (VoIP), video conferencing, and file transfer.

5. Point-to-point networks can be implemented using either copper or fiber optic cables.

6. Point-to-point networks are typically more reliable than other types of networks, such as mesh or ring networks, as they are not affected by the failure of other nodes in the network.
