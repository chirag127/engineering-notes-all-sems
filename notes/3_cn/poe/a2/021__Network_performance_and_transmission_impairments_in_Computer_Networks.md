#### Network performance and transmission impairments in Computer Networks.

1. Bandwidth - The amount of data that can be transmitted through a network at a given time. Higher bandwidth leads to faster data transmission. Limited bandwidth can lead to slower access of resources and latency.
2. Latency - The time taken for data to travel from source to destination. Higher latency leads to slower response times and can affect real-time applications like video calling. Causes include limited bandwidth, long physical path, and processing delays.
3. Jitter - Variation in latency between packets. Can affect real-time applications and voice/video quality. Causes include congestion, improper queuing, etc. Buffering and delay compensation techniques are used to minimize jitter.
4. Packet Loss - Loss of packets during transmission due to congestion, corruption, etc. Can affect application performance and lead to retransmission of packets, increasing latency. Techniques like forward error correction (FEC) and retransmission are used to recover from packet loss.
5. Security attacks - Malicious actors can launch attacks to breach network security, access data, or disrupt network performance. Examples include denial-of-service (DoS) attacks, man-in-the-middle (MITM) attacks, and spoofing attacks. Strong authentication and encryption techniques are used to prevent such attacks.

The above points cover the major network performance parameters and transmission impairments that can impact the performance of applications and services running on the network. Optimizing and monitoring these parameters is critical for providing a good quality of service and user experience.
