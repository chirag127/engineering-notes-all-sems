### Physical Layer in Computer Networks

1. Converts binary data into signals and transmits over a physical medium.
2. Specifies electrical, mechanical, procedural, and functional specifications for activating, maintaining, and deactivating a physical link between end systems.
3. Responsible for the following major functions:
 - Bit synchronization: Synchronizing the bit rates between the sender and receiver.
 - Data transmission: Converting bits into signals and transmitting over the medium.
 - Data encoding: Converting bits into signals suitable for transmission.
 - Data decoding: Converting received signals back into bits.
4. Different transmission media and respective techniques:
 - Twisted pair cable: Used with Ethernet, employs carrier sense multiple access with collision detection (CSMA/CD).
 - Coaxial cable: Thick coaxial cable used for 10BASE5 Ethernet, thin coaxial cable for 10BASE2 Ethernet.
 - Optical fiber: Uses light pulses to transmit data, provides very high bandwidth with low attenuation.
 - Wireless: Radio waves used as transmission medium, enables mobility but can have interference.
