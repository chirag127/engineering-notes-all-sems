#### Switching techniques and multiplexing in Computer Networks.

1. Switching techniques:
- Circuit switching: Establishes a dedicated end-to-end path between two nodes before the actual transmission of data. inefficient use of bandwidth. Example: Traditional telephone network.
- Message switching: Messages are forwarded from one node to the next without establishing a dedicated end-to-end path. Each message may take different route to reach the destination. Example: Email.
- Packet switching: Data is split into packets which are then transmitted independently and can take different routes to reach the destination. Packets are reassembled at the destination. Example: Internet.

2. Multiplexing:
- FDM (Frequency Division Multiplexing): Dividing the bandwidth into different frequencies to carry multiple signals simultaneously. Example: Radio and television broadcasts.
- TDM (Time Division Multiplexing): Dividing time into slots and allocating each slot to a different signal. Example: Traditional telephone networks.
- CDM (Code Division Multiplexing): Using different codes to differentiate between multiple data streams transmitted over the same bandwidth. Example: 3G/4G cellular networks.
