### Challenges of conventional systems compared to Big Data

Conventional systems have been used for a long time to process and manage data. However, with the advent of Big Data, conventional systems face several challenges. Some of the key challenges of conventional systems compared to Big Data are:

1. **Data Volume:** Conventional systems are not designed to handle large volumes of data. They have limitations in terms of storage capacity, processing power, and memory. Big Data, on the other hand, can handle massive amounts of data without any issues.

2. **Data Variety:** Conventional systems are designed to handle structured data, such as data in a database. They are not capable of handling unstructured data, such as social media feeds, sensor data, and multimedia files. Big Data, on the other hand, is designed to handle both structured and unstructured data.

3. **Data Velocity:** Conventional systems are not designed to handle real-time data processing. They have limitations in terms of processing speed and response time. Big Data, on the other hand, is designed to handle real-time data processing and can provide immediate insights.

4. **Data Veracity:** Conventional systems are not designed to handle data quality issues, such as incomplete or inconsistent data. Big Data, on the other hand, can handle data quality issues and can provide accurate insights even with incomplete or inconsistent data.

5. **Cost:** Conventional systems can be expensive to set up and maintain. They require specialized hardware and software, along with trained personnel. Big Data, on the other hand, can be set up on commodity hardware and can be maintained with minimal resources.

6. **Scalability:** Conventional systems have limitations in terms of scalability. They can only handle a limited amount of data and users. Big Data, on the other hand, is designed to be highly scalable and can handle massive amounts of data and users.

7. **Security:** Conventional systems are vulnerable to security threats, such as hacking and data breaches. Big Data, on the other hand, is designed to be highly secure and can provide advanced security features such as encryption and access control.

In summary, conventional systems face several challenges when compared to Big Data. Big Data is designed to handle the challenges of large volumes of data, diverse data sources, real-time data processing, data quality issues, cost, scalability, and security. As a result, Big Data has become an essential tool for organizations to gain insights from their data and make informed decisions.
