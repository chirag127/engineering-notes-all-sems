#### Introduction to NoSQL databases

NoSQL databases are the databases that do not use the traditional table-based relational database model. Instead, they use a variety of data models, including document, key-value, graph, and column-family. NoSQL databases are designed to handle large volumes of unstructured and semi-structured data and can scale horizontally across multiple servers.

Here are some important points to know about NoSQL databases:

- NoSQL databases are designed to handle large volumes of unstructured and semi-structured data, making them ideal for big data applications.

- NoSQL databases use a variety of data models, including document, key-value, graph, and column-family.

- Document databases store data in a document format, such as JSON, and allow for flexible and dynamic schemas.

- Key-value databases store data as a collection of key-value pairs and can be used for caching and session management.

- Graph databases store data as nodes and edges to represent relationships between data points.

- Column-family databases store data as columns instead of rows, allowing for efficient querying and storage of large amounts of data.

- NoSQL databases can scale horizontally across multiple servers, making them highly scalable and fault-tolerant.

- NoSQL databases offer high performance and low latency when compared to traditional relational databases.

- NoSQL databases do not support the complex transactions and joins that relational databases do, making them less suitable for applications that require complex queries and transactions.

Learning Trick:

One useful mnemonic to remember the different types of NoSQL databases is "Do Keep Good Coffee." Each letter stands for a different type of NoSQL database:

- D: Document
- K: Key-value
- G: Graph
- C: Column-family

Examples:

Some popular NoSQL databases include MongoDB, Cassandra, Redis, and Neo4j.

Applications:

NoSQL databases are used in a variety of applications, including social media, e-commerce, gaming, and big data analytics. For example, MongoDB is used by companies like eBay and Forbes, while Cassandra is used by Apple and Netflix.

Advantages and Disadvantages:

Advantages:

- Can handle large volumes of unstructured and semi-structured data
- Highly scalable and fault-tolerant
- High performance and low latency
- Flexible and dynamic schemas

Disadvantages:

- Do not support complex transactions and joins
- Limited support for ad-hoc querying
- Lack of standardization and interoperability between different NoSQL databases.
