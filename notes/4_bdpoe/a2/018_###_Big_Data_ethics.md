### Big Data ethics

The following points discuss the important ethical considerations with Big Data:

1. Privacy: The personal and sensitive data collected from users and systems can be misused if not handled properly. It is important to take consent from users before collecting their data and using it for analysis. The data should be anonymized and aggregated before using it for analytics to maintain privacy.

2. Bias: The data and algorithms can reflect biases of its creators. For example, facial recognition systems have shown signs of racial and gender bias due to not having diverse data. It is important to have diversity in data and teams to avoid such biases. The algorithms should be audited for such biases before deployment.

3. Job disruption: As AI and automation technologies powered by Big Data get deployed, many existing jobs may be eliminated or transformed. This can disrupt many lives. The changes should be gradual to give people time to adapt and retrain themselves. Universal basic income can also be explored to address such job losses.

4. Manipulation: There have been cases of data manipulation by companies and people to influence opinions or stock prices. The analytics and data should be transparent and accountable to avoid misleading people or manipulating them. The algorithms should be explainable to determine any manipulation.

5. Access: The benefits of Big Data and AI may not be distributed equally across all sections of society. This can further increase inequality. Affordable and accessible solutions should be made available to all to enable equal opportunity. Digital literacy and education can help address this issue.

**Mnemonics:**

- Privacy: Take consent, aggregate data
- Bias: Diverse data and teams, audit algorithms
- Job disruption: Gradual changes, retraining, basic income
- Manipulation: Transparency, explainability
- Access: Affordable access, digital literacy

**Examples and applications:**

- Healthcare analytics to personalized patient care while maintaining privacy
- Recommendation systems with diverse data and explainable algorithms
- Predicting job transformations and retraining programs
- Transparent financial algorithms and systems
- Accessible education and skills development opportunities
