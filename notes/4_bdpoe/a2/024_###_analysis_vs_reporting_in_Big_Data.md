### Analysis vs Reporting in Big Data

Analysis in Big Data refers to the process of inspecting and manipulating the data to uncover hidden patterns, unknown correlations, market trends, customer preferences and other useful business information. The goal is to extract actionable insights and make predictions from the data that can provide business value.

Reporting in Big Data refers to the process of aggregating and organizing the data into a presentable format for easy understanding and consumption. The goal is to convey the insights and trends in the data to the stakeholders in a simple and intuitive way using data visualizations, charts, graphs, etc.

Key differences:

- Analysis focuses on discovering insights while reporting focuses on communicating insights
- Analysis requires data mining and machine learning techniques while reporting requires data visualization techniques
- Analysis outputs are trends, patterns and predictions while reporting outputs are reports, dashboards, infographics, etc.

Mnemonics:

- A for Analysis and discover insights
- R for Reporting and communicate insights

Learning Tips:

- Hands-on practice of analysis and reporting on sample data sets
- Understanding the goals and outputs of analysis and reporting
- Knowing the techniques and tools used for analysis and reporting e.g. SQL, Python, R, Tableau, Power BI, etc.

Examples:

- Analyzing customer data to find purchasing patterns and predict next product to buy
- Reporting sales data in the form of regional charts and graphs for business stakeholders

Applications:

- Retail: Analyze customer data and report purchasing trends
- Banking: Analyze loan data and report risk patterns
- Healthcare: Analyze patient data and report disease trends
