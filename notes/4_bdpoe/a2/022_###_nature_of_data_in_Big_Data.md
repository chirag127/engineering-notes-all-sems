### Nature of Data in Big Data

The nature of data in Big Data refers to the characteristics of the data in terms of the 3 V's - Volume, Velocity and Variety.

**Volume**: The volume of data refers to the sheer amount of data present. Big Data systems handle data of sizes from terabytes to petabytes to exabytes. The data is too large to be stored and processed by traditional systems.

**Velocity**: The velocity of data refers to the speed at which the data is generated and processed. In many cases, the data needs to be processed in near real-time to derive business value from it. The speed of generation and processing is beyond the capabilities of traditional systems.

**Variety**: The variety of data refers to the different forms of data present. The data can be in structured, semi-structured or unstructured forms. Structured data refers to the data that resides in fixed fields within a record or file. Semi-structured data refers to the data that does not have a strict data model or is not organized in a pre-defined manner. Unstructured data refers to the data that does not have a data model and is unorganized such as text, audio, video, etc. Traditional systems handle only structured data while Big Data systems can handle all varieties of data.

**Other characteristics**: In addition to the 3 V's, the data in Big Data can be inaccurate, sparse, noisy and biased. The data may contain missing values or outliers and may not be representative of the entire population. Special techniques are required to handle such data.
