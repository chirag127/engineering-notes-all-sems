## History of Big Data Innovation

-   **1970s:** The origin of Big Data can be traced back to the 1970s. The US government funded projects to analyze satellite images to study weather and geographical patterns. This led to the development of relational databases and data storage technologies.
-   **1980s:** In the 1980s, Walmart started using databases and data analytics to manage supply chain and gain business intelligence. This was one of the first business applications of Big Data.
-   **1990s:** In the 1990s, the growth of the Internet and digital technologies led to an explosion in data generation. This data was largely unstructured and companies started realizing the potential value in analyzing this data. However, the computational power and analytics tools were limited, which constrained the progress.
-   **2000s:** In the early to mid-2000s, companies like Google, Facebook, and Amazon started utilizing Big Data and data analytics at massive scales to understand user behavior, serve targeted ads, and make personalized recommendations. This led to advancements in data collection, storage, and processing technologies. The term "Big Data" was coined during this period to refer to these datasets that were huge in volume and variety and growing exponentially.
-   **2010s and beyond:** In the 2010s, Big Data and data analytics became increasingly democratized. Open source tools and cloud-based platforms made it easier for companies to store and process data. There were also improvements in machine learning, deep learning, and artificial intelligence which enabled automated discovery of complex patterns in data. Today, Big Data and AI continue to enable innovations and disrupt various industries.

-   Think '90s: Internet and digital data growth but limited analytics capabilities
-   Think 2000s: Google, Facebook, and Amazon pioneered Big Data and analytics
-   Think 2010s: Big Data and analytics democratized with open source tools and cloud platforms
